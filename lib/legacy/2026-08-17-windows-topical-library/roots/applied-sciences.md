Applied sciences translate knowledge from the natural and formal sciences into practices, technologies, and systems that meet human needs. Where pure science asks what is true, applied science asks what can be built, grown, healed, or transmitted.

This root holds five domains:

**Agriculture** covers the cultivation of food, fiber, and land — including traditional practices, soil science, crop systems, and the political economy of food production.

**Communication** covers the transmission of information between agents: linguistics, semiotics, media, and the technical infrastructure of signaling. Because communication is foundational to almost everything else in the library, cross-references here are dense.

**Domesticity** covers the organization of household and home: domestic labor, material culture, and the built environments of everyday life.

**Medicine** covers health and healing: anatomy, pharmacology, neurology, public health, and the clinical and social dimensions of illness and care.

**Technology** covers designed artifacts and engineered systems — from mechanical tools to software infrastructure to the theories of technical practice.
