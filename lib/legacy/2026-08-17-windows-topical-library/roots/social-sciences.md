The social sciences study human societies and the relationships between individuals within them. They apply systematic methods — empirical, interpretive, and critical — to questions about how people organize themselves, distribute resources, make decisions, and maintain or challenge order.

This root holds seven domains:

**Anthropology** covers the study of human cultures, societies, and evolution — including cultural anthropology, ethnography, archaeology, and the critique of anthropology's colonial history.

**Economics** covers the production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services: microeconomics, macroeconomics, political economy, and heterodox traditions including degrowth, commons theory, and solidarity economics.

**Education** covers the theory and practice of teaching and learning: pedagogy, curriculum, educational philosophy, and the politics of schooling.

**Law** covers legal systems, jurisprudence, and the theory of rights and obligations: natural law, legal positivism, critical legal theory, and the comparative study of legal traditions.

**Politics** covers power, governance, and the organization of collective decision-making: political theory, comparative politics, international relations, and the study of social movements.

**Public administration** covers the practice and theory of government and public institutions: bureaucracy, policy implementation, and the relationship between administrative capacity and democratic governance.

**Sociology** covers the structure and dynamics of human societies: institutions, stratification, norms, collective behavior, and the interplay between social structure and individual agency.
