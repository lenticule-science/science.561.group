  • 2021-02-04
    • After-market research

      So, I don't know what I'm doing, yet. But I know I want to diversify my holdings within Esther's IRA, ASAP, and without selling the Gamestop I shouldn't have, the only way to do that is to add more to the fund. (Not through transfers, but in the books: increase my liability to Esther.)

      The only thing I had time to do any research on was Silicon Motion Technology: their earning report1 came out earlier today, so I decided to take a look. Despite supply issues, they did better than expected. So, I'm going to take a small equity position in them.

      That will move me from exclusively a single consumer staple (if you can consider video games a staple? Apparently the "markets" do…) to also a chip manufacturer. Obviously, I'm highly ambivalent about the morality of participation in this at all, but I use computers, I think they're useful, and if I'm required to hold capital or be slowly killed by my environment, I'll try and choose to put it toward what I think is good. (Or, if I am putting it toward something I think is bad, I need to invest more heavily - substantially more heavily - against it, and work toward future divestment.2)

      In addition, I've decided to take small positions in YOLO and CNBS, two cannabis-focused equity trust funds, one focused on Turtle Island, and the other all of the Earth.

      XLV, is an equity trust fund representing the healthcare sector that I've bought into, and ARKG is focused on genetics research. This is probably my least ethical holding I'm planning to buy into. My excuse is that I don't plan on holding it for very long, I just want to put more into health care beside XLV, which has been underperforming and likely will continue to for a while. (All the money in the healthcare market is tied up in COVID research and other cutting-edge things. I'd really prefer to keep my healthcare holdings, long-term, in XLV and then try and find small companies whose medical approach I agree with and diversify across them.)

      There's also ICLN, a "clean energy" ETF, that I'll use to represent my energy holdings until I can figure out something better. IFRA is an infrastructure ETF, and under the Biden administration I can assume that'll grow, possibly quite well.

      VCLO is an ETF focused on "market disruptors" in electric cars, cybersecurity, and cloud technology. Definitely an iffy place to put money, but spreads me into domestic IT. Not something to hold onto though, if I can help it.

      KBE is an investment in banks, and that brings me across quite a few domestic sectors, with a small bit of global touch.

      To go further globally, I'm going to take a small position in EWW, which (simplified) represents the Mexican market. Mexico is probably the country whose politics I understand the most right now, though through the lens of the Zapatistas, so… why not.

      In other parts of the market, I've seen continued talk of institutional investors continuing their migration into cryptocurrencies, with Bitcoin serving as the launchpad. For example, Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance took a $100 million position in BTC last December.3

      With the new Biden administration, Bitcoin is likely to see government interest as well, after being left relatively un-observed during the 2017 crypto bubble. In addition, Ethereum serves as an application platform, and has become one of the largest other crypto platforms.

      In addition to crypto, there's the world of decentralized finance (DeFi). I'm not familiar with that, and currently don't have a way to buy into it: the services that would let me require a state ID.
