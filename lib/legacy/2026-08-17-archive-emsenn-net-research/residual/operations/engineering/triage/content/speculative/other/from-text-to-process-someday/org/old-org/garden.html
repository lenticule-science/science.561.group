Makȟá Šá Ethnoecological Workshop

Introduction

Makȟá Šá Ethnoecological Workshop is a place to develop contemporary indigenous ways of living within a North American settler-community, and explore actions to live in reciprocity with the Earth.

Summary for Settlers: This is a small community garden in Chapel Hill, North Carolina.

planning B4: Northwest Bowbar Bed

Overview

Of all the beds currently [2020-07-07 Tue] a part of the workshop, this bed gets the best light, and receives runoff from Bowbar's patio during heavy rains. Unfortunately the soil is mostly sand right now: good for the abundance of peppers and (either melons or cucumbers) I planted there, but not good for a long-term planting.

Plans

With the long sun and dark wall of Bowbar to its immediate north, I'm thinking this bed will stay fairly warm even in the winter, since it's protected from the wind by that wall, which'll also soak up heat.

That's making me wonder if it can't be used to grow a few things that otherwise might struggle in our winter: two that come to mind are Aloe vera and Stevia rebaudiana. Those will both do well in the well-drained soil, but Stevia requires a fair bit more water than Aloe, so the Aloe would have to go on the eastern, higher side, while the stevia can go more toward the south.

But to accomplish this I'll have to amend the soil heavily with organic matter, fired clay chips, and charcoal.
