A **predicate object-projection** is a morphism in the [predicate category](predicate-category.md), denoted $f : q \to p$, induced by the collapse of the entity-pair index to the object diagonal:

$$(s, o) \mapsto (o, o).$$

The induced fiber-algebra map $f^* : A_p \to A_q$ acts cellwise:

$$f^*(v)(s, o) = v(o, o).$$

The resulting predicate $q$ has values that depend only on $p$'s object-diagonal content: at every cell $(s, o)$, $q$ inherits whatever value $p$ takes at $(o, o)$. The subject coordinate $s$ becomes irrelevant — it doesn't enter the definition of $q$'s value at all. Object-projection is the dual of [subject-projection](predicate-subject-projection.md), and one of the four canonical generator classes of the [predicate category](predicate-category.md).

## What object-projection encodes

Object-projection is the formal source of *unary predicates that report on the object* of a binary relation. While [subject-projection](predicate-subject-projection.md) extracts subject-diagonal content (the predicate becomes effectively a property of the subject alone), object-projection extracts object-diagonal content (the predicate becomes effectively a property of the object alone).

Examples:

- A predicate *target-is-employed* (effectively unary on the object) appears as the object-projection of *employs*, where the diagonal content of *employs* records the employment status when the object coincides with itself.
- A predicate *recipient-is-eligible* appears as the object-projection of *eligible-for-receiving-from-self*, broadcast across all subject coordinates.

## Subject vs. object projection

The two projections capture the two ways of deriving a unary predicate from a binary one:

| Projection | Diagonal | Resulting unary content |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject-projection** | $(s, o) \mapsto (s, s)$ | depends on the subject only |
| **Object-projection** | $(s, o) \mapsto (o, o)$ | depends on the object only |

Both projections collapse one of the cell's two entity coordinates onto the other. Subject-projection collapses object onto subject; object-projection collapses subject onto object. The resulting binary predicate is constant in the collapsed coordinate.

## Composition with converse

Object-projection and subject-projection are interconvertible via [converse](predicate-converse.md): composing object-projection with converse yields subject-projection, and vice versa. The swap $(s, o) \mapsto (o, s)$ converts collapse-onto-object-diagonal into collapse-onto-subject-diagonal:

$$f_{\mathrm{conv}} \circ f_{\mathrm{op}} = f_{\mathrm{sp}}$$

at the generator-class level. The four canonical generators are not entirely independent — they admit composition relations of this kind, which together encode the predicate category's structure under $\mathrm{Sym}(E)$-equivariance.
