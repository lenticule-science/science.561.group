** Looking at structure editing and movement in Org-mode
   :PROPERTIES:
   :EXPORT_DATE: [2020-12-30 Wed 09:37]
   :END:

   #+index: Text editing!Methods!Structure editing!"Looking at structure editing and movement in Org-mode"

   [[file:../phil_hagelberg.org][Phil Hagelberg]] announced the currently-scheduled speakers for this year's [[file:../fennelconf.org][FennelConf]], and the thing he's going to be talking about is [[file:../structure_editing.org][structure editing]]. It's something I've been thinking about, ever since reading through [[file:../stepan_parunashvili.org][Stepan Parunashvili]]'s [[file:../stepan_parunashvili_an_intuition_for_lisp_syntax.org]["An Intuition for Lisp Syntax"]], where at the end they talk about how [[file:../programming_languages.org][programming languages]] in the [[file:../lisp.org][LISP]] family easily facilitate working with the code as a structure. (I also saw, somewhere on the [[file:../fediverse.org][Fediverse]], someone post a demonstration video of an [[file:../ide.org][IDE]] they're developing that /only/ allows for structure editing.

   It's gotten me thinking that I'm not that familiar with /structure editing/ and movement within [[file:../org_mode.org][Org-mode]]. /(I also, at present, don't have any sort of structure stuff set up in [[file:../groundhog_emacs.org][Groundhog Emacs]] for working with/ LISP /code either, but I'm also not really working intensively with any/ LISPs /right now, either.)/

   So, while I wait on things this morning, I figured I'd take a look at the the Org-mode manual's [[https://orgmode.org/manual/Document-Structure.html#Document-Structure][chapter on document structure]]. 

   One thing I've learned just now is there's a setting to help catch "invisible edits." From the documentation on =org-catch-invisible-edits=: "Check if in invisible region before inserting or deleting a character."

   I've set it to =show-and-error= for now, since that seems to be a good way to learn where the feature will speak up, but I'll probably toggle it it just "show" later; I tend to be pretty aware when I'm at my keyboard.

** Planning my next Teraum role-playing campaign

   The last [[file:../role_playing_game_campaign.org][role-playing game campaign]] I set in the [[file:../world.org][world]] of [[file:../teraum.org][Teraum]] was called /Brave Old World/. (Now that name is used for a [[file:../brave_old_world.org][gaming system]].) In it, there were two player groups, the /Red Team/ and the /Blue Team/. The Red Team all had migrated to Ack, the largest city, from across the biggest ocean, and found themselves through a series of mishaps working with the Red Union, the largest government. The other /Blue Team/ was part of a secret society trying to figure figure out if the Red Union was using magic, which was supposed to have vanished in an apocalypse 80 years prior.

   I've recently decided I'd like to start a new campaign, with more of a /West Marches/ vibe: I'd like to start with a big group of players, and then just, whoever is available when I'm available to narrate a session, that's who plays.

   To accommodate this, it's common to have all the players work out of some sort of hub for adventure. I've been stuck on how to accomplish this. I had a few other criteria in mind. I wanted to set the campaign the spring after the previous campaign. Over the winter, the Red Union would have firmed up their presence across the area they're called the /Greater Ack Metropolitan Enclave/, or /GAME/. New neighborhoods would be showing up around the existing towns, and new towns would be showing up too, to provide housing for the people displaced by the /Ack Fire/, that spring.

   With high aspirations, the /Red Union/ wants to keep convincing more communities to start working with them. One way they're exploring that, beyond taking advantage of displacements caused by their own disasters, is through propaganda. To facilitate the creation of that propaganda, they're building a settlement in the southern part of /Arathel County/, that they're calling /Rosebanks/. There, artists will be able to share backdrops for painting portraits, screenwriters can coordinate their plays so they aren't competing to tell the same contemporary events, and so on.

   This town is where the people who will become our adventurers will come, from all around the /GAME/, looking for work to do.

   I'm not sure what adventures will come from this, but I like this as the idea for the setting!
