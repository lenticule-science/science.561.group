****** <2019-05-15 Wed 07:25>
I've just woken up - or just gotten to my computer after having woken
up too early, and I'm sitting here looking at my "personal record," an
Org-mode file that I'd like to use as my main method of tracking my
goals and past activities.

Right now the file has 4 real sections, somewhat conveniently making
an acronym, *LOAT*:
- Logs
- Objectives
- Actions
- Tasks

Objectives are for big picture goals. Each one should have a checklist
of how to measure its completion.

Actions are things to do.
****** <2019-05-15 Wed 11:10>
I thankfully fell back asleep for a while. Now I'm sitting at my desk,
my kitty happy staring over my shoulder while I type.

So, actions are things to do, and are usually made up of tasks, which
are granular things that can be done.

What I haven't done yet is write an objective for my recordkeeping,
because, well, that's a hard thing to think about.

I'm going to start by adding the objective /Keep good records/, the
results of which I'll say are keeping track of all obligations,
personal inventory, work done, and interactions.

Easier said than done. Let me try and write some actions to get to
those results:

For keeping track of my obligations, I think I'll want /capture
templates/, a quick way of adding data to Org-mode... but before I can
really do that, I'll need to write a little schema for what
Objectives, Actions, and Tasks contain...

Which should probably be written into my recordkeeping manual... which
I don't think I have yet.
****** <2019-05-15 Wed 12:43>

***** 2019-05-17 Friday

****** <2019-05-17 Fri 11:53>
Today I plan on working on a few small things around the apartment
building, but my main plans are to write a short letter to my parlour,
to try and explain, mostly to myself, how it is I'm going to try and
prioritize projects moving forward. I'm fairly comfortable with my job
and so have the opportunity to step back and give some consideration
to what would be best for my sense of fulfillment, long-term.

In that spirit I've recently been trying to feel more comfortable
identifying as a writer, and feeling comfortable believing that
writing fantasy writing is something worth doing. I've been chewing
that thought over the past few days, especially yesterday since Esther
had the day off, and so I was just sitting in a car for a while.

...Actually I just realized, in trying to write about it here, what I
mean to be thinking:

Teraum - my fantasy setting, /is/ a MUD. It's just not necessarily an
/online/ MUD.

So, for my memory, the backstory:

When I was little I wrote fantasy stories and played the Discworld
MUD. That made me want to write my own MUD to have people be able to
play in my world, the way they could play in Pratchett's. But making a
MUD is hard, and programming has never been what I've been focused on,
so it's been a poor way to implement my fantasy setting. For most of
my life, other interests kept the fantasy stuff on the backburner.

Now, though, I have the opportunity to bring that stuff onto the fire,
I'm hestitant about how to do it.

The simple solution would be to just write stories, the traditional
way. But I think that might be under-utilizing my other skills and
interests, specifically those in tabletop role-playing games and MUDs,
but also, visual art, book layout and design, and other small skills.
