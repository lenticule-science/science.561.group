   #+index: U.S.A.!US culture!"Looking at the Capitol as a 'sacred' space", 2020-01-09
   #+index: sacred!sacred spaces!"Looking at the Capitol as a 'sacred' space", 2020-01-09
   
   In response to the [[file:../2021_storming_of_the_u_s_capitol.org][Storming of the U.S. Capitol]] back so long ago, on the [[file:2021-01-06.org][6th]], many members of our [[file:../u_s_federal_government.org][federal government]] referred to the [[file:../u_s_capitol.org][U.S. Capitol]] as a "[[file:../sacred-space.org][sacred space]]."

   The Capitol building is known for its white dome. Under that dome is a large room known as the Rotunda. There are, around this room, eight alcoves, each containing an oil painting. Each painting is twelve feet tall and eighteen feet wide. The figures in these paintings are almost life-sized.

   Four paintings depict the relationship between the [[file:../united-states-of-america.org][U.S.A.]] and [[file:../uk.org][Great Britian]]: the signing of the [[file:../declaration-of-independence.org][Declaration of Independence]], two British surrenders during the [[file:../u-s-revolutionary-war.org][U.S. Revolutionary War]], and [[file:../george-washington.org][George Washington]] preparing to assume the [[file:../u-s-president.org][U.S. presidency]].

   Those four paintings were added between [[file:../1820.org][1820]] and [[file:../1826.org][1826]]. The other four were added between [[file:../1840.org][1840]] and [[file:../1855.org][1855]].

   The /Baptism of Pocahontas/ was installed in 1840, demonstrating the [[file:../assimilation.org][assimilation]] of [[file:../pocahontas.org][Pocahontas]], notably from before the U.S. existed. /Embarkation of the Pilgrims/ went up in 1844 and is also from before the creation of the U.S., and shows the [[file:../pilgrims.org][Pilgrims]] in [[file:../england.org][England]], about to depart for [[file:../turtle_island.org][Turtle Island]]. A year after the /Embarkation/ was installed, the term "[[file:../manifest-destiny.org][manifest destiny]]" was coined, and two years later, [[file:../colonial.org][colonials]] were openly [[file:../massacre.org][massacring]] [[file:../indigenous_people.org][Indigenous people]] in what would become [[file:../nevada.org][Nevada]], [[file:../texas-u-s-a.org][Texas]], and [[file:../washington-u-s-a.org][Washington]].

   And the /Landing of Columbus/ was put up in the Rotunda. This one goes even further back before the creation of the United States, to lands very distant from [[file:../concord-massachussetts.org][Concord]] and [[file:../plymouth-colony.org][Plymouth]]. In the /Landing/, [[file:../christopher-columbus.org][Christopher Columbus]] is shown driving a flag into the sand as naked [[file:../taino.org][Taino]] women run into the forest.

   In 1855 the final painting was installed, as the [[file:../indian-wars.org][Indian Wars]] were escalating across the [[file:../great-plains.org][Great Plains]]. The /Discovery of the Mississippi/ shows [[file:../hernando-de-soto.org][Hernando de Soto]] riding into a [[file:../native_american.org][Native]] camp on a bank of the [[file:../mississippi-river.org][Mississippi River]], heavily armed, while women cower before him and his white [[file:../horse.org][horse]].

   So, in the center of this "sacred" space, there are three giant paintings depicting naked or captive Native women, women who have been raped or are about to be raped. That is what is sacred to this space: a myth of [[file:../white-supremacy.org][white supremacy]] and entitlement to [[file:../ethnic-cleansing.org][ethnic cleansing]] and [[file:../genocide.org][genocide]].
