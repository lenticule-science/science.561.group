Planning potatoes, 2023

Fifth Season Gardening Co. sent out an email letting folk know that their pre-orders for potatoes are available, and this seems like a good opportunity to try and use my digital garden for stewarding the available information into a good decision.

This communication is going to be a journal of how I'm doing that. This is going to be exceptionally interesting - or boring - because of how new this digital garden is; I don't have a precedent for doing something like this.

The garden is so new in fact that I don't actually have definitions for Fifth Season Gardening Co. or potatoes, which reminds me that I should write a capture template for inserting blank definitions - so I'll go add that to my plans for InfoPonEmacs…

An important facet of this is actually setting up the plan. I've got a file for information about the garden - like other projects, it has sections for routine and special operations. Puchasing potatoes is actually a routine operation; it's done every spring.

Next up is planning where in the garden potatoes will go, and which potatoes to get.

Like I mentioned, both of these will be bigger tasks than they might otherwise be, since I need to do some prep work, but then in the future such things should be a little easier.

One thing that gives me pause is, I know I will be wanting to use Beancount in the future, and I'm not sure how that'll look, integrated into the digital garden as a whole.

So, that leaves us with three types of potato I wanna get: Yukon Gold, Austrian Crescent, and Red Endeavor.

The last step is figuring out how much of each one I need, which will mean actually measuring the garden beds, something I haven't actually done yet!

…In getting started looking at that, I've learned there are two types of potato cultivar: determinate and indeterminate, which may affect the above choices. My early- and mid- choices should be determinate, and my late-season chioce indeterminate… or… hmm.

I think I might need to entirely reconsider how I'm making this choice, with this information; a task for the next time I sit down to plan this.

Skimming Wikipedia further, I wanna be careful about the glycoalkaloid content of the cultivars I choose, as well…

Alright so coming back to this question again, I think my orientation should be… wait; first a note: I'm going to start talking about groundnuts as I go on, now, because they grow in similar spots to potatoes.

Apios americana, commonly known as the groundnut, is a perennial vining legume cultivated for its large edible tubers.

Coming back to this, I think my orientation should be:

  • I'll be growing potatoes in containers, and in the ground
  • In the ground?
    • Determinate or indeterminate?
      • If indeterminate, I can stack up material from the carbon heap around them to get higher yields.
      • But, might take longer before they start to form tubers, and our spring cool weather is getting shorter… So that's something to look up.
      • I don't want them to get in the way of being able to plant summer stuff in these beds.
  • In containers?
    • Determinate or indeterminate?
      • In most containers, can't pile on more dirt, but in the bins I might be able to; depends on how deep the dirt needs to be for the first layer, I suppose? So that's another question

So that's two questions to look up:

  1. Do indeterminate potatoes take longer to start to form tubers?
    • The answer seems to be yes.
  2. How deep does the soil under potatoes, in containers, have to be?
    • Instructions online seem to advise six to ten inches (or more) of soil under the potato when growing it in containers. So
    • Indeterminate potatoes can grow tubers up to a foot above where they were planted, so if we have ~14 inches of head above the base, that's enough to fully crop indeterminates! (The bins we use as planters are about two feet deep.)
    • So! It looks like we can grow indeterminate potatoes in a lot of our containers, too!

So, I think I can do indeterminate in the bin-containers, and determinate in the beds.

That doesn't give me an answer to how much, but it does let me go back to the "what sort" question and boil it down to two, instead of three cultivars. The three I originally picked were Yukon Gold, Austrian Crescent, and Red Endeavor. Looking up their determinate…ness… determination?

(One thing I read - and its confirmed above - is that whether a potato cultivar is determinate or indeterminate is rarely recorded.)

Obviously Yukon Gold is the choice for planting out in the lawn, but as for containers… I'm not sure!

I lean toward Austrian Crescent and groundnuts… are groundnuts determinate or not? I can't find any information advocating they be grown mounded like potatoes are, but also there's not that much information about cultivating them, at all. I don't they they'd do the same trick as indeterminate potatoes; my plan is to grow them with sorghum in containers, so they wouldn't be getting mounded anyway.

So, that's the choices: Yukon Gold, Austrian Crescent, and groundnuts.

Next is figuring out amounts, which relies on knowing how much space - which will have to wait, because it's currently dark and rainy.

I thought about it over night, and I'm actually not sure if I want to plant much in the way of potatoes out in the garden proper, outside of containers - the soil is still clay, even if its starting to have some life in it. So maybe it might be best to focus on getting Austrian Crescents and groundnuts.

Groundnuts prefer riparian conditions, sandy, dappled shade, and so on, so in terms of planting them in containers, they can go up against the east side of the Burrow, while the potatoes can go up against the fenceline.

That's 6 bins for groundnuts, and I can do 8 or 24 bins for Austrian Crescent; each bin taking 3 seed potato chunks - given the Crescents are fingerlings, let's say that I need 2 or 3 per bin, they weigh 4-7 ounces each, so that's (/ (* 3 4 24) 16); 18 pounds of Austrian Crescent.
