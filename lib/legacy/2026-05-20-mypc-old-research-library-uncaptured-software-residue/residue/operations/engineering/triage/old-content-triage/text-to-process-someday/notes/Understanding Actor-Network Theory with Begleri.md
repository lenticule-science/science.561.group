Thinking more about the physical begleri, let's zoom in on those beads. In ANT terms, each bead can be seen as an actor with its own role to play. But here's where it gets interesting: because they're strung together, they don't act individually—they're part of a two-node actor network.

The string that binds them creates this inseparable relationship where the action of one bead inherently involves the other. It’s like they've got this pact: whatever happens to one is going to affect the other because they’re literally tied together. This setup means their agency isn’t just singular; it’s collective by design.

This connection between the beads reflects how entities within networks are interdependent—how their identities and actions are shaped by their relationships with others in the network. Just like how individual people within a community influence and are influenced by each other.

So when you manipulate one bead, you're not just moving that single piece; you're engaging with the whole system—the weight distribution, momentum, even tension on the string—all these factors come into play and shape your experience with them.

It's a cool metaphor for thinking about our place in social or cultural networks too—we might feel like we have independent agency but often our ability to 'act' is bound up with others around us (just like those beads). Our choices ripple through these connections whether we see it or not.

I guess what I'm getting at is that nothing exists in isolation—even something as simple as begleri teaches us about interconnectedness and relational dynamics. Makes me appreciate even more how deep-rooted traditions carry so much wisdom about life beyond what meets the eye! 📿
