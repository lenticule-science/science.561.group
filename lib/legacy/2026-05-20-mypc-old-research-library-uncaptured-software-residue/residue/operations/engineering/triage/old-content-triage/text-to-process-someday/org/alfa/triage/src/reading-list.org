#+TITLE: Reading List
** Reading List
   :PROPERTIES:
   :EXPORT_FILE_NAME: reading-list
   :END:
* Introduction
This is a list of documents that others have written and chosen to
release for free, that I find valuable in some or another way.
* On Business
I have a special note for people interested in reading texts "on
business." As Noah Madden says in [[https://twitter.com/naval/status/1002103360646823936]["How to Get Rich (without getting
Lucky)"]]:

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
"There is no skill called 'business[...]' Study
microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics,
mathematics, and computers."
#+END_QUOTE

In that spirit, I recommend anyone interested in writing "on business"
read pieces from all categories of this list.

- Matt Mochary's [[https://docs.google.com/document/d/1ZJZbv4J6FZ8Dnb0JuMhJxTnwl-dwqx5xl0s65DE3wO8/preview#heading=h.pdmqf3646hgt]["Founder to CEO"]], a guide on how to build a a company
  from the ground up. It's a great read for seeing just what you don't
  know about building a company.
- Vivek Haldar's [[http://blog.vivekhaldar.com/post/79843441901/the-automatic-corporation]["The Automatic Corporation"]].
- Paul Graham's [[http://paulgraham.com/opensource.html]["What Businesses Can Learn from Open Source"]].
- Gitlab.com's [[https://www.remoteonly.org/][/Remote Only/]], about running an organization without a
  central location.
* On Technology and Programming
- Bret Victor's [[http://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/]["Learnable Programming"]].
- C. Titus Brown's post asking: for an open-source project, [[http://ivory.idyll.org/blog/2018-how-open-is-too-open.html]["How Open
  Is Too Open?"]]
- Jeff Atwood's [[https://blog.codinghorror.com/why-cant-programmers-program/]["Why Can't Programmers... Program?"]].
- Eric S. Raymond's [[http://www.catb.org/esr/writings/taoup/html/ch01s06.html][/Basics of Unix Philosophy/]].
- Neal Stephenson's [[http://cristal.inria.fr/~weis/info/commandline.html]["In the Beginning Was The Command Line"]], which
  takes a long time to paraphrase Alan Kay, who said, "Keep simple
  things simple, and make complex things possible."
- Paul Ford's [[https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2015-paul-ford-what-is-code/]["What is Code?"]].
- Karl Fogel's [[https://producingoss.com/][/Producing Open-Source Software]]: probably the best book
  about making a collaborative project work. The title and language
  are almost unfortunate, it's message is relevant for every market.
- Nick Heer's criticism of [[https://pxlnv.com/blog/bullshit-web/]["The Bullshit Web"]].
- Kieran Healy's [[http://plain-text.co/][/The Plain Person's Guide to
  Plain Text Social Science/], which is an argument for "[using] tools
  that give you /more control/ over the process of data analysis and
  writing."
- Nadia Eghbal's [[https://nadiaeghbal.com/project-health]["Methodologies for Measuring Project Health"]].
- Jessica Kerr's [[https://the-composition.com/the-origins-of-opera-and-the-future-of-programming-bcdaf8fbe960]["The Origins of Opera and the Future of Programming"]].
- Ory.sh's [Guide to Web Access Control](https://www.ory.sh/api-security-cloud-guide-overview/).
* On Design
- The [http://motherfuckingwebsite.com/](http://motherfuckingwebsite.com/).
- Justin Jackson's [Words](https://justinjackson.ca/words.html), which is a more family-friendly version of the Motherfucking Website.
- Kevin Goldman's [Material Honesty on the Web](https://alistapart.com/article/material-honesty-on-the-web/).
- Grigoria Pontiki's [Accessibility low hanging fruit](http://www.grigoria.gr/2018/08/25/accessibility-low-hanging-fruits.html).
* On Society
- Scott Alexander's [I Can Tolerate Anything Except the Outgroup](http://slatestarcodex.com/2014/09/30/i-can-tolerate-anything-except-the-outgroup/).
* On Writing
- Nicole Fenton's [Words as Material](https://www.nicolefenton.com/words-as-material/).
- Pramila Rai's [Archetypal Imagination in Mark Twain's Works](http://202.45.147.21:8080/jspui/bitstream/123456789/154/1/PhD_Thesis_Pramila_Rai_Aug_2016.pdf).
- Einar Bjarnason's [The Hero, the Shadow and The Wise Old Man](Einar).
- The [Chicago Manual of Style](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/book/). (Similarly, I recommend Joseph M. William's [Style: The Basics of Clarity and Grace](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0321953304/) and Kate L. Turabian's [A Manual for Writers of Research Papers, Theses, and Dissertations](https://www.amazon.com/dp/022649442X/) but they cost money.)
- I mention it under the technology category as well, but [The Plain Person's Guide to Plain Text Social Science](http://plain-text.co/) by Kieran Healy is an argument for "[using] tools that give you _more control_ over... writing."
- Kate Kinnear's ["The aesthetics of science fiction spaceship design"]() which can be taken as a detailed analysis of "how a trope comes into existence."
- Sam Schlinkert's [[https://write.as/sts10/digital-security-guide-for-friends-and-loved-ones][Digital Security Guide for Friends and Loved Ones]].
* Others
- W Ben Hunt's piece about [how coyotes are too
  clever](http://www.epsilontheory.com/too-clever-by-half/), and what
  that means for us. This led me to another article by Hunt, about
  [sheep logic](http://www.epsilontheory.com/sheep-logic/).
