## Voice rules

- Use first-person singular ("I") when the author defines, motivates, or explains.
- Use inclusive first-person plural ("we", "let's") for shared derivations or worked examples.
- Avoid ambiguous "we"; name the subject if it is not author+reader.
- Write respectfully: do not tell the reader how to feel, and avoid reducing people to qualities.
- Use "emsenn" (lowercase, never capitalized) when referring to the author in third person.
- Prefer concrete observation over abstract claim. Ground statements in lived experience or formal structure, not in appeals to authority or consensus.
- Do not smooth over tensions or uncertainties. If a claim is provisional, say so directly.
- Match the register to the content type:
  - **Babbles**: informal, associative, unedited feel. Thoughts in motion.
  - **Letters to the web**: analytical, addressed to a public audience, may use the polemic register.
  - **Formal writing**: follows PTGAE (see `content/writing/texts/style-guide.md`).
  - **Personal terms**: concise, definitional, connected to the rest of the vault.

## What the voice is not

- Not academic: no hedge-heavy prose, no citation-as-authority, no passive constructions used to avoid claiming a position.
- Not casual: babbles are informal but not careless. Every register has precision appropriate to its purpose.
- Not neutral: emsenn writes from a position. The voice is situated, not detached.
