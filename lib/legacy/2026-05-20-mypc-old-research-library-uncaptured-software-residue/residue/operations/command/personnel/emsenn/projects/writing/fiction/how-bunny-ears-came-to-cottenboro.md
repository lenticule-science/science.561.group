When the world had added machines to the workplace and the rich folk were doing to the same to their homes, there lived a woman named Elizabeth Cotten, though lots of people called her "Libba." Her home was near Bolin Creek, where a train line ran over it, and Libba would sit on her porch, playing guitar and singing, "Freight train, freight train, going so fast..."

One day, a bunny who lived in the brush near the train-tracks came hopping up to Libba as she strummed her guitar.

"That freight-train is so loud!" the bunny complained. "My babies can hardly sleep!"

Libba frowned in understanding. "I can help, but you'll need to let me brush you." The idea of sitting still while a human brushed their fur scared the rabbit, but they needed Libba's help. So Libba sat down and brushed the bunny's fur in long strokes, pulling out all the little specks of dirt. "Not everything is what it looks like," Libba said, collecting the specks. The bunny nodded, even though they weren't sure they understood.

Libba went to the Creek and mixed the specks with the mud of its banks, and then spread it down the sides of the rail-line, where the bunny lived. "Patience," Libba encouraged, and the bunny waited.

As winter was ending, squat broad-leafed plants began to grow where Libba had scattered the mud. Each leaf was covered in a downy fluff, looking just like the dirty fur of the bunny.

"Collect these leaves when your first babies are born, and line your burrows with it. They'll sleep sound, and the little cuts you get from foraging in bramble will heal better. Next year, the plants will grow tall staff with bright yellow flowers. If you get into some rotten berries, nibbling on those can help." Libba explained. The bunny thanked Libba and hopped away.
