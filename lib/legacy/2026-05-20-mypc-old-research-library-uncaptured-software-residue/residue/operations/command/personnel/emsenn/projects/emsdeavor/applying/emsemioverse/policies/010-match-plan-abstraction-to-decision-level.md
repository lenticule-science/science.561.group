Plans should match the abstraction level of the decision they encode.


- A general capability needs a general plan. Do not write implementation
  details into a plan before the approach has been decided.
- If the next question is "what approach?", write a plan for deciding
  the approach. If the next question is "how exactly?", write a plan
  for the implementation.
- A plan that presupposes a specific technology or method when that
  choice hasn't been made yet is a plan written at the wrong level.
  It will be superseded as soon as the approach is reconsidered.
- When a plan turns out to be too specific, supersede it with a
  more general plan — don't try to make the specific plan general
  by editing it.
- The test: can you describe the plan's goal without naming the
  implementation? If not, you may have the abstraction level wrong.
