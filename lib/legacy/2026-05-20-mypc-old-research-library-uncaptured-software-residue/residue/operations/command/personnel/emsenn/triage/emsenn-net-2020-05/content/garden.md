## <span class="section-num">1</span> Introduction {#introduction}

**_Makȟá Šá Ethnoecological Workshop_** is a place to develop contemporary indigenous ways of living within a North American settler-community, and explore actions to live in reciprocity with the Earth.

_Summary for Settlers_: This is a small community garden in Chapel Hill, North Carolina.

#### <span class="org-todo todo planning">planning</span> <span class="section-num">2.2.1</span> Southeast Bowbar Bed (B1) {#southeast-bowbar-bed--b1}


##### <span class="section-num">2.2.1.1</span> Overview {#overview}

This bed gets some mid-morning sun and dappled sunlight through the afternoon: it's shaded by [Peacemaker's Mulberry Tree and Grape Vine](#peacemakers-mulberry-tree-and-grape-vine). The soil is sandy gravel with a few inches of sandy soil on top.

As of <span class="timestamp-wrapper"><span class="timestamp">[2020-07-07 Tue]</span></span>, this bed is planted with a mix of squash and melons and a bee balm with some bright red flowers.


##### <span class="section-num">2.2.1.2</span> Plans {#plans}

This area doesn't get runoff from anyplace and is at the top of a steep hill: it is pretty arid. It also gets, at best, dappled sunlight. Something with large roots that can establish itself into the hillside would probably be suitable - a perennial herb bush: perhaps this is where the two _[Salvia officinalis](#salvia-officinalis)_ plants I have in [the pallet garden](#pallet-garden) can go? I could also see the _[Stachys Byzantina 'Cotten's'](#stachys-byzantina-cottens)_ doing well in this space - though perhaps it'd be better in [the Northeast Bowbar Bed](#northeast-bowbar-bed)


##### <span class="section-num">2.2.1.3</span> Tasks {#tasks}

#### <span class="org-todo todo planning">planning</span> <span class="section-num">2.2.3</span> Northeast Bowbar Bed (B3) {#northeast-bowbar-bed}


##### <span class="section-num">2.2.3.1</span> Overview {#overview}

This bed is at the top of the hill behind Bowbar's patio. It doesn't get much runoff, and drains quickly down the hillside. It gets a fair amount of wind from the north and some from the south.

As of <span class="timestamp-wrapper"><span class="timestamp">[2020-07-07 Tue]</span></span>, it's planted with a mix of cacti at the eastern end, a border of succulents north against Bowbar's patio, and corn, beans, and squash and melons filling the rest of the area.


##### <span class="section-num">2.2.3.2</span> Plans {#plans}

It's on a hill, it doesn't get much water, and it gets a lot of wind. It's in a visually prominent spot. I don't have any plans, but these are some considerations about it.

Maybe the _[Stachys Byzantina 'Cotten's'](#stachys-byzantina-cottens)_ would work in this spot? It was from a hill near the train tracks.


#### <span class="org-todo todo planning">planning</span> <span class="section-num">2.2.4</span> Northwest Bowbar Bed (B4) {#northwest-bowbar-bed--b4}


##### <span class="section-num">2.2.4.1</span> Overview {#overview}

Of all the beds currently <span class="timestamp-wrapper"><span class="timestamp">[2020-07-07 Tue] </span></span> a part of the workshop, this bed gets the best light, and receives runoff from Bowbar's patio during heavy rains. Unfortunately the soil is mostly sand right now: good for the abundance of peppers and (either melons or cucumbers) I planted there, but not good for a long-term planting.


##### <span class="section-num">2.2.4.2</span> Plans {#plans}

With the long sun and dark wall of Bowbar to its immediate north, I'm thinking this bed will stay fairly warm even in the winter, since it's protected from the wind by that wall, which'll also soak up heat.

That's making me wonder if it can't be used to grow a few things that otherwise might struggle in our winter: two that come to mind are Aloe vera and Stevia rebaudiana. Those will both do well in the well-drained soil, but Stevia requires a fair bit more water than Aloe, so the Aloe would have to go on the eastern, higher side, while the stevia can go more toward the south.

But to accomplish this I'll have to amend the soil heavily with organic matter, fired clay chips, and charcoal.

#### <span class="section-num">4.3.1</span> Names {#names}


##### <span class="section-num">4.3.1.1</span> Colonial {#colonial}

-   lamb's ear


##### <span class="section-num">4.3.1.2</span> Contemporary {#contemporary}

-   Cotten's lamb's ear: named for Libba Cotten, who lived in the neighborhood adjacent to the train tracks where this plant was transplanted from.


#### <span class="section-num">4.3.2</span> Uses {#uses}

Useful as toilet paper, bandage, anti-bacterial, esp. staph.


#### <span class="section-num">4.3.3</span> Habitat {#habitat}


##### <span class="section-num">4.3.3.1</span> Original {#original}

This plant was originally transplanted from a the west side sandy bank adjacent to the train tracks that (at least in 2020) go through the settler community of Carrboro, in North Carolina. These are the same train tracks that were written about in Libba Cotten's song, "Freight Train," so naming the plant after her seemed appropriate, given its common names.
