# What is a theory, in a predication category?

## The question

The corpus has a definition called [predication-theory](../../../objects/predication-theory.md): a quadruple $T = (\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E, \mathrm{Cl})$ — a residuated lattice, a predicate category, an entity set, and a closure operator on the predication fiber functor $F : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{DBiLat}(\mathbf{L})$. It has two named theory-to-theory morphism classes: [subtheory-inclusion](../../../morphisms/subtheory-inclusion.md) and [researching](../../../morphisms/researching.md). It has a placed-settling notion ([resource](../../../objects/resource.md)) and a theory-relative ideal that interfaces between resource and theory.

But these are just the first surface features of a much larger question. Mathematics has produced many *distinct* notions of "theory" across the 20th and 21st centuries — Tarskian abstract-consequence operators, Lawvere algebraic theories, Ehresmann sketches, Goguen-Burstall institutions, Lawvere hyperdoctrines and Maietti-Rosolini doctrines, geometric theories and their classifying topoi, type-theoretic categories-with-families and natural models, model-theoretic first-order theories, modal/Kripke theories, operadic and PROP theories, TQFT-style functorial theories, and more. Each tradition has its own carrier, its own consequence/derivation structure, its own model side, its own theory-morphism, its own sub-theory relation. These traditions communicate via translation theorems and equivalences (Lawvere theories ↔ finitary monads, geometric theories ↔ Grothendieck topoi, algebraizable consequence operators ↔ quasivarieties), but no existing framework has assembled them into a *single category*.

The research thesis this document opens is the conjecture that they all do live in a single category, but that category has only ever been visible from inside predication-category theory:

> **Working thesis.** Every classical mathematical notion of "a theory" is an object of a single category $\mathbf{Th}$ that predication-category theory naturally houses. The reason no other tradition has been able to assemble $\mathbf{Th}$ is that the assembling requires three structural ingredients simultaneously: (i) a fibered carrier — predicates indexing the formula-cells; (ii) an inferential closure operator on the fiber functor — consequence structure as data, not as a derived notion; and (iii) bilattice-valued fibers — letting partiality, paraconsistency, and the knowledge/truth distinction be expressed without external machinery. Each classical tradition has one or two of these. None has all three. Predication categories have all three by construction.

This is what we are trying to find out. It is not a theorem to be proved next; it is a research hypothesis to be tested by comparative survey of the literature, tradition by tradition, with the goal of identifying for each tradition what its (carrier, consequence, models, morphisms, sub-theory, equivalence, presentation) data is and what its predication-categorical encoding would be. When enough traditions have been surveyed and their encodings collected, the unifying category should reveal itself rather than be imposed — its objects, morphisms, and 2-cellular structure should be forced by the encodings rather than chosen.

Concretely the question splits into seven sub-questions that have to be answered together:

1. **Objects.** What is the data of a theory in $\mathbf{Th}$? Each classical tradition gives a candidate shape — a quadruple, a small category with structure, a sketch, an indexed family of algebras, a functor from a syntactic category, ... The predication-categorical encoding must accommodate all of them as instances, not as a single privileged shape. What is the common shape?
2. **Morphisms.** Each tradition has its own theory-morphism: structure-preserving functor (Lawvere), morphism of sketches (Ehresmann), signature morphism + sentence translation (institutions), doctrine morphism (Maietti-Rosolini), translation of consequence (Tarski-Blok-Pigozzi), geometric morphism (topos-theoretic). What is the predication-categorical theory-morphism that has all of these as cases?
3. **2-cells.** Closure operators on a fixed fiber functor are a complete lattice under pointwise order; doctrine morphisms have natural transformations between them; geometric morphisms have natural transformations; institutions have signature-morphism natural transformations. $\mathbf{Th}$ is at least 2-categorical.
4. **Sub-theory.** The corpus's [subtheory-inclusion](../../../morphisms/subtheory-inclusion.md) is one shape (scope-restriction with pullback closure). Algebraic-logic sub-logic is another (closure-restriction at fixed carrier). Topos-theoretic sub-theory is a quotient by additional axioms (closure-extension at fixed signature). Sketch sub-sketch is yet another. Each tradition's sub-theory is a special case of the unified theory morphism — but which special case?
5. **(Co)limits.** Theory amalgamation is institution-theoretic pushout. Theory intersection is sub-theory pullback. Free theory on a presentation is a colimit. Conservative-extension is a faithful-and-fully-monic morphism. Do these match across traditions? Does $\mathbf{Th}$ have all (co)limits?
6. **Models and the Mod-Th adjunction.** Tarski has $\mathrm{Mod} \dashv \mathrm{Th}$ at the elementary level. Lawvere theories have $\mathrm{Mod}$-of-a-theory as a category of algebras with a free-forgetful adjunction. Geometric theories have $\mathrm{Mod}$ valued in any topos. Doctrines have $\mathrm{Mod}$ as a 2-functor. The predication-categorical [predication-model](../../../objects/predication-model.md) is the model of a single theory; the *2-functor* $\mathrm{Mod} : \mathbf{Th}^{\mathrm{op}} \to ?$ is the question.
7. **Equivalence and presentation.** Bi-interpretability, Morita equivalence of algebraic theories, equivalence of categories of models, geometric equivalence of geometric theories. Theory-presentations by axioms / rules / generators-and-relations. All traditions have these; none has a uniform language for them across traditions.

A complete answer to (1)–(7) — and crucially, an answer in which the encoding of each classical tradition into $\mathbf{Th}$ falls out as a single instance of the unified structure rather than as a separate construction — is what would justify the working thesis.

## What the existing definitions commit to

Before any literature, it is worth reading off what the existing files already force.

**The closure operator is the inferential content.** A theory is a closure on a fiber functor, not a set of axioms. This places predication theories closer to algebraic-consequence-operator presentations (Tarski) than to syntactic theories (Lawvere algebraic theories, sketches, institutions). Axioms enter only via presentations of the closure.

**The fiber functor lands in distributive bilattices.** $F : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{DBiLat}(\mathbf{L})$ — the values are bilattices, not Heyting algebras or frames. The "knowledge order" $\leq_k$ and "truth order" $\leq_t$ both exist; the closure operator is monotone with respect to $\leq_k$. Any theory of theories here must respect bilattice structure: a theory morphism must preserve the bilattice maps fiber-wise, not just the truth order. This is the predication-categorical departure from any classical or intuitionistic story.

**The base $(\mathbf{L}, E)$ is *not* part of the syntax.** Subtheory and researching both hold $\mathbf{L}$ and $E$ fixed. The corpus already flags that varying these requires the full machinery of [predication-settling-base-morphism](../../../morphisms/predication-settling-base-morphism.md). So at minimum we have two strata: $\mathbf{Th}(\mathbf{L}, E)$ for fixed base, and a more general $\mathbf{Th}$ that fibers over a category of $(\mathbf{L}, E)$ pairs via change-of-base.

**Closures are pointwise on the fiber functor.** A closure operator $\mathrm{Cl}$ assigns to each $p \in \mathcal{C}$ a closure operator $\mathrm{Cl}_p$ on the fiber algebra $A_p$, with naturality in $\mathcal{C}$. This makes the set of all closures on $F$ a complete lattice under pointwise order. The bottom is $\mathrm{Cl} = \mathrm{id}$ (the trivial theory; all cells settled). The top is $\mathrm{Cl} = \top$ (every cell goes to top; the absurd theory). A theory is therefore a point in this lattice; closure-comparison cells are the order relation.

**The settled state is a Knaster-Tarski fixed point.** The theory's model is not given separately; it is the least fixed point of $\mathrm{Cl}$ above $\bot$, constructed by transfinite iteration. This is exactly the structure of *abstract consequence operators*: the closed sets are the theory's theorems, the fixed points are the models, and the operator is determined by either.

**Subtheory-inclusion's closure condition is equality, not inequality.** The corpus subtheory definition demands $\mathrm{Cl}^S = \pi^*\mathrm{Cl}^T$ exactly, not $\mathrm{Cl}^S \leq \pi^*\mathrm{Cl}^T$. This is a strong condition: $S$ is the *unique* theory at scope $\mathcal{C}^S$ with closure pulled back from $T$. Any weakening would yield a 2-cellular family of "sub-things" — sub-theories in a looser sense — and the question of which is right is open.

**Researching's closure condition is silent.** Researching specifies only conditions on the settled state ($v^{T'}_\infty(\pi p) \geq_k v^T_\infty(p)$ plus strict advance). It does not require any pullback or pushout relation between $\mathrm{Cl}^T$ and $\pi^*\mathrm{Cl}^{T'}$. So researching is *weaker on the closure axis than subtheory-inclusion is on the scope axis*. This asymmetry is not currently explained and may be a research artifact — a uniform formulation might require strengthening researching or weakening subtheory-inclusion.

These five commitments are non-negotiable inputs to any answer.

## The traditions to survey

Each of the traditions below has produced a substantial mathematical account of "what a theory is." Each will be the subject of one survey in `prose/learning/surveys/`. The point is not to compare these and pick one; the point is that the unifying category $\mathbf{Th}$ should turn out to contain *every* one of them as a class of objects. Each survey tracks the same data per tradition: (carrier, consequence, models, morphisms, sub-theory, equivalence, presentation), with explicit attention to how each maps into the predication-categorical apparatus.

**Tarskian abstract consequence operators** (Tarski 1930, Wójcicki, Czelakowski). A consequence operator is a closure $\mathrm{Cn}$ on the power set of a set of sentences. The closed sets are the theories; the lattice of theories is the fixed-point lattice; theory morphisms are translations preserving $\mathrm{Cn}$. The match to predication-theory at fixed base is structural — both are closure-operator stories — but Tarskian theories live on a flat set of sentences, not a fibered structure over a predicate category. The translation literature on consequence operators (Czelakowski's "Protoalgebraic Logics," Font-Jansana "Behavioural Equivalences") is the place to learn how morphisms behave; whether the closure-restriction proof generalizes is the first concrete portability question.

**Lawvere algebraic theories** (Lawvere 1963). An algebraic theory is a small category $\mathbf{T}$ with finite products generated by an object $X$; a theory morphism is a finite-product-preserving functor. The category $\mathbf{Th}_{\mathrm{Law}}$ of algebraic theories has all (co)limits and a free–forgetful adjunction with sets. The mismatch: Lawvere theories are *syntactic* (the category is the syntax), and the closure-operator layer is absent — there is no separate inferential content. Predication-theory has both a syntactic layer ($\mathcal{C}$) and a semantic-inferential layer ($\mathrm{Cl}$); Lawvere theories collapse the two. But the *category-of-theories* design pattern — small-categories-with-structure, structure-preserving functors as morphisms, (co)limits as theory operations — is the canonical reference for shape.

**Sketches** (Ehresmann 1968, Barr-Wells "Toposes, Triples and Theories"). A sketch is a small category equipped with chosen cones and cocones to be sent to limits and colimits. Sketch morphisms preserve the marked structure. The category $\mathbf{Sk}$ admits a notion of sub-sketch (a faithful inclusion preserving marked structure) directly analogous to subtheory-inclusion. Whether the closure operator can be encoded as additional marked structure on a sketch — turning "predication theory" into "predication sketch + closure axioms" — is a possible reformulation worth investigating.

**Institutions** (Goguen-Burstall 1992, Diaconescu "Institution-Independent Model Theory"). An institution is a quadruple $(\mathrm{Sign}, \mathrm{Sen}, \mathrm{Mod}, \models)$ — a category of signatures, sentences and models functors, and a satisfaction relation natural in signature morphisms. Theories are pairs $(\Sigma, T)$ with $\Sigma$ a signature and $T$ a set of $\Sigma$-sentences. Theory morphisms are signature morphisms preserving derivable sentences. The institution-theoretic notion of sub-theory and theory amalgamation (Goguen-Roşu "Institution Morphisms") is the closest literature analog to subtheory-inclusion + amalgamation in predication categories. The question is whether predication-theory presents an institution, with $\mathrm{Sign} = $ some category of $(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E)$ triples, $\mathrm{Sen}$ giving closure rules or axioms, $\mathrm{Mod}$ giving settled states, and $\models$ giving fixed-point membership.

**Hyperdoctrines and doctrines** (Lawvere 1969; Maietti-Rosolini 2013 "Elementary quotient completion"). A hyperdoctrine is a functor $P : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Pos}$ (or to Heyting algebras, etc.) satisfying Beck-Chevalley and Frobenius conditions. The corpus already has [predication-hyperdoctrine](../../../objects/predication-hyperdoctrine.md). A theory in the doctrinal tradition is a doctrine with chosen axioms (a "theory in a doctrine," in Maietti-Rosolini's terminology). Morphisms are doctrine morphisms preserving the chosen axioms. This is the closest *fibrational* analog and is likely the right reference for how to relate $\mathbf{Th}$ to the existing predication-hyperdoctrine structure.

**Closure operators on locales/frames** (Johnstone "Stone Spaces" ch. II, Picado-Pultr "Frames and Locales"). Closure operators on a frame correspond bijectively to sublocales. The map $\mathrm{Cl} \leftrightarrow \mathrm{Fix}(\mathrm{Cl})$ is the fundamental adjunction. For predication theories the analog is $\mathrm{Cl} \leftrightarrow$ (closed cells in the fiber functor), and the question is whether this is similarly an adjunction or merely a many-to-one map (as the corpus's "two theories sharing a model" remark suggests).

**Bilattice logic and Avron's framework** (Avron 1996, Arieli-Avron 1998, the corpus's [avron-representation](../../../theorems/avron-representation.md) theorem). The corpus's bilattice layer is closer to Avron than to anything else; Avron's later work on "logical bilattices" defines a theory-like object internal to a bilattice, with consequence relations valued in the four-element bilattice. Whether Avron's notion of theory matches what closure-on-a-fiber-functor gives at the four-bilattice fiber is a concrete checkable question.

**Models, theories, and Galois** (Hodges "Model Theory" §6, Marker "Model Theory" §4). Classical $\mathrm{Mod} \dashv \mathrm{Th}$ adjunction: sets of theories closed under derivability ↔ classes of models closed under elementary equivalence. The Galois connection collapses to a bijection at the elementary level. The predication-categorical analog — even at fixed base — is the question of when closures and fixed-point sets correspond bijectively.

## Method

The first deliverables — and the primary work of this research thread — are surveys in `prose/learning/surveys/`, one per tradition. Each survey:

1. Lays out the tradition's account of (carrier, consequence, models, theory-morphism, sub-theory, equivalence, presentation), with accurate references.
2. Identifies what is structurally distinctive — what the tradition can express that others cannot, and what it cannot express that others can.
3. Sketches the predication-categorical encoding: which $(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E, \mathrm{Cl})$ data the tradition's theories correspond to, and which theory-morphisms in the tradition correspond to which morphisms in the predication-categorical setting.
4. Records what fails or resists encoding. The resistances are the most informative data: they tell us what additional structure the unifying $\mathbf{Th}$ must carry.

Claims (`prose/learning/claims/`) and structures/transformers (`objects/`, `morphisms/`, `theorems/`) are downstream. They get written only when the surveys have produced enough comparative material that the unifying structure is *forced* — when the encoding of multiple traditions into the same predication-categorical apparatus makes the next claim inevitable rather than chosen. Premature claim-writing — writing down "the general theory morphism" or "the category $\mathbf{Th}$" from one or two surveys — distorts the research by committing to a shape before the data is in.

The expected end state is: a body of surveys covering enough of the literature that the encodings collectively determine $\mathbf{Th}$; then a small core of claims giving the category, its morphisms, its 2-cells, and the encoding theorems showing each tradition's theories embed into $\mathbf{Th}$ as a sub-2-category; then the lift into the corpus's `objects/morphisms/theorems` layer.

The thesis is ambitious enough that it may turn out false — the surveys may reveal traditions that genuinely resist predication-categorical encoding. That is also a result worth having; it would characterize precisely the boundary of what predication-category theory can house.
