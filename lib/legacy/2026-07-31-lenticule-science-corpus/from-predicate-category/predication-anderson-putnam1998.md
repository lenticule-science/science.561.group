## Annotation

The **Anderson–Putnam complex** and its inverse-limit characterization of substitution tiling spaces. Methodological backbone for the aperiodic-regime classification.

For a substitution tiling $\omega$, the Anderson–Putnam complex $\Gamma_\omega$ is a finite cell complex; the tiling space $\Omega_\omega$ is the inverse limit under the substitution self-map $\omega: \Gamma_\omega \to \Gamma_\omega$:

$$\Omega_\omega \cong \lim_{\leftarrow}\;(\Gamma_\omega \xleftarrow{\omega} \Gamma_\omega \xleftarrow{\omega} \cdots).$$

Cohomology of $\Omega_\omega$ is the **direct limit** of induced cohomology maps:

$$\check{H}^n(\Omega_\omega) = \varinjlim\;(\check{H}^n(\Gamma_\omega) \xrightarrow{\omega^*} \check{H}^n(\Gamma_\omega) \to \cdots).$$

**The cohomological layer** uses the analogous direct-limit structure: settling cohomology at the closure ordinal $\kappa$ is the direct limit of finite-stage cohomologies under the settling endofunctor's action.

The analogy is **structural**, not technical — tiling cohomology uses abelian-group coefficients on simplicial-complex inverse limits; we use abelian-group coefficients on bitopological-site direct limits. The underlying direct-limit framework transfers cleanly. The cohomological layer makes the disambiguation explicit.

Origin of the **MLD-equivalence** notion (mutually locally derivable) used as an equivalence relation on aperiodic settlings.
