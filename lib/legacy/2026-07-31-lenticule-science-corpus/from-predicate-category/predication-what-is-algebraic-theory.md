# What is algebraic theory?

The companion survey [survey-of-algebraic-theory](surveys/survey-of-algebraic-theory.md) lays out what a Lawvere algebraic theory is in standard mathematical literature: a small category $\mathbf{T}$ with strict finite products generated by an object $X$, with morphisms read as operations and equations between morphisms read as axioms; models are finite-product-preserving functors $\mathbf{T} \to \mathbf{Set}$.

This file is for working out what algebraic theory *is* — i.e., for figuring out the encoding of $\mathbf{T}$ into the predication-categorical apparatus $(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E, \mathrm{Cl})$ of [predication-theory](../../../objects/predication-theory.md). The investigation will run until we can write down a claim precise enough to prove, at which point the claim goes into `prose/learning/claims/` and (once proved) the structural object goes into `objects/algebraic-theory.md`.

## Where we are right now

The shape of the encoding is not yet known. Three candidate shapes from earlier conversation:

- **(a) Direct.** Every Lawvere theory *is* a predication theory of a specific form: the data of $\mathbf{T}$ literally constructs $(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E, \mathrm{Cl})$.
- **(b) Functorial.** A canonical encoding functor $\mathrm{LawvereTh} \to \mathrm{PredTh}$, faithful but not bijective on objects.
- **(c) Parametric.** Each Lawvere theory determines a *family* of predication theories indexed by some choice (e.g., one per model, one per chosen carrier $E$).

Current instinct (provisional): (a), with
- $\mathbf{L}$ = the trivial residuated lattice (Boolean $\mathbf{2}$, or even $\{*\}$), since algebraic theories have no truth-value gradation,
- $E$ = the carrier set the theory is interpreted over (still parametric, but parameter is honestly part of the data),
- $\mathcal{C}$ = a predicate category built from $\mathbf{T}$'s operations,
- $\mathrm{Cl}$ = the closure enforcing $\mathbf{T}$'s equational axioms.

The least-confident piece is $\mathcal{C}$. Operations in $\mathbf{T}$ are *morphisms* between objects $X^n$; predicates in $\mathcal{C}$ are *objects*. Naively turning operations into predicates of arity $n+1$ ("$\omega(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = y$") works for a single operation but doesn't immediately respect $\mathbf{T}$'s product structure or composition. Predicate categories also have their own generating morphisms (specialization, converse, subject/object projection) which have no obvious correspondent in $\mathbf{T}$.

## What this question opens

A satisfying answer would produce:

- A precise claim: "Given a Lawvere theory $\mathbf{T}$, the quadruple $\Phi(\mathbf{T}) = (\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{T}}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{T}}, E, \mathrm{Cl}_{\mathbf{T}})$ defined as follows is a predication theory whose settled state $v_\infty$ corresponds bijectively to a $\mathbf{T}$-algebra with carrier $E$."
- A proof of that claim.
- An `objects/algebraic-theory.md` file defining the construction $\Phi$ as a structural object.

Failing that, an answer to *why* such a $\Phi$ does not exist — i.e., what about Lawvere theories cannot be expressed in $(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E, \mathrm{Cl})$ form — would also resolve the question, and would tell us either that the predication-theory definition is missing structure or that the unification thesis ([[project-pred-cat-theory-of-theories-unification-thesis]]) needs to be weakened.
