# Meta-realizability map

The [meta-moduli stack](meta-moduli-stack-theorem.md) $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$ and the [settling moduli stack](settling-moduli-stack-theorem.md) $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}$ are connected by a canonical morphism

$$\pi^{\mathrm{real}} \,:\, \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}} \to \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$$

sending each settling $[v] \in \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}(B)$ to the closure-equivalence class of the rule system generating $v$'s closure operator.

This is predication categories' analog of the model-theory-to-theory forgetful map at the moduli level: each settling has an underlying closure-rule system (the "theory" of which the settling is a "model"), and $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ records this. The fiber of $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ over a meta-point $[\mathcal{S}] \in \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$ is the substack of settlings generated by $\mathcal{S}$ — the settlings $v_\mathcal{S}^U$ as $U$ varies.

## Statement

**Theorem (meta-realizability map).** The assignment

$$\pi^{\mathrm{real}} \,:\, [v] \mapsto [\mathcal{S}_v] := \bigl[\text{any closure-rule system generating } \mathrm{cl}^v\bigr]$$

— where $\mathrm{cl}^v$ is the closure operator of the settling $v$ — defines a morphism of 1-stacks $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}} \to \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$.

The morphism is **essentially surjective**: every closure-equivalence class $[\mathcal{S}] \in \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$ has at least one settling in its preimage (the trivial settling $v_\mathcal{S}^{U = \bot}$ with empty increments).

The morphism is **regime-respecting**: $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ maps the regime-$R$ sub-moduli of $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}$ into the meta-regime-$R'$ sub-moduli of $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$, where $R \leq R'$ in the regime hierarchy (strict $<$ SC $<$ TB $<$ aperiodic). The inequality is strict in general — a meta-aperiodic system $\mathcal{S}$ has settlings of *all* regimes (depending on $U$), so the regime-$R$ sub-moduli over $[\mathcal{S}]$ is non-empty for every $R \leq $ meta-regime of $\mathcal{S}$.

The fibers of $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ are the **regime-stratified increment-sequence moduli**: the fiber over $[\mathcal{S}]$ is the moduli of increment sequences $U$ over $B$, modulo the equivalence "two increment sequences are equivalent iff they generate MLD-equivalent settlings under $\mathcal{S}$."

## Proof

The argument has four steps: well-definedness, functoriality, essential surjectivity, and regime-compatibility.

**Step 1 — well-definedness.** Each settling $v \in \mathrm{KP}(B)$ has a closure operator $\mathrm{cl}^v$ (the closure operator generating $v$'s closure stages). This operator is generated by *some* closure-rule system $\mathcal{S}_v$ — for instance, by the canonical generator built from $\mathrm{cl}^v$'s cellwise inflation data (Step 2 of the [meta-moduli stack theorem](meta-moduli-stack-theorem.md)). Any other generator is closure-equivalent. Hence $[\mathcal{S}_v] \in \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}(B)$ is well-defined.

The assignment respects MLD-equivalence: if $v \sim_{\mathrm{MLD}} v'$, then $\mathrm{cl}^v$ and $\mathrm{cl}^{v'}$ agree (after the settling-shift), hence $[\mathcal{S}_v] = [\mathcal{S}_{v'}]$. So $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ descends to the visible quotient $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}} = \mathrm{KP}^{\mathrm{vis}}/\!\sim$.

**Step 2 — functoriality on $\mathbf{B}$-morphisms.** A base-change $(\ell, e, \pi, \phi_\lambda) : B \to B'$ pulls back settlings and rule systems compatibly: $[(\ell, e, \pi, \phi_\lambda)^* \mathcal{S}_{v'}] = [\mathcal{S}_{(\ell, e, \pi, \phi_\lambda)^* v'}]$ because both are generated by the pulled-back closure operator. Hence $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ is a stack morphism.

**Step 3 — essential surjectivity.** Given $[\mathcal{S}] \in \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}(B)$, the trivial increment sequence $U = (u_\tau = \bot)_\tau$ gives a settling $v_\mathcal{S}^{U = \bot}$ over $B$. This settling has closure operator $\mathrm{cl}^\mathcal{S}$ (the closure operator of the rule system, applied to the trivial valuation). So $[\mathcal{S}_{v_\mathcal{S}^{U = \bot}}] = [\mathcal{S}]$, exhibiting $[\mathcal{S}]$ in the image.

**Step 4 — regime compatibility.** A settling $v_\mathcal{S}^U$ has regime determined by both $\mathcal{S}$ and $U$:

- The *meta-regime* of $\mathcal{S}$ bounds *above* the regime of $v_\mathcal{S}^U$ (per the [meta-stratification theorem](meta-stratification-by-regime.md)).
- Within this bound, $U$ can drive $v$ to any regime $\leq$ meta-regime.

So $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ maps the regime-$R$ stratum of $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}$ into the union of meta-strata $\geq R$ in $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$. The fiber over $[\mathcal{S}]$ contains settlings of all regimes $\leq$ meta-regime of $\mathcal{S}$, with the regime determined by the increment-sequence equivalence class. $\square$

## Fibers as increment-sequence moduli

The fiber of $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ over $[\mathcal{S}]$ has natural structure: it is the **moduli of increment sequences for $\mathcal{S}$**, modulo the equivalence "$U_1 \sim U_2$ iff $v_\mathcal{S}^{U_1} \sim_{\mathrm{MLD}} v_\mathcal{S}^{U_2}$."

This sub-moduli inherits a regime stratification: the regime of $v_\mathcal{S}^U$ depends on $U$, with the *minimum* increment-content giving regime-strict (trivial closure activity) and the *maximum* giving the meta-regime of $\mathcal{S}$. The fibers are therefore *non-trivially stratified by regime*, even when the meta-regime of $\mathcal{S}$ is a single fixed regime.

## Connection to the realizability program

The corpus's existing [realizability program](prose/teaching/explanations/realizability-program.md) characterizes which classifying classes $[v] \in H^1(\mathbf{T}^*_{\leq \kappa})$ are *realized* — i.e., arise from some closure-rule system. The realizability map $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ operationalizes this at the moduli level:

- A classifying class is *realized* iff its preimage under the [combined cohomology classifying morphism](../morphisms/combined-cohomology-classifying-morphism.md) is non-empty in $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}$.
- The realized classifying classes correspond to the IMAGE of $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ composed with the combined cohomology morphism, evaluated over the realizability program's three structural axes.
- The meta-realizability stratification — meta-strict / meta-SC / meta-TB / meta-aperiodic — refines the realizability picture: a classifying class realized by a meta-aperiodic system is "deeper" than one realized by a meta-strict system.

The [realizability cardinality bound](realizability-cardinality-bound.md) ($\leq 2^{\aleph_0}$ realizable classes) bounds the *image* of $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ composed with the cohomology morphism. The bound is tight in the meta-aperiodic stratum (where the full $2^{\aleph_0}$ is achieved); coarser strata realize fewer classes.

## What the realizability map gives

Three concrete payoffs:

**1. Settings and rules are formally connected at the moduli level.** Where the corpus has previously treated settlings and closure rules as related-but-separate (settlings classified by $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}$, rules implicit in the parameter base), the realizability map $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ makes the connection a stack morphism with explicit content. *Settings ARE the models of rules*, with the moduli structures making the "models / theories" duality precise.

**2. The realizability program's invariants are functorial.** The three realizability-program axes (bigraded, $\mathcal{C}$-naturality, rule-language fragment) become functorial under $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$: each axis lifts from the settling moduli to the meta-moduli. This is the precise structural lift the realizability program needed.

**3. Concrete examples become bilateral.** The corpus's worked examples (strict-regime examples, transfinite-bounded mixed-stratum, etc.) can be classified at the meta level as *which* closure-rule system they are over (their fiber image), giving a more refined classification than the existing settling-side description. The [trivial-base meta-classification example](../examples/trivial-base-meta-classification.md) (forthcoming) is the first concrete instance.
