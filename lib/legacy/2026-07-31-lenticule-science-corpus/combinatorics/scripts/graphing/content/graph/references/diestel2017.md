# Diestel 2017 — Graph Theory

The standard modern graduate-level textbook of graph theory: a comprehensive treatment of the field from foundations through extremal, structural, and infinite graph theory. Now in its 5th edition; the standard reference for almost every result in this directory.

## Why this is cited

Diestel is the corpus's default citation when a standard graph-theoretic result is invoked without qualification. The directory's [graph](../objects/graph.md), [directed-graph](../objects/directed-graph.md), [simple-graph](../objects/simple-graph.md), [tree](../objects/tree.md), [forest](../objects/forest.md), [bipartite-graph](../objects/bipartite-graph.md), [planar-graph](../objects/planar-graph.md), [subgraph](../objects/subgraph.md), and [complete-graph](../objects/complete-graph.md) objects all match Diestel's definitions; the [tree-equivalences](../theorems/tree-equivalences.md), [bipartite-iff-no-odd-cycle](../theorems/bipartite-iff-no-odd-cycle.md), [eulerian-circuit-criterion](../theorems/eulerian-circuit-criterion.md), and [handshake-lemma](../theorems/handshake-lemma.md) theorems follow Diestel's presentations.

## Form

A graduate-level textbook treatment of graph theory in the European pure-mathematics tradition. Roughly 400 pages. The fifth edition (2017) substantially revises and expands the earlier editions (1997, 2000, 2005, 2010); subsequent editions have appeared but the 5th remains the most-cited reference.

## Content summary

Diestel's text covers the standard graduate graph-theory curriculum:

- **Chapters 1-3** — basic notions, matchings, connectivity. The foundational vocabulary: vertex, edge, degree, path, cycle, connectedness, distance, subgraph, induced subgraph, contraction, minor.
- **Chapters 4-5** — planar graphs, Kuratowski's and Wagner's theorems, drawings. The classical planarity material.
- **Chapter 6** — colouring. Chromatic number, list colouring, edge colouring, the four-colour theorem (proof outline).
- **Chapter 7** — flows. Max-flow-min-cut, Menger's theorem, the integrality of network flows.
- **Chapter 8** — substructures in dense graphs. Turán-type problems, the Erdős-Stone theorem.
- **Chapter 9** — Ramsey theory. Ramsey numbers, the canonical Ramsey theorem, applications.
- **Chapter 10** — Hamilton cycles. Sufficient conditions (Dirac, Ore, Chvátal), the Hamiltonian-graph problem's complexity.
- **Chapter 11** — random graphs. The Erdős-Rényi model, threshold functions, the giant component.
- **Chapter 12** — minors, trees, well-quasi-ordering. The Robertson-Seymour graph-minor theorem and its consequences.
- **Appendix** — infinite graphs, the topological appendix on ends and the topological cycle space.

The treatment is *structural* throughout: results are stated and proved as facts about graph homomorphisms, embeddings, minors, and quotients, with the algorithmic side largely deferred to the references.

## Notable claims

- §1.1 — basic definitions of graphs, subgraphs, isomorphism, the complete graph $K_n$.
- §1.5 — trees and forests, including the tree-equivalence characterizations.
- §1.6 — bipartite graphs and the no-odd-cycle characterization.
- §1.8 — Eulerian tours and Hamilton cycles.
- §1.10 — multigraphs, hypergraphs, directed graphs.
- Ch. 4 — planar graphs and Kuratowski's theorem.

## Open availability

The author maintains a free open-availability electronic edition at <https://diestel-graph-theory.com/>, with the same content as the print edition. This is unusual for a Springer GTM and is what makes Diestel especially convenient as a reference in open-corpus work.

## Status

The 5th edition (2017) is the current edition. The book is also available in open-access form from the author's website.
