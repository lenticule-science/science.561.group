## What this node is
Linear algebra is generated by **predicate**, the second step in the unfolding articulate -> predicate -> posit -> settle, here applied under its parent **algebra**. To predicate is to assert that a law-bearing operation holds of its arguments; linear algebra is the predication category **Vect** whose objects are vector spaces and whose morphisms are linear maps — the functions that preserve the two predicated operations, addition and scalar multiplication. It is a restriction of algebra: where algebra fixes any composition law, linear algebra fixes scalars from a field and demands the additive (abelian-group) and monoidal (compatible scaling) structure that makes maps representable by matrices and composition by matrix multiplication.

## What it generates / contains
No canonical subconcepts have been promoted under this node yet. The node's own body (collected in `_archive`) develops finite-dimensional linear algebra:
- **Structures**: vector spaces over a field, vectors, bases, and dimension — the invariant that determines a finite-dimensional space up to isomorphism.
- **Maps**: linear maps as the structure-preserving morphisms (kernel, image, rank–nullity), and matrices as their basis-dependent representations.
- **Invariants and transformers**: the determinant (volume scaling, orientation, invertibility) and eigenvalues/eigenvectors (the directions along which a map acts by pure scaling, via the characteristic polynomial).
- **Code and prose**: executable structure/transformer definitions and teaching texts on vector spaces and on linear maps and matrices.
