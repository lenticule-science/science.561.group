There are two ways to read a tree of directories. One treats a directory as a resemblance bucket and filing as choosing a conventional shelf. The other — the one this corpus is built on — treats the directory tree as a materially operative lattice of arrows: a chain of entailments and a typed term standing as the proof of its own classification. This document is about why the proof-bearing reading is correct and how it cashes out concretely in `lib/relating`, `lib/animating`, and `lib/communicating`.

## The object-oriented temptation

The object-oriented reading of a taxonomy is *containment*. A thing is **placed under** a category. Classification is an `is-a` edge that fixes exactly one location: the object lives in one bucket, the directory *is* the class, and the hierarchy *is* the truth. To file something is to commit it to a single coordinate; to find it is to descend the tree to its one home. The container owns the object, and the object's identity is reduced to its position in the tree.

This is intuitive because filesystems and class hierarchies both reward it locally. It is wrong because it confuses a projection with the thing projected. Two symptoms follow directly. First, the *single home* problem: a document that is genuinely about both information science and category theory must be torn in two, or arbitrarily assigned to one parent and made invisible under the other. Second, the *provenance leak*: because the container owns identity, the easiest path is to name the container after where the material came from — a source repository, an ingestion date, an agent session. The corpus names and forbids exactly this. `content/library/README.md` opens with the rule that "a path name is a claim about what the material is about," and warns: "Do not flatten a source corpus into a single bucket because the source had a name." The kernel restates the same rejection in code: in `lib/relating`'s `holography/index.mts`, a type is "DEFINED BY these morphisms (Yoneda), not by a folder or a spec." The folder is not where the truth lives. The folder is a shadow.

## The categorical reading: an object is its arrows

The categorical alternative inverts the order of explanation. A thing is not *placed under* anything; it is **determined by its morphisms**. This is the content of the Yoneda embedding. For an object `A` in a category `C`, the embedding sends `A` to its hom-functor:

> 𝒴(A) = Hom_C(−, A)

— the entire bundle of arrows into `A` (and, dually, out of it). The embedding is full and faithful: nothing about `A` is lost in passing to its arrows, and nothing is added. The slogan the kernel keeps is the operational form of this theorem — "to understand an object, study how it relates to other objects" — and its sharper consequence: distinct morphisms `f, g : A → B` induce distinct natural transformations, so the arrows are not decoration on top of an identity, the arrows *are* the identity.

Read this back onto a filesystem and the whole picture rotates. The **path is the term**. A POSIX filepath is not a shelf coordinate naming a location; it is a typed term — a proof — that this resource carries a specific type. `lib/relating` states it without hedging: "a path is a categorical address, not a bag of file metadata." The classification facets one would naively make into folders — UDC numbers, Library of Congress class marks, controlled subject headings — are not containers at all. They are **tags**: morphisms the document *asserts about itself*, arrows it offers into the wider category. The crisp difference is this. Where the object-oriented reading says *the directory is the class* and fixes one `is-a` edge, the categorical reading says the directory segment is a *restriction/projection morphism*, and the truth is the whole hom-set — every arrow in and out — of which the tree is merely one rendered projection (a fiber over role, a section over domain) of a content-addressed store.

## Curry-Howard directionality: the path is a proof

The arrows are not undirected. Reading a path from root to leaf is reading a proof from premise to conclusion, and the engine of that reading is the Curry-Howard correspondence — extended, in this corpus, to its Lambek and Hoare faces. A proof of `A ⊢ B`, a program `A → B`, a categorical morphism `A → B`, and a Hoare triple `{A} C {B}` are one and the same arrow: precondition is domain, postcondition is codomain, sequencing is composition. To descend one path segment is to take one step in a proof.

The mechanism that makes this literal is `restrict` in `lib/relating`'s `holography/index.mts`. A `Projector<T>` is a node defined by its morphisms — `recognize`, optional `read`, optional `write` — together with a `parent` edge the source comments call "the Curry–Howard entailment edge." When you `restrict(parent, spec)`, the child's recognition is *conjoined* with the parent's:

> recognize_child = recognize_parent ∧ local

The comment spells out the entailment this enforces: "you cannot be a `json` without being a `flatfile` (Curry–Howard directionality)." This is not an analogy; it is the definition of the child predicate, visible at lines 67–76 of that file. The real codec lattice instantiates the chain — `path → object → attribute → file → flatfile → serialization → json → jsonl → eavt` — and each descent of one segment is exactly one entailment edge. `entails(projector)` returns the chain back to the root: the full proof from conclusion to premises. `classify(resource, catalog)` returns the **deepest** projector that recognizes a resource — the most-specific true proposition the resource inhabits. So the question "which projector recognizes this?" is, precisely, the act of inhabiting a type by traversal. The address structure *is* the proof structure.

Two design judgments fall out of directionality and deserve to be stated plainly, because they are where most intuitions break. First, the leaf carries the least, not the most. At the `eavt` node the codec "carries no polarity and no operation" — it is evidence only, `[entity, attribute, value, time]` witnesses with no assertion and no denial. The codec path you have traversed *bounds what you may express*: negation is a derived gauge symmetry of settling, never something serialized at the flatfile leaf. Second, the address is *honest*. Every derived fact — distance, centrality, cycle structure, the fibration grid — is computed from the one `(domain, role)` coordinate parsed off each node, then projected outward through transports (JSON, Turtle, HTML) without reshaping the store. New views are new namespace entries over the same coordinate, not new core data models.

## The term-path codec: one genuine morphism

The claim "the path is the term" would be empty rhetoric if the term and the path were not actually inverse to each other. They are, and the inverse is the codec in `lib/relating`'s `codecs/index.mts`. `termKey` canonicalizes a `JsonValue` to a stable string — strings pass through, non-strings serialize with object keys sorted, so two terms equal as values produce one key. `sanitizeSegment` encodes that key as a single filesystem-safe POSIX segment, percent-escaping anything outside `[A-Za-z0-9._-]` and special-casing the empty string, `.`, and `..` so the encoding is total and injective. `address(root, term, type)` places the term in the tree, appending a leaf file named after the last segment with the file type as its extension. And `termOf(root, path)` runs the whole thing backward — stripping the root, dropping the leaf, desanitizing each segment — recovering the term. A term like `creatures/ada` maps to `<root>/creatures/ada/ada.<type>` and back without loss.

This round-trip is the canonicality test for a node. A path is canonical *iff* it is `address(root, term, type)` for some term and `termOf` recovers that term; a path that does not round-trip is non-canonical, no matter how sensible it looks. This is why provenance segments are forbidden as a matter of the codec, not merely of taste: a segment naming an ingestion date or a source repo is not the encoding of any topical claim, so it cannot be the term, so the path is not a proof of anything the document is about. `pathProjector` in `codecs/path/projector.mts` is the root of the lattice — it recognizes a resource exactly when `isPathname` holds against `POSIX_PATHNAME_PATTERN` — and `pathPoint` gives each normalized path its stable identity. The path projector recognizes that *this is a path at all*; every restriction beneath it adds one conjunct of meaning.

## The recursive unfolding: topic, library, topic, library

A node is not a leaf in a fixed tree; it is a representable presheaf that grows its own subtree as its content earns one. `content/library/README.md` fixes the recursive shape as `topic/content/topic/content` — "topic/content/topic/content" in shorthand. Each `topic/` directory carries the *topical claim* — "a path name is a claim about what the material is about" — and its `library/` holds the promoted, organized body that the claim births. Categorically, each node is a representable presheaf `𝒴(A) = Hom(−, A)`; as its fiber enriches — as it accumulates "enough internal structure to deserve its own branch," in the README's phrasing — it births subnodes. The unfolding is governed by content, not by a schema imposed in advance. You add a deeper `library/` branch only when a narrower topic has earned it; you never manufacture depth to satisfy a shape.

The Yoneda embedding also dictates *where evidence is recorded*. Because an object is its arrows-in, "the run is recorded only at the codomain, so a node's log accumulates its arrows-in." A morphism is logged at its destination, never at its origin. A node's history is therefore literally its hom-set `Hom(−, A)` materialized — the incoming arrows are the node's accumulated identity, exactly as the embedding says they must be.

## UDC is a tag, never a directory

This is the single most consequential practical corollary, and it follows from everything above rather than from a separate rule. A classification code — a UDC number, an LCC mark — is a *morphism the document offers*: an arrow asserting "I claim membership in this scheme's class." An arrow is not a container. So the code lives in frontmatter, as a tag, exactly as tags are defined to be: "kebab-case slugs for key topics actually mentioned in the file, not redundant restatements of the path." A facet already spent as a path segment is not repeated as a tag, and a classification code never becomes a path segment.

There is a real division of labor here that is easy to miss. Library of Congress *topical headings* may govern the path — they shape the *claim*, the broad-level topical naming the README mandates "when those headings are available." But the classification *code* is a tag. The heading is a statement about what the material is about (and so belongs to the term); the code is an attestation the document makes about itself (and so belongs to the hom-set as an arrow). Collapsing the code into a directory would re-commit the object-oriented error in its most seductive form — turning a perfectly good standard taxonomy into a single forced home — and would break the round-trip, since the code is not the encoding of the topical term.

## `_archive` and `_triage`: fibers not yet settled

If a node is a fixed point of classification, then material that has not yet reached its fixed point needs somewhere to sit that does not pretend to be canonical. That is what `archive/` and the dated triage intake are. Under any topic, `archive/` holds "source-dump notes, old notes, conflicting notes, and unmerged material" until a human or explicit task promotes it; `content/triage/YYYY/MM/DD/` holds intake awaiting a categorization decision. Categorically, these are *fibers over the same base whose section has not yet settled to a fixed point*. They are not a separate truth and not a parallel hierarchy — they are an un-canonicalized projection of the one store. Intake lands first in a topic's `archive/`, then is promoted into `library/` only on settling; and one does not promote into `library/` merely because a topical home is *known*, because knowing the home is not the same as the section having settled.

## The same law in three libraries

The reason to trust this reading is that it is not a story told about one module; it is one law enacted three times, at three different bases, by restriction.

`lib/relating` is the kernel. `holography/index.mts` defines `Projector<T>` by its morphisms (`recognize`, `read`, `write`) — "DEFINED BY these morphisms (Yoneda)" — and gives `restrict`, `entails`, and `classify` the meanings above. The codec in `codecs/index.mts` (`termKey`, `sanitizeSegment`, `address`, `termOf`) makes the path the term, and `codecs/path/projector.mts` (`pathProjector`, `pathPoint`, `isPathname`, `POSIX_PATHNAME_PATTERN`) roots the lattice. The descent `articulate → predicate → posit → settle` — `settlementFiltration`, indexed by ordinal and monotone so no stage drops its predecessor's designated cells — is the path from an utterance to a settled cell.

`lib/animating` projects agency from the same kernel, restating it as a stack of nested morphisms: a `SettlementFiltration` becomes an `AgentCell` (Yoneda recognition: a type is what settles into it), which opens through its settling trace into an `AgentSite`, which lifts to an `AgentPresheaf`. The boundary reader (`embodying/boundary/index.mts`) reads the agent's knowledge-least `fixedPointReadings` as its perspective; `boundary/transition.mts` measures change as it must be measured under this discipline. Its `transition(before, after)` enumerates per-cell `ChargeShift` on the knowledge order, and crucially treats *absence as data*: a cell that has entered or left appears as a shift to or from the **absurd** charge, never as a silent gap (see `transition.mts` lines 89–97). Animating's edge mirrors relating's exactly: "you cannot be an agent site without being an agent cell; you cannot be an agent cell without settling a relating `SettlementFiltration`" — the same Curry-Howard entailment, one base up.

`lib/communicating` projects language. A bottom-up morphism chain lifts a raw string through `admitCommunication` into a carrier wrapping relating's `UtteranceCarrier` — "no text span, parse result, or grammatical reading is primitive at the root" — and `understanding/index.mts` projects semantic frames *from the settled parse without re-settling*, which is to say as natural transformations `Hom(ParseResult, −)`. The headline is `read()` in `communicating/holography/index.mts`: it layers parse, roles, grammaticality, cohomology, and reference over one settled parse, an endomorphism that is itself a Yoneda embedding of the parse into its multi-layer holographic representation (lines 33–44). Same law, third base.

The unification is the point. `relating` supplies the kernel — the `Projector`, `restrict`, `entails`, `classify`, the codec, the descent, the four-valued charge. `animating` and `communicating` each evaluate that same fibration at a concrete base, by restriction, reading the settled fixed point as named projections and preserving the four charges throughout. None of the three reconstructs the tree as a container; all three read identity off the arrows.

## What the difference comes to

The object-oriented taxonomy makes the tree a resemblance shelf and pays for it with torn documents, leaked provenance, and unearned classification. The categorical taxonomy makes every directory segment an earned morphism and the complete tree their durable composition. A path is a typed term; descending it is taking steps in a proof; the deepest projector that recognizes a resource is the most specific true proposition about it; a UDC code is an arrow the document offers, not a wall the document is filed behind; and the recursive `topic/content` unfolding is a presheaf growing the subtree its fiber has earned. The filepath is the Curry-Howard proof coordinate and cannot be flattened, relocated, or replaced by an index without changing the object.
