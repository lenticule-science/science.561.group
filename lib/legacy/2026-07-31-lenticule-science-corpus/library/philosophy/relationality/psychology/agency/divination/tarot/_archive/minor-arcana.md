The **minor arcana** are the 56 suit cards in a tarot deck, divided into four suits — typically Wands, Cups, Swords, and Pentacles (names vary by deck tradition). Each suit contains numbered cards from Ace through Ten and four court cards (Page, Knight, Queen, King).

The four suits are conventionally associated with broad domains of experience:
- **Wands** — action, creativity, will, energy
- **Cups** — emotion, relationships, intuition, the inner life
- **Swords** — thought, conflict, clarity, communication
- **Pentacles** — material reality, work, health, resources

Where the [major arcana](./major-arcana.md) point to large-scale forces and life themes, the minor arcana address the day-to-day: specific situations, practical challenges, and the texture of lived experience. A reading heavy with minor arcana suggests that the situation is grounded in choices and circumstances the querent can directly act on.

The numbered cards (Ace through Ten) trace a progression within each suit's domain — from the seed potential of the Ace through the completion or culmination of the Ten. The court cards represent people, aspects of personality, or modes of engaging with the suit's theme. Whether a court card refers to an external person or an internal quality depends on the reading context and the reader's interpretive approach.
