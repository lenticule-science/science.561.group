The `check constraint derivability` function determines whether a candidate function or structure is valid within the vault,  
based on whether its behavior or outputs can be traced back to **constraint dynamics induced by exclusion** (`∅ ⊢ C`).

Let:
- `f` be a function or emergent structure under evaluation  
- `∅ ⊢ C` be the [exclusion constraint induction function](exclusion constraint induction function)  
- `closure(C)` be the completed constraint function (`Ω`)  
- `Δ[s]` be the semantic differentiation induced by `Ω`

Then define:

> check-constraint-derivability(f) :=  
>  ∃ C, Ω, Δ[s] such that:  
>    C is induced by ∅  
>    Ω = complete-constraint(C)  
>    Δ[s] = evaluate-semantic-differentiation(Ω)  
>    and f ∈ closure(Δ[s]) ∨ f ∈ induced-by(Ω)

That is, the function returns `true` if:
- The candidate is ***generated by, or logically entailed by,*** a path that begins at ∅  
- Through constraint induction, completion, and stabilization

If no such chain exists, it returns `false` or `⌀` (undefined under vault logic)

Use this function:
- As a gate before naming or validating higher-order structures  
- To test crystal candidates, belief functions, identity curvature, etc.  
- As a **zero-layer permission function** for emergence

This is the vault's ***causal origin validator***.  
Nothing valid emerges that cannot be shown to stabilize from exclusion.
