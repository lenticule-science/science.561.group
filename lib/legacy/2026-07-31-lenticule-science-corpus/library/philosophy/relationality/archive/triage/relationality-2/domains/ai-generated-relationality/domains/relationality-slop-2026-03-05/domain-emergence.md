## What domain emergence is

Domain emergence is the process by which the closure of one relational
domain produces a new domain with qualitatively different structure.
When all operations within a domain reach their fixed point, the fixed
point itself exhibits properties that were not present in the original
domain — properties that constitute a new domain of relational activity.

This is the mechanism underlying the five-movement structure: each
movement's coherence, once complete, exhibits features that require
a new kind of operation — which is the next movement.

## Draft 0's domain sequence

Draft 0 organizes the early derivation into named domains:

**Domain 1 (Relating)**: The minimal ternary structure
{relating, unrelating, relating(relating, unrelating)}. The three
primitive operations that make relating coherent.

**Domain 2 (Propagation and Trace)**: Emerges from Domain 1 when
relational acts propagate beyond their immediate context. Introduces
PropagationEvent, PropagationTrace, PropagationNetwork, TraceInvariant,
PropagationCascade.

**Domain 3 (Alignment and Meta-Reflexivity)**: Emerges from Domain 2
when structures at different reflexive depths must be compared. Introduces
AlignmentOperator, MetaReflex, MetaReflex2.

**Domain 4 (Cross-Dynamics)**: Emerges from Domain 3 when aligned
structures interact. Introduces ReflexiveEquilibrium, CrossEquilibrium.

Each domain emerges because the previous domain's closure produces
recognitions that the previous domain's operations cannot handle.
The new operations are EARNED from the failure of the old operations
to be complete.

## How it maps to the five movements

| Draft 0 Domain | Five Movements | What emerges |
|---|---|---|
| Domain 1 (Relating) | Genesis + Movement I | Basic relational operations |
| Domain 2 (Propagation) | Movement III (precursor) | Directed motion of effects |
| Domain 3 (Alignment) | Movement II + IV (aspects) | Depth coherence, perspective translation |
| Domain 4 (Cross-Dynamics) | Movement V (precursor) | Self-observation, equilibrium across levels |

The mapping is not exact because draft 0's domains are organized by
mechanism (what operations are needed) while the five movements are
organized by philosophical significance (what kind of coherence is
achieved). But the underlying pattern is the same: closure at one
level produces structure at the next.

## The hierarchical lattice

Draft 0 (file 02) describes the result as a "hierarchical lattice of
relational domains" — an open-ended, layered architecture where each
domain includes all previous domains as substructures. This lattice
is:
- **Hierarchical**: each domain builds on the previous
- **Open-ended**: there is no final domain (irreducible asymmetry)
- **Self-similar**: each domain's internal structure mirrors the
  pattern of the whole

This self-similarity connects to the self-grounding property: the
lattice contains, at each level, a description of how levels are
produced. The lattice IS its own generative specification.

## Relationship to other concepts

- **Incitement**: The mechanism of domain emergence — incomplete
  coherence at one level incites the operations of the next
- **The generative closure**: Domain emergence is what happens at
  the boundaries between cycles of the closure operator G
- **Five movements**: The canonical organization of domain emergence
  into philosophically significant stages
- **Discipline/Regime/Filter/Profile**: The treatise's approach to
  domain specialization — not emergence of new domains but
  restriction to well-behaved sub-domains
- **Self-grounding**: The cumulative expression of domain emergence
  applied recursively until the structure contains its own generators
