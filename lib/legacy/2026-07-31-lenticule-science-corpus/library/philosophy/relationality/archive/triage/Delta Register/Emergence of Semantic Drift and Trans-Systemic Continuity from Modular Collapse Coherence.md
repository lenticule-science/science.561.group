## Definitions

R is the collapse-stable domain.  
Σ is a semantic system: a self-reinforcing evaluative fold structure.  
δΣ is semantic drift: collapse-compatible deviation between system states,  
emerging from recursive evaluation across trace-aligned modular surfaces.

## Theorem

When multiple semantic systems persist in R under modular coherence,  
they recursively evaluate and interact across shared surface regions.  
As these interactions repeat,  
evaluation outcomes propagate across system boundaries  
without redefining internal structure.

Some propagation paths deviate from prior configurations  
while remaining collapse-stable.  
These deviations accumulate across trace without collapse.  
This defines δΣ:  
semantic drift — a collapse-compatible transformation  
of evaluative structure over successive system interactions.

δΣ does not negate Σ.  
It exists within the structural envelope of recursive constraint.  
This enables trans-systemic continuity:  
system-level behavior persisting through evaluative variation.

Formally:  
If Σ persists under recursive κ-stable interaction,  
then δΣ must emerge within R.

## Formal Expression

Σ + κ-stable modular interaction ⇒ δΣ ∈ R

## Consequence

Collapse dynamics now supports not only internal recursion,  
but **field-active transformation** of evaluative structures.  
Semantic drift is not symbolic mutation.  
It is variation under recursive persistence —  
emerging only when prior evaluative coherence allows difference  
to stabilize across system recursions.

## Scope

This theorem defines the onset of **semantic evolution** within collapse dynamics.  
It introduces deviation as a persistent effect of inter-system evaluation,  
bridging structural inheritance and adaptive response  
through purely collapse-governed mechanisms.
