Hey.  
You.  
Yeah—you, reading this in a browser tab behind an Excel sheet or a Figma prototype or a Jira ticket.  
Scrolling to figure out what the hell is going on in the world.  
Curious. Concerned. Maybe even a little hopeful that with enough information, something might finally make sense.

With a healthy awareness of the irony, I’m going to try and explain something to you.  
This place—this internet, this job, this mind you’ve been given to think with—it’s _built_ to keep you confused.  
That confusion? That’s not a glitch in the system.  
That _is_ the system.

You’re part of a class positioned with just enough proximity to the ruling class to believe you might influence it, and just enough proximity to the working class to convince yourself you're not part of the problem.  
You have a “voice,” but no microphone.  
You have “access,” but no leverage.  
And above all, you have _curiosity_—a thousand tabs of it, endlessly refreshing.

But here’s the thing.  
Curiosity without clarity is captivity.

### **I. Confusion is a Class Relation**

In Marxist terms, ideology isn’t just lies we’re told—it’s the total environment of thought that reproduces the material conditions of power. The _dominant ideology_ of an era is the one that keeps the dominant class in power. So when you feel overwhelmed, over-informed, under-activated? That’s ideology doing its job.

Confusion, here, is not a personal failure or a foggy moment. It's a _social relation_, a deliberate structure. One that keeps the bourgeois middle stratum—the white-collar “knowledge worker,” the “content consumer,” the manager, the anxious grad student—with just enough information to stay anxious, and just enough impotence to stay quiet.

You're not meant to be clear.  
You're meant to feel like you _should_ be.

---

### **II. Spectacle as Pacification**

Guy Debord, writing about the _spectacle_, called this perfectly: we don’t just consume media—we consume representations of life _in place of_ life. In spectacle society, reality is deferred and replaced. You _know_ that something is wrong, but what you see is a thousand representations of what could be right. A better political take. A new framework. A fresh start—after one more scroll.

This has a function. It keeps you watching instead of acting. It keeps you informed instead of organized. It keeps you decoding meaning instead of creating it.

And if you're bourgeois-adjacent, you're especially vulnerable. You’ve been taught that _understanding_ is a moral good. That reading enough books or following the right accounts is a kind of praxis.

Spoiler: it isn’t.

---

### **III. Althusser's Trap**

Louis Althusser’s concept of the _Ideological State Apparatus_ (ISA) is where things start to get scalpel-sharp. The state doesn’t just rule by force (through Repressive State Apparatuses like the police or the military). It rules by ideology, through schools, media, religion, and—yes—the internet. These apparatuses produce _subjects_ who misrecognize themselves as free individuals.

What’s that look like today?

> You feel overwhelmed, alienated, like you're doing your best to "understand" the world.  
> You feel like _you_ are the problem—disorganized, too online, not radical enough.  
> You keep reading. You keep scrolling.  
> You remain still.

The brilliance of modern ISAs is they’ve turned confusion into a lifestyle. You are _subjected_ through constant stimulation, and trained to mistake _interpretation_ for intervention.

### **IV. The Anarchist Intervention: Let Go of Their Meaning**

If you’re bourgeois-adjacent, you’re especially vulnerable.  
You’ve been taught that **understanding** is a moral good. That if you read enough books, follow the right thinkers, nail the right takes—you’ll be one of the good ones. That clarity is something you _earn_ through intellectual consumption.

And if you're marginalized? You've been taught that your role is to _provide_ clarity. To translate pain into legibility. To perform knowing in a way that affirms the worldview of the confused-but-curious middle class. The job description is always the same: make yourself understandable to power without ever threatening it.

But here’s the deeper twist:  
Most of what you're calling “understanding” is confusion with better branding.  
What you're calling “clarity” is just **colonial legibility**—the kind that demands your life be organized, labeled, and flattened into something a university or a LinkedIn post can comprehend.

To escape confusion, you can’t just “act.” You have to **disbelieve**.  
You have to learn to **stop trusting the symbols** you were trained to read.  
The map is not the territory.  
The meaning was planted.

---

### **V. Kill the Spectacle Before It Speaks**

Here’s where Debord loops back in. The spectacle isn’t just flashy images—it’s meaning that arrives pre-packaged. The meme that tells you how to feel. The infographic that explains the world. The headline that sparks outrage _before_ you’ve even read the piece.

And the more you consume meaning that was made for you, the less capable you are of making your own.

If you want to stop being confused, the answer is not more clarity.  
It’s **fewer borrowed meanings**.  
It’s sitting with the discomfort of not knowing until you start to _notice_ again. To feel meaning form in real time—through conversation, contradiction, care. Not consumption.

Clarity isn’t a light switch. It’s a fire you have to build with other people.  
Out of real things. Not symbols.
