# Sleep-Circadian

## Formal definition

A **SleepCircadian** triple $(S, t, \mathrm{org})$:

$$(S \in \mathcal{S}_t,\; t \in T,\; \mathrm{org} \in \mathrm{Organism})$$

where:
- $\mathcal{S}_t$ is the **organism sleep fiber** at history $t$ — the complete Heyting algebra of sleep state propositions at $t$; elements of $\mathcal{S}_t$ are statements about the sleep stage, synaptic potentiation level, adenosine burden, circadian phase angle, and arousal threshold of organism $\mathrm{org}$ at history $t$; the fiber inherits its Heyting algebra structure from $H_t$
- $t \in T$ is the **analysis history** — the position in the history monoid at which the organism's sleep state is evaluated; each step $s \in \Sigma$ advances the organism through one regulation cycle — one period of wakefulness, one sleep session, one zeitgeber pulse (dawn, dusk, meal, social contact); sleeping and waking transitions are specific step atoms in $\Sigma$
- $\mathrm{org}$ is the **organism** — the biological individual whose sleep-wake state is evaluated; the organism's chronotype (genetically determined free-running period $\tau$), body temperature amplitude, and adenosine receptor density are fixed parameters of the locale

**The sleep nuclear pair.** The fiber $H_t$ at history $t$ carries the two canonical nuclei, each corresponding to one of the two independent regulatory processes that Borbély (1982) identified as necessary and sufficient to predict sleep timing and structure in mammals. Let $\Sigma$ be the step monoid for the organism site $(T, J)$. Each step $s \in \Sigma$ represents one zeitgeber cycle — one light-dark transition, one temperature nadir, one meal, one social clock pulse. The commutation condition $s \perp t$ means zeitgeber $s$ is genuinely independent of the current history $t$: a morning light pulse is independent of whether the previous night's sleep was shallow or deep (the sun rises regardless). The one-step extension $i_{s,t} : t \to s \star t$ is the history extended by one zeitgeber event; the restriction map $H(i_{s,t}) : H_{s \star t} \to H_t$ sends a post-zeitgeber sleep state back to the pre-zeitgeber state.

**RelationalHistoryFiberSaturatingNucleus** ([math](../math/relational-history-fiber-saturating-nucleus.md)) — the **homeostatic sleep pressure operator**:

$$\sigma_t(S) \;=\; \bigvee\bigl\{D \in H_t \;\big|\; H(i_{t_0,t})(D) \leq H(i_{t_0,t})(S) \;\text{for all } t_0 < t\bigr\}$$

where $H(i_{t_0,t}) : H_t \to H_{t_0}$ projects the current sleep state back to the prior history. The restriction profile quantity entering $\sigma_t$ is **Process S** — the homeostatic sleep pressure function:

$$S_{\mathrm{process}}(t) \;=\; \begin{cases} S_0 + (U - S_0)(1 - e^{-(t - t_{\mathrm{wake}})/\tau_w}) & \text{during waking} \\ S_0 e^{-(t - t_{\mathrm{onset}})/\tau_s} & \text{during sleep} \end{cases}$$

where $U$ is the upper threshold, $S_0$ is the lower threshold, $\tau_w \approx 18\,\mathrm{h}$ is the waking time constant, and $\tau_s \approx 4\,\mathrm{h}$ is the sleep time constant (Daan, Beersma & Borbély 1984, *Sleep* 7, 354–381). The cellular substrate is **adenosine**: extracellular adenosine in the basal forebrain accumulates during waking (reflecting neural activity and ATP hydrolysis) and is cleared during non-REM sleep via glymphatic flux; adenosine acts on A1 and A2A receptors to suppress arousal. $H(i_{t_0,t})(S)$ is the prior adenosine burden — the restriction profile class of $S$ is the set of sleep states with at most $S$'s accumulated adenosine record; $\sigma_t(S)$ is the maximum sleep state consistent with that homeostatic history. $S \in \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t)$ iff $S$ is already the most consolidated sleep state its pressure history supports.

**RelationalHistoryFiberTransferringNucleus** ([math](../math/relational-history-fiber-transferring-nucleus.md)) — the **circadian phase-locking operator**:

$$\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t) \;=\; \bigcap_{\substack{s \in \Sigma \\ s \perp t}} \mathrm{image}\bigl(H(i_{s,t}) : H_{s \star t} \to H_t\bigr)$$

and:

$$\Delta_t(S) \;=\; \bigwedge\bigl\{D \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t) \;\big|\; D \geq S\bigr\}$$

$S \in \mathrm{image}(H(i_{s,t}))$ means there exists a post-zeitgeber state $S' \in H_{s \star t}$ with $H(i_{s,t})(S') = S$ — the sleep state $S$ has a forward preimage through zeitgeber event $s$; the organism's circadian phase can carry $S$ through one instance of zeitgeber $s$ and recover $S$. $S \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ iff for every independent zeitgeber step $s \perp t$, the sleep state $S$ has a forward preimage — the circadian oscillator's current phase commitment persists through every independent light, temperature, and social cue. The biological substrate is the **suprachiasmatic nucleus** (SCN): the master circadian clock in the hypothalamic anterior region, whose ~\$24\,\mathrm{h}$ oscillation is encoded in the CLOCK-BMAL1 / PERIOD-CRYPTOCHROME transcriptional feedback loop and entrained to external zeitgebers via the retinohypothalamic tract. $\Delta_t(S)$ is the minimum circadian-phase-locked sleep state above $S$ — the smallest sleep state that persists through all independent zeitgeber events and is at least as deep as $S$.

**The four shadow classes for sleep states:**

| Shadow class | Condition | Sleep name | Physiological state |
|---|---|---|---|
| $S \in \mathcal{S}^*_t$ | Both operators fix $S$ | **Consolidated sleep** | Full slow-wave and REM cycling; synaptic downscaling operative; phase-coherent |
| $S \in \mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{S}}_t$ | Pressure fixes $S$; phase does not | **Sleep-debt state** | High adenosine burden; pressure recognized; circadian phase disrupted or misaligned |
| $S \in \mathrm{TrShadow}^{\mathcal{S}}_t$ | Phase fixes $S$; pressure does not | **Circadian-lag state** | Circadian rhythm committed to correct phase; homeostatic pressure not yet cleared |
| $S \in \mathrm{FreeShadow}^{\mathcal{S}}_t$ | Neither operator fixes $S$ | **Sleep collapse** | Neither homeostatic pressure nor circadian rhythm maintains sleep |

**The nuclear reflection and holographic projection.**

$$\pi_t \;:=\; \sigma_t \circ \Delta_t \;:\; H_t \to H_t$$

is the [RelationalHistoryPropositionFiberSaturatingTransferringCommutingNuclearReflection](../math/relational-history-fiber-saturating-transferring-commuting-nuclear-reflection.md) with $\mathrm{Fix}(\pi_t) = H^*_t$. The canonical holograph $(\mathcal{S}_t, \mathcal{S}^*_t, \pi_t)$ has the consolidated sleep stratum $\mathcal{S}^*_t$ as the **boundary** $\mathsf{Phase}$ and the full sleep fiber as **bulk**; $\pi_t$ sends every sleep state to its nearest consolidated-sleep stabilization. The [RelationalHistoryProjectionGap](../math/relational-history-fiber-projection-gap.md) $[S, \pi_t(S)]$ has size

$$\lambda_S \;:=\; \bigl|[S,\, \pi_t(S)]\bigr| - 1 \;\geq\; 0$$

— the **sleep recovery depth** — zero iff $S \in \mathcal{S}^*_t$. The defect operator $\delta_t(e_S) = \lambda_S \cdot e_S$ on $K^{\mathcal{S}_t}$ grades sleep states by distance to consolidation; the calibration defect $M_t(\delta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\delta_t) = \sum_{S \notin \mathcal{S}^*_t} \lambda_S$ is the total spectral sleep debt of the organism at history $t$.

**The internal holograph: sleep stages.** The sleep session has an internal holographic structure $(W, R, \pi_{\mathrm{REM}})$:
- $W$ = the **slow-wave bulk** — the space of N1/N2/N3 (non-REM) sleep states; the domain of Process S clearance; the space in which synaptic downscaling (Tononi-Cirelli synaptic homeostasis hypothesis) operates
- $R$ = the **REM boundary** — the phase of the internal holograph; the space of REM sleep states where slow-wave-cleared synaptic architecture is inscribed with the circadian rhythm's emotional and procedural memory consolidation
- $\pi_{\mathrm{REM}} : W \to R$ = the slow-wave-to-REM transition operator — the internal projection that carries a non-REM sleep state to its REM-consolidated partner; $\pi_{\mathrm{REM}}(w) = w$ iff the slow-wave clearance has fully resolved into a stable REM-consolidated state

The sleep session as a whole is the iterate of the \$90$-minute ultradian cycle: each cycle applies $\pi_{\mathrm{REM}}$ once, deepening the consolidation. Early-night cycles are mostly slow-wave-heavy (high Process S, large $W$ component); late-night cycles are mostly REM-heavy (Process S cleared, circadian drive for REM accumulating). The internal holograph is complete iff $W = R$ — when every slow-wave state is already its REM-consolidated partner; total sleep restriction makes $W \gg R$ because Process S forces slow-wave dominance and truncates REM.

**The sleep equilibrium condition.** $(S, t, \mathrm{org})$ is **consolidated sleep** iff $S \in \mathcal{S}^*_t$ — equivalently, any of:
1. $S$ is in the boundary $\mathsf{Phase}$ of the canonical holograph: $\pi_t(S) = S$
2. The projection gap is trivial: $[S, \pi_t(S)] = \{S\}$, i.e., $\lambda_S = 0$
3. $S$ is a zero-eigenmode of the defect operator: $\delta_t(e_S) = 0$
4. $S \in \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$: homeostatic pressure clears AND circadian phase commits simultaneously

## What this is

Sleep is the one physiological state that remains, after decades of investigation, without an unambiguous survival function. Every tissue can rest; only brains seem to require the specific ritual of consolidated sleep, and the evolutionary cost — hours of immobility and vulnerability — demands a powerful benefit. The nuclear reading does not explain why sleep exists, but it makes precise what consolidated sleep IS: the simultaneous fixed point of two independent regulatory pressures that are always running, always generating gap between the current state and the two fixed-point sets, and always pulling the organism toward their respective attractors.

**Borbély's two-process model** (1982, *Human Neurobiology* 1, 195–204; Daan, Beersma & Borbély 1984, *Sleep* 7, 354–381) is the nuclear pair written down by a sleep scientist. Borbély identified the two independent regulatory signals — homeostatic pressure (Process S) and circadian rhythm (Process C) — and showed that sleep timing and structure are determined by their interaction: sleep begins when S rises above the upper C-threshold, ends when S falls below the lower C-threshold, and the C-thresholds oscillate at ~24 h driven by the SCN. The model predicted the timing, duration, EEG slow-wave activity, and REM distribution of human sleep with quantitative accuracy. The nuclear reading is the same structure in categorical dress: Process S = restriction profile of the saturation nucleus; Process C = Grothendieck topology of the transfer nucleus's independent steps.

## The adenosine mechanism and the sleep pressure substrate

The cellular substrate of Process S is **adenosine** — the nucleoside produced by hydrolysis of ATP during neural activity (Porkka-Heiskanen et al. 1997, *Science* 276, 1265–1268). During waking, extracellular adenosine accumulates in the basal forebrain and cortex proportional to neural activity. Adenosine acts on inhibitory A1 receptors (suppressing wake-active neurons in the basal forebrain) and excitatory A2A receptors (activating sleep-promoting neurons in the ventrolateral preoptic area, VLPO). The net effect: progressive inhibition of arousal proportional to accumulated waking time.

This is the restriction-profile quantity entering $\sigma_t$: $H(i_{t_0,t})(S)$ is the adenosine burden at prior history $t_0$ on the path to the current state. The saturation nucleus $\sigma_t(S) = \bigvee\{D \mid H(i_{t_0,t})(D) \leq H(i_{t_0,t})(S) \text{ for all } t_0 < t\}$ computes the maximum sleep depth consistent with the current adenosine accumulation profile. A sleep state $S$ is homeostically saturated ($S \in \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t)$) iff there is no deeper sleep state consistent with the same prior adenosine record.

**Caffeine** is a competitive antagonist at A1 and A2A receptors: it does not eliminate adenosine accumulation but blocks its signaling. In nuclear terms, caffeine artificially disrupts the restriction-map signal of the saturation nucleus without changing the actual adenosine record: $H(i_{t_0,t})(S)$ still accumulates, but the nucleus's access to that record is partially blocked. The sleep debt (high adenosine burden) is not eliminated — only masked. When caffeine clears, the full saturation nucleus signal is restored and sleep pressure is suddenly readable, producing the characteristic caffeine-crash.

**Glymphatic clearance** (Xie et al. 2013, *Science* 342, 373–377): during non-REM sleep, interstitial space in the brain expands ~60% and cerebrospinal fluid flows through perivascular spaces, clearing adenosine, amyloid-beta, tau, and other metabolic waste. This is the mechanism of $\sigma_t(S)$ acting: consolidation sleep clears the adenosine burden from the restriction profile, reducing $H(i_{t_0,t})(S)$ for future $t_0$, and allowing the saturation nucleus to backward-recognize a lower-pressure state.

## The circadian clock and zeitgeber topology

The **suprachiasmatic nucleus** (SCN) is a paired structure of approximately \$20{,}000$ neurons in the anterior hypothalamus, directly above the optic chiasm. Its ~\$24\,\mathrm{h}$ oscillation is encoded in the CLOCK-BMAL1 / PERIOD-CRYPTOCHROME transcriptional-translational feedback loop: CLOCK-BMAL1 heterodimers activate *Period* and *Cryptochrome* transcription; PER-CRY complexes accumulate, inhibit CLOCK-BMAL1, and are then degraded, allowing a new cycle to begin. The period of the free-running clock (in constant dim light) averages $\tau \approx 24.2\,\mathrm{h}$ in humans — slightly longer than the solar day, so the clock must be reset daily by zeitgebers.

**Zeitgebers** (German: *time-givers*; Jürgen Aschoff 1954) are the external cues that entrain the circadian oscillator. In the nuclear reading, each zeitgeber class is a step type $s \in \Sigma$, and the circadian phase-locking condition $S \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ means the organism's sleep state has a forward preimage through every independent zeitgeber step. The primary zeitgebers and their independence structure:

| Zeitgeber | Mechanism | Independence condition |
|---|---|---|
| Light (dawn/dusk) | Retinohypothalamic tract → SCN | Independent unless organism is completely isolated from light cycles |
| Ambient temperature nadir | Direct SCN input; skin thermoreceptors | Independent unless in a constant-temperature environment |
| Meal timing | Peripheral clocks (liver, gut) can run independently of SCN | Independent unless food is withheld entirely |
| Social contact / activity | Diffuse; mediated by cortisol, social timing cues | Weakly independent; suppressed by complete social isolation |

The **phase response curve** (PRC) is the transfer nucleus's topology on the history site: light in the early subjective night (evening) delays the circadian phase (step type "delay" $s_-$: $s_- \perp t$ for most history states, pushing the clock later); light in the late subjective night/early morning advances it (step type "advance" $s_+$: $s_+ \perp t$ for histories in the advance-responsive window). The PRC is the structure of which zeitgeber steps are genuinely independent of which history states — it is the Grothendieck topology $J$ on the circadian history site.

## Jet lag and shift work as shadow-class misalignment

**Jet lag** is the temporary $\mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{S}}_t$ state induced by rapid transmeridian travel that moves the social time zone faster than the circadian clock can re-entrain. After flying eastward by 8 time zones:

- Homeostatic pressure: $\sigma_t$ is operative — the organism feels tired at the local subjective evening (pressure has accumulated across the waking day)
- Circadian phase: the SCN is still on origin-timezone time; it is driving arousal (subjective afternoon) at local midnight; the independent steps $\{s \perp^J t\}$ of the local zeitgeber set have not yet become the covering family they will need to be for forward-commitment; $\Delta_t(S) > S$ — the circadian phase is not yet forward-committed through the new local zeitgeber set

The organism is in $\mathrm{SatShadow}$: homeostatic pressure backward-recognizes exhaustion, but the circadian operator has not yet forward-committed to the new zeitgeber topology. Re-entrainment rate: approximately \$1\,\mathrm{h}$ per day eastward (phase advance is harder), \$1.5\,\mathrm{h}$ per day westward (phase delay is easier), because the free-running period $\tau > 24\,\mathrm{h}$ makes the clock naturally inclined to phase delay.

**Chronic shift work** is the persistent $\mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{S}}_t$ state in which the social schedule enforces a zeitgeber topology that the SCN cannot fully re-entrain to, and the re-entrainment of peripheral organ clocks (liver, gut, pancreas) is permanently misaligned with the central SCN signal. The independent zeitgeber steps $\{s \perp t\}$ include both the social schedule (light-dark exposure forced by work hours) and the internal SCN signal (driving arousal at the wrong local time). The organism's sleep state cannot forward-commit through both simultaneously; the transfer nucleus's fixed-point condition cannot be satisfied; chronic $\mathrm{SatShadow}$ is the structural outcome. The epidemiology confirms the nuclear prediction: chronic shift work is associated with increased rates of metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, cancer (IARC Group 2A carcinogen: "probably carcinogenic to humans"), and all-cause mortality — the pathology of a sleep state permanently excluded from $\mathcal{S}^*_t$.

**Social jet lag** (Roenneberg et al. 2012, *Current Biology* 22, 939–943): the discrepancy between biological clock time (determined by chronotype, i.e., free-running period $\tau$ and PRC shape) and social clock time (enforced by school, work, and social schedules). The average social jet lag in industrialized populations is ~1.5–2 h, peaking in evening chronotypes; it correlates with BMI, smoking, alcohol use, and mood disorders. Social jet lag is chronic $\mathrm{SatShadow}$: the homeostatic pressure record faithfully accumulates the mismatch, but the zeitgeber topology imposed by social schedules prevents the circadian phase-locking operator from forward-committing the organism to consolidated sleep.

## The Tononi-Cirelli synaptic homeostasis hypothesis and the REM boundary

**Giulio Tononi and Chiara Cirelli** proposed the Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis (SHY; Tononi & Cirelli 2003, *Brain Research Bulletin* 62, 143–150; Tononi & Cirelli 2006, *Sleep* 29, 145–165; Tononi & Cirelli 2014, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 15, 169–179): waking experience and learning potentiate synapses throughout the cortex; slow-wave sleep restores the signal-to-noise ratio by downscaling synaptic weights globally, removing the net potentiation while preserving the relative pattern of learned associations. The evidence: slow-wave activity (0.5–4 Hz EEG oscillations) is proportional to the amount and intensity of prior waking neural activity; high-frequency stimulation of cortex before sleep increases local slow-wave power; genetic manipulations that increase synaptic potentiation increase slow-wave activity.

In the internal holograph $(W, R, \pi_{\mathrm{REM}})$: SHY operates in the slow-wave bulk $W$. The internal projection gap $\lambda_w^{\mathrm{holo}} = |[w, \pi_{\mathrm{REM}}(w)]| - 1$ is the synaptic potentiation debt of a slow-wave state — how many downscaling steps separate the current synaptic weight distribution from the REM-consolidatable configuration. High waking activity expands this gap; deep slow-wave sleep closes it. The REM boundary $R$ is the destination: REM sleep is where the downscaled synaptic architecture is reconsolidated with the emotional and procedural tagging that circadian-driven noradrenaline withdrawal and acetylcholine elevation permit (Walker 2009, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 10, 114–126).

**REM rebound**: after selective REM deprivation, organisms show a disproportionate increase in REM time on recovery nights (Dement 1960, *Science* 131, 1705–1707). In holographic terms: depriving the organism of REM accumulates debt in the internal projection gap — the slow-wave states are cleared but the REM consolidation projection $\pi_{\mathrm{REM}}$ cannot complete; the deficit in the $R$ sector is logged as a projection-gap debt. Recovery nights show the REM boundary "pulling" the system strongly: $\pi_{\mathrm{REM}}$ accelerates, REM onset occurs earlier in the night, and REM percentage exceeds baseline until the internal projection gap is closed.

## Fatal familial insomnia and the Schwarzschild threshold

**Fatal familial insomnia** (FFI; Lugaresi et al. 1986, *New England Journal of Medicine* 315, 997–1003) is an autosomal dominant prion disease caused by the PRNP D178N mutation on the methionine-129 polymorphism. The disease destroys the thalamic nuclei — specifically the mediodorsal and anterior thalamic nuclei — through prion-mediated neurodegeneration, producing progressive inability to sleep followed by dementia and death within 7–73 months of onset. Approximately 40 families worldwide carry the mutation; no treatment is available.

The thalamic nuclei are critical to sleep because they gate the corticothalamic dialogue that generates sleep spindles and slow-wave oscillations: the reticular nucleus of the thalamus inhibits relay nuclei rhythmically, producing the 11–15 Hz spindle bursts that characterize N2 sleep; the coordination between thalamus and cortex generates the slow oscillation of N3. Without thalamic function, neither the slow-wave machinery nor the spindle-gating of the transfer operator can operate.

In nuclear terms: FFI is the organic destruction of both nuclei simultaneously. The thalamic degeneration eliminates the cellular mechanism of $\sigma_t$ (slow-wave downscaling requires thalamo-cortical coordination) and $\Delta_t$ (spindle-gated forward-commitment requires reticular thalamic inhibition) in tandem. The sleep state migrates to $\mathrm{FreeShadow}^{\mathcal{S}}_t$ — and, crucially, the migration is permanent: the thalamic tissue is not regenerated, so the restriction profiles and covering families that $\sigma_t$ and $\Delta_t$ depend on are structurally destroyed.

This is the **Schwarzschild threshold crossing** in the most severe form: $M_t(\delta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\delta_t)$ rises without bound as the thalamic destruction removes the cellular machinery that closes projection gaps. The spectral horizon $r_s(\delta_t) = 2M_t(\delta)$ rises to encompass all sleep modes; every sleep state $S$ has $\lambda_S > M_t(\delta)$; all modes are spectrally trapped in the waking sector. No external intervention has been found that reduces $M_t(\delta)$ once the thalamic degeneration passes a critical threshold — consistent with the prediction that modes above the spectral horizon cannot decay to $E_{t,0}$ through internal dynamics alone, and that external energy input (here: medical intervention equivalent to the structural deficit) would be required to effect recovery.

**Rechtschaffen's rat sleep deprivation** (1989, *Sleep* 12, 68–87): total sleep deprivation in rats using the disk-over-water apparatus produced death in all subjects within 2–3 weeks, characterized by thermoregulatory failure, hyperphagia, septicemia, and multi-organ failure. The cause of death was not determined conclusively, but the lethality confirmed that consolidated sleep is not optional for mammalian survival. In nuclear terms: chronic total deprivation prevents the organism from ever reaching $\mathcal{S}^*_t$; $M_t(\delta)$ increases monotonically; the spectral debt accumulates without the glymphatic clearance and synaptic downscaling that would reduce it; and when $M_t(\delta)$ crosses the Schwarzschild radius, the organism enters a trapped spectral state from which metabolic recovery cannot proceed.

Sleep offset occurs when $\sigma_t$ clears the pressure record and the circadian gate closes.
