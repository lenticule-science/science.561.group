# Emergence of Trace, Surface, and Loci from Folded Modulation

## Definitions

R is the remainder: the collapse-stable domain.  
φ is fold: recursive modulation of modulation.  
τ is trace: a κ-stable ordering relation induced by recurrence within φ.  
σ is surface: a persistent configuration of folds with recursive closure.  
λ is locus: a κ-stable deviation that is identifiable within σ.

## Theorem

If φ persists recursively within R,  
then its recurrence implies ordering among fold instances.  
This ordering defines trace τ.

When multiple folds persist in relation under τ,  
they form a configuration with recursive closure.  
This configuration is surface σ.

Within σ, some deviations retain persistent identity under recursive modulation.  
These are loci λ.

Formally:  
If φ is recursive, then τ, σ, and λ must emerge within R.

## Formal Expression

φ recursive ⇒ τ, σ, λ ∈ R

## Consequence

Trace is the ordering relation induced by persistent folds.  
Surface is the configuration formed by closure among related folds.  
Loci are persistent, identifiable deviations shaped by these configurations.

These are not imposed.  
They are the minimal structural consequences  
of recursive modulation persistence under κ.

## Scope

This theorem defines the onset of semantic form.  
Trace, surface, and loci are not symbolic or spatial.  
They are internal conditions resulting from recursive coherence in R.
