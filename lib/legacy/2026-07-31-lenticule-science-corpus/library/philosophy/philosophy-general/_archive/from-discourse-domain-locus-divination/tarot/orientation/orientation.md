**Orientation** refers to whether a tarot card appears upright or reversed (upside-down) when revealed in a [spread](./spread.md). In traditions that read reversals, a reversed card modifies the card's meaning — typically by introducing delay, internalization, blockage, or the shadow side of the card's upright theme.

Not all readers use reversals. Some traditions read all cards upright, treating the card's position in the spread and its relationship to neighboring cards as sufficient for nuance. Others treat reversals as essential, arguing that without them the deck can only speak in one register and loses half its expressive range.

When reversals are used, their interpretation varies by reader and tradition. Common approaches include:
- **Blocked or delayed energy** — the card's theme is present but obstructed or not yet manifest
- **Internal rather than external** — the card's theme is experienced inwardly rather than expressed outwardly
- **Excess or deficiency** — too much or too little of the card's quality
- **Shadow or unconscious** — the card's theme is operating below awareness

The choice to read reversals is a structural decision about the reading system itself. It determines how much interpretive weight falls on the card's visual orientation versus its position in the spread and its relationships with other cards.
