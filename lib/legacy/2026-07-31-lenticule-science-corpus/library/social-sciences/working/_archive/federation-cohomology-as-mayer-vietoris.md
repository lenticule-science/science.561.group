# Federation cohomology as Mayer–Vietoris

A federated polity — one whose jurisdiction $\mathcal{J}$ presents as the union of constituent jurisdictions $\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}_1 \cup \mathcal{J}_2$, each itself a [jurisdiction](../objects/jurisdiction.md) per [jurisdiction-as-base-change](jurisdiction-as-base-change.md) — has its cohomological signature computed by a **Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence**, applying [replica-mayer-vietoris](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/replica-mayer-vietoris.md) to the jurisdictional cover.

The substantive content: **the cohomological signature of a federation is not the sum of its constituent signatures.** It is the homology of the comparison map between the constituents and their overlap — a much subtler invariant. The Mayer–Vietoris sequence makes precise *which* federation phenomena are generated by single constituents (image content), *which* by the overlap (kernel content), and *which* by the assembly itself (connecting-homomorphism content). The third class is the substrate-algebraic content of *"federation produces structures that exist only at the federal level."*

## Setup: the jurisdictional cover

Per [jurisdiction-as-base-change](jurisdiction-as-base-change.md), each jurisdiction $\mathcal{J}_i$ presents as an inclusion $\iota_{J_i} : \mathcal{C}_{J_i} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}$ on the closure-rule system, with associated base-change $\iota_{J_i}^* : \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{E}_{J_i}$ on the predication fibration.

A **jurisdictional cover** of a federated jurisdiction $\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}_1 \cup \mathcal{J}_2$ is the data:

- Two constituent inclusions $\iota_{J_1}, \iota_{J_2}$.
- An overlap jurisdiction $\mathcal{J}_1 \cap \mathcal{J}_2$ with its own inclusion $\iota_{J_{12}} : \mathcal{C}_{J_{12}} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}$ where $\mathcal{C}_{J_{12}} = \mathcal{C}_{J_1} \cap \mathcal{C}_{J_2}$.
- The federation inclusion $\iota_J : \mathcal{C}_J \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}$ where $\mathcal{C}_J = \mathcal{C}_{J_1} \cup \mathcal{C}_{J_2}$.
- Compatibility: the cover is **subcanonical** in the sense of the [parameter-base-topology](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/parameter-base-topology.md), meaning the diagram of inclusions presents as a covering family in the bitopological site of [bitopological-settling-site-axioms](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/bitopological-settling-site-axioms.md).

The **federation settling** $v^J$ is the polity's full predication settling restricted to $\mathcal{C}_J$. The **constituent settlings** $v^{J_i}$ are restrictions to $\mathcal{C}_{J_i}$. The **overlap settling** $v^{J_{12}}$ is the restriction to $\mathcal{C}_{J_{12}}$.

The associated [settling-associated sheaves](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/settling-associated-sheaf.md) $\mathcal{F}^{J}, \mathcal{F}^{J_i}, \mathcal{F}^{J_{12}}$ inherit the cover structure.

## Statement

**Theorem (federation Mayer–Vietoris).** *Let $\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}_1 \cup \mathcal{J}_2$ be a federated jurisdiction with subcanonical jurisdictional cover. Suppose:*

1. *(Cover compatibility.) The cover $\{\iota_{J_1}, \iota_{J_2}\}$ satisfies the covering-family axiom of the [parameter-base-topology](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/parameter-base-topology.md) on $\mathcal{C}_J$.*

2. *(Sheaf condition.) The settling-associated sheaf $\mathcal{F}^J$ satisfies the sheaf condition on the cover (per [d-frame-valued-sheaf-condition](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/d-frame-valued-sheaf-condition.md)).*

*Then there is a long exact Mayer–Vietoris sequence:*

$$\cdots \to H^n(\mathcal{F}^J) \xrightarrow{(\iota_{J_1}^*, \iota_{J_2}^*)} H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_1}) \oplus H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_2}) \xrightarrow{\iota_{J_{12}}^* - \iota_{J_{12}}^*} H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}}) \xrightarrow{\delta} H^{n+1}(\mathcal{F}^J) \to \cdots$$

*by [replica-mayer-vietoris](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/replica-mayer-vietoris.md) applied to the jurisdictional cover, where:*

(a) **(Constituent-image content.)** *The image of the restriction map $H^n(\mathcal{F}^J) \to H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_1}) \oplus H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_2})$ records federation cohomology generated by single-constituent content — phenomena visible at $\mathcal{J}_1$ or $\mathcal{J}_2$ alone.*

(b) **(Overlap-kernel content.)** *The kernel of the difference map $H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_1}) \oplus H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_2}) \to H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}})$ records cohomology classes that agree on the overlap — phenomena that lift consistently from constituents to the federation.*

(c) **(Federation-only content.)** *The cokernel of $\iota_{J_{12}}^* - \iota_{J_{12}}^*$ in degree $n$ — equivalently, the image of the connecting homomorphism $\delta : H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}}) \to H^{n+1}(\mathcal{F}^J)$ in degree $n+1$ — records federation cohomology that is **not** generated by either constituent: classes existing only at the federal level. This is the substrate-algebraic content of "federation produces emergent structures."*

(d) **(Federation-disagreement obstruction, one-way.)** *If the difference map $\iota_{J_{12}}^* - \iota_{J_{12}}^* : H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_1}) \oplus H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_2}) \to H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}})$ is **not surjective** — equivalently, there is a class on the overlap that no pair of constituent classes can collectively realize — then by exactness $\delta$ produces a non-trivial $H^1(\mathcal{F}^J)$ class. This is the obstruction-theory reading of "the federation cannot decide because there is overlap-content that the constituents cannot jointly express." The converse — that every $H^0$-disagreement between constituents produces a non-trivial $\delta$-class — does not hold without additional hypotheses; pairs $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ in the image of $H^n(\mathcal{F}^J) \to H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_1}) \oplus H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_2})$ have $\iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_1 - \iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_2$ in $\ker(\delta) = \mathrm{Im}(\text{diff})$, so produce no $H^1$ obstruction.*

## Sketch

The cited [replica-mayer-vietoris](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/replica-mayer-vietoris.md) theorem establishes a Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence for any subcanonical cover of a settling-associated sheaf in the bitopological setting. The jurisdictional cover satisfies the covering-family axiom by hypothesis (1), and the federation sheaf satisfies the sheaf condition by hypothesis (2); the cited theorem applies directly.

The interpretation of the three pieces:

- The **restriction map** $(\iota_{J_1}^*, \iota_{J_2}^*) : H^n(\mathcal{F}^J) \to H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_1}) \oplus H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_2})$ is the cohomological lift of the base-change pair. Its image is exactly the federation cohomology that is determined by constituent cohomology — content (a).
- The **difference map** $\iota_{J_{12}}^* - \iota_{J_{12}}^* : H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_1}) \oplus H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_2}) \to H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}})$ takes a pair $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ to $\iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_1 - \iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_2$ — measuring the disagreement between constituent classes on the overlap. The kernel — pairs that agree on the overlap — is the content that lifts to the federation, content (b).
- The **connecting homomorphism** $\delta : H^n(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}}) \to H^{n+1}(\mathcal{F}^J)$ is the standard Mayer–Vietoris connecting map. Its image in $H^{n+1}(\mathcal{F}^J)$ records federation classes that are not in the image of constituent restrictions — content (c).

For (d): by exactness, $\ker(\delta : H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}}) \to H^1(\mathcal{F}^J)) = \mathrm{Im}(\iota_{J_{12}}^* - \iota_{J_{12}}^*)$. Equivalently, $\delta$ is non-trivial exactly on classes in $H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}}) \setminus \mathrm{Im}(\text{diff})$. So $\delta$ produces a non-trivial $H^1(\mathcal{F}^J)$ class iff the difference map fails to surject onto $H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}})$ — iff there is overlap-content that no pair of constituent $H^0$-classes can collectively realize. *Direct constituent disagreement* — a pair $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ with $\iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_1 \neq \iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_2$ — produces a non-zero element of $\mathrm{Im}(\text{diff})$, which lies in $\ker(\delta)$ and hence yields *no* $H^1$ obstruction by itself. The federation-level $H^1$ obstruction comes from the failure of surjectivity, not from disagreement per se. $\square$

## Worked content of the three classes

### (a) Constituent-image content — *"phenomena inherited from below"*

These are the federation-cohomology classes that are pulled back from a single constituent. Examples:

- **A constituent's idiosyncratic norm-class.** A norm $n$ existing in $\mathcal{J}_1$'s closure rules but not in $\mathcal{J}_2$'s, contributing an $H^0$-class to $\mathcal{F}^{J_1}$, lifts (via the restriction map's section, when one exists) to an $H^0$-class on $\mathcal{F}^J$ that is in the image of $\iota_{J_1}^*$ and zero in $\iota_{J_2}^*$.
- **A constituent's adjudication-class.** Per [adjudication-modes-and-h1](../../../theorems/adjudication-modes-and-h1.md), each adjudication mode contributes an $H^1$-generator. A constituent's distinctive adjudication mode (e.g., $\mathcal{J}_1$ uses precedent, $\mathcal{J}_2$ uses statute) generates a constituent-level $H^1$-class that is in the image of the restriction map.

Operationally: federations preserve constituent idiosyncrasies as long as they don't conflict on the overlap. The substrate-algebraic content is that the restriction map's image catches exactly these "preserved-from-below" structures.

### (b) Overlap-kernel content — *"phenomena that lift consistently"*

These are pairs $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ from the constituents that agree on the overlap. Examples:

- **A jointly-recognized norm.** A norm explicitly adopted by both constituents and identical on the overlap — the constituent classes $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ have $\iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_1 = \iota_{J_{12}}^* \alpha_2$. The lift to the federation produces a single class $\alpha \in H^n(\mathcal{F}^J)$ with $\iota_{J_i}^* \alpha = \alpha_i$.
- **A treaty-recognized obligation.** Two constituent jurisdictions both recognize the same treaty obligation; the obligation's $H^1$-class agrees on the overlap and lifts.

Operationally: federations consolidate jointly-recognized content without needing federation-level adjudication. The kernel of the difference map is the substrate-algebraic content of "what the constituents already agreed on without the federation having to act."

### (c) Federation-only content — *"emergent structures"*

These are $H^{n+1}(\mathcal{F}^J)$ classes in the image of the connecting homomorphism $\delta$ — classes that do not pull back to any constituent. Examples:

- **Federal supremacy clauses.** A federation-level rule that *no constituent could state alone* — e.g., "in case of conflict between $\mathcal{J}_1$-law and $\mathcal{J}_2$-law on a shared matter, federal-law prevails." This rule is a federation-level $H^1$-class generated by *overlap content not in the image of the difference map* (per (d)) — specifically, an $H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}})$-class encoding "what counts as a conflict-resolution rule" that no constituent pair $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ can collectively present.
- **Inter-jurisdictional adjudication bodies.** A federal court whose jurisdiction is exactly the overlap $\mathcal{J}_{12}$ — its existence requires the federation to have lifted overlap-class adjudication into its own $H^1$.
- **Federation-only offices.** A presidency or council that has no analog at the constituent level. Substrate-algebraically, these are objects in $\mathcal{F}^J$ whose entity-cells are not in the image of $\iota_{J_i}^*$ for either constituent.

Operationally: federations *create* normative content that constituents cannot create alone. The connecting homomorphism's image is the substrate-algebraic content of *"federation as productive synthesis,"* not merely consolidation. This distinguishes federation from confederation (which has no federation-only content because no $\delta$-image).

### (d) Federation-only adjudication burden — *"the federation must adjudicate what its constituents cannot"*

Per the theorem's (d), the federation acquires a non-trivial $\delta$-image $H^1(\mathcal{F}^J)$ class exactly when the difference map fails to surject onto $H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}})$ — when there is overlap-content (a class in $H^0(\mathcal{F}^{J_{12}})$) that *no* pair $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ of constituent classes can collectively realize. Importantly: direct constituent disagreement — two constituents that disagree on a shared cell — by itself produces no $\delta$-obstruction; the disagreement-pair lives in $\mathrm{Im}(\text{diff}) = \ker(\delta)$. The federation-level burden arises specifically when the overlap admits *content unrepresentable as a constituent-pair*.

By [adjudication-modes-and-h1](../../../theorems/adjudication-modes-and-h1.md), each $H^1$-class corresponds to an adjudication-mode obligation. The federation's adjudication burden is therefore not the sum of constituent disagreements (which is consumed by $\mathrm{Im}(\text{diff})$) but the *cokernel* of the difference map — overlap-content the constituents cannot jointly express. Operationally: federations acquire adjudication load from *constitutional gaps* (overlap matters with no constituent representation), not from *constitutional conflicts* (overlap matters where constituents disagree). The cohomological-signature distinction is the substrate-algebraic content of why some federations face routine inter-constituent litigation (high disagreement, but $\delta = 0$) without acquiring federal-level normative burden, while others acquire significant federation-only adjudication load from a small overlap with no surjective constituent presentation.

The size of $\mathrm{coker}(\text{diff}) \cong \mathrm{Im}(\delta) \subseteq H^1(\mathcal{F}^J)$ is the **federation-only adjudication load** — content that requires federal-level resolution because no constituent can express it. Confederations with surjective difference maps (every overlap class is the difference of some constituent pair) carry no such load; tight federations with large $\mathrm{coker}(\text{diff})$ carry large loads.

## Three-or-more constituents

The Mayer–Vietoris sequence for $\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}_1 \cup \mathcal{J}_2$ generalizes to $n$ constituents via the Čech double complex of the cover, per [cech-derived-comparison-on-bitopological-site](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/cech-derived-comparison-on-bitopological-site.md). For a federation $\mathcal{J} = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \mathcal{J}_i$ the Čech-Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence

$$E_2^{p,q} = \check{H}^p(\{\mathcal{J}_i\}; \mathcal{H}^q(\mathcal{F})) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(\mathcal{F}^J)$$

computes the federation cohomology from the cover's Čech cohomology with values in the constituent-cohomology presheaf. The $n = 2$ case reduces to the long exact sequence above; the $n \geq 3$ case introduces higher-order intersection cohomology — operationally, *"three-way overlap content not jointly expressible by pairwise constituent presentations generates additional federation-level structure."*

## Compatibility with tradition pluralism

The companion theorem [tradition-pluralism-as-fragment-coproduct](tradition-pluralism-as-fragment-coproduct.md) treats a polity carrying multiple governance traditions as a coproduct of fragments. The Mayer–Vietoris reading is dual to this: where tradition pluralism handles *coexistence* of distinct closure-rule fragments, federation handles *covering* by overlapping jurisdictions. A polity with multiple traditions across multiple jurisdictions has both structures — coproduct over traditions, cover over jurisdictions — and its cohomology is computed by combining the fragment-coproduct's spectral-sequence contribution with the federation Mayer–Vietoris contribution. The full cohomological signature lives in the bicomplex.

## Status

`sketch` because:

- The cover-compatibility hypothesis (1) — that jurisdictional inclusions form a covering family in the bitopological site — is plausible but not verified case-by-case. Real federations whose constituent jurisdictions overlap *non-cleanly* (e.g., constituent $\mathcal{J}_1$'s closure rules fire on cells outside $\mathcal{C}_{J_1}$ via shared subroutines) may not satisfy it cleanly.
- The sheaf condition (2) is verified for d-frame-valued sheaves over the bitopological site by the cited theorem, but the federation's full predication settling is not always presented as such a sheaf without restriction.
- The (d) obstruction-theory reading uses the Mayer–Vietoris connecting homomorphism in a way that requires the difference map to be exact at the relevant degree; the proof above establishes the direction "non-surjectivity of the difference map ⟹ non-trivial $\delta$-image" by direct exactness, with the converse holding by the same exactness, but the operationally salient identification of "overlap-content not in $\mathrm{Im}(\text{diff})$" with concrete failure modes is itself a per-cover empirical question.
- The Čech-Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence for $n \geq 3$ constituents is invoked from the cited PCT theorem but not unfolded for the federation-specific case; the spectral-sequence differentials carry operational content that this sketch does not extract.

The directional content — that federation cohomology decomposes via Mayer–Vietoris into constituent-image, overlap-kernel, and federation-only content, with adjudication-load determined by the federation-only $H^1$ — is the substantive proposal of this sketch.

## Open downstream

- *Asymmetric federations.* Real federations often have constituent jurisdictions of different "weight" — a federal capital region with constitutional special status, an autonomous province, a treaty-protectorate. Whether the standard Mayer–Vietoris suffices or a *weighted* / *Verdier* generalization is needed deserves explicit treatment. Conjecture: the [verdier-duality-bitopological-site](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/verdier-duality-bitopological-site.md) machinery applies, with constituent weights determining the dualizing complex.
- *Secession as cover-change.* A constituent's secession from a federation is a cover-change: $\{\iota_{J_1}, \iota_{J_2}\} \mapsto \{\iota_{J_1}\}$. The cohomological displacement — what changes in $H^*(\mathcal{F}^J)$ when a constituent leaves — is a long-exact-sequence calculation. Whether this aligns with practitioner intuitions about "what is lost in secession" is open empirical-formal territory.
- *Federation-creation as cover-formation.* Inversely, the formation of a federation from previously-independent polities is the introduction of an overlap structure $\mathcal{C}_{J_{12}}$ where none existed. The federation-only content (c) generated by this is the substrate-algebraic content of "what new structures emerge from federation." A characterization of which new structures must emerge — perhaps a minimum $H^1(\mathcal{F}^J) / \mathrm{Im}$ — would be a federation-formation invariant.
