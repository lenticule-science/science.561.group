[Dialogic education](./dialogic-education.md) is [Paulo Freire](../../../../../../references/people/paulo-freire.md)'s alternative to the [banking model](./banking-model.md) [@freire_PedagogyOppressed_1970]. In dialogic (or problem-posing) education, teacher and student are co-investigators who examine the world together through dialogue. The teacher does not transmit knowledge; instead, teacher and student pose problems drawn from their shared situation and investigate them collaboratively.

Dialogue, in [Freire](../../../../../../references/people/paulo-freire.md)'s sense, is not casual conversation. It requires:

- **Humility**: no participant claims to possess the complete truth.
- **Faith in people**: trust that others are capable of critical thinking and action.
- **Love**: a commitment to the well-being and liberation of others ([Freire](../../../../../../references/people/paulo-freire.md) was explicit about this).
- **Hope**: the belief that the situation can change.
- **Critical thinking**: willingness to question assumptions, including one's own.

The purpose of dialogue is not consensus but [conscientization](./conscientization.md) — the development of critical awareness that enables transformative action.

[bell hooks](../../../../../../references/people/bell-hooks.md) extended this in *Teaching to Transgress* [@hooks_TeachingTransgress_1994], emphasizing that dialogue must attend to power differences within the learning space and that [engaged pedagogy](./engaged-pedagogy.md) requires the teacher's own vulnerability and willingness to be changed by the encounter. See [engaged pedagogy](./engaged-pedagogy.md) for hooks' distinct framework.
