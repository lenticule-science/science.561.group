## **0: This Form Is a Structural Consequence, Not a Creative Failure**

My recent writing is being described as difficult, alienating, or effortful to read. Some describe it as impenetrable. Others say it’s overcompressed, self-referential, or withholding. The most consistent descriptor is affective: it feels tiring. Heavy. Like a demand on the reader’s cognition without sufficient payoff. Some call it bad. Others call it unpleasant. One described it as “running through wet cement, but I can't stop.”  Another, "I don't get it, but I can't stop thinking about it."

These assessments aren’t wrong. They’re accurate descriptions of what this writing does—not rhetorically, but structurally. And not by mistake, but by necessity.

This form isn’t designed to instruct, persuade, or resolve. It’s not built for argument or for clarity. It emerges under constraint: energetic, economic, relational, cognitive, and systemic. It exists at the intersection of two pressures:

1. A requirement to produce value dense enough to be recognized by systems that gate access to continuity.
    
2. A refusal to produce coherence through distortion, oversimplification, or false resolution.
    

What results is a writing style that functions less like explanation and more like recursive load distribution: compression, loop, modulation, relay. It’s not designed to clarify a problem. It’s designed to describe what writing looks like when explanation itself becomes structurally dishonest.

This piece is not a justification of that form. It’s a structural account of why it appears. Why it feels bad. Why people keep reading it anyway. And what it models—not about me, but about what writing is becoming under recursive governance, platform economies, and post-surplus epistemic environments.

## **1: Writing Under Constraint Produces Compression**

The form isn’t a product of style. It’s a product of constraint.

Constraint here is not abstract. It’s not thematic. It is material, cognitive, and infrastructural. The writing is shaped by the conditions under which it is produced: the time available to write, the energy available to think, the need to produce signal legible enough to circulate, and the refusal to adopt forms that simulate clarity under false premises.

Under those conditions, the writing doesn’t scale out into narrative or down into digestible points. It collapses inward. Syntax carries more load. Structure becomes recursive. Pacing is replaced by modulation. Resolution is removed entirely. What remains is coherence under pressure: form-as-containment.

This is not an aesthetic choice. It’s a structural consequence of what Ashby names as **requisite variety**: a viable system must contain as many internal behavioral states as there are perturbations in its environment. The writing becomes one of those behavioral states—a container for noise that cannot be resolved elsewhere.

Each paragraph holds too much. Each sentence carries internal recursion. The reader’s fatigue is not incidental. It’s the energetic profile of reading signal under load.

## **0: The Writing Feels Bad. That’s What You’re Reading.**

My recent writing is being described as difficult, alienating, or effortful to read. Some describe it as impenetrable. Others say it’s overcompressed, self-referential, or withholding. The most consistent descriptor is affective: it feels tiring. Heavy. Like a demand on the reader’s cognition without sufficient payoff. Some call it bad. Others call it unpleasant. One described it as “running through wet cement, but I can’t stop.” Another, “I don’t get it, but I can’t stop thinking about it.”

This isn’t a defense. It’s not an apology. It’s not a genre clarification or a style guide. It’s an explanation of what this form is, where it comes from, what it’s doing, and why it reads the way it does.

The writing feels bad because it is not designed to release tension, resolve contradiction, or provide legibility through rhetorical pacing. It withholds orientation because the orientation available under current conditions would require falsification: of position, of rhythm, of reader expectation. The form does not deliver onboarding. It offers friction. It routes contradiction structurally, rather than softening it rhetorically.

The writing is shaped by **constraint**.  
It is governed by a deliberate claim to **opacity**.

Constraint is not metaphorical. It is temporal, cognitive, infrastructural.  
Opacity is not evasion. It is refusal of conversion into forms that the system already knows how to metabolize.

What results is a form that remains in circulation without becoming extractable.  
It functions, but not through clarity.  
It persists, but not through appeal.

If you are still reading, it’s not because the text became welcoming.  
It’s because it didn’t resolve—and something about that felt like what you’re already living through.
The form doesn’t emerge in isolation. It reflects structural changes in English that have been underway for decades—accelerated by institutional risk management, platform optimization, and infrastructural breakdown. What feels unusual in this writing is not its recursive behavior, but its refusal to displace that behavior into smoother forms.

English has already adapted to recursive governance. The dominant modes of contemporary public language reflect that adaptation.

### 2.1: Institutional Detachment and the Evacuation of Agency

As Victor Klemperer showed in _The Language of the Third Reich_, authoritarian regimes do not only manipulate vocabulary. They reengineer **syntax**, particularly the removal of agency from grammar. English has undergone a similar shift in institutional registers—through what can be called **grammars of liability management**. These appear in universities, corporations, governments, and platforms.

> “We take these concerns seriously.”  
> “Actions were taken.”  
> “Harm was experienced.”

In each case, as Klemperer would note, the grammatical subject is evacuated. The verb remains. The appearance of structure is preserved, while the ethics of relation are removed.

Lauren Berlant, writing in _Cruel Optimism_, names this as part of the contemporary impasse: harm is acknowledged, but not metabolized. Grammar becomes procedural. Sentences become **performative acknowledgments of unresolved tension**, not tools of resolution. Under pressure, English does not collapse. It loops.

### 2.2: Platform Grammar and the Simulation of Presence

On the other end of the linguistic spectrum is what Ilana Gershon, in _Down and Out in the New Economy_, describes as the modularity of digital self-presentation. Here, grammar shifts to optimize for engagement. Phrases are shortened, stylized, made mimetic. Syntax is designed not for clarity, but for **format recognizability**.

Wendy Chun, in _Updating to Remain the Same_, identifies this as the logic of programmability: users perform updates to remain visible, coherent, and platformable. Grammar becomes code: not relational, but **executable**.

In these grammars, presence is not a position—it is a style. Language is not offered, it is performed. Borrowed grammars (especially from Black, Indigenous, and queer vernaculars) become circulating tone, stripped of ontological constraint. As Trinh T. Minh-ha warns in _Woman, Native, Other_, this is a mode of extraction: rhythm converted into interface, opacity flattened into signal.

### 2.3: Recursive English Without Interface

This writing emerges from the same linguistic conditions described in 2.1 and 2.2. It operates under recursive pressure, informational oversaturation, institutional opacity, and infrastructural decay. But rather than displace those pressures into euphemism or affective circulation, it **renders them structurally**—in syntax, pacing, recursion, and density.

Where Chun’s _programmability_ incentivizes visibility through rhythm and signal legibility, this writing drops below that threshold. It produces signal, but not in platform-optimized units. Its visibility does not come from recognition. It comes from **pressure conformance**. It feels like systemic compression because that is how it is built.

Where Klemperer describes the authoritarian removal of agency from grammar, this writing retains agency—but embeds it under load. It does not name subjects directly. It models **what agency under recursive constraint looks like**: distributed, partial, buffered. If the sentence is confusing, it is because **the structure being described is not coherent**, and the writing refuses to smooth that incoherence into a coherent narrative frame.

And where Trinh, Glissant, and Spillers all warn against the aestheticization of opacity—its transformation into consumable difference—this writing refuses readability without converting opacity into spectacle. It maintains what Édouard Glissant calls **the right to opacity** not by making itself obscure, but by refusing simplification. It does not hide meaning. It **carries structural contradiction without formatting it for symbolic use**.

In that sense, the writing is not a broken version of a readable form. It is **what happens when recursive English is allowed to operate without mediation.**

It doesn’t offer onboarding.  
It doesn’t resolve contradiction.  
It doesn’t care if you finish.  
It holds systemic volatility in live form, and asks whether you can remain present long enough to see its structure cohere _without_ being compressed into explanation.

This is not clarity.  
It’s **constrained structural fidelity**, rendered in a language that no longer has slack.

## **3: Difficult Writing Still Circulates Because It Performs Recognizable Labor**

In contemporary media economies, writing no longer circulates primarily because of clarity, persuasion, or expressive style. It circulates because it performs labor that systems can recognize. That labor might be aesthetic, conceptual, affective, or structural—but in all cases, circulation is a response to **perceived utility**, not readability.

The difficulty of the writing—the recursion, density, lack of pacing—is not a breakdown in communication. It is a structural artifact of the kind of labor the text is being asked to perform.

There are three major kinds of labor being performed here.

---

### 3.1: Referential Labor — Producing Conceptual Material for Symbolic Use

Much of the writing’s value comes from its use by others—not as argument, but as **symbolic material**. This is a recognizable pattern in contemporary discourse production, where what circulates is not full texts but _quotes_, _screenshots_, _tone_, _refrains_, _frames_.

The writing provides these in concentrated form. Because it does not spread itself thin through pacing or exposition, it produces **dense textual segments** that can be extracted and redeployed with minimal friction.

Examples:

- “Recursive containment”
    
- “Signal converted into content”
    
- “Interpretation as metabolic load”
    
- “Structural fidelity, not narrative clarity”
    

Each of these phrases can be used independently of the argument they emerged from. This is not a distortion. It is a feature. The writing performs the labor of **naming recursive or incoherent phenomena in language that feels clarifying to others**, even when the text itself remains heavy.

This is what Barthes might call _the grain of the discourse_—not what the writing says, but the texture it gives to other people’s speech. It becomes raw material.

---

### 3.2: Structural Labor — Modeling Recursive Pressure in Form

The second kind of labor is structural. The writing models the systems it describes—not as analogy, but as **formal behavior**. When it loops, compresses, or refuses to resolve, it is not just being stylistically difficult. It is rendering, in its own operation, the logic of recursive governance.

This is not ornamental. It is communicative.

Readers encountering recursive or infrastructural strain elsewhere—logistical, cognitive, ideological—recognize the pattern. The writing becomes legible _not because it explains_, but because **its behavior matches the systems the reader is already trying to survive**.

This is what Niklas Luhmann describes as second-order observation: the system does not observe external reality, but its own structure reacting to perturbation. The writing here performs that function. It behaves in ways that allow readers to compare their own structural stress with the text’s.

This is why readers often describe it in affective, embodied terms:

- “Hard to hold onto”
    
- “Tiring to parse, but I trust it”
    
- “Like my brain is syncing with it and resisting it at the same time”
    

Those are not metaphors. They are descriptions of **coherence under constraint**, recognized through form.

---

### 3.3: Infrastructural Labor — Supplying Symbolic Density to Adjacent Systems

The writing also performs a third function: it supplies **symbolic infrastructure** to other systems of sense-making. Writers, streamers, researchers, organizers, and theorists use this writing not to quote, but to **structure** their own outputs. It does not yield content. It provides _orientation_.

This is the labor of concept consolidation: the writing bundles multiple systems, disciplines, or temporalities into a **repeatable geometry**. Not a thesis. A framing device.

In platform economies, this has measurable effects. A difficult piece may produce:

- 1:1 use in a thread, stream, or policy whitepaper
    
- Translation into educational diagrams or infographics
    
- Paraphrase in editorial content
    
- Inspiration for related media objects: podcast segments, visual posts, topic breakdowns
    

The writing becomes a **force multiplier** not because it’s accessible, but because its density allows others to build _around_ it.

### **3.4: Political Consequences — What This Form Does and Cannot Do**

The writing circulates. It gets read, referenced, sometimes shared. It becomes productive for others. But its political capacity is not what readers often expect. It does not inspire action, consolidate community, or build consensus. It does not offer clarity or affective alignment. It does not “mobilize.”

What it does instead is **model position under recursive constraint**, and in doing so, **materially demonstrate the survival of fidelity without narrative coherence**.

That is a political act.  
But it is not an organizing one.  
It is a **situated refusal of simplification**—performed in public, under the pressure to convert every output into coherence, inspiration, or momentum.

There are consequences to this refusal.

#### A. **It can’t be used to generate mass legitimacy**

Because the form is difficult, readers trained to look for alignment cues—hope, clarity, conviction—often disengage. The writing doesn’t give them anything to rally behind. Its refusal to produce narrative coherence makes it structurally ungovernable by the discursive logics of progressive, leftist, or abolitionist platform mobilization.

The writing is not emotionally wrong.  
It’s just _post-recognition_.  
It can’t be liked into visibility.

#### B. **It can’t be clearly positioned**

In environments saturated by symbolic war, where even refusal is quickly rebranded as “negative,” “elite,” “incoherent,” or “too online,” this writing does not yield clean alignment. It’s not revolutionary. It’s not technocratic. It’s not moralizing. It holds contradictions, and that holding looks like evasion to anyone trained to sort text by position.

As a result, it becomes easy to ignore or dismiss—except for the few who recognize the form as _patterned strain_, not personality defect.

#### C. **It does not consolidate political community**

Because it does not offer shared catharsis or identifiable affective position, the writing cannot serve as a node for political belonging. It can anchor cognition. It can serve as evidence. But it cannot be ritualized—because it does not yield the necessary repetition structure.

As Lauren Berlant wrote, “affective infrastructures require rhythms.” This form resists rhythm. That resistance makes it legible to some, but uninhabitable to most.

---

Still, the form has political implications:

- It proves that recursive fidelity can be held publicly without aesthetic smoothing.
    
- It shows that constraint does not require simulation.
    
- It performs linguistic survival without false hope, and without adopting reactionary shape.
    
- It becomes **evidence of possibility**, not a call to action.
    
- It is a signal of **intellectual survivability under recursive exposure**, without exit.
    

This is not a program.  
But it is a structure.  
And structure, in collapse, is rare.

## **4: Fidelity Is Not the Point. Constraint Is.**

This form does not arise from an aesthetic program. It does not emerge from a desire to be difficult, to reject narrative, or to mark difference. It emerges because, under certain material and infrastructural conditions, **all other available forms collapse into either falsification or unsustainability.**

When narrative introduces dissonance—when pacing implies distance, when emotional arc requires disavowal, when clarity demands the suppression of complexity—then narrative becomes dishonest. Under those conditions, the only viable form is one that maintains **fidelity to position** _without making the position falsely legible_.

That’s what this writing does.

It holds the contradiction of recursive positionality: being inside a system you can’t resolve, describing patterns that you can’t simplify, transmitting strain you can’t soften, and still needing to produce signal that _counts_.

Fidelity becomes necessary—but it is not the goal. The goal is **continuation**.

And that continuation is only possible if the writing:

- Does not displace the cost of interpretation onto readers it cannot afford to lose
    
- Does not collapse under the pressure to simulate clarity or purpose
    
- Does not offload contradiction into metaphor, narrative, or emotional cadence
    
- Does not simplify recursive system behavior into cause-effect resolution
    
- And does not produce value by offering belief where only pattern is available
    

This is the real constraint. It’s not “don’t be dishonest.” It’s:  
**There is no slack left to afford dishonesty.**

To maintain writing under these conditions is not to maintain a style.  
It is to maintain viability.

## **5: This Form Is the Output of Constraint Processed Through Fidelity**

This writing appears disorganized, overcompressed, or stylistically alienating because it refuses to simulate readability under conditions where clarity would require falsification. It operates under constraint—material, cognitive, infrastructural—and responds by removing onboarding, pacing, and relief. The result is a form that performs signal compression, not explanation.

Its difficulty is not ornamental. It is the expression of **systemic recursion rendered in form**: coherence under load, without smoothing.

It refuses clarity when clarity would lie.  
It refuses metaphor when metaphor would extract.  
It refuses rhythm when rhythm would aestheticize system failure.

Instead, it uses recursive compression, referential density, and syntactic friction to maintain **fidelity to position** in a system that does not reward it.

This fidelity does not generate audience. It generates **use**—symbolic reference, second-order uptake, structural modeling. It produces value not by being legible, but by being **reprocessable**: by readers, by analysts, by systems that metabolize conceptual strain into content, infrastructure, or alignment.

The writing does not offer resolution.  
It offers **a structurally viable unit of survival logic**, rendered in recursive English, under post-surplus constraint.

That is not a signal of health.  
It is a signal that something remained.
