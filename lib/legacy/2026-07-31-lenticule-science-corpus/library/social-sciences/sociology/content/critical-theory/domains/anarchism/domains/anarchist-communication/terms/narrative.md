Narrative is the organization of experience into story. Something happens, then something else happens, and the narrative connects them: this happened *because* of that, this led to that, this means that. Without narrative, experience is a sequence of events. With narrative, it becomes meaningful — but the meaning is produced by the narrative structure, not discovered in the events themselves.

This is the basic insight: narrative does not describe reality. It organizes reality into a form that makes sense. And "makes sense" is not a neutral operation. Every narrative includes some events and excludes others, foregrounds some actors and backgrounds others, implies some causes and obscures others. The question "what happened?" is always answered from a position — and the position shapes the answer.

## Narrative and power

Who gets to narrate? Whose story becomes the official account? These are political questions. The state narrates through law, official history, policy documents, and press conferences. [Capitalism](../schools/anarchism/terms/capitalism.md) narrates through advertising, brand stories, and the entrepreneurial myth. [Colonialism](../schools/anarchism/terms/colonialism.md) narrates by replacing the colonized people's account of themselves with the colonizer's account of them.

[Domination](../schools/anarchism/terms/domination.md) operates narratively in at least two ways:

**Controlling the story**: Determining what counts as the official account — what happened, why, and what it means. History is written by the victors not because the victors are more truthful but because they control the institutions (schools, archives, [media](./media.md)) that produce and distribute narrative.

**Controlling the form**: Determining what kinds of stories are available. [Genre](../../writing/terms/genre.md) is narrative form made conventional — the redemption arc, the progress narrative, the trauma-and-healing story, the hero's journey. When these genres become the only available forms for organizing experience, experiences that don't fit the form become unspeakable. Structural [violence](../schools/anarchism/terms/violence.md) that has no villain, no climax, and no resolution does not fit the narrative forms available, so it becomes invisible — not because it is hidden but because it cannot be narrated.

## Narrative and californication

[Californication](../schools/cybernetic-postliberalism/terms/californication.md) operates through narrative by formatting structural crisis as personal story. Systemic collapse becomes individual trauma. Economic dispossession becomes a "journey." Structural impossibility becomes a failure of personal resilience. The narrative form — crisis, struggle, growth, redemption — absorbs the structural content and converts it into individually manageable meaning. You are not oppressed; you are "going through something." The system is not collapsing; you are "finding yourself."

[Genre calibration](../schools/cybernetic-postliberalism/terms/genre-calibration.md) names this process: the tuning of expectations for how a life should go, so that crisis is experienced as personal narrative rather than recognized as structure. The narrative form does the political work of preventing structural recognition.

## Narrative and anarchism

[Anarchist](../schools/anarchism/index.md) practice has a complex relationship with narrative. On one hand, narratives of resistance — stories of uprisings, of communities organizing, of people refusing [domination](../schools/anarchism/terms/domination.md) — are essential to sustaining movements and transmitting knowledge across generations. On the other hand, the narrative form itself can become a mechanism of [recuperation](../schools/anarchism/terms/recuperation.md): the radical story becomes a media product, the uprising becomes a documentary, the movement becomes a brand narrative.

[Propaganda of the deed](../schools/anarchism/terms/propaganda-of-the-deed.md) represents one anarchist response: action that communicates directly, without requiring narrative mediation. The action is its own meaning. [Insurrectionary anarchism](../schools/anarchism/terms/insurrectionary-anarchism.md)'s emphasis on immediacy — acting in the present rather than narrating toward the future — is partly a refusal of narrative's tendency to subordinate the present to a story about what comes next.
