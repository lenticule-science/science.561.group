# Power

*The role concept for the capacity to bring about effects — to produce action, to shape conduct, to determine the conditions under which others act, and to set the terms of what counts as legitimate. Power is what sociology analyzes whenever it asks how a social arrangement is held in place against the interests, will, or possibility of those it constrains.*

The discipline does not have a single account of power. Weber's classical definition — the probability that an actor will be able to carry out his will despite resistance — treats power as a possession of actors, exercised in conflict. Marx and the Marxist tradition treat power as structural: it is the position in the relations of production, not the personal will of any owner, that gives capital its grip. Lukes's three faces of power (1974) gathers a generation of dispute: power is exercised in decisions, in setting which decisions are made (agenda-setting), and in shaping what the affected come to want.

Foucault's analytic does the most to displace the possessive picture. Power is not held but exercised; it circulates through capillaries rather than descending from a peak; it produces subjects rather than only repressing them. Where Marxism asked *whose interests* power serves, Foucault asks *how* power works — through which institutions, knowledges, and techniques. The displacement is not innocent: critics across the Marxist and Black-radical traditions have argued that Foucault's analysis of power-without-a-subject can obscure who, in fact, benefits.

The Black-radical tradition (Du Bois, Robinson, Davis, Gilmore) has insisted that any account of power in modernity must reckon with the racial state, racial capitalism, and the prison; abstracting from these is itself an exercise of power. Decolonial sociology insists analogously about the colonial structure of the modern world-system. Feminist sociology has insisted that the household and reproductive labor are sites of power, not pre-political nature.

[Capital](capital.md), [agency](agency.md), and [structure](structure.md) all bear on power; none of them is a substitute for it. Power names the operative question — who can make whom do what, on what terms, against what resistance — that those concepts inform.
