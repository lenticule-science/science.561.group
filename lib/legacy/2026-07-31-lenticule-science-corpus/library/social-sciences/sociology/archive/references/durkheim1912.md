Durkheim's last major work and the third pole — alongside *The Division of Labor* (Durkheim 1893) and *The Rules of Sociological Method* (Durkheim 1895) — of the canonical Durkheim corpus. A study of Australian Aboriginal totemism that the book treats as a clear case from which to read the general structure of religion, arguing that religion is fundamentally a way societies represent themselves to themselves, that the sacred/profane distinction is a society-constituting categorical operation, and that ritual is the periodic re-instantiation of the *conscience collective*. The argument grounds the entire subsequent literature on collective effervescence, ritual, and civil religion.
