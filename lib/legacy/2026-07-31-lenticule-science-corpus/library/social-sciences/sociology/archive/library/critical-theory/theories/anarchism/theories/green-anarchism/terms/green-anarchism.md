Green [anarchism](../schools/anarchism/index.md) is the convergence of [anarchist](../../../terms/anarchism.md) politics and ecological analysis. It holds that the domination of the natural world and the domination of human beings are not separate problems but aspects of the same structure — and that addressing one without addressing the other leaves the root system intact.

The tradition draws on multiple sources: [Kropotkin's](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/pyotr-kropotkin.md) integration of ecology and [anarchism](../schools/anarchism/index.md), Murray Bookchin's social ecology (which argues that ecological destruction follows from social hierarchy), the Earth Liberation Front's direct-action tradition, and Indigenous land defense practices that predate the European [anarchist](../../../terms/anarchism.md) tradition entirely. The relationship between [anarchism](../schools/anarchism/index.md) and ecology is not additive ([anarchism](../schools/anarchism/index.md) + environmentalism) but structural: both identify domination as the problem and its [abolition](../../../../abolition/terms/abolition.md) as the response.

Green [anarchism](../schools/anarchism/index.md) connects to [total liberation](/general/terms/total-liberation.md) — the position that human, animal, and ecological liberation are facets of the same struggle. It connects to [climate justice](/ecology/disciplines/climate/terms/climate-justice.md) through the recognition that the climate crisis is produced by the same structures of [extraction](/ecology/disciplines/climate/terms/extractivism.md) and [colonialism](../../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/settler-colonialism.md) that [anarchism](../../../terms/anarchism.md) opposes. And it connects to [Indigenous resurgence](../../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/indigenous-resurgence.md) through the recognition that Indigenous peoples have practiced sustainable relationships with the land for millennia — not as a romanticized ideal but as actually existing governance systems that [colonialism](../../../terms/colonialism.md) attempted to destroy.
