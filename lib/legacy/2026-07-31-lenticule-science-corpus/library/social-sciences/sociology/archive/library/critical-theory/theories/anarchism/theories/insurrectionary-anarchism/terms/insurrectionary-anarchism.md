Insurrectionary [anarchism](../schools/anarchism/index.md) is the tendency within [anarchism](../../../terms/anarchism.md) that emphasizes informal organization, the immediacy of action, and the [refusal](../../../terms/refusal.md) of programmatic politics — pre-written platforms, party structures, mass organizations, and the deferral of freedom to a post-revolutionary future. Its primary theorist in the late twentieth century was [Alfredo Bonanno](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/alfredo-bonanno.md).

The insurrectionary position holds that revolutionary organization should be informal and temporary: small [affinity groups](../../../terms/affinity-group.md) that form around specific actions, not permanent organizations that accumulate bureaucracy and reproduce internal hierarchies. Formal organizations — even [anarchist](../schools/anarchism/index.md) ones — tend to develop self-preservation instincts that compete with their stated aims. The federation, the union, the party: each becomes a thing to be maintained, and maintenance displaces insurgency.

Insurrectionary [anarchism](../schools/anarchism/index.md) insists on the unity of theory and practice: action is not the implementation of a pre-existing analysis but the site where analysis is produced. The [armed joy](../../../texts/the-armed-joy.md) [Bonanno](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/alfredo-bonanno.md) describes is not recklessness but the [refusal](../../../terms/refusal.md) to separate the experience of freedom from the struggle for it. This connects to [anarcho-nihilism](../../../terms/anarcho-nihilism.md) in its [refusal](../../../terms/refusal.md) to require hope or a program as preconditions for action, though not all insurrectionary anarchists adopt nihilist positions.
