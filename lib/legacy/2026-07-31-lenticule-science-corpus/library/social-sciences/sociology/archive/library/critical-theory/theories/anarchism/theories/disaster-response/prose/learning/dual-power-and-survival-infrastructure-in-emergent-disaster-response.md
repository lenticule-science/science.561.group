One explicit current within emergent disaster response treats disaster
relief as part of building alternative infrastructure. In this current,
food distribution, clinics, wellness spaces, popular education, and
energy systems are not only temporary emergency measures. They are part
of a [dual power](../../../terms/dual-power.md) strategy of
building capacities people can continue to use beyond the immediate
crisis [@madrabout2025; @madrprograms2024].

## Survival infrastructure

Mutual Aid Disaster Relief's language of [survival programs](../terms/survival-programs.md)
makes this current explicit. Immediate aid is linked to long-term power,
consciousness, and community capacity rather than being treated as
neutral service [@madrprograms2024]. This is one of the clearest ways
that disaster response becomes a site of institution building.

## Relief tied to liberation struggles

MADR's core values connect disaster work to the interconnectedness of
liberation struggles and the need to dismantle harmful systems while
creating what people need to live [@madrcorevalues2019]. This gives the
current a clearer political content than generic resilience language.
Infrastructure is not built simply to bounce back. It is built to reduce
dependence on oppressive institutions.

## Beyond the event

The `Solar Power to the People` account shows how this current moves
from immediate response toward durable autonomous infrastructure
[@solarpower2018]. Energy systems, water systems, and shared facilities
matter because they extend disaster response into ongoing material
self-governance.

## Significance

This current matters because it makes the political horizon of emergent
disaster response more explicit. It asks not only how people survive the
crisis, but what infrastructures of survival can become the basis for a
less dependent and more collectively self-determined life afterward.
