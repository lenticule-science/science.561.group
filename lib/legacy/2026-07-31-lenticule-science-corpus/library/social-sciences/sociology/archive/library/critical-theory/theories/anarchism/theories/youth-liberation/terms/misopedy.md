Misopedy is the hatred or denigration of children and childhood. The term derives from Greek *miso-* (hatred) and *paidos* (child). Unlike individual acts of cruelty toward children, misopedy names a structural condition: the systematic devaluation of childhood as a category, the treatment of children as pre-persons or incomplete adults, and the exclusion of young people from political agency and self-determination.

Toby Rollo, a political theorist at Lakehead University, has developed the concept's most sustained theoretical treatment. In "Feral Children: Settler Colonialism, Progress, and the Figure of the Child" (*Settler Colonial Studies*, 2018), Rollo argues that the relationship between misopedy and European colonialism is not an analogy but a structuring principle. The conceptual apparatus used to justify colonial domination — the savage as child, the colonized as requiring tutelage and civilization — is itself rooted in the prior subordination of actual children. Modern colonial thinkers inherited the conceptual legacy of misopedy: the figure of the child as sinful, bestial, or irrational provided the template through which Indigenous peoples were categorized as unfit for self-governance.

This argument has implications for how the vault understands [settler colonialism](../../../../../sociology/topics/settler-colonialism/index.md). If the colonial relationship reproduces the logic of [adult supremacy](./adult-supremacy.md) — the assumption that the mature, rational, civilized subject is entitled to govern those deemed immature — then critiques of colonialism that do not also interrogate the subordination of children leave the deepest layer of the justificatory structure intact.

Misopedy is related to but distinct from [adultism](./adultism.md) (the systemic discrimination against young people), [childism](./childism.md) (prejudice against children as a group), and [adult supremacy](./adult-supremacy.md) (the political arrangement in which adults hold power over children). Where adultism names the discrimination and adult supremacy names the power structure, misopedy names the antipathy — the ideological orientation that treats childhood itself as a condition to be overcome, corrected, or governed rather than respected.

The term also appears in educational theory. Practices that deny or subordinate the agency of children — including those carried out with benevolent intent under the banner of "the best interest of the child" — can be understood as expressions of misopedy when they proceed from the assumption that children are incapable of contributing to decisions about their own lives.
