bell hooks (Gloria Jean Watkins, 1952–2021) wrote the books of contemporary U.S. Black feminism that reach the broadest readership, in the prose style she insisted feminism had to develop if it was to be more than an academic tradition. The lowercased name (her great-grandmother's, taken to put the focus on the work rather than on the writer) is the politics in miniature.

The full biographical note lives at [`library/references/people/bell-hooks`](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/bell-hooks.md). This page records what the [black-feminism](../_index.md) subdomain in particular takes from her.

## What this subdomain takes from her

- **The critique of the white-feminist subject.** *Ain't I a Woman: Black Women and Feminism* (1981, drafted while hooks was an undergraduate) is the long-form demonstration that the U.S. women's movement of the 1960s and 1970s was structured around the figure of the white middle-class woman and could not, from that starting point, describe Black women's experience or analyze the structure that produced it. The book recovers the long history of Black women's writing, organizing, and theory — Sojourner Truth, Frances Harper, Anna Julia Cooper, Ida B. Wells, Claudia Jones — that the white-feminist canon had erased. The title comes from Truth's 1851 speech: the demand to be counted as a woman without ceasing to be Black is the analytic the book extends.
- **From margin to center.** *Feminist Theory: From Margin to Center* (1984) is the methodological reconstruction. Standpoint matters: theory built from the margin (the position of those whom the dominant categories make invisible) sees what theory built from the center cannot. The book is one of the foundational texts of standpoint feminism in the U.S. tradition.
- **Imperialist white-supremacist capitalist patriarchy.** The phrase recurs across hooks's later work as the analytical name for the integrated system. The naming is the argument: the system has to be named integrated for the political project to be adequate to it. Naming each modifier separately does the analytical work of refusing the single-axis framings (white feminism, Black nationalism, class-only Marxism) that the tradition has been arguing against since Combahee.
- **Engaged pedagogy.** *Teaching to Transgress* (1994) develops, with Paulo Freire's critical pedagogy as one of its sources and Black feminist analysis as the other, an account of teaching as a practice of freedom that requires the teacher's own self-actualization. The pedagogical analysis is continuous with hooks's larger theoretical project: Black feminism is not only the analysis of how the structure works but the cultivation of the practices that would constitute living otherwise.
- **Love as political.** *All About Love: New Visions* (2000) and the love trilogy that follows. Love, in hooks's framing, is not sentiment but ethical commitment — the potential will to extend oneself toward another's well-being, in M. Scott Peck's formulation that hooks adopts and develops. The political analysis is that the dominant culture systematically obstructs the conditions under which love in this sense is possible, and that building those conditions is a political project.

- *Ain't I a Woman: Black Women and Feminism* (1981).
- *Feminist Theory: From Margin to Center* (1984).
- *Talking Back: Thinking Feminist, Thinking Black* (1989).

- *We Real Cool: Black Men and Masculinity* (2004).
