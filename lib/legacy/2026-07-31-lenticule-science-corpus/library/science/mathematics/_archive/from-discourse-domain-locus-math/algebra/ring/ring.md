A ring $(R, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$ is a set equipped with two binary operations — addition and multiplication — such that $(R, +, 0)$ is an [abelian group](./abelian-group.md), $(R, \cdot, 1)$ is a [monoid](./monoid.md), and multiplication distributes over addition on both sides.

The integers $\mathbb{Z}$, [polynomial](./polynomial.md) rings, and matrix rings are standard examples. A ring is commutative if $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$ for all elements. The study of commutative rings and their [ideals](./ideal.md) forms the foundation of algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory.

Rings connect to lattice theory through the notion of [residuation](../../teaching/explanations/residuated-lattice/index.md): the [Heyting implication](../../logic/morphisms/heyting-implication.md) in a [Heyting algebra](../../teaching/explanations/heyting-algebra/index.md) satisfies an adjunction analogous to the division-like operation in a ring.
