Traditional Chinese wind instruments can be classified using both the Hornbostel-Sachs system and traditional Chinese organological principles.

## Hornbostel-Sachs Classification

In the Hornbostel-Sachs system, Chinese aerophones fall primarily under:
- **421.2** - Edge-blown aerophones (flutes): dizi, xiao
- **422.2** - Reed aerophones with single reed: guanzi, suona
- **422.3** - Reed aerophones with double reed: sheng (though sheng is more complex)
- **423** - Free aerophones: bawu, hulusi

## Traditional Chinese Classification

Traditional Chinese classification often considers:
- **Material**: bamboo (dizi, xiao), wood (guanzi), metal (some suona), gourd (hulusi)
- **Playing position**: transverse (dizi), vertical (xiao, sheng), oblique (some xiao)
- **Sound production mechanism**: edge tone (dizi, xiao), single reed (guanzi, suona), free reed (bawu, hulusi), multiple reeds (sheng)
- **Function**: melodic (dizi, xiao), harmonic/sheng (sheng), signaling/festival (suona), expressive (bawu, hulusi)

## Key Distinctions

1. **Flutes vs. Reed Instruments**: Edge-blown flutes (dizi, xiao) vs. reed instruments (guanzi, suona, sheng, bawu, hulusi)
2. **Number of Pipes**: Single pipe (most) vs. multiple pipes (sheng)
3. **Reed Type**: No reed (flutes), single reed (guanzi, suona), free reed (bawu, hulusi), beating reed (sheng)

## Relationship to Predication Theory

Each instrument type can be seen as realizing different predication patterns:
- Flutes: Simple pitch predication with continuous control
- Reed instruments: Discrete pitch predication with timbre modulation
- Sheng: Complex polyphonic predication with chordal capabilities
- Free reed instruments: Expressive predication with vocal-like qualities
