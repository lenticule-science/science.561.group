Building from [[202507120933a1|thinking about]] what [[my ideal software]] would be like, another [[idea]] I had, that I didn't get [[writing|write]] down anywhere, is that, as far as I know, most if not all [[game engine|game engines]] are strictly [[deterministic]], where the [[game system]] allows for one potential outcome of any situation.

This seems, to me, to not respect that [[digital computer|digital computers]] have gotten really good at [[inference]], in a way that might let [[tension]] be a more fundamental aspect of a game engine.

But, I'm also [[struggling]] to [[imagining|imagine]] a situation where this would [[emerging|emerge]].
I can picture two [[mobile thing|mobs]] both attempting to open a [[door]] from opposite sides, but which one would succeed at opening it is [[determining|determined]] by who's [[strength|stronger]], or less [[etiquette|polite]], or whatever.

Where would [[Bayesian inference]] come up? I think part of the [[problem]] is that it would only come up in [[situation|situations]] whose [[complexity]] precludes an easy imaginary.

Perhaps where other systems might use [[random number generation|RNG]], this software would try and infer the most likely outcome - and any "randomness" is an [[apparent quality]] induced by [[mysterious complexity]]: complexity that is [[illegible]] to the [[user]].

Saying all that, I feel like I have, in the past, written something, or [[babbling|babbled]] at someone, about how I felt RNG was a way to cope with video games not being able to be complex enough, given the computers of the day, and how we should really try and do better.

Which is affirming to me that I really do just need to keep chugging at putting ideas to words.
