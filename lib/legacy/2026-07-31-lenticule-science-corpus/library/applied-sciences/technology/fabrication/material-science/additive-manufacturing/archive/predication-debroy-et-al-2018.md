# DebRoy et al. (2018) — *Additive manufacturing of metallic components – Process, structure and properties*

Definitive review of the **process–structure–property (PSP) chain in metal AM**, organizing the field around heat- and mass-transfer physics rather than machine-by-machine empiricism. Over 4,500 citations.

**Central thesis.** Metal AM mechanical properties are determined by the local thermal history (cooling rate, peak temperature, reheats), which is determined by the deposition strategy (laser power, scan speed, hatch spacing, layer thickness) through transport physics that can be modeled and predicted. This frames AM as a controllable manufacturing process rather than a craft.

**Key contributions.**

- Closed-form scaling laws for melt-pool dimensions (Rosenthal solution and corrections).
- Maps of cooling rate → microstructure (cellular vs columnar vs equiaxed) for common alloys (316L, IN718, Ti-6Al-4V, AlSi10Mg).
- Catalog of defect types (lack-of-fusion, keyhole porosity, cracking) and their thermal-history signatures.
- Framework for residual stress prediction.

**Why it matters here.** Sets the model for how a "mature" AM science should look: physics-based, predictive, and tied to controllable process parameters. The polymer-AM (FFF) literature is a decade behind on this PSP framing; Brenken et al. (2018) and Phan et al. (2020) are the FFF analogs in progress.
