# Seifi et al. (2017) — *Progress towards metal additive manufacturing standardization to support qualification and certification*

Status review and roadmap for AM standards (ASTM F42, ISO TC261), focused on the certification problem in regulated industries (aerospace, medical implants).

**The certification problem.** Conventional manufacturing certifies the *process* once, then certifies parts statistically by sampling. AM, because of part-to-part and machine-to-machine variation, has resisted this model. Three approaches in tension:

1. **Equivalence.** Show AM part has same allowables as the conventional reference part (current FAA/EASA approach for metal aerospace parts; very expensive, limits design freedom).
2. **Qualified part / qualified process.** Lock down everything (machine, powder lot, parameters, post-processing) and certify the resulting part envelope (current medical-implant approach).
3. **Pedigree-based.** Use in-situ monitoring + ML to build a per-part pedigree certifying that *this individual part* meets spec (the frontier, not yet accepted by any regulator as of 2026).

**Why it matters.** The standards story is the choke-point preventing AM from displacing conventional manufacturing in safety-critical applications. Without a qualification framework, AM remains a prototyping and low-volume technology in regulated sectors.
