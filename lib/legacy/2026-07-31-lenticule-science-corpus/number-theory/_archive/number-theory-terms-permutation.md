A permutation of a set is an arrangement of its elements in a definite order. The number of permutations of $n$ distinct objects is $n!$ (n factorial).

Permutations can also be viewed as bijective [functions](../../../concepts/function/index.md) from a set to itself. Under [composition](../../../objects/categories/ordinary-categories/terms/composition.md), the permutations of a set form a [group](../../../objects/groups/terms/group.md) — the **symmetric group** $S_n$. This is one of the most important examples of a non-[abelian](../../../objects/groups/terms/abelian-group.md) group (for $n \geq 3$).

A $k$-permutation of $n$ objects is an ordered selection of $k$ objects from $n$, numbering $\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}$.
