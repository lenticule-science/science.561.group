> While bullets shower the earth, we turn our heads and cover our faces. Now flames devour the sky, and I hear these words in the back of my mind: "Here we stand at this fork in the road; which way shall we go? This Old World's spinning out of control; which way shall we go; which way shall we go?" I can't believe this: *it makes me sick*. - [[Cage the Elephant]], "Drones in the Valley"

After the sun went below the horizon on [[2024-02-09|February 9th, 2024]], but before it rose again elsewhere on the [[2024-02-10|10th]], the world as we know it ended.

At least, that's the decision we, my cadre, my milieu, my family, made, as the world regained its color in the morning light.

The world ended, for us, not with a bang, or a whimper.

The world ended with a *croak*. A *ribbit*. And it made me sick.

Quite literally: once I figured out what I was hearing, I retched, then came inside, and told my family, and we cried.

Some readers will be empathizing by now; those who don't live in the same region, might need some explanation.

Me and my family live (for now - more on that later) - at the headwaters of what we call *Wakpala Hmi* - "[[Crooked Creek, Cape Fear River Basin|Crooked Creek]]", the northernest tributary of the [[Cape Fear River]], within the settler-city of [[Durham, North Carolina|Durham]], in the settler-state of [[North Carolina]], within the [[United States]].

The weather here is what most [[liberal people|Western folk]] would say is "normal": it's [[hot weather]] in the [[summer]], it's [[cold weather|cold]] in the [[winter]]. Oh, excuse me: It *was* hot in the summer, it *was* cold in the winter. This year, we've been part of the plurality of regions experiencing significantly anomalous warm conditions: it was hot in the summer, it was hot in the fall, it was cold for the first couple weeks of winter, and then, things [[climate breakdown|fell apart]].

I don't remember which calendar [[day]] it happened - it was when the [[polar vortex]] got pushed toward [[Siberia]], and a rush of [[rain]] and warmth flowed up by way of Atlanta. The rain warmed the [[soil]], and this inhibited the [[dormant|dormancy]] period of a lot of living things: [[millipede|millipedes]], [[beetle|beetles]], [[squirrel|squirrels]], [[bird|birds]], [[tree|trees]], [[bush|bushes]]. There are more [[consequence|consequences]]: the natural cycle of [[winter decomposition]] has been broken, leading to increasingly [[anaerobic soil]] conditions, killing trees, and the habitats they provide...

> Fresh smell of death on your tongue: you bait the hook and here come the children. Another gravestone to sell, while you get rich in the valley of hell.

These conditions were the backdrop for city council bickering, school shutdowns, [[Asteria]]...

And then on Feb 10th, the soil was warm enough the frogs woke up. [[Year of the Dragon]] began.

> The devil's creeping, he's got us eating from his dirty hands. We feast on lies, which makes it hard for us to take a stand. Out in the desert there's a forest full of fallen trees. It's what they want, but it's not what we need.

People cannot see what is happening around them because of alienation, isolation, hyperreality.

> Screaming at the ceiling doesn't make it better: slipped into a coma, lost my favorite sweater. Ask them normal questions, you'll never catch them lying. It's time to make our move, and strike them right between the eyes.

Me and my family are moving to [[Pine Ridge Indian Reservation]].
