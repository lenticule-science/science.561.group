# Paraconsistent variants

The bilattice value $\top$ — *told both* — is a legitimate epistemic state of the settling, not a degeneracy: a predication cell is $\top$ when sources have asserted both positive and negative evidence. The Derivation cohomology treats $\top$-states symmetrically with $\mathbf{t}$-states, but a finer development *singles out* the $\top$-content for its own track.

This chapter develops that paraconsistent track:

- The [paraconsistent settling-associated sheaf](../objects/paraconsistent-settling-associated-sheaf.md) $\mathcal{F}_U^{(k)}$, the Belnap-designated parallel of $\mathcal{F}_U$ that tracks the full $\{\mathbf{t}, \top\}$ designated set instead of just $\{\mathbf{t}\}$.

- The [inconsistency sheaf](../objects/inconsistency-sheaf.md) $\mathcal{F}_U^{(\top)}$ — the $\top$-only cokernel of $\mathcal{F}_U \hookrightarrow \mathcal{F}_U^{(k)}$, tracking inconsistency events as a discrete invariant.

- [Paraconsistent classification](../theorems/paraconsistent-classification.md) — a refined classification result that distinguishes settlings by their inconsistency-cohomology in addition to the standard truth-cohomology.

- The [paraconsistent transfinite settling](../examples/paraconsistent-transfinite-settling.md) example, exhibiting the inconsistency content explicitly.

The paraconsistent track is a structural specialization of the main cohomology — it doesn't replace it, but adds resolution. A settling with no inconsistencies is fully captured by the standard $\mathbf{t}$-cohomology; a settling with inconsistencies needs the paraconsistent variant to be classified completely.
