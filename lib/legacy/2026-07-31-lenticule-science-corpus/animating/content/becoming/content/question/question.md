# question

A **question** is a determinate content raised and held open: formed enough to be asked, not yet charged with any evidence.
"Is the sky blue?" puts the content *the sky is blue* fully into view as something to settle, while deliberately leaving its answer-slot empty.
A question is the seed of inquiry — the first member of the doxastic family (question → judgement → belief → knowledge), which walks one inquiry from no-evidence to settled.

## The priority of the question

R.G. Collingwood made the question prior to the proposition: in his *logic of question and answer*, every statement is an answer to a question, and you cannot grasp what a statement means without knowing the question it answers.
Gadamer (*Truth and Method*) develops the point into a method — "the art of questioning is the art of thinking" — and fixes what a genuine question is: it has a determinate *sense* (it is really *about* something) and yet holds its answer genuinely *open* (it does not already contain the verdict).
Both conditions matter here.
Without determinacy there is no question, only vague unrest; without openness there is no question, only a statement awaiting recitation.

This is why inquiry begins, for Peirce, with the "irritation of doubt" — a *real* doubt with a determinate object, not the paper doubt of saying the words.
The question is that doubt given form: a content held in the open, awaiting evidence.

Two stances can share the empty, uncharged corner, and telling them apart is the whole work of asking:

- a **datum** is a content held *as given* — taken as so, with no live way for it to be otherwise.
  Inquiry *stops* here.
- a **question** is the same uncharged content held *as open* — raised in order to be inquired into.
  Inquiry *starts* here.

(*Wonder* and its object *mystery* are not members of this family.
Mystery is the un-articulated ground that precedes any determinate question — there is not yet a definite content to ask about.
Questioning is the act that carves a determinate question *out* of that ground; the mystery itself stays prior to inquiry, undiminished by any answer.)

## What generates it

A question is generated by **questioning**: the act that takes some content and raises it *as a question* — determinate, but uncharged.
To ask is exactly to hold a formed content open for evidence rather than asserting or denying it.

## What it generates

A question generates the rest of the inquiry.
It is handed to [judgement](../../../doing/library/judgement/docs/judgement.md) — a content ready to be taken up and weighed — and everything downstream is the working-out of the question.
And it generates its own re-entry: when an inquiry closes in [knowledge](../../../doing/working/governing/library/knowledge/docs/knowledge.md), the settled answer opens the next question.
Every answer, as Gadamer says, reveals new questions; the mystery the family draws from is never used up.
