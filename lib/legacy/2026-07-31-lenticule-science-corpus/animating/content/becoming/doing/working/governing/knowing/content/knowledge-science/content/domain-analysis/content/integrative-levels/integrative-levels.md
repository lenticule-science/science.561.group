# Integrative-levels

*Integrative-levels* are successive forms of order arranged in a scale of complexity: atoms, molecules, cells, organisms, minds, societies, cultures. Joseph Needham coined the term in 1937, drawing on earlier work in emergentist philosophy. The corpus carries the concept because the discipline of knowledge organization that the [predicated subtheory](theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/) generalises rests on it.

## What the concept commits to

The core claim is that matter organizes itself into increasingly complex structures, each level integrating the components of the level below while producing qualities that did not exist at that lower level. James Feibleman (1954) formalized the concept into twelve laws. The load-bearing ones are four:

1. **Each level organizes entities from the level below into new wholes.** Cells are organisations of molecules; organisms are organisations of cells.
2. **Each level has at least one emergent quality not present at lower levels.** Life is not present at the molecular level; mind is not present at the cellular level.
3. **Higher levels depend on lower levels for their existence.** A cell cannot exist without its molecular substrate; a society cannot exist without its individual members.
4. **Higher levels cannot be fully explained by (reduced to) lower levels.** This is the anti-reductionist claim — emergent qualities are real and require their own vocabulary.

Feibleman's further laws extend these in both directions: higher levels also exert constraints on lower levels (downward causation), and disruption at any level propagates both up and down.

## Application to knowledge organization

Douglas Foskett (1962) and the British Classification Research Group adopted integrative-levels as the theoretical basis for a new general classification scheme, arguing that the natural order of knowledge follows the sequence of integrative levels rather than the administrative structure of universities. The Bliss Bibliographic Classification, Second Edition (BC2) implements this: its main classes run from physics through chemistry, biology, psychology, and the social sciences, each domain located at the level appropriate to its objects of study.

Integrative-levels supplies the *vertical* axis that complements [faceted-classification](faceted-classification.md)'s *horizontal* axis: levels order the domains, facets decompose subjects within each domain. Together they offer a two-dimensional framework that neither enumerative classification nor pure facet analysis can provide alone.

## Substrate-level reading

The [predicated-knowledge-systems](theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/) subtheory reads integrative-levels as a *stratification of the predicate category*: predicates of higher levels are realised by predicates of lower levels via the predicate-category morphisms. The natural order of knowledge becomes the predicate category's order on the vocabulary's level structure; faceted analysis (per [faceted-classification](faceted-classification.md)) is the orthogonal horizontal decomposition within each level's stratum. See [knowledge-organization-survey](theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/texts/knowledge-organization-survey.md) §3 and §7 for the structural translation, and the (sketch) theorem [four-group-register-as-regime](theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/theorems/four-group-register-as-regime.md) for the regime-classified reading of stratified vocabularies.
