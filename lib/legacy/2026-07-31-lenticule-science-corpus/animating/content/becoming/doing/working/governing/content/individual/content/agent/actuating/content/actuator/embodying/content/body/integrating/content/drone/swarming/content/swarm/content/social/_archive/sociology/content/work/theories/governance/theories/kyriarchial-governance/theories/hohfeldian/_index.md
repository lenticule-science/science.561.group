The Hohfeldian framework is the analytical scheme that Wesley Newcomb Hohfeld developed in two seminal *Yale Law Journal* articles ("Some Fundamental Legal Conceptions as Applied in Judicial Reasoning," 1913 and 1917) for analyzing what is conventionally called a "right" into four distinguishable jural relations.
A *claim* (right) correlates with another's *duty*.
A *privilege* (liberty) correlates with another's *no-right*.
A *power* — the capacity to alter one's own or another's legal position — correlates with another's *liability* (susceptibility to that alteration).
An *immunity* correlates with another's *disability* (lack of power to alter the immune party's position).

The framework's analytical force is in the disambiguation.
What ordinary speech calls "having a right" can be any of these — and the analytical work that an institutional analysis of [governance](../../) must do depends on knowing which. *Saying* one has a right to free expression conflates the claim against another's interference (what the First Amendment establishes), the privilege to act, the power to authorize others to speak on one's behalf, and the immunity from certain kinds of regulation.
Hohfeld's scheme separates these so that an actual analysis can proceed.

This subdomain's terms extend the Hohfeldian scheme into the analysis of *operational* positions — what happens to legal positions as they are exercised, transferred, defended, lost, and recovered over time. *Hohfeldian assignment* names the transfer of a position from one party to another. *Hohfeldian novation* names the substitution of a new position for an old one with consenting parties. *Hohfeldian succession* names the transfer of position by operation of law (death, dissolution, statutory succession). *Hohfeldian waiver* names the voluntary relinquishment of a position. *Hohfeldian ratification* names the retroactive validation of an act that was originally unauthorized. *Hohfeldian revocation* names the unilateral retraction of an authority previously granted. *Hohfeldian estoppel* names the foreclosure of a position by prior conduct. *Hohfeldian appeal* names the procedural challenge of a determination. *Hohfeldian relief* names the remedy granted on a successful claim. *Hohfeldian position* is the abstract type all of these specific operations act on.

The subdomain has been developed in this library at the term level beyond what the standard secondary literature on Hohfeld typically does; the operational extensions are part of the substantive contribution.
A future pass might lift the more-stable terms to *theorems* — characterization theorems for Hohfeldian succession, for waiver-versus-revocation, for the algebra of ratification — once the formal apparatus is recovered.

The Hohfeldian framework is one of three the [`governance/`](../../) subdomain develops alongside [`jurisdiction/`](../jurisdiction/) (the analysis of the scope conditions under which an authority's positions are operative) and [`military-command/`](../military-command/) (the analysis of command authority in a specific institutional substrate).
All three share the project of giving institutional positions an analyzable structure rather than treating them as primitive.
