## What this node is

Semiotics is [language](../../language.md) generated by the **articulate** morphism. Articulate is the first move of the unfolding `articulate → predicate → posit → settle`: it is the bare bringing-into-relation, the act of making a relation *speak* before anything is yet said *of* it or done *with* it. Run at the base of language, articulate produces the **sign**: something standing for something to someone.

This is first-class in two convergent traditions. In Peirce, the sign is irreducibly triadic — a representamen (vehicle) stands for an object to an interpretant (the meaning-effect it produces). None of the three can be dropped: a mark with no interpretant signifies nothing, and a meaning with no vehicle is not yet communicable. In Saussure, the sign is the union of signifier and signified. Either way, what semiotics names is the minimal articulation of meaning — the relation by which one thing *counts as* another — and nothing more. There is no well-formedness here yet (that is predication / grammar) and no force or value (that is positing / speech-acts). Semiotics holds the relation at the instant it first signifies, which is exactly what the articulate morphism is.

## What it generates / contains

The node's canonical body treats the sign relation and its dynamics: the **sign** itself, the **representamen** (vehicle), the **interpretant** (the meaning produced, itself a sign), and **semiosis** — the open-ended chain in which each interpretant becomes a new sign generating further interpretants (Eco's "unlimited semiosis"). It carries Peirce's classifications (icon / index / symbol), Saussure's dyadic signifier / signified, and the schools that extend the base: French semiology, social and cognitive semiotics, the Moscow–Tartu *semiosphere*, the Bakhtin circle's dialogism and heteroglossia, and the bio-, eco-, and computational extensions of the sign concept.

## Orientation

This node descends from [language](../../language.md), whose generating morphisms are articulate / predicate / posit / settle. Its siblings on that base are [grammar](../grammar/grammar.md) (the **predicate** step — saying something well-formed *of* signs) and [speech-acts](../speech-acts/speech-acts.md) (the **posit** step — sign with force and value); settled, accepted meaning closes into common ground. Semiotics is the articulate root they all presuppose: grammar orders signs and speech-acts charge them, but both require that something already signify. Un-canonicalized material — term files, school surveys, and dated drafts — sits in [`_archive`](_archive).
