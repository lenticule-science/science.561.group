# Bourdieu 1980 — The Logic of Practice

**Bourdieu, Pierre.
(1980). *Le Sens pratique*.
Paris: Les Éditions de Minuit.
(Trans.
Richard Nice, 1990, as *The Logic of Practice*.
Stanford University Press.)**

## Annotation

The central theoretical statement of Bourdieu's mature sociology.
The book develops the concept of *habitus* — the system of durable, transposable dispositions inculcated through practical experience — against both subjectivist (phenomenological) and objectivist (structuralist) approaches that Bourdieu finds inadequate.
The logic of practice is not rule-following, and it is not mechanical reproduction of structural patterns; it is the habitual, pre-reflexive generation of practice from dispositions acquired through one's positional history.

For predicated sociology, this text is the primary source for the [habitus-closure](../objects/habitus-closure.md) object, the [capital-fiber-bundle](../objects/capital-fiber-bundle.md), and the [structural-determination](teaching/explanations/structural-determination.md) concept.
Bourdieu's distinction between habitus as acquired dispositions and field as relational structure is given substrate-algebraic form as the distinction between the closure-rule subsystem $\mathcal{H}_e$ (habitus as agent-indexed rules) and the social settling's predicate category (field as relational structure).

The treatment of *hysteresis* — the lag between structural change and habitus revision — is particularly useful: in predicated sociology's terms, hysteresis is the continued firing of rules acquired in a prior positional context on a current positional context that no longer matches the rules' antecedent patterns, producing $\top$ states on action-predicate cells.
