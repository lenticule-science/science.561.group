Transport loss is what falls out of an artifact when its content is moved from one register to another. The diagram restated as a sentence loses the spatial parallelism that made the diagram fast to read. The sentence restated as a diagram loses the temporal structure that the words carried. The proof restated as code loses the readable narrative; the code restated as a proof loses the executability. There is no register-pair that transports without loss, and the framework asks every transport to be honest about what it forfeits.

The honest transport names the loss. It says, "the diagram captures the parallelism between cases that the symbolic statement only implies." It says, "the formal expansion captures the discharge of every hypothesis that the prose argument leaves to the reader." Transport-honest documents typically include both forms because each is doing work the other cannot do: the diagram is consulted for one operation, the prose for another. The two forms together carry what neither carries alone.

## Why it matters

Transport loss is the underlying reason [medium-imitation](medium-imitation.md) is a defect. If transports were lossless, the choice of register would be cosmetic and the most "rigorous" register could replace all others. They are not, so the choice of register is substantive, and a practice that imitates the most prestigious register at the cost of everything else has shed competences it cannot recover by restating the content again.

The same argument applies inside an artifact. A textbook that omits diagrams in favor of prose has decided that the spatial-parallelism work the diagram does is not worth its cost. The decision may be right (some content does not need diagrams) or wrong (the prose is now four times longer and harder to scan). The framework asks the decision to be made knowingly: the omission of a register from an artifact is a decision about what work the artifact has chosen not to do.

## Where it shows up

Translation across natural languages is the classical case. A code fragment ported from one language to another. A statistical result restated as a confidence interval rather than a posterior. A surgical procedure restated as a checklist for nurses to verify. In every case, the transport is useful but not transparent: the destination register carries some of the source's content, sheds some, and adds register-specific commitments of its own. The artifact's responsibility is to make the loss inspectable, not to pretend it does not happen.
