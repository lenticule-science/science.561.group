Every ideology presupposes a social world.
The presuppositions are often implicit — the ideology presents itself as a description of or prescription for *the* social world in general, without specifying the conditions under which the description or prescription holds.
Measuring an ideology by its statistical prerequisites is the methodological practice of making those presuppositions explicit, identifying the specific distributional conditions the ideology requires, and then asking whether those conditions obtain.

If the conditions obtain, the ideology may be functioning as it claims.
If the conditions do not obtain, the ideology is functioning differently than it claims — specifically, as [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md): metabolizing the gap between its premises and the actual distribution through the subjects who inhabit it.

## What a statistical prerequisite is

A statistical prerequisite is a distributional condition that an ideology presupposes without stating.
It is *statistical* because it concerns distributions in a population, not the properties of individual cases.
It is a *prerequisite* because the ideology's operation requires it.

The distinction between ideology-as-description and ideology-as-californication turns on whether the statistical prerequisites are met.
Consider the liberal-individualist ideology of economic mobility: the belief that individual effort and talent, applied in a market economy, produce outcomes proportional to merit.
This ideology functions as description if the statistical distribution shows that individual effort and talent are the principal determinants of economic outcomes — if the correlation between effort/talent and outcome is high relative to the correlation between structural position (class of origin, race, geography) and outcome.
If the distribution shows the reverse — if structural position is the principal determinant — then the ideology is not functioning as description but as [moral displacement](../teaching/explanations/moral-displacement.md): relocating accountability from structure to individual under conditions where individual variation is not the principal cause of variance.

## The seven key assumptions

Liberal ideology, as the operating ideology of the apparatus predicated sociology analyzes, presupposes seven statistical conditions.
Each is identifiable, each is measurable in principle, and each is currently violated to some degree in the societies where the ideology operates.

**1.
Individual agency is the primary determinant of outcomes.** The ideology presupposes that what individuals do determines what happens to them.
This requires that the variance in outcomes across individuals is primarily accounted for by variance in individual choices and characteristics, not by variance in structural position.
The empirical distribution, documented extensively by Piketty (2014) and adjacent researchers, shows that structural position — inherited wealth, class of origin, race, geography — accounts for the substantial majority of outcome variance for most populations.

**2.
Information is available and approximately equal.** Liberal-market ideology and liberal-democratic ideology both presuppose that agents have the information needed to make choices that serve their interests.
This requires that information asymmetries be not so severe as to systematically advantage one party over another.
The empirical distribution — in financial markets, in healthcare, in political information — shows systematic and substantial information asymmetries.

**3.
Choices are voluntary.** Liberal ideology presupposes that choices are made without coercion — that the choosing agent is not under compulsion that would make the choice unfree in the relevant sense.
This requires that the background conditions of choice not include threats or deprivations so severe as to negate voluntariness.
The empirical distribution of economic conditions — including what it means to accept a job under conditions of housing cost, healthcare cost, and debt load — challenges the voluntariness assumption across large populations.

**4.
Long-run outcomes converge across starting positions.** Liberal meritocracy ideology presupposes that starting position matters less than individual action over time — that people can rise from disadvantaged starting positions and that the starting gap closes over generations.
The empirical distribution shows persistent and in many metrics increasing outcome gaps across generations, correlated with structural starting position.

**5.
Markets clear efficiently.** Liberal economic ideology presupposes that price signals aggregate information and coordinate production and distribution efficiently, producing outcomes that are broadly welfare-enhancing.
The empirical distribution of market outcomes — including climate externalities, healthcare market failures, housing market dysfunctions — shows systematic inefficiencies that markets have not cleared.

**6.
Political representation is approximately equal.** Liberal democratic ideology presupposes that political voice is distributed approximately equally — that each person's political standing is roughly equal.
The empirical distribution of political influence — documented across lobbying data, campaign-finance data, and policy-outcome correlation analyses — shows substantial inequality.

**7.
Institutions can be reformed through legitimate political means.** Liberal-reformist ideology presupposes that the political process is capable, in principle, of producing institutional changes that address structural conditions.
This requires that the political process be sufficiently insulated from the structural interests that would block such changes.
The empirical distribution of legislative outcomes relative to structural-interest lobbying challenges this assumption.

## How the violations produce californication

When the statistical prerequisites are violated — when individual agency is not the primary determinant, when information is asymmetric, when choices are constrained, when structural position persists, when markets fail, when political voice is unequal, when institutional reform is blocked — the ideology cannot function as it claims.
It cannot describe a system in which individual effort produces proportional rewards, because the distribution doesn't produce this.
It cannot prescribe for a political system in which legitimate-means reform is achievable, because the political system doesn't provide this.

What the ideology does instead, when its prerequisites are violated, is provide the moral vocabulary through which subjects *experience* the violated conditions.
The subject who is in the structural position of having violated prerequisites for [structural determination](../teaching/explanations/structural-determination.md) — the subject whose outcomes are substantially determined by structural position rather than individual action — inhabits the ideology's vocabulary as the framework within which their experience is intelligible.
They experience their structural position as personal inadequacy, their structural constraints as personal choices, their structural outcomes as personal results.
This is [moral displacement](../teaching/explanations/moral-displacement.md) operating through the ideology.

## What the method produces

Measuring ideologies by their statistical prerequisites produces three kinds of analytical output.

**Identification of the conditions under which the ideology functions as described.** For any ideology, the method identifies the distributional conditions under which its operation is coherent — when it can be a description of actual social relations rather than a misrepresentation.
This is useful because it prevents the error of treating all ideology as californication; some ideologies may actually be describing conditions that obtain.

**Identification of the conditions under which the ideology functions as californication.** The method identifies where the prerequisites are violated and the ideology is metabolizing the violation through subjects' self-understanding.
This is the analytical contribution to predicated sociology's specific project.

**Specification of the magnitude of the californication.** Because the statistical prerequisites are measurable in principle, the method allows for approximate assessment of *how much* the ideology is functioning as californication versus description.
As structural determination rises relative to individual-agency determination, the ideology's californication function rises accordingly.
This allows for a more precise account than the dichotomous description/californication distinction.
