# What semiotics inherits when it goes structural

The parent theory [linguistics](../../../_index.md) is the science of natural language and its sub-systems. Its semiotic child generalises beyond natural language to *any* system of standing-for. This narrative records the inheritance ledger in three columns: what semiotics inherits *with proof* from upstream, what it inherits *as substantive commitment* (`status: sketch`), and what *does not inherit* (the parameters by which the theory adapts to its instances).

The ledger is in the spirit of the [predicated-knowledge-systems inheritance ledger](../../../../information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/narratives/what-knowledge-systems-inherits.md): the discipline holds that *being explicit about what does and does not inherit* is itself part of the structural commitment.

## What inherits with proof

These inheritances are *proved* by the upstream literature directly:

- **The sign-relation is irreducibly triadic** (Peirce; per [Peirce CP](references/peirce-cp.md)). Reductions to dyadic relations lose the *interpretant*; reductions to one-place predicates lose the *object*. The Peircean argument-by-collapse is the proof.
- **The langue is a system of differences with no positive terms** (Saussure; per [Saussure 1916](references/saussure1916.md)). Sign-vehicles have *valeur* by their structural position, not by intrinsic content; the proof is the substitution-test.
- **Connotative semiotics iterate** (Hjelmslev; per [Hjelmslev 1943](references/hjelmslev1943.md)). A connotative system takes a denotative system as its expression-plane; the iteration composes. The proof is structural: the connotative semiotic's components are well-defined when the denotative is.
- **The semantic square has four positions related by contrariety, contradiction, and implication** (Greimas; per [Greimas 1966](references/greimas1966.md)). The structural relations among the four positions are determined by the binary semantic axis selected; the proof is the axis-of-contrariety construction.
- **The semiosphere is the condition of possibility for sign-life** (Lotman; per [Lotman 1990](references/lotman1990.md)). Outside a semiosphere, sign-vehicles are inert; the proof is by exhibition (cultural-historical case studies of sign-vehicles persisting across semiosphere change).
- **Register / field / tenor / mode are independent variables of social meaning** (Halliday; per [Halliday 1978](references/halliday1978.md)). The four axes can be varied independently; the proof is the systematic-functional analysis of natural utterances.
- **Sign-life is biospheric in scope** (Sebeok; per [Sebeok 1994](references/sebeok1994.md)). Semiosis is not specific to humans or to symbol-using systems; the proof is the comparative biosemiotic survey.

## What inherits as substantive commitment (status: sketch)

These inheritances are *plausible structural commitments* that the structural framing names but that have not been established in full generality. They are `status: sketch`:

- **Unlimited semiosis converges to a bilattice fixpoint** (per [unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint](../theorems/unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint.md)). The Peircean open chain is the substrate's transfinite-bounded settling; the encyclopedia-bounded chain is Scott-continuous. The full faithfulness of the identification is not yet established.
- **Translation is substrate federate under identity-mapping** (per [translation-as-fiber-morphism](learning/questions/translation-as-fiber-morphism.md) open question). The identification is plausible and operational but not yet settled.
- **Code and system are distinct, with code being one component of the system tuple** (per [code-vs-system-distinction](learning/questions/code-vs-system-distinction.md) open question). The substrate-side analogy favours this reading; the discipline's traditional usage admits both.
- **Semiosphere = discourse-community-as-fiber on the substrate** (per the [composability narrative](composability-with-predicated-knowledge-systems.md)). The bridge is operational but its faithfulness is sketch-level.
- **Codeswitching is per-cell scope-restriction** (per the [codeswitching morphism](../morphisms/codeswitching.md)). The reading absorbs the codeswitching literature into the substrate's scope discipline; the absorption's faithfulness is sketch-level.

## What inherits as engineering correspondence

These are inheritances at the operational surface — what a working semiotic-aware system gets when it adopts the structural framing:

- **Semiotic-system competence is operationalised as multi-scope substrate operation.** A working system competent in two codes operates the substrate in their two scopes; codeswitching is per-cell scope-restriction; translation is federate.
- **Connotative iteration is operationalised as iterated closure.** The substrate's settling at higher iterates is the connotative tower made operational.
- **Semiosphere boundary is operationalised as scope-membership.** The substrate's [identity-by-relations](../../../../information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/concepts/discourse-community-as-fiber.md) discipline determines what crosses the semiosphere boundary.

## What is *not* inherited

Some commitments of the parent (and sibling) theories are *not* preserved by the structural framing. Naming them is part of the discipline:

- **The choice of natural language as primary instance.** The parent linguistics theory takes natural language as its primary subject; semiotics is open over the inventory of admissible sign-vehicles. Natural-language-restricted analyses are special cases of the more general framing.
- **The choice of synchronic primacy.** Saussure's synchronic-primary commitment is a parameter of his system, not of the discipline. Diachronic semiotic systems (Lotman's semiosphere with its memory; Sebeok's biosemiotic with its evolutionary depth) do not collapse into synchronic ones.
- **The choice of human interpreter.** The Peircean and Saussurean systems presuppose human interpretation; the Sebeokean biosemiotic does not. The discipline is open over the kind of interpreter the system serves.
- **The choice of arbitrariness of the sign.** Saussure's commitment to the arbitrariness of the signifier-signified pairing is a parameter; iconic systems (where the pairing is motivated by resemblance) and indexical systems (where it is motivated by causal contiguity) reject the arbitrariness commitment without leaving the discipline.

These un-inheritances are *features*, not bugs: they are the parameters by which the structural framing adapts to different sign-systems. A structural framing that imposed any of them would be less general than the discipline it claims to formalise.

## What the parent gains by adopting the substrate

The parent theory linguistics is enriched, not replaced, by the structural-semiotic framing committed to here. What the parent gains:

- **A substrate-level reading of every semiotic primitive.** Sign-relations, codes, interpretants, semiospheres become substrate-level operations rather than analytical posits.
- **A composability discipline with the predicated-knowledge-systems substrate.** Linguistic content and predicated knowledge content live on the same substrate, federated via the same operations.
- **A regime-classification of semiotic systems.** The four-regime substrate classification (strict / Scott-continuous / transfinite-bounded / aperiodic) provides a structural-mathematical taxonomy of semiotic systems, supplementing the historical-philosophical one.
- **An operational binding to working systems.** The corpus's own predication-cell substrate inherits the semiotic reading, which becomes the substrate's structural account of what *being-about* means at a working scale.
