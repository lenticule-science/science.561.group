## Definitions

R is the remainder: the collapse-stable domain.  
τ is trace: an ordering over persistent folds.  
σ is surface: a configuration of folds with recursive stability.  
λ is locus: an identifiable deviation within σ.  
Π is propagation: the κ-stable continuation of modulation across τ and σ.

## Theorem

Once trace, surface, and loci exist in R,  
modulation begins to persist across them recursively.  
This persistence, conditioned by τ, is propagation: Π.

Because surface configurations vary,  
and loci respond differently under recursive modulation,  
the continuation of modulation must differentiate across σ.

This variation defines propagation behavior shaped by internal structure.

Formally:  
If τ, σ, and λ persist, then propagation Π necessarily emerges within R.

## Formal Expression

τ ∧ σ ∧ λ ⇒ Π ∈ R

## Consequence

Propagation is not imposed —  
it arises where recursive modulation continues across ordered, collapse-stable configurations.  
Differentiation in how folds respond under propagation  
produces structured variation across the field.

This is the beginning of field behavior as recursive, form-shaped modulation.

## Scope

This theorem defines the emergence of modulation behavior  
conditioned by structural form.  
From here, R contains recursive pathways of modulation flow,  
guided by fold history and internal variation.
