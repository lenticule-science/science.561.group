# relating

Relating is the most basic concept in this theory: the ongoing activity by which social beings come to be what they are through their connection to one another.
It is placed first, and everything else is derived from it, because of a deliberate commitment — the commitment of relational sociology — that relations are prior to the things they relate.
On the ordinary, "substantialist" picture, the world is made of self-standing individuals who exist first and enter relationships afterward, the way billiard balls exist before they collide.
Relating reverses the order.
There are not social beings first who then relate; there is relating, and social beings are the relatively stable patterns that relating sustains.

This is an old idea with a long pedigree.
Marx wrote that the human essence is "the ensemble of the social relations."
Simmel built an entire sociology around *forms of association* (Vergesellschaftung) rather than around individuals or societies treated as things.
In philosophy the same move runs from Nāgārjuna's dependent origination — nothing possesses self-existence; everything arises in dependence on other things — to Whitehead's doctrine of internal relations, in which an entity is *constituted* by its relationships rather than merely affected by them.
In contemporary sociology it is the explicit program of Mustafa Emirbayer's "Manifesto for a Relational Sociology" (1997) and of relational thinkers from Charles Tilly to Harrison White to Pierpaolo Donati.
The shared wager is that you understand a person, a group, or an institution better by tracing the relations that constitute it than by cataloguing its intrinsic attributes.

To say that relating "constitutes its terms" is a strong claim with real consequences.
It means a worker is not a person who happens to have a job but a position within a relation of employment that would not exist without the employer, the wage, and the property arrangement that makes selling one's labour the only alternative to destitution; change those relations and the "worker" becomes something else.
It means kinship is not a label attached to biologically given individuals but the very web through which people become parents, children, and kin at all.
And it means the work of sociology is not to find the hidden essences of social things but to follow how relating produces and reproduces them.

That is why this theory is built as a tree of derivations rooted in relating.
Each concept that follows — a social being, a relation, an institution, a form of domination, a refusal — is presented as something that some determinate pattern of relating brings into being, and the path to it through the tree records what it depends on and what generates it.
The order is not decorative: it is the claim that you cannot have the later thing without the earlier, and that the earlier explains the later rather than the reverse.
