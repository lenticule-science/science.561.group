## Annotation

The founding monograph of the *situated learning* tradition. Argues that learning is not the acquisition of decontextualised knowledge by an individual but a *change in participation* within a community of practice — the newcomer is *legitimately peripheral*, performing real but low-stakes tasks at the community's edge, and learning consists in the trajectory from peripheral to full participation as the newcomer takes on more central, more consequential, more identity-forming roles. The book grounds this through ethnographic case studies of Yucatec midwives, Vai and Gola tailors, U.S. Navy quartermasters, supermarket meat-cutters, and AA members.

For the predicated framing, Lave and Wenger supply the answer to the question "what is the *user* of a knowledge system?" The user is not an isolated agent querying a database; the user is a *participant* in a discourse community whose [discourse-community-as-fiber](teaching/explanations/discourse-community-as-fiber.md) the system serves. The user's *trajectory* across roles within the community corresponds to a trajectory across the bilattice value-domain on relevant cells: a peripheral participant's assertions carry $\bot$-or-near-$\bot$ evidence weight; a central participant's assertions carry near-$\mathbf{t}$ or near-$\mathbf{f}$ weight; the *conferring of legitimacy* is the discourse community's protocol for moving an asserter's contributions toward fuller knowledge-order on the bilattice.

The framing inherits Lave and Wenger's claim that *knowledge is constituted through participation*, not stored in heads. This is operationalised as: the predication-cell substrate's value-domain admits multi-source aggregation (Belnap's four states), and the closure regime governs how a community's settling protocol promotes peripheral assertions toward central status.
