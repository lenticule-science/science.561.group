# Bent (2025) — The Term 'Agent' Has Been Diluted Beyond Utility

**Full citation:** Bent, O.
"The Term 'Agent' Has Been Diluted Beyond Utility." arXiv 2508.05338, 2025.

## Summary

Bent argues that the AI industry's overuse of "agent" has rendered the term definitionally useless: it applies to systems ranging from single-turn function callers to month-long autonomous software engineers.
The paper proposes three necessary conditions for an agentic system (active environmental impact, goal-directed behavior, state awareness) and a five-dimensional spectrum for measuring agentness above the floor.

## Contribution to the agents theory

This paper is the primary empirical foundation for the [three-condition characterization](../theorems/three-condition-characterization.md) and the [agentness-spectrum](teaching/explanations/agentness-spectrum.md) concept.
Bent's three conditions are reproduced with one terminological change (Bent's "active environmental impact" → this theory's "environmental action") and the [toward-a-falsifiable-definition](../texts/toward-a-falsifiable-definition-of-agentic-systems.md) text elaborates the operational tests for each condition.

Bent's five-dimensional spectrum (environmental interaction sophistication, goal-directed behavior complexity, temporal coherence, learning/adaptation, autonomy) is adopted verbatim as the agentness-spectrum concept.

## Relationship to other sources

Bent's necessary conditions are more rigorous than Anthropic's workflow/agent distinction (which identifies a real structural difference but is too broad to falsify), Ng's four design patterns (a pattern catalog, not a definition), and the MIT AI Agent Index operationalization (which operationalizes autonomy with an arbitrary three-call threshold).
Sapkota et al.'s five-dimensional taxonomy (arXiv 2505.10468) is complementary — a classification tool rather than a necessary-conditions claim.
