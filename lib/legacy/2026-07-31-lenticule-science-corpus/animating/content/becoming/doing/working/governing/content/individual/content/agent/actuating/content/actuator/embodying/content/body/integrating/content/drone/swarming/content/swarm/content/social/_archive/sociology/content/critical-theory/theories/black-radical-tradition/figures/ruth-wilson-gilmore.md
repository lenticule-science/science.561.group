Ruth Wilson Gilmore (b.
1950) is the figure through whom the [black-radical-tradition](../_index.md) holds its analysis of how racial capitalism becomes spatial — how the unevenness of life chances is organized geographically, and how the prison fixes (in both senses) the surpluses that racial capitalism produces.
Professor of Earth and Environmental Sciences at the CUNY Graduate Center, co-founder of Critical Resistance with [Angela Davis](angela-davis.md) and others.

The full biographical note lives at [`library/references/people/ruth-wilson-gilmore`](../../../../../../../../references/people/ruth-wilson-gilmore.md).
This page records what the subdomain in particular takes from her.

## What this subdomain takes from her

- **Racism as state-sanctioned production of group-differentiated vulnerability to premature death.** The definition refuses the framing of racism as attitude or individual prejudice.
  It locates racism in the political organization of differential mortality and makes it analyzable as a structural-spatial-political condition.
  The definition is load-bearing for the subdomain's term [racial-capitalism](../terms/racial-capitalism.md).
- **The prison fix.** U.S. carceral expansion from the 1980s onward as a geographic-political-economic response to capital's surplus crises — surplus land, surplus labor, surplus capital, surplus state capacity. *Golden Gulag* (2007) develops this reading for California: prisons as the spatial fix that absorbs each surplus and reproduces the conditions for its own expansion.
  The reading extends Robinson's racial-capitalism analysis into a concrete geography.
- **Abolition geography.** Abolition is principally the building of the relations and infrastructures that make prisons unnecessary — schools, clinics, housing, the dense civic life racial capitalism organizes against.
  The geographic register names what the abolition is *for*: the production of life-supporting place.
- **Organized abandonment.** The withdrawal of public infrastructures and life-supporting capacities from particular populations and places — and the production of the conditions the carceral state then claims to "address" through containment.
  The term is used in the subdomain's term [racial-capitalism](../terms/racial-capitalism.md) and is shared with the [povinellian](../../povinellian/terms/organized-abandonment.md) lineage.
- **Non-reformist reforms.** Reforms that reduce the scale and capacity of carceral institutions without strengthening or legitimating them.
  Distinguished from reforms that improve conditions in ways that consolidate the institution.

## Key works

- *Golden Gulag: Prisons, Surplus, Crisis, and Opposition in Globalizing California* (2007)
- *Abolition Geography: Essays Towards Liberation* (2022, ed. Brenna Bhandar and Alberto Toscano)
- *Change Everything: Racial Capitalism and the Case for Abolition* (2022)
