Three things to specify.
First, what [settlerism](../teaching/explanations/settlerism.md) means in its operative sense, distinguished from settler colonialism understood as a finished historical event.
Second, how the account of [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md) relies on the settler-colonial analysis as one of its load-bearing registers — not as moral framing but as historical-material substrate.
Third, how the *subject under californication* is a settler subject in a specific structural sense, regardless of the individual subject's awareness or self-identification.

## What settlerism is

The term *settlerism* names settler colonialism understood as a *continuing structure* rather than a finished event.
The foundational article is Patrick Wolfe's "Settler Colonialism and the Elimination of the Native" (2006), which established the analytical position that has organized the field since: settler colonialism is structured by a continuing logic of *elimination* of the indigenous, where elimination operates through many means (removal, killing, assimilation, narrative-erasure, legal disposal of indigenous standing) and is not concluded by any historical moment but continues to operate as the structural form of governance.

A history that frames settler colonialism as event ("America was settled," "the frontier closed") makes the elimination operation invisible by treating it as concluded.
A structure analysis frames the elimination as continuing: the settler state's contemporary operations — legal-jurisdictional disposal, infrastructural displacement, economic incorporation, cultural-narrative formatting — are not aftermath but continuation.
Eve Tuck and K.
Wayne Yang's "Decolonization Is Not a Metaphor" (2012) makes the corollary argument: decolonization has a specific material referent — the repatriation of indigenous land and life — and is not satisfied by metaphorical extensions to other liberatory projects.

## How settlerism bears on the analysis

The reading of [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md) depends on the settler-colonial substrate in three structurally specific ways.

**The land that the apparatus operates on.** California, the historical laboratory of the apparatus, is settler-colonial territory.
The indigenous peoples of that coast — Ohlone, Tongva, Chumash, Miwok, Pomo, Yurok, and others — were not vacated by the apparatus's arrival; they were continuously displaced and continue to be displaced.
The reading of California as laboratory — Misiones, Gold Rush, agriculture, Hollywood, Esalen, Silicon Valley, wellness — as one apparatus requires reading it on this substrate.
The apparatus could test rapidly because its substrate could be ignored: the Indigenous peoples whose displacement underwrote the rapidity.
[Crisis settlerism](../teaching/explanations/crisis-settlerism.md) names how the structure intensifies its frontier-projection when its prior settlements destabilize.

**The subjects that the apparatus produces.** [Genring](../teaching/explanations/genring.md) is structurally adjacent to the settler-colonial operation of producing the indigenous as a fixed type: the Noble Indian, the Mountain Kurd, the Tribal-Authentic figure who provides the settler-cultural imaginary with its access to authenticity precisely by remaining frozen in the position the imaginary requires.
The settler-colonial structure produces, as its constitutive outside, the *indigenous-as-genre-figure*; the settler subject's self-image as modern, mobile, and futural depends on that figure's stability.
This is what [genre coherence](../teaching/explanations/genre-coherence.md) names: the settler-narrative integrity requires the indigenous figure to remain in the genre, and any deviation — the indigenous mathematician, the indigenous urban-cosmopolitan — registers as a problem for the narrative.

**The temporal middle that the apparatus eliminates.** The [temporal middle](../teaching/explanations/temporal-middle.md) — the position of inheriting, continuing, and transforming a tradition without either renewing it wholesale or abandoning it — is the structural position settlerism systematically eliminates.
The settler-colonial apparatus offers indigenous subjects two positions: traditional-authentic (frozen in pre-contact form, eliminable as residue) or assimilated-modern (severed from indigenous tradition, eliminable as continuance).
The temporal middle — the actual position every living indigenous subject occupies — is structurally absent from the settler-colonial frame.

## The subject under californication as settler subject

The subject under californication — the subject who self-regulates through the californication-circuit, who routes structural contradiction through personal affect, who participates in the platform's feedback loops as the loops require — is a *settler subject in a specific structural sense*, regardless of whether the individual subject is descended from settlers, recently arrived as an immigrant, racially marked as inheritor of the settler position, or any other autobiographical specification.

The claim is structural, not biographical.
Several features mark the subject-position as settler-structural.

**Operation on land whose prior relations are not registered.** The subject under californication lives on territory that has been subjected to the elimination operation; the territory's prior relations — indigenous land-tenure, kinship-place practices, ceremonial obligations, ecological knowledges — are not part of the subject's relation to the territory.
Even subjects who consciously acknowledge the prior relations participate in an everyday infrastructure that operates as if the prior relations did not exist.

**Subject-formation by genres that presuppose settler coherence.** The [ontogenric](../teaching/explanations/ontogenring.md) genres in which the subject under californication is formatted — the wellness-self, the activist-self, the productive-self, the resilient-self — presuppose a settler-cultural imaginary.
The wellness-self, in particular, draws on appropriations of indigenous and non-Western practices (yoga, mindfulness, plant-medicine ceremony) reformatted as personal-developmental tools, with the original relations stripped.
This is a continuation of the settler-colonial apparatus's logic of elimination: the practice is incorporated; the relation that gave the practice its meaning is eliminated.

**Affective metabolism that prefers personal management to structural confrontation.** The subject's response to structural contradiction is the californication-circuit: route the contradiction through personal affect, manage the affect, return to functional participation.
This is structurally consistent with the settler-cultural reflex of treating structural injustice as a personal-developmental opportunity.
[Harm governance](../teaching/explanations/harm-governance.md) names the specific institutional form this metabolism takes when it scales: care collapsed into risk management, performed by people who have lost faith in the state but reproduce its administrative logic with each other.

## What is drawn from indigenous critical thought

Indigenous critical thought is not supplementary material but analytically constitutive.
Several specific contributions:

**The structural reading of settler colonialism.** Wolfe's article provides the analytical apparatus that makes [settlerism](../teaching/explanations/settlerism.md) a usable category.
Without the structure-not-event distinction, the reading of californication's continuity from the Misiones to the platform would not be available.

**The temporal-middle analysis.** The [temporal middle](../teaching/explanations/temporal-middle.md) is drawn directly from Wolfe, Simpson, and Coulthard's analyses of the position settler colonialism eliminates.
The account of how californication operates across temporal registers — the fixed-traditional / assimilated-modern binary that the apparatus offers indigenous and non-indigenous subjects alike — is built on this analysis.

**The practice of refusal.** Audra Simpson's analysis of refusal — the practice of declining settler-state recognition in order to preserve the prior political relation — is the most important resource for thinking about what subjects can do when the apparatus's coherent subject-positions have been metabolized.
Refusal is one of the practices that may survive metabolization; the closure holds this question open.

**The distinction between metaphor and material.** Tuck and Yang's "Decolonization Is Not a Metaphor" is the foundational corrective for keeping the analysis honest about what its own conceptual moves do and do not produce.
Predicated sociology is a sociology, and its moves are conceptual; the conceptual moves do not, on their own, perform decolonization.
