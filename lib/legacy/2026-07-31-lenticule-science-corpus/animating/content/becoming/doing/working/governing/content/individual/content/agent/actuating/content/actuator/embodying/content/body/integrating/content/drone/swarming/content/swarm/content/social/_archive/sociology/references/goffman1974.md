The companion canonical Goffman work to *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life* (Goffman 1959): the late, methodologically reflexive study that introduces *frame* as the analytical apparatus for asking *what is going on here?* in any stretch of social interaction.
The book reads social activity as organized by primary frameworks (natural and social) and as susceptible to *keying* (transformations such as play, ceremony, rehearsal) and *fabrication* (deceptive transformations); the apparatus has subsequently propagated through the sociology of mass communication, the analysis of political discourse, and the contemporary literature on algorithmic and platform-mediated framings.
