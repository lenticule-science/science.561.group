An icon amplifies when it stands for more than what it depicts. McCloud's account of icons in *Understanding Comics* (HarperCollins, 1993) is that the simpler the icon, the more readers project themselves and their understanding into it. A photograph of a face is a specific person; a circle with two dots and a line is everyone. The compression is not a loss but a gain: the icon collects a class of cases under a single recognizable form, and the reader can manipulate the form without having to manipulate the underlying particulars.

Outside comics, the same operation pervades disciplined communication. A commutative diagram in algebra is not a picture of three objects and three arrows — it is the entire pattern of all triples of morphisms with that shape, available for inspection in the form of one drawing. A pictogram on a road sign is not a picture of one truck — it is the class of vehicles to which the sign applies. A circuit symbol is not a picture of a particular component — it is the structural role the component plays in any circuit.

## What amplification requires

For an icon to amplify, three things must hold:

1. **The icon is recognizable.** The reader has been trained, formally or informally, to read this icon, and the training is shared with the audience the artifact addresses.
2. **The icon stands for something specific.** The shape of the icon is correlated with a structural pattern, not arbitrarily assigned. Replacing the icon would be replacing the pattern itself.
3. **The icon supports operations.** The reader can do something with the icon — chase it, invert it, paste it into a larger diagram — that they could not do as quickly with the unfolded form.

When all three hold, the icon is an operative compression: the artifact has made structure available in a form that is fast to use. When any of the three fails, the icon is in danger of degenerating into a [prestige-icon](prestige-icon.md): something the writer uses for what its presence signals rather than for what it accomplishes.

## The diagnostic move

Audit a candidate icon by asking what operations it supports that its expanded form does not. If the icon survives the audit, it is amplifying. If the icon is doing only what its expanded form would do, it is not earning its presence.
