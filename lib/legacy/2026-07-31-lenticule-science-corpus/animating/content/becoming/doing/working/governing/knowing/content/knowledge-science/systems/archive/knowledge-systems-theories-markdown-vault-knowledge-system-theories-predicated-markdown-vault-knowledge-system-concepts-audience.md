# Audience

The **audience** of a typed [note](note.md) is the reader the note's [pragmatic register](pragmatic-register.md) and body conventions are calibrated for. Each [note-type](note-type.md) implicitly addresses a different audience — a `concept` note addresses a reader needing orientation; an `object` note addresses a reader needing precise structure to check a proof; a `theorem` note addresses a reader needing to know what is and is not proved; a `question` note addresses a future researcher who might attempt the question.

The per-note-type substantive specification is held open at [audience-and-pragmatics-of-each-note-type](questions/audience-and-pragmatics-of-each-note-type.md); the upstream vocabulary is loaded from both communication theories per `depends-on:`.

## Why audience is its own anchor

Pragmatic mis-register — a `concept` note drifting into theorem-style claim-and-evidence; a `theorem` note drifting into narrative recap — is invisible to validators that check frontmatter and structure. It is the largest source of corpus disorder that *cannot* be caught by FARS. The discipline's response is to make audience an explicit anchor of every note-type spec rather than leaving it implicit.

## Relation to upstream

Beyond the two communication theories, the concept's broader literature is *audience design* (Bell 1984; Clark & Murphy on common ground), *rhetorical situation* (Bitzer 1968), and *recipient design* (Sacks-Schegloff-Jefferson). These works live (or will live) under the science-side communication theory's references; the curation theory cites them transitively via that home rather than re-citing them locally.
