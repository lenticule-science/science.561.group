Youth liberation is the current within anarchism that names age-based hierarchy as a structure of domination on the same analytic register as the structures the rest of the tradition engages — coercive state authority, capital, patriarchy, white supremacy, settler colonialism — and that refuses the assumption that the asymmetric power adults hold over young people is natural rather than produced.

The subdomain's terms — *adultism*, *adult-supremacy*, *childism*, *misopedy* — name the specific structural relations the analysis works with. *Adultism* is the discrimination against young people as a class, parallel in structural form (though not in substance) to other -isms the tradition analyzes. *Adult supremacy* names the systemic arrangement under which adults hold institutional authority over young people across nearly every domain of life (family, school, medicine, law, labor, housing, speech). *Childism* names the prejudice that treats childhood as a deficit state to be governed and corrected rather than a way of being with its own legitimacy. *Misopedy* names the more direct hatred of children that runs through ostensibly child-protective institutions — the hatred visible in how children are spoken about by adults who believe themselves to be children's advocates.

The current's substantive work has run through the youth-liberation movement (1970s onward), through John Holt and the unschooling tradition, through the radical-pedagogy tradition that draws on Freire, and through the contemporary Indigenous-led work on youth sovereignty in their own communities.
It is in conversation with Black-radical and feminist analyses of how the family is itself a site of domination — Hortense Spillers's *Mama's Baby, Papa's Maybe* on the gendering and ungendering of Black children under slavery is one canonical reference — and with the contemporary disability-justice tradition's analysis of how institutional power over children with disabilities operates.

The subdomain is currently small.
The forward work is registered: substantive engagement with the youth-liberation movement's canonical texts, the unschooling and democratic-schooling tradition, and the Indigenous youth-sovereignty work that the broader [`indigenous-anarchism/`](../indigenous-anarchism/) subdomain points toward.
