**Fascist grammar** is the morphism on social settling that drives affective-predicate fibers through a canonical six-stage sequence: **crisis → community → enemy → sacrifice → destiny → inevitability**.
It is called a grammar because it is a structural operation on discourse and social affect, not a set of doctrinal beliefs; it can be run through any substantive content, and it is recognizable by its sequence regardless of the particular crisis, community, enemy, or destiny named.

In predication-categories terms: fascist grammar is a composed morphism $\phi_F = \phi_6 \circ \phi_5 \circ \phi_4 \circ \phi_3 \circ \phi_2 \circ \phi_1$ on the social settling $\mathcal{S}$, where each $\phi_i$ is a closure-rule modification that drives a specific predicate-fiber toward a specific value.
The composition produces a settling in which the community's political-legitimacy predicates have converged to $\mathbf{t}$ (we are the authentic people, under threat, with a destiny that makes our actions necessary), and the designated enemy's predicates have converged to $\mathbf{f}$ (the enemy is inauthentic, dangerous, and must be removed for the rebirth to occur).

## The six stages as fiber operations

**Crisis** ($\phi_1$): The settling's political-legitimacy predicates are driven to $\top$ — the situation is simultaneously catastrophic and demanding of response.
The destabilized settling provides the raw material; the crisis-attribution drives the interpretation of $\top$-valued predicates toward catastrophe.
Subjects whose affective fibers are at saturation are maximally available to this attribution.

**Community** ($\phi_2$): From the crisis, a particular community is constituted as the authentic sufferers and rightful respondents.
The community's social-position predicates are driven to $\mathbf{t}$ (these people are real, legitimate, historically grounded), and the [genring](teaching/explanations/genring.md) operation is applied to produce a stable genre figure — the authentic folk type whose rebirth is the solution the crisis demands.

**Enemy** ($\phi_3$): A designated enemy is constituted as the cause of the crisis and the obstacle to the community's rebirth.
The enemy's predicates are driven to $\mathbf{f}$ (inauthentic, corrupting, dangerous).
The enemy can be named by any substantive content — ethnic group, ideological formation, cosmopolitan elite — but must be structurally positioned as internal-yet-alien: not simply an external threat but a contaminating presence within the community.

**Sacrifice** ($\phi_4$): The community's response to the crisis is formatted as sacrifice: the giving up of individual comfort, safety, or life in service of the collective rebirth.
The sacrifice-predicate is driven to $\mathbf{t}$ (sacrifice is honorable, necessary, and constitutive of authentic community membership).
This stage makes violence available as an affective positive: suffering in service of the destiny is rewarded rather than condemned.

**Destiny** ($\phi_5$): The community's particular political-legitimacy predicates are converged to a fixed point: the community *will* be reborn; the destiny is given by history, nature, or divinity, not by contingent choice.
This is the stage that Griffin (1991) designates *palingenesis* — the mythic rebirth from decadence — which he identifies as fascism's structural constant across all its substantive variants.

**Inevitability** ($\phi_6$): The closure rule is completed: the destiny's achievement is not merely desirable but inevitable; resistance to it is futile; the sequence from crisis to rebirth is presented as a natural law whose operation the community is joining rather than choosing.
This is the stage Benjamin targeted: the faith that history already leans toward justice (in the liberal form) or toward the community's rebirth (in the fascist form) is the same fatalist grammar in two substantive registers.

## Liberalism's inhabitation of fascist grammar

The critical theorists (Benjamin, Arendt, Adorno) who diagnosed fascism also diagnosed liberalism's entanglement with the same grammar.
Liberalism does not escape fascist grammar by opposing it; it runs the grammar with inverted content:

- Fascism: crisis → liberalism: progressive stability (Dewey's managerial optimism)
- Fascism: organic community → liberalism: universal citizenship (Kelsen's procedural *Rechtsstaat*)
- Fascism: enemy → liberalism: toleration (Popper's *Open Society and Its Enemies*)
- Fascism: sacrifice → liberalism: compromise (Rawls's veil of ignorance)
- Fascism: destiny → liberalism: progress (incremental justice)
- Fascism: inevitability → liberalism: procedure (Berlin's value pluralism)

The predication-categories reading of this is precise: liberalism runs the morphism $\phi_F$ with the community fiber pointed at the universal citizen rather than the ethnic folk, and the destiny fiber pointed at progressive institutions rather than national rebirth, but the *morphism* is structurally the same.
The [liberalism-legitimacy-crisis](../objects/liberalism-legitimacy-crisis.md) exposes this entanglement: as liberalism loses its ability to deliver on its inverted content, the grammar's full structure becomes visible without the liberal inversion.

## Anarchist grammar as formal contrast

The CPL corpus names an anarchist grammar as the structural alternative: **condition → commons → relation → solidarity → desire → immanence**.
In predication-categories terms, anarchist grammar is a different morphism sequence that does not converge the settling toward a fixed-point destiny but toward an open-settling in which the present possibilities of relation, solidarity, and desire are the operative target.
Where fascist grammar drives affective fibers toward closure at $\mathbf{t}$ (the destiny is determined), anarchist grammar holds the settling in an open-$\bot$ orientation toward the not-yet-determined — what Arendt calls *natality*, Benjamin calls *now-time*.
The formal contrast is between a closure-converging morphism and an openness-preserving one. ---
