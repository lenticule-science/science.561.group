## Annotation

The book that introduced *cruel optimism* as a structured concept: a relation in which the object of attachment is itself an obstacle to the flourishing the attachment is meant to secure.
The attachment persists not because the attached are deluded but because losing it would mean losing the structure of desire that makes the present bearable.
The relation is what is cruel; the object's failure is downstream.

Three further concepts the library uses heavily are developed in the same book. *Crisis ordinariness* names the condition in which crisis does not arrive as rupture but persists as the ordinary texture of life. *Slow death* names the wearing-out of populations through chronic conditions (precarity, debt, pollution, obesity) that are cumulatively shortening but individually not dramatic enough to register as catastrophe. *Affective infrastructure* names the emotional and procedural frameworks through which people manage their ongoing attrition.

Berlant's prose is recursive and adverbial in a way that resists summary; the concepts above are usable extracts from a method that does its work at the sentence level.
The book reads adjacent traditions — affect theory, queer theory, neoliberalism critique, genre theory — without subordinating to any of them.
