The Combahee River Collective was a Black lesbian feminist socialist collective active in Boston from 1974 to 1980.
The collective took its name from Harriet Tubman's 1863 raid on the Combahee River, in which she led a Union Army assault that freed more than 750 enslaved people in South Carolina — the only U.S. military action in the nineteenth century planned and led by a woman.
The naming is the politics: a continuity claimed with Black women's organized resistance going back to the slavery period.

The collective's "A Black Feminist Statement," drafted principally by Barbara Smith, Beverly Smith, and Demita Frazier and circulated in 1977, is one of the founding documents of contemporary Black feminism.
Two moves in the statement are constitutive for everything the [black-feminism](../_index.md) subdomain does after it.

## What this subdomain takes from the collective

- **Interlocking systems of oppression.** The statement's central analytical move: "The most general statement of our politics at the present time would be that we are actively committed to struggling against racial, sexual, heterosexual, and class oppression, and see as our particular task the development of integrated analysis and practice based upon the fact that the major systems of oppression are interlocking.
  The synthesis of these oppressions creates the conditions of our lives."
  The word "interlocking" is doing analytical work — these are not separate oppressions that happen to coincide in the lives of Black women; they are a single structure whose operation produces the specific conditions of Black women's lives.
  The framing is upstream of [Patricia Hill Collins](patricia-hill-collins.md)'s matrix of domination, of Crenshaw's intersectionality, and of the kyriarchism analysis in the sibling [`queer/kyriarchism`](../../../../queer/theories/kyriarchism/_index.md) subdomain.
  See the term [`interlocking-systems`](../terms/interlocking-systems.md).
- **Identity politics in the non-trivial sense.** The statement: "This focusing upon our own oppression is embodied in the concept of identity politics.
  We believe that the most profound and potentially most radical politics come directly out of our own identity, as opposed to working to end somebody else's oppression."
  The phrase "identity politics" enters U.S. political vocabulary here.
  Its later reduction to a slur for "narrow group interest" is a mis-citation: the statement's argument is that politics rooted in the analysis of one's own conditions of oppression is more radical, not less, because it takes analytical responsibility for those conditions and refuses the position of solidarity-from-above.
- **The freedom of Black women as the universal case.** The statement: "If Black women were free, it would mean that everyone else would have to be free since our freedom would necessitate the destruction of all the systems of oppression."
  This is the inverse of the additive reading.
  Black women's situation is not a special case of more general oppressions; it is the case where the structure is most fully exposed, and the dismantling of the structure that produces that situation would necessarily dismantle all the systems that constitute it.
- **Black lesbian socialism.** The collective was explicitly socialist and explicitly lesbian, and made both commitments analytical rather than additive: "We realize that the liberation of all oppressed peoples necessitates the destruction of the political-economic systems of capitalism and imperialism as well as patriarchy."
  The Black feminist analysis of capitalism is one of the lineages from which the contemporary racial-capitalism literature draws.

## Key text

- "A Black Feminist Statement" (1977), reprinted widely; collected in *Capitalist Patriarchy and the Case for Socialist Feminism*, ed. Zillah Eisenstein (1978), and in *Home Girls: A Black Feminist Anthology*, ed. Barbara Smith (1983).
  Reference note: [`combahee1977`](../../../../../../references/combahee1977.md).
- Keeanga-Yamahtta Taylor, ed., *How We Get Free: Black Feminism and the Combahee River Collective* (Haymarket, 2017).
  The reception document for the statement at its fortieth anniversary; interviews with Barbara Smith, Beverly Smith, and Demita Frazier.

## Where the collective figures elsewhere

The "interlocking systems" framing is the proximate source for the [`kyriarchism`](../../../../queer/theories/kyriarchism/_index.md) subdomain's central claim.
The Combahee position on identity politics is upstream of contemporary debates about standpoint, recognition, and coalition.
