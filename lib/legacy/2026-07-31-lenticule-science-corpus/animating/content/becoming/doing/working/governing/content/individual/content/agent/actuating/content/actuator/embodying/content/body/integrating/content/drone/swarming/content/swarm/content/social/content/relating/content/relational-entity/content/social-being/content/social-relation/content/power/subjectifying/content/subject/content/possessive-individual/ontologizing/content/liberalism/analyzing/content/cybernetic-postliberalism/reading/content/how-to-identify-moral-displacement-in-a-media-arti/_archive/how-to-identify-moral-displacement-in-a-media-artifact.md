[Moral displacement](../teaching/explanations/moral-displacement.md) is the operation by which accountability for structural conditions is moved from the structural to the personal.
Identifying it in a specific media artifact requires distinguishing between representations that accurately describe personal moral responsibility and representations that perform displacement — locating accountability at the personal level when the conditions that produce harm are structural.

The distinction is not always easy.
Personal moral responsibility is real; structural conditions are also real; a sophisticated representation may accurately account for both.
The analytical task is not to find the representation of personal moral responsibility and call it displacement — that would falsify the concept.
The task is to identify the specific representational moves through which the attribution of accountability is shifted away from structural causes that are present in the artifact's world and toward individual agents who are not its principal determinants.

## Step 1: Identify the structural conditions in the artifact's world

Before identifying displacement, you need to identify what structural conditions the artifact's world contains that might be displaced.
These may be:

- Explicit structural conditions (the artifact acknowledges climate collapse, institutional failure, economic inequality, or other structural harms)
- Implicit structural conditions (the narrative premises involve conditions that are structurally determined even if not named as such — the character's precarious employment is the artifact's premise, but the structural conditions of labor markets are not addressed)
- Background structural conditions (the artifact's world contains structural conditions that a sociologically informed viewer recognizes even if the artifact does not name them)

For each structural condition you identify, note: **who is structurally accountable for this condition?** Not who the artifact presents as accountable — who is actually accountable in a structural analysis.
Institutions, systems, historical decisions, economic structures, legal arrangements, and political configurations are the usual candidates.

## Step 2: Identify how the artifact distributes accountability

Read the artifact for its explicit and implicit distribution of moral accountability.
What responses to the structural conditions does the artifact recommend, model, authorize, or valorize?
What kinds of agents are represented as the appropriate locus of accountability?
What kinds of responses are represented as the appropriate resolution?

Pay particular attention to:

- The protagonist's response to structural conditions (personal cultivation, individual action, relational repair, professional responsibility)
- The artifact's narrative resolution (is the condition resolved, managed, or accepted?
  By whom and through what mechanism?)
- The affect the artifact assigns to the conditions (guilt and shame, which locate accountability personally; indignation and outrage, which can locate accountability structurally; grief, which can go either way; determination, which depends on what action is valorized)
- The agency the artifact assigns to its characters (do characters have structural agency — capacity to transform the structural conditions — or only personal agency?)

## Step 3: Test for displacement

Compare step 1 (structural accountability) with step 2 (the artifact's distributed accountability).
Displacement is occurring when:

**The artifact locates accountability personally for conditions whose structural causation is identifiable and omitted.** The wellness advertisement that represents personal self-care as the response to conditions of institutional burnout is displacing accountability: the institutional conditions are the structural cause, but the advertisement locates accountability in the individual's self-care practice.

**The artifact's resolution leaves structural conditions intact.** A narrative that resolves through personal change (the protagonist becomes more resilient, more empathetic, more aware) without addressing the structural conditions that the protagonist's story has revealed is performing displacement: the narrative's resolution is personal even though the narrative's content has implicated structural causes.

**The artifact valorizes personal accountability under conditions of [structural determination](../teaching/explanations/structural-determination.md).** When the artifact's world contains conditions in which individual outcomes are substantially determined by structural position — and the artifact valorizes personal effort, choice, or virtue as the appropriate response — the valorization is displacing accountability from the structural conditions that the artifact's own world reveals.

**The artifact uses moral language to individualize structural harm.** Representations that use the vocabulary of personal failure (irresponsibility, poor choices, lack of character) to describe outcomes that are structurally produced are explicitly performing displacement in the moral vocabulary.

## Step 4: Assess the displacement's operation

Not all moral displacement in an artifact has the same political function.
Assess:

**Is the displacement serving [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md)?** Does the displacement route structural contradiction into personal affect in a way that metabolizes the contradiction rather than addressing it?
The artifact that represents ecological collapse through a personal-renewal narrative is performing californication through moral displacement.

**Is the displacement producing a [savior-slave subject](../teaching/explanations/savior-slave-subject.md)?** Does the displaced accountability land on a subject who is simultaneously tasked with managing the structural condition and structurally incapable of transforming it?
The nonprofit-worker narrative that represents the professional-helper as personally responsible for conditions they cannot structurally transform is producing the savior-slave subject through moral displacement.

**Is the displacement operating through genre?** Does the displacement work through the artifact's genre conventions — through the narrative logic of the hero story, the self-help text's structural requirement that the protagonist transform personally, the news story's individualization of structural phenomena through its requirement for human-interest protagonists?
Genre-mediated displacement is particularly important to identify because it operates below the level of the artifact's explicit content.

## What the analysis does not establish

Moral displacement analysis identifies a representational operation.
It does not establish that:

- The persons represented as personally accountable are *not* personally accountable (they may be; personal accountability is compatible with structural accountability, and the analysis of displacement should not deny the former)
- The artifact's producers are acting in bad faith (moral displacement can occur through sincere belief in personal accountability)
- The artifact should be avoided or condemned (analytical identification of a representational operation is not a verdict on the artifact's value)

The analysis establishes that a specific representational operation is running in the artifact and identifies what political-affective work that operation performs.
The conclusions drawn from this establishment are a further step that requires additional analysis.
