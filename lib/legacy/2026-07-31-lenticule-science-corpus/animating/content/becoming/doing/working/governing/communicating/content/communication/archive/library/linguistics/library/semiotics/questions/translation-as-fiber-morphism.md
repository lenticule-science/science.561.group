# Is translation a substrate federate under explicit identity-mapping, or a genuinely different morphism?

The discipline's [translation](../morphisms/translation.md) note commits provisionally to reading translation as the substrate's [federate](../../../../information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/morphisms/federate.md) operation under an explicit identity-mapping. The commitment is plausible but not yet established.

## Statement

> Is the translation operation between two semiotic systems faithfully captured by the substrate's federate operation under an explicit cross-system identity-mapping, or does translation introduce structural content that federate does not capture?

## Partial decomposition

- **(P1) Faithfulness direction.** Does federate-with-identity-mapping reproduce *every* feature of translation, including translation's cultural-historical asymmetries (Lotman's core-periphery)?
- **(P2) The "new sign-relations" question.** Translation generates *new* coded-pairings in the federated system that did not exist in either source (per the translation note's O3). Federate also generates new substrate-cells via the identity-mapping. Are these the same generation, or does translation's cultural creativity exceed what federate's mathematical operation produces?
- **(P3) Translatability vs. federability.** Federate requires only an identity-mapping; in principle any two scopes can be federated under an arbitrary identity-mapping, however lossy. Translation, in working semiotic practice, is *limited* — some sign-relations resist translation in any sense. The question: is untranslatability the same as the federate-mapping being undefined or trivial, or is it a stronger structural fact?

## What is established

- The substrate-level reading gives a clean account of translation's identity-mapping, contestation handling ($\top$-pressure for incompatible interpretants), and non-destructiveness; these match working translation practice in non-trivial ways.

## What remains open

- The full faithfulness of the identification (P1, P2).
- The structural status of untranslatability (P3) — whether it is a substrate-level constraint or a contingent practice-level limitation.
