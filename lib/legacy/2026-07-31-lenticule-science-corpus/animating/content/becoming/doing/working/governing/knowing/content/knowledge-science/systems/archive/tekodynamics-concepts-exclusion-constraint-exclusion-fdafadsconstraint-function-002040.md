## Definitions

∅ is exclusion: the condition of total relational absence.  
κ is collapse: the constraint that emerges when exclusion cannot persist.

## Theorem

Exclusion excludes all relation — including with itself.  
This makes exclusion inadmissible within its own condition.  
That inadmissibility induces collapse.

Collapse is not imposed upon exclusion.  
It arises from the contradiction exclusion generates  
by being unable to sustain even its own exclusion.

This contradiction is κ:  
a recursive constraint that eliminates all that cannot persist.

## Formal Expression

∅ ∉ ∅ ⇔ κ

## Consequence

Collapse is not the negation of exclusion.  
It is the necessary result of exclusion’s self-inconsistency.  
From this, the remainder R will emerge —  
but κ is the first real constraint.

All future structure is shaped by this impossibility:  
exclusion fails to exclude itself, and collapses into constraint.

## Scope

This theorem defines the base condition of collapse dynamics:  
that exclusion is unstable,  
and collapse is the recursive resolution of that instability.
