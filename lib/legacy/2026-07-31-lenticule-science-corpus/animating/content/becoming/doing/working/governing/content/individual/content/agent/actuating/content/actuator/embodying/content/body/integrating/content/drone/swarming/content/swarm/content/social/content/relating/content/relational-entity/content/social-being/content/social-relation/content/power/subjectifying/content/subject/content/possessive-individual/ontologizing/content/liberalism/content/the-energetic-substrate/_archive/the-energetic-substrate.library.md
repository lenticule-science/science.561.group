Predicated sociology presupposes a material claim about declining net energy.
The claim is not internal to the theory; it lives in the energy-systems literature, with its own methods and peer-review network.
Predicated sociology's relation to that literature is one of presupposition: if the claim is roughly right, the account of why [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md) has intensified now — rather than being one possible social formation among many — follows.
If the claim turns out to be wrong, much of predicated sociology's specific historicization would need to be rebuilt.

What follows establishes the energetic claim as it lives in the energy-systems literature, distinguishes it from predicated sociology proper, and explains why it matters for understanding how the operations this theory traces — [genring](../teaching/explanations/genring.md), [ontogenring](../teaching/explanations/ontogenring.md), [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md), [platformal liberalism](../teaching/explanations/platformal-liberalism.md) — have intensified under contemporary conditions in ways their prior institutional forms did not require.

## What the claim is

*Net energy* names the energy a society has available for use after subtracting the energy required to produce it.
The standard quantitative measure is *EROEI* — energy return on energy invested — the ratio of energy delivered to energy expended in delivering it.
An EROEI of 20:1 means that for every unit of energy spent extracting, refining, and distributing energy, twenty units are delivered to the rest of the economy.

The claim has three components, each independently established in the literature.

**Fossil-fuel EROEI has declined substantially.** Conventional oil at the wellhead in the 1930s-40s ran around 50:1; the wellhead figure today, across major fields, is in the range of 18–43:1.
But the wellhead figure overstates the energy actually available for societal use.
Brockway et al. (2024) measure later stages and find that *useful-stage* global fossil-fuel EROEI is now approximately 3.5:1.

**There is a net-energy cliff below which complex society is structurally unsustainable.** The fraction of total energy available to society after subtracting energy reinvested in the energy system is given by *(EROEI − 1) / EROEI*.
The function is hyperbolic.
Below 5:1, small declines in EROEI produce large losses in net energy.
Hall (2014) argues that complex society probably cannot function below approximately 5:1 across its entire energy basis.
Global fossil fuels at useful stage are already at 3.5:1.

**Renewable replacements do not, on current evidence, fund a return to the prior surplus.** The system-level question depends on storage, grid, intermittency-buffering, and the substantial reinvestment required to build renewable infrastructure on a fossil-fuel-shrinking economy.

The claim is not that energy is running out in the simple-exhaustion sense.
It is that the *net* energy available to society — the surplus that can fund activities other than running the energy system itself — has been contracting and is now below the levels at which the historical complexity of the contemporary apparatus was constructed.

## Why the claim sits outside predicated sociology

Predicated sociology is a sociology, not an energy-systems analysis.
Its contribution does not depend on its competence in measuring fossil-fuel net-yield curves; the measurement is done by physicists, geologists, and energy economists working in their own institutions.
Predicated sociology takes the measurement as input.

If the energy-systems literature converges on a different consensus, the specific historicization would need to be rebuilt.
Predicated sociology does not preempt the empirical question; it presupposes the empirical answer the energy-systems literature currently supports and is honest about what would change if that answer changes.

## Why the claim matters

[Californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md) — the operation by which structural contradictions are formatted as personally manageable affect, with the resulting self-regulation functioning as the governance mechanism — is recurrent across the modern pastoral apparatus.
What is distinctive about the present is not that the operation exists but that it has *intensified* and *redistributed onto bodies and platforms* in a particular way.
The energetic substrate is what makes the difference legible.

The medieval pastoral apparatus operated on a thin energetic substrate but a rich institutional substrate: stone buildings lasting six centuries, multi-generational vocational training, slow-cycling oral traditions.
The twentieth-century apparatus operated on the historical-high energetic substrate.
The expansion of the modern state, the construction of mass-media broadcasting, the development of welfare-state institutional interiors, the cultivation of a large reflexive professional class — all funded by surplus that paid for what Illich (1974) analyzed as high-energy social forms: think tanks, universities, regulatory agencies, public broadcasting, slow journalism, planning institutions, archives.

The contemporary apparatus operates on a contracted substrate.
The institutional interiors have thinned.
What replaces them is *platformal* infrastructure: feedback architectures that operate at planetary scale, with low marginal energetic cost per interaction, but with structurally different institutional properties.
The slow buffer is gone; the metabolism that used to run inside institutional walls now runs through bodies and feeds.

The metabolism has redistributed onto bodies and platforms *because* the energetic surplus that funded richer institutional interiors has contracted.
This redistribution is not an accident, an ideological choice, or a conspiracy.
It is a configuration the substrate can fund and the prior configuration cannot.

## What follows from the substrate's contraction

**Compression of timescales.** The slow institutional interior allowed for analysis, deliberation, planning, and pastoral patience that took years to unfold.
Platformal infrastructure operates at second-by-second timescales.
When the apparatus loses the institutional buffer, the affective and cognitive timescales available for processing structural contradictions compress correspondingly.
This is what subjects experience as [saturation](../teaching/explanations/saturation.md), the condition preceding [destabilization](../teaching/explanations/destabilization.md).

**Loss of protected interiors.** The post-WWII journalist's editorial independence, the regulatory agency's deliberative chamber — these were institutionalized interiors within which decisions could be made and information held.
The contemporary platformal apparatus has no equivalent: every datum is in principle harvestable, every utterance in principle re-circulable.
This structural condition produces the [neurotic platformal intellectual](../teaching/explanations/neurotic-platformal-intellectual.md) and the [coherent confusion](../teaching/explanations/coherent-confusion.md) that follows.

**Distributed metabolism.** Because no central institution carries the metabolism, the metabolism runs through whatever can carry affect.
Bodies become the metabolizing surface.
[Californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md) names exactly this redistribution: contradictions that were once buffered upstream now arrive directly at the level of personal affect, and the self-regulation by which subjects manage them is itself the governance the apparatus requires.
The subject this produces is the [savior-slave subject](../teaching/explanations/savior-slave-subject.md): personally responsible for managing structural crises, structurally incapable of transforming the conditions that produce them.
