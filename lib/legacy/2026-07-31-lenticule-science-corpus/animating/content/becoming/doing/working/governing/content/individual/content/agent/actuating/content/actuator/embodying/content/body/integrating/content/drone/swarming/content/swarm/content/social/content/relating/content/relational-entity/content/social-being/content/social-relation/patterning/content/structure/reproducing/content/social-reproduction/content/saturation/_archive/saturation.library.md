**Saturation** is the systemic condition in which a social settling's contradiction-absorption capacity is exceeded.
The settling has been operating under closure rules that route contradictions into personal affect, deferral, or institutional management; saturation is the point at which those routings stop converging — where the proportion of overdetermined fibers (those holding $\top$, simultaneously confirmed and denied) exceeds the system's restabilization rate.

In predication-categories terms: a social settling $\mathcal{S}$ over entity set $E$ and predicate category $\mathcal{P}$ is *saturated* when the density of $\top$-valued fibers across the socially salient predicate dimensions exceeds the rate at which the operative closure rules can resolve them back toward $\mathbf{t}$ or $\mathbf{f}$.
The settling continues to iterate — contradictions keep arriving — but each iteration adds more overdetermination than the closure rules remove.
The system is still running; it is losing ground.

## Relation to affect-governance

The [late-liberal social base](../objects/late-liberal-social-base.md) operates through [affect-governance](affect-governance.md): structural contradictions are formatted into personal affect and routed away from institutional surfaces.
This routing has a capacity.
Californication's core mechanism — converting structural incoherence into self-regulatory behavior — requires that the subject remain functional: confused but still processing, distressed but still participating.
Saturation is what happens when the formatting load exceeds that capacity.
The subject cannot absorb the next contradiction; the platform cannot generate enough genre-compliant content to metabolize the arriving volume; the institutional feedback layers fall behind the perturbation rate.

The [cybernetic postliberalism](../../cybernetic-postliberalism/) literature names several empirical markers of saturation: the proliferation of self-help genres that increasingly contradict one another without any of them delivering resolution; the acceleration of news-cycle throughput past the interpretive capacities of the subject; the exhaustion of the therapeutic infrastructure by demand it cannot meet; the collapse of the affective economy's ability to produce positive-affect stickiness for normative objects (career, family, civic participation).
Each is a local symptom of the same underlying dynamics: the closure rules are falling behind.

## Regime-theoretic characterization

On the predication-categories substrate, saturation marks a phase transition in settling regime.
Below the saturation threshold the settling operates in the *strict regime*: contradictions introduced at time $t$ are resolved by time $t+k$ for some bounded $k$.
At the saturation threshold the settling enters an *aperiodic regime*: resolution time becomes unbounded, and new contradictions arrive faster than old ones clear.
The saturated settling is not broken — it continues to iterate — but it is no longer restabilizing.
[Destabilization](destabilization.md) is the social-phenomenological name for what this transition feels like to subjects embedded in the saturated settling.

The saturation threshold is not fixed.
It depends on: - The variety of closure rules available (Ashby's requisite variety: a richer rule-set can absorb more contradiction) - The rate of contradiction-production (which the late-liberal formation has been increasing through platform-mediated engagement) - The affective reserve of subject populations (which is depleted by sustained contradiction-processing)

The late-liberal trajectory is toward saturation because the rate of contradiction-production grows with platform-mediated engagement while the affective reserve is finite and the closure-rule variety is constrained by the formation's need to produce genre-coherent subjects.
