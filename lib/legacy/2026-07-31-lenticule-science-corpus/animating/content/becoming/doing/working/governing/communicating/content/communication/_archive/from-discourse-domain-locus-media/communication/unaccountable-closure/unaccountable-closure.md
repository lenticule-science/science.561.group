Unaccountable closure is the central defect the framework names. An artifact has an unaccountable closure when it asks its reader to cross a gap that the reader has no principled way to cross — no shared apparatus, no audience-licensed competence, no cited authority, no explicit reasoning chain. The artifact treats the closure as if it were [acceptable](acceptable-gap.md) but has not actually licensed it.

The reader who hits an unaccountable closure has three options, all of them costly:

1. **Guess.** Supply some completion that seems plausible and continue. The artifact has now communicated whatever the reader's guess produced, which may not be what the author intended.
2. **Stop.** Refuse to continue without resolution. The communication has failed.
3. **Reverse-engineer.** Try to recover what the author must have meant by working from later passages backward. This succeeds only if the artifact later constrains its earlier closures, and even then is expensive.

None of these are good outcomes for the artifact. All of them produce communication that is unverifiable: the reader cannot tell whether the closure they performed is the one the author would have endorsed, and the author has no way to be challenged about what they actually meant.

## Why it matters

A great deal of communicative malpractice consists of unaccountable closures dressed as confident assertion. The lecture that says "obviously" before a step that is not obvious. The proof that says "WLOG" before a step that does not actually generalize. The interface tutorial that says "configure your environment" without specifying what configuration. The policy document that says "in accordance with normal procedures" without specifying which procedures. In each case, the artifact is leaning on a closure it has not earned.

## Distinguishing unaccountable closure from honest gaps

A gap is not unaccountable simply because it is hard. Hard but disciplined gaps — closures that take the reader genuine work to perform but that the reader is licensed and equipped to perform — are the substance of demanding communicative practice. The defect is specifically *unaccountable*: closure for which no account can be given by either author or reader of why this completion rather than another is the right one.

The repair is one of: cite the apparatus, cite the cited authority, write out the step, narrow the audience claim, or drop the closure. The repair is never to ignore the gap.
