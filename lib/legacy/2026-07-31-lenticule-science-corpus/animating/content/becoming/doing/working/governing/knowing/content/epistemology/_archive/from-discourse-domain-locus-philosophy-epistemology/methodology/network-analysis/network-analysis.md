# Network analysis

*The method family that represents social structure as a graph of nodes and edges and analyzes the resulting graph — its degree distributions, cohesion, centrality, structural equivalence, community structure, dynamics — to explain social outcomes by relational position rather than by node attributes.*

The method has classical roots in Moreno's sociometry (1934) and in Radcliffe-Brown's structural anthropology, but the modern form was consolidated in the 1970s by Harrison White and the Harvard structural school, formalized algorithmically through the 1980s and 1990s, and then transformed by the network-science wave (Newman, Barabási, Watts, Strogatz) that brought computational and physics methods to bear on very large empirical networks.

What the method does. Constructs a network from data (relations of friendship, kinship, citation, trade, migration, hyperlinks, communication, co-authorship). Computes structural measures: degree, betweenness, closeness, and other centralities; clustering and transitivity; community detection; structural equivalence and blockmodeling. Tests structural hypotheses: that position predicts outcome, that diffusion follows specific paths, that brokers between weakly-connected clusters reap returns to their position (Burt's structural holes). Models dynamics: how networks change, how diffusion propagates through them, how strategic action shapes their evolution.

The method has been used across substantive sociology — labor markets (Granovetter), elite cohesion (Mintz and Schwartz on interlocking directorates, Domhoff on the power elite), social movements (Diani, Tindall), inter-organizational fields (Powell, Padgett), kinship and friendship, and the analysis of platform-mediated communication.

Limits and critiques. Network data is partial — what gets recorded as a tie is a methodological choice with substantive consequences. Static networks miss dynamics; dynamic networks introduce hard estimation problems. Treating a tie as an undifferentiated edge can erase the qualitative differences between, say, a friendship and an obligation. Critical sociologies have pressed: who gets to count as a node, whose ties are made visible, and which structural properties get optimized are political questions, especially in the platform era where the network of social life is increasingly the network read by corporate measurement.

The method and the [network](../network.md) concept developed together; in practice, they are not separable.
