# Inconsistency Latency

Inconsistency latency is the time or process distance between a descent-failed witness propagation and the team reaching a resolved or acknowledged representative cell-pattern. It measures how long a non-gluing witness class remains operationally live.

The source theorem [Witness-network propagation as fibration-comonad descent](/library/social-sciences/economics/work/archive/witness-network-propagation-as-fibration-comonad-descent.md) identifies this with the decay rate of a first cohomology class under adjudication operations. Low latency means the team quickly collapses or stabilizes the inconsistency; high latency means the team operates with persistent unresolved witness divergence.

This term connects to [Organizations as bilattice network sheaves](/library/social-sciences/economics/work/archive/organizations-as-bilattice-network-sheaves.md), where disagreement is localizable to consistency-failure cells rather than treated as vague social friction.

## Use

Use this term when evaluating agent teams, review workflows, audits, or governance systems. The question is not merely whether disagreement exists, but how quickly and by what adjudication mode its obstruction class is resolved, preserved, or made actionable.
