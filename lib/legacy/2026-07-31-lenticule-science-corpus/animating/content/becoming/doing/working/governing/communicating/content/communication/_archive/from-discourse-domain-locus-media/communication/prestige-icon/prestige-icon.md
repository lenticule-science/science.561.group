A prestige icon is an [icon](teaching/explanations/icon.md) that has been chosen because of what it signals about the writer's affiliation rather than because of what it accomplishes for the reader. The icon is doing prestige work, not compression work. The reader is meant to recognize that the writer belongs to the community where this icon circulates, not to recover the structure the icon was originally introduced to compress.

The diagnostic test is whether the icon's omission would impair the artifact's communicative work. A genuinely operative icon — a commutative diagram, a circuit symbol, a pictogram on a sign — *carries* something: replacing it with prose would balloon the page. A prestige icon does not carry. Replacing it with plain prose would make the artifact clearer. The icon is there because the community uses it, and the artifact uses it to display membership.

## Where it appears

In academic writing: technical notation invoked beyond what the argument requires, gesturing at a school's vocabulary without using its tools. In product communication: jargon and tooling references that signal "we are real engineers" but that the document's task does not need. In design: visual conventions borrowed from a higher-prestige design tradition without the structural justification those conventions had in their original context. In policy writing: legalistic notation deployed where plain language would do, signaling "this document is serious" rather than disambiguating its commitments.

The prestige icon is parasitic on the work that real icons perform. Communities trust icons because operative icons have, historically, paid their way. The prestige icon spends that trust without contributing to it.

## The diagnostic move

An audit for prestige icons asks of every icon in the artifact: what would replace it, in plain register, if it were removed? If the answer is "the same content, slightly longer but readable," the icon is not paying for itself and the artifact owes its readers a justification for its presence. If the answer is "a wall of dense prose nobody could follow," the icon is doing real work — it is achieving [icon-amplification](icon-amplification.md), which is what icons are for.
