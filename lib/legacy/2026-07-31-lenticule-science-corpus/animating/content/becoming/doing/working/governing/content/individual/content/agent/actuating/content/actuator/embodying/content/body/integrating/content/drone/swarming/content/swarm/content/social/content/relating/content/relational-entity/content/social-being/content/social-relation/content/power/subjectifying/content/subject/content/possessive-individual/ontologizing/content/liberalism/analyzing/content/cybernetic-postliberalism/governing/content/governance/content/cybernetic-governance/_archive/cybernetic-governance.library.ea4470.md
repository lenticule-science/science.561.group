Governance under late-liberal conditions is *cybernetic* in the strict technical sense of the term: it operates through continuous feedback loops rather than through command, coercion, or ideology, maintaining stability by adjusting to deviations rather than preventing them.
This is what distinguishes late-liberal governance from its prior institutional forms and what explains features of the contemporary situation that prior frameworks for understanding governance — disciplinary, ideological, juridical — incompletely specify.

## Governance as feedback

Classical liberal governance theory is juridical: it understands governance as the specification and enforcement of rules.
The sovereign commands; the law forbids and permits; the subject is juridically free within the permitted space.
Foucault's *Discipline and Punish* (1977) broke from the juridical model to describe governance as *disciplinary*: it operates not through explicit rules but through the organization of space, time, and body such that subjects normalize themselves to what the apparatus requires.
Deleuze's "Postscript on the Societies of Control" (1992) extended the analysis: disciplinary society had discrete enclosures (school, factory, prison) with explicit transitional moments; the control society operates through continuous modulation, with no discrete transitions and no discrete enclosures.

Cybernetic governance is the analytical specification of what the control society is, precisely.
Wiener's cybernetics provides the conceptual apparatus: a cybernetic system maintains its state through *negative feedback* — it detects deviation from a desired state, generates a corrective response, and returns toward the desired state.
The system does not need to prevent deviation; it needs only to correct for deviation after it occurs.
The correction is distributed across the system's components; no central command is required.

Applied to governance: the late-liberal apparatus maintains its state — the subjective metabolism of structural contradiction through personal self-regulation — through feedback loops that detect deviation and generate corrective responses.
The deviation is the subject's potential response to structural contradiction that would not metabolize through californication: political mobilization that would address conditions structurally, organized collective action that would transform the apparatus's operations, forms of relation that would operate outside the apparatus's legibility circuits.
The corrective responses are the apparatus's genre-routing, requisite-variety provision, and affective-genre enforcement.

## What cybernetic governance looks like at scale

At the level of the platform: the recommendation algorithm is a feedback system.
It detects the user's response to content (engagement, share, time-on-screen, negative signals), adjusts what content is served, detects the response to the adjusted content, and iterates.
The governance is the algorithm's continuous adjustment; there is no point at which the algorithm commands anything or forbids anything.
The subject is structurally free to scroll, click, share, or log off; the algorithm adjusts to whatever the subject does, and the adjustment is the governance.

At the level of the professional: the platform's feeling rules — the affective norms that govern what can be said in what register — are also feedback-governed.
A post that violates the feeling rules generates negative response signals (ratio, mockery, callout); the subject adjusts their subsequent posts; the adjustment is the governance.
No authority needs to issue a directive; the feedback is sufficient.

At the level of community: the community's accountability norms are feedback-governed.
A member who violates a norm is responded to with the community's accountability process; the member adjusts their behavior or is removed; the process is the governance.
The norms themselves are maintained through continuous feedback: a community that enforces a norm too weakly watches the norm erode; a community that enforces too strongly watches members defect.
The equilibrium is maintained through feedback.

## Why cybernetic governance does not require coordination

This is the key structural point distinguishing cybernetic governance from conspiracy analysis.
The apparatus's coherence is not the product of coordination among powerful actors who share a plan.
No one is running the system.
The coherence is the product of *structural alignment*: the platform's economic incentives align with the feedback architecture that metabolizes structural contradiction; the professional's reputational incentives align with the feeling rules that calibrate affect; the community's membership-maintenance incentives align with the accountability norms that reproduce the confessional structure.

These alignments do not require anyone to intend them.
They are the emergent product of each actor following their local incentives within a structural context that makes certain configurations stable.
Cybernetics provides the theoretical apparatus for analyzing this without residue — without requiring the conspiracy inference.

## The affective-governance mechanism

[Zen fascism](../teaching/explanations/zen-fascism.md) is the cybernetic-governance mechanism for affective regulation.
The serenity-distress sorting operation is a feedback system: the distressed subject generates negative signals (it makes others uncomfortable, it produces disengagement, it disrupts the apparatus's authorized affective register); the subject receives corrective feedback (offered therapy, managed through the accountability process, encouraged toward inner work); the subject adjusts their affective expression or is sorted out of the space.
No authority needs to command serenity; the feedback is sufficient.

[Harm governance](../teaching/explanations/harm-governance.md) is the cybernetic-governance mechanism for relational regulation.
The harm-governance apparatus generates a feedback signal for any relational act that deviates from the harm-reduction norm: it is identified as a harm, processed through an accountability mechanism, and responded to with remediation.
The subject adjusts their relational practice or is removed from the community.
No authority needs to command the harm-free relationship; the feedback is sufficient.

## What cybernetic governance cannot govern

Cybernetic governance governs through feedback loops that require *legible signals*.
The feedback mechanism needs to detect deviation in order to generate a corrective response.
If the deviation is not legible — if it does not produce a detectable signal — the feedback mechanism cannot respond.

This is the structural limitation of cybernetic governance.
Practices that operate outside the legibility circuits — that do not produce engagement signals, that are maintained in face-to-face relations that do not generate digital exhaust, that are embedded in ceremonial obligations or ancestral relations that the apparatus cannot read — are structurally invisible to the cybernetic governance mechanism.
They are not thereby protected from all governance; they may be governed by other mechanisms.
But they are outside the specific governance mechanism that cybernetic governance provides.

This is one of the structural openings that predicated sociology leaves for practice that does not reproduce the apparatus.
It is not a blueprint; it is a structural description of where the feedback mechanism's legibility limits are.
