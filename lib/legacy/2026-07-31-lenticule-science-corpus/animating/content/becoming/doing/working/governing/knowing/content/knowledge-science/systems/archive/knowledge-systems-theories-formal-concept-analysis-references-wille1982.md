## Annotation

The founding paper of formal concept analysis. Wille's central proposal is to read lattice theory through the lens of Aristotelian *concept hierarchies* — concepts paired by extension and intension, ordered by sub-concept-of — and to take the Galois connection between $\mathcal{P}(G)$ and $\mathcal{P}(M)$ induced by a binary incidence as the canonical mechanism producing such hierarchies.

The paper introduces:

- The **formal context** $(G, M, I)$ as the substrate.
- The **derivation operators** $({-})'$ and the induced Galois connection.
- The **formal concept** as a closed pair $(A, B)$ with $A' = B, B' = A$.
- The **concept lattice** $\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{K})$ ordered by extent inclusion.
- The basic theorem characterising concept lattices as the complete lattices.

The paper also stakes the lineage's *philosophical* position: lattice theory should be done *constructively*, with each lattice element interpretable as a concept of an underlying empirical context. This is the methodological commitment that distinguishes FCA from purely-algebraic lattice theory and from purely-empirical clustering: the formal concept is held *in reciprocal balance* between extension and intension by the Galois closure.

For the predicated framing, Wille's proposal is the historical antecedent of the substrate's commitment that the unit of stored knowledge is a relation (the predication-cell, generalising Wille's incidence) carrying Galois-style closure structure (generalising Wille's derivation operators) producing a fixed-point structure (generalising Wille's concept lattice).
