# Norm revision as cocompletion of the closure-fixed-point lattice

A **norm revision** — the substrate-algebraic content of *"a polity adds, removes, or modifies a normative cell, and the resulting normative system is the new closure of the modified cell-set"* — presents as a **cocompletion of the closure-fixed-point lattice** of the polity's [normative system](../objects/normative-system.md).
Adding a norm $n$ to the system $\mathcal{N}$ produces the new closure-fixed-point lattice $\mathrm{Fix}(C_{\mathcal{N} \cup \{n\}})$ — the cocompletion of $\mathrm{Fix}(C_\mathcal{N})$ along the cell $n$, computed by re-running the closure-2-monad on the augmented cell-set.

By [closure-fixed-points-priestley-correspondence](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-fixed-points-priestley-correspondence.md), the closure-fixed-point lattices of a closure system are in Priestley correspondence with their dual posets — so norm revision induces a corresponding transformation on the dual Priestley space.
By [closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md), the closure 2-monad is lax-idempotent, so re-applying closure to an already-closed system is the identity at the 1-categorical level — a consistency property the revision operation respects.
By [closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits.md), the **internal closure-iteration of a single revision** (closing-and-re-closing the augmented cell-set under fixed closure rules) cannot stabilize at a non-trivial periodic orbit — each individual revision reaches a fixed-point in finite or transfinite time.
Whether a *sequence of distinct revisions* (each potentially changing the closure rules or generators) can periodically cycle is a non-autonomous dynamics question to which the cited theorem does **not** apply.

The substantive claim is that **the practitioner-recognized phenomena of "norm conflict on revision," "revision cascade," "stable revised system," and "legislative gridlock" are precisely the phenomena of cocompletion-non-trivial-overlap, closure-cascade-iteration, fixed-point-convergence, and non-convergence in the closure-2-monad** — and that these are operationally diagnosable from the structure of the closure-fixed-point lattice rather than from the surface form of the revision.

## Setup: charter, normative system, closure operator

Per [charter-as-closure-system](charter-as-closure-system.md), a polity's charter presents as a closure system: a closure operator $C : 2^{\mathcal{E}} \to 2^{\mathcal{E}}$ on the polity's normative cell-universe $\mathcal{E}$ that satisfies idempotence ($C \circ C = C$), monotonicity ($A \subseteq B \Rightarrow C(A) \subseteq C(B)$), and extensiveness ($A \subseteq C(A)$).
The **normative system** $\mathcal{N}$ is the closed set $C(\mathcal{N}_0)$ generated by the polity's charter-axiom cell-set $\mathcal{N}_0$.

The **closure-fixed-point lattice** $\mathrm{Fix}(C)$ is the lattice of $C$-closed subsets of $\mathcal{E}$, ordered by inclusion.
By [closure-fixed-points-priestley-correspondence](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-fixed-points-priestley-correspondence.md), $\mathrm{Fix}(C)$ is a Priestley space dual to a corresponding bounded distributive lattice; the duality gives both a topological and an algebraic handle on the structure of normative systems.

A **norm revision** is the operation of replacing $\mathcal{N}_0$ with a modified cell-set $\mathcal{N}_0' = (\mathcal{N}_0 \setminus R) \cup A$ where $R$ is the *removed* cells (typically a small subset of axioms or precedents being repealed) and $A$ is the *added* cells (new axioms, new precedents, new statutory provisions).
The revised normative system is $\mathcal{N}' = C(\mathcal{N}_0')$.

## Statement

**Theorem (norm revision as cocompletion).** *Let $\mathcal{N} = C(\mathcal{N}_0)$ be a polity's normative system and $\mathrm{Fix}(C_\mathcal{N})$ its closure-fixed-point lattice.
Suppose a revision $\mathcal{N}_0' = (\mathcal{N}_0 \setminus R) \cup A$ is proposed.
Then:*

(a) **(Pure addition is cocompletion.)** *If $R = \emptyset$ — the revision is a pure addition — the revised system $\mathcal{N}' = C(\mathcal{N}_0 \cup A)$ presents as the **cocompletion** of $\mathrm{Fix}(C_\mathcal{N})$ along the new generators $A$.
Operationally: re-run the closure-2-monad with the augmented cell-set; the colimit of the closure-iteration is the new fixed-point lattice.
Per [closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md), this colimit is well-defined and stable under further closure application.*

(b) **(Cascade-iteration measures revision impact.)** *The number of closure-iteration steps required for the cocompletion to stabilize is a measurable invariant of the revision: a **revision cascade depth**.
Revisions with cascade depth 0 are inert (the new generators were already in $\mathcal{N}$); cascade depth 1 adds only the immediate consequences of $A$; deeper cascades trigger consequences-of-consequences.
The cascade depth is bounded by the chain length of $\mathrm{Fix}(C_\mathcal{N})$ above the level of $A$.*

(c) **(Pure removal is order-ideal collapse.)** *If $A = \emptyset$ — the revision is a pure removal — the revised system $\mathcal{N}' = C(\mathcal{N}_0 \setminus R)$ presents as the **order-ideal collapse** of $\mathrm{Fix}(C_\mathcal{N})$ that removes $R$ and all closure-consequences of $R$ that are not also closure-consequences of $\mathcal{N}_0 \setminus R$.
The dual Priestley space loses the closed-set elements corresponding to the removed and orphaned closure-classes.*

(d) **(Mixed revision is composition with potential conflict.)** *A mixed revision $\mathcal{N}_0' = (\mathcal{N}_0 \setminus R) \cup A$ presents as the composition of (c) with (a): collapse along $R$, then cocompletion along $A$. **Norm conflict on revision** is the configuration in which this composition does not commute with the alternative composition (cocompletion-then-collapse) — equivalently, when $A$ contains cells that re-derive (some of) the removed $R$ via closure.
The conflict is operationally significant because the order of (c) and (a) can produce different revised systems.*

(e) **(Single-revision iteration converges; multi-revision sequences have no such guarantee.)** *Iterating the **single closure operator** $C_{\mathcal{N}'}$ (after a fixed cocompletion) — i.e., asking whether closing-and-re-closing the augmented cell-set stabilizes — converges to a fixed point in finite or transfinite time, by [closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits.md): a single inflationary monotone idempotent closure operator has no non-trivial periodic orbits.
This guarantees that **each individual revision** (per (a)) terminates and produces a well-defined revised system.
However, the cited theorem does **not** apply to a **revision sequence** $\mathcal{N} \to \mathcal{N}^{(1)} \to \mathcal{N}^{(2)} \to \cdots$ in which the closure operator (or generator set) **changes** at each step — that is a non-autonomous dynamical system to which the cited periodic-orbits-exclusion does not extend.
The substrate-algebraic content of **legislative gridlock** is therefore conjectural here: gridlock corresponds to a non-converging revision sequence, but whether such sequences can stabilize at a periodic orbit (genuine cycling between two normative systems via alternating revisions) is **open** and not settled by the cited single-operator theorem.
The polity may be in a non-convergent transient, a periodic cycle of revision-pairs, or genuinely divergent; distinguishing these requires substrate-specific analysis.*

## Sketch

**(a)** The closure 2-monad's colimit over the augmented cell-set computes the cocompletion of the original fixed-point lattice along the new generators.
The cited [closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md) ensures that re-applying closure after the colimit is the identity at the 1-categorical level, so the cocompletion is stable.
The substrate-algebraic content tracks the categorical: adding axioms is a colimit construction in the lattice of closed sets. $\square$ (Sketch (a))

**(b)** The cascade depth is the number of closure-iteration rounds required for the new fixed-point lattice to settle.
This is bounded by the chain length of $\mathrm{Fix}(C_\mathcal{N})$ above $A$ because each iteration can extend the closed set by at least one chain-step, and the chain has finite length.
The substrate-algebraic content: a revision's impact extends "as far as" the closure rules can derive new cells from the new generators; the cascade depth is the operational measure of that extent. $\square$ (Sketch (b))

**(c)** Removal of $R$ produces an order-ideal collapse of the dual Priestley space corresponding to the elements that depended on $R$ for their closure derivation.
The Priestley correspondence of [closure-fixed-points-priestley-correspondence](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-fixed-points-priestley-correspondence.md) makes this concrete: removed cells correspond to removed Priestley-space points, and orphaned closure-consequences correspond to points whose neighborhood-structure depended on the removed points. $\square$ (Sketch (c))

**(d)** A mixed revision composes the collapse (c) and the cocompletion (a).
When the closure-rule structure makes these operations commute (the typical case, as both are functors on the lattice), the composition's result is independent of order.
When the composition does not commute — when $A$ contains cells that re-derive removed cells — the order matters: collapse-then-add removes $R$ then adds $A$ which may re-derive $R$ via the closure rules; add-then-collapse adds $A$ first (which may make $R$ derivable from $A$ alone) then attempts to collapse $R$ but cannot remove it (since it is now derivable from $A$).
The non-commutation is the substrate-algebraic content of "norm conflict on revision." $\square$ (Sketch (d))

**(e)** [closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits.md) establishes that **iterating a single inflationary monotone idempotent closure operator** has no non-trivial periodic orbits — the iteration always converges to a fixed point.
This applies to the **internal** closure-iteration of a single revision step (per (a)): closing-and-re-closing the augmented cell-set under the *fixed* closure rules stabilizes.
The cited theorem does **not** apply to a *sequence of distinct revisions* in which each revision changes either the cell-set or the closure rules; this is a non-autonomous dynamical system whose periodic-orbit structure is not governed by the cited result.
The practitioner-recognized phenomenon of "gridlock" therefore admits multiple substrate-algebraic possibilities — non-convergent transient, periodic cycle, or genuine divergence — and the cited theorem does not adjudicate among them.
The substantive content of (e) is therefore the *single-step* convergence guarantee plus the explicit acknowledgment that *multi-step* convergence is open. $\square$ (Sketch (e))

## Operational consequences

### Revision-cascade depth = legislative impact assessment

The (b) reading gives a substrate-algebraic version of the practitioner-recognized notion of *legislative impact assessment* — *"how much of the existing normative system is changed by this proposed revision?"* The cascade depth is the precise measure: it is the number of closure-iterations the system requires to incorporate the change.
Substrates that practice impact assessment via cascade-depth analysis (e.g., civil-code commission staff modeling consequences before introducing amendments) are operating on the closure-fixed-point lattice directly; substrates that approve revisions without such analysis frequently encounter unexpected cascade effects after enactment.

### Norm conflict on revision = non-commuting composition

The (d) reading clarifies why some legislative revisions produce *conflicts* — *"the new statute repeals provision X but the new provision Y, by inference, restores X"* — as a categorical non-commutation.
The substrate-algebraic content: the order in which removal and addition are applied matters when the closure rules can re-derive removed cells from added cells.
Drafting practices that explicitly check this commutation (e.g., reviewing a proposed amendment for "savings clauses" or "anti-circumvention" provisions) are addressing the non-commutation directly.

### Gridlock: single-step convergence vs. multi-step open behavior

The (e) reading has two layers. **Internally to a single revision**, the cited [closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-monad-no-non-trivial-periodic-orbits.md) theorem guarantees the closure-iteration converges (no internal cycling) — so the revised system is well-defined as a single act. **Across multiple revisions**, however, the theorem does not apply: each revision changes either the cell-set or the closure rules, producing a non-autonomous sequence whose periodic-orbit structure is not constrained by the cited result.

The substrate-algebraic content of **legislative gridlock** therefore admits multiple possibilities, and substrate-algebra alone does not yet adjudicate among them.
The polity may be in (i) a non-convergent transient (each revision genuinely new), (ii) a periodic cycle of revision-pairs (the cell-system genuinely re-visits prior states), or (iii) divergent behavior.
Distinguishing these requires substrate-specific analysis of the revision-sequence's generator structure — which generator-level obstructions are preventing convergence, whether the cycle is "honest" (re-visits states exactly) or "appearance" (states differ in fine detail), and what the closure-rule structure of the polity permits.

This is a corrective to a tempting but unwarranted reading: substrate-algebra does **not** rule out periodic legislative cycles.
The cited theorem rules out *internal* periodic cycling within a single closure operator's iteration, not *external* cycling between distinct revisions.

### Charter amendment vs. norm revision

[charter-amendment-as-visible-automorphism](charter-amendment-as-visible-automorphism.md) handles the case where the *closure rules themselves* are revised; the present theorem handles the case where the *cell-set* is revised under fixed closure rules.
The two interact: a polity that revises its charter (changing closure rules) can render previously-converging revision sequences gridlocked, and vice versa.
The full revision algebra of a polity is the composite of (i) charter-amendment-induced visible automorphisms of the closure system and (ii) cocompletion-collapse of the cell-set under the (revised) closure rules.

## Status

`sketch` because:

- The cocompletion-as-closure-2-monad-colimit identification is correct in direction; the precise formal statement requires verification that the closure-2-monad's colimits in the relevant 2-category compute the desired cocompletion (rather than a strict colimit that loses some of the lax-idempotence content).
- The chain-length bound on cascade depth in (b) is correct when the fixed-point lattice has finite chain length (typical for finite normative systems with finitely many closure rules); for infinite normative systems (e.g., common-law systems with unbounded precedent), the cascade may be transfinite, which the present sketch does not handle.
- The non-commutation analysis of (d) is correct in direction but the precise structural condition on $A, R$ that produces non-commutation deserves explicit characterization.
- The "no non-trivial periodic orbits" claim of (e) follows directly from the cited theorem; the operational implication for legislative gridlock is correct in direction but ascribes to substrate algebra what is partly an empirical observation about real polities — substrate algebra establishes the *mathematical* impossibility of periodic orbits in the closure 2-monad, not the *empirical* claim that no real legislature is in a periodic-revision cycle (which is true but for substrate-algebraic reasons).

The directional content — that norm revision is cocompletion-or-collapse on the closure-fixed-point lattice, with cascade depth as impact measure, non-commutation as conflict signal, and non-convergence (not periodicity) as gridlock — is the substantive proposal of this sketch.

## Open downstream

- *Transfinite cascade depth in common-law systems.* Common-law systems with unbounded precedent have (potentially) infinite-chain fixed-point lattices, so cascade depth may be transfinite.
  Whether the cocompletion converges at any countable ordinal, and what the operational meaning of transfinite cascade is, is open.
- *Bayesian / weighted norm revision.* Some legal-philosophical accounts treat norm revision as updating prior probabilities over normative cell-systems rather than discrete cell-set changes.
  Whether this maps to a weighted-cocompletion in an enriched closure-2-monad (similar to the round-4 weighted-colimit conjecture in [quorum-as-colimit-of-voter-fibers](quorum-as-colimit-of-voter-fibers.md)) is open — and would require the same enrichment-or-multi-counting analysis.
- *Constitutional-moment revisions.* The practitioner-recognized notion of a "constitutional moment" (per Ackerman) — a revision that changes the closure-rule structure itself rather than merely the cell-set — is the [charter-amendment-as-visible-automorphism](charter-amendment-as-visible-automorphism.md) operation.
  Whether the combined "constitutional + cell-set revision" admits a unified categorical description (e.g., as a 2-cell on the lattice of closure systems) is open structural territory.
