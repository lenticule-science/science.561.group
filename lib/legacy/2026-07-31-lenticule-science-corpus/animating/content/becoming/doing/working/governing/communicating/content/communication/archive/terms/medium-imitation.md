Medium imitation is the policy of holding every register to the rigor norms of one privileged register, usually the most formal one available. The signature move is: "this practice will become serious when it adopts the conventions of *that* practice." A diagram is to be replaced by symbolic equations. A natural-language explanation is to be replaced by code. A chart is to be replaced by a table of numbers. An interview transcript is to be replaced by a coded categorical schema.

The mistake is not that the imitating register is bad. It is often quite good *in its own register*. The mistake is that the imitating register sheds exactly the operational competences the original register was designed to carry. The reason a diagram exists is that it makes spatial and relational structure available to the eye in a form symbolic notation does not. Replacing it with the symbolic form does not improve the rigor — it transports the content into a register where its operative compression is gone, and the artifact loses the work the diagram was doing.

## The mathematics case

In the philosophy of mathematical practice, this debate is well-developed. The pure-symbolic-derivation criterion of mathematical rigor — that a proof is rigorous iff it can be expanded into a sequence of formal logical steps — has been contested precisely because the actual practice of mathematics relies on registers (diagrams, narrative explanations, computational artifacts, didactic appeals) that the pure-derivation account cannot accommodate. Avigad ("Reliability of mathematical inference," *Synthese*, 2020), Macbeth (*Realizing Reason*, Oxford, 2014), Brendan Larvor, Ashton, and others have argued that real rigor is *medium-specific*: each register has its own standards of well-formedness, and remediating across registers can be done but is not automatic and incurs [transport-loss](transport-loss.md).

## The general case

Outside mathematics, medium imitation appears whenever a community decides that one register is the gold standard of seriousness. Quantitative-only social science. All-prose legal writing. UI documentation that refuses screenshots. Architectural review that refuses sketches. Each policy strips the practice of registers it actually needs and forces all the work into one form, usually the form whose practitioners have the most institutional authority.

The framework's positive criterion is [medium-specific-rigor](teaching/explanations/medium-specific-rigor.md): each register held to the rigor proper to it, transports between registers acknowledged as transports, and no single register treated as the universal measure.
