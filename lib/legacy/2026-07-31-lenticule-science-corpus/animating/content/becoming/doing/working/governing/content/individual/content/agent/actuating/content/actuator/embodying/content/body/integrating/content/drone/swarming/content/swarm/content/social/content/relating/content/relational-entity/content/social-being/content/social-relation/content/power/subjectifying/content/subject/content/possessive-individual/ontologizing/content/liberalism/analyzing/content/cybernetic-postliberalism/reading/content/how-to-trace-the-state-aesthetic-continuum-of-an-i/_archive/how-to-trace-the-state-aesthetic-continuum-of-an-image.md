The [state-aesthetic continuum](../teaching/explanations/state-aesthetic-continuum.md) runs from full aestheticization of political power (the sublime of force) through normalized representation of violence (rubber-hose realism's consequence-foreclosure) to demystification (the full material specificity that activates recognition).
Tracing an image along this continuum is an analytical practice that locates the image's specific position and identifies what political-affective work that position performs.

The procedure works for any still image: photograph, illustration, advertisement, news image, social media image, propaganda poster, product design, data visualization, or any other visually bounded image that bears on political power, state violence, or structural conditions of governance.

## Before you begin: establish the image's context

An image's position on the continuum is not fully readable from the image alone; context determines some of what the image is doing.
Establish:

- **Source:** where did the image appear — a state institution's publication, a news organization, an advocacy group, a commercial brand, a social media account?
- **Audience:** who was the intended audience for this image?
- **Purpose:** what was the image produced to do — inform, recruit, sell, commemorate, protest, document?
- **Surrounding text:** what text, caption, or context framed the image at its original site?

The context does not determine the analysis — an image can operate differently in a different context — but it provides the frame within which the image's position is legible.

## Step 1: Identify the political-power relation the image contains

What relation to political power, state violence, or structural conditions does this image contain?
Every image on the state-aesthetic continuum is in a relation to political power — it is representing, aestheticizing, normalizing, or demystifying some aspect of that relation.
Identify what aspect:

- The exercise of state force (police action, military operation, border enforcement)
- The effects of state force on bodies and communities (injury, displacement, death, incarceration)
- The structural conditions state policy produces (poverty, precarity, ecological degradation)
- The aesthetics of state institutions (official portraiture, architecture, ceremonial representation)
- The subjects who carry state policy in their bodies (soldiers, police officers, detained persons, displaced communities)

If the image does not contain a political-power relation, the continuum analysis does not apply.
Not every image is on the state-aesthetic continuum.

## Step 2: Locate the image on the continuum using the five anchor positions

Apply the five positions (from the [state-aesthetic continuum](../narratives/the-state-aesthetic-continuum.md) narrative) as a locating framework:

**Near Position 1 (Sublime militarism):** the image aestheticizes the state's power — presents it as beautiful, majestic, or sublime.
Look for: formal composition emphasizing scale and order, lighting that glorifies the subject, visual rhetoric of the monument or the icon, elimination of any element that would interrupt the aesthetic's completeness.

**Near Position 2 (Heroic narrative):** the image contextualizes state force within a narrative of noble purpose.
Look for: figures in active and purposeful poses, visual cues of mission and dedication, representation of sacrifice as meaningful, backgrounding or absence of the persons on whom the force is exercised.

**At Position 3 (Rubber-hose normalization):** the image represents state force while foreclosing its consequences.
Look for: consequence-foreclosure (the force is visible; its effects on persons are not), aestheticization that forecloses recognition (the weapon is beautiful, the explosion is spectacular, the interface is clean), technical abstraction that forecloses identification (the map, the data visualization, the aerial view that presents persons as data points).

**At Position 4 (Bureaucratic documentation):** the image presents state conditions in a register that admits cognitive recognition but forecloses affective identification.
Look for: documentary flatness, neutral framing, statistical or systematic presentation, absence of individual characterization of the persons affected.

**Near Position 5 (Demystification):** the image activates the recognition-response by representing conditions with full material specificity.
Look for: individual characterization of persons (names, faces, specific situations), representation of suffering in a form that allows identification, specific rather than abstract representation, temporal anchoring (this specific moment, these specific persons).

Place the image at the position or between the positions that best describes its specific representational choices.

## Step 3: Identify the specific aesthetic operations producing the position

For the position identified in Step 2, identify specifically which aesthetic choices are producing that position:

- **Framing:** what is included and excluded from the frame?
  What is the frame's proximity to the subject?
- **Lighting and color:** what affective register does the lighting produce?
  Does it aestheticize (dramatic chiaroscuro), naturalize (ambient natural light), or document (flat neutral light)?
- **Composition:** how are elements arranged within the frame?
  Does the composition produce a hierarchy of attention that foregrounds capability and backgrounds consequence?
- **Scale:** what is the relative scale of the represented subjects?
  Does scale produce monumentality, equivalence, or diminution?
- **Motion (if applicable):** if the image represents motion (a news photograph capturing action, a screenshot from video), what does the captured motion-moment show?
  Does it capture the act without its consequence?

Each identified aesthetic choice is a specific element of the analysis.
The analysis should cite specific visual features, not general impressions.

## Step 4: Identify what a demystifying representation would require

To complete the analysis, identify what representational choices would move the image toward the demystification end of the continuum — what would be required for the image to activate the recognition-response rather than foreclose it.

This is not a prescription (the analysis does not command the image to be different) but a diagnostic: identifying what the current image's choices are foreclosing reveals what the political function of the foreclosure is.

For a rubber-hose realist image: demystification would require representing the consequences the current image forecloses — the persons on whom force is exercised, the suffering the act produces, the structural conditions the act participates in.
The distance between the current image and a demystifying image is the measure of the rubber-hose operation's extent.

## Step 5: Write the continuum analysis

A complete continuum analysis states: the image's position on the continuum, the specific aesthetic choices producing that position, what recognition-responses those choices foreclose, and what a demystifying representation would require.
The analysis should be specific to the image — citing specific visual features — and should contextually locate the image's operation in relation to its source, audience, and purpose.
