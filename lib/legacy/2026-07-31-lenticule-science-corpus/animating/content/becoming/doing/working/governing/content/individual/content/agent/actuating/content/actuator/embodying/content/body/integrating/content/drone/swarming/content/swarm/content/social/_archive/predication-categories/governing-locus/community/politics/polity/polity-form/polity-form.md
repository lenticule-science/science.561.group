# Polity-form

The structural pattern by which a collectivity constitutes itself as a political body — the formal shape of who counts as a member, how authority is distributed, how decisions bind, and how the collectivity stands in relation to other collectivities.
Polity-form is not a synonym for *state*: it is the genus of which the modern territorial state is one species among many.

The concept is needed because the dominant tradition of political sociology treats the modern Westphalian state as the unmarked case and other polity-forms as developmental precursors or pathological variants.
That framing is empirically wrong and politically loaded.
Pierre Clastres ([clastres1974](references/clastres1974.md)) showed that many Indigenous polities are *actively organized* to prevent the consolidation of coercive power — statelessness as achievement, not absence.
David Graeber and David Wengrow ([graeber-wengrow2021](references/graeber-wengrow2021.md)) extended this point across the human archaeological record: polity-forms are diverse, switchable, and not arranged on a developmental ladder.

## Dimensions

Reading a polity-form means specifying, at minimum:

- **Membership**: who is in the polity, by what mechanism (descent, residence, ritual, contract, refusal).
  The Oceti Sakowin's tiyospaye-based membership is structured differently from territorial citizenship.
- **Authority distribution**: where binding decisions are made, by whom, with what scope.
  A council of shirt-wearers ([lakotaean-politics](theories/lakotaean-politics/_index.md)) differs in kind from a parliament.
- **Bindingness**: what gives a collective decision force on members.
  Consent?
  Coercion?
  Withdrawal as the limiting case?
  Lakota polities relied on the right-of-withdrawal as a structural feature, not a failure mode.
- **External standing**: how the polity is recognized — or refuses recognition — by other polities.
  Audra Simpson's [simpson2014](references/simpson2014.md) treats refusal of settler-state recognition as itself a polity-constituting move.

## Polity-form vs. state-form

Every state is a polity, but most polities have not been states. *State* names a specific polity-form characterized by centralized coercion, territorial monopoly on violence, and bureaucratic administration.
The concept of polity-form refuses to collapse the genus into this species.
