To **attribute** is to assign a property, identity, or condition to a being or situation.
The act looks descriptive — *she is depressed*; *they are racist*; *this is trauma*; *that is collapse* — but the predicated-sociology claim is that under [ontogenring](ontogenring.md), the act is not descriptive but constitutive.
Attributing installs the named being or situation into a genre-formatted ontology, with the genre's arcs, entitlements, and anticipations arriving along with the installation.
Attributing is the principal micro-operation through which ontogenring proceeds.

## Etymology

The verb derives from Latin *attribuere* — *ad-* (to) + *tribuere* (to assign, allot, give), which comes from *tribus* (tribe): originally the act of assigning citizens to their tribes for political and military purposes.
The etymological residue is precise.
To attribute is to assign, to allot — to do something to a person or thing rather than merely to describe it.
Attribution is an act of allocation with administrative and political consequences, not a neutral predicate.

## Three defining features

**It is unidirectional.** A descriptive predicate, in the conventional view, is true or false of an independently existing thing.
Attributing is constitutive: under [liberal ontologizing](liberal-ontologizing.md), the predicate constitutes the thing's place in the genre, and the thing's subsequent behavior is read through that place.
There is no neutral attributing; attributing always installs.

**It is binding.** Once a being or situation has been attributed, the genre's feeling-rules and resolution-arcs apply.
The trauma-attributed subject is in the recovery genre and is both entitled to and obliged to perform the affective arc the genre prescribes.
The racism-attributed situation is in the dialectical-overcoming genre and is both entitled to and obliged to perform the abolitionist arc its critics expect.
The binding is not psychological; it is genre-structural.

**It can be self-reflexive.** A subject can attribute conditions to themselves — *I am exhausted*; *I am traumatized*; *I am the kind of person who* — and the self-attribution operates with the same constitutive force as attribution by others.
Self-attribution is the principal route by which subjects install themselves into a genre rather than waiting to be installed by external actors.

## Examples

**Medical diagnosis.** A clinician diagnoses major depressive disorder.
The diagnosis appears to describe a pre-existing condition.
Under ontogenring, it installs the patient into the depression genre, with its arc (acknowledgment, treatment, response, recovery-or-management), its affective register (low mood, anhedonia, slowed cognition that the patient now has vocabulary for), and its resolutions (medication, therapy, lifestyle change).
Hacking (1995) showed how this looping operates: the diagnosis changes who the patient is, which changes how the diagnosis applies, which changes the diagnostic category.

**Racial categorization.** The state administers race; individuals are racially attributed at birth, in legal documents, in administrative records.
The attribution does not describe a pre-existing race; it installs the named person into a racial genre with its own arcs and entitlements.
Wynter's analysis of the coloniality of *Man* traces this to the constitutive moves of liberal modernity.

**Expertise attribution.** A person becomes a recognized expert through being treated as one — cited, invited to speak, asked for opinions.
The attribution installs the person into the expert genre.
Once installed, the genre's arc (productivity, authoritativeness, eventual displacement by the next generation) shapes what the person does and how their statements are received.
This is the precise mechanism that [expert naivete](expert-naivete.md) exploits.

## What attributing is not

Attributing is not the same as **describing**.
Description, in the conventional view, tracks pre-existing features.
Attributing is constitutive: the act of assigning the property is part of what the property's bearer becomes.

Attributing is not the same as **labeling** in the sociological sense.
Labeling theory holds that deviance is constituted by the labeling response.
Attributing is broader — it covers identifications, diagnoses, recognitions, and self-categorizations whether or not the resulting position is deviant.

## Relation to genre and social structure

Attributing connects the micro-scale (an interaction, a diagnosis, an administrative record) to the macro-scale (the genre-figures through which a social formation organizes itself).
[Genring](genring.md) names the population-level production of genre-figures; attributing names the interaction-level operation by which individuals are assigned to those figures.
[Genre coherence](genre-coherence.md) then exerts pressure to keep attributed individuals behaving in accordance with their assignment.

The political significance of attributing is its asymmetry.
Some populations are systematically attributed into constraining genres (the welfare recipient, the immigrant, the mentally ill) while others are attributed into genres with expansive arcs (the entrepreneur, the visionary, the reformer).
The attribution does not merely describe these differences; it perpetuates them by installing each attributed person into the social-settling dynamics the genre constrains.
