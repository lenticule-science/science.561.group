Predicated sociology's load-bearing structural claim is the *closure claim*: late-liberal governance is operationally closed in Niklas Luhmann's specific sense.
No coherent subject-position produces an outside.
[Anarchist grammar](../teaching/explanations/anarchist-grammar.md), primitivist exit, accelerationist hyperengagement, abolitionist construction, melancholic critique, off-grid retreat, and spiritual transcendence are genres within the same metabolism, each producing the affective material the apparatus requires.
The closure is descriptive sociology with a specific structural claim, not a political verdict.

## What operational closure means

Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela developed the concept in their biology of cognition: a living system is operationally closed in the sense that its operations refer only to its own states and produce only its own further operations.
The environment perturbs the system but does not directly determine its operations; meaning is generated within the system rather than received from outside.

Niklas Luhmann (1995) extends the concept into a general theory of social systems.
A social system consists of *communications*, and communications are the operations the system performs.
The system is operationally closed in the sense that its communications refer only to its own prior communications and produce only its own further communications.
What looks like external information becomes legible within the system only as the system's own communication about it.

This is the technical apparatus the closure claim runs on. *Late-liberal governance is operationally closed* means: the apparatus's operations refer only to its own prior operations and produce only its own further operations.
What looks like an outside — a critique, a refusal, an alternative — becomes legible within the apparatus only as the apparatus's own operation upon it.
The critique is metabolized as a genre of critique; the refusal as a genre of refusal; the alternative as a genre of alternative.
The metabolism does not require coordination; it follows from the operational structure of the apparatus.

## The closure claim's specific content

The closure claim has three operative components.

**Every coherent subject-position is metabolizable.** A subject-position is *coherent* if it is articulable as a stable identity, a stable practice, or a stable politics within the apparatus's discursive infrastructure.
[Anarchist grammar](../teaching/explanations/anarchist-grammar.md) as a coherent subject-position is metabolizable: it produces engagement, it has a genre, it can be served as content.
Primitivist exit as a coherent subject-position is metabolizable: it has its genre, its content economy, its supporting industries.
The closure claim is that this metabolizability holds in general.

**Metabolization does not require coordination.** The metabolism is performed by the apparatus's operational structure: the recursive readability of the platform, the algorithmic genre-routing, the requisite-variety provision, the cross-platform coherence.
No one is steering.
The metabolism is the apparatus's normal operation.

**The closure does not preclude all action.** Coherent subject-positions are distinct from *practice* in the strict sense.
Practice — the unmanaged act, the care that does not wait for permission, the relation that does not lend itself to legibility within the apparatus — is something different from a subject-position.
The closure claim is about coherent subject-positions; whether practice in this strict sense also gets metabolized is the open question addressed below.

## Why the closure is structural, not pessimistic

The closure is *descriptive*.
It states what the apparatus is, not what subjects should do.
A descriptive claim that water flows downhill is not a counsel against irrigation; it is information about the structural conditions any irrigation system must engage with.
Practices that ignore the closure tend to find themselves metabolized; practices that take the closure seriously may find ways to operate that the closure does not foreclose.

The closure is also *historically specific*.
Operational closure is not the eternal condition of governance.
The medieval pastoral apparatus operated under different structural conditions — its institutional interiors were richer, its metabolism slower, its coupling to the energetic substrate different.
The closure claim is about *late-liberal governance under contracted energetic substrate and platformal feedback architecture*.
If the substrate or the architecture changes substantially, the closure may attenuate.

The closure is also *not totalizing*.
Luhmann's social systems are operationally closed *as systems*, but subjects are not reducible to the social systems they participate in.
A subject's biological-organismic life, their dreams, their somatic states, their ancestor-relations, their kin-bonds — these are not coextensive with the social communications the apparatus metabolizes.
The closure operates at the level of communication, not at the level of all human life.

## What may survive metabolization

The question is held open with care.
There is no settled answer; what follows are candidates and the structural reasons they are candidates.

**Practice that does not produce engagement.** Practice that the platform's algorithms cannot see — slow reading without commentary, sustained attention to one thing, conversation that does not lend itself to share, friendship maintained through presence rather than mediation, ancestral or ceremonial relations that do not generate digital exhaust — is structurally invisible to the apparatus.
It does not produce the engagement signal the apparatus's metabolism requires.

**Refusal in the strict sense.** Audra Simpson's refusal — the practice of declining the recognition-circuit by which settler states confer standing — is not a coherent subject-position within the recognition-circuit; it is a relational-political practice that operates outside the circuit's terms.
Its persistence in actual indigenous political life suggests the apparatus's metabolism is not as comprehensive as a maximal closure claim would assert.

**Care that does not wait for permission.** Care-practice that operates outside the [harm governance](../teaching/explanations/harm-governance.md) apparatus — friendship that holds without reporting, mutual aid that does not seek recognition, sustained presence with the dying that produces no content — is another candidate.

**Embodied and ceremonial relations.** The subject under the apparatus participates in a great deal of life that is not communications in Luhmann's sense: somatic states, ancestor-relations, ceremonial obligations, ecological relations.
These are not metabolizable as communications because they are not communications.

None of these candidates is automatically protected.
The boundary between "outside the metabolism" and "metabolized in a way the practitioner cannot see" is not always clean, and the apparatus includes strategies for absorbing what looks initially like the outside.

## What the closure claim is not

It is not the claim that critique is futile.
Critique is one of the apparatus's metabolizable subject-positions, but the metabolizing of critique-as-content is not the same as the futility of critique-as-action.
Critique can be metabolized as content while still producing material consequences at other registers.

It is not the claim that revolutionary politics are impossible.
Revolutionary politics in any classical sense are not currently in operation.
But the conditions of operational closure do not preclude their future emergence.

It is not the claim that liberation is impossible.
Predicated sociology is not in the prescription business.
It says what the apparatus is.
What can be done, and by whom, and on what conditions, exceeds the theory's competence and is deferred to other traditions — indigenous resurgence, abolitionist construction, ecological-relational practice, ancestral renewal — that develop these questions more fully.
