# Latent world model identifiability

## Statement

Let $(Z, T, e, d)$ be a latent world model with latent state space
$Z$, transition $T : Z \times A \to Z$, encoder $e : O \to Z$,
and decoder $d : Z \to O$. For any invertible map $\phi : Z \to Z$,
the alternative model

$$
(Z,\; \phi \circ T \circ \phi^{-1},\; \phi \circ e,\; d \circ \phi^{-1})
$$

produces **identical observation predictions** for every
observation history.

If reward and policy heads are also represented as functions of
$z$ and are correspondingly transported by $\phi$ (i.e.
$\hat{R}' = \hat{R} \circ \phi^{-1}$, $\pi' = \pi \circ \phi^{-1}$),
the transported model produces identical reward predictions and
action selections as well. Without that compatibility — for
example, if a downstream consumer reads particular latent
coordinates as having fixed semantic meaning — the symmetry
breaks.

That is: the encode-transition-decode pipeline alone is **not
identifiable** from observation data, and any latent
representation is determined only up to invertible
reparameterization across the entire pipeline.

## Proof

By construction, the diagram

```
   o   --e-->   z   --T-->   z'   --d-->   o'
   o   --φe--> φz   --φTφ⁻¹-> φz'  --dφ⁻¹-> o'
```

commutes for every observation $o$ and action sequence. Both
models map identical inputs to identical outputs through the
encode-transition-decode pipeline. They are observationally
indistinguishable.

## Why it matters

The unidentifiability is not a bug to be fixed but a structural
feature with substantial consequences:

- **Latent dimensions have no intrinsic meaning.** What ends up
  in latent dimension 7 of a Dreamer model is whatever
  optimization happened to put there. There is no fact of the
  matter about whether dimension 7 represents "ball position"
  or "x-velocity" or some entangled mixture, *unless* additional
  constraints are imposed.
- **Two models trained on the same data have unrelated latents.**
  Independent training runs converge to incommensurable latent
  representations even if both achieve the same predictive
  accuracy. Direct comparison of latent activations across runs
  is meaningless.
- **Probing only proves correlation, not representation.** A
  probe that decodes "ball position" from a Dreamer latent
  proves the information *is encoded* in the latent space (in
  some basis), not that the model *uses* a ball-position
  representation internally.
- **Transfer is hard at the latent level.** A policy trained on
  latents from model A cannot be applied to latents from model B
  even if both model the same environment.

## What identifiability requires

Recovering identifiability requires additional structural
assumptions. The literature on disentangled representations
(Locatello et al. 2019, etc.) establishes that **without
inductive bias, disentanglement is impossible** (no algorithm
can recover ground-truth factors from observations alone).
Sufficient conditions for identifiability include:

- **Auxiliary supervision.** Labels or partial observations of
  ground-truth factors break the symmetry.
- **Sparsity priors.** Penalizing dense latent-to-observation
  mappings can favor disentangled solutions, though uniqueness
  is rarely guaranteed.
- **Causal structure assumptions.** If the data is generated by
  a known causal graph with intervention data, identifiability
  results follow.
- **Temporal contrastive structure.** Time-contrastive learning
  with appropriate assumptions can identify latent dynamics up
  to a known equivalence class (Hyvärinen et al.).

In practical world-model training, none of these conditions are
typically met. The latent space is left non-identifiable, and
this is accepted as the cost of unsupervised representation
learning.
