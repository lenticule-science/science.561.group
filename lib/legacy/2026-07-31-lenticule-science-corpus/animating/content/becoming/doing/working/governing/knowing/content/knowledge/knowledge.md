# knowledge

**Knowledge** is a claim *settled* — held to a fixed point by the evidence, no longer moving under the considerations in hand.
It is the fourth member of the doxastic family: what [belief](../../../../library/belief/docs/belief.md) becomes when accumulation tightens to the point that adding more changes nothing.
Where belief is held and revisable, knowledge is settled.

## More than true belief — and short of certainty

The question of what settles belief into knowledge is the oldest in epistemology.
Plato's *Theaetetus* shows that true belief alone is not knowledge — a jury rightly persuaded still does not *know* — and proposes that knowledge is true belief with an account (*logos*).
The justified-true-belief picture that descends from this, and the Gettier cases that unsettle it, are all circling the same point: knowledge is belief that has been brought to rest in the right way, not merely belief that happens to be true.

The sense of "settled" used here is Peirce's.
The settled opinion is "the opinion which is fated to be ultimately agreed to by all who investigate" — knowledge as the *terminus of inquiry*, the state in which the struggle of doubt has ended because the evidence no longer pushes.
But settled is not infallible.
Peirce's **fallibilism** is built into this: a claim can be settled — internally coherent, stable under all the evidence so far — and still be wrong.
Certainty would be a global guarantee that no possible evidence could overturn the claim; this family makes no such claim.
Knowledge is the fixed point *under the evidence in hand*, re-openable if genuinely new material arrives — which would be a fresh inquiry, seeded by a fresh question.
Determination, not certainty.

## What generates it

Knowledge is generated by **knowing**: the act that closes belief: holding the evidence-weighted position until accumulation reaches the point where further evidence would leave it unchanged.
Knowing is believing brought to its fixed point.

## What it generates

Knowledge generates a settled claim — the terminus of the inquiry that ran from question to judgement to belief.
It may be settled-true, settled-false, or settled-conflicted; what makes it knowledge is that it is *settled*, not which way it leans.
And because settlement is only ever up to the current evidence, a piece of knowledge can re-open as the [question](../../../../../../library/question/docs/question.md) of a further inquiry.
The fixed point of one inquiry is the seed of the next.
