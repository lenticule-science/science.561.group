## What it is

A **Leitourgia** is a quadruple $(\lambda,\; \varphi,\; C,\; \iota)$:

$$\Lambda = (\lambda,\; \varphi,\; C,\; \iota)$$

where:

- $\lambda$ is the **leitourgos** — an Entity in Hohfeldian liability position with respect to the community $C$: designated by the appointing authority's power, not by self-selection.
  Olson's positional compulsion (*The Logic of Collective Action*, 1965, p.
  2): the obligation follows from station, not consent.
  The leitourgos is not an agent who has decided to serve; the station designates the service.
- $\varphi$ is the **prescribed-form proposition** — a determinate normative specification of what correct enactment consists in.
  Austin felicity condition A.
  1 (*How to Do Things with Words*, 1962, p.
  14): "there must exist an accepted conventional procedure having a certain conventional effect."
  The prescribed form is not a general contribution but a definite specification with a yes/no satisfaction condition.
- $C$ is the **witnessing community** — not a pre-existing assembly but the entity constituted by the gathering of a coherent family of witnessing observations.
  Bell (*Ritual Theory, Ritual Practice*, 1992, pp.
  69–93): "ritualization constitutes the community rather than expressing it." $C$ exists as $C$ because and only because the enactment is collectively witnessed across a coherent cover.
  Before the cover gathers there is no community — there are only local observers.
- $\iota$ is the **discharge proposition** — the claim that *$\lambda$ enacted $\varphi$ before $C$*.
  The discharge proposition is what records whether the enactment has occurred; its [settlement](../../../../../../math/theories/voidmath/terms/settlement.md) is what determines whether the leitourgia has been fulfilled.

**Type note.** $\varphi$ and $\iota$ are distinct claims.
The prescribed form $\varphi$ is a standing normative specification — it exists whether or not any leitourgos has acted.
The discharge proposition $\iota$ is contingent on $\lambda$'s actual performance.
What earlier formulations conflated — the normative form and the enactment record — are distinct.
This parallels the distinction in [Incumbent](../../../terms/incumbent.md) between the incumbency proposition $\iota$ (whether $a$ holds $O$) and the office structure $O$ (which holds regardless of any occupant).

**Full discharge** is the condition that two distinct predicates over $\iota$ have both reached affirming settlement at the discharge history $t$:

- *Form-enactment* — the predicate that $\varphi$ has been correctly performed in front of $C$
- *Forward-stability* — the predicate that the discharge carries forward into continuations of $t$ (no successor will be called upon to discharge what $\lambda$ has already discharged)

When only one of the two predicates has settled affirmingly, the service is partially discharged.

**Personal cost.** The enactment step that settles $\iota$ carries a structurally irreducible cost: the witnessings $\lambda$ produces about $\iota$ are not retractable, and the resources $\lambda$ commits to performing $\varphi$ correctly are spent.
The leitourgos bears this cost as a structural consequence of the step, not as a free parameter.
Demosthenes (*Against Meidias* 21.155) documents the trierarch's irreversible personal expenditure as the structural marker of genuine leitourgia.
The irreversibility is not a defect but the defining mark distinguishing genuine leitourgia from a performance of leitourgia.

This irreversibility has a thermodynamic analog in Landauer's principle (*IBM Journal of Research and Development* 5:183–191, 1961): logical operations that erase information in their final state require non-trivial physical resources.
Whether the leitourgos's personal cost is formally identical to the Landauer cost of the enactment step, or merely structurally analogous, is an open question — see below.

## The Athenian structure

**Leitourgia** (Greek: *leitos*, public + *ergon*, work) was in classical Athens a legally required public service borne by wealthy citizens at their own expense: funding a trireme (trierarchy), equipping a dramatic chorus (choregia), provisioning a sacred embassy (theoria).
Not a tax, which is distributed; not charity, which is voluntary.
A positional obligation — compelled by civic standing, borne at irreversible personal cost, directed toward a specific form whose correct enactment benefited the whole community.

The structural features that persist from this civic institution:

1. **Designation by position, not choice**: the liturgical class was determined by assessed wealth.
  A man who crossed the wealth threshold owed the service.
  Nomination by a rival (*antidosis*) was the mechanism for identifying who held the position (Davies 1971).
2. **A determinate prescribed form**: not "contribute to the navy" but "provide a specific trireme, crewed and maintained, for one year."
  The form had a yes/no satisfaction condition.
  Demosthenes *Against Meidias* 21.155 documents the legal content of correct trierarchic performance.
3. **A community constituted by the performance**: the Athenian theatrical festivals were not performances for an audience that existed independently.
  The audience was constituted as the *demos* — the political body of Athens — by assembling in the theater and witnessing the choruses funded by the liturgy.
  Aristotle *Politics* 1280b30: the city is not a commercial alliance but is constituted by shared participation in its own institutions.
4. **Irreversible personal cost**: the trierarch could not recover the expenditure.
  This irreversibility was the liturgy's marker of genuine civic commitment — it distinguished it from a refundable levy or a performance for fee.

## The two-stage discharge

The [Incumbent](../../../terms/incumbent.md) spec derives two installation stages — institution and induction — from ecclesiastical law.
The leitourgia has a structurally parallel two-stage discharge:

**Form-enacted** (*perfectus actus*): The leitourgos enacts $\varphi$ in the correct form before $C$.
The accumulation of relational history — the gathering of the witnessing family — settles the form-enactment predicate over $\iota$ in the affirming direction.
The past has no further demand.
This corresponds to Austin's felicity condition A.
2 (complete execution) — the procedure is complete.

**Forward-active** (*perpetua effectus*): The discharge carries forward into continuations of $t$; the forward-stability predicate over $\iota$ has reached affirming settlement.
Every future history inherits the discharge as already-accomplished.
No successor can be called upon to discharge what $\lambda$ has already discharged.

| Stage | Settlement state | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Enacted only | Form-enactment predicate settled affirmingly; forward-stability predicate not yet | Past has recognized the form-enactment; the discharge is not yet carried into all continuations — future histories could re-open the question |
| Forward-active only | Forward-stability predicate settled; form-enactment predicate not yet | All future histories carry the discharge forward; the accumulated past has not yet fully recognized correct enactment — anomalous, normally impossible without form-enactment first |
| Full discharge | Both predicates settled affirmingly | Past-settled and forward-active; the community inherits the enacted service across all extensions; no further normative demand can be made |

The ordering — form-enactment before forward-stability — is the normal sequence.
The reverse order is structurally anomalous: ongoing forward-stability without past-recognition would be a discharge that propagates without ever having been performed.

## Community-constitution

The witnessing community $C$ at history $t$ is constituted as follows: a family of local witnessing observations — one at each history in a coherent covering of $t$ — forms a *compatible family* iff the observations agree where their domains overlap.
The framework's covering structure on histories ensures that every compatible family gathers into a unique global witnessing record.
The community $C$ is the entity indexed by this gathered record: it exists as a unified entity iff the witnessing observations cohere across the cover.

Before the covering gathers, there are local witnesses but no community.
The community comes into existence at the moment the compatible family becomes a global record.
This is the formal content of Bell's claim that ritualization constitutes the community: the gathering of compatible witnesses *is* the constitution.

The claim that $\iota$ has reached affirming settlement requires this community-constitution: not only must the enactment occur, but the witnessing must cohere.
An enactment witnessed by an incoherent family — one that cannot be gathered into a unified record — does not discharge the leitourgia, because there is no community before whom discharge could be recognized.
Austin's felicity condition B.
1 (*How to Do Things with Words*, 1962, p.
15): "the particular persons and circumstances in a given case must be appropriate for the invocation of the particular procedure invoked."
Inappropriate witnesses = incoherent cover = no gathered record = no community = no discharge.

## Leitourgia levels

Four levels of discharge stability, from least to most complete:

| Level | Condition | What it names |
|---|---|---|
| **Designated / pending** | Neither predicate settled | $\lambda$ is designated and the obligation exists, but the service has not been rendered |
| **Form-enacted only** | Form-enactment predicate settled; forward-stability not yet | The enactment has been performed and the accumulated past recognizes it; the discharge is not yet forward-inherited |
| **Forward-active only** | Forward-stability predicate settled; form-enactment not yet | All future histories inherit the discharge; the past has not yet settled the recognition — structurally anomalous |
| **Fully discharged** | Both predicates settled affirmingly | Past-recognized and future-inherited; the service is permanently complete |

## Theological development

The Septuagint uses *leitourgia* for Temple priestly service (1 Chr 23:28; 26:30; Num 8:22); the New Testament and patristic literature extend it to corporate worship.
The structural transformation: the designated minister enacts a prescribed form before the assembly; the cost shifts from financial (trierarchy's outlay) to consecrated time, purity, and submission to form; the recipient shifts from the city to the divine.
Bradshaw (*The Search for the Origins of Christian Worship*, Oxford, 2002, pp.
1–30) traces how the Eucharist inherits the positional-designation structure from both civic and Temple antecedents.

The invariant structure persists across this transformation: designated leitourgos ($\lambda$), prescribed form ($\varphi$), community constituted by the assembly's witnessing ($C$), personal cost.
The two-stage discharge — form-enactment recognized by accumulated normative tradition, then forward-stability inherited by all future histories — maps onto the distinction in sacramental theology between valid celebration (*perfecta executio*) and the permanent effect of valid celebration (*character indelebilis*: the priesthood, once conferred, cannot be rescinded).
Whether this theological extension generates derivable claims or remains interpretive is an open question of the same kind as the institution/induction mapping in [Incumbent](../../../terms/incumbent.md).

## Open questions

- **Whether the framework's covering structure on histories captures social witnessing**: The community-constitution hypothesis states that the witnessing family must form a coherent cover for gathering to occur.
  But the framework's covering structure is defined by the step-commutation and history-extension relations on histories — it is not defined by social witnessing.
  Whether social witnessing (the presence of the relevant witnesses at $t$) is captured by the existing covering structure, or whether a leitourgia-specific enrichment is required, is not yet derived.

- **Whether form-enactment settlement entails forward-stability for discharge propositions**: The two-stage table above lists form-enactment and forward-stability as independent conditions.
  But for discharge propositions of a specific type ("$\lambda$ enacted $\varphi$ before $C$"), the structural conditions may suffice to show that form-enactment settlement implies forward-stability.
  If this implication holds for the discharge proposition specifically, the two-stage distinction collapses to a single condition for leitourgia and the distinction from [Incumbent](../../../terms/incumbent.md) is formal, not substantive.
  This derivation is not yet carried out.

- **Whether the leitourgos's personal cost is formally identical to the thermodynamic cost of the enactment step**: The hypothesis identifies the personal cost borne by $\lambda$ with the irreducible cost of an irreversible logical step (Landauer 1961).
  This is motivated: the step produces witnessings about $\iota$ that cannot be retracted; the leitourgos, occupying the position that performs the step, bears the irreversible production.
  But this identification requires a model of what it means for $\lambda$ — an Entity at history $t$ — to bear the cost of a step that produces witnessings at the successor history.
  Whether the Entity $\lambda$ is formally the system that undergoes the irreversibility, or merely occupies the position that triggers the step, is not yet derived.
