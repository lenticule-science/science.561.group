# Revise Under Catholic Doctrine

When writing for audiences that require doctrinal consistency, the constraint is not just accuracy but compatibility: the text should not implicitly or explicitly contradict core Catholic teachings.

## When to use

You are writing or revising for a context where Catholic doctrinal compatibility matters — material for a parish, a Catholic-affiliated institution, or an audience that takes doctrine as a given. The constraint is silent compatibility, not active apologetics.

## How it works

1. Identify doctrinal claims in the draft. Anything making theological assertion — about God, salvation, the sacraments, moral teaching, ecclesiology — is in scope.
2. For each claim, check against authoritative sources (Catechism, magisterial documents). Defer to those sources rather than reasoning from first principles.
3. Where a claim conflicts with doctrine: revise to neutral language, recast as a non-doctrinal observation, or remove.
4. Where a claim is uncertain — could be read as compatible or as conflicting — flag the passage for theological review rather than asserting one reading.

## Rules

- Compatibility is silent. The skill is not to insert doctrinal claims; it is to make sure the text does not contradict them.
- Defer to authoritative sources rather than reconstructing doctrine from inference.
- Mark uncertain passages explicitly. "I think this is fine" is not enough for a context that requires doctrinal consistency.
