# Collins 1990 — *Black Feminist Thought*

Patricia Hill Collins. *Black Feminist Thought: Knowledge, Consciousness, and the Politics of Empowerment*.
Boston: Unwin Hyman, 1990.
Second, revised edition: New York: Routledge, 2000.
Thirtieth-anniversary edition: Routledge, 2022.

## Status

Read locally (second edition, 2000); foundational reference for the [black-feminism](theories/critical-theory/theories/feminism/theories/black-feminism/_index.md) subdomain.

## Annotation

The book is the systematic statement of Black feminist thought as a critical social theory.
Collins's central methodological argument is that Black feminist thought is a distinctive and coherent body of social theory produced by Black women out of their particular position in the U.S. social structure — that the standpoint produces analytical resources that other standpoints do not, and that the body of work has to be read as theory, not only as testimony.
The book is one of the founding documents of contemporary U.S. standpoint sociology.

Three analytical contributions structure the book:

1. **The matrix of domination.** Collins's name for the structure the Combahee Collective called interlocking.
  Domination is organized through the intersection of multiple systems (race, class, gender, sexuality, nation, age) operating across multiple domains of power.
  In the developed version, the matrix operates through four domains — the structural, the disciplinary, the hegemonic, and the interpersonal — and a given case is structured at all four levels simultaneously.
  The framing refuses both additive and hierarchical readings of oppression and is one of the most consequential analytical tools the subdomain inherits.

2. **The outsider-within standpoint.** Black women historically occupied positions inside white households (as domestic workers) and inside white institutions (as scholars, professionals) from which they could observe both the inside operations of those institutions and the structures of difference that made them outsiders within.
  The standpoint is epistemically productive: it produces a double vision the dominant standpoint cannot have.
  The framing extends Du Bois's double consciousness to the gendered case and is one of the founding texts of standpoint epistemology in U.S. sociology.

3. **A Black-feminist epistemology.** Collins articulates four criteria the Black-feminist tradition uses to test knowledge claims: lived experience as a criterion of meaning; the use of dialogue in assessing knowledge claims; the ethics of caring; and the ethics of personal accountability.
  The four are alternatives to (and critiques of) the positivist criteria that dominate U.S. social science.
  The articulation is one of the foundational texts in the Black-feminist epistemology literature.

The second edition (2000) substantially expands the discussion of intersectionality and adds material on Black women's organizational politics, on transnational connections, and on the academic reception of the first edition.
Collins's later books — *Fighting Words* (1998), *Black Sexual Politics* (2004), *Intersectionality* (with Bilge, 2016), *Intersectionality as Critical Social Theory* (2019) — extend specific arguments from this book; *Black Feminist Thought* remains the systematic statement.
