# What relations cannot be individually waived, and why?

Multiple non-Western governance traditions recognize categories of jural relations — rights, responsibilities, obligations — that cannot be individually surrendered, even by the relation's apparent holder.
The predicated-governance waiver morphism requires a substrate account of this non-waivability: specifically, what makes certain relations trust-held (held for the community, future generations, or non-human kin), and what is the structural consequence of attempted individual waiver?

## Why this matters for the substrate

The [waiver morphism](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/morphisms/waiver.md) represents waiver as installation of a new cell (not retraction of the right cell).
Its pre-condition requires individual disposal standing.
For trust-held relations, this standing is blocked, producing a $\top$-valued authority cell when individual waiver is attempted.
The trust-held cell is a governance-pattern-specific parameter — its content depends on which tradition's account of trust-holding is operative.

## Specific questions by tradition

### Haudenosaunee

1. The seventh-generation principle commits current decision-makers to consider future generations' interests.
  Does this create explicit categories of *un-waivable* responsibilities — things a chief cannot surrender even if they want to?

2. Are land and water relations in Haudenosaunee law held by individuals, by nations, or in trust for all beings (including non-human kin and future generations)?
  Can an individual or even a nation waive these?

3. What is the structural consequence in Haudenosaunee law when someone attempts to alienate a relation they do not have individual disposal authority over — is the act void, contested, or reversible by clan-mother action?

### Ubuntu

1. Ubuntu's account of dignity as relational capacity (the ability to identify with and exhibit solidarity toward others) suggests dignity cannot be individually alienated — it is constituted by relationship, not held as a personal property.
  Is dignity-waiver formally unrecognized, or is it recognized but treated as self-harm that the community has standing to prevent?

2. Are there other relational claims in Ubuntu jurisprudence — to community belonging, to participation in collective decision-making, to communal care — that are similarly non-waivable at the individual level?

### Comparative

3. Do other Indigenous governance traditions (Anishinaabe, ayllu-based Andean, others documented in the indigenous-governance-traditions survey) have explicit accounts of trust-held non-waivable relations?
  What vocabulary do they use for this distinction?

## Notes for the answering researcher

The substrate formalization places the trust-held cell as a parameter in the authority-gate pre-condition.
The goal is to populate this parameter accurately from the traditions' own accounts — not to impose a Western legal theory of trust on them.
If a tradition's account of non-waivability is not well-captured by the trust-held cell structure, that is itself a finding that should be reported.
