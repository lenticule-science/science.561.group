The **forces** are the four basic stances an [illocution](../../../docs/illocution.md) can take toward the truth of a content: **asserting**, **denying**, **abstaining**, and **contesting**.
They are the kinds of force in the narrow domain of taking-true and taking-false — the illocutions that put a content forward as so, as not-so, as neither, or as both.

## Assertion and denial as coordinate primitives

The tradition of *bilateralism* in the philosophy of logic takes assertion and denial to be two independent primitive acts, not one reducible to the other.
Frege already marked assertion with a special sign — the judgement-stroke — to distinguish *putting a content forward as true* from merely entertaining it; bilateralists (Rumfitt's "Yes and No"; the work of Restall and Ripley) add that *denial* is an equally basic act, not just the assertion of a negation.
Asserting and denying are the two directions along a single axis: what one does with a content's truth.

A second axis runs across that one, and it is the axis of *information*: how much the evidence has told.
Nuel Belnap's four-valued logic ("A Useful Four-Valued Logic," "How a Computer Should Think") was designed for exactly the situation where a reasoner is fed reports that may be incomplete or conflicting.
Its four values are the four informational states of a content: told-true only, told-false only, told *neither*, and told *both*.
Those are the four forces:

| force | stance | informational state |
|---|---|---|
| **asserting** | put forward as true | told true |
| **denying** | put forward as false | told false |
| **abstaining** | put forward as neither — withholding | told neither (the empty stance: no illocution at all) |
| **contesting** | put forward as both — holding the conflict open | told both |

The two axes are genuinely transverse.
Asserting and denying are opposites on the truth axis but agree in *committing*; abstaining and contesting are opposites on the information axis — saying nothing versus holding both at once — but agree in not taking a single side.

## What it generates — how stances combine

Belnap's logic is *paraconsistent*: it is built so that a contradiction does not explode the system.
This is what the forces generate when they meet on a shared content.
Two assertions agree; two denials agree; but **an assertion and a denial of the same content produce a contested content** — the conflict is *held*, not collapsed into nonsense.
This is why a field on which one contributor affirms and another rejects the same claim escalates to a contested state rather than crashing, and why [adjudication](../../../../../../../working/governing/library/adjudication/docs/adjudication.md) must be able to *hold* contestation as a real state of the record rather than treating it as mere error.
Abstaining adds nothing and changes nothing; contesting, once present, stands until the evidence that produced it is resolved.
