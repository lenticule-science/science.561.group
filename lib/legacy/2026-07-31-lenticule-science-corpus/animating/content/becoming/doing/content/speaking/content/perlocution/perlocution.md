# perlocution

**Perlocution** is the third layer of [speaking](../../../docs/speaking.md): *the effect* — Austin's perlocutionary act, "what we bring about *by* saying" something: convincing, persuading, alarming, deterring, consoling.
Where [illocution](../../illocution/docs/illocution.md) is the force put into the utterance, perlocution is what that force actually achieves in the hearer and the world downstream.
A speech act is not complete at the saying; it has consequences, and those consequences are perlocution.

Austin's own distinction is worth keeping sharp: illocution is *conventional* — its force is fixed by the act-type, so that in saying "I warn you" one has warned — whereas perlocution is *not* conventional.
Its effects depend on the actual uptake: a warning may frighten, or fail to; an argument may convince, or harden the opposition.
Perlocution includes effects that were never intended.
This open-endedness is part of what it is: the act's force is determinate, but its effects run out into a world the speaker does not control.

## What generates it

Perlocution is generated by illocution: there is nothing to take effect until a content has been charged with a force.
Given a charged utterance, perlocution is its working-out — the consequences that follow from its having been said.

## What it generates

Perlocution generates the act's standing contribution: the change it leaves in the shared state that others will read and respond to — the field that [teaching](../../../../../working/library/teaching/docs/teaching.md) writes to and [learning](../../../../../working/library/learning/docs/learning.md) reads from.
