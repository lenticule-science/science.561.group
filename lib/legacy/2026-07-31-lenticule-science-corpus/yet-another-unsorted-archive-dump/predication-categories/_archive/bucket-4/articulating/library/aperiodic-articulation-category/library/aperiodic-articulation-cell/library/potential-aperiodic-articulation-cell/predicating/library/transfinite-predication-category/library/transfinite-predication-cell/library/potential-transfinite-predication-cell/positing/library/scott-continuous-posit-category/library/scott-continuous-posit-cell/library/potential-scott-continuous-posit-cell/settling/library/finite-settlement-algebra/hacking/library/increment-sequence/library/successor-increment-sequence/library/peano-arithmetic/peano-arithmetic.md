# Peano arithmetic

The [[successor-increment-sequence]] settles to ℕ. Peano arithmetic is what that orbit *is*,
read in arithmetic vocabulary — built entirely from the one morphism `S` (`scripts/peano.mjs`).

## ℕ is the natural-number object

Run `#settling-endofunctor` on `successor(0)`. The orbit is `0, 1, 2, …`; the **stages are the
ordinals** (value at stage τ = τ); every step reaches a genuinely new cell, so the orbit never
finitely closes — its closure ordinal is `ω` and ℕ sits in the **Scott-continuous** regime
(`κ = ω`). Categorically this orbit is the **initial algebra of the endofunctor `X ↦ 1 + X`**
("a natural is either the seed or a successor") — the natural-number object. The `1` is the
seed; the `X` is the step; the colimit at `ω` is ℕ. So the NNO is not posited — it is the
Scott-continuous settlement of the successor.

## 0, S, +, ·, ↑ — everything from the one step

| operation | definition | reading |
|---|---|---|
| `0` | the seed | the unique cell with no predecessor in the orbit |
| `S(n)` | the increment step | one turn of the orbit |
| `a + b` | `b`-fold `S` from `a` | iterated successor (`a+0=a`, `a+S b = S(a+b)`) |
| `a · b` | `b`-fold `(+a)` from `0` | iterated addition (`a·0=0`, `a·S b = a·b + a`) |
| `aᵇ` | `b`-fold `(·a)` from `1` | iterated multiplication — the next hyperoperation rung |

Each rung is the same move — *iterate the rung below* — which is exactly primitive recursion, and
exactly "define a settling by giving its increment." The recursion theorem is the statement that
this construction is well-defined; here it is just: the orbit of a generator exists.

## The five axioms are orbit properties (verified)

Tested in `peano-axioms-fall-out-of-the-successor-orbit.test.mjs`:

1. **0 is a natural** — the seed exists.
2. **closure** — `orbit[k+1] = S(orbit[k])`.
3. **0 is not a successor** — nothing steps to `0`; `0` occurs only at the seed (no predecessor).
4. **S is injective** — every step is a new cell, so distinct naturals have distinct successors.
5. **induction** — the orbit exhausts every initial segment `{0,…,N}`; full induction *is* the
   `κ=ω` exhaustion of ℕ.

The operation laws (commutativity, associativity, distributivity, identities) are verified in
`peano-operations-are-iterated-settling.test.mjs` — they hold *because* + and × are iterates of
the one monotone step.

## Two shadows of arithmetic: additive vs multiplicative

Peano's two operations are two different settlings:

- **Additive ℕ** — the **linear** successor tower of this object: one generator, the orbit is a line.
- **Multiplicative (ℤ_{>0}, ·)** — the **free commutative monoid on the primes** (see the
  consolidated note `arithmetic-history-system`): a *different* settling whose generators are the
  primes and whose History-Separation axiom IS the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic. Its
  saturation-nucleus depth is the `p`-adic valuation (see `two-adic-valuation-is-saturating-depth`).

Distributivity `a·(b+c) = a·b + a·c` is how the linear (successor) tower and the free-monoid
(prime) tower interlock — the one law that ties the two shadows into a single arithmetic.

## Where the regime tower shows itself

Strength lives in the induction. Statements provable by the `κ=ω` induction are exactly Peano's
reach. Statements that need to climb *past* ω — Goodstein's theorem, the Hydra — are visible only
when the orbit is read in the transfinite regime (their termination lives at a colimit no finite
stage witnesses). That regime gap is the categorical shadow of Gödel/Goodstein independence; it is
left as an honest pointer here, not derived.
