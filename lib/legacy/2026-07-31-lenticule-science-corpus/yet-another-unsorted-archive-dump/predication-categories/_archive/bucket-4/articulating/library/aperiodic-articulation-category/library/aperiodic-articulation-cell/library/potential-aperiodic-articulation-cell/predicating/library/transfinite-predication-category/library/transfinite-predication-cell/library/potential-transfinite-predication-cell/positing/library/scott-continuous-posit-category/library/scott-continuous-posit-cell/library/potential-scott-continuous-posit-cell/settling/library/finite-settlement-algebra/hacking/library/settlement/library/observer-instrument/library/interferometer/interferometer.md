---
title: "Interferometer"
type: term
component-of: astronomical-instruments
---

An **interferometer** combines light from multiple separated apertures (or single elements of a phased array) coherently, achieving angular resolution corresponding to the longest baseline between elements rather than the diameter of any single element. Resolving power scales as $\lambda/B$ where $B$ is the baseline; an array spanning kilometres can achieve milliarcsecond resolution at radio wavelengths, far beyond what any single dish could.

Interferometry is the dominant high-resolution technique in radio astronomy (VLA, ALMA, MeerKAT, the upcoming SKA) and has been extended to infrared (CHARA, VLTI) and to very long baselines via VLBI (the Event Horizon Telescope, which produced the first images of the supermassive black holes M87\* and Sgr A\* by combining radio dishes across continents into an Earth-sized synthetic aperture). Interferometric measurements yield sparse sampling of the source's Fourier transform; image reconstruction from these sparse samples is a non-trivial inverse problem that has driven much of the development of modern image-reconstruction algorithms.

In the gravitational-wave domain, **laser interferometers** (LIGO, Virgo, KAGRA, the upcoming LISA) detect gravitational-wave passage by measuring the differential change in arm length of a kilometre-scale Michelson interferometer to better than $10^{-18}$ m. The 2015 detection of GW150914 (Abbott et al.) was the first direct observation of gravitational waves and inaugurated multi-messenger astronomy.
