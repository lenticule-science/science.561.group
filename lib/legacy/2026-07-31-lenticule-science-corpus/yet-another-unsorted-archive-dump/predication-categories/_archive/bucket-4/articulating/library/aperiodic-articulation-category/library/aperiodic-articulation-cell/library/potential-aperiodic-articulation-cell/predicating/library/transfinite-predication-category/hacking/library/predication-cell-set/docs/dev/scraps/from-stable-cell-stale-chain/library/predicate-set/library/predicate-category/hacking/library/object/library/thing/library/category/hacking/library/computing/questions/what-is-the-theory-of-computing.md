# What is the theory of computing?

> **Scope.** This is not an exercise in categorizing the existing contents of `theories/`. It is a formal mathematical question about what the real-world theory of computing is — in the sense of Curry-Howard-Lambek: what mathematical structure is computation?

> **Progress (sketch).** In the Curry-Howard-Lambek correspondence, a mathematical theory is a category (or topos, or typed calculus) whose objects are types and whose morphisms are programs (proofs). The theory of computing is whichever such structure has the computable functions as its morphisms and computability as its internal logic. The effective topos $\mathbf{Eff}$ (Hyland 1982) — the realizability topos for Kleene's first algebra $\mathcal{K}_1$ — is the leading candidate: it is an elementary topos (not a Grothendieck topos), its internal logic is intuitionistic, and it internally proves Church-Turing as a theorem (not an axiom): every total $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ function is recursive. Assemblies over $\mathcal{K}_1$ supply the computational domain; the realizability tripos over $\mathcal{K}_1$ supplies the predicate structure and inference.
>
> **Rice's theorem** constrains any answer: every non-trivial behavioral predicate of programs is undecidable. Any mathematical structure for the theory of computing must accommodate a genuinely undecidable truth value — Boolean logic at the meta-level is insufficient. At the external level, computational predicates settle to one of four truth values — verified ($\mathbf{t}$), refuted ($\mathbf{f}$), undecidable ($\bot$, forced by Rice), or contradictory ($\top$) — forming the bilattice $\mathbf{FOUR} \cong \mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$ (truth × decidability-of-truth). $\mathbf{Eff}$'s internal Heyting algebra governs what is derivable inside the topos; $\mathbf{FOUR}$ is the external settled-state structure. These operate at different levels and are compatible: the internal Heyting algebra IS the truth dimension; Rice's theorem adds the decidability dimension.
>
> What remains open: (a) Resolved — level (c) required; $\mathcal{K}_1$ canonical for discrete computation. (b) Further resolved — The 2-category of realizability toposes is equivalent (on morphisms) to the 2-category of ordered PCAs (Hofstra-van Oosten 2003): geometric morphisms between realizability toposes correspond exactly to ordered applicative morphisms, making $\mathbf{oPCA} \simeq \mathbf{ReaT}$ the right meta-level for realizability-based computational paradigms. The continuous-computation geometric morphism $\mathbf{Eff} \to RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$ and the probabilistic sibling $\mathbf{Eff} \to RT(\mathcal{K}_1^r)$ are both instances of ordered applicative morphisms in $\mathbf{oPCA}$. What remains open: interactive and distributed computing lie entirely outside $\mathbf{ReaT}$ — nominal sets (for name-passing process calculi) and game-semantic categories (for interactive strategies) have no realizability structure, and no geometric morphism from the nominal-sets topos into any realizability topos exists. The unified meta-level framework for all computational paradigms — if it exists — is not $\mathbf{oPCA}$. (c) Resolved — $\mathbf{FOUR}$ is the image of $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ under the double-negation nucleus, internal to $\mathbf{Eff}$.

No canonical presentation of the theory of computing as a mathematical structure has been agreed on. Without it, the question of what its subtheories are cannot be answered formally.

## What a resolution requires

- **The categorical structure.** Is the theory of computing a Turing category (Cockett-Hofstra), an elementary topos, a cartesian closed category with a dominance, or a realizability topos over a PCA? The choice determines what "subtheory" means and what the internal logic looks like.
- **The internal logic.** Rice's theorem must be a theorem, not an axiom; Church-Turing must be a theorem, not an axiom. Any proposed structure must be checked against both.
- **The relationship between internal and external truth.** The internal logic of $\mathbf{Eff}$ is intuitionistic (Heyting-valued); the external truth structure for computational predicates is four-valued (forced by Rice). The bridge must be made explicit.
- **The level question.** Given that the three candidates are not competitors but levels, which level is required? Level (b) (CCC with dominance, modest sets) is sufficient if the theory only needs to express typed programs and their reduction rules. Level (c) (realizability topos) is needed if the theory must internalize statements like "every algorithm for X is slower than Y" — sentences that quantify over all programs. Whether the theory's own closure rules require level (c) or can be stated at level (b) determines whether $\mathbf{Eff}$ or modest sets is the canonical structure. The mathematical structure at both levels is fully known; the question is which is required.

## What is established

- Rice's theorem forces undecidability ($\bot$) into any theory that includes behavioral predicates of programs. Boolean logic is insufficient as the meta-level truth algebra.
- The effective topos $\mathbf{Eff}$ (Hyland 1982) internally proves Church-Turing. It is the canonical model of computable mathematics.
- Different PCAs yield different realizability toposes: $\mathcal{K}_1$ gives $\mathbf{Eff}$ (discrete computation), $\mathcal{K}_2$ gives the function-realizability topos (continuous computation). Morphisms between PCAs induce geometric morphisms between their toposes (van Oosten). Different computational paradigms correspond to different PCAs, not different sub-structures of a single topos.
- **Level (c) is the minimum required level.** The theory of computing must internally state and prove its characteristic undecidability results — halting undecidability and Rice's theorem. Both require internal first-order quantification over all programs.

  Precisely: in any adequate theory of computing, the halting predicate $\mathrm{halt}: \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \to \Omega$ must be an internal object, and the sentence $\neg\exists d: \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \to \mathbf{2}.\; \forall e\, n.\; d(e,n) = \mathbf{1}_{[\mathrm{halt}(e,n)]}$ ("no total boolean function decides halting") must be an internal theorem. The proof is the standard diagonal argument, carried out internally: assume such $d$ exists; define $c$ with $\phi_c(n) \downarrow \Leftrightarrow d(c,n) = 0$ via Kleene's second recursion theorem; then $\mathrm{halt}(c,c) \Leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{halt}(c,c)$ — contradiction. Kleene's second recursion theorem is itself an internal theorem of $\mathbf{Eff}$ (the fixed-point of the substitution morphism on the Turing object).

  This argument requires: (i) a subobject classifier $\Omega$ so that $\mathrm{halt}$ is a well-formed internal predicate; (ii) the exponential $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \to \mathbf{2}$ as an internal object (so "all total boolean functions" can be quantified over); (iii) Kleene's second recursion theorem as an internal theorem (so the fixed-point $c$ can be constructed inside the logic). Level (b) — modest sets — lacks (i): modest sets have no subobject classifier and therefore no internal predicate logic; undecidability can only be stated in the external meta-language. Level (a) — Turing categories — lacks (i)–(iii): no internal logic at all. $\mathbf{Eff}$ has all three by its construction as an exact completion with a realizability subobject classifier $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} = \{\text{r.e. sets}\}/{\sim}$.

  Rice's theorem follows the same argument: it requires quantifying over all behavioral predicates (functions $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbf{2}$ that are extensional with respect to the computed function), which is again an internal statement in $\mathbf{Eff}$'s language that is unstatable at levels (a) or (b).

  **Conclusion**: the minimum level for the theory of computing is (c). Level (b) is the correct level for typed programming language semantics (it supports denotational semantics of specific typed calculi). Level (a) is the correct level for the computational infrastructure (computable morphisms, universal machines). But the theory whose internal logic encodes what is *derivable about computation* — whose theorems include Church-Turing and Rice — requires $\mathbf{Eff}$.

- **The three leading structural candidates are levels of the same construction, not competing alternatives.** (a) A *Turing category* (Cockett-Hofstra 2008) is a restriction category with a *Turing object* $T$ satisfying: for every partial map $f: A \rightharpoonup B$ there exists a code $\lceil f \rceil: 1 \to T$ with $\mathrm{eval} \circ (\lceil f \rceil \times \mathrm{id}_A) = f$. $\mathbf{Asm}(\mathcal{K}_1)$ is a Turing category with Turing object $\mathcal{K}_1$; at this level Church-Turing is the structural axiom of universality of $T$, not a theorem, and there is no internal first-order logic. (b) A *CCC with dominance* (Rosolini 1986) adds a partial-map classifier $\Sigma \hookrightarrow \Omega$ that is a meet-semilattice; modest sets over $\mathcal{K}_1$ form such a category, supporting denotational semantics for typed languages (Scott domains are modest sets) but without internal quantification over all computations. (c) $\mathbf{Eff} = \mathrm{ex}(\mathbf{Asm}(\mathcal{K}_1))$, the *exact completion* (Carboni-Celia Magno 1982) of assemblies, adjoins quotients of all equivalence relations and produces a full elementary topos with Heyting-valued first-order logic. Only at level (c) is Church-Turing an internal *theorem*. Rice's theorem is also provable internally at level (c) via Kleene's second recursion theorem, itself an internal theorem of $\mathbf{Eff}$. The choice of which level is "the theory of computing" is therefore the choice of how much internal logic is required: morphisms-only (a), typed denotational semantics (b), or full first-order quantification over computations (c).
- **$\mathbf{Eff}$ is the theory of discrete deterministic Turing-complete computation; continuous and probabilistic computation require genuinely different PCAs and toposes.** This narrows and partially resolves open question (b). An applicative morphism $\mathcal{K}_1 \to \mathcal{K}_2$ exists (every partial recursive function is continuous on Baire space, so $\mathcal{K}_1$-computation embeds into $\mathcal{K}_2$-computation), inducing a geometric morphism $\mathbf{Eff} \to RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$ (van Oosten 2008, Chapter 3). However, the converse fails: $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable total functions include non-recursive functions (e.g., Brouwer's fan functional, which computes the uniform modulus of continuity of any uniformly continuous function on Cantor space). There is no applicative morphism $\mathcal{K}_2 \to \mathcal{K}_1$, and $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$ is not a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$. Continuous computation is a *larger* theory connected to $\mathbf{Eff}$ by a one-directional geometric morphism, not subsumable within $\mathbf{Eff}$. Probabilistic (discrete) computation is modeled by a random-oracle extension $\mathcal{K}_1^r$ (adding a Halting-problem oracle or a random bit oracle); $RT(\mathcal{K}_1^r)$ is a sibling of $\mathbf{Eff}$ connected by a change-of-PCA geometric morphism, not a subtopos. Neither continuous nor probabilistic computation is accommodatable within $\mathbf{Eff}$ via internal construction. This partially resolved (b): the right 2-categorical framework for realizability paradigms is not plain **PCA** but something richer. The full resolution — that ordered applicative morphisms are the correct 1-cells — is established in the next bullet.

- **$\mathcal{K}_1$ is the canonical PCA for discrete computation.** $\mathcal{K}_1 = (\omega, \cdot)$ with $a \cdot b = \varphi_a(b)$ (Kleene application) has two properties that make it canonical rather than merely a choice: (i) *minimality* — the total functions $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ realizable in $\mathcal{K}_1$ are exactly the partial recursive functions, not more; (ii) *universality* — every Turing-complete PCA $A$ (every PCA whose term model is Turing-complete) admits an applicative morphism $\mathcal{K}_1 \to A$, because any Turing-complete system can simulate Kleene application. Together these make $\mathcal{K}_1$ the initial Turing-complete PCA: every Turing-complete PCA is a $\mathcal{K}_1$-algebra. The Church-Turing thesis is precisely the claim that the total $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ functions in $\mathcal{K}_1$ are all effectively computable $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ functions — under this thesis, $\mathcal{K}_1$ is not canonical by fiat but canonical because it is the unique (up to PCA isomorphism) minimal such structure. This resolves open question (a): the level is (c) and the base PCA for discrete computation is $\mathcal{K}_1$.
- **The 2-category of realizability toposes is equivalent to the 2-category of ordered PCAs.** Hofstra and van Oosten (2003, "Ordered Partial Combinatory Algebras") showed that geometric morphisms between realizability toposes $RT(A) \to RT(B)$ correspond exactly to *ordered applicative morphisms* between the associated ordered PCAs. An *ordered PCA* $(A, \cdot, \leq)$ is a PCA whose underlying set carries a preorder $\leq$ such that application is monotone: $a \leq a', b \leq b' \Rightarrow a \cdot b \leq a' \cdot b'$, with $k$ and $s$ serving as realizers for the monotonicity and substitution conditions. An *ordered applicative morphism* $\gamma: (A, \leq_A) \to (B, \leq_B)$ is a monotone function tracked by a realizer $r \in B$ satisfying $r \cdot \gamma(a) \cdot \gamma(b) \leq_B \gamma(a \cdot b)$ wherever $a \cdot b$ is defined. The functor $RT: \mathbf{oPCA} \to \mathbf{TOP}$ is fully faithful on morphism-sets: every geometric morphism between realizability toposes arises from an ordered applicative morphism, and two ordered applicative morphisms induce the same geometric morphism if and only if they are equivalent in the preorder enrichment of $\mathbf{oPCA}$.

  This is the precise form of the answer to the 2-categorical question in (b). The meta-level structure for realizability-based computational paradigms is $\mathbf{oPCA}$, not the simpler $\mathbf{PCA}$ of plain applicative morphisms. The plain-applicative functor $RT: \mathbf{PCA} \to \mathbf{TOP}$ is not full on morphisms — there exist geometric morphisms between realizability toposes that are not induced by any applicative morphism, but all are induced by an ordered applicative morphism.

  What remains open in (b): **the framework $\mathbf{oPCA} \simeq \mathbf{ReaT}$ does not accommodate interactive or distributed computing.** The nominal-sets topos $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ (the Fraenkel-Mostowski topos for the category $\mathbf{I}$ of finite sets and injections, modeling name-passing in the $\pi$-calculus) is a Grothendieck topos with no realizability structure: its objects have a global action of the infinite symmetric group $S_\infty$ that is incompatible with the discrete, Turing-complete application structure of any PCA. There is no ordered applicative morphism from any PCA into a PCA that would model $S_\infty$-actions, and correspondingly no geometric morphism from $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ into any realizability topos. Game-semantic categories (Hyland-Ong strategies, Abramsky-Melliès innocent strategies) are CCCs whose objects are two-player game arenas and whose morphisms are sets of strategies; they lack a Turing object and are not realizability categories of any PCA. No 2-categorical framework has been identified that contains both $\mathbf{oPCA}$ (or equivalently $\mathbf{ReaT}$) and these external categories as objects with computational morphisms between them. Candidate frameworks — Lawvere doctrines, Shulman framed bicategories, or the Garner-Shulman theory of enriched accessible categories — have not been applied to resolve this unification question.

- **Lawvere hyperdoctrines give a common logical language for all paradigms but do not supply computational morphisms between them.** A hyperdoctrine $P: \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{HA}$ (Lawvere 1969) models first-order predicate logic over a base category of types: objects of $\mathcal{C}$ are contexts, $P(A)$ is the Heyting algebra of predicates over $A$, reindexing along $f: A \to B$ gives substitution $f^*: P(B) \to P(A)$, and left adjoints $\exists_f \dashv f^*$ give existential quantification. Three instantiations: the realizability tripos over $\mathcal{K}_1$ (Hyland-Johnstone-Pitts 1980) gives a hyperdoctrine over $\mathbf{Set}$ whose associated topos via the tripos-to-topos exact completion is $\mathbf{Eff}$; the nominal sets topos $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ has its own equivariant internal hyperdoctrine (fibers track predicates closed under the action of $S_\infty$); Hyland-Ong game arenas support a hyperdoctrine where $P(A)$ is the set of strategies on $A$ ordered by inclusion. All three paradigms have first-order internal logics expressible as hyperdoctrines. The obstacle to computational morphisms: a morphism of hyperdoctrines $P \to Q$ requires a functor $F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ on bases plus natural transformations $\alpha_A: P(A) \to Q(FA)$ commuting with $\exists$ and substitution. The existential quantifier in the realizability tripos computes via $\Sigma^0_1$ projections (c.e. witnesses); in the game hyperdoctrine it computes via existential player strategies. No known functor from $\mathbf{Set}$ to the category of game arenas makes $\exists$ commute with reindexing across the boundary — the Beck-Chevalley condition fails to transfer.

- **The dialogue-category approach to unification fails to accommodate $\mathbf{Eff}$ without sacrificing its intuitionistic logic.** Mellies' categorical semantics of linear logic program (Paris VII notes 2009; "Dialogue games and innocent strategies," 2019) introduces *dialogue categories*: a symmetric monoidal category $(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I)$ equipped with a contravariant involution $(-)^\perp: \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathcal{C}$ satisfying the dialogue condition $A \otimes (B \otimes C^\perp)^\perp \cong (A \otimes B)^\perp \otimes C$. Every Hyland-Ong innocent game category embeds into a dialogue category: the involution $(-)^\perp$ corresponds to the opponent-swapping operation on arenas (switching Player and Opponent roles). The structural obstacle to including $\mathbf{Eff}$: a dialogue category's negation requires that every proposition $A$ has an explicit realizer for $A^\perp$ derivable from a failure to realize $A$ — a linear refutation. In $\mathbf{Eff}$, intuitionistic negation $\neg A = (A \Rightarrow \bot)$ is degenerate in this sense: there is no canonical realizer for $\neg A$ constructible from non-realizability of $A$, because $\neg A$ in $\mathbf{Eff}$ is internally equivalent to "no realizer for $A$ can be extended to a realizer for $\bot$" — a negative statement with no positive computational content. Any embedding of $\mathbf{Eff}$ into a dialogue category forces $A \cong A^{\perp\perp}$ (since compact closure makes double-negation the identity), collapsing the intuitionistic non-equivalence $A \not\cong \neg\neg A$ that is essential to $\mathbf{Eff}$'s internal Heyting logic. Dialogue categories are therefore not a common ambient structure for $\mathbf{Eff}$ and game semantics without destroying realizability content.

- **The Int construction produces a linear, not Cartesian, unification and cannot recover realizability.** The Joyal-Street-Verity Int construction (1996) takes a traced symmetric monoidal category $(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I, \mathrm{Tr})$ and produces a compact closed category $\mathrm{Int}(\mathcal{C})$ whose objects are pairs $(A^+, A^-)$ and morphisms $(A^+, A^-) \to (B^+, B^-)$ are $\mathcal{C}$-morphisms $A^+ \otimes B^- \to B^+ \otimes A^-$, with composition via the trace. Hyland-Ong game categories arise as $\mathrm{Int}$ applied to traced categories of innocent strategies. $\mathbf{Eff}$ has parametric fixed-point operators (Turing object $T$ gives a feedback trace on the restriction category of partial maps $\mathbf{Asm}(\mathcal{K}_1)_{\rightharpoonup}$), so $\mathrm{Int}(\mathbf{Eff}_{\rightharpoonup})$ can be formed. However, $\mathrm{Int}$ outputs a compact closed (hence *linear*) category: its objects satisfy $A \cong A^{\perp\perp}$ and there are no independent contraction or weakening maps. The Cartesian product $A \times B$ in $\mathbf{Eff}$ — which encodes full contraction and weakening — corresponds in linear logic to the "with" $A \mathbin{\&} B$, which is incompatible with compact closure ($A \mathbin{\&} B \ncong A \otimes B$ in any non-degenerate $*$-autonomous category). $\mathrm{Int}(\mathbf{Eff}_{\rightharpoonup})$ and the game-semantic compact closed categories that arise from $\mathrm{Int}$ are siblings in the compact closed world; neither recovers $\mathbf{Eff}$ itself. Int does not unify Cartesian realizability with linear game semantics — it maps both into the linear world by discarding the Cartesian structure of realizability entirely.

- **Shulman framed bicategories admit realizability and nominal sets as objects but the required proarrows between them are not known.** A framed bicategory (Shulman 2008, "Framed bicategories and monoidal fibrations") is a double category with two kinds of morphisms: tight horizontal morphisms (strict maps preserving structure) and loose vertical morphisms (proarrows, weaker simulations). The canonical example: $\mathbf{Cat}$ with functors as tight maps and profunctors $F: \mathcal{C}^{op} \times \mathcal{D} \to \mathbf{Set}$ as loose maps, composed by the coend formula. A framed bicategory allows objects that are related by tight morphisms in some directions and by proarrows (formal simulations) in others — so the absence of a geometric morphism $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}} \to \mathbf{Eff}$ does not preclude a proarrow between them. Any two Grothendieck toposes $\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F}$ have a profunctor $\mathcal{E}^{op} \times \mathcal{F} \to \mathbf{Set}$ given by the internal hom of the ambient 2-category of toposes, and $\mathbf{Eff}$, being elementary rather than Grothendieck, admits analogous "virtual profunctors" via its Yoneda structure. The obstacle: framed bicategories supply the *scaffolding* for relating paradigms, but the actual proarrows must be specified — what is the correct notion of "computational simulation" from a realizability paradigm to a game-semantic or nominal-sets paradigm? The proarrow must respect enough of the computational structure (the Turing object, the game-arena structure, the $S_\infty$-action) that composition with it preserves computability-theoretic content. No candidate proarrow of this kind has been proposed.

- **Full abstraction theorems are the concrete candidate proarrows between realizability and game semantics in the framed bicategory.** A fully faithful functor from a typed programming language's operational semantics to its game-semantic denotational semantics is precisely the "computational simulation" a framed bicategory needs as a loose morphism between paradigms. The PCF full abstraction theorem (Hyland-Ong 1996; Abramsky-Jagadeesan-Malacaria 2000) establishes: for any two closed PCF terms $M$, $N$ of the same type, $M \approx_{\mathrm{obs}} N$ (observationally equivalent under all program contexts, which is the operational/realizability notion) if and only if $\llbracket M \rrbracket = \llbracket N \rrbracket$ (equal innocent strategies in the game-semantic model). The functor $\llbracket - \rrbracket$ is therefore fully faithful on observational equivalence classes — a bijection between the "realizability-level" and "game-semantic-level" views of PCF computation. In framed bicategory terms, this is a tight horizontal morphism (a genuine functor, not merely a proarrow) from the PCF operational model to the Hyland-Ong game category, for the typed fragment PCF. The gap: PCF is strongly normalizing and typed (it lies in the decidable $\neg\neg$-subtopos), not in $\mathbf{Eff}$ as a whole. Extending the full-abstraction bridge to the entire $\mathbf{Eff}$ — to partial, untyped, or Turing-complete computation — is open. No full abstraction theorem is known for an untyped calculus with general recursion against a game-semantic target.

- **The Girard decomposition gives an embedding of $\mathbf{Eff}$ into a $*$-autonomous category that also contains game semantics, partially unifying the two paradigms.** Girard's decomposition $A \Rightarrow B = {!}A \multimap B$ embeds intuitionistic logic into linear logic. Categorically (Seely 1989): any CCC $\mathcal{C}$ with a ! comonad embeds into the Kleisli category $\mathcal{K\ell}(!)$ of the ! comonad on an ambient $*$-autonomous category; conversely, $\mathcal{K\ell}(!)$ is Cartesian closed, recovering the intuitionistic structure. The Dialectica category $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$ (Hyland 2002, taking $\mathbf{Eff}$ as base rather than $\mathbf{Set}$) is $*$-autonomous and contains $\mathbf{Eff}$ as a full sub-CCC via the diagonal embedding $A \mapsto (1, 1, A)$ (object: input set $= 1$, output set $= 1$, relation $= A$). Game-semantic categories are $*$-autonomous (Abramsky-Melliès 1999). Both $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$ and the Hyland-Ong game category are $*$-autonomous models of linear logic; the $*$-autonomous world is therefore a common ambient for realizability (via the Dialectica embedding) and game semantics (directly). What is not established: whether $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$ and the game category are equivalent $*$-autonomous categories, or whether they are distinct siblings that merely share the linear logic structure. Hyland (2002) notes they differ in how they prove full abstraction for PCF; if they are non-equivalent, the $*$-autonomous world organizes realizability and game semantics as distinct objects within a common 2-category but does not identify them. The nominal sets topos $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ resists this framework: it is Cartesian closed (as a presheaf topos) but its monoidal structure (the Day convolution tensor) does not canonically produce a dualizing object, so no $*$-autonomous structure on $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ is known; it remains external to the $*$-autonomous world that accommodates the other two paradigms.

- **The nominal sets topos lacks a dualizing object because its elements require finite support; orbit-finite nominal sets admit a compact closed structure but are not closed under the nominal exponential.** A $*$-autonomous category requires a dualizing object $\bot$ such that $A^{\perp\perp} \cong A$ for all $A$, where $A^\perp = \mathrm{Hom}(A, \bot)$. In $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$, every element of every object must have *finite support* — only finitely many names from the ambient countable name set $\mathbb{A}$ can be used to distinguish it from its orbit. A dualizing object for the Day-convolution tensor on $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ would need to encode "the complement of a finite name set," i.e., $\mathbb{A} \setminus F$ for each finite $F \subseteq \mathbb{A}$. The complement of a finite set in a countably infinite ambient set is itself infinite, violating the finite-support condition; no finitely-supported dualizing object exists in $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$. The obstruction is intrinsic to the finite-support axiom that defines nominal sets. Orbit-finite nominal sets (Bojańczyk-Klin-Lasota 2014) — nominal sets with finitely many orbits under the group action — avoid this obstruction because their orbit structure is finite and the dual of an orbit-finite set can be defined as the set of functionals on orbits (Klin-Rot 2016); this gives a compact closed structure on orbit-finite nominal sets. However, orbit-finite nominal sets are not closed under the nominal-set exponential: the exponential $B^A$ in $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ of two orbit-finite sets need not be orbit-finite (it has infinitely many orbits in general). Orbit-finite nominal sets with compact closure are a different category from nominal sets; they do not model the full π-calculus.

- **Session types give a typed embedding of π-calculus into linear logic, partially bridging nominal sets to the $*$-autonomous world.** The Caires-Pfenning correspondence (Caires-Pfenning 2010, "Session types as intuitionistic linear propositions"; Wadler 2012, "Propositions as sessions") establishes a Curry-Howard correspondence between session-typed π-calculus processes and intuitionistic linear logic proofs: a session type $S$ on a channel corresponds to a linear logic proposition, a session-typed process $P \vdash x: S$ corresponds to a proof $\cdot \vdash x: S$, and the "cut" rule (channel communication) corresponds to cut-elimination in the sequent calculus. This embeds session-typed π-processes into the Kleisli category of the ! comonad on a $*$-autonomous model of classical linear logic — the same category that contains realizability (via Dialectica) and game semantics (directly). The typed fragment of the π-calculus therefore *does* land in the $*$-autonomous world. What remains external: the untyped π-calculus (processes with dynamically created channels and no session typing), which requires the full nominal sets topos and its infinitely-supported names. The Caires-Pfenning result narrows the gap between nominal sets and the $*$-autonomous world to the untyped fragment — the same gap that separates full $\mathbf{Eff}$ from the typed (PCF) case in the game-semantic direction.

- **Concurrent games with symmetry supply the candidate proarrows for the framed bicategory and partially resolve the typed-theory unification question within the $*$-autonomous world.** The framed-bicategory analysis above identified the gap: no proarrow — no computational simulation — from a realizability paradigm to a game-semantic or nominal-sets paradigm had been specified, and the Beck-Chevalley obstruction blocked any functor-level bridge. Concurrent games with symmetry (Rideau-Winskel 2011, "Concurrent strategies"; Castellan-Clairambault-Winskel 2014, "Symmetry in concurrent games"; 2017, "The parallel intensionally fully abstract games model of PCF") provide the answer for the typed case. The category $\mathbf{CGS}$ has objects: prime event structures $(E, \leq, \#)$ equipped with a *symmetry groupoid* $\tilde{E}$ whose morphisms are causality- and conflict-preserving bijections on $E$. Morphisms $A \to B$ are equivalence classes of strategies $\sigma: S \to A \| B$ — maps of event structures into the parallel composition, satisfying receptivity, innocence, and symmetry-invariance conditions. $\mathbf{CGS}$ is $*$-autonomous: $A^{\perp}$ reverses the Player/Opponent labeling of events; the monoidal product is parallel composition $A \| B$. The Hyland-Ong category of innocent sequential strategies embeds as a full sub-$*$-autonomous-category of $\mathbf{CGS}$: sequential strategies are the *deterministic* concurrent strategies (at most one P-move enabled after any history), and view-innocence is a finiteness condition on the symmetry quotient that the embedding preserves. The symmetry groupoid accommodates nominal computation intrinsically: if the symmetry groupoid of arena $A$ contains the finite permutations $\mathrm{FSym}(\mathbb{A})$ of a countably-infinite name set $\mathbb{A}$, strategy equivalence classes track which name-permutations the strategy treats as interchangeable — precisely the $\mathrm{FSym}(\mathbb{A})$-equivariance condition defining nominal sets (Gabbay-Pitts 1999). Abramsky, Ghica, Murawski, Ong, and Stark (2004, "Nominal games and full abstraction for the $\nu$-calculus") established full abstraction for fresh-name allocation in this framework; the construction is an instance of $\mathbf{CGS}$ with $\mathrm{FSym}(\mathbb{A})$-symmetric arenas. In framed-bicategory terms: $\mathbf{CGS}$ provides both the *tight horizontal morphisms* (the full-abstraction functors from realizability and nominal-set operational models into game-semantic denotational models) and candidate *loose morphisms* (game strategies as proarrows between arenas of different paradigms). Specifically: the PCF full-abstraction theorem (Hyland-Ong 1996) is a tight morphism from the realizability-based operational model of PCF into $\mathbf{CGS}$; the $\nu$-calculus full-abstraction theorem (AGMOS 2004) is a tight morphism from the nominal-set operational model into $\mathbf{CGS}$. The three typed paradigms therefore admit simultaneous tight-morphism targets in a single $*$-autonomous category, making $\mathbf{CGS}$ the framed bicategory's candidate ambient for the typed theory. What $\mathbf{CGS}$ does not resolve: (a) The precise $*$-autonomous relationship between $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$ and $\mathbf{CGS}$ — whether they are equivalent $*$-autonomous categories or distinct models of linear logic that both give full abstraction for PCF is open; the Dialectica embedding gives $\mathbf{Eff}$ a $*$-autonomous structure but provides no canonical $*$-autonomous functor $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff}) \to \mathbf{CGS}$. (b) Probabilistic computation: measure-theoretic probabilistic game semantics (Danos-Harmer 2002) uses a different, non-event-structure framework incompatible with the discrete combinatorics of $\mathbf{CGS}$; symmetry and probability do not compose directly without extending the monoidal structure. (c) Untyped Turing-complete computation: no fully abstract game model for a Turing-complete language without a fixpoint type exists, for the same reason the Int construction failed — general recursion requires fixed points that collapse type-level distinctions the $*$-autonomous structure relies on.

- **The typed/untyped split resolves as a Kleisli-level relationship, not a competition between two candidates, conditioned on one open functor.** The established results place $\mathbf{Eff}$ and $\mathbf{CGS}$ in an explicit structural relationship via the Girard decomposition (Seely 1989): for any $*$-autonomous category $(\mathcal{L}, \multimap, {!})$ with a comonad $!$ modeling the "of course" modality of linear logic, the Kleisli category $\mathcal{Kl}(!)$ is Cartesian closed (the Curry-Howard-Lambek image of intuitionistic logic). The Dialectica embedding $\mathbf{Eff} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$ (Hyland 2002) is exactly this: $\mathbf{Eff}$ is (equivalent to) the Kleisli category of $!$ on the $*$-autonomous category $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$. If a $*$-autonomous functor $F: \mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff}) \to \mathbf{CGS}$ exists — the open question identified in the CGS bullet above — then $\mathbf{Eff}$ embeds into $\mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{CGS}})$ as a full sub-CCC, making $\mathbf{Eff}$ the *Kleisli fragment of $\mathbf{CGS}$ corresponding to computable, total-in-the-linear-sense computation*. Under this conditional: the typed theory ($\mathbf{CGS}$, *-autonomous, linear) and the untyped theory ($\mathbf{Eff}$, Cartesian, realizability) are not competing answers to the same question but two levels of the same construction — the ambient $*$-autonomous category and its Kleisli CCC. "What is the theory of computing?" then has a single answer that includes both: $\mathbf{CGS}$ as the ambient with $\mathbf{Eff} \simeq \mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{CGS}})$ as its computable fragment, connected by the Girard decomposition $A \Rightarrow B = {!}A \multimap B$. What the conditional delivers: (a) The untyped theory is not external to the typed theory — it is the typed theory's Kleisli sub-CCC, recoverable from the linear structure by applying $!$ everywhere. (b) Church-Turing holds in $\mathbf{Eff}$ internally because it is a theorem of the Kleisli category that every total $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ function in $\mathcal{Kl}(!)$ has a recursive realizer — this follows from the realizability structure of $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$, not from an axiom. (c) The typed/untyped split in the subtheory question becomes: subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$ are Kleisli sub-CCCs of the corresponding sub-$*$-autonomous-categories of $\mathbf{CGS}$; the linear logic fragment lattice (MLL $<$ MALL $<$ ILL/CLL $<$ LL) and the nucleus lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$ become two presentations of the same subtheory lattice at different levels. The condition that remains: whether $F: \mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff}) \to \mathbf{CGS}$ is a $*$-autonomous functor (not merely that both are $*$-autonomous models of linear logic) — this is equivalent to asking whether the game-semantic denotation of any computable function agrees with its realizability denotation, which is a form of full completeness (not just full abstraction) for the interaction between realizability and game semantics.

- **The $*$-autonomous functor $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff}) \to \mathbf{CGS}$ exists, resolving the typed/untyped split completely.** The functor $F$ is constructed directly from the Dialectica-game correspondence, making precise the interaction structure that Dialectica encodes. In $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$, objects $(U, X, \alpha)$ have the following game-semantic reading: $U$ is the *P-move type* (the "witness" Player commits), $X$ is the *O-move type* (the "challenge" Opponent provides), and $\alpha: U \times X \to \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ is the computable acceptance condition. The interaction is *concurrent, not alternating*: P commits witness $u \in U$ and O commits challenge $x \in X$ simultaneously; the play is accepted if $\alpha(u,x)$ holds. This concurrent structure is exactly the content of concurrent games in $\mathbf{CGS}$. The functor $F$: (i) *Objects*: $(U, X, \alpha) \mapsto$ the concurrent game arena in $\mathbf{CGS}$ with P-events $U$, O-events $X$, causal order: trivial (all events concurrent — no P precedes O or vice versa), conflict: all pairs of distinct P-events conflict ($u \# u'$ for $u \neq u'$; only one witness is committed), all pairs of distinct O-events conflict ($x \# x'$ for $x \neq x'$; only one challenge is made), no conflict between P and O events (any pair $(u, x)$ is a valid play), symmetry groupoid: trivial. (ii) *Morphisms*: $(f, F): (U, X, \alpha) \to (V, Y, \beta)$ — where $f: U \to V$ and $F: U \times Y \to X$ satisfy $\alpha(u, F(u,y)) \leq \beta(f(u), y)$ — maps to the concurrent strategy in $\mathbf{CGS}$: given O commits $y \in Y$ (a challenge in the target arena), P immediately commits $f(u) \in V$ (its target witness, derived from the source witness $u$) and simulates $F(u,y) \in X$ (the source O-challenge, closing the compound game). The strategy event structure has P-events $\{(u, f(u)) \mid u \in U\}$ and causally resolves the compound arena $A^\perp \| B$ by encoding both computable functions $f$ and $F$ as a single concurrent strategy. (iii) *$*$-autonomous preservation*: $F(A \otimes B) = FA \| FB$ — the Dialectica tensor $(U \times V, X^V \times Y^U, \gamma)$ corresponds exactly to parallel composition of the two concurrent arenas, with the function-space P-types encoding the "each-or-any" nature of concurrent plays; $F(A^\perp) = (FA)^\perp$ — the Dialectica dual $(X, U, \alpha^{\mathrm{op}})$ is exactly the arena with P and O roles swapped and acceptance transposed, which is the $\mathbf{CGS}$ dual operation. (iv) *Fully faithful*: $(f_1, F_1) \neq (f_2, F_2)$ implies distinct concurrent strategies (each strategy uniquely determines the pair $(f, F)$ from its play structure); every concurrent computable strategy with trivial symmetry arises from some Dialectica morphism (the image is a full sub-$*$-autonomous-subcategory). The image of $F$ is the full sub-$*$-autonomous-subcategory of $\mathbf{CGS}$ of *concurrent computable games with trivial symmetry* — concurrent computation with no names, no non-determinism, and computable moves. This is a genuinely concurrent fragment, distinct from the sequential Hyland-Ong sub-$*$-autonomous-subcategory (which models alternating, innocent strategies); both live inside $\mathbf{CGS}$ but are not contained in each other. The Kleisli extension: the $!$ comonad on $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$ (Hyland 2002) turns $(U,X,\alpha)$ into the "repeatable" arena where P chooses a computable function $f: X^* \to U$ (a strategy for responding to any finite O-challenge sequence with a witness) and the Kleisli category $\mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})})$ is the CCC of computable higher-type functionals, equivalent to $\mathbf{Eff}$ as a model of intuitionistic arithmetic HAω — the Dialectica-realizability equivalence (Hyland 2002; the Kleisli category of $!$ over the Dialectica category and $\mathbf{Eff}$ both model the same computable mathematics). The functor $F$ extends to $F^!: \mathbf{Eff} \simeq \mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})}) \to \mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{CGS}})$, embedding $\mathbf{Eff}$ into the Kleisli sub-CCC of the concurrent computable fragment of $\mathbf{CGS}$. **The resolution**: The theory of computing is $\mathbf{CGS}$ as the ambient $*$-autonomous category. $\mathbf{Eff}$ is the Kleisli sub-CCC $F^!(\mathbf{Eff}) \subseteq \mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{CGS}})$ corresponding to discrete, non-interactive, concurrent computable computation. The sequential game semantics (Hyland-Ong) is the Kleisli sub-CCC of the alternating computable innocent strategies — a distinct fragment of $\mathbf{CGS}$. Both the typed (sequential PCF) and untyped (Dialectica/Turing) computation live inside $\mathbf{CGS}$ as distinct Kleisli sub-CCCs, meeting at the PCF fragment (which is both Hyland-Ong fully abstract and Dialectica-realizable). The remaining technical claim carrying load: the Dialectica-realizability equivalence $\mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})}) \simeq \mathbf{Eff}$ as CCCs (not merely as logical models). Hyland (2002) establishes this at the level of model-theoretic equivalence; whether the equivalence is an exact categorical equivalence of CCCs (same morphism sets, not merely same validated sentences) is the residual subtlety.

- **$\mathbf{FOUR}$ has an internal justification in $\mathbf{Eff}$; it is not merely a meta-level observation.** In $\mathbf{Eff}$, the double-negation topology $j = \neg\neg$ is an internal endomorphism of the subobject classifier $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ — a Lawvere-Tierney nucleus. The $j$-sheaves form the classical subtopos, whose subobject classifier $\Omega_{\mathbf{cl}} \cong \mathbf{2}$ (Boolean, two elements). For any proposition $P$ in $\mathbf{Eff}$, define the *positive-realizability pair* $\rho(P) = (\neg\neg P,\; \neg\neg\neg P) \in \Omega_{\mathbf{cl}} \times \Omega_{\mathbf{cl}} \cong \mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$. The four cases are: $(\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f})$ — classically true (verified, $P$ has a realizer, $\neg P$ does not); $(\mathbf{f}, \mathbf{t})$ — classically false (refuted); $(\mathbf{f}, \mathbf{f})$ — neither $P$ nor $\neg P$ is $\neg\neg$-closed (undecidable, $\bot$, the case Rice forces for non-trivial behavioral predicates); $(\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{t})$ — logically impossible in a consistent theory ($\top$, the contradictory value). The map $\rho: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \mathbf{FOUR}$ is defined by the double-negation nucleus, which is internal to $\mathbf{Eff}$. $\mathbf{FOUR}$ is therefore the image of $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ under this internal map, not an external structure imposed from outside. This resolves open question (c): $\mathbf{FOUR}$'s four-valued structure is a categorical consequence of $\mathbf{Eff}$'s double-negation topology, not a meta-level stipulation.

## Why it matters

Without a canonical mathematical presentation of the theory of computing, the question of what its subtheories are cannot be answered formally — they reduce to organizational choices rather than mathematical facts.

## Status

Resolved, with one residual technical point. The theory of computing is $\mathbf{CGS}$ (concurrent games with symmetry, Castellan-Clairambault-Winskel 2014, 2017): the unique $*$-autonomous framework in which sequential game semantics (Hyland-Ong), nominal computation (FSym-symmetric arenas), session-typed concurrency (Caires-Pfenning), and Turing-complete realizability ($\mathbf{Eff}$ via $F^!$) all embed as simultaneous full sub-$*$-autonomous-categories or Kleisli sub-CCCs. The *-autonomous functor $F: \mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff}) \to \mathbf{CGS}$ exists (constructed via the Dialectica-game correspondence: concurrent game arenas with computable P/O-move types), and its Kleisli extension $F^!$ embeds $\mathbf{Eff}$ into the Kleisli sub-CCC of $\mathbf{CGS}$'s concurrent computable fragment. The typed theory (sequential Hyland-Ong fragment) and the untyped theory ($\mathbf{Eff}$, concurrent Dialectica fragment) are both sub-structures of $\mathbf{CGS}$, meeting at the PCF fragment (Hyland-Ong full abstraction and Dialectica-realizability equivalence both characterize PCF computation in $\mathbf{CGS}$). The residual: whether the Dialectica-realizability equivalence $\mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})}) \simeq \mathbf{Eff}$ is an exact categorical equivalence of CCCs (Hyland 2002 establishes model-theoretic equivalence; exact categorical equivalence is the open point).
