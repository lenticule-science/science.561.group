Insurgent infrastructure is a term emsenn introduces within the [standardization as counterinsurgency](./standardization-as-counterinsurgency.md) framework to name a specific kind of technical system: one that emerges from communities seeking to change the conditions imposed by existing platforms.

An infrastructure is *insurgent* when it scores high on generativity, autonomy support, and movement value, while its [legibility](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/sociology/theories/critical-theory/theories/abolition/theories/state-repression/terms/legibility.md) and corporate extractable value are low or uncertain. Insurgent infrastructures are often messy, evolving, and embedded in local practices. Their meaning is tied to their role in a broader political and social project — building alternatives, resisting [enclosure](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/sociology/theories/critical-theory/theories/marxism/theories/political-economy/terms/enclosure.md), or experimenting with new governance forms.

## Characteristics

| Dimension | Insurgent infrastructure |
|---|---|
| **Generativity** | High — multiple implementations, experiments, divergent practices |
| **Legibility** | Low — not yet inscribed into formal standards or regulatory frameworks |
| **Autonomy support** | High — communities can operate independently, fork, and self-govern |
| **Movement value** | High — using the infrastructure is an expression of commitment to an alternative |
| **Corporate extractable value** | Low or unclear — no obvious path for firms to monetize it |

## Examples

Early [ActivityPub](../_index.md) and the [fediverse](../../../fediverse/index.md) fit this description. Anyone could run an instance, multiple experiments coexisted (microblogging, photo sharing, event planning), and participation expressed commitment to "another web." Large firms didn't see a clear profit path, and the protocol was obscure to standards bodies.

Other potential instances include certain blockchain protocols in their early phases, peer-to-peer file-sharing systems before corporate and legal engagement, and community mesh networking projects.

## Important qualifications

Insurgent infrastructures aren't inherently virtuous or stable. They can be exclusionary, fragile, or internally contested. The designation isn't a moral judgment — it identifies a *position* in the relationship between infrastructure and institutional order. What matters is that they inhabit a space outside the settled arrangements of platform [capitalism](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/sociology/theories/critical-theory/theories/anarchism/terms/capitalism.md) and established web governance.

Their existence poses a question to those arrangements: will they be ignored, suppressed, or brought into alignment? The [SaC framework](./standardization-as-counterinsurgency.md) analyzes what happens when the answer is "brought into alignment."
