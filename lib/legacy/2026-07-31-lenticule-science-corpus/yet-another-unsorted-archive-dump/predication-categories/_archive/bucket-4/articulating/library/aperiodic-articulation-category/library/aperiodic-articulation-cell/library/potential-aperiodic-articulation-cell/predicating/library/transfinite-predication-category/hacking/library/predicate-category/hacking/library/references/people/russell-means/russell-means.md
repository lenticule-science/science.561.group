Russell Charles Means (1939–2012) was an Oglala Lakota organizer, polemicist, and political thinker who, through the American Indian Movement (AIM) and a long subsequent career of writing and confrontation, became one of the most visible advocates for Indigenous sovereignty in the second half of the twentieth century. He was a leader in the 1973 occupation of Wounded Knee, the 1972 Trail of Broken Treaties, the long-running defense of the Black Hills, and the founding of the Republic of Lakotah declaration of withdrawal from United States treaties.

## Core ideas

- **Sovereignty as the unbargained-for premise.** Indigenous nations are nations: the question is not whether the settler state will recognize that sovereignty but how Indigenous nations exercise it regardless. Treaties are nation-to-nation instruments, not domestic civil-rights claims, and the settler state's chronic failure to honor them is a fact about the settler state, not about the standing of Indigenous nations.
- **Against the European revolutionary frame.** Means's 1980 essay "For America to Live, Europe Must Die" argues that Marxism and the broader European revolutionary tradition share with capitalism a productivist, materialist, and state-centric metaphysics — a worldview that treats the earth as resource and the polity as machine. Indigenous traditions, he argues, do not need this frame and are deformed by it. Anarchism is closer than Marxism but is still mostly a European inheritance answering European problems.
- **The body as the line.** Means's politics was a politics of physical confrontation — Wounded Knee, the BIA building, the courts. The body of the Indigenous organizer placed in the path of the settler-state's violence is the medium through which the structure becomes visible. This is not a tactical preference but a recognition that the structure operates by being unseen.
- **Refusal of assimilation, including its progressive forms.** The progressive settler offer — civil-rights inclusion, multicultural recognition, "Native American" as a hyphenated American identity — is, in Means's analysis, the same elimination logic in a softer register. The point is not to be included in the settler polity on better terms but to remain a separate polity.

## Key works and statements

- "For America to Live, Europe Must Die" (Black Hills International Survival Gathering, 1980)
- *Where White Men Fear to Tread: The Autobiography of Russell Means* (with Marvin J. Wolf, 1995)
- The Trail of Broken Treaties Twenty Points (1972, AIM, co-authored)
- Numerous speeches, court statements, and the 2007 Lakotah Freedom Delegation declaration

## Where his work figures in this library

Means is foundational for the [indigenous-anarchism](../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/critical-theory/theories/anarchism/theories/indigenous-anarchism/_index.md) subdomain's argument that the encounter between anarchism and Indigenous political tradition cannot proceed by absorbing Indigenous politics into a European revolutionary lineage. His critique of European revolutionary metaphysics is the load-bearing reference for that subdomain's refusal of the "anarchism-as-export" framing.
