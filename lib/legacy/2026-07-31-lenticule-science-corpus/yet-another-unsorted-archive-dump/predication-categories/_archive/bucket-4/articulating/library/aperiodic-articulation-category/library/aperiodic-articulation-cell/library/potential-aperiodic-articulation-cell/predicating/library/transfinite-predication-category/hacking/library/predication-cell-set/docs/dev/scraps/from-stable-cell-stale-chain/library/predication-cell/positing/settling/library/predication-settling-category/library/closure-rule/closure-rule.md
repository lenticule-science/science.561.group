name: closure rule
description: A positive Π_1-schema on a predication signature (L, C, E). Each rule has an antecedent (a condition on existing cell values) and a consequent (a cell-value update). The closure operator on a predication fiber algebra is generated by firing all rules until quiescence. Closure rules are the syntactic specification of how a predication settling evolves; the closure operator is their semantic effect.
---

## Shape

A closure rule has:
- An **antecedent**: a condition on cell values. E.g., "if cell (a, parent-of, b) is at t and cell (b, parent-of, c) is at t, then..."
- A **consequent**: a cell-value update. E.g., "...then cell (a, ancestor-of, c) is at t."

Rules are positive (the consequent strictly adds information; closure is inflationary in knowledge-order). They are Π_1 (universally quantified over the entity set; the antecedent's variables range over E).

## Effect

The closure operator cl_p on a predication fiber algebra A_p is the least operator satisfying every applicable rule's consequent whenever its antecedent holds. Operationally: at each step, find a rule whose antecedent is satisfied; apply its consequent. Repeat until no rule fires (quiescence).

## Why the syntactic level matters

The rule-level structure is what makes the operator's regime visible. Two examples:

- Rules with **quantifier-free antecedents** (no ∀-block over E) generate Scott-continuous closures. The closure can be computed by chasing finite-support updates.
- Rules with **∀-blocks over infinite E** generate non-Scott-continuous closures. The closure requires reasoning over the whole entity set; finite-support chasing is insufficient.

A settling's regime (Scott-continuous or not) affects what's computable, what's decidable, and what scheduling discipline the firing requires.
