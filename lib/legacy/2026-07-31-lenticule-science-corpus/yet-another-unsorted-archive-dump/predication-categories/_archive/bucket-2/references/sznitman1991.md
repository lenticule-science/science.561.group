## Annotation

The standard exposition of the **propagation-of-chaos** technique for proving mean-field limits of large interacting particle systems. Given $N$ exchangeable particles evolving via interactions of the form "drift on particle $i$ depends on the empirical measure of all $N$ particles," propagation-of-chaos establishes that as $N \to \infty$, the joint law of any finite subset of particles converges to a product of identical *limit measures*, with each limit measure satisfying a self-consistent McKean–Vlasov equation. The technique provides the rigorous bridge from finite-population microscopic dynamics to continuous-population macroscopic dynamics — central to mean-field game theory, kinetic theory, and the broader interacting-particle-systems literature.

For the corpus, this is the standard reference for **scaling-limit theory of populations of interacting agents**, relevant to the agentic-swarm-theory study plan's **Direction 5 (mean-field / scaling limits)**. The corpus's existing material is purely finite (the [multi-observer system](../objects/multi-observer-system.md) has a generic small observer category, with worked instances at small $|O|$); a scaling-limit theory would extend this to large-$N$ behavior. The corpus's distinctive content for this direction: bilattice-valued multi-observer systems likely have a *qualitatively different* mean-field limit than the standard real-valued case, because the bilattice's $\top$-content (paraconsistency from disagreement) may persist in the limit in regimes where pairwise disagreement is dense — a phenomenon with no real-valued analogue.

This is research-program-scale work, not session-scale; the reference is included for the literature-engagement record rather than for an immediate application.
