# Study and use

The Derivation built the spatiotemporal knowledge presheaf and the structural apparatus around it. This part turns to topics that *consume* that apparatus — applications, refinements, and side excursions whose existence depends on the Derivation but whose results don't feed back into the main spine.

These are the leaf clusters of the program — they sit at the periphery of the dependency graph: each takes the central machinery as input and produces something new (a decision procedure, an alternative formulation, a refined invariant, a connection to outside literature). They can be read independently of one another, and a reader interested in only one application can skip the others.

The topics:

- **Modal calculus on bilattice fibers**: a four-valued modal logic on the fiber algebras, with finite model property and decidable consequence. The classical modal-logic apparatus, lifted to bilattice values.

- **Distributed-systems applications**: CRDT convergence, the Mayer–Vietoris reformulation, and CALM-style monotonicity. The settling machinery interprets directly as a model of distributed inference.

- **Paraconsistent variants**: $\top$-tracking, the inconsistency sheaf, and a paraconsistent settling-associated sheaf that carries refined structure when the settling hosts contradictions as legitimate states.

- **Bigraded cohomology refinements**: a two-axis cohomology that resolves truth-order and knowledge-order data separately, with comparison long exact sequences and mapping-cone presentations.

- **Bilattice-valued cohomology**: a structurally-faithful refinement that values sheaves in d-frames rather than $\mathbf{Ab}$, recovering the bigraded content as a *shadow* and adding consistency / totality structure. The d-frame chart has its definitions and per-regime cohomology theorems (strict and Scott-continuous regimes at draft level; transfinite-bounded and aperiodic regimes pending), the Q-enriched cohomology framework, and the comparison-with-shadow theorem.

- **Verdier duality on the bitopological settling site**: the cosheaf side of the bilattice-valued cohomology chapter. A derived equivalence between Q-enriched sheaves and cosheaves, with the d-frame's intrinsic swap symmetry composing with the spatial sheaf-cosheaf duality to give a "two dualities composing" structure unique to the bilattice-flavored setting. Established at draft level across all four regimes via the cellular extension plan (Layers 1, 2, and 3); downstream consequences (aperiodic co-classification, strong-MLD refinement, six-functor formalism) are open.

The list will grow. As the program develops further — six-functor formalisms, $(\infty, 1)$-refinements, deformation theory, representation theory of the predicate category — each new development becomes a candidate for a chapter here when the math matures. The Derivation's spine remains stable; this part grows.
