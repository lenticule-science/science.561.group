## Annotation

Develops Austin's ([@austin1962]) speech-act theory into a systematic taxonomy and gives precise sincerity conditions for each illocutionary type. The five-class taxonomy: **assertives** (asserting, claiming, stating, hypothesizing — direction of fit: word-to-world; sincerity condition: speaker believes the proposition); **directives** (ordering, requesting); **commissives** (promising, vowing); **expressives** (thanking, congratulating); **declaratives** (pronouncing, excommunicating — direction of fit: both simultaneously; no sincerity condition; constitutive by performance).

Within the assertive class, Searle distinguishes the strength of illocutionary commitment: stating is neutral; asserting is strong (no hedging); claiming is intermediate (personal commitment acknowledged as contestable); hypothesizing is weak (explicitly provisional). This gradation within the assertive class is the empirical phenomenon that the bilattice's truth-order $\leq_t$ might formally capture: asserting is truth-designated ($\geq_t \mathbf{t}$), claiming is truth-designated but multi-observer-contested (individual settling has $\mathbf{t}$, merged settling has $\top$), hypothesizing is knowledge-weakened (low positive evidence, $q$ near $\bot$ but above it on the truth side).

Cited in the [agentive positing morphism](../morphisms/agentive-positing-morphism.md) and [assertive positing morphism](../morphisms/assertive-positing-morphism.md) for the fine-grained assertive taxonomy and the claim/assert distinction: asserting = individual posit agrees with the merged settling; claiming = individual posit diverges (merged settling is contested at $\top$). Searle's gradation within the assertive class (asserting, claiming, hypothesizing) is captured by the relation between individual and merged settling values in a multi-observer system.
