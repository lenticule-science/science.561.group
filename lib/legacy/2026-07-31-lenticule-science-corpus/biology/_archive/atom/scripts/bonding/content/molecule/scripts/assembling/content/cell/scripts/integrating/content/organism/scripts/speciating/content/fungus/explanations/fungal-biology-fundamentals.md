## Assumed audience

General adult with no formal biology background. The goal is to understand what fungi are and how they work.

## What fungi are

Fungi are their own kingdom — not plants, not animals. They are [heterotrophs](../terms/heterotroph.md), meaning they cannot photosynthesize. Instead, fungi obtain nutrition by [extracellular digestion](../terms/extracellular-digestion.md) — secreting [enzymes](../../terms/enzyme.md) that digest organic matter externally, then absorbing the released nutrients. Their cell walls are made of [chitin](../terms/chitin.md), not [cellulose](../terms/cellulose.md) like plants. Genetically, fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants (see [fungal cell biology](../concepts/fungal-cell-biology.md) for more on what makes a fungal cell distinctive).

## The fungal body

The main body of most fungi is the [mycelium](../terms/mycelium.md) — a network of threadlike cells called [hyphae](../terms/hyphae.md). Hyphae grow at their tips, branch, and can fuse with other hyphae (a process called [anastomosis](../terms/anastomosis.md)) to form interconnected networks. Hyphae may be divided into cells by cross-walls called [septa](../terms/septum.md), or they may be continuous tubes without divisions (a condition called [coenocytic](../terms/coenocytic.md)). The mycelium grows through its [substrate](../terms/substrate.md) — soil, wood, leaf litter, or a living host — with [cytoplasm](../terms/cytoplasm.md) streaming through the network to distribute nutrients and signals.

## Fruiting bodies

The mushroom, bracket, puffball, or truffle that most people recognize as "a fungus" is actually a [fruiting body](../terms/fruiting-body.md) — a temporary reproductive structure. Its job is to produce and disperse [spores](../terms/spore.md). The organism itself is the mycelium hidden in the substrate.

## Spores and reproduction

Spores are the reproductive units of fungi. They can be produced sexually (involving mating of compatible types) or asexually (clonal). A single fruiting body may release billions of spores. When a spore lands on a suitable substrate, it germinates to produce a new hypha, which grows into a new mycelium.

## Major groups

The major phyla of fungi include:

- **Ascomycota** (sac fungi) — morels, truffles, yeasts, and many molds. They produce spores inside sac-like structures called [asci](../terms/ascus.md).
- **Basidiomycota** (club fungi) — mushrooms, brackets, puffballs, rusts, and smuts. They produce spores on club-shaped structures called [basidia](../terms/basidium.md).
- Other phyla include Zygomycota (bread molds), Chytridiomycota (aquatic fungi with flagellated [zoospores](../terms/zoospore.md)), and Glomeromycota (arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi).

For more detail, see [Fungal Taxonomy](../topics/fungal-taxonomy.md).

## Why this matters

Understanding basic fungal biology is prerequisite to understanding their ecological roles, their symbiotic partnerships with plants, and their practical applications in cultivation and fermentation.
