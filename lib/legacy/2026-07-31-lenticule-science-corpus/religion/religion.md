Religion encompasses the beliefs, practices, institutions, and cosmologies through which human communities orient themselves toward what they take to be ultimate — whether conceived as divine, sacred, natural, or transcendent.

This root is organized around religious systems as coherent traditions rather than comparative categories. The aim is to let each tradition be legible on its own terms before it is placed in relation to others.

**Religious systems** is the primary branch, holding material on the world's major religious traditions: their histories, theologies, texts, rituals, and social forms.

The study of religion as an academic discipline — the sociology of religion, phenomenology of religion, religious studies methodology — appears here alongside primary sources from the traditions themselves.
