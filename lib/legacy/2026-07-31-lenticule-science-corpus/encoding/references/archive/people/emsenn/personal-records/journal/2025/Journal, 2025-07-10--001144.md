Last night and the previous one, I actually [[sleeping|slept]] more than I expected, which is nice in some ways but also is giving me the energy I need to feel [[anxious]] about my [[Gaming Vacation, 2025, Summer]], and what I'm going to do afterward.

In fact, today (so far) and most of [[2025-07-09|yesterday]], I probably spent more time [[thinking]] about [[video games]] than I did [[playing]] them, and I think I spent more time thinking about [[my researching]] than anything else. Whoops!

The main thing keeping me [[emotionally unstable]] about this stuff is the [[encoding]] issues I keep running into: [[Obsidian]], [[Emacs]], or a [[my ideal software|bespoke software]]. I think I'm continuing to triangulate around it in meaningful ways, though: yesterday I did some good work recognizing all the acts I want to induce through my researching and writing, and today I went ahead and set up a file for "[[my ideal software]]", where I'll start trying to record these ideas, so I can get toward a more complete understanding of what I want.

On that note, another idea I had about the sotware, that I had with some iteration of trying to make my perfect software, is: at some point I had it so that in-engine things basically emitted data structures of their own actions to whoever they were linked with, and there wasn't necessarily any assumption there'd be anything hard-coded to deal with that emission, already. This let me write entries for *anything*, knowing it'll trigger a "failed to parse" in the log that'll prompt me to write new functions.

This let me record efverything from fixing a pot of coffee to a new essay draft as emissions coming from a synthetic "me" within the system, without having to worry about parsing it right away.

That was a way of dealing with one big benefit of a soft, qualitative system like text markup, whether done in Emacs or Obsidian: the ability to record data that isn't quantifiable, *yet*.

Another facet of things is, I have... really murky language around the dynamics I'm trying to program, and that is a very bad starting point. By developing from [[telemetricism]] to [[navonics]] and [[praxonics]] through [[relational calculus]] into the current [[tekodynamics]], there's been a lot of different ways I've talked about the same concepts. Developing a unified grammar, that has a way of representing the concepts that got developed across those fields, is going to be critical.

And making it so that grammar is immediately programmable is probably one of the best ways to make sure it actually *works*.

...Which could be an endorsement of doing things in Org-mode, so that I can write out these dynamics in soft speech, [[Cubical Agba]], [[Python]], [[LISP]], and so on. Especially if I can use [[Org-mode]] as a way of encoding the tekodynamics of Org-mode entries themselves...

...Which indicates the [[ouroboros]] of the problem: I can only do any of that, *once* I make the grammar. If I develop the grammar through developing the space, the space will end up essentially littered or polluted, the way I've run into with the current notes.

Of course, maybe if I made it the *goal* of, creating a way to encode the tekodynamics of soft-and-hard tekodynamics, rather than just futzing around with "writing" being the goal, it would get me to clean up that litter as part of developing the project?

Pragmatically, that sounds like a lot of time spent creating and revising metadata to see what works, when it may be better to use real critical thinking to come up with an actual solid plan.

And it may be that my habits of work are just, not inclined to actually investing in that kind of planning, and I'm attempting various ways of getting around that, and my anxiety around this matter is coming in part from reaching a point of needing to recognize that I am not, at present, skillful or perhaps even able to derive and implement a plan.

But, finding that as a [[keystone]] within [[deconstructing]] these problems feels like [[progress]]. If I stop and think about it, it does feel like I'm making actual progress on the issue every day: slowly building a loose [[shape]] of what I want to be doing, that can serve as a [[mirage]] on the [[horizon]] for me to find what is actually out there.

Additionally, these daily journals are helping seed [[my research notes]] with more writing that is [[authoring|authored]] solely by me, not me and [[ChatGPT]], which should help (ironically) me better [[co-authoring|co-author]] [[text|texts]] with [[Large Language Model|LLMs]].

I can't explain the jump in thought, but the multi-encoding capability - the ability for Org-mode to let me use it as Obsidian, *and* write actual functions that do what the text says - combined with the vibe of "the more I seed an LLM, the better it does," really does mean I need to decide to switch back to Org-mode, rather than keep using Obsidian. ChatGPT fed off raw soft data can't help but treat it as a plant treats compost: only useful in the ways it knows it to be useful. ChatGPT with an actual documented API that it can use will help make sure it isn't faking its participation.


And it does feel like LLMs - especially big central ones - should be a participant, maybe not in thinking, at that point, but at providing the information about how the tekodynamics are understood by it, a weird deviant collective unconsciousness.

LLMs, including small in-house ones, with custom prompting, can be useful for then helping translate soft information into tekodynamics, once there is fine-tuning on *how* to do that.

Of course, with Emacs there isn't an easy way to give my whole notes to an AI, the way [[Obsidian Copilot]] enables. So that alone honestly might be enough to make [[Obsidian]] the right move - at least for now, while I work on a solid plan for software, that I can then take straight to [[Replit]].

So that brings me back again to, sticking with Obsidian until I'm ready to write something truly my own, and then just, do that directly in Replit's IDE. There's bits of that decision which make me uncomfortable, clearly, but talking through that discomfort in a way that refines the goal takes less time, even if I'm doing it daily, than switching back and forth. (And, given I'm in Obsidian, is giving the Copilot a greater-and-greater repository of information about why I should stick to Obsidian, and what I should be doing while I'm here.)

And of course there's this notion in the back of my head that I'm simply not the programmer sort and what I need to do is figure out the minimal scripting necessary (probably really clever AI prompts) to make Obsidian do enough of what I want to do that I'm putting out the raw text I want, and then using, gasp, [[community]] to handle bringing it to the world in a more polished way.

But like if my writing is actually good, I should be able to just, write on the Web end up with the support to keep doing better writing. The trick then is focusing on which parts of these tools help make my writing better.

Of course the alternative is that I'm dodging doing the real work of formalizing an important thing, with tekodynamics, by choosing to want to write. To which I say, an abundance of stories seems more important for climate breakdown than yet another form of novel calculus.

But, then I can say, the novel calculus could induce an abundance of stories far greater than anything I can handwrite - and they might end up being better.

Or they might end up being worse, and there is some creative spark I bring to storytelling that a calculator would require too much programming to do, relative to how much programming I can do.

So the question becomes: write now, or engineer now a system to write later?

To which I answer myself: the first step of actually engineering a system is to create a real specification or blueprint, and you don't have that. Stick with Obsidian for now, until you "cannot not" make [[my ideal software|your perfect software]].
