Spent the day playing a mix of [[video game|video games]], none for very long: [[Factorio]], [[RimWorld]], and [[Foundation]] made up the bulk of my playtime. Still trying to do a [[Gaming Vacation, 2025, Summer]], [[orienting]] toward being able to be better, or at least, less distressed about what I want to do with my time and passion moving forward in life. Just to [[affirming|affirm]] it, the answer is definitely somewhere in the [[domain]] of [[writing]], and I even think I have a rough idea of the main [[genre]] I'll focus on: [[tabletop role-playing game setting|TTRPG settings]], but the *how* of doing that is something I'm going to keep struggling with. I think an important step will be, for me, creating something like a [[MUD engine]], something like an [[ontoeidodynamic calculator]] (the closest a [[digital computer]] can computer to a [[tekodynamic calculator]])... but also I really need to figure out what I need to adopt as a belief that will let me just, genuinely accept using [[Obsidian]] as the right software for me, for now. Or, accept that it is unacceptable and I need to move toward a bespoke software much sooner.

But then I get to, do I really need that, or want to take the time with such things, if I'm trying to write. I've already spent oodles of time writing scripts for [[Emacs]] that I've lost, ultimately making just 1 [[TTRPG adventure]] with it before losing it.

And the truth is, that with [[Large Language Model|LLMs]] developing as they are, it may end up being irrelevant, to perhaps even harmful, putting in a bunch of work to quantify my writing in a way computers are getting better at inferring, each day. A minimum amount of metadata at the front to help contextualize a [[Markdown document]], and a selection of Markdown documents, might be what's really important. It's at that point where I think my idea like [[HashUp]] might become even more important, as AI begins to do the work of linking stuff through its own thinking, and all I need to do is say whether I'm referencing something (one `#` before the term) or want the term to use the material as reference for its own defining (two `##` before the term).

Of course, every use of the word does some part to determine its definition, so maybe that's an inappropriate idea, in the first place, especially with respect toward the ability of AI to weigh in on thinsg.

Anyway, I've been thinking a lot because so many of the games I like to play involving some combination of [[simulating]] and [[worlding]], but I'm finding myself, even after an 8-year-long break from video games, finding it hard to not want to put those games down and do my own worlding and simulating, with my writing. Which was getting me stuck on the whole, how to do that, Obsidian or what, especially if I want to do *simulating*, not just *worlding*. And this is without considering where [[imagining]] comes into things, and puts [[playing]] and [[gaming]] and [[working]] and [[earning]] on the backburner.

But even listing out all the acts that need to be realized through [[my writing]] helps validate the sense its [[overwhelming]] to find a [[good way]] to do it: earning, gaming, imagining, simulating, working, worlding.

And I'm sure there's others I've missed. But the point that's relevant is that video games don't (at present) manage to fulfill all of those, and so while those sorts of games are fulfilling in some ways (as material for critical engagement), they aren't really [[fun]] in the way that play needs to be.

Which leaves me games that are affective, and that basically gives me two categories: [[stressing|stressful]] and [[relaxing]], and games that move between the two.

[[Red Dead Redemption 2]] is a great example of that third way, especially because the cadence is informed by player decisions that are often guided by the player wanting to change the mood the game is giving them, while also throwing in vibe-shifts with random events.

[[Freeman Guerilla Warfare]] is another example of this, but the [[complexity]] and [[emergent storytelling]] mean it doesn't rely on [[aesthetic]] as much as *Red Dead*, which I'm realizing is part of what I want from a "good" vacation game: aesthetic or otherwise non-cognitive (or low-thinking, at least) modulation of my affective and somatic fields.

[[Superhot]] nails this in some ways, and I was playing that for a minute last night, but it's *one* vibe. *Red Dead* might be the best game in my library for this, but I'm really hoping to find at least a couple that offer a similar thing, so I can switch it up when I'm bored on Red Dead.

Of course, now that I'm honing in on what I think my work should be - at least enough to give it a shot, maybe I'll just come into my Obsidian notes to write about my own worlds and stories, when I'm not wanting to do Red Dead... and to do that, I'll certainly end up producing lots of other writing, like the [[essay|essays]] folk have come to hate reading.
