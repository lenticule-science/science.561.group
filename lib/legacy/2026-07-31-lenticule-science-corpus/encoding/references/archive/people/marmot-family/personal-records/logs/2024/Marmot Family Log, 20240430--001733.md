20240430T095120 [[zeds/old-notes/Delta Register/emsenn]]: Our main [[goal]] for the day is to [[writing|write]] and [[publishing|publish]] our [[Groundhog Herb Project]] project, so that folk like [[Ralph McDonald|Ralph]] and [[Peter Gelderloos|Peter]] have something to point folk to. Previous discussion of this idea happened in [[Marmot Family Log, 20240425]]. We talked about wanting to be able to purchase a selection of native medicinal perennials, to bootstrap our own nursery to supply folk in [[Porcupine, South Dakota|Porcupine]] and [[Wounded Knee, South Dakota]], and being able to process herbs according to [[Current Good Manufacturing Practice]]. These two things, the nursery and processing facility, weave together to allow our [[climate weirding adapting]] to challenge some mechanisms of [[apartheid]] that exist on [[Pine Ridge Indian Reservation|Pine Ridge]]. If we have a processing facility (to be clear, we are talking about a frame tent and other mobile equipment here, not constructing any building), then we can turn the abundance of invasive plants that have use into a source of income for folk here: we can collect things like mullein and catnip, which are displacing plants that have been here much longer, and make them available to herbalists across the Reservation and Turtle Island, turning what is [[bad medicine]] for this land into [[good medicine]] for people here and elsewhere. And if we have a nursery, we can ensure that plants more suited to the region's present and future are being introduced to replace the invasives. And that's about the whole idea: a way to process invasives so we can give them away or sell them, and a way to grow other plants to replace what we harvest. One thing I want to integrate is the... near-total lack of opportunity to participate in the existent economic system. And while joining that economy is obviously not a long-term solution to any problems anyone is facing, the current reality is, people don't have money, and people need money to get the things they need. (That's... kind of what apartheid is: only those within a certain group (in this case, economic participants) are able to access the social infrastructure). After talking it over with families here in [[Porcupine, South Dakota|Porcupine]] and [[Wounded Knee, South Dakota|Wounded Knee]], facilitating a flow of harvested herbs into the US economy would be a relatively feasible way for folk to participate in things. Already, harvesting plants to sell is one of the common ways a person here might get themselves some money: find some sweetgrass or sage, bundle it up, and sell it to tourists who stop to check out the [[Wounded Knee Massacre Site]]. The thing is, [[Verbascum thapsus|mullein]] is way more abundant, has a bigger market, *and* is us, essentially, selling [[pollution]] back to those that polluted it: the mullein is a form of pollution caused by cattle ranching, which is being done to feed the same economy that would buy our plants. Everyone I asked, would rather harvest and sell that, than harvest and sell things we view as sacred medicines, knowing that as soon as they're sold, they stop being the medicine they were. So that's the idea, a real Groundhog idea: take colonial pollution, sell it back to the polluters in a way that encourages the pollution to end, while using the change that activity causes to be ready to fill the void left by the pollution ending. The specifics of it: where to buy the plants, how we'll meet CGMP, how we'll sell the herbs, that's stuff we still need to plan. But the intention, the idea, it all aligns with our values, and what the folk around us are asking for. We should take the day to draft out this explanation in a good way, leaving space for the details we still need to plan.
