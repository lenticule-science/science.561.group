# Walker 1983 — *Lakota Myth*

The narrative-cosmogonic volume of the posthumous Walker trilogy.
Contains the full cosmogonic cycle as Walker reworked it from Sword's and Tyon's recordings — beginning with Íŋyaŋ alone in Haŋhépi and continuing through the creation of Makȟá, Mahpíya (Sky), Wí (Sun), Hanwí (Moon), the four winds, and the subsequent emergence of beings.

## On the editorial reconstruction

Jahner notes in the introduction that this volume contains Walker's own *literary reconstruction* of the cosmogonic material — an essayistic working-up of the recorded fragments into a continuous narrative.
The reconstruction was done by Walker, drawing on his Lakota informants' testimony, with the aim of presenting the cosmogonic cycle as a coherent whole.

This editorial fact matters: *Lakota Myth* is not direct-transcript material in the way some of the *Lakota Belief and Ritual* sections are.
It is Walker's reconstruction.
The underlying material is informant-recorded, but the connective tissue is Walker's.
Readers should treat the *narrative* as Walker's-reconstruction-of-the-cosmogony and the *cosmogonic facts* as the informant-recorded substrate.

## Key sections cited in this subtheory

### The Íŋyaŋ creation cycle

The volume's first major narrative section. Íŋyaŋ alone in Haŋhépi; the structural impossibility of remaining alone; the self-opening of his veins; the rising of his blood as Makȟá and mní; the rising of the power-in-the-blood as Škáŋ; the creation of Wí to warm Makȟá; the creation of Hanwí to keep Wí company; the creation of the four winds (*Tȟaté*) to move the sky; the emergence of the subsequent beings.

This cycle is the source for the cosmogonic content of [iyan](teaching/explanations/iyan.md), [iyan-hanhepi](teaching/explanations/iyan-hanhepi.md), [hanhepi](teaching/explanations/hanhepi.md), and [skan](teaching/explanations/skan.md).

### The emergence of the Pte Oyate (the Buffalo Nation)

The volume continues with the cycle by which the Buffalo Nation (and subsequently the human beings) emerged.
The structural pattern of *Iyókheya* (setting-apart) runs through this section: each emergence is a setting-apart of a new being-form from its predecessor.
See [iyokheya](teaching/explanations/iyokheya.md).

### The Wakíŋyaŋ and the heyókha

The Wakíŋyaŋ — the Thunder-beings of the West — appear in this volume's narrative as the powers of sudden-storm and contradiction-of-clear-sky.
The heyókha-vocation is grounded in dreams of these beings.
See [heyokha](teaching/explanations/heyokha.md).
