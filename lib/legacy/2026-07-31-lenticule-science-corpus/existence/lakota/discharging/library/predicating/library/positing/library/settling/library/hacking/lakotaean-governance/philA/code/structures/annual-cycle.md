# Annual cycle

The polity's life runs on a recurrent annual settling-cycle.
The cycle structures the polity's coordinated activity: when and where to camp, when and where to hunt, when to convene the omníčiye, when to conduct the sun dance, when to honor the dead, when to recognize new adults.
The cycle is the polity's *temporal scaffolding* — the time-axis over which the polity's settling-evolution is organized.

## The cycle's structure

The cycle is keyed to four ceremonial markers and to the seasonal availability of subsistence:

**Wíiyaȟpeyatuya-wí (Spring, roughly March–May).** Camps disperse from the larger winter-gatherings into tiyóšpaye-scale or small-cluster camps.
Spring hunts (small game, early returning birds), gathering of early plants (prairie turnips, etc.).
The Wakíŋyaŋ return with the first thunder; heyókha-dreamers fulfill their year's obligations under the renewed thunder-presence.

**Bloketu (Summer, roughly June–August).** The seven Tȟitȟúŋwaŋ oyate gather at the sun-dance grounds — historically at varying locations, with Pȟahá Sápa as the most central.
The sun dance is conducted (cf. [sun-dance-as-polity-renewal](../morphisms/sun-dance-as-polity-renewal.md)).
The confederacy-scope omníčiye is in active session (per (K1) of [omniciye-council](omniciye-council.md)).
Major buffalo hunts are coordinated.
Marriages are arranged across oyate (kin-extension morphism).
Naming ceremonies for new adults are conducted.

**Pteȟíŋčhaŋ-wí (Autumn, roughly September–November).** The oyate disperses again toward winter camps.
Final fall hunts.
Stocking of dried meat (wasná, pemmican) for winter.
Settling-down of marriage-arrangements made at the summer convocation.

**Waníyetu (Winter, roughly December–February).** active winter-camp at the oyate's customary winter-place.
Story-telling (oyaka-pi), apprenticeship and teaching, the long form of the polity's substrate-transmission.
Children learn kin-relations, language, virtue-content, ceremony-protocol through winter teaching.
New names of the year are settled and recorded in the winter-count (waniyetu-iyawapi).

## Substrate-algebraic structure

The annual cycle is a *periodic settling*: a sequence of settlings $v_{y_1}, v_{y_2}, \ldots$ where $y_i$ is the year-index, with the structural property that each year's settling has the same *shape* (same convening-events, same ceremonies, same coordination-events) while specific cells differ.

The cycle's period-bound is structural: by year's end, each polity-unit's standing-cells are at a known stage of the cycle's progression.
The polity is *never not* in some-or-other point of the cycle; the cycle is the polity's continuous-time-scaffolding.

The winter-count (waniyetu-iyawapi) is the substrate-record-keeping mechanism for the annual cycle: each year's *most-distinctive event* is recorded as one named year-cell.
Examples from the Brulé winter-count: *Pȟahíŋ Síca* (Bad Porcupine) was a year named for a man; *Tʼa Wičháša Khípi* (Two-Men-Killed) was a year named for a notable event.
The winter-count is the polity's distributed-memory of its history — each waniyetu-iyawapi keeper holds a continuous record going back many generations.

## The cycle and the four-times-four

The cycle's four-season structure mirrors the four-superior-Wakȟáŋ structure of the substrate (cf. [tobtob-kin](../../../../../../../philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/tobtob-kin.md)).
The cosmogonic four runs through the annual cycle's four-season structure: spring (Wakíŋyaŋ-return, the Wakȟáŋ-of-the-West) → summer (sun-dance, Wí-presence at the celestial apex) → autumn (the gathering of stores, Makȟá-receiving) → winter (story-teaching, Íŋyaŋ-stability).
The four superior Wakȟáŋ are co-present with each season's substrate-dynamics.

This is *not* an arbitrary correspondence imposed on the seasonal cycle.
The substrate's four-valued structure organizes both the cosmogonic ontology and the temporal scaffolding; the same four-pattern runs at every scale (cf. [tobtob-kin](../../../../../../../philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/tobtob-kin.md) §"The lesser twelve" on the fractal property of the structure).

## Disruption of the cycle

The forced-reservation period (1880s onward) disrupted the cycle's structural integrity:

- **Confined to specific lands**: the seasonal migration was constrained; winter and summer camps could no longer move freely.
- **Sun dance suppressed**: criminalization of the sun dance (1881–1934 under federal Indian policy, partially restored thereafter, fully restored after the 1978 American Indian Religious Freedom Act) removed the cycle's confederacy-scope convocation.
- **Buffalo gone**: the central subsistence-coordination of summer (the big hunt) was eliminated by the extermination, dissolving the structural reason for summer convocation at scale.
- **Boarding-school separation**: children were removed from the winter-teaching cycle, the substrate-transmission's primary site.
  The winter-count's intergenerational transmission was disrupted; many keepers lost continuity.

The contemporary reclamation reconstitutes the cycle in modified form:

- **Annual sun dance** is conducted at multiple sites across the reservations and at Pȟahá Sápa.
- **Winter-count** is being reconstituted from extant records and contemporary record-keeping.
- **Buffalo hunt** is conducted at small scale via the InterTribal Buffalo Council and at specific reservations' herds.
- **Confederacy-scope convocations** are convened at NoDAPL (Standing Rock 2016), at LandBack events, at language-revitalization summits — re-establishing the structural-pattern of periodic-convocation under modified content.
