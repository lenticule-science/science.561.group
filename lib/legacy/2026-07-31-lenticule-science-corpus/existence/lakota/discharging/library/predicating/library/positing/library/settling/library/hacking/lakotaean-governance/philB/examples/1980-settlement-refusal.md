# The 1980 Settlement Refusal as polity-scale iyúhá-šni

In *United States v.
Sioux Nation of Indians*, 448 U.S. 371 (1980), the Supreme Court awarded approximately $106 million in compensation for the 1877 unconstitutional taking of Pȟahá Sápa.
With accumulated interest, the award has grown to over $2 billion as of the present.
The Lakota Nation has continuously refused to accept the settlement.
This example walks through the substrate-content of that refusal and what makes it structurally-stable across four-plus decades.

## The Court's substrate-operation

The Court's substrate-operation, in $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$:

1. **Acknowledgment-cell**: $\langle \mathrm{US}, \texttt{acknowledges-1877-act-as-unconstitutional}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$.
  The Court ruled that the 1877 Black Hills Act was a "rank case of dishonorable dealings" by the United States — a substantive substrate-recognition of wrongdoing from within $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$.

2. **Compensation-cell**: $\langle \mathrm{US}, \texttt{owes-monetary-compensation}, \mathrm{Sioux\ Nation}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ with value $\approx \$106\mathrm{M}$ (plus interest from 1877).

3. **Substitution-rule firing (within $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$)**: $\langle \mathrm{Sioux\ Nation}, \texttt{receives-compensation-in-lieu-of-land-return}\rangle$ proposed at $\mathbf{t}$ — the substrate-rule of *just compensation* under the Fifth Amendment's takings clause, which substitutes monetary value for the taken property.

The Court's substrate-operation was *within $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$* — it took the property-cell-framework as substrate-given and resolved the case within that framework's rules.
The Court did not (and structurally could not, from within its own substrate) recognize the kin-cell over Pȟahá Sápa as a non-property-class cell.

## The polity's iyúhá-šni response

The Lakota polity's response was [iyúhá-šni](../morphisms/iyuha-sni-withdrawal.md) at confederacy scope from the settlement-engagement:

- The polity did not accept the offered compensation.
- The compensation funds sat in trust at the Department of the Interior, accumulating interest.
  As of 2025, the trust holds approximately $2 billion.
- No tribal-government or other Lakota-recognized body has taken steps to claim or distribute the funds.
- The polity's substrate-state: $\langle \mathrm{O\check{c}h\acute{e}thi\ \check{S}ak\acute{o}wi\eta}, \texttt{accepts-monetary-substitution}\rangle = \mathbf{f}$.

The iyúhá-šni preserves:

- $\langle \mathrm{O\check{c}h\acute{e}thi\ \check{S}ak\acute{o}wi\eta}, \texttt{kin-relation-with-place}, \mathrm{Pa\check{h}\acute{a}\ Sapa}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked — the kin-cell continues at $\mathbf{t}$.
- $\langle \mathrm{O\check{c}h\acute{e}thi\ \check{S}ak\acute{o}wi\eta}, \mathrm{US}, \texttt{at-treaty-with-1868}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked — the 1868 treaty remains in force on the Lakota substrate.

The refusal *is* the substrate-mechanism that preserves these cells.
Accepting the settlement would *substantively* fire the substitution-rule, dropping the kin-cell to $\bot$ (substituted-by-compensation).
The polity has refused to participate in the firing.

## The structural-impossibility argument

Per [paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution](../theorems/paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution.md), the substitution is *substrate-algebraically inadmissible*.
The kin-cell is in $\Pi^\mathrm{plc}$; the settlement-value cell is in $\Pi^\mathrm{off}$; no morphism in $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ converts one into the other.
The Court's substitution-rule does not exist in $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$.

The substrate-content of "the Black Hills are not for sale": it is not a moral position.
It is a *structural fact about which closure-rule firings are admissible in the polity's substrate*.
There is no $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$-rule whose consequent is "kin-cell-converted-to-property-cell."
The conversion *cannot happen* through $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$-firing.

The Court's substitution-rule fires *within $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$* — there, it is a valid rule.
But the Lakota polity is not bound to $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$; the polity holds its own autarkeia (per [wolakhota-as-autarkeia](../theorems/wolakhota-as-autarkeia.md)).
The $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$-rule does not propagate to $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$.

## The continuing $\top$-state

The refusal sustains a *persistent* $\top$ at the inter-polity overlap:

- $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$-substrate: $\langle \mathrm{Sioux\ Nation}, \texttt{compensated-and-case-resolved}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ from the U.S. side.
- $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$-substrate: $\langle \mathrm{O\check{c}h\acute{e}thi\ \check{S}ak\acute{o}wi\eta}, \texttt{kin-relation-with-Pa\check{h}\acute{a}\ Sapa}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked.
- Merged-multi-observer-substrate: $\langle \cdot, \texttt{Black-Hills-status}\rangle = \top$, contested.

The $\top$ is structural and *cannot be resolved* without a wakȟáŋ-bearing operation that the Lakota polity would have to perform — and that the polity has refused, per [paha-sapa-kin-cell](../objects/paha-sapa-kin-cell.md), because it would require the wakȟáŋ-beings to ceremonially-leave Pȟahá Sápa, which is substrate-impossible.

## What sustains the refusal across decades

The refusal has been substrate-stable across more than four decades.
The substrate-mechanisms:

1. **No central decision-point**: the refusal is not a single resolution that could be overturned.
  It is the continuing non-firing of the substitution-rule across many separate substrate-events.
2. **Inter-generational substrate-transmission**: the kin-cell and the refusal-substrate are taught to each generation through wótakuye-work (intergenerational teaching).
  The substrate-position is renewed annually at sun-dance and at confederacy-scope gatherings.
3. **Iyúhá-šni-as-standing-act**: the refusal is not a "no" given once; it is a continuing iyúhá-šni position that the polity *renews* at each potential settlement-event (each new federal proposal, each new monetary-trust accumulation, each suggestion of accepting partial-payment in exchange for partial-LandBack).
4. **Federal-overlay's structural-misrecognition**: the federal-overlay continues to operate as if the case is settled-and-the-Lakota-just-haven't-claimed.
  The Lakota substrate operates as if the case is *not settled*.
  The asymmetry has been substrate-stable because neither side can compel the other.

## LandBack as substrate-restoration

The contemporary LandBack movement is substrate-restoration toward direct kin-cell-firing on the Black Hills.
Specific operations:

- The 2020 protests on the Mount Rushmore visit by President Trump — substrate-restoration of the wahúŋkpa-work and iyúhá-šni position at the site.
- The 2021 Pe' Sla land-purchase by the Sicangu Lakota Oyate and other Lakota groups — direct kin-cell-substrate-restoration on specific parcels within Pȟahá Sápa.
- The continuing Sioux Nation Treaty Council work — sustaining the inter-polity overlap-position.
- The annual Big Foot Memorial Ride and similar substrate-bearing-events.

The substrate-content of LandBack: *not* securing compensation for the loss, but *restoring direct kin-cell firing* on the place.
The two are not interchangeable; the substrate distinguishes them.
