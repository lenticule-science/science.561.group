# Iyúhá-šni is `?`-side weakening

**Claim.** *Iyúhá-šni* — the Lakota political operational primitive of withdrawal-without-severance — is the substrate's $?
A$-side weakening, the dual to the $!$-comonad in linear logic.
Operationally: a member of a tiyóšpaye exercises iyúhá-šni on a particular collective action by declining to participate; the kin-relations persist, but the *engagement* with that action drops to $\bot$ (under-determined for that engagement).
The relations are preserved; the participation withdraws.

Categorically, this is discarding on the negative side without violating MALL's no-copy/no-discard on the positive side.
The `?
` comodality in linear logic permits weakening on its argument; its $\eta$-rule allows the move from "I am engaged" to "no commitment about engagement," without forcing severance of the underlying $!$-structured kin-relations.

## Argument

MALL (multiplicative-additive linear logic — the predication fiber algebra's home per [`theory-of-predicated-computing`](../../../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/prose/learning/claims/theory-of-predicated-computing.md)) does not admit weakening on positive resources: each cell is used exactly once.
This is the structural fact that makes kin-relations *non-discardable* in the tiyóšpaye — a relative cannot simply discard the relation.
The $!$-comonad on top of MALL adds duplicability of positive resources (kin-relations can be acted on across multiple contexts); its dual $?$-comodality adds *weakening on negative resources* — engagement-cells can be left under-determined.

The Lakota tradition's iyúhá-šni is exactly this structural move.
A member who disagrees with a collective action does not need to leave the tiyóšpaye; they exercise weakening on the *engagement-cell* (the substrate cell encoding "I commit to this action") by letting it remain at $\bot$ — no commitment in either direction.
The kin-relations (the $!$-structured cells) persist.
The collective action proceeds when no member exercises iyúhá-šni; if too many do, the action does not proceed.

At the federation level: a tiyóšpaye that disagrees with an oyate-level decision exercises iyúhá-šni at its own scale — declines to participate without dissolving the federal relation.
Same structural move at the parallel-composition scale.

## Significance

Western liberal political theory treats dissent as either *consent* (the dissenter has been heard and accepts the outcome) or *exit* (the dissenter leaves the polity).
The exit-or-consent dichotomy does not have vocabulary for *withdrawal-without-severance* as a positive political primitive.
Indigenous political theory does — both in the Lakota tradition (iyúhá-šni; consensus-by-withdrawal) and in adjacent traditions (Audra Simpson's *Mohawk Interruptus* (2014) develops the refusal-politics framework explicitly for the Mohawk case with structural commonalities).
The categorical apparatus that names this primitive is the $?$-comodality in linear logic, which is dual to the $!$-comonad and which permits weakening without copying.

The structural point: kin-relations are $!$-structured (can be acted on across contexts, cannot be discarded); engagements are $?$-structured (can be left under-determined).
The two comodalities operate on opposite polarities of the same substrate, and the tradition has named exactly this distinction in its operational vocabulary.
The framework's $!$/$?$ duality and the Lakota tradition's relation/engagement distinction are the same structural object.
