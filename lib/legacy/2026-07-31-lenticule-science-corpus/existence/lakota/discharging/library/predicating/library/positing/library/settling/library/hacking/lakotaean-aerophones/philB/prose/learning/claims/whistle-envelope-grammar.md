# Claim. The eagle wing-bone whistle and related single-pitch aerophones articulate using a small grammar of timbral envelopes: sharp-attack-short-sustain, sharp-attack-long-sustain-with-warble, rolled-attack-long-sustain, and pulsed-sustain.

**Claim.** The [eagle-wing-bone-whistle](teaching/explanations/eagle-wing-bone-whistle.md) and the related single-pitch Lakotaean aerophones ([crane-bone-whistle](teaching/explanations/crane-bone-whistle.md), [grass-dance-whistle](teaching/explanations/grass-dance-whistle.md), and other whistles built on the same fixed-pitch end-blown principle) draw their principal music-theoretic content from a small grammar of *timbral envelopes* — characteristic shapes of attack, sustain, and decay applied to the fixed [fundamental](teaching/explanations/fundamental.md) pitch.
The four principal envelope archetypes are: (a) *sharp-attack, short-sustain* — a brief percussive call typically used for alerts or accents; (b) *sharp-attack, long-sustain, warble-decay* — a sustained signal with terminal [warble](teaching/explanations/warble.md), used for structural highlights; (c) *rolled-attack, long-sustain* — a gradually-onsetting sustained tone, used for ceremonial entries; (d) *pulsed-sustain* — a sustained tone articulated by [breath-pulse](teaching/explanations/breath-pulse.md) into a rhythmic structure.
A whistle "tune" is a sequence of these envelopes deployed against the song's structural moments.

**Argument.**

*Single-pitch instruments have music-theoretic content located in non-pitch dimensions.* A single-pitch instrument has, by definition, no melodic pitch variation.
The conclusion that such an instrument therefore has no music-theoretic content would be a category error — it would assume that pitch is the only music-theoretic dimension.
Other dimensions are available: rhythm (how note-events are distributed in time), timbral envelope (the shape of each note-event in time), and ensemble function (how the note-events relate to the surrounding musical context).
The [eagle-wing-bone-whistle](teaching/explanations/eagle-wing-bone-whistle.md) and related instruments locate their music-theoretic content in exactly these dimensions, and a music theory of single-pitch instruments must address them substantively.

*The four envelope archetypes are identifiable and named.* The four envelope archetypes correspond to musically distinct types of whistle articulation, identifiable in performance and (in living tradition) named by players when discussing the repertoire.

The *sharp-attack, short-sustain* envelope (a) is the briefest and most percussive of the four — a sharp [embouchure](teaching/explanations/embouchure.md)-initiated attack followed by a brief sustain (typically less than half a second) and a clean cutoff.
Its principal use is as an *alert call* (drawing attention) or as an *accent* (marking a beat or structural moment within a song-and-dance context).
The [grass-dance-whistle](teaching/explanations/grass-dance-whistle.md)'s step-change accents typically use this envelope.

The *sharp-attack, long-sustain with warble decay* envelope (b) is the most expressive of the four — a sharp attack initiates the tone, the tone sustains at full volume for an extended duration (one to several seconds), and the tone decays through a [warble](teaching/explanations/warble.md) into silence.
Its principal use is as a *signal* of structural significance — marking the climax of a [phrase](teaching/explanations/phrase.md), the peak of a sung [strophe](teaching/explanations/strophe.md), or a moment of ceremonial heightening.
The terminal warble is the principal expressive content of this envelope and is what distinguishes it from a simple plain sustain.

The *rolled-attack, long-sustain* envelope (c) is the most settled of the four — a gradual onset (a "rolled" attack, in which the breath pressure rises gently from zero rather than initiating sharply) reaches full volume over a fraction of a second, the tone sustains at full volume, and the tone decays cleanly without warble.
Its principal use is as a *ceremonial entry* — marking the beginning of a sung [strophe](teaching/explanations/strophe.md) or the opening of a ceremonial moment — where the gradual onset signals deliberation rather than alertness or surprise.

The *pulsed-sustain* envelope (d) is the most rhythmic of the four — a sustained tone is articulated by a regular or patterned sequence of [breath-pulses](teaching/explanations/breath-pulse.md), producing a rhythmic figure on top of the fundamental pitch.
Its principal use is as a *rhythmic-textural element* — providing a rhythmic structure that interacts with the [drum](teaching/explanations/drum.md)'s pulse in [heterophony](teaching/explanations/heterophony.md), or providing a rhythmic articulation within a sustained ceremonial moment.

*The grammar is the basis for whistle "tunes".* A whistle "tune" — a sequence of whistle articulations deployed across a song — is not a pitch-melody (since the whistle has only one pitch) but a *sequence of envelope-and-rhythm choices*, drawn from the four-envelope grammar plus the [whistle-rhythm-vocabulary](./whistle-rhythm-vocabulary.md) of compositional rhythmic figures.
The whistle tune is recognizable to knowledgeable listeners as that whistle's contribution to the song, and skilled whistle-bearers can learn and reproduce whistle tunes as definite musical material.

*The grammar parallels but differs from the cedar-flute ornament vocabulary.* The cedar-flute [ornament-vocabulary](./ornament-vocabulary.md) is a vocabulary applied to melodic content as variation on a melodic line.
The whistle envelope-grammar is a vocabulary that *constitutes* the music — there is no underlying melodic line for the envelopes to ornament, the envelopes themselves are the music.
The two vocabularies share some elements (both have a [warble](teaching/explanations/warble.md) cadential figure; both have a [breath-pulse](teaching/explanations/breath-pulse.md) articulation device), but they operate at different structural levels.

*The grammar is small and learnable.* The four-envelope-archetype grammar is small (four items) and the combination rules (with the [whistle-rhythm-vocabulary](./whistle-rhythm-vocabulary.md)) are reasonably regular.
This is consistent with the whistle's role as a structural-articulation instrument rather than a melodic instrument: a small grammar provides exactly the expressive vocabulary needed for structural articulation, without the burden of an extensive vocabulary that an unmediated melodic role would require.
