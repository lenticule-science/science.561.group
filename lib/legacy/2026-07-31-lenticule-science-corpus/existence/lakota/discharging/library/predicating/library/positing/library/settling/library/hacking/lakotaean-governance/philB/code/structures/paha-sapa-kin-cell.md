# Pȟahá Sápa kin-cell

The relation between the Lakota people and Pȟahá Sápa — the Black Hills — is encoded in the substrate as a kin-cell at polity scale, *not* as a property-cell.
This is the structural fact that makes the United States' attempted property-substitution structurally inadmissible and the polity's continuing iyúhá-šni from the 1980 monetary settlement substrate-coherent rather than merely political.

## The cell

$$\phi_{\mathrm{PS}} := \big\langle \mathrm{O\check{c}h\acute{e}thi\ \check{S}ak\acute{o}wi\eta},\ \texttt{kin-relation-with-place},\ \mathrm{Pa\check{h}\acute{a}\ S\acute{a}pa} \big\rangle$$

The cell is in the place-relation predicate-class $\Pi^\mathrm{plc}$ (cf. [polity-cell-system](polity-cell-system.md)).
Its value is wakȟáŋ-marked: $w_\mathrm{lak}(\phi_\mathrm{PS}) = 1$.

The cell settles at $\mathbf{t}$ — has been, persistently, throughout recorded Lakota history and (by Lakota tradition) since the people's emergence onto the Plains.
The wakȟáŋ-marking is sustained by continuing ceremonial practice in the Hills.

## Why this is not a property-cell

A property-cell is a relation in the $\Pi^\mathrm{off}$ predicate-class — specifically a Hohfeldian *power* over the place: the right to exclude, to alienate, to use, to exploit.
Property-cells are settled by external recognition (legal title, deed, court ruling); they are alienable; they can be transferred or extinguished.

A kin-cell is a relation in the $\Pi^\mathrm{plc}$ predicate-class — a relational fact about the entity's standing-in-relation.
Kin-cells are settled by *continuing-conduct* (the people's ongoing ceremonial and lived relation with the place); they are *not alienable* in the property-cell sense; they cannot be transferred to a different party because the cell is *about* the specific kin-relation, not a transferable right-over.

The substrate-algebraic point: $\phi_\mathrm{PS}$ and a putative property-cell over Pȟahá Sápa live in *different predicate-classes*.
The 1877 act and subsequent U.S. operations attempted to assert *property-cells*; the operations did not — could not — touch the kin-cell.
The kin-cell persists in the substrate regardless of what was asserted in the property-class.

## What sustains the kin-cell

The kin-cell's value is maintained by (Ω5) of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ at confederacy scope:

$$\langle \mathrm{O\check{c}h\acute{e}thi\ \check{S}ak\acute{o}wi\eta}, \texttt{ceremony-conducted-at-paha-sapa}\rangle \geq_k \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; w_\mathrm{lak}(\phi_\mathrm{PS}) = 1$$

The continuing-ceremonial-practice cell is the sustaining condition.
As long as Lakota people continue to conduct sun dance, haŋbléčheyapi (vision quests), and other ceremony in the Hills, the wakȟáŋ-marking on $\phi_\mathrm{PS}$ is sustained.
The continuing-practice has been resumed openly after 1978's American Indian Religious Freedom Act; it persisted in restricted form throughout the suppression period.

## The 1877 Act and the persistent $\top$

The 1877 act of Congress purported to alienate Pȟahá Sápa from the Lakota polity and incorporate it into U.S. territory.
In substrate-algebraic terms, the operation was an attempted *property-cell installation*: $\langle \mathrm{US}, \texttt{exclusive-property-of}, \mathrm{Pa\check{h}\acute{a}\ Sapa}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ from the U.S. side.

The operation has three substrate-flaws from the Lakota side:

1. It is *non-wakȟáŋ-marked*: no wičháša wakȟáŋ witnessed; no pipe was smoked; no confederacy-scope omníčiye consented.
2. It violates the wakȟáŋ-marked 1868 Fort Laramie treaty (cf. [treaty-as-wakhan-declaration](../morphisms/treaty-as-wakhan-declaration.md)).
3. It attempts to install a *property-cell* over a *kin-cell* — predicate-class mismatch.

The Lakota polity's substrate-state on $\phi_\mathrm{PS}$ continues to be wakȟáŋ-marked $\mathbf{t}$.
The U.S. asserted-property-cell is also at $\mathbf{t}$ from the U.S. side.
The merged-substrate (the multi-observer-system with the two polities as observers) has these cells at $\top$ in the predicate-classes where they overlap — contested in the substrate-record but not in the underlying kin-cell.

## The 1980 Supreme Court ruling and the persistent refusal

In 1980, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in *United States v.
Sioux Nation of Indians* (448 U.S. 371) that the 1877 act was an unconstitutional taking and awarded the Sioux Nation approximately $106 million in compensation (with interest, ~$1.9 billion by some accounts and accumulating).
The ruling acknowledged the substrate-violation from within U.S. legal substrate while attempting to *resolve* the contested cells through monetary compensation.

The Lakota polity's response is the polity-scale [iyúhá-šni-withdrawal](../morphisms/iyuha-sni-withdrawal.md) morphism applied to the *settlement-engagement*: the polity does not accept the compensation.
The funds sit in trust, accumulating interest, unaccepted.

The substrate-algebraic content: the U.S. attempted to substitute a *value-cell over money* for the *kin-cell over place*.
The substitution is in different predicate-classes; the substitution is structurally inadmissible.
The kin-cell is not a thing that can be substituted-for by a monetary value because monetary cells live in $\Pi^\mathrm{off}$ (the property-and-value predicate-class) while kin-cells live in $\Pi^\mathrm{plc}$ (the place-relation predicate-class).
The polity's continuing refusal is the polity *holding the kin-cell at* $\mathbf{t}$ *rather than allowing the substitution morphism to fire*.

By [paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution theorem](../theorems/paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution.md), this is *structural*, not merely a political stance: no morphism in $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ converts a kin-cell into a property-cell or a value-cell.
The settlement-substitution can only fire if the polity *adds* a non-$\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ rule that does this conversion.
The polity has not done so and (by the wakȟáŋ-marking discipline) cannot do so without breaking its own substrate-discipline.

## LandBack as substrate-restoration

The contemporary LandBack movement — at Pȟahá Sápa specifically, and at other lost-territory sites — is the substrate-operation of *re-installing kin-cells* over places the kin-cells have been disrupted.
The operations:

- **Renewed-ceremonial practice** at the place: ceremonies conducted on the Hills sustain $w_\mathrm{lak}(\phi_\mathrm{PS}) = 1$.
- **Sustained-political claim** to the Hills: keeps the inter-polity cell-conflict at $\top$ rather than letting it default-resolve toward the U.S. property-assertion.
- **Direct repossession** (return of specific parcels, federal-government transfer of small areas, purchase by Lakota organizations): installs additional cells over the property-class while leaving the kin-class undisturbed.

The substrate-algebraic structure: kin-cell at $\mathbf{t}$ throughout; property-cell at $\top$ during the contested period; property-cell trending toward $\mathbf{t}$ from the Lakota side as LandBack progresses.
The eventual full-property-restoration is the conformation of the property-cell to the long-standing kin-cell, not the *creation* of the kin-relation.
