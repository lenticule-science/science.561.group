# Predicated Lakotaean Work

Lakota work on the predication-categories mathematics.
Sibling to [predicated-lakotaean-governance](../../../../../work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/theories/predicated-lakotaean-governance/) (currently housed in the Western tree for substrate reasons; conceptually a sibling here).
Both ride on the polity-cell-system and $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$.

## What the theory commits to

The Lakota work-form differs structurally from the Western work-form in the following load-bearing ways:

1. **Work flows through kin-obligation, not dispatch.** The predicated-work theory's central morphism is *dispatch* — an authority assigning a task to an agent.
  The Lakota form has no such central morphism; work is enacted because (W3) of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ has installed an owes-virtue-to cell that the agent's kin-position obligates them to discharge.

2. **Capability is kin-embedded, not standalone.** The predicated-work's capability-fiber-bundle treats capabilities as separable bundles attached to agents.
  The Lakota form has capabilities that are *kin-position-and-gender-position-embedded* and that propagate through kin-relations and through apprenticeship under kin.

3. **Cadence is annual, not project.** The predicated-work's cadence-as-modal-frame is a periodic recurrence-pattern set by project-scope.
  The Lakota form's cadence is the *annual-cycle* (cf. [annual-cycle](../../../../../work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/theories/predicated-lakotaean-governance/objects/annual-cycle.md)), keyed to ceremonial markers and seasonal availability.

4. **Completion is obligation-discharge, not task-done.** The predicated-work's completion-as-quiescence theorem treats completion as the settling reaching its quiescent state on the task-cells.
  The Lakota form's completion is *obligation-discharge*: the owes-virtue-to cell drops to $\bot$ when the corresponding wówačhantognaka-conduct (or analogous virtue) has been fired.
  Completion is *relational*, not artifact-final.

5. **Work-classes are predicate-class-distinguished.** The predicated-work treats all tasks as elements of a generic task-cell-system.
  The Lakota form distinguishes work-classes by which predicate-class of $\Pi_\mathrm{lak}$ the work-cells live in.
  Wakȟáŋ-work fires under different rule-classes than wótakuye-work; the substrate distinguishes them.

6. **Surplus-cell does not fire.** The predicated-work's [labor-extraction-as-surplus-cell theorem](../../../../../work/theories/predicated-work/theorems/labor-extraction-as-surplus-cell.md) characterizes labor-extraction by surplus-cell-installation over the worker.
  The Lakota form's $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ contains *no* closure rule that installs a surplus-cell.
  The absence is constitutive: work flows through gift-economy redistribution, not through accumulation.

The reservation-period imposition of wage-labor and forced-labor systems is the operational form of *importing $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wage}$* — a closure-rule subsystem that does install surplus-cells — onto the polity's substrate.
The resulting accountability-disposition conflict (analogous to the [restorative-retributive-norm-conflict](../../../../../work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/theorems/restorative-retributive-norm-conflict.md)) is a continuing substrate-feature of reservation-era labor.

## The five work-classes

Lakota work decomposes structurally into five classes by predicate-class of the work-cells:

| Class | Predicate-class | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Wówašake-work | physical-skill cells | Hunt, butcher, tan, fight, ride, horse-care, tipi-pole-cutting |
| Wakȟáŋ-work | wakȟáŋ-bearing cells | Pipe-making, bundle-keeping, ceremony-conducting, healing, vision-interpreting |
| Wótakuye-work | kin-relation cells | Child-rearing, elder-care, sick-care, food-preparation, feeding-guests |
| Káğapi-work | making-cells | Quill-work, bead-work, leather-work, pottery, weapons-making, regalia |
| Wahúŋkpa-work | collective-coordination cells | Camp-circle-formation, big-hunt-coordination, sun-dance-preparation, migration |

The classes are *not strictly disjoint*: ceremony-preparation is wakȟáŋ-work *and* wahúŋkpa-work; tipi-construction is wówašake-work *and* káğapi-work; child-rearing under a wičháša wakȟáŋ's training is wótakuye-work *and* wakȟáŋ-work.
The classes are *predicate-class-distinguished* — the work's *primary* predicate-class determines its rule-firing — but cells can be multi-classed.

## Gendered division

The Lakota work-form has a gendered division that is complementary rather than hierarchical:

- *Men's primary*: wówašake (hunt, war, butchering, weapons, horse-care), some wakȟáŋ (the wičháša wakȟáŋ office is typically male).
- *Women's primary*: wótakuye (kin-care, food-preparation), káğapi (quill, bead, leather, pottery), some wakȟáŋ (the wíŋyaŋ wakȟáŋ — sacred-women — exist and have specific ceremonial roles), the tipi (women own and move the lodge).
- *Wíŋkte and tȟokáhe (two-spirit) positions*: structurally recognized work-positions that don't fit the binary.
  Wíŋkte are typically associated with name-giving for children (a specifically wakȟáŋ-marked work-role), with some wakȟáŋ-work, and with cross-form work-tasks.
- *Wahúŋkpa* (collective work): mixed; everyone participates in camp-circle-formation, sun-dance preparation, migration.

The substrate-algebraic content of "gendered work is complementary": the closure rules condition on *who-is-doing-the-work* (the agent's gender-cell), and different work-classes have different gendering-conditions.
The complementarity is that *neither gendered set is structurally dominant*: women own the lodge, the family's wealth, the children, and the food; men own their weapons and horses.
The polity's wealth-cells run through women's substrate-side; the polity's hunting-and-war-cells run through men's substrate-side.
Both are needed; neither is sufficient.

The reservation-period imposition of patriarchal-property forms (especially via the Dawes Act 1887 — allotment in 160-acre lots assigned to male "heads of household") was the operational form of *removing women's substrate-position* from the polity's work-form.
The contemporary reclamation of women's leadership in tribal-government, in land-defense (the prominence of women at Standing Rock), and in language-revitalization is substrate-restoration of the complementary-gendering form.
