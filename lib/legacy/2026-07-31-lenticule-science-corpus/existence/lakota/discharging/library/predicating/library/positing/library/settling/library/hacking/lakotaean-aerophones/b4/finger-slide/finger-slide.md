# Finger Slide

The finger slide is the gradual — rather than instantaneous — covering or uncovering of a [finger-hole](./finger-hole.md) on the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md), producing a continuous pitch glide between the two scale-degrees that the fully-covered and fully-open states of that hole respectively produce.
The slide is the cedar flute's principal *continuous-pitch* [ornament](./ornament.md), occupying the role that *glissando* fills in European-classical practice and the role that *meend* fills in Hindustani classical practice — a pitch-bend that crosses through frequencies not in the discrete scale, producing an expressive smear between scale-degrees.

The mechanical basis of the slide is partial hole-coverage.
A [finger-hole](./finger-hole.md) that is fully covered terminates the air column at the bore-foot; a hole that is fully open terminates the air column at the hole's position.
A hole that is partially covered — fingertip resting on part of the hole's opening — terminates the air column at an intermediate "effective position," producing an intermediate pitch.
The pitch depends continuously on the proportion of the hole that is covered: as the fingertip slides off the hole (or onto the hole) over a span of perhaps 100 to 300 milliseconds, the sounding pitch glides continuously between the two scale-degree values.

The slide can go either direction.
An *upward slide* (from the lower scale-degree to the upper) is produced by gradually uncovering a hole that begins covered; a *downward slide* (from the upper scale-degree to the lower) is produced by gradually covering a hole that begins uncovered.
Both are idiomatic, but the upward slide is somewhat more common in cedar-flute practice — it is the natural ornament for ascending phrase-motion, and it appears characteristically as an approach to the cadential terminus (a slide upward into the b3, followed by the descending b3 → 1 [cadence](./cadence.md)).

The slide's range is in principle bounded by the interval between adjacent scale-degrees — a slide on a single hole crosses the interval that hole's covering controls.
For most holes on the cedar flute, this is a major-second or minor-third interval. *Multi-hole slides* are possible — the player can coordinate the partial covering of two adjacent holes to produce a slide across a larger interval, though this is technically demanding and used sparingly.
The slide does not, on the cedar flute, normally cross more than a minor third in a single gesture.

The slide's musical functions are principally three.
First, *expressive coloration*: the slide adds vocal-like inflection to the flute line, replacing a clean discrete-pitch transition with a continuous pitch motion.
Second, *cadential approach*: the slide into the tonic (or into the cadential approach-tone b3) is a common cadential gesture, often used in alternation with a clean discrete approach across strophes.
Third, *instrumentalization of vocal glissando*: when the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) [instrumentalizes](./instrumentalization.md) a [wiilowan](./wiilowan.md) vocal melody that contains vocal glissando (vocal pitch-bends), the flute slide is the principal corresponding ornament — the vocal-to-flute mapping renders vocal slides as flute slides where possible.

The slide is distinct from the [finger-pop](./finger-pop.md) in being a continuous-pitch event rather than a discrete brief lift-and-return.
It is distinct from the [trill](./trill.md) in not involving alternation between two scale-degrees but rather a single passage from one to the other.
It is distinct from the [warble](./warble.md) in being a *pitch* event (the sounding frequency changes continuously) rather than a *harmonic-mode* event (the bore stays in the same mode and the pitch alternates between bore-modes).
Together with the pop, cut, trill, tap, and warble, the slide forms the cedar flute's principal [ornament](./ornament.md) vocabulary.
