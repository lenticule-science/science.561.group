# Fort Laramie 1868 as wakȟáŋ-bearing treaty

The Treaty of Fort Laramie of 1868 is the central worked example of the [treaty-as-wakhan-declaration](../morphisms/treaty-as-wakhan-declaration.md) morphism in the polity's substrate.
This example walks through the substrate-firings that constituted the treaty, the wakȟáŋ-marking it produced, and the persistent substrate-state it has maintained since.

## Pre-treaty substrate-state

In 1865–1868, the substrate-state of the U.S.–Lakota inter-polity overlap was:

- $\langle \mathrm{O\check{c}h\acute{e}thi\ \check{S}ak\acute{o}wi\eta}, \texttt{enemy}, \mathrm{US}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked.
  The state of zuya-engagement on the Bozeman Trail (Red Cloud's War, 1866–68) made the enemy-cell wakȟáŋ-marked at confederacy scope.
- Multiple zuya-instances had fired: Fetterman fight (December 1866), Hayfield fight (August 1867), Wagon Box fight (August 1867), plus continuing raids.
- The Bozeman Trail's three forts (Phil Kearny, C.F. Smith, Reno) were the U.S. military positions the Lakota war-parties were targeting.
- Multiple coup-cells, kill-cells, and harm-cells were fired on both sides over the engagement.
- The substrate's BC square on the overlap was *deep in failure* — the two polities' paths produced wildly non-isomorphic cell-patterns on shared cells.

## The omníčiye-decision to negotiate

Following the substantial Lakota tactical successes of 1866–67, the U.S. government — recognizing that the Bozeman Trail was militarily-untenable — initiated peace-negotiations.
The Lakota response was a confederacy-scope omníčiye-deliberation:

- The omníčiye convened with senior naca from multiple oyate, including Red Cloud (Maȟpíya Lúta) representing the Oglala, plus naca from Húŋkpapȟa (Sitting Bull *did not* participate — his omníčiye-position differed), Sicangu, and others.
- Red Cloud's specific position: no negotiation until the U.S. closed the Bozeman Trail and abandoned the three forts.
  The substrate-content was iyúhá-šni from any premature engagement: Red Cloud refused to participate in pre-treaty meetings until conditions were met.
- The U.S. eventually agreed; the forts were abandoned in summer 1868; Red Cloud entered the negotiations.

The substrate-content of this exchange: the omníčiye-deliberation produced a *non-resolution* on the question of peace until iyúhá-šni-availability had been respected.
The eventual treaty-negotiations could only proceed because the polity's withdraw-mechanism worked.

## Post-treaty substrate-state

Following the signing:

- The Bozeman Trail forts were burned by the Lakota (a triumphal substrate-act after their abandonment — the substrate-content of the iyúhá-šni-position having succeeded).
- The Lakota substrate-state was *at-peace* with the U.S., wakȟáŋ-marked.
- The treaty-pipe was kept (its current location is uncertain — the pipe-bearer-chain in this specific instance is one of the unresolved substrate-questions for contemporary research).

## The 1877 unilateral act

The 1877 Act of Congress purported to seize Pȟahá Sápa and reduce the Great Sioux Reservation.
Substrate-analysis:

- **No Lakota signatory** to the 1877 act.
  The Manypenny Commission of 1876 had attempted to get Lakota signatures, but the substrate-fact is that the required three-fourths-of-adult-male-Lakota-signatures (required by Article XII of the 1868 treaty for any future land-cession) was *not obtained*.
  The Commission's signatures collected were a small fraction, often under duress (food was being withheld at agencies).
  The 1877 act passed Congress without the substrate-required Lakota assent.
- **No wakȟáŋ-witness, no pipe.** The 1877 act was a U.S. statutory operation.
  There was no Lakota ceremonial-witness, no pipe-ceremony, no confederacy-scope omníčiye consenting.
- **Predicate-class mismatch.** Per [paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution](../theorems/paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution.md), the 1877 act attempted property-cell installation over the wakȟáŋ-marked kin-cell — substrate-algebraically invalid.

The substrate-consequence (per [treaty-binding-iff-wakhan-marked](../theorems/treaty-binding-iff-wakhan-marked.md)): the 1868 treaty's wakȟáŋ-marked cells *did not retract*.
The 1877 act installed *new* cells from the U.S. side that asserted-against the 1868 cells; the merged substrate-state on the relevant cells went to $\top$.
The contested-substrate has persisted since.

## Substantive substrate-state as of writing (May 2026)

The 1868 treaty's substrate-status today:

- The wakȟáŋ-marked treaty-cells remain at $\mathbf{t}$ under $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ — the polity has not withdrawn its assertion.
- The contested-cells are at $\top$ in the merged-multi-observer substrate.
- The polity's continuing treaty-defense work (the Black Hills claim, the Sioux Nation Treaty Council, ongoing litigation and advocacy) sustains the cells.
- The 1980 *United States v.
  Sioux Nation* ruling acknowledged from within $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-fed}$'s substrate that the 1877 act was an unconstitutional taking — but proposed monetary-compensation as resolution, which the polity has refused (see [paha-sapa-kin-cell](../objects/paha-sapa-kin-cell.md) for the substrate-content of the refusal).

The substrate-state is therefore: a wakȟáŋ-marked treaty *that is still in force* on the Lakota substrate, contested by an unauthorized statutory operation, with the contested-state persistent.
This is what "the 1868 treaty is still in force" means in substrate-algebraic terms — it is not rhetoric; it is a substrate-state.
