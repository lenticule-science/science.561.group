# Wótakuye-work

Wótakuye-work is the work-class whose cells fire on kin-care predicates — raising children, caring for elders, caring for the sick, preparing and serving food, language-teaching, grief-companionship.
The class is the *largest* in continuous-volume terms across the polity's life: every adult agent has many simultaneous owes-cells in wótakuye-work-class to multiple kin, and the discharge-firing is continuous.

The class is structurally distinguished from other work-classes by:

1. Cells fire on kin-pairs by default — every kin-pair installs owes-wótakuye-w-cells via (W3) of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$.
2. Cells are *not* wakȟáŋ-marked by default (most wótakuye-work is not ceremonial), but can become wakȟáŋ-marked when performed under specific ceremonial conditions (a meal prepared for a sun-dancer; a vigil during a haŋbléčheyapi).
3. Capability-cells are installed through *intergenerational teaching* under kin — not formal apprenticeship — typically mother-daughter, aunt-niece, grandmother-granddaughter, father-son, uncle-nephew lines.

## Cells in the class

$\Pi^\mathrm{wótakuye\text{-}w}$ contains:

- *Raises-child*, *teaches-child-language*, *sings-to-baby*.
- *Cares-for-elder*, *visits-elder*, *prepares-food-for-elder*.
- *Cares-for-sick*, *attends-birth*, *attends-dying*.
- *Prepares-food-for*, *cooks-for*, *serves-food-to*.
- *Gathers-water-for*, *gathers-firewood-for*, *prepares-tipi-for* — household-substrate work.
- *Grieves-with*, *sits-with-bereaved*, *prepares-body-for-burial*.
- *Mends-clothes*, *makes-moccasins-for* — overlap with káğapi-work.

## Continuous-volume property

A typical adult Lakota agent has wótakuye-w owes-cells for:

- *Each* of their nuclear-family kin (parents, siblings, children).
- *Each* of their extended kin within practical-range (cousins, aunts, uncles, grandparents, grandchildren).
- Often additional huŋkápi-installed kin.

For an agent in a tiyóšpaye of 30 members, that is often 20+ potential owes-cells in wótakuye-w-class at any time.
The discharge-firing is therefore not occasional but *continuous*: the agent is firing wótakuye-w-cells most of every day.

This is the substrate-content of "everyone is always feeding someone, caring for someone, teaching someone."
The continuous-volume is not a feature of busy modern life; it is structurally constitutive of pre-reservation Lakota work-form.

## Gender-conditioning

Wótakuye-work has strong gender-typical primary firing — women's primary work-class, in the complementary-gendering sense (cf. [predicated-lakotaean-work _index](../_index.md)).
The substrate-conditions on rule-firing for many wótakuye-w predicates are gender-conditioned: *prepares-food-for* fires more cells on women-agents than on men-agents; *cares-for-sick* similarly; *teaches-child-language* in early-childhood has strong women-primary firing.

Men's wótakuye-w primary firing concentrates in: - *Protects* / *defends-camp* (overlap with wówašake). - *Provides-game-meat-for* — the hunt-side of food-provision. - *Teaches-warrior-skills-to-young-boys* (later-childhood). - *Cares-for-elderly-parents* — particularly elderly fathers (mothers are typically cared for by daughters and granddaughters).

The complementarity: women's substrate-work-time is more continuous-distributed; men's is more episodic-concentrated.
Both produce $\leq_k$-monotone wówačhantognaka-conduct cell accumulation; the patterns differ but the standing-cell outputs converge.

The wíŋkte-position has specific wótakuye-w primary firing in: - *Names-child* (a wakȟáŋ-overlap role). - *Cares-for-elderly* with special standing. - *Adopts-orphans* — the wíŋkte-as-parent-figure for kids without primary kin available.

## Mother-daughter capability transmission

The capability for many wótakuye-w predicates installs through mother-daughter transmission specifically.
Recorded patterns:

- *Cooks-for* / *prepares-pemmican* / *dries-meat*: mother teaches daughter from age 4–5 onward.
- *Tans-and-finishes-hide*: structurally women's work; mother-daughter chain; the capability-cell fires from teaching-cell-accumulation.
- *Bears-and-rears-child*: mother is the model; older female kin (aunts, grandmothers) participate in the chain.

The substrate-form: capability-cells fire through a directed-graph of *teaches-X-to* relations across generations.
The graph has structural-properties: high connectivity within tiyóšpaye, lower connectivity across tiyóšpaye, restored connectivity at sun-dance gatherings where cross-tiyóšpaye teaching opportunities occur.

The boarding-school period broke many of these chains — children removed from their teaching-graphs lost the cell-firing of mother-daughter transmission.
The contemporary reclamation work (Lakota-language-revitalization, traditional-foods education, mother-grandmother teaching circles) is substrate-restoration of the capability-installation chain.

## Wótakuye-w and wicozani

Per [wicozani-as-quiescence](../../../../../../../philosophy/theories/predicated-philosophy/theories/predicated-lakotaean-philosophy/objects/wicozani-as-quiescence.md), the polity's wicozani-cells are *structurally* dependent on the wótakuye-w discharge pattern.
Continuous wótakuye-w firing across the populace produces relational-settling-quiescence; interruption produces non-quiescence.

The contemporary Sicangu Lakota *Wicozani* initiative on the Rosebud Reservation explicitly addresses wótakuye-w substrate-restoration as a primary intervention for polity-scale wicozani.
