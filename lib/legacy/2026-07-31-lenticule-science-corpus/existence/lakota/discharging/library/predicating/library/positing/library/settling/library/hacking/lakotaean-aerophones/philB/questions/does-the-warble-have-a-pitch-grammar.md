# Question. Does the warble have a pitch grammar — does it occur on specific scale degrees more often than others?

The warble is an ornament in the Lakotaean flute vocabulary, deployed at phrase entries, on sustained low notes, and at phrase peaks.
But within the pitch space Z₅ × R⁺, is the warble distributed uniformly across scale degrees, or does it cluster on specific degrees?

Hypothesis: the warble clusters on the tonic and on the perfect fifth (the structural pillars of the pentatonic) and on the minor seventh (frequent peak note).
Alternative: the warble clusters on whichever degree happens to fall in the breath-pressure-warble-onset regime, which depends on bore geometry and may vary per instrument.

If the warble has a pitch grammar, it is a function w : Z₅ → [0,1] giving warble-incidence-density per scale degree.
That function is itself a music-theoretic structure characterizing the idiom.
