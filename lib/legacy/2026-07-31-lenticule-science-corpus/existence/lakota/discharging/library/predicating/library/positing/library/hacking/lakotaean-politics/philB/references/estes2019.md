# Estes 2019 — Our History Is the Future

**Estes, Nick.
(2019). *Our History Is the Future: Standing Rock Versus the Dakota Access Pipeline, and the Long Tradition of Indigenous Resistance*.
London: Verso.**

## Annotation

Nick Estes is Lower Brule Sioux Tribe, member of the Oceti Sakowin and of the Oceti Sakowin Writers Society, and a historian. *Our History Is the Future* traces the political life of the Oceti Sakowin from the days of the Missouri River trading forts through the Indian Wars, the Pick-Sloan dams (which flooded large parts of Oceti Sakowin land in the mid-twentieth century), the American Indian Movement, the campaign for Indigenous rights at the United Nations, and into the 2016 #NoDAPL movement at Standing Rock.

The book's structural argument is in its title: the long Indigenous resistance is one tradition, not a series of episodes.
The Water Protectors at Standing Rock are in continuous political relation with their relatives who fought at Greasy Grass, who refused the Black Hills cash, who stood at Wounded Knee.
The traditions of refusal, of confederate action, of polity-form continuity across centuries of settler-state pressure are themselves the political form being demonstrated.

## Why this is a political reference

For the politics of contemporary Oceti Sakowin life, Estes is the primary current source.
The book grounds the analysis in Estes's own Oceti Sakowin standing while treating the political-theoretical material seriously.
The continuity claim — *our history is the future* — is itself a thesis with implications for political sociology: the Lakota polity-form is not historical, the resistance is not episodic, the polity is in operation now.

## Related works

The Red Nation (the organization Estes co-founded) and its published programs and pamphlets — including *The Red Deal* (2021) — are companion contemporary sources.
Estes's academic articles in *American Quarterly* and elsewhere extend the analysis.
