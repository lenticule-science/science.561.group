# Question. Is the instrumentalization morphism Voice → Flute injective on the documented courting-song corpus?

The instrumentalization morphism ι : Voice → Flute (see [instrumentalization](code/transformers/instrumentalization.md)) sends a vocal courting song to its flute realization, preserving pitch content (modulo pitch-set quantization), preserving phrase grammar, dropping text, and adding flute timbre and ornament.

Open: within the documented Lakotaean courting-song corpus, do distinct vocal songs always map to distinct flute melodies, or do some vocal songs collapse to the same flute realization (because the flute's pitch-set is coarser than the voice's)?

If ι is injective on the documented corpus, the flute realization preserves song identity within the tradition.
If ι is not injective, the flute carries less information than the voice and the courting-song-via-flute is a strict reduction.

This question affects whether the flute's role in courtship — "playing your song to the woman you want" — depends on the song being recognizable to her, which in turn depends on ι being injective on the songs she knows.

Sub-survey needed: Densmore transcriptions of vocal songs vs. her transcriptions of flute melodies; identification of vocal-song / flute-melody pairs; injectivity check.
