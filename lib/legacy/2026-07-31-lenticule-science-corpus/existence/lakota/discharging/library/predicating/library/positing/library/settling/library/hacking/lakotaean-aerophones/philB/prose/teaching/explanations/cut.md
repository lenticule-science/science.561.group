The cut is a very brief touch — typically a fingertip just grazing the surface of an uncovered [finger-hole](./finger-hole.md) — that momentarily lowers the sounding pitch before the original held pitch resumes.
It is the mirror-image gesture to the [finger-pop](./finger-pop.md): where the pop briefly *uncovers* a held-covered hole (producing an upward pitch deviation), the cut briefly *covers* a held-uncovered hole (producing a downward pitch deviation).
The cut functions as an *upper grace note* in melodic terms — a brief excursion to a pitch above the current held pitch, accomplished by a finger-action *below* the current held pitch's controlling hole.

Wait — the previous paragraph contains a subtlety worth working through explicitly.
The held pitch is determined by the lowest open hole on the bore (counting from the foot); covering a hole below the current lowest open hole has no acoustic effect (the air column is already terminated above that hole), but *uncovering a hole above the current lowest open hole* would change the pitch.
The cut, properly understood, is the brief covering of the *currently-lowest open hole* — which briefly *re-extends* the air column to the next-lower open position, producing a momentary *lower* pitch (the next-lower scale-degree), after which the finger lifts and the original held pitch resumes.
So the cut produces a downward pitch deviation, not an upward one, and it is properly an *under-grace* in the melodic sense — a brief excursion to the pitch one scale-degree below the held pitch.

Mechanically, then: the player is sustaining, say, scale-degree 5 on the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) (holes 1, 2, 3 uncovered, holes 4, 5, 6 covered).
To cut, the player briefly touches hole 3 (the lowest of the open holes), re-covering it just long enough to drop the pitch to scale-degree 4 — perhaps 30 to 80 milliseconds — then lifts the finger again, restoring scale-degree 5.
The resulting articulation is a brief downward chirp on top of the held tone.

The cut's pitch interval depends on which hole is briefly covered, which depends on the current held pitch.
For a tone sustained at scale-degree 5, a cut to hole 3 produces a brief drop to scale-degree 4 (a major second below).
For a tone sustained at scale-degree b3, a cut to hole 1 (the foot-most) produces a brief drop to the fundamental scale-degree 1 (a minor third below).
Most cuts in idiomatic cedar-flute practice are to small intervals — a major second or minor third — and serve as quick decorative gestures rather than as melodic motions in their own right.

The cut's musical functions parallel those of the [finger-pop](./finger-pop.md) but in the opposite pitch-direction. *Articulation*: the cut marks a structural moment within a sustained tone without retaking breath or moving to a new melodic note. *Rhythmic accent*: as a sharp pitch event, the cut draws perceptual attention and can be used to accent a beat. *Idiomatic flavor*: the cut is one of the characteristic sounds of cedar-flute playing, and its presence is part of what makes the flute line sound like cedar-flute music.
Where the [finger-pop](./finger-pop.md) gives an upward chirp, the cut gives a downward chirp, and skilled players use both in alternation across strophes of a [courting-song](./courting-song.md), via [ornament-substitution](../../code/transformers/ornament-substitution.md).

The cut also has a specific role in [instrumentalization](./instrumentalization.md) of [wiilowan](./wiilowan.md) vocal song.
Vocal cuts — brief vocal-pitch dips on a held tone, often produced by a quick laryngeal or breath gesture — are a small-but-present feature of Lakotaean vocal style, and their flute analogue is the cedar-flute cut.
The vocal-to-flute mapping renders vocal cuts as flute cuts where the source admits.

The cut is distinct from the [tap](./tap.md) in being a brief covering of a hole rather than a brief opening — though the two ornaments are sometimes named the other way around in different player traditions, and the terminology is not universally standardized.
The cut is distinct from the [trill](./trill.md) in being a single brief event rather than a sustained alternation.
It is distinct from the [finger-slide](./finger-slide.md) in being a discrete on-off event rather than a continuous-coverage event.
