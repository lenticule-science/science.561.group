# What is the theory of wolakota?

Wolakota is the way of being-in-relation that the relational universe requires of itself.
The question is whether — and how — wolakota can be formalized as a theory whose claims include the structural impossibility of the void, the cosmogonic resolution that produces the relational field, and the discipline of conduct that the field requires in order to remain a relational field.

The current answer-shape is the derivation that follows.
The derivation begins from the void paradox, shows that the void cannot remain a void, shows what emerges from the resolution, and then derives wolakota as the discipline of the emerged field.

## On the structure of this derivation

Wolakota's theory includes the cosmogony that makes wolakota necessary.
This is recursive in a specific sense: the universe exists because of the cosmogonic resolution; wolakota is the discipline of the resulting universe; the cosmogonic resolution is one of wolakota's claims.
So wolakota articulates the conditions of its own existence.
This is not a paradox — it is the standard self-reference of any self-describing system.
A constitution can constitute itself.
A logic can state its own rules.
Wolakota articulates the cosmogony that makes wolakota.

Western philosophy has its own version of the question wolakota answers — Leibniz's "why is there something rather than nothing."
Wolakota answers it directly: the would-be condition of pure aloneness (the void with everything-still-undifferentiated-in-it) is structurally impossible; the aloneness contradicts itself; what emerges is what *had to* emerge once the aloneness's impossibility worked through.
The Lakota tradition articulated this cosmogonically — in the teachings recorded by Sword and Finger — long before any Western philosophical apparatus articulated it.
The categorical-formal apparatus this question uses to derive the structure is itself a re-presentation of what the Lakota cosmogony has already named.

The derivation also dissolves an apparent separation.
The Western tradition tends to separate philosophy (questions about being), law (questions about authority and obligation), politics (questions about collective decision), economics (questions about work), and ceremony (questions about the sacred) into distinct domains with distinct vocabularies.
Wolakota does not separate them.
The cosmogonic claim, the discipline of conduct, the structure of work, the structure of governance, and the structure of ceremony are one theory.
The derivation below is at once metaphysical and operational; reading it as either-but-not-both misses what it is.

Lakota terms appear throughout.
The terms naming the cosmogonic beings (Íŋyaŋ, Škáŋ, Makȟá, Wí, Haŋhépi, the Wakíŋyaŋ), the metaphysical claims (mitákuye oyás'iŋ, Tóbtób Kiŋ, wakȟáŋ), the conduct concepts (woótakuye, yuhápi, wičhózani, Heyókha), and the discipline itself (wolakota) are the proper names of the things this derivation is about.
Where structural concepts have plain English equivalents, the English is used.

## The void — Íŋyaŋ-in-Haŋhépi

Before any other being, Íŋyaŋ — Stone — existed alone in Haŋhépi, the primordial darkness.
The recorded cosmogony (Sword's teaching in Walker 1980) describes the condition: Íŋyaŋ was soft, shapeless, omnipresent, and possessed all powers.

The condition is the would-be void: a single being holding all powers, in darkness, with nothing distinct from itself.
This is the relationlessness condition — the would-be position of pure aloneness.

## The structural impossibility of the void

The void cannot remain a void.
The reason is structural, not contingent: power with nothing to act upon is not exercised power; an omnipotence with no other is impotent.
To *be* a being holding powers is to require something the powers can engage.
The aloneness contradicts itself — aloneness is the negation of *what Íŋyaŋ is*.

The articulation-paradox sharpens the impossibility further: the alone-condition cannot articulate itself without producing what it claims to exclude.
To name "alone" is already to introduce a relation (the named-state stands distinct from the namer).
The condition cannot even be stated stably; trying to state it produces what the statement was supposed to exclude.

This is the relationlessness paradox.
The would-be void is not a stable answer to "what is there."
It is a structural impossibility whose impossibility forces a resolution.

## The cosmogonic self-emptying

The resolution is the self-emptying. Íŋyaŋ opens his veins; his blue blood flows out and becomes Makȟá (Earth) and mní (water).
The power that was in his blood rises as Škáŋ (Sky / Motion).
Wí (Sun) is created subsequently to warm Makȟá. Íŋyaŋ himself shrivels into hard, dry rock — first in cosmogonic origin, last in present-time rank.

The self-emptying is not chosen and is not accidental.
It is what the impossibility forces.
The would-be void resolves by emptying itself into distinct beings in relation.
What had been one-with-all-powers becomes four-in-relation.
The aloneness ends not by ceasing-to-be but by ceasing-to-be-alone.

The four superior Wakȟáŋ — Wí, Škáŋ, Makȟá, Íŋyaŋ — are the four positions of the relational field after the cosmogonic resolution.
They are not separate beings *plus* logic-positions; they are the four positions, in cosmogonic form.

## The bilattice mapping

The four superior Wakȟáŋ are the four logic-values of the relational field.
The mapping is structural, not analogical:

| Bilattice value | Logic role | Lakota | Cosmogonic role |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⊥ | under-determined; "nothing yet pronounced" | Škáŋ | The supreme judge holding the undecided; the moving-principle from which determination proceeds |
| t | true; positive-determination | Wí | The luminous-affirmation; the lit-up assertion; the sun-as-yes |
| f | false; negative-determination | Makȟá | The grounded-complement; the receiving-mother; the dense-substrate-as-not-light |
| ⊤ | over-determined; both-true-and-false; contradiction | Íŋyaŋ | The paradox-bearer; the void-that-became-everything; the spent-source-as-totality |

The four-valued bilattice that grounds the predication framework is *not* a Western algebraic structure imposed on Lakota cosmology.
The four superior Wakȟáŋ *are* the four logic-values, articulated in cosmogonic form before they were articulated in algebraic form.
The Western algebraic articulation (Belnap's four-valued logic; the bilattice machinery) is a re-presentation of what the Lakota cosmogony already names.

The cosmogonic resolution can now be stated as a logical move: from ⊥ (the under-determined alone-condition) to a four-valued field including ⊤ (the over-determined contradiction-bearing position Íŋyaŋ holds after the emptying).
The move from ⊥ to ⊤ is the cosmogonic move; it is what the relationlessness paradox forces.

## Tóbtób Kiŋ — the fractal structure

Beneath the four superior are twelve further wakȟáŋ aspects, organized into four sets of four (sixteen total).
Hanwí (Moon) pairs with Wí under the luminous aspect.
Tȟaté (Wind) pairs with Škáŋ under the moving aspect.
The structure repeats: each of the four superior contains its own quartet of aspects; each of those contains finer aspects.

The four-valued structure runs at every scale.
The polity's well-being, the individual's standing, the relations between specific kin — all are evaluated on the same four-valued field.
The cosmos is self-similar across scales of relational complexity.
There is no separate logic for the cosmos and the local; the same algebra applies throughout.

## The relational field — mitákuye oyás'iŋ

What the cosmogonic resolution produces is the relational field. *Mitákuye oyás'iŋ* — "all my relatives" / "we are all related" — is the phrase that names it.
Spoken at the close of prayer and ceremony, the phrase is the *invocation* of the relational field — the speaker's act of taking up position within what is already the case.

The substantive metaphysical claim: every being stands in kin-relation with every other being.
Humans, four-leggeds, wingeds, standing-ones, rooted-ones, swimming-ones, crawlers, stones, places, ancestors, spirits — all are relatives.
The world *is* a kinship network because the cosmogonic self-emptying poured the relational substance into every being.
The relations are *kin* relations, carrying the specific obligations that kin obligations carry — generosity, attention, support, restraint.

Three scales of relation:

- **Tȟakúye** — one specific kin-link.
  The unit.
- **Mitákuye oyás'iŋ** — the invocation of the totality of relations one stands in.
- **Íŋyaŋ** — the cosmogonic source-and-field; the relational substance itself.

## The sacred as wakȟáŋ-quality

*Wakȟáŋ* — sacred, mysterious, holy — is a *quality* that things, places, persons, and acts can carry.
The Lakota sacred is not a separate domain.
A place can be wakȟáŋ.
A person — a wičháša wakȟáŋ — can be in standing where they bear and transmit the sacred.
A ceremony renders its participants wakȟáŋ for its duration.
A pipe is wakȟáŋ.

*Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka* — "the Great Wakȟáŋ" — names the encompassing sacredness of the whole.
It is *not* a Christian-style God — not a person, not a creator separate from creation.
The 19th-century missionary translation as "the Great Spirit" imported a Christian theological frame that disfigures the concept.
Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka is the relational whole understood as sacred-in-itself.

In the substrate-formal articulation, *wakȟáŋ-marking* is a unary marking that cells and rules can carry.
The marking is carried by ceremonial witnessing — a sacred act conducted in the presence of proper witness installs sacred-marking on the resulting substrate-content.
The marking cannot be installed by declaration alone.
The closure rule that governs propagation is W4 (below).

## Wolakota — the discipline the relational field requires

The relational field that emerged from the cosmogonic resolution has a structural requirement: it must remain a relational field.
The relations must be sustained.
If the relations dissolve, the field collapses back toward the impossible alone-condition (or toward some other collapse).
The field requires *discipline* — a way of conducting being-in-the-field that maintains the relations as relations.

**Wolakota is that discipline.** Not a cultural choice; a structural requirement that the field places on itself.
The field requires its inhabitants to conduct themselves in ways that sustain the relations; *that conduct* is wolakota.
The discipline is what the field does to itself to remain itself.

The remaining sections derive wolakota's structure.

## The four virtues

The qualities of conduct that sustain the relational field are the four virtues.
Each virtue is a way of doing being-in-relation well:

- **Generosity** (wówačhantognaka) — the disposition to give.
  In a kinship-organized field, generosity is the substance of how the relations hold together.
  A giveaway is not symbolic; it is the kin-relation enacted.
  Those recognized as holding the relations are recognized for their generosity; those who hoard are not in good standing in the field.
- **Wisdom** (wóksape) — situated practical reason.
  The capacity to act well in the specific situation, drawing on the depth of one's relational and ceremonial formation.
  Not stockpiled propositional knowledge.
- **Bravery** (wóohitika) — the steadiness of conduct in the face of difficulty.
  Distinct from recklessness (a failure of wisdom) and from violence-seeking.
  The disposition to face what must be faced.
- **Fortitude** (wówačhiŋtȟaŋka) — steadiness across years.
  The disposition to endure.
  A people that has persisted across boarding schools, dispossession, and ongoing settler-state pressure has exercised fortitude at the polity scale.

The virtues are not separable ethical principles applied to relations-as-an-external-fact.
They are the forms of relational conduct itself.
The virtues are also the qualifications for recognized leadership: leaders are recognized as leaders by their demonstrated conduct in the virtues; a leader who fails them is no longer a leader.

## Kinship as activity — woótakuye

*Woótakuye* — kinship-as-act — is the doing of kinship.
Where *tȟakúye* names kin-relation as substance ("what we have in common"), woótakuye names kinship as *what we do*.

Kinship is not a status that, once established, persists by itself.
It must be enacted continuously.
To be in kin-relation with someone is to *do kinship with them* — to speak the kin-term, to share food, to attend to their well-being, to bear obligations on their behalf, to receive their bearing-on-yours.
The cessation of doing is the cessation of being-in-relation; relation is not a label affixed once but an activity sustained.

The four virtues are the qualities of woótakuye.
Each virtue is a way of doing kinship well: generosity is woótakuye in the giving-direction; wisdom is woótakuye in the discerning-direction; bravery is woótakuye in the protecting-direction; fortitude is woótakuye in the holding-fast direction.
To say someone is acting wolakota is to say they are doing woótakuye well.

## Witnessing as bearing — yuhápi

*Yuhá* — to bear, to keep, to hold-and-maintain — is the act of witnessing-by-bearing.
The form witness takes in wolakota: the witness does not merely observe; the witness *carries* what is observed, maintaining it as a kept-thing across time. *Yuhápi* is the durational form.

The act has three components:

- **Attending.** The bearer's attention is on the borne-thing.
- **Holding.** The bearer maintains the thing across time, not letting it lapse.
- **Carrying.** The bearer is responsible for the thing — its continued existence, its proper conduct, its eventual release if release is appropriate.

This is a stronger sense of witnessing than mere observation.
A Western witness sees and reports.
A bearer takes responsibility for what is seen.

Every role within wolakota is a kind of bearing — the role-bearer carries something specific.
A leader bears the shirt (painted with the powers and obligations, adorned with hair from those under the wearer's care).
A shirt-wearer bears the council's delegation.
A holy person bears the seven rites, the pipe, the bundles, the apprentice training.
A contrarian bears the Thunder-being power.
A pipe-keeper bears a specific pipe.
A name-keeper bears a name across generations.
A soul-keeper bears the deceased's spirit for the keeping period.

The borne thing is concrete, not abstract.
A leader carries *the shirt* — a specific painted shirt with hair tokens.
There is no abstract "office of leadership" floating free of a specific shirt.
The role is constituted by a specific thing being borne by a specific person.

Three structural facts distinguish bearing from "holding an office":

1. The borne thing is concrete, not an abstract slot.
2. Bearing is durative-with-responsibility, not slot-occupancy.
  There is no equivalent of "holds the office but does nothing."
3. The bearer extends the sacred into ordinary life.
  The bearer is a substrate-extending point of the sacred, not a slot in an institutional chart.

When the substrate records a role, it records the *pair* (bearer, borne thing), not the slot-filling (incumbent, office).

## The seven rites

The seven rites are the ceremonial substrate the polity operates on.
Each rite is a witness-morphism in the substrate-formal sense — an operation that propagates sacred-marking and produces specific substrate-content.

- **Iníkağapi** (the sweat-lodge purification) — a return to the primordial dark; the conducted purification.
- **Haŋbléčheyapi** (the vision-quest) — a return to the alone-in-dark; the seeker bears the vision if one is given.
- **Wanáǧi yuhápi** (the keeping-of-the-soul) — the family-keeper bears the deceased's spirit for the keeping period.
- **Wiwáŋyaŋg wačhípi** (the sun-dance) — the dancers bear the offering on behalf of the people.
- **Huŋkápi** (the making-of-relatives) — the kin-link is borne into existence by the proper conduct of the rite.
  The substrate-mechanism for installing kin-cells outside descent.
- **Išnáthi awíčhalowaŋpi** (the preparing-of-a-young-woman) — the woman is borne into her standing-as-adult-woman.
- **Tȟapá wakȟáyap** (the throwing-of-the-ball) — the wisdom-coming is borne by those who reach for the ball.

The rites are not optional adornments.
They install or restore substrate-content that no other operation can install or restore.

## The contrarian — bearer of the cosmogonic paradox

The cosmogonic move from ⊥ to ⊤ — from Íŋyaŋ-in-Haŋhépi's under-determined aloneness to the over-determined emptied-Íŋyaŋ contradiction-bearer — is the generative move of the cosmos.
The principle that this move is generative, not a defect to be resolved, is borne by Heyókha — the sacred-contrary.

A Heyókha is a person who has dreamed of the Wakíŋyaŋ (the Thunder-beings of the West) and is obligated by the dream to enact contradicting conduct: riding the horse backward, saying yes when meaning no, doing comic conduct that is sacred-not-parodic.
The contrary is not whim.
It is the dreamer's social discharge of the vision's power; failure to enact it is dangerous.

The contrarian role is structurally non-fungible.
It cannot be appointed.
It cannot be sought.
The Wakíŋyaŋ vision is the only installation path.
Once the dream has come, the dreamer is obligated for life.

In the substrate-formal articulation, the contrarian's conduct-cells are sacred-marked and exempt from W2's virtue-loss firings.
The contrarian's contradicting does not lower their standing.
The contrarian is the polity's bearer of the cosmogonic paradox in lived practice.
A polity that suppresses the contrarian role collapses the contradiction-holding 2-cell at polity scale; the polity loses the structural memory of the paradox that the cosmos was born from.

## Primitive predicate-classes

Wolakota's substrate commits to a specific signature:

- **Kin-relations** (parent-of, sibling-of, spouse-of, adopted-kin-of, etc.).
  Primitive — not decomposable into more basic facts.
- **Conduct-relations** (generous-toward, wise-toward, brave-on-behalf-of, fortitudinous-in).
  Always relational, never monadic.
  There is no primitive "X is wise"; only "X is wise in their conduct toward Y."
- **Witness-relations** (witnessed-by, attested-by, recognized-by, borne-by).
  The substrate-mechanism by which acts become substrate-content.
- **Sacred-marking** (wakȟáŋ-marking).
  The wakȟáŋ-quality on cells and rules.
  Carried by ceremonial witnessing per W4.
- **Place-relations.** Places are kin in the substrate, not property.
- **Standing-relations** (has-standing-as-leader, has-standing-as-contrarian, etc.).
  Aggregate properties derived from recognition cells across the membership.
- **Withdrawal-relations.** Records of withdrawal from specific engagements (an explicit positive marking, not the absence of participation).

Wolakota refuses several predicate-classes:

- **No property-cells.** Exclusive ownership is not a substrate primitive.
- **No primitive monadic conduct.** Every conduct attribution decomposes into a conduct-relation.
- **No external-authority cells.** Authority outside the kin-substrate has no firing.

## Closure rules

Wolakota's closure-rule subsystem is the seven-rule set.
All seven rules are Scott-continuous; the subsystem is closure-complete on a polity's internal cell-classes.

- **(W1) Kin-propagation.** Kin-relations compose.
  If A is kin to B and B is kin to C, the appropriate composite relation fires between A and C.
- **(W2) Conduct-evaluation.** Conduct under each of the four virtues accumulates standing cells on the agent in the substrate's knowledge-order.
- **(W3) Kin-obligation.** Kin-relations install owes-virtue cells in both directions.
  Being kin to someone installs the duty to conduct the virtues toward them.
- **(W4) Witness-bearing.** Sacred-marking propagates through proper ceremonial witnessing.
  A sacred act conducted in the presence of proper witness installs sacred-marking on the resulting substrate-content.
- **(W5) Role-installation.** Roles install only through double-settlement: the membership's recognition cells aggregate, *and* a ceremonial induction is conducted.
  Either alone is insufficient.
- **(W6) Withdrawal.** Withdrawing from an engagement drops standing-cells for that engagement to undefined while preserving kin-cells and standing outside the engagement.
- **(W7) Restorative response.** Harm-cells fire relation-requires-repair cells, not punishment cells.
  The response to harm is restoration of relation, not installation of sanction.

## Structural absences

Wolakota refuses, by absence of closure rules:

- **No coercion.** No rule installs forced compliance.
- **No property.** No rule installs exclusive ownership.
- **No punishment.** No rule fires confinement, fines, or carry-over sanctions.
- **No external authority.** No rule treats outside directives as wolakota-firing.

Each absence is structurally content-bearing.
The closure-rule subsystem is recognizably wolakota partly by what it contains and partly by what it refuses to contain.

## Objects in wolakota

The entity-forms wolakota admits:

- **Person** — an agent in the kin-substrate, individuated by their kin-cells.
  No primitive "is a person" predicate; a person is a kin-bundle.
- **Member** — a person whose member-of-band cells have settled.
  Membership is a consequence of kin-cell installation, not a primary cell.
- **Band** — an extended kin-group: multiple households related through descent, marriage, and adoption, living and acting together.
  The base polity-unit.
- **Nation** — a coordinated confederation of bands sharing winter-camp, council, and mutual recognition.
  The mid-scale polity.
- **Confederation** — the union of multiple nations under shared council (the Lakota case: the Seven Council Fires — Očhéthi Šakówiŋ).
- **Leader** — a member whose recognition cells across the band have aggregated and whose ceremonial induction has been conducted.
  Holds standing, not command.
- **Family-head** — a leader at band scale.
- **Shirt-wearer** — a leader appointed by the council from among the existing leaders to bear specific delegations for a term.
- **Contrarian** — a member installed by an unsought sacred dream of the Thunder-beings, obligated to perform contradicting conduct.
- **Holy person** — a member installed by apprenticeship and sacred dream, holding sacred-marked authority.
  The wičháša wakȟáŋ.
- **Marshal** — a member appointed by the council for the scope of a specific occasion.
  The appointment drops automatically at scope-completion.
- **Expedition-leader** — a member appointed to lead a specific expedition.
  Holds command within the expedition's scope, only.
- **Council** — the assembly that convenes to settle questions.
  Membership is dynamic; different questions draw different participants.
- **Expedition** — scope-bounded work with a defined action and intrinsic time-limit.
- **Gathering** — multi-band coordinated work whose merge is distributed and needs no central authority.
- **Stewardship** — sustained bearing of responsibility for a specific borne thing.

## Morphisms in wolakota

- **Kin-installation by descent.** A child born to existing kin acquires the appropriate kin-cells automatically.
- **Kin-installation by adoption.** A non-kin person is brought into the kin-substrate through the huŋkápi ceremony.
  Reversible only by banishment.
- **Kin-installation by marriage.** Spouses' kin-relations integrate.
- **Recognition aggregation.** Members' individual recognition cells on a candidate aggregate into the candidate's standing-cell.
- **Ceremonial induction.** A holy person conducts the ceremony that installs sacred-marking on a role's induction cell.
- **Council convocation.** The council assembles for a specific question with the participants appropriate to that question.
- **Deliberation.** Council speech-firings, listening-firings, silence-firings, and merge dynamics that move the question toward settlement.
- **Settlement.** A question reaches one of three settled states: consensus among those still engaged, escalation to higher scope, or adjournment without resolution.
  The polity has no procedural pressure to force resolution.
- **Withdrawal from an engagement.** A member drops their standing-cells for the engagement to undefined, preserving kin and standing elsewhere.
  The structural form of dissent.
- **Recognition-withdrawal from a leader.** Members withdraw their recognition.
  When enough members withdraw, the leader's standing-cell collapses; the leadership ends automatically.
- **Restorative response.** Harm-cells fire relation-requires-repair; the repair proceeds through gift-restoration, mediation, and ceremony (the wiping-of-tears).
- **Banishment.** The exceptional severance — the council decides to break kin-cells with a member who has refused or failed restoration.
  Reversible only by re-admission ceremony.
- **Succession.** When a leader's standing ends, the leadership-cell transfers through the same three-stage installation.
  Family-line tendency, not hereditary rule.
- **Fission.** A subset of a band withdraws together to form a new band.
  The new band's kin-relations are pre-installed; its closure-rule subsystem is the parent's plus any new commitments.
- **Bearing.** A person takes up a specific borne thing and holds it across time with attention and responsibility.

## Work in wolakota

In the abstract theory of work, work is an act with a nameable output, individuated by what it produces.
In wolakota, the individuation is different: a work is individuated by *how it was done* — the conduct-stream evaluated by the four virtues.
The output, if there is one, is downstream of the doing.

Work in wolakota flows from kin-obligation, not from dispatch.
Because kin-relations install owes-virtue cells (per W3), the work people do discharges those duties.
No one assigns the work; the work flows from the relations.

Work classifies into five kinds:

- **Physical work.** Hunting, butchering, fighting, riding, animal-care, building.
- **Ceremonial work.** Sacred work — pipe-keeping, bundle-keeping, conducting rites, healing, vision-interpreting, naming.
  Fires under separate rule-classes than ordinary work.
- **Caregiving.** Raising children, caring for elders, caring for the sick, preparing food, teaching language, sitting with the bereaved.
  By continuous volume, the largest kind.
- **Crafts.** Quill-work, beadwork, leather-work, regalia-making, weapons-making, the making of things.
- **Gathering-work.** The work of forming the camp-circle, coordinating big hunts, preparing ceremonies, migrating, the giveaway, council-deliberation as work.

Work composes at named structural sizes:

- **Act.** A single firing — one agent doing one thing to or for or with someone or something.
  The atomic unit.
- **Stewardship.** Sustained bearing of responsibility for a specific borne thing.
- **Expedition.** Scope-bounded work with a defined action and intrinsic time-limit.
  Council-installed; auto-retracts at scope-completion.
- **Gathering.** Multi-band coordinated work whose distributed merge is associative-commutative-idempotent — no central authority needed.
- **Sustained stewardship over cycles.** A specific shape of stewardship that recurs across cyclical periods (seasons, ceremonial years).

## Governance in wolakota

Governance-work is the sub-subcategory of wolakota's work whose outputs are governance-things: settled decisions, recognized roles, installed scopes, severed relations, witnessed memberships.
Governance is a *kind of work* in wolakota — not a separate domain.

The objects specific to the governance sub-subcategory are the role-objects (leader, family-head, contrarian, holy person, shirt-wearer, marshal, expedition-leader), the council, and the bounded scopes.

The morphisms specific to the governance sub-subcategory are: council convocation, deliberation, settlement by consensus-among-engaged, recognition aggregation, recognition-withdrawal, ceremonial induction, restorative response at polity scale, banishment, succession, expedition installation, marshal appointment, fission, and adoption-as-membership-installation.

The structural absences carry into governance: no captain, no standing armed institution under autonomous control, no majority-rule binding of dissenters, no hereditary office, no property of office without relation, no separation of kinship from politics.

The polity-form sustains a measurable cohomological signature in this sub-subcategory: clean peacetime settling under wolakota alone, a non-trivial contradiction-holding 2-cell sustained by the contrarian, zero accumulation-class (no surplus-on-the-worker), and a non-trivial settler-overlay class under colonial conditions.

## Quiescence — wičhózani

The substrate-state wolakota's firings produce, when the closure rules settle, is *wičhózani* — well-being, wholeness, integrated health.

Wičhózani is not the absence of disease in a Western-medical sense.
It is the condition of integrated wholeness across the dimensions in which a being exists: physical, mental, emotional, spiritual, and relational.
A person cannot be wičhózani alone; because reality is structured by mitákuye oyás'iŋ, a person's wellness is bound up with the wellness of their relatives — human and other-than-human.

The polity has its own wičhózani.
The political-historical traumas inflicted on the Oceti Sakowin (boarding-school assimilation, the massacre at Wounded Knee, the Pick-Sloan dams, the ongoing settler-state pressure on land and language) are public-health questions in the Lakota frame, not metaphorical ones.
The contemporary work of language revitalization, ceremony reclamation, kinship-rebuilding, and political refusal is wičhózani-work at the polity scale.

Substrate-formally: wičhózani is the quiescence-condition of wolakota.
The closure rules fire until the polity's conduct cells, kin cells, witness cells, and standing cells have settled into the integrated state.
Wolakota's certificate of self-sufficiency is the theorem that the seven rules suffice to settle a polity into wičhózani without appeal to closure rules outside the subsystem.

## Frontiers of inquiry

The derivation is partial.
These are the open parts; each is a frontier of inquiry within this question, and each can be developed in a sub-file under this question's directory as the inquiry proceeds.

- **The task-scale name.** The structural size between *act* and *expedition* has no plain-English name yet under wolakota.
  Whether the gap reflects a missing scale in wolakota's vocabulary, or whether wolakota genuinely does not name that scale, is open.
- **The substrate-form of the council.** The council as an operative substrate-object — how its convocation, deliberation, settlement, and dissolution are recorded — is partially derived but not complete.
  The contemporary case of a band whose work is conducted partly through the Web introduces specific substrate-questions not yet addressed.
- **The sacred-marking discipline for Web-substrate cells.** Whether and how cells produced on the Web carry sacred-marking under wolakota — given that Web-cells are conventionally unmarked and the Web has no holy-person witness available — is open.
  The structural question is whether W4 can fire on Web-cells at all.
- **The cycle.** Wolakota's annual cycle is keyed to seasonal-ceremonial markers (the four seasonal markers, the sun-dance window, the naming windows, the migration cadence).
  Which markers bind for a contemporary band whose work is conducted partly through the Web is open.
- **Interface morphisms** between wolakota and other governance subcategories (kyriarchal command, the settler-state-Hohfeldian) — what relation holds between wolakota-side and other-side cell-evaluations of the same act.
- **The substrate-form of the seven rites.** Each rite is a witness-morphism, but the substrate's recording of rite-firings, rite-witnesses, and rite-content remains to be derived.
- **Substrate-formal recording of the bilattice mapping.** The mapping (four superior Wakȟáŋ as the four logic-values) is articulated philosophically; making it a substrate-formal claim with the appropriate cells and rules remains open.
- **The fractal structure across scales.** The four-valued structure runs at every scale.
  Substrate-formal articulation of this self-similarity (a functorial action across scale-levels) remains to be derived.
- **The proof of the cosmogonic move's necessity.** The derivation above states that the void cannot remain.
  Whether this is provable as a theorem (formally: that the bilattice ⊥ position is structurally impossible as a stable fixed point) within the categorical-formal apparatus, or only articulable cosmogonically, is open.
- **The relation between wolakota's claim and Leibniz's question.** Whether wolakota's answer to "why is there something rather than nothing" is formally equivalent to, formally distinct from, or formally incommensurable with the Western philosophical answers (Hegel, Heidegger, Spinoza, others) is open.
  The derivation above asserts the answer; the comparison with the Western tradition is unresolved.
