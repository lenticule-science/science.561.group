# Eternal inflation

A refinement of [cosmic inflation](../../evolution/terms/cosmic-inflation.md) in which inflation, once started, does not stop globally.
Quantum fluctuations of the inflaton field continually re-energize new regions of space into the inflationary phase faster than other regions exit it.
The result is a fractal, ever-growing inflating background out of which causally disconnected "bubble" universes — each a [world](world.md) in the multiverse sense — continually nucleate.

Proposed by Vilenkin (1983) and Linde (1986), eternal inflation is one of the few generative mechanisms for a [multiverse](multiverse.md) that is a relatively direct consequence of single-universe physics rather than an additional postulate.
If inflation occurred in our past (well-supported by CMB observations) and the inflaton potential satisfies certain mild conditions (debated), then eternal inflation follows.

## Empirical status

The bubble universes generated by eternal inflation are causally disconnected from ours after they nucleate.
Direct observation is impossible.
The exception is bubble *collisions* — if our bubble collided with a neighbor early in its history, the collision would leave a signature on the [cosmic microwave background](../../structure/terms/cosmic-microwave-background.md) (a "bruise" or anomalous circular feature).
Searches have been conducted; no significant signal has been confirmed.

The conditions on the inflaton potential that produce eternal inflation are not directly observed; they are inferred from broader theoretical considerations.
The strength of the eternal-inflation prediction depends on assumptions about the high-energy behavior of the inflaton field that lie outside currently testable physics.

## In this library

Eternal inflation is the most empirically grounded mechanism for producing the kind of population of worlds that the [predicated-multiverse](../../predicated-multiverse/_index.md) subtheory abstracts.
The categorical formulation does not depend on eternal inflation being correct — it would describe a population of worlds however generated — but eternal inflation is the standard reference picture for "where would the worlds come from."
