# Isotropy (in the Wood sense)

In this subtheory, **isotropy** is the strong homogeneity condition the [Wood Between the Worlds](../objects/wood-between-worlds.md) is required to satisfy: its automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Wd})$ acts transitively on the complement of its pools.
Formally, for all points $x, y$ in the underlying object of the Wood with $x, y \notin \bigcup_i P_i$, there exists $g \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Wd})$ with $g(x) = y$.

This is **not** the standard cosmological notion of [isotropy](../../terms/cosmological-principle.md) (rotational invariance about a point as a feature of the observed CMB and large-scale matter distribution).
The two notions are formally cousins — both express "no preferred direction / no preferred location" — but the Wood's isotropy is a categorical statement about $\mathrm{Aut}$-action on a mediating object, while the cosmological principle is a metric-geometric statement about a single universe's spatial structure.

## What it forces

Together with [pool degeneration](hole.md) and the [universal mediating property](../theorems/wood-as-initial-mediator.md), isotropy is one of the three axioms that pin down the Wood up to isotropy-equivalence.
Drop isotropy and the universal mediator is no longer unique: many mediators with different internal anisotropies could equally well factor every other mediator through themselves.
Isotropy is what makes the Wood *the* canonical mediator rather than *a* canonical mediator.
