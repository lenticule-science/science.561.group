Baryonic matter is ordinary matter made of protons, neutrons, and electrons -- the stuff of atoms, molecules, rocks, water, air, stars, planets, and living things.
"Baryonic" refers to baryons (protons and neutrons), which carry nearly all the mass.
Electrons are technically leptons, not baryons, but they are included in the category because they are bound to baryonic atoms and contribute negligibly to the total mass.

Baryonic matter is everything visible in the universe.
Every photon ever collected by a telescope came from baryonic matter or its interactions.
Despite this, baryonic matter constitutes only about 5% of the total energy content of the universe.
The remaining 95% is [dark matter](./dark-matter.md) (~27%) and [dark energy](./dark-energy.md) (~68%).

The total amount of baryonic matter in the universe is precisely constrained by two independent observations: the abundance of light elements produced during [nucleosynthesis](../../evolution/terms/nucleosynthesis.md) in the first minutes after the [Big Bang](../../evolution/terms/big-bang.md), and the pattern of fluctuations in the [cosmic microwave background](./cosmic-microwave-background.md).
Both measurements agree: the baryon density parameter $\Omega_b \approx 0.049$.
