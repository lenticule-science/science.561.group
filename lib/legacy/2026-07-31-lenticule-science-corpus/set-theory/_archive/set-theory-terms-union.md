The union of two [sets](./set.md) A and B, written A ∪ B, is the set of [elements](./element.md) belonging to A, to B, or to both: A ∪ B = {x : x ∈ A or x ∈ B}. More generally, the union of a family of sets {Aᵢ}ᵢ∈I is ⋃ᵢ Aᵢ = {x : x ∈ Aᵢ for some i ∈ I}.

Union is commutative (A ∪ B = B ∪ A), associative ((A ∪ B) ∪ C = A ∪ (B ∪ C)), and idempotent (A ∪ A = A). The empty set is the identity: A ∪ ∅ = A. Together with [intersection](./intersection.md), union gives the power set of any set the structure of a [Boolean algebra](../../../concepts/boolean-algebra/index.md).

In a [lattice](../../posets/terms/lattice.md), union corresponds to [join](../../posets/terms/join.md) (least upper bound). In the [open sets](../../../disciplines/topology/terms/open-set.md) of a [topological space](../../../disciplines/topology/terms/topological-space.md), arbitrary unions are allowed — this is one of the topology axioms — and the join of open sets is their union. In the [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) H of the [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe, join (a ∨ b) is the semantic analogue of union: the weakest value that entails both a and b.
