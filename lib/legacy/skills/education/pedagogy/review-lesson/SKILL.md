# Review Lesson

Reviewing a lesson is a structured pass against the criteria that distinguish teaching from depositing. The output is a report, not a rewrite.

## When to use

A lesson exists and needs auditing — you want to know what it does well, what it lacks, and where to point at specific lines. Use this when you intend to feed the report back to whoever (including yourself) will revise the lesson.

## How it works

Read the target lesson, then check it against each criterion. For each, note **Pass / Partial / Fail** with a line number and quoted text, plus a specific suggestion if Partial or Fail. End with a short summary: what the lesson does well, what needs the most work. Do not rewrite — report only.

### Structure

- **Motivation before definition.** Does the lesson explain why the topic matters before introducing formal content? A lesson opening with a definition or formula fails this check.
- **Prerequisites stated.** Are prerequisites listed and specific about what the reader needs (not just "familiarity with X")?
- **Prerequisites achievable.** Do linked prerequisite pages exist? Do they cover what the lesson assumes?
- **Scope.** Does the lesson focus on one core idea, or does it try to cover several? More than 3–4 distinct concepts in a single lesson is a warning.

### Concept introduction

- **One at a time.** Are concepts introduced sequentially, each grounded before the next, or are multiple new terms introduced in one paragraph?
- **Concrete before abstract.** For each concept, is there an example or intuitive explanation before the formal definition?
- **Examples present.** Does each major concept have at least one concrete example?

### Engagement

- **Worked example.** A full walkthrough applying the concepts — not just "for example, ..." but a multi-step demonstration.
- **Exercises or checkpoints.** Can the reader test their understanding? Self-check questions, exercises, or prompts to try something all count.
- **Active voice.** Does the lesson address the reader and invite participation, or does it read like a textbook passage?

### Connections

- **Cross-references.** Are links used for terms, people, and related content? Count key terms missing a link.
- **Citations.** Are scholarly claims attributed to a source? Flag unsourced claims that should be cited.
- **Sequence links.** Does the lesson say where it fits — what comes before and what comes next?

### Style

Run the same checks the prose-style review would: vague adverbs, mixed contractions, telling the reader how to feel, passive voice where active reads better.

## Output format

For each criterion: **Pass / Partial / Fail**, line number(s) and quoted text, a specific suggestion if Partial or Fail. Then a final summary: strengths and the highest-leverage improvements.

## Prerequisites

- `learn-lesson-design` — you cannot review against criteria you don't yet hold.
