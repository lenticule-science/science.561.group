# Can AI alignment be anti-capitalist?

**Yes, an AI system can be designed to advance cooperative, commons-oriented or other anti-capitalist goals. But a technically aligned agent is not thereby anti-capitalist, democratically governed, or safe.** An agent can perfectly execute an employer's instructions while worsening working conditions or excluding people affected by its decisions. Conversely, a collective that uses AI may still reproduce hierarchy, surveillance or ecological extraction. Those are institutional and empirical questions, not labels that can be inferred from a model's objective function.

This article makes a bounded argument from the 561 Group's own research into concurrent agents, formal evidence and federated institutions. It distinguishes what a program *can verify*, which persons and institutions *have authority*, and whose lives improve *in practice*. It describes proposals and tests; it is not a declaration that the Group has already achieved democratic AI governance.

## What is AI aligned *to*, and who decided?

Technical alignment usually asks whether a system reliably behaves in accordance with some objective, instruction or constraint. That question matters. An agent that invents evidence, leaks private information or ignores a lawful refusal remains defective regardless of its owner's politics.

But at least four independently answerable questions sit behind that objective:

1. **Behavior.** Does the implementation follow the specified constraints under normal and adversarial conditions? What would falsify that claim?
2. **Authority.** Who can authorize, revise, contest or withdraw the objective? Can the affected parties withhold data and challenge a decision without relying on the same operator they are contesting?
3. **Political economy.** Who owns the model, data, infrastructure and downstream output? Who gets paid, displaced, surveilled or locked in when the agent succeeds?
4. **Material reproduction.** What energy, water, hardware, maintenance labor and centralized providers does the arrangement consume? Would it remain viable if those inputs became scarce or too expensive?

None implies the others. Publishing code does not transfer ownership of production infrastructure. Democratic deliberation does not prove a tool's reliability. A cryptographic payment protocol does not establish a commons. A technically correct model can be part of an unjust organization; a desirable organizational purpose does not make unsafe software acceptable.

In March 2026, [One Project's public-governance proposal](https://oneproject.org/how-to-make-ai-serve-the-public/) framed the structural question in terms of **rules, incentives, wealth and ownership**. The [Center for Distributed Governance of AI](https://cdgov.org/) describes proposals for decentralized policy enforcement and democratic oversight. These are useful comparisons, not evidence that either institution has certified the 561 Group or that any proposed governance design has achieved its stated social outcomes.

## A worked example: an agent asked to redistribute environmental observations

Imagine an agent operating a local watershed archive. A municipal contractor instructs it to export observations to an AI service. A community steward says reuse needs a separate agreement. The archive contains an earlier document suggesting permission, but its provenance and applicability are disputed.

A conventional single-bit permission flag can silently turn that disagreement into either approval or rejection. The Group's [Belnap four-valued logic research](/belnap-four-valued-logic/) instead provides a way to *represent* a claim as supported, refuted, both, or neither:

| Evidence reading | What the evidence says | Safe institutional reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| True only | Support for a particular permission claim is present | Still verify the claimed authority, scope, expiry and purpose |
| False only | Refutation of that permission claim is present | Do not treat the action as authorized |
| Both | Support and refutation are recorded together | Preserve the conflict and require its competent resolution |
| Neither | Neither form of evidence is present | Missing evidence is not permission |

This is **an illustrative decision protocol, not an executable access-control proof**. The four-valued logic preserves incompatible testimony but does *not* identify the rightful steward, infer Indigenous consent, or appoint anyone to settle the conflict. A deployment still needs a rights-aware authorization rule with legitimate decision makers, revocation, logs and an actual refusal path.

The 561 Group's [concurrent-agent integration audit](/paraconsistent-transfinite-linear-agent-integration/) makes a related technical distinction: combining monotone positive claims can be coordination-friendly, while **landing a decision, establishing absence or declaring a result settled** may require a specific owner and ordering. The paper does *not* claim to possess a finished consequence relation or fully proven formal-inconsistency operator. A four-valued storage layer alone therefore cannot establish that an authorized institution is making valid decisions.

For communities handling their own data, the [CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples) supply an important external source about collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility and ethics. Invoking CARE is not consent, accreditation or a substitute for consultation with a specific rights holder.

## Can crypto and autonomous agents make this less exploitative?

Possibly, under additional conditions. A pay-per-call interface can make a computation accessible without signing a long-term platform contract. The 561 Group's [x402 market assessment](/x402-economy-machine-payable-markets/) studies real payments and the gap between aggregate on-chain activity and warranted claims about autonomous agents. The [x402 protocol explanation](/what-is-x402/) addresses mechanics. Those observations do not show that the resulting economic relationships are post-capitalist.

An April 2026 argument by [Joshua Dávila for the Platform Cooperativism Consortium](https://platform.coop/blog/why-the-cooperative-movement-should-care-about-crypto-still/) identifies cooperative opportunities in shared treasuries and programmable coordination, but also recounts token governance captured by speculative and venture incentives. It distinguishes technical *affordances* from cooperative results. A self-hosted API or blockchain receipt says little about who controls the corporate seller, who bears losses, or who can exit.

For the same reason, the 561 Group should not advertise itself as a cooperative or charitable commons merely because some of its research supports collective autonomy. It is a commercial institution conducting public research and selling services; potential conflicts between revenue, governance and the public good must be disclosed and independently examined. Its [machine market](https://market.561.group/) is evidence of an offered service surface, not proof of non-extractive ownership or settled public benefit.

## What would an actual non-extractive alignment test require?

One bounded field trial could place an agent between two independent, consenting organizations that need to exchange a limited amount of environmental information. Before implementation, its record would need to identify:

- The persons or institutions legally and practically entitled to authorize the specified information flow, including a withdrawal and redress procedure.
- The exact task, data-minimization boundaries, permissions, refusal cases and unauthorized attempts in a reproducible test suite.
- The custody of source data, infrastructure, computation, receipts and downstream value, along with actual costs and who pays them.
- An adversarial scenario where sources disagree, authority is revoked, infrastructure disconnects, or the requested operation would harm a third party.
- Independently observed outcomes for the users and affected parties, including any complaints, failed refusals, burdens, costs and alternatives.

A successful formal proof about a program's transition rules would satisfy **only part** of the second requirement. A true HTTP 402 settlement receipt would satisfy an economic transaction claim, not the whole third requirement. A governance charter could satisfy part of the first requirement, but not show that people can exercise its rights during conflict.

The Group's [Rendanheyi/federated institutions synthesis](/rendanheyi-living-union-organizational-science/) proposes a practical two-territory disconnection and reconnection experiment. That experiment remains **not demonstrated** in the public research. Claiming that a real federation survives disconnection before the test would substitute a design for an operation.

## What if the right answer is not to deploy AI?

It sometimes will be. A local organization may need enforceable data rights, staff time, electrical resilience or a workable appeals process more than another agent. For an environmental decision, [the Group's climate nonstationarity and water-governance analysis](/climate-breakdown-ergodicity-and-water-governance/) asks whose lived trajectory is obscured by aggregate averages and which rights remain outside a hydrological graph. Those questions can expose a reason *not* to automate.

A serious alternative-AI research program must be able to test itself against a **no-model**, human-operated or low-energy baseline, and to report where the technological option loses. It should also consider opposing arguments: concentrating safety oversight might achieve more consistent regulation than fragmented local agents; some decentralized architectures make responsibility harder to enforce; and a nominally cooperative technology can conceal dependence on highly centralized cloud, semiconductor or payment providers. These disagreements cannot be resolved by declaring an ideology.

## What is the 561 Group's actual claim?

The distinctive *research hypothesis* is that rights-aware agents, explicit refusals, paraconsistent evidence and federated operating structures can make complicated organizations more accountable and harder to exploit. The Group has public mathematics, audits, software and commercial services; those have different maturity and evidence. It has **not** established that the resulting system is anti-capitalist in operation, that a community has adopted its governance, or that its programs consistently produce better ecological or labor outcomes.

A useful next scientific contribution would be a reproducible comparison of one authorized community task under three conditions: no automation, centralized automation, and locally accountable agent assistance. Report not only execution correctness but the distribution of time, energy, money, control, errors and exit costs. Reject the favored design when independent evidence warrants rejection.

The opening question thus has a conditional answer: **AI alignment can be part of an anti-capitalist project only if the technical objective is accompanied by genuine institutional authority, material accountability and independently demonstrated outcomes.** An algorithm cannot supply those conditions by definition.
