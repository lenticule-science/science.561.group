# Climate breakdown changes the baseline

**What kind of organization remains useful when the past is an unreliable description of the future—and when the outcome experienced by one participant differs from an average over many hypothetical participants?** That question connects climate science, probability, institutional design and economic practice. It does not collapse them into one theory.

The [IPCC Sixth Assessment synthesis](https://report.ipcc.ch/ar6syr/headline.html) finds human-caused warming unequivocal, with extensive observed impacts on the physical world. Its [assessment of the water cycle](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-8/) describes interacting changes to precipitation, evaporation, runoff, groundwater and regional extremes. **Climate breakdown** is our institutional phrase for the disruption of the environmental assumptions on which infrastructure, livelihoods and organizations have been designed. The phrase expresses a concern about the consequences; each quantitative claim still needs its own observed region, period, instrument and uncertainty.

## Stationarity: what can history still tell us?

In statistical modeling, stationarity is an assumption about how a process's probability law behaves under shifts in time. A weaker practical assumption is that the particular distributional quantities used for a decision—flood peaks, water availability, dry-spell duration—remain sufficiently stable for past records to characterize future risk. These are not identical statements. Stationarity also does not mean the observed series has no variability.

Milly and colleagues' [*Stationarity Is Dead: Whither Water Management?*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1151915) (2008) challenged the use of a fixed historical envelope as the default foundation of water infrastructure planning. Climate forcing, changing land cover, engineered drainage and extraction can all alter the processes generating observations. A nominal “100-year” event computed from a historic frequency model must not be treated as a timeless promise.

The response is not to declare every dataset unusable or every stationary model false. It is to identify the *specific* stability assumption, its spatial and temporal support, evidence for change, competing models and decision sensitivity. Sometimes a locally stationary approximation remains useful within explicit boundaries. Sometimes the risk decision must examine multiple nonstationary futures.

## Ergodicity: whose average counts?

Ergodicity is a different question. For a specified observable and dynamical system, it concerns whether a suitable long-run average along a realized trajectory equals an average across a probability ensemble under the relevant assumptions. Stationarity neither automatically establishes ergodicity nor is interchangeable with it.

Consider a deliberately simple repeated wager: a participating budget is multiplied by 1.5 in one period and 0.6 in another, in that order. The arithmetic mean multiplier is 1.05, above one, but the budget ends after two periods at `1.5 × 0.6 = 0.9` of its starting value. The per-period compounded multiplier for this *particular trajectory* is `sqrt(0.9) ≈ 0.949`. This is an illustration of arithmetic-versus-compound growth, **not** by itself a proof that a specified stochastic process is nonergodic.

Ole Peters' [*The Ergodicity Problem in Economics*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-019-0732-0) (2019) develops the importance of distinguishing expected and time-averaged outcomes. That program is a theoretical perspective, not a universal empirical finding about every investment, institution or human preference. For 561 Group work it suggests practical questions: Can a participant survive repeated exposures? Who actually bears loss and recovery time? Is expected aggregate improvement masking irreversible failure for some receivers? What depends on path, delay, reserves or rights?

A climate process can be nonstationary while a given decision model still makes an explicitly conditional probabilistic prediction. An economic process can be stationary and fail an ergodicity condition for a chosen observable. We test these propositions separately.

## The water cycle as a topology of consequences

A catchment is not merely a colored polygon on a map. Water, sediment, heat, pollutants, energy and obligations move through connected stores and flows: atmosphere; vegetation; soil; groundwater; wetlands; streams; storm drains; reservoirs; estuaries; and infrastructure. Some connections are directed, delayed, seasonal, reversible or only partly observed. Watershed boundaries and surface flow do not wholly describe groundwater, atmospheric moisture transport or human diversion.

A proposed *water-cycle topology-derived governance* design would start from these physical dependencies. It would identify upstream actions and downstream exposure, affected communities, feedbacks across boundaries, seasonal changes and evidentiary blind spots. It could inform representation, consultation, burden sharing, monitoring, investment and dispute resolution.

**Topology is not a title deed or a source of jurisdiction.** A hydrological graph does not appoint a sovereign, override Indigenous and treaty rights, determine consent, or displace competent governments. Its role is to expose dependencies that municipal, corporate and administrative maps can conceal. The [OECD Principles on Water Governance](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/349/body-text.en.html) already emphasize basin-appropriate scales, cooperation across levels, clear authority, consistent data and stakeholder engagement. The 561 Group's research question is how to express those responsibilities with precise, revisable relational evidence and lawful decision boundaries.

## Energy-based accounting is not yet an energy currency

Energy and available work impose real physical constraints on every computational, agricultural and industrial endeavor. Recording joules or kilowatt-hours consumed, capacity available at particular times, location, conversion losses and storage limits may make a service's material dependencies more legible than a financial price alone.

But an **energy-based unit of account**, an **energy-backed claim**, an **energy-denominated exchange instrument** and a **legally recognized currency** are distinct institutions. One kilowatt-hour available now at a given location is not necessarily economically interchangeable with one elsewhere or later. Energy quality (exergy), power delivery, access rights, reliability, storage, emissions and distributional claims all matter. Monetary convertibility and settlement require actual counterparties and law, not a thermodynamic analogy.

The Group can investigate energy-denominated obligations and accounts without pretending to have issued a currency. Existing 561 Group machine-market offers priced in USDC are **not** energy-backed by that fact. They are a separate commercial mechanism whose real execution and settlement need their own receipts.

## What would make the synthesis scientific and useful?

An admissible research case should identify: a decision and the people affected; a geographically and temporally bounded environmental observation; its measurement provenance; the tested stationarity assumption; the chosen ergodicity question and observable; an explicit water-flow relation model; a lawful institutional decision maker; and a receiving test for the actual intervention.

A tractable first study could compare a fixed-baseline stormwater screening rule with a rule that recomputes exposure under several precipitation and land-use scenarios. Evaluation would measure not merely prediction score but false reassurance, avoided or displaced harm, cost of revision and downstream receiving. A second study could compare energy-denominated operating ledgers with cash-only estimates, keeping physical quantities and monetary claims separate.

This is where the four houses meet without merging their authority: [Lenticule Science](https://science.561.group/) examines the propositions, [Red Cup Engineering](https://engineering.561.group/) fabricates interoperable measurement and decision systems, [Coesite Operations](https://operations.561.group/) tests them in real work, and [Causeway Services](https://services.561.group/) puts usable results before outside receivers.

The distinguishing commitment is not that climate, ergodicity, hydrology or energy has supplied a universal formula for governance. It is that **our organizational claims should remain answerable to material flows, lived trajectories, changing evidence, competent authority and independently received effects.**
