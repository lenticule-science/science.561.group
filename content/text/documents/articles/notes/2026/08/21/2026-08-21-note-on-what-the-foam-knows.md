# What the Foam Knows

Foam physics is unusually generous: it has EXACT theorems in
nonequilibrium dynamics, which almost nothing else does. That makes it
the perfect donor for weird, falsifiable, hyperspecific claims in the
Hubble-work style — real laws, ported with their falsifiers attached.

**Claim 1 — the von Neumann settling law.** In 2D dry foam, a cell's
area changes at a rate depending ONLY on its neighbor count:
dA/dt = k(n − 6). Not size, not shape, not pressure — sides. It is an
exact theorem. The port: belief-communities and DIALECT REGIONS under
settling dynamics obey a von Neumann law — a community's growth or
shrinkage depends only on how many distinct neighbors it borders (its
count of contested boundaries), not on its size. Six-sided communities
are stationary; few-sided ones are eaten. Falsifiers: (a) the
pymetrologic foam-settling simulation (running at time of writing) —
does bilattice-charge boundary settling reproduce (n−6)?; (b) dialect
atlas time-series — do dialect-region areas coarsen by neighbor count?
If size predicts survival better than neighbor count, the port dies.

**Claim 2 — no stable four-fold vertices.** Plateau's laws: in 2D foam,
four films never meet stably at a point; a four-fold vertex instantly
resolves into two three-fold vertices joined by a short edge. The port:
four-party disputes are structurally unstable and decay into paired
three-party configurations bridged by a thin shared boundary.
Falsifier: coalition and negotiation datasets — four-way standoffs
should show anomalously short lifetimes versus three-way ones, with
decay products that are pairs of triads. If four-fold social junctions
prove stable, the port dies.

**Claim 3 — the Laplace pressure of disagreement.** Bubble pressure
scales inversely with radius: P = 4γ/r; the smallest bubbles press
hardest. The port: the smaller an irreducible disagreement, the higher
its discourse pressure — activity density around tiny contested claims
exceeds that around vast ones (the narcissism of small differences as a
physical law rather than an aphorism). Falsifier: any contested-claims
corpus with size and engagement measures; if pressure does not
anticorrelate with claim-extent, the port dies.

**Claim 4 — Aboav-Weaire anticorrelation.** In real foams, many-sided
cells preferentially neighbor few-sided ones: m(n) ≈ A + B/n. The port:
high-contact communities preferentially border low-contact ones (hubs
ringed by hermits). Falsifier: any community-adjacency graph.

**Claim 5 — T1 avalanches.** Foam relaxes through discrete
neighbor-swap events (T1 transitions) with characteristic avalanche
statistics. The port: opinion cascades propagate through discrete
boundary-swap events, and cascade-size distributions should match foam
rheology's avalanche laws. Falsifier: cascade datasets versus the
simulation's T1 statistics.

**Claim 6 — the hubble-bubble bridge.** The universe's large-scale
structure IS a foam — voids walled by filaments — and structure
formation is a coarsening process; void statistics are an established
cosmological probe. So the Hubble flagship and the soap film share a
mathematics (coarsening dynamics, void topology), and the estate's
pending g(z) construction and this note's von Neumann port are cousins:
both ask whether a settling law explains the observed partition of a
space into holes. A bubble is a hole wearing a minimal surface — the
film exists because of the pressure of the emptiness it encloses.
Laozi 11, in soap: the void does the work; the film just witnesses it.

All Research-grade; the simulation and the named datasets are the
referees. FOAM already stands in the bestiary — this note is what it
was waiting to mean.
