## 4. Semiotic Foam and Lexical Equilibrium

The corpus of formal records is modeled as a "foam" where cells are claims and walls are tokens (distinctions).

### 4.1 Plateau’s Laws and Isostaticity
A dry foam at equilibrium fixes local coordination (walls meet at 120°). In a semiotic context, this means a vocabulary at equilibrium is **isostatic**: constraints exactly balance freedoms.
*   **Floppy Vocabularies:** Contain terms that can be moved or removed without impact.
*   **Over-constrained Vocabularies:** Feature definitions competing for the same region, leading to "brittleness" where the corpus cannot absorb new data without systemic failure.

### 4.2 von Neumann’s Law (The Degree Threshold)
The survival of a term is dictated by its number of neighbors (degree).
*   **Threshold of Six:** In a 2D foam, a claim with fewer than six neighbors loses territory and is eventually absorbed.
*   **Implication:** A term is not protected by being "well-defined." Its survival is topological. Coarsening is a consolidation of territory, not necessarily a loss of coverage.
