Nestor Makhno (1888–1934) was a Ukrainian anarchist communist from Huliaipole in the Zaporizhzhia region. Born the son of a peasant, he worked in factories and was imprisoned from 1910 to 1917 in Moscow's Butyrka prison, where the anarchist Pyotr Arshinov taught him revolutionary theory. The February 1917 amnesty freed him and marked the beginning of his most significant political work.

In 1917, Makhno led the Huliaipole peasants in seizing landlords' estates and reorganizing them as communes. From 1918 to 1921, he commanded the Revolutionary Insurgent Army of Ukraine—known colloquially as the "Black Army"—a peasant partisan force that fought the Austro-German occupation, the Ukrainian Hetmanate, White armies under Denikin and Wrangel, and ultimately the Red Army. The force gained mobility through the tachanka, a horse-drawn cart mounting a machine gun. Its most celebrated engagement came in September 1919 at Peregonovka, where it routed Denikin's rear. The Black Army allied twice with the Red Army (in 1919 and again in October–November 1920 during the Crimea campaign against Wrangel), only to be attacked by Bolshevik forces after each alliance.

Makhno's political vision rested on free soviets without party control and the Regional Congresses of Peasants, Workers, and Insurgents (established in 1919) to hold the army accountable. His territory granted land to those who worked it, maintained freedom of the press and of all socialist parties, and suppressed state organs. The Makhnovists were known to use multiple currencies in circulation; accounts claiming they stamped banknotes with anarchist slogans and declared the forgery unpunished remain unverified here.

In 1921, Makhno fled Ukraine into exile—Romania, Poland, Danzig, and finally Paris from 1925. He worked in factories and suffered from tuberculosis, dying in Paris in 1934. In Paris, he joined Pyotr Arshinov, Ida Mett, and others in the Dielo Truda group. Together they authored the *Organizational Platform of the Libertarian Communists* (1926), which codified four principles for a general anarchist organization: theoretical unity, tactical unity, collective responsibility, and [federation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/federation.md). This platform became foundational to the tradition later called platformism. Makhno also wrote memoirs: *The Russian Revolution in Ukraine* (1926) and two posthumous volumes.

## Core ideas

- **[Self-organized](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/self-organization.md) territory without party rule.** The army's structure answered to congresses of peasants, workers, and insurgents, not to a centralized party apparatus.
- **Collective responsibility.** All members of a [federation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/federation.md) bear responsibility for the organization's line—a principle that became platformism's theoretical anchor.
- **Mobility over fixed position.** The tachanka and the Black Army's speed gave the Makhnovists advantage against stationary forces.
- **Communes as the land's form.** [Expropriated](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expropriation.md) estates were reorganized as working communes.
- **[Federation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/federation.md) of autonomous units.** Congresses coordinated but did not command the territory's communes.

## Key works

- *The Russian Revolution in Ukraine* (1926, memoirs)
- *Organizational Platform of the Libertarian Communists* (1926, co-authored with the Dielo Truda group)
- Two further volumes of memoirs, published posthumously

## Where their work figures in this library

The Makhnovist territory exemplifies the principles documented in the library's terms [federation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/federation.md), [self-organization](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/self-organization.md), [dual-power](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/dual-power.md), and [expropriation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expropriation.md). The *Organizational Platform* presents collective responsibility as the answer to centralized party control, standing against the [informal organization](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/informal-organization.md) practiced by [Alfredo Bonanno](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/alfredo-bonanno.md)—the library holds both as the tradition's unresolved organizational question. Makhno visited [Pyotr Kropotkin](./pyotr-kropotkin.md) in Moscow in 1918, positioning the Makhnovists within the lineage of Russian-Ukrainian anarchism.
