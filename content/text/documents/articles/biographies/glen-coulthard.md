Glen Sean Coulthard is a member of the Yellowknives Dene First Nation, a political philosopher, and an associate professor of First Nations and Indigenous Studies at the University of British Columbia. His work argues that the politics of recognition — the demand that the [settler-colonial](../../sociology/topics/settler-colonialism/index.md) state acknowledge Indigenous rights — has become a mechanism of colonial governance rather than a path to Indigenous freedom.

## Core ideas

- **Critique of recognition**: recognition, when granted by the colonizer on the colonizer's terms, reproduces the colonial relationship. It makes Indigenous freedom conditional on settler approval and channels Indigenous political energy into state-defined processes (treaty negotiations, land claims, constitutional reform) that leave settler sovereignty intact.
- **[Grounded normativity](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/grounded-normativity.md)**: ethical and political frameworks rooted in Indigenous relationships to specific places and practices, rather than in the abstract universal norms of liberal political theory. The land itself teaches how to live; governance arises from that relationship.
- **Primitive accumulation as ongoing**: Coulthard argues (following [Marx](./karl-marx.md) but redirecting him) that the dispossession of Indigenous peoples from their land is not a historical precondition for [capitalism](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/capitalism.md) that has already occurred — it is an ongoing, constitutive feature of [settler-colonial](../../sociology/topics/settler-colonialism/index.md) [capitalism](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/capitalism.md).
- **Indigenous resurgence over recognition**: the constructive alternative is to turn away from the settler state's recognition apparatus and toward the land-based practices, languages, and relations that constitute Indigenous political life on its own terms. Refusal is productive here, not merely negative.

## Significance for this research

Coulthard's [grounded normativity](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/grounded-normativity.md) connects directly to the [relational framework](/relationality/index.md): relations to land constitute the ethical subject, rather than the ethical subject arriving at land with pre-formed principles. His critique of recognition parallels the relational critique of entity-first [ontology](../../philosophy/terms/ontology.md) — both refuse to treat the terms offered by the dominant framework as the only available starting point.
