adrienne maree brown is an American writer, facilitator, and doula for social movements whose work translates patterns observed in living systems and in the science fiction of Octavia Butler into a practice for organizing. Her central claim is that the shape of change at a small scale — a conversation, a team, a single relationship — is not separate from the shape of change at the scale of a movement or a society, and that intentional practice at the small scale is how the large scale is actually shaped.

## Core ideas

- **Emergent strategy.** A way of organizing that takes its model from how complex living systems change: through small, local, adaptive actions taken by interdependent parts, rather than through a plan imposed from a center. Change is understood as constant and iterative rather than as a single decisive event to be won.
- **Fractal patterns.** What happens at the smallest scale of a group or relationship is taken to be a pattern of what happens at the scale of the whole — "the patterns of the universe repeat at scale." Attending to how people treat each other in a room is, on this view, a way of shaping how a movement behaves.
- **Octavia Butler as source.** brown draws directly on Butler's fiction, especially the Earthseed novels' framing that "the only lasting truth is change," reading Butler's work as already articulating principles for adaptive, resilient organizing under conditions people cannot fully control.
- **Biomimicry as method.** Alongside Butler, brown draws on biomimicry — learning organizational form from how living systems adapt, decentralize, and become resilient — as a second source for the elements and principles she collects under emergent strategy, including commitments such as "small is good, small is all," moving at the speed of trust, and trusting the people to organize themselves.

## Key works

- *Emergent Strategy: Shaping Change, Changing Worlds* (2017)
- *Pleasure Activism: The Politics of Feeling Good* (2019)
- *We Will Not Cancel Us: And Other Dreams of Transformative Justice* (2020)
- *Holding Change: The Way of Emergent Strategy Facilitation and Mediation* (2021)
