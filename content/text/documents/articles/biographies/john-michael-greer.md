John Michael Greer is an American writer, long associated with the Druidic revival in the United States (formerly Grand Archdruid of the Ancient Order of Druids in America), and one of the most prolific popular writers on peak oil and societal decline of the 2000s and 2010s, principally through the blog he ran for over a decade, "The Archdruid Report," and its successor "Ecosophia."

## Core ideas

- **[Catabolic collapse](../../../../../services/history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/glossary/terms/catabolic-collapse.md).** Greer's 2005 essay "How Civilizations Fall: A Theory of Catabolic Collapse" argues that societies build up "capital" — infrastructure, population, institutions — that requires ongoing maintenance energy to sustain. When the resources available for maintenance fall short, a society lets some of that capital decay or be scavenged for its resource content ("catabolized") in order to keep the remaining capital functioning, producing an uneven, stepwise, lurching decline rather than a single clean collapse event.
- **"Collapse now and avoid the rush."** Greer coined this phrase, urging voluntary, early downshifting of consumption and expectations as preferable to being forced into the same adaptation later, under worse and less chosen conditions.
- **Book-length development.** *The Long Descent* (2008), *The Ecotechnic Future* (2009), *Decline and Fall* (2014), and *Dark Age America* (2016) extend the catabolic-collapse framework across the trajectory of industrial civilization and, in the last, specifically to the United States.
