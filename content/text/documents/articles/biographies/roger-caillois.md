Roger Caillois (1913-1978) was a French sociologist, literary critic, and writer whose work spans the sociology of play, the sacred, and the fantastic. In the 1930s he co-founded, with Georges Bataille and Michel Leiris, the Collège de Sociologie, a short-lived group devoted to a "sacred sociology" of modern collective life. He later directed UNESCO's publications program and edited the journal *Diogène*.

## Core ideas

- **Agon, alea, mimicry, ilinx.** In *Les jeux et les hommes*, Caillois classifies games and play into four fundamental categories: agon (competition), alea (chance), mimicry (simulation and role-play), and ilinx (vertigo, the pursuit of disorienting sensation). The four categories are not exhaustive of any single game but name the dominant impulse organizing it.
- **The paidia-ludus continuum.** Cutting across the four categories is a second axis running from paidia — free, spontaneous, rule-improvising play — to ludus — play increasingly bound by arbitrary, imperative, and purposely difficult conventions. Any game can be located along this continuum as well as within one of the four categories.
- **Play and the structure of society.** Caillois argues that the dominant configuration of play in a given society is diagnostic of that society's broader institutional and cultural structure — a claim extending his earlier sociology of the sacred into a sociology of games.
- **The sacred and the profane in modern life.** In *L'Homme et le sacré*, Caillois develops an account of the sacred as a structuring category of collective life that persists, transformed, within ostensibly secular modern societies — the theoretical ground from which the Collège de Sociologie's project proceeded.

## Key works

- *L'Homme et le sacré* (1939)
- *Les jeux et les hommes* (1958; translated as *Man, Play and Games*, 1961)
