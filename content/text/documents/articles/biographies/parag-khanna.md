Parag Khanna (1977-) is a global strategist and author whose work maps global power through infrastructure and connectivity rather than through the traditional lens of national borders and military capacity.

## Core ideas

- **[Connectography](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/political-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/connectography.md).** *Connectography: Mapping the Future of Global Civilization* (2016) argues that transportation corridors, energy pipelines and grids, and fiber-optic and data cables are superseding political borders as the primary map of global power: a city or region's economic fate is increasingly set by what it is connected to rather than by which country it nominally belongs to.
- **A broadly optimistic reading of integration.** Khanna reads continued infrastructure buildout, urbanization, and connective density as largely growth-enabling and durable, a more sanguine forecast of continued globalization than the demographic- and energy-security-driven deglobalization argued by [Peter Zeihan](./peter-zeihan.md).
- **Asia-centered forecasting.** *The Future Is Asian* (2019) extends the connectivity thesis to argue that the twenty-first century's center of gravity — trade, technology, urbanization — is shifting decisively toward Asia's interconnected economies. *Move: The Forces Uprooting Us* (2021) applies the same framework to human migration, treating movement toward connective hubs as continuous with the infrastructure thesis.

## What the 561 Group reads them for

Khanna is the house's clearest statement of the position it reads against, per the brief's own framing: where the [energy-return-on-investment](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/energy-return-on-investment.md) position the house holds — aligned with [Nate Hagens](./nate-hagens.md), [Richard Heinberg](./richard-heinberg.md), and [Joseph Tainter](./joseph-tainter.md) — treats declining net energy as the base physical pressure that finance and connectivity can buffer but not remove (the [2025-12-25 oil-sanctions log](../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/logs/personal-logs/2025/12/25/2025-12-25-emsenn-personal-log-about-latest-oil-sanctions.md): "a financialized buffer around breakdown"), Khanna's connectivity thesis reads infrastructure integration as a largely independent, durable source of continued growth. The house's own stated position is that this is Khanna's claim, not a settled fact: connectivity can reroute and rebuffer declining energy returns, as the choke-point analysis of shipping insurance, benchmarking, and clearing systems in the oil-sanctions log documents in detail, but the house does not read rerouting as resolving the underlying constraint the way Khanna's framing implies. Khanna's optimism is also in direct tension with [Peter Zeihan](./peter-zeihan.md)'s demographic-deglobalization forecast — the two are read as opposed forecasts about the same near-term period, to be scored against events rather than both accepted.
