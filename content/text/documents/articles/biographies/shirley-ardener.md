Shirley Ardener was a British social anthropologist whose work compared rotating credit associations across a wide range of societies and who paid particular attention to women's economic organizing, a topic much of the earlier literature had passed over. She founded the Centre for Cross-Cultural Research on Women at Queen Elizabeth House, Oxford, a base for research connecting anthropology to women's economic and social lives across regions.

## Core ideas

- **The comparative study of rotating credit associations.** Ardener's 1964 survey brought together evidence on rotating credit associations from across Africa, Asia, and the Caribbean, showing the institution to be a widespread and independently arising response to shared problems of saving and credit rather than a feature confined to any one culture.
- **Money-Go-Rounds and women's participation.** Edited with Sandra Burman, *Money-Go-Rounds: The Importance of Rotating Savings and Credit Associations for Women* argued that these associations are especially significant for women, who in many of the societies studied have had less access than men to formal banking and to individually held collateral, making a savings and credit instrument built on group membership rather than collateral especially valuable to them.
- **Perceiving women.** Ardener's edited volume *Perceiving Women* argued for taking women's own categories and perceptions as a starting point for anthropological analysis, rather than analyzing women's lives solely through categories developed from the study of men.
- **Institution-building for cross-cultural women's research.** Beyond her own scholarship, Ardener built a research center dedicated to comparative, cross-cultural study of women's lives, giving the topic an institutional home within Oxford anthropology.

## Key works

- "The Comparative Study of Rotating Credit Associations" (*Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute of Great Britain and Ireland*, 1964)
- *Perceiving Women* (edited, 1975)
- *Money-Go-Rounds: The Importance of Rotating Savings and Credit Associations for Women* (edited with Sandra Burman, 1995)
