Albert Libertad was born Joseph Albert (1875-1908) in Bordeaux and raised in public foundling care. He came to Paris in the late 1890s and worked as a proofreader and typographer in the anarchist press. His legs were paralyzed from early on, and he moved on crutches, which he was reportedly known to use as weapons. In 1902, he began organizing the Causeries populaires, public talks open to working people discussing anarchist ideas. In April 1905, with Anna Mahé, he founded the weekly newspaper *l'anarchie*, which became the central publication of French individualist anarchism. He died in Paris in November 1908 following a beating; the exact cause is disputed in the sources. He willed his body to the medical school.

## Core ideas

- **Anarchy as immediate practice.** The individual does not defer life and pleasure to a future revolutionary rupture. Living anarchically means refusing wage labor and property law now, in the present.
- **The vote as self-negation.** The voter is the criminal because voting delegates one's own power to another and legitimizes the state. One who votes is complicit in every act the elected representative commits in their name.
- **Camaraderie and free association.** The individualist anarchist milieu around *l'anarchie* practiced free love, communal living, and voluntary association without hierarchy. Association is entered and left at will, dissolving when it no longer serves those in it.
- **Critique of sacrifice.** Libertad opposed both sacrifice to past figures and sacrifice of the living present to a future cause. The militant who renounces their own life for the movement or for an ideal has made the cause into one more thing that rules them.
- **The illegalism debate.** *l'anarchie* carried active debate over whether theft, counterfeiting, and fraud against property constitute legitimate anarchist practice. Libertad's position was that living without wage labor and without obeying property law is itself the practice. After his death, the milieu produced the Bonnot gang (1911-1912), members of which had written for or read the paper.

## Key works

- "Le Culte de la charogne" (The Cult of Carrion), against the worship of the dead and sacrifice of present life to future causes
- "Le criminel, c'est l'électeur" (The Criminal Is the Voter, 1906), against voting and electoral participation
- *l'anarchie* (weekly newspaper, founded 1905, edited until his death in 1908)
- Short polemical articles and notes in the press, collected posthumously in *Le Culte de la charogne: anarchisme, un siècle après* (Agone, 2006)

## Where their work figures in this library

Libertad is the founding figure of the French individualist anarchist milieu and stands at the origin of the [illegalism](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/illegalism.md) debate that shaped early twentieth-century anarchism. His emphasis on living anarchically now, without deferring value to an accumulated future, places him in the same philosophical current as [Max Stirner](./max-stirner.md) and directly informs the library's engagement with [voluntary association](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/voluntary-association.md), [propaganda of the deed](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/propaganda-of-the-deed.md), and [direct action](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/direct-action.md). The paper *l'anarchie* and the milieu around it produced the Bonnot gang, which the library names under illegalism. His position that living without wage labor and property law is itself anarchist practice — rather than a strategy, preparation, or crime requiring separate moral justification — is an argument the library's economics notes on sizing and pooling make again in another vocabulary.
