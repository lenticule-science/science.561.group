Ole Peters is a physicist affiliated with the London Mathematical Laboratory and the Santa Fe Institute. He developed ergodicity economics, a research program applying the concept of ergodicity from statistical mechanics to economic decision-making — asking not what an ensemble of parallel actors would earn on average from a gamble, but what a single actor's wealth does over time as that gamble repeats.

## Core ideas

- **The ergodicity problem in economics.** Standard economic decision theory evaluates gambles by their expected value — an average across a hypothetical ensemble of parallel outcomes. Peters argues this ensemble average is the wrong quantity whenever the process is non-ergodic: for multiplicative wealth dynamics, the ensemble average and the time average of a single trajectory diverge, and it is the time average that describes what actually happens to a real, non-resettable individual.
- **Time-average growth vs. expected value.** Because wealth compounds multiplicatively, a gamble can have positive expected value while nonetheless driving any individual's wealth to near-certain ruin over repeated play — the two criteria can recommend opposite decisions.
- **Recovering the Kelly criterion from first principles.** Ergodicity economics recovers betting and investment rules structurally equivalent to the Kelly criterion — maximizing the time-average growth rate rather than expected value — without needing utility functions or risk-aversion parameters as primitives; risk aversion emerges from the dynamics themselves.
- **Optimal leverage under non-ergodicity.** Applied to leveraged investment, the time-average framework yields an optimal leverage level distinct from that implied by expected-value or standard utility-based analyses, with over-leveraged positions exposed to time-average ruin even when their expected value looks favorable.

## Key works

- "Optimal leverage from non-ergodicity" (*Quantitative Finance*, 2011)
- "Evaluating gambles using dynamics" (with Murray Gell-Mann, *Chaos*, 2016)
- "The ergodicity problem in economics" (*Nature Physics*, 2019)
