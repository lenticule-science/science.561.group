Alfredo Bonanno (1937–2023) was an Italian anarchist whose theoretical and practical work defined insurrectionary anarchism as a distinct current. His central argument was organizational: permanent structures — parties, unions, and federations with fixed membership — reproduce hierarchy and bureaucracy regardless of their stated purpose. His alternative was informal organization: temporary, task-specific coordination among affinity groups that dissolves when its purpose is achieved.

## Core ideas

- **Armed joy**: freedom experienced in the act of resistance is not a byproduct of struggle but its purpose. A revolution that demands sacrifice and deferred gratification reproduces the logic of domination it claims to oppose.
- **Informal organization**: the structural alternative to both formal organization, which accumulates hierarchy, and individualism, which cannot coordinate. Affinity groups form around shared projects, coordinate through direct relationships and trust, act, and dissolve.
- **The insurrectionary project**: a stance of permanent hostility toward existing authority, refusal of negotiation, and direct action against structures of domination. Its object is not a counter-power but ruptures that demonstrate both the fragility of the existing order and the possibility of living otherwise.
- **Critique of the movement**: anarchism-as-identity can become a comfortable substitute for resistance and a form of recuperation—radical aesthetics without radical practice.

## Significance for this research

Bonanno's work poses a fundamental organizational question: does every permanent structure eventually reproduce hierarchy? His affirmative answer, and his proposal to organize informally instead, is contested within anarchism, particularly by anarcho-syndicalists who regard durable organization as necessary. The question nevertheless forces every anarchist organizational form to justify itself against the charge that it reproduces what it opposes.
