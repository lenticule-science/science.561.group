Buenaventura Durruti (1896–1936) was a Spanish anarchist born in León who worked as a railway mechanic. He participated in the 1917 general strike and, after dismissal for his activism, fled Spain. In the early 1920s he joined the Confederación Nacional del Trabajo (CNT), the anarcho-syndicalist union.

With Francisco Ascaso, Juan García Oliver, and Gregorio Jover, Durruti formed the affinity group Los Solidarios in 1922. The group carried out armed actions to fund the anarchist movement: the killing of the archbishop of Zaragoza, Cardinal Soldevila, in 1923, and the armed robbery of the Bank of Spain branch in Gijón in September 1923. Proceeds from these actions financed arms, the movement's publications, and support for imprisoned comrades. Durruti and Ascaso travelled through Latin America between 1925 and 1926; details of robberies attributed to them during this period remain unverified.

Arrested in Paris in 1926 over an alleged conspiracy against King Alfonso XIII, Durruti and Ascaso were expelled from France. Under the Spanish Republic that followed, Durruti was repeatedly imprisoned. He became a leading figure in both the FAI (Iberian Anarchist Federation) and the CNT.

In July 1936, after the military rising in Spain, Durruti was among the workers who defeated the rising in Barcelona. He then led the Durruti Column—a militia numbering several thousand fighters—to the Aragon front. Villages along its advance collectivized their land and means of production under working-class control. On 20 November 1936, Durruti died in Madrid of a gunshot wound; the exact circumstances of his death remain disputed.

Durruti is remembered for a statement from an interview with the journalist Pierre van Paassen published in the Toronto Star in 1936: he declared that the workers feared not the ruins of the old world, because they carried a new world in their hearts that was growing.

## Core ideas

- **[Expropriation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expropriation.md) as movement finance.** Armed expropriations funded the anarchist press, armament, and prisoner support—the expropriated wealth served the movement's capacity, not the expropriators' personal gain.
- **The [affinity group](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/affinity-group.md) as action unit.** Small, trusted groups capable of coordinated [direct action](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/direct-action.md).
- **Militia discipline by agreement.** Military coordination without hierarchical rank; [solidarity](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/solidarity.md) and shared commitment replace commanding officers.
- **Immediate collectivization.** The villages captured by the Durruti Column collectivized production and land from the outset—the revolution is not deferred to a future day but built as the fighting happens.
- **The refusal to defer.** The new world is not a promise after victory but a lived present in collective work and [self-organization](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/self-organization.md).

## Key works

Durruti left no books. The record is the interview with Pierre van Paassen (Toronto Star, 1936) and the biographies by Abel Paz and Hans Magnus Enzensberger.

## Where their work figures in this library

Durruti exemplifies the affinity group at its most consequential and the working-class collectivization of production at its most complete. The library's terms [expropriation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expropriation.md), [anarcho-syndicalism](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/anarcho-syndicalism.md), [federation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/federation.md), [direct-action](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/direct-action.md), and [solidarity](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/solidarity.md) describe what the Durruti Column and its collectivizations realized. [Lucio Urtubia](./lucio-urtubia.md) documents the later Spanish exile practice of [expropriation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expropriation.md) and forgery; [Errico Malatesta](./errico-malatesta.md) and [Mikhail Bakunin](./mikhail-bakunin.md) represent the collectivist lineage within anarchism that Durruti inherited and practiced.
