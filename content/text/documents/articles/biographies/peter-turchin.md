Peter Turchin (1957-) is a Russian-American complexity scientist, originally trained in population biology, who applies quantitative, model-driven methods to long-run historical dynamics under the name he coined for the field, "cliodynamics." His structural-demographic theory extends earlier work by Jack Goldstone into a general account of why complex agrarian and industrial societies undergo recurring multi-generational cycles of instability.

## Core ideas

- **[Structural-demographic theory](../../../../../services/history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/glossary/terms/structural-demographic-theory.md).** Societies move through "secular cycles" of roughly two to three centuries, driven by the interaction of population growth outrunning the carrying capacity of the existing economic and political order, "elite overproduction" (more aspirants for a fixed or slow-growing number of elite positions than the system can absorb, producing intra-elite competition and conflict), "popular immiseration" (falling real wages and declining living standards for the general population), and state fiscal distress (a state caught between rising costs and a shrinking or resistant tax base).
- **A dated forecast.** In a 2010 commentary in *Nature*, Turchin projected rising political instability in the United States on a trajectory peaking around 2020, based on measured trends in elite overproduction (the rapid growth of the credentialed, wealth-holding population relative to available elite positions) and wage stagnation since the 1970s. The forecast is widely cited as prescient given the convergence of pandemic disruption, mass protest, and the January 6, 2021 Capitol events, though Turchin himself frames the finding as structural rather than as prediction of specific events.
- **Book-length development.** *Secular Cycles* (with Sergey Nefedov, 2009) develops the quantitative historical case across multiple pre-industrial societies; *Ages of Discord* (2016) applies the model to US history since 1780; *End Times: Elites, Counter-Elites, and the Path of Political Disintegration* (2023) restates the theory for a general audience against the backdrop of the 2020s instability the 2010 forecast anticipated.

## What the 561 Group reads them for

Turchin's structural-demographic account is read alongside [George Friedman](./george-friedman.md)'s cyclical account of American internal stress (*The Storm Before the Calm*) as two independently derived structural readings converging on the same 2020s period, which the house treats as a case where two different methods reaching the same conclusion is evidence worth weighing rather than a coincidence to ignore. Turchin's method is also read as a genuinely falsifiable, dated, scoreable forecast in the spirit [Philip Tetlock](./philip-tetlock.md)'s calibration standard asks for — the 2010 *Nature* piece names a mechanism, a direction, and a rough date in advance, unlike much pundit-style geopolitical commentary.
