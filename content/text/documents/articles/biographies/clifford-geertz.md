Clifford Geertz (1926-2006) was an American cultural anthropologist whose fieldwork in Indonesia and Morocco and whose method of interpretive, "thick" description of cultural life shaped anthropology and the social sciences more broadly. His early economic anthropology, based on fieldwork in Java, gave rotating credit associations one of their first sustained treatments as an economic institution doing real developmental work rather than a curiosity of traditional life.

## Core ideas

- **The rotating credit association as a "middle rung."** Geertz described the rotating credit association as an institution positioned between a traditional, kin-based economy and a fully commercialized market economy — a device that let people accustomed to informal, personalized economic relations begin to practice the discipline and trust required by impersonal credit markets.
- **Agricultural involution.** In his study of Indonesian rice agriculture, Geertz described a pattern of economic development in which a system absorbs population growth and increasing labor input by intensifying and elaborating its existing techniques, rather than transforming into a qualitatively different, more productive system.
- **Thick description.** Adapting a term from the philosopher Gilbert Ryle, Geertz argued that cultural anthropology's task is to interpret the layered meanings a given practice or gesture has for the people performing it, not merely to record the observable behavior — a stated commitment to the same interpretive move as his account of the rotating credit association's meaning to its members.
- **Peddlers and princes.** Geertz's comparative study of two Indonesian towns examined how different existing social structures — one built around commercial trading networks, the other around a court-centered aristocracy — shaped how each town took up modern economic change differently.

## Key works

- "The Rotating Credit Association: A 'Middle Rung' in Development" (*Economic Development and Cultural Change*, 1962)
- *Peddlers and Princes: Social Development and Economic Change in Two Indonesian Towns* (1963)
- *Agricultural Involution: The Processes of Ecological Change in Indonesia* (1963)
- *The Interpretation of Cultures* (1973)
