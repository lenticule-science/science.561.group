K. Wayne Yang is a scholar on the faculty at the University of California, San Diego, working across ethnic studies and education. With Eve Tuck he co-founded and co-edits the journal *Critical Ethnic Studies*. Writing under the pseudonym la paperson, he is the author of *A Third University Is Possible*.

## Core ideas

- **Decolonization is not a metaphor.** The 2012 essay co-authored with Eve Tuck argues that decolonization has a specific, non-figurative referent — the repatriation of Indigenous land and the dissolution of settler sovereignty — and that its adoption as a general metaphor for any justice-oriented project ("decolonize the curriculum") evacuates that material demand.
- **Settler moves to innocence.** Developed with Tuck in the same essay, the concept names the strategies — settler adoption of Indigenous identity, disavowal of complicity, gestures of inclusion — through which settlers attempt to reconcile their sense of justice with their continued occupation of Indigenous land, without relinquishing it.
- **Incommensurability.** Decolonization, on Yang and Tuck's account, cannot be folded into multicultural inclusion, critical pedagogy, or human-rights discourse without losing its specific demand; solidarity across decolonial and other justice projects requires working across this incommensurability rather than collapsing it.
- **A third university.** Writing as la paperson in *A Third University Is Possible*, Yang examines how universities function as settler-colonial institutions — not only through curricula but through their structural role in land appropriation and knowledge extraction — and proposes the "third university" as an institutional form organized around Indigenous self-determination rather than settler-state credentialing.
