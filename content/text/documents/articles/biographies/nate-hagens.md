Nate Hagens is an American ecological economist who worked as a trader at Salomon Brothers and Lehman Brothers before earning a PhD in natural resources at the University of Vermont under Charles Hall, the ecologist who developed the concept of energy return on investment (EROI). He edited *The Oil Drum*, an influential peak-oil analysis site, and now teaches at the University of Minnesota and hosts *The Great Simplification*, a podcast and newsletter synthesizing energy, economics, and human behavior.

## Core ideas

- **The human superorganism.** Hagens's 2020 paper "Economics for the future — Beyond the superorganism" (*Ecological Economics*, vol. 169) argues that humanity functions collectively as a single energy-processing superorganism, whose aggregate behavior is driven by the availability of surplus energy rather than by the sum of individually rational choices — an extension of the ultrasociality concept from evolutionary biology to industrial civilization as a whole.
- **Energy blindness.** Mainstream economics treats energy as one substitutable input among many (capital, labor, land) rather than as the master resource whose surplus — measured as [energy return on investment](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/energy-return-on-investment.md) — makes every other form of economic activity, including money creation and social complexity, possible in the first place. Hagens argues this blindness leads policy and markets to systematically misprice the consequences of declining net energy.
- **Synthesizing Hall and Tainter.** Hagens explicitly builds on Charles Hall's EROI research and [Joseph Tainter](./joseph-tainter.md)'s diminishing-returns-on-complexity theory, adding an account of evolved human reward psychology — reward circuitry shaped for a scarcity environment now operating inside a temporary high-throughput fossil-fuel economy ("the carbon pulse") — as the behavioral mechanism that keeps the system pursuing growth past the point its own energy base can support.
