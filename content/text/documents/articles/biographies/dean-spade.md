Dean Spade is an American legal scholar and activist who founded the Sylvia Rivera Law Project and has written on trans politics, the administrative state, and mutual aid. His work argues that meeting people's survival needs and organizing to change the conditions that produce those needs are not separate activities but the same one, carried out by people who are themselves affected rather than by outside providers.

## Core ideas

- **Mutual aid's key elements.** Spade sets out mutual aid as work that does three things at once: it meets people's immediate survival needs; it builds shared understanding, among everyone involved, of why those needs are going unmet by existing systems; and it mobilizes participants into collective action, rather than treating aid recipients as passive beneficiaries.
- **Mutual aid against charity.** Charity is organized top-down, treats poverty and crisis as individual misfortune rather than as produced by unjust systems, and separates a class of givers from a class of recipients. Mutual aid instead is participatory and horizontal: the people affected by a crisis are also the people deciding how to respond to it, and the work is meant to build lasting solidarity and organizing capacity, not just deliver a service.
- **Big Door Brigade.** A project Spade runs collecting mutual aid resources, guides, and examples, meant to make the practical work of starting and running a mutual aid project more accessible.
- **Normal life and administrative violence.** In earlier work, Spade analyzed how administrative systems — identity documents, benefits eligibility, criminal law — produce harm for trans people through their ordinary, non-exceptional operation, arguing that legal recognition alone does not reach this kind of violence.

## Key works

- *Normal Life: Administrative Violence, Critical Trans Politics, and the Limits of Law* (2011)
- *Mutual Aid: Building Solidarity During This Crisis (and the Next)* (2020)

## Where their work figures in this library

Spade's account of mutual aid — meeting need, building shared understanding, and organizing collectively, as distinct from charity — is this library's [mutual aid](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/mutual-aid.md) term, cited to his own [Mutual Aid (2020)](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/bibliographies/spade2020.md).
