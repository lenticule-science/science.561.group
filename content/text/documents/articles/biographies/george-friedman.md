George Friedman (1949-) is an American geopolitical forecaster and author. He founded Stratfor in 1996, building it into one of the best-known private geopolitical intelligence firms before leaving in 2015 to found Geopolitical Futures. His method treats states as bounded by durable structural constraints — geography, demography, economic necessity — that determine the range of actions available to a state's leadership regardless of ideology, personality, or stated intentions; a state's behavior, on this account, is forecastable as the outworking of "impersonal forces" rather than as the product of individual decision-makers' choices.

## Core ideas

- **[Geopolitical forecasting](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/political-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/geopolitical-forecasting.md) as structural necessity.** Friedman's method holds that a nation's leaders, whoever they are, tend to converge on similar strategic choices because geography and demography leave them little real room to do otherwise — Russia's perennial anxiety about buffer states on its western flatlands, or China's structural need for coastal control, recur across regimes because the underlying constraint does not change with the government.
- **Long cycles in American politics.** *The Storm Before the Calm* (2020) argues the United States moves through roughly 50-year institutional cycles and roughly 80-year socioeconomic cycles, and that the country was due, around 2020, for a convergence of both cycles' stress points at once.
- **Specific, dated, near-term forecasting.** *The Next 100 Years* (2009) and *The Next Decade* (2011) commit to specific predictions about the rise and fall of particular powers (a resurgent Poland and Turkey, a more assertive Japan, sustained American primacy through the century) on a named timetable — a style that invites direct scoring against outcomes as the timetable elapses, and that critics have faulted for false confidence in cases where the specifics did not play out as forecast.

## What the 561 Group reads them for

Friedman is read as the clearest statement of the "geography and demography as destiny" school that also includes [Peter Zeihan](./peter-zeihan.md) (Friedman's former employee) and, in a more popular register, [Tim Marshall](./tim-marshall.md). The house's interest in his method is methodological rather than adoptive: [Philip Tetlock](./philip-tetlock.md)'s calibration research found that confident, specific, undiscounted forecasts of Friedman's kind are exactly the style that underperforms structured probabilistic judgment in controlled scoring, which is why the house's own [forecasting-services-section](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/forecasting-services-section/README.md) records a Brier score against every forecast rather than treating a plausible-sounding structural narrative as sufficient on its own. Friedman's substantive claims about American internal cyclical stress are read alongside [Peter Turchin](./peter-turchin.md)'s structural-demographic account of the same period, as two independently derived readings of the same 2020s stress that the house treats as evidence to be weighed, not as a forecast to inherit outright.
