Joy James is an American political philosopher who teaches at Williams College. Her work develops a Black feminist political theory of state violence, captivity, and abolition, tracing how the reproductive and caretaking labor she names the captive maternal is extracted under carceral and racial-capitalist conditions.

## Core ideas

- **The captive maternal.** Developed in "The Womb of Western Theory: Trauma, Time Theft, and the Captive Maternal" (*Carceral Notebooks* 12, 2016), the captive maternal names the figure — historically raced female, but not reducible to a gender — whose reproductive, nurturing, and stabilizing labor is captured and extracted by the state and by political-economic structures, including by radical movements that reproduce the extraction they claim to oppose.
- **Revolutionary love against carceral extraction.** *In Pursuit of Revolutionary Love* (2022) develops an ethics and politics of care that refuses the captive maternal's structural position, distinguishing love as extracted labor from love as a revolutionary practice.
- **Abolition beyond reform.** James's editorial and theoretical work insists that abolition names the dismantling of the carceral state's structuring logics, not incremental reform of its institutions — a position she develops across her own writing and through the intellectuals she anthologizes.
- **State violence as ordinary, not exceptional.** *Resisting State Violence* (1996) and *Shadowboxing: Representations of Black Feminist Politics* (1999) analyze state violence against Black communities as continuous with, rather than a deviation from, the normal operation of American political institutions.
