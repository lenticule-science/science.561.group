Timothy Besley is a British economist at the London School of Economics whose work in development and political economy includes the formal economic modeling of informal financial institutions and the political economy of good government. His papers with Stephen Coate and Glenn Loury gave rotating savings and credit associations and joint-liability lending their standard economic treatment: showing how each arrangement can substitute for the collateral and enforcement that formal lenders lack, using participants' own information about and leverage over each other.

## Core ideas

- **The economics of rotating savings and credit associations.** With Coate and Loury, Besley modeled ROSCAs as a way for members without access to formal credit or insurance markets to reallocate savings over time, showing conditions under which participation is a rational response to constrained credit markets rather than a suboptimal substitute for a bank.
- **Joint liability and repayment incentives.** With Coate, Besley showed how making a group of borrowers jointly liable for each other's loans can improve repayment relative to individual lending, because it gives group members reason to select reliable partners, monitor each other's use of the loan, and pressure a partner who could repay but is not doing so.
- **Social collateral.** The concept, developed in this work, that a borrower's relationships with other people function as collateral: those people can and will impose costs on the borrower for defaulting, substituting for the physical or financial collateral a formal lender would otherwise require.
- **The political economy of government.** In later work, including *Principled Agents?* and, with Torsten Persson, *Pillars of Prosperity*, Besley extended this style of formal, incentive-based analysis to why some states build effective institutions of taxation, legal capacity, and public-good provision and others do not.

## Key works

- "The Economics of Rotating Savings and Credit Associations" (with Stephen Coate and Glenn Loury, *American Economic Review*, 1993)
- "Group Lending, Repayment Incentives and Social Collateral" (with Stephen Coate, *Journal of Development Economics*, 1995)
- *Principled Agents? The Political Economy of Good Government* (2006)
- *Pillars of Prosperity: The Political Economics of Development Clusters* (with Torsten Persson, 2011)
