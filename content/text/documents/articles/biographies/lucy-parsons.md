Lucy Parsons (c. 1851–1942) was an American anarchist, labor organizer, and writer whose life and work demonstrated the inseparability of racial oppression, [class](../../terms/class.md) [exploitation](../../terms/exploitation.md), and gendered [domination](../../terms/domination.md). Born in Texas — likely to enslaved or formerly enslaved parents, with Black, Mexican, and possibly Native American heritage — she became one of the most prominent radical organizers in the United States, co-founding the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW) and advocating [the general strike](../../terms/the-general-strike.md), [direct action](../../terms/direct-action.md), and the abolition of [capitalism](../../terms/capitalism.md).

## Core ideas

- **The interconnection of race, class, and gender**: Parsons argued from experience that racial oppression and economic exploitation are not separate systems but aspects of the same structure of [domination](../../terms/domination.md). The same system that exploits workers exploits Black people; the same [hierarchy](../../terms/hierarchy.md) that subordinates labor subordinates women. This analysis anticipated intersectional frameworks by nearly a century.

- **[Direct action](../../terms/direct-action.md) and the general strike**: Parsons advocated the [general strike](../../terms/the-general-strike.md) as the most powerful weapon of the working class — the collective withdrawal of [labor](../../terms/labor.md) that reveals the dependence of [the state](../../terms/the-state.md) and [capital](../../terms/capitalism.md) on workers' [obedience](../../terms/obedience.md). "Never be deceived that the rich will allow you to vote away their wealth."

- **Against charity, for [solidarity](../../terms/solidarity.md)**: Parsons distinguished between charity (which reinforces hierarchy by positioning the giver above the receiver) and [solidarity](../../terms/solidarity.md) (which stands together against shared domination). The poor do not need benefactors; they need to organize.

- **The critique of law**: Law, Parsons argued, is not the protector of the weak but the instrument of the strong. "The law is only for those who possess." Legal equality without economic equality is a fiction that legitimizes [exploitation](../../terms/exploitation.md).

## Significance for this research

Parsons's life demonstrates what anarchist analysis looks like when it begins from the intersection of racial, economic, and gendered domination rather than from European political theory. Her work challenges the whiteness of much anarchist historiography — she was doing intersectional anarchist analysis in the 1880s, drawing on lived experience of slavery, racial terror, and labor exploitation, not on Bakunin or Kropotkin.

The state's response to her work — her husband Albert Parsons was executed after the Haymarket affair, her home was raided after her death, and her papers were seized and never returned — also illustrates the [coercion](../../terms/coercion.md) that maintains [the state](../../terms/the-state.md): not the coercion of law enforcement alone, but the targeted destruction of radical knowledge and memory.
