I keep going back-and-forth with myself on whether to use [[Emacs]] and [[Org-mode]] for my writing, or [[Obsidian]]. I think [[Emacs]] would let me do a lot of what I [[imagining|imagine]], but would take longer to get to what I want, and never be as good at it as bespoke software would be, which I can write with a [[Web-based IDE]] like [[Replit]] through the winter, especially if I'm working from a better base of notes. So, instead, I should keep moving Obsidian toward something that gives me [[essay|essays]] I can easily release elsewhere, even if I never release any form of the underlying [[research archive]].

I'm also realizing that at this point, [[Obsidian Copilot]] might be able to help [[coaching|coach]] me on [[remembering]] and [[adhering|sticking with]] that [[decision]], by being able to query across [[my research notes]] all the reasons I've given for using Obsidian, or alternatives.

[[testing|Testing]] that, the answer is yes. Copilot says:

> **Stick with Obsidian** for now, especially as you consolidate and organize your research notes. It gives you:
>
> - A frictionless environment for writing, linking, and publishing.
> - The ability to gradually formalize your knowledge structures.
> - Plugins and AI support to help automate and coach your workflow.
> 
> **Revisit Emacs** if/when you have a clear, compelling need for deeper customization that Obsidian cannot provide, and the time/energy to invest in building that system.
> **Continue experimenting** with semantic markup (like HashUp) as a side project, but don’t let it block your main writing and research work.
> 
> **Summary:**  
> Obsidian is your best tool for productive, flexible, and publishable research note development right now. Use it to build your base of knowledge and writing. Let your system evolve as your needs and capacities change.

I like the mention of [[HashUp]] there, which is an [[imagining|imagined]] [[text markup language]] I had come up with as a way of handling the type of [[hyperlinking]] I find myself doing most frequently. (See [[HashUp, Semantic Plaintext Markup]] for the [[emsenn's Letters to the Web|Letter]] where I announced the idea.)
