Fred Moten (b. 1962) is an American poet and critical theorist whose work develops a sustained engagement with the Black radical tradition, fugitive thought, jazz, the relation between aesthetics and politics, and the conditions of Black study. He teaches at NYU's Tisch School of the Arts. His collaborations with Stefano Harney — especially *The Undercommons* (2013) — have been some of the most generative interventions in contemporary critical theory and university studies.

## Core ideas

- **The undercommons.** With Stefano Harney: a maroon space within and against the university, where the work of subversive intellectual labor happens in the cracks of the institutional apparatus. The undercommons is not a place to colonize the university but a site of fugitive practice — study without institutional credentialing, scholarship without disciplinary capture, intellectual life that refuses the apparatus's ratification.
- **Black study.** Distinguished from Black studies (the institutional formation): black study is the ongoing intellectual-political-aesthetic practice of the Black radical tradition. Study as a form of life, prior to and continuing alongside any institutional credentialing.
- **Fugitivity.** A mode of being that refuses the institutional capture-and-recognition that forms of resistance within institutions presuppose. Drawn from the historical practice of fugitive enslaved persons; extended into a general analytical category for thought and practice that cannot be brought back into the institutional grammar.
- **The break.** The space-time of Black aesthetic-political production where the dominant categories break down and other modes of being become available. The break is jazz's improvisatory moment, the slave song's moment of refusal, the underground railroad's moment of departure.
- **Sentimental disposition.** The feeling-mode — sociality, generosity, refusal of the proper — that characterizes the Black radical tradition's aesthetic-political-existential practice. Distinguished from the sentimentality the dominant culture markets.

## Where his work figures in this library

Moten is foundational for the [black-radical-tradition](../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/critical-theory/theories/black-radical-tradition/_index.md) subdomain and is upstream of cybernetic-postliberal critiques of [industrial-intellectualism](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/industrial-intellectualism.md) — Moten and Harney's undercommons names what is structurally lost when intellectual labor becomes industrially organized for the academic-corporate apparatus.

Fred Moten (born 1962) is an American poet, scholar, and critical theorist whose work on Blackness, aesthetics, and fugitivity has reshaped the fields of Black studies, performance theory, and political philosophy. A professor at New York University, Moten's writing moves between [poetry](../../../../../services/information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/writing/disciplines/poetry/index.md), philosophy, and improvisation, enacting the forms of thought it describes.

- **The [undercommons](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/black-radical-tradition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/undercommons.md)**: developed with [Stefano Harney](../../../../../services/information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/stefano-harney.md) in *The Undercommons: Fugitive Planning and Black Study* (2013), the [undercommons](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/black-radical-tradition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/undercommons.md) names the spaces of study, care, and collective life that persist within and against institutional structures. It is not a place but a condition — inhabited through [refusal](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/services/strategy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/refusal.md) of institutional [legibility](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/abolition-services-section/services/state-repression-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/legibility.md), through fugitive relation, through the ongoing practice of being together in ways the institution cannot capture.
- **Study**: for Moten, study is not scholarship but the ongoing, informal, collective practice of thinking-together that happens in the breaks — in the moments and spaces not captured by institutional accounting. Study is what the university tries to contain and what the [undercommons](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/black-radical-tradition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/undercommons.md) practices without permission.
- **Fugitivity**: not escape but the ongoing practice of being uncaptured — existing in ways that resist the categories ([property](../../../../../services/information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/sociology/terms/property.md), person, citizen, student) through which [the state](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/the-state.md) and the institution manage life.
- **The Black radical tradition**: Moten's work locates Blackness not as identity but as a position — the position of those who have been constituted as [property](../../../../../services/information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/sociology/terms/property.md) and who, from that position, generate forms of sociality, beauty, and thought that exceed every attempt at containment.

Moten and [Harney](../../../../../services/information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/stefano-harney.md)'s [undercommons](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/black-radical-tradition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/undercommons.md) appears in emsenn's "The theory that survived the war" and "Citing for containment" as the condition that disappears when it is made legible — the forest defense was the [undercommons](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/black-radical-tradition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/undercommons.md) when people refused to render it, and ceased to be the moment it became a case study. Their concept of study names the kind of embedded, relational knowledge-making that [opacity](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/opacity.md) protects and that institutional [legibility](../../../../../services/philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/abolition-services-section/services/state-repression-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/legibility.md) destroys.
