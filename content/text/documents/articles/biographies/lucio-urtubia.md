Lucio Urtubia (1931–2020) was a Spanish anarchist born in Cascante, Navarre, who deserted from military service and crossed into France in 1954. He lived in Paris as a bricklayer, a trade he kept all his life. He met the guerrilla Quico Sabaté in Paris and joined the exiled CNT and the FIJL (Iberian Federation of Libertarian Youth), engaging in bank robberies ("expropriations") in the 1950s and 1960s to fund the exiled movement and forging identity documents and passports for anarchist exiles and Latin American movements.

## Core ideas

Urtubia stated a practice of [expropriation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expropriation.md) structured around institutional targets and [solidarity](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/solidarity.md) networks. His core principles: the bank, not the person, is the target; a forger's skill is a form of labour that the movement owns; a worker keeps his trade so that the cause never becomes his livelihood; the negotiation with the issuer is the expropriation's completion — when the counterfeit is good enough, the issuer pays to make it stop.

These principles were tested at scale in the Citibank affair. Between about 1977 and 1980 he had counterfeit Citibank travellers' cheques printed and distributed through political networks in large quantities. The total face value reached tens of millions of dollars by the accounts of the time. He was arrested in 1980. Citibank, whose travellers'-cheque business had been hurt, negotiated: in exchange for the printing plates the charges were reduced and he served a short sentence, about six months by his own account (unverified here). Proceeds went to anarchist groups and Latin American armed movements.

## Later life

After his release, Urtubia founded the Espace Louise Michel, a cultural centre in Paris that became a hub for anarchist activity, education, and [solidarity](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/solidarity.md).

## Where his work figures in this library

Urtubia completes the lineage from [Clément Duval](./clement-duval.md)'s doctrine of individual recovery through [Alexandre Marius Jacob](./alexandre-marius-jacob.md)'s organized targeting to a practice where the issuer settles with the counterfeiter, converting what would be theft into a form of institutional negotiation. His theory that skill belongs to the movement (not to the skilled person) and that the worker keeps his ordinary livelihood ensures that the expropriation never calcifies into a career — the bricklayer remains a bricklayer. His practice of building [solidarity](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/solidarity.md) networks across movements (Spanish exiles, Latin American armed struggle) and geographies grounds the library's understanding of [expropriation](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expropriation.md) as action toward a shared world. His founding of Espace Louise Michel demonstrates the principle that [direct action](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/direct-action.md) in service of the cause can end in a durable institution. He belongs beside [Buenaventura Durruti](./buenaventura-durruti.md) as the later Spanish exile practice of the expropriation Los Solidarios began.
