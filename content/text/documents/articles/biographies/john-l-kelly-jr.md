John Larry Kelly Jr. (1923-1965) was an American scientist at Bell Labs who worked at the intersection of information theory and speech technology. In 1956 he published "A New Interpretation of Information Rate" in the *Bell System Technical Journal*, showing that Claude Shannon's channel-capacity theorem could be reinterpreted as an optimal betting and investment rule — the result now known as the Kelly criterion. In 1961 he led work on computer speech synthesis at Bell Labs, producing a vocoder rendition of "Daisy Bell" that is among the earliest instances of a computer singing.

## Core ideas

- **Information rate as optimal growth rate.** Kelly showed that Shannon's channel capacity — the maximum rate at which information can be reliably transmitted over a noisy channel — has a second interpretation: for a gambler with private information (a "side information" channel) about the outcome of repeated bets, the channel capacity gives the maximum achievable exponential growth rate of wealth, attained by betting a fraction of one's bankroll proportional to one's edge.
- **The Kelly criterion.** The specific betting/investment rule derived from this reinterpretation: wager a fraction of available capital equal to one's edge divided by the odds, which maximizes the long-run, time-average growth rate of wealth rather than its single-period expected value.
- **Speech synthesis at Bell Labs.** Kelly's 1961 vocoder synthesis of "Daisy Bell" ("Bicycle Built for Two"), produced with colleagues including Carol Lochbaum and with musical accompaniment programmed by Max Mathews, demonstrated computer-generated singing speech and became a landmark early artifact of computer music and speech synthesis.

## Key works

- "A New Interpretation of Information Rate" (*Bell System Technical Journal* 35, 1956)
- Vocoder synthesis of "Daisy Bell" (Bell Labs, 1961)
