Boaventura de Sousa Santos (b. 1940) is a Portuguese sociologist of law who holds appointments at the University of Coimbra and the University of Wisconsin-Madison. His work develops a critical sociology of global knowledge production that names and contests the epistemic hierarchies through which Northern, Western academic knowledge has claimed universal validity while marginalizing or destroying other ways of knowing.

## Core ideas

- **Abyssal thinking.** Modern Western thought is organized by a foundational abyssal line dividing this side of the line — where knowledge, law, and being are recognized as valid — from the other side of the line, occupied by colonized and subaltern populations, where the same categories are treated as absent, nonexistent, or irrelevant.
- **Epistemicide.** The destruction of non-Western and subaltern knowledge systems as a structural accompaniment to colonial and capitalist expansion — the epistemological analogue of genocide, naming the loss of entire ways of knowing rather than of individual claims or texts.
- **Epistemologies of the South.** A counter-hegemonic epistemological project that takes seriously the knowledges produced by those who have systematically suffered injustice and oppression under global capitalism and colonialism, treating them as valid sources of theory rather than objects of study alone.
- **The ecology of knowledges.** An alternative to the monoculture of scientific knowledge: a framework in which scientific and non-scientific, Western and non-Western knowledges are recognized as coexisting, contending, and potentially complementary, rather than the latter being absorbed into or subordinated to the former.
- **The sociology of absences and emergences.** A method pairing two operations — the sociology of absences, which recovers social experiences rendered nonexistent by dominant rationality, and the sociology of emergences, which expands and amplifies the not-yet-realized possibilities latent within those recovered experiences.

## Key works

- *Toward a New Legal Common Sense* (1995; 2nd ed. 2002)
- *Epistemologies of the South: Justice Against Epistemicide* (2014)
- *The End of the Cognitive Empire: The Coming of Age of Epistemologies of the South* (2018)
