Max Stirner was the pen name of Johann Kaspar Schmidt (1806-1856), a Berlin-based German philosopher and member of the Young Hegelians, the radical intellectual circle around Bruno Bauer known as the "Free Ones." He taught at a girls' school while developing his philosophical work and died in poverty in Berlin in 1856. His single major work, *Der Einzige und sein Eigentum* (printed late 1844, dated 1845), was attacked at length by Marx and Engels in *The German Ideology* (written 1845-46, first published 1932). He also translated economics texts — Jean-Baptiste Say and Adam Smith — into German in the late 1840s to support himself.

## Core ideas

- **The spook (Spuk).** Abstractions like the State, Humanity, Law, Morality, Religion, and Property-as-right are ruling ideas that dominate those who serve them. These fixed ideas grip the mind and shape behavior while claiming objectivity and inevitability. Recognizing an idea as a spook is the first step to refusing its rule.
- **Ownness (Eigenheit).** Distinct from political freedom as a grant or right, ownness means belonging to oneself — being one's own. Ownness is not something given but something seized and held as power. It describes the individual not asking permission to exist or act but taking responsibility for their own existence as an irreducible fact.
- **Property as power, not right.** Property is not a sacred right protected by the state; rather, it is what one holds and uses through one's own power. The state's protection of property is the state's violence made lawful, while the individual's seizure of goods is branded as crime. This asymmetry exposes the state's claim to neutrality as false.
- **The union of egoists (Verein von Egoisten).** A voluntary association existing only as long as it serves each member and dissolved when it does not. The union is opposed to society and the state, which claim permanent authority and demand sacrificial loyalty regardless of individual benefit. Each member remains sovereign.
- **Insurrection versus revolution.** Stirner distinguished the rising up of the individual out of the existing order as an act of will (insurrection) from the overthrow of the order to install another (revolution). Insurrection does not aim to build a better society but to recover one's own existence within it.

## Key works

- *Der Einzige und sein Eigentum* (1844)
- *Stirner's Critics* (1845), a reply to Feuerbach, Szeliga, and Hess
- Translations of Jean-Baptiste Say and Adam Smith (late 1840s)

## Where their work figures in this library

Stirner is the founding figure of individualist anarchism and holds a constitutive place in the tradition of [illegalism](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/illegalism.md), which reads [property](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/property.md) through his understanding of it as power rather than right. The [union of egoists](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/voluntary-association.md) mirrors the shape of both [informal organization](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/informal-organization.md) and the [affinity group](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/affinity-group.md) — associations that persist by consent and dissolve without it. His distinction between insurrection and revolution inflects the library's engagement with [direct action](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/direct-action.md). He stands as a standing counterweight within the anarchist tradition to [Kropotkin's](./pyotr-kropotkin.md) emphasis on [mutual aid](../../../../../services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/mutual-aid.md) and [Proudhon's](./pierre-joseph-proudhon.md) mutualism — an association valid only while it serves each party rather than one grounded in a duty of reciprocity. This is a contrast the library holds rather than a verdict between them.
