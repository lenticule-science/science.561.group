# The same thing, otherwise

A document survives being renamed. It can survive a move between computers, a change in file format, and the replacement of every byte in a carefully recorded revision. Yet a newly generated document can contain exactly the same bytes as an original and still not be the original document. One has a history of authorship, correction, obligations, permissions, and reception that the other may lack.

Which one is the same thing?

The answer changes with the question. A checksum may treat two byte-identical documents as interchangeable. A bibliographer must distinguish their histories. A reader interested only in the text might have no reason to distinguish them at all. Someone authorized to revise the original cannot assume that the same authority extends to a copy.

The differences are not mysterious properties hiding inside the files. They are relations: how the documents arose, how they were encountered, what was witnessed, what may be changed, and whose responses count. A complete description of the screen in front of us need not be a complete description of the thing we have encountered.

This is an old question within Lenticule Science, not a new conclusion drawn from a clever example. Our research in **Relationality** proceeds from a stronger position: things do not first acquire identities and then enter into relations. Distinguishable things, and the ways they persist, are formations of relating.

## The problem with taking the object first

It is easy to begin an investigation by naming two objects and asking which relation connects them. That procedure is useful for counting documents, constructing diagrams, and performing ordinary mathematics. It can also hide the question we meant to ask: where did the objects, their positions, and their apparent separateness come from?

Relationality begins with the attempt to exclude relating altogether.

Absolute relationlessness would exclude every difference, dependency, recognition, orientation, and boundary. But to determine that something has been excluded is already to distinguish an excluding from an excluded position. The attempt to make relating absent performs a directed distinction that defeats its own proposed completion.

The claim is not that saying the word "nothing" creates a universe. Nor is relationlessness an empty container waiting for its first object. The proposed originating difficulty concerns the **act of determination**: what an attempted exclusion must do, and what its performance prevents it from completing.

On this account, relating does not connect independently finished things. It generates positions that may subsequently be named, compared, identified, and revised. An entity is an articulated formation within that activity. Its persistence has to be explained through the relations that can continue, not through an unexamined substance underneath them.

This is the philosophical commitment. Deriving all of its proposed mathematical consequences is a separate and unfinished scientific program.

## A picture can be equal while the encounter is not

Suppose two histories, h₁ and h₂, give an observer the same presentation under an admitted observation P. In illustrative notation:

**P(h₁) = P(h₂).**

That equality tells us something real about the observation. It does not establish that the histories are identical, nor that every subsequent question will treat them alike.

Consider a research record that was never entered and one that was entered and later withdrawn. A particular database view can show an empty field in both cases. Yet an authorized auditor may be able to examine a withdrawal witness in the second and none in the first. An unavailable witness could also mean the auditor lacks access, rather than that no witness exists.

One projection has identified several situations that a richer encounter can distinguish.

The important qualifier is **admitted**. Not every conceivable test is available, lawful, meaningful, or even well formed. What can be asked depends on actual observers, capabilities, evidence, purposes, and obligations. A relation that matters to a source custodian may be invisible to a public reader without being unreal.

We can therefore distinguish two questions that are routinely collapsed.

*Observational equivalence* asks whether specified tests presently distinguish two formations. *Continuity of identity* asks what makes one formation a warranted continuation of another across change. The first depends on a declared observation; the second requires an account of generation, retained witnesses, transformation, and admissible further relations.

Different histories can have the same available next observations. Likewise, the fact that we can imagine two different future responses does not itself prove two present identities. The research cannot settle numerical identity by inventing an all-seeing observer or an unrestricted set of counterfactual experiments.

The narrower and useful result is this: **equality under a projection never, by itself, licenses claims about distinctions that projection does not preserve.**

## Persistence is not immobility

A living formation can remain itself while its material, relationships, descriptions, and available actions change. There need not be one property left untouched by every transformation. But neither does calling something a "process" tell us which transformations preserve it.

Return to the document. Its path can change without interrupting its custody. Its bytes can change under an authorized correction. A copy with identical bytes might instead begin another custody history. The continuity in question is not just a matching string, and not a magical identifier that declares itself true. It involves the witnessed relations by which one state follows another.

This does not mean every relation must survive. Some must be allowed to end. A revoked permission must not persist simply because a system wishes to preserve continuity; an obsolete scientific claim must remain correctable; an institution must be able to recognize a refusal and a departure.

Persistence, in this account, is stability **under admitted changes**, not permanent retention of every feature. What matters is which relations are preserved, which are legitimately transformed, which are lost, and what those differences make possible or obligatory next.

That formulation puts an immediate limit on any attempt to use Relationality to classify people. No database's finite tests exhaust a person's relationships, capacities, or rights. A machine's observational equivalence is not a warrant to substitute one person for another or to disregard how either can accept, contest, or refuse an encounter.

## Attending: how a determination can be completed without ending relation

The current Relationality program names **Attending** as the composite act of *articulating, predicating, positing, and settling*. Its **Turn** is the connected family of codomains produced through those stages, not another name for the composite itself.

Articulating retains an oriented distinction and its possibility of continuation. Predicating forms a directed claim position. Positing admits a value or evidence under stated conditions. Settling completes the consequences warranted by an admitted rule.

An instance can settle locally while leaving material for further Attending. A correction, a retained conflicting witness, or an unabsorbed obstruction can become the subject of another act. The return is one of **method with changed content**, not necessarily a loop back to the same state.

That distinction matters mathematically. On one admitted four-valued charge fiber, the settling step can be modeled as an idempotent closure: applying that closure again does not change its fixed result. It does **not** follow that the whole four-stage, changing-carrier Attending composite is itself one idempotent map, or that no further Turn is possible.

The existing Obsmetrologia work reports formal laws for such bounded settlement. It also identifies a concrete remaining problem: some current constructions receive their evidence charge and closure rule as inputs. Those are not yet all generated from the originating Mark and its witnessed relations. The stronger transition from unresolved obstruction to a source-derived next act remains an open derivation.

That is an unusually consequential distinction between *a mathematical system that represents the thesis* and *a derivation that earns the thesis from its own proposed beginning*.

## Older conversations, different arguments

Lenticule's earlier survey of relation and identity compared substance metaphysics with accounts in which process, dependence, and individuation have greater explanatory priority. These comparisons remain useful, provided we do not turn them into a single historical endorsement.

Peter Geach's disputed theory of relative identity makes us ask "the same what?" when a reconstructed ship is the same vessel but not the same collection of planks. That helps expose how a criterion of sameness can depend on the domain of inquiry. It does not derive Relationality's originating act.

Gilbert Simondon's individuation asks us to understand the individual through the processes and associated milieu in which it forms. Alfred North Whitehead's process philosophy likewise challenges the picture of permanent substances to which events merely happen. Their systems are not identical to each other or to Obsmetrologia.

Nāgārjuna's analysis of dependent origination and intrinsic nature offers a further, historically distinct challenge to independently self-grounding identities. Its philosophical and Buddhist setting should not be reduced to a slogan about network graphs.

These are different ways of resisting an easy answer to the same difficulty: treating a present description as though it supplied its own conditions of existence. Relationality's contribution must be judged by what it can derive, discriminate, and explain beyond the resemblance of its vocabulary to theirs.

## A practical way to test an identity claim

When two things seem indistinguishable, ask what admitted encounter could separate their histories or continuations. When two presentations look radically different, ask what witnessed transformation permits one to continue the other.

This is the useful **counterfeit test**: construct or find an apparent replacement that satisfies the chosen visible criteria but fails a consequential relation that the original actually bears.

For a document, the missing relation could be custody or authority to change it. For an automated agent, it could be the continuing obligation to a particular receiver rather than an identical model checkpoint. For an experiment, it could be a calibration or source-history witness absent from an otherwise matching graph. For an institution, it could be whether the people affected can really withdraw consent or receive the promised result.

A good test also admits its own failure. Two distinct source histories can legitimately be interchangeable for a bounded purpose. Some differences cannot be observed with the currently authorized instruments. A supposed residual may produce only more copies of the same observation, with no demonstrated new relation. Repetition alone does not prove that increasingly complex structure must emerge.

The point is to discover what the classification forgets and whether that loss is consequential—not to declare that every unseen distinction matters equally.

## What follows for a living scientific Web

The same research changes how we think about the systems through which scientific knowledge is shared.

A representation ought not be confused with its source. A corrected page can remain the continuation of an article while changing its claims. Two agents may render the same answer while differing in their permissions and the obligations they have actually undertaken. A public interface can preserve the meaning of a source through different languages, sensory presentations, and participant capabilities without presuming one presentation is the universal default.

Each of those is an engineering or institutional question with specific receiving conditions; none is settled by metaphysics alone. But they share a scientific demand: preserve the distinctions necessary to reconstruct **why this result**, **from this source**, **under these conditions**, can lawfully continue.

Relationality therefore changes the question we ask of identity. Not merely "What features does this object possess?" and not "What immutable thing remains underneath change?" We ask which performed relations make the formation distinguishable, which transformations it can undergo without losing its warranted continuity, and what further Attending those transformations permit.

**Something can change almost beyond recognition and continue. Something can look exactly the same and have a different history. The work is to determine which difference matters, for whom, under what evidence, and why.**
