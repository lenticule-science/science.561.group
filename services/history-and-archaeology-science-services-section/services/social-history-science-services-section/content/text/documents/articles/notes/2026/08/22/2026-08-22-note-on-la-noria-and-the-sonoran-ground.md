# La Noria and the Sonoran ground

Research pass 2026-08-22, Sonoran round, stream two. Verification statuses preserved: confirmed (direct read) / reported (single-source) / gap.

## La Noria

The settlement facing Lochiel: a locality of the Municipio de Santa Cruz, Sonora, population on the order of **5** (single demographic aggregator — indicative only), directly opposite the abandoned U.S. port. The **Garita La Noria operated limited hours (a posted sign dated June 1978 gave 10–4 daily) and closed in 1984** for lack of traffic — one to five vehicles a day — documented in El Imparcial's 2021 photo essay "queda en el olvido" (confirmed, direct read). Note the near-symmetry with the U.S. side's 1983 closure: **the two governments abandoned this crossing within about a year of each other**, four decades before one of them spent $309M to wall it. Beyond this, La Noria has almost no documentary record — no monograph, no INAH community profile — and the corpus records that as a *finding*: the archival silence is itself part of the line's asymmetry, present even within Mexico's own archive.

## Santa Cruz, Sonora

First settled 1637; became a population center in **1775 when the presidio of Santa Cruz de Terrenate was relocated there** under O'Conor's reorganization — the same failed San Pedro presidio from this corpus's Sobaipuri research; the community facing the wall is the direct successor of the presidio whose residents' descendants took the 1825 Zanja grant (the grant-to-presidio-families link is documented on the U.S. side via [H&S 1995]/CPLC records; this pass found no independent Spanish-language confirmation — a no-find, not a contradiction). Municipio status 1931. Population (INEGI 2020, confirmed): **1,835** in the municipio, 846 in the town, down 8.16% from 2010, spread across 1,001 km². Presidenta municipal (2024–27): **Alma Guadalupe Téllez Salomón** (Movimiento Ciudadano). Her engagement with the wall is real but rests on secondary summarization (reported): meetings with Sky Island Alliance, agreement to **host the relocated border wildlife cameras**, concern that construction water harms community and wildlife — sandwiches exchanged across the barbed wire. Rancher sentiment is confirmed by direct read (El Imparcial, May 26, 2026): César Peralta — "Desde que somos niños, los rancheros del lado de Arizona y de Santa Cruz siempre han tenido una relación cordial" — with the double wall ending binational gatherings by design (people kept 10 meters apart).

The Río Santa Cruz per Mexican sources (confirmed): rises in the San Rafael Valley, flows south through Santa Cruz town, bends west near Miguel Hidalgo/San Lázaro, turns northeast to re-enter the U.S. east of Nogales; ~296 km total.

## The binational facts of the Grandmother (Mexican press)

El Imparcial's coverage (confirmed, direct reads) adds facts the U.S.-side record lacks:

- **The tree stands ~18 m north of the boundary, with roughly 60% of its root and canopy structure on Mexican soil** (El Imparcial, Aug 5, 2026 — reported by the paper; not independently surveyed). If accurate, felling her destroys living material substantially located in Mexico — a fact with CILA/La Paz Agreement relevance no §102 waiver reaches, and one the corridor-hold operation should carry forward.
- **Aug 18: Santa Cruz residents formally joined; parallel permanent camps atop a protected álamo on each side of the line** — "un representante de cada país" — 20+ activists, 22 consecutive days by that date.
- **The construction company paused work in the La Noria zone specifically, leaving ~200 m of wall unbuilt pending a decision** (El Imparcial, Aug 18) — the most concrete measure yet of what the hold has materially held.
- Correction to the record: the **Mexican state police detention of two activists** (~Aug 17–19) appears **only in the Democracy Now!/activist source lineage; El Imparcial's same-window coverage does not mention it** — downgraded to single-source-lineage, uncorroborated by Mexican press.

## Rancho Los Fresnos — premise corrected

Los Fresnos is real and important — ~3,900–4,000 ha, bought 2005 by TNC in alliance with **Naturalia A.C.**, Naturalia-managed, CONANP-certified ADVC (2013/14), native grasslands and ciénegas, and the site where **beavers recolonized Mexico after a century's absence**, arriving from Arizona populations (confirmed via Naturalia's own site and El Imparcial 2015). But it lies in the **Upper San Pedro watershed ~35 km NNW of Cananea — not the San Rafael/Santa Cruz headwaters** — same municipio, different valley. The "Mexican mirror of the TNC easement" framing survives institutionally (same TNC partnership logic, same municipio) but not geographically; no equivalent conservation holding was found at the La Noria strip itself. Sonoran-side land tenure at the headwaters strip (ejido vs. private) remains a **gap** requiring Registro Agrario Nacional records; the one named neighbor is Ejido José María Morelos, east of Los Fresnos.

## La zona restringida — Mexico's own border strip

Article 27, fracción I of the 1917 Constitution bars foreigners from **direct ownership within 100 km of any border** (50 km of coasts) — la zona restringida (confirmed across legal sources). Lineage: 1824 colonization law (20 leagues), 1829 expulsions, Santa Anna's 1842 extension, constitutionalized 1917. The mechanism today is the **fideicomiso**: a Mexican bank holds title in trust for the foreign beneficiary, 50-year renewable terms, SRE permit required — **48,559 permits authorized 2000–2012**, so the strip is heavily mediated rather than absolute; a 2013 reform to abolish it passed the Chamber nearly unanimously and stalled in the Senate. The finding the corpus wants: the 1917 constitutional debate record contains **essentially no systematic rationale** for the restriction — a legal-history analysis (AMEDIP) concludes no strategic or economic justification was ever formally established. So the corpus now holds **three border-strip exclusion-powers with three polarities**: the Roosevelt strip (pointed at private entry, now energized for the wall), the Medicine Line (pointed, for four years, at the U.S. Army), and the zona restringida (pointed at foreign ownership) — the third being the purest case of an exclusion-power sustained for a century without a stated reason, running on inherited sentiment alone. Its operation in Sonora's border municipios specifically is a flagged gap.
