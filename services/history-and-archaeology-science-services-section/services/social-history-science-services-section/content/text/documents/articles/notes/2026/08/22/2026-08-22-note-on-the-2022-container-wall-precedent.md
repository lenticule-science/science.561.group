# The 2022 container wall: the winning precedent, from the same valley

Research pass 2026-08-22. This is not merely an analogous fight; it is the *same corridor* (Coronado National Forest / San Rafael Valley, Cochise County — PBS located the container site in "a remote area of San Rafael Valley"), substantially the *same people* (Kate Scott organized the 2022 camp and leads the 2026 Grandmother Camp; Russ McSpadden was CBD's on-site advocate both times; Sheriff Hathaway was sympathetic both times), and the same jaguar-ocelot corridor. Lookout News: the 2022 win was "partial inspiration for the tree sit"; CrimethInc's 2026 dispatch names the continuity explicitly.

## What happened

- Aug 2022: Gov. Ducey orders DEMA to plug border-wall gaps with shipping containers — first Yuma (130 containers, ~0.7 mi, ~$6M, contractor AshBritt, no-bid), then from Oct 26 the Coronado NF plan: up to 3,000 containers over 10 miles, ~$95M projected. USFS repeatedly notified the state in writing it was unauthorized. Only ~4–4.25 miles were built.
- Nov 20 – Dec 7: "Camp Ocelot" — a camp on the border access road. Named participants (all from public reporting): Kate Scott (MAWC), Michael and Christie Brown, Jennifer Wrenn and Andy Kayner, Russ McSpadden and Ethan Bonnin (CBD). Tactics: holding the road daytime, adding overnight shifts when contractors tried night placement, bodies in front of machinery. About two weeks of active blockade; no container placed after Dec 7–8.
- Dec 14, 2022: DOJ sued (*United States v. Arizona*, D. Ariz.) — as **landowner**: trespass on the Roosevelt Reservation strip, Forest Service land, and Reclamation/Cocopah land, seeking removal. Ducey had preemptively sued in October, arguing the 1907 Roosevelt proclamation was *unconstitutional* and invoking an Article I §10 "invasion" theory — never resolved.
- Within a week: stipulated settlement; halt and removal (Yuma by Jan 3, 2023; Coronado substantially complete by mid-2023); both suits dismissed Sept 15, 2023 after Arizona paid $2.1M extra for restoration.
- Cost of the episode to Arizona: ~$175M total (build + removal + restoration) — over $47M per built mile — recouping under $4M at auction. Lookout's line: "the first international barrier to go down since the fall of the Berlin Wall."

## The mechanism, stated plainly

The 2022 fight was won on **property, not policy**. The blockade's function was to buy roughly two weeks of delay, cost, and attention; the kill was an ordinary trespass claim by a landowner with clean title — the federal government — against a builder without one. The court never reached the constitutional questions; trespass sufficed, and it resolved in days, arriving as Ducey's term expired (a natural off-ramp).

## The 2026 inversion — and the counter-move

In 2026 the builder *is* the federal government, waiving its own review statutes on land it presumptively controls. The party that held the property card in 2022 is now the one building; the Roosevelt Reservation, the government's sword against Ducey, is inert in federal hands against federal construction. No cavalry rides in by default — physical presence and slower channels (waiver-scope litigation, contractor cost pressure, oversight, embarrassment) must carry more of the load.

**Unless the 2022 posture can be rebuilt with a different landowner.** That is exactly what the Roosevelt-scope memo (lochiel-corridor-hold-services) attempts: if the corridor at Lochiel crosses land the federal government does *not* hold — the San Rafael Ranch State Natural Area (State of Arizona), The Nature Conservancy's easement, or private parcels at the townsite, on former grant land patented before the 1907 proclamation — then a property-holding plaintiff exists again, and 2022 demonstrates precisely what such a plaintiff's trespass claim does to a wall on a schedule. The pending title research (1899 plat, patent, county parcels) determines whether that plaintiff exists. The 2022 precedent also supplies the cost arithmetic for the political argument: Arizona's misadventure on this same ground cost $175 million and ended in removal.

One further 2022 artifact worth holding: **Ducey's own filed position that the Roosevelt Reservation proclamation is unconstitutional** — a state-of-Arizona legal theory, on the record, against the very instrument the 2026 federal ruling leaned on. Never adjudicated; available to whoever finds it useful.
