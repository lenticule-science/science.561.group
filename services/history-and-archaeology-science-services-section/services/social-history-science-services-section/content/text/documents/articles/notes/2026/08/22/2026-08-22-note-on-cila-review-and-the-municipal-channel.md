# CILA review and the municipal channel

Research pass 2026-08-22, fourth stream of the Sonoran round. Task: pin the actual
procedure by which a Mexican ayuntamiento or citizen requests CILA (sección
mexicana) review of works on the boundary, verify the treaty jurisdiction language,
and check four load-bearing claims carried elsewhere in this evidence layer.
Verification statuses — primary-verified / attested-secondary / unpinned — as
elsewhere in this series.

## The jurisdiction, from CILA's own manual (primary-verified)

CILA's own site (cila.sre.gob.mx) is fully bot-walled to automated retrieval —
confirmed again this pass; every page tried returned a Radware bot-check redirect.
The primary document that got through is the SRE's own **Manual de Organización de
la Sección Mexicana de la CILA** (código CILAMEUA-B00, v4.0, 01/07/2006,
sre.gob.mx/images/stories/docnormateca/manadmin/cila_nte.pdf), a 59-page
internal-structure document that quotes the governing treaty articles verbatim.
Primary-verified, direct text pull (pdftotext) this pass.

**1944 Water Treaty, Article 24(b)** (quoted in the manual, p.22): "Cada Sección
tendrá jurisdicción sobre las obras construidas exclusivamente en el territorio de
su país" — each Section holds jurisdiction over works built exclusively within its
own country, within the scope needed to fulfill the treaty. This is the general
jurisdictional grant. Article 2's specific "land boundary line... and works
constructed... on [it]" language — the phrase this evidence layer has been leaning
on — turned up again this pass via search of CILA's institutional-history page, but
that live page is itself bot-walled, so the quote remains **attested-secondary**
until pulled from a primary treaty text (a candidate PDF, faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/
bi-51880.pdf, was located but not yet fetched and verified).

**1970 Boundary Treaty, Article IV** (quoted in the manual, pp.23–24,
primary-verified verbatim):

> "Con el fin de reducir a un mínimo los desalojamientos de los cauces del Río
> Bravo y del Río Colorado, en sus tramos limítrofes, y los problemas que
> originaría la segregación de porciones de territorio, los Estados contratantes
> convienen en que: B.1) Tanto en el cauce principal del río como en las tierras
> adyacentes, hasta una distancia a cada lado del límite internacional que
> recomiende la Comisión y aprueben los dos Gobiernos, cada Estado contratante
> deberá prohibir la construcción de obras en su territorio que, a juicio de la
> Comisión, puedan causar desviación u obstrucción de la corriente normal del río
> o de sus avenidas. 2) Si la Comisión llegara a determinar que cualquiera de las
> obras construidas... causa tales efectos adversos en el territorio del otro
> Estado contratante, el Gobierno del Estado contratante que haya construido tal
> obra deberá removerla o modificarla y, según acuerdo de la Comisión, deberá
> reparar o compensar los daños."

**Correction to flag back to the walls-versus-water note**: Article IV's text
names *el Río Bravo y el Río Colorado* specifically — not "international rivers"
generally. Read strictly, its removal/modification remedy is textually anchored to
those two rivers, not the Santa Cruz. This narrows that note's framing of Article
IV as a general floodplain rule.

But CILA's own manual does not confine its stated functions to that narrow
reading. The **Dirección de Ingeniería**'s function (p.30, primary-verified) is to
"asegurar que las obras que cada país construye dentro de la zona de inundación de
los ríos internacionales no provoquen obstrucción o desviación del escurrimiento
normal o de avenidas de los ríos internacionales, revisando y dictaminando sobre
su diseño y proceso de construcción" — *ríos internacionales*, unqualified,
plural, general. And the **Subdirección de Ingeniería**'s stated objective (pp.31–
32, primary-verified, restated twice) is to "determinar si se lesiona la
integridad territorial y demás intereses nacionales en materia de límites, con
cualquier obra construida o que se proyecte construir sobre la línea divisoria
internacional... (LDI), o en la zona fronteriza adyacente" — any work built or
planned on the LDI or in the adjacent border zone, full stop, no river-name
qualifier. This is CILA's own definition of its engineering mandate, and it
reaches the Santa Cruz crossings and the wall corridor generally on its face — the
institutional practice is broader than Article IV read narrowly. The stronger
textual hook for a Santa Cruz petition is therefore the Subdirección de
Ingeniería's LDI/zona-fronteriza-adyacente mandate and Article 2's general
jurisdiction grant (attested-secondary, pending primary pull) — not Article IV.

The manual also quotes 1889 Convention Article VIII (pp.20–21, primary-verified):
when the two Commissioners disagree, "cada Comisionado formulará un dictamen por
escrito que presentará a su respectivo Gobierno." This is the origin of the
*dictamen* mechanism: a written technical opinion each Commissioner files with
their own government when the Commission cannot agree internally, then resolved
diplomatically. On this text it is an inter-governmental instrument, not a form a
municipality fills out.

## How dictámenes actually get requested — what the record shows, and doesn't

No provision in the 59-page manual, and nothing found on any live CILA page,
describes a citizen- or municipality-initiated request procedure for a dictamen
técnico. What the manual does establish (confirmed as CILA practice, though not a
named formal trámite):

- CILA is bound by Mexico's federal transparency law — the manual cites the **Ley
  Federal de Transparencia y Acceso a la Información Pública Gubernamental** and
  its reglamento as governing instruments (p.12, primary-verified), and CILA's own
  mission statement commits to "participación ciudadana en un marco de
  transparencia" (p.24, primary-verified). CILA is listed as a **sujeto obligado**
  on SRE's Sistema de Portales de Obligaciones de Transparencia, SIPOT
  (attested-secondary, sre.gob.mx/transparencia). **This is the real, working
  channel**: a solicitud de información via the **Plataforma Nacional de
  Transparencia** (plataformadetransparencia.org.mx), addressed to the Secretaría
  de Relaciones Exteriores / Comisión Internacional de Límites y Aguas as sujeto
  obligado, does not depend on CILA's own bot-walled web portal and carries a
  legally mandated response obligation. This is the Mexican-side mirror of the
  FOIA lever the corridor-hold operation already uses on the U.S. side.
- CILA's disaster-coordination function names an explicit **municipal-liaison
  precedent**: "Coordinar con autoridades federales, estatales y municipales de
  ambos países a través de Mecanismos de Enlace Fronterizo de Cd. Acuña y Piedras
  Negras" (p.50, primary-verified) — a standing binational municipal-liaison
  mechanism CILA already operates, at that specific city pair. No equivalent named
  mechanism was found for Nogales/Santa Cruz — a gap, not a contradiction; it
  shows the institutional form exists elsewhere on the border and could be asked
  for here.
- The Subdirección de Ingeniería's function list includes "participar, en
  coordinación con las dependencias federales, estatales y **municipales**
  competentes, en los estudios y medidas... que afecten la línea divisoria
  internacional terrestre" (p.32, primary-verified) — municipalities are a named
  counterpart category in LDI-affecting matters generally, though not in a
  citizen-request procedure specifically.

No dedicated "Oficina de Atención Ciudadana," complaint hotline, or online trámite
for citizen-initiated dictamen requests was located for CILA specifically, distinct
from the general transparency-request channel above. Stated as a search gap, not a
proven non-existence — the live site could not be crawled to check exhaustively.

## Four claims checked against the evidence layer

**Sky Island Alliance, June 2026, dirt-road dam on the Santa Cruz** — confirmed as
reported. The source page (skyislandalliance.org/2026/06/video-feds-dam-santa-
cruz-river-ahead-of-border-wall-construction/) again returned 403 to direct
WebFetch, matching the walls-versus-water note's prior finding — but the article's
title and a search-engine content summary both independently describe CBP
"damming the Santa Cruz River with a dirt road" pending the permanent crossing
structure (30-ft wall, floodgates, concrete, bridge), sourced to an SIA video
interview with Wildlife Program Manager Eamon Harrity. Status:
**attested-secondary**, same as before — still needs a direct capture
(screenshot/archive) before use with a principal, since the source itself remains
unreachable by automated fetch.

**USGS 09480000, flood of record 12,000 cfs** — **primary-verified**, upgraded
from the prior pass. A direct pull of the gauge's peak-streamflow RDB data
confirms: **1977-10-09, 12,000 cfs**; **1984-08-15, 12,000 cfs** — tied flood of
record, exactly as the walls-versus-water note states. Also confirmed in the same
pull: **2022-08-21, 5,820 cfs**, consistent with that note's "4,800–5,800 cfs"
range for the recent-events list. (Caution for future passes: an earlier, less
careful fetch of the same page mis-summarized a 2022-10-08 entry as "17,000 cfs" —
the raw data actually shows 2022-10-08 at **67.3 cfs**. The 12,000 cfs
flood-of-record figure is solid; a stray AI-summarized figure from this same gauge
should not be trusted without a raw-data cross-check, as demonstrated here.)

**Fisher Sand & Gravel, 2022 IBWC settlement** — confirmed as stated. IBWC's April
20, 2020 letter to Fisher Industries found **10.32% deflection at one location**,
"well in excess of the threshold limit of +5%" (KRGV, attested-secondary, matches
the walls-versus-water note). The May 31, 2022 settlement terms — quarterly
inspections, an existing floodgate maintained, a **$3 million bond held 15
years**, and repair of bollards leaning more than 6° out of alignment — are
independently attested across ENR and BorderReport coverage (attested-secondary,
consistent with the existing note; the settlement document itself was not pulled
this pass).

**The Grandmother cottonwood, ~60% of roots/canopy on Mexican soil, El Imparcial
Aug 5 2026** — **could not be confirmed, and the underlying geometry may be
misstated.** I fetched El Imparcial's August 5, 2026 article twice directly (the
standard URL and its AMP variant) — it is the correct article: the 200+-year
cottonwood, the July 27 felling of three neighbors, the tree-sit. Neither fetch
contains a percentage figure for roots or canopy on either side of the line; the
article states only that the tree's trunk stands on federal land within the
~18-meter-wide Roosevelt Reservation strip (that 18 meters is the strip's width,
not — as the prior note's phrasing risks implying — the tree's distance from the
boundary). Separately, English-language reporting (Tucson.com, via search)
describes what appears to be a **second, distinct tree** at the parallel
Mexican-side camp — "located mostly in Mexico, though its roots and part of its
canopy almost certainly extend into the U.S." — the opposite direction from the
note's claim, and probably a different álamo than the one the July/August tree-sit
defended (the la-noria note's Aug 18 item, "parallel permanent camps atop a
protected álamo on each side of the line," independently confirms there are two
separate trees in play by mid-August, one per side). **Recommendation: downgrade
this claim to unpinned pending a direct pin to whichever specific El Imparcial
sentence or article actually carries the 60% figure, and clarify which of the two
trees (US-side Grandmother vs. Mexican-side companion álamo) any cross-border-
geometry claim refers to before it goes into a dossier.** A felling-destroys-
Mexican-soil argument is only as strong as the specific tree and figure it's
pinned to.

## What this adds to the operation

The CILA hold-window is a request the ayuntamiento or the Consejo could make right
now, through a channel that does not depend on CILA's own bot-walled site: a
Plataforma Nacional de Transparencia solicitud to SRE/CILA asking (a) whether the
Santa Cruz crossings or the wall corridor generally were ever submitted for
Subdirección de Ingeniería review under its LDI/zona-fronteriza-adyacente mandate,
and (b) whether any dictamen exists. The Nogales field office (Sonoyta–Agua Prieta
jurisdiction, Ing. Jesús Quintanar) is the geographically correct in-person or
written point of contact, pending confirmation of its phone/email. Offer these
facts to Santa Cruz ayuntamiento counsel and to whoever advises the Consejo
Supremo de Ancianos; the choice of whether and how to file is theirs.
