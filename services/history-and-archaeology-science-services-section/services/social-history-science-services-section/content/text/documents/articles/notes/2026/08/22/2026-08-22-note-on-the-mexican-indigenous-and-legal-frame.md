# The Mexican Indigenous and legal frame

Research pass 2026-08-22, Sonoran round, stream three. Statuses: confirmed / reported / not-found (stated as absence-in-search, never as proven negatives).

## O'odham in Sonora

The Tohono O'odham/Pápago in Mexico center on **Quitovac** (municipio General Plutarco Elías Calles), with presence at Las Norias, Pozo Verde (ejido, decreed 1928 under Calles — 2,823 ha), Pozo Prieto, San Francisquito, Sonoyta, Caborca, and Puerto Peñasco; the combined ejido grants represent **under 15% of the historic Pápago territorial base** (confirmed via INPI and UNISON thesis sourcing). INEGI 2020 records **169 O'otam speakers in Sonora** (203 nationwide) — INALI classifies the language as at very high risk of disappearing; these are speaker counts, undercounting ethnic affiliation, but the order of magnitude beside the U.S. side's 34,000 enrolled is itself a finding about which side of the line the category was allowed to survive on. Recognition friction is documented: INPI "names and recognizes [them], but does not take into account our voice" (community member, Once Noticias). The Mexican-side traditional authority is the **Consejo Supremo de Ancianos del Pueblo Tohono O'odham** (spokesperson/translator Matías Valenzuela), on record protesting the CFE's Plan Sonora solar megaproject for negotiating with an Arizona-side governor while bypassing the Council — the named body any wall-related Mexican-side consultation should have gone through, and evidently did not. The **Vikita** rain ceremony at Quitovac continues (INAH journal documentation); its month is inconsistently reported (June vs. July full moon — unresolved, do not cite a date). Documented O'odham action on the wall from the Sonoran side: the **November 2025 "La Bota" protest** at Santa Cruz — O'odham members dancing and singing at the wall line alongside Sierra Club activists and Sonoran residents, objecting to wildlife corridors severed and "freedom of transit of ancestral peoples" restricted; no Mexican officials present. No O'odham-Sonora claim through Mexican legal channels was found — the documented strategies are U.S. litigation and cross-border civil presence.

## Yoeme (Yaqui)

The Ocho Pueblos (est. 1610 under Jesuit missionization): the commonly cited roster is Cócorit, Bácum, Tórim, Vícam, Pótam, Ráhum, Huírivis, Belem — with INPI's ethnography recording that Cócorit and Bácum were lost and **Loma de Guamúchil and Loma de Bácum** re-established the eight (roster substitution needs a named-scholar source to settle; flagged). Cárdenas ratified **485,235 hectares** of exclusive Yaqui territory in 1937. The Porfirian catastrophe — the Guerra del Yaqui, mass deportation to Yucatán henequen haciendas and Valle Nacional, 300+ documented cases of Yaqui women kidnapped along rail routes — is documented above all in the scholarship of **Raquel Padilla Ramos** (INAH Sonora). **Padilla Ramos was murdered November 7, 2019** — stabbed to death at El Sauz, Ures; investigated as femicide; she was a defender of Yaqui territorial rights as well as their historian, and this corpus records her death accurately and cites her work as the field's foundation (confirmed, multiple sources). The Mexican state's reckoning: **AMLO's public apology to the Yaqui pueblos, September 28, 2021**, and the **Plan de Justicia Yaqui** (three axes — land/territory, water rights, wellbeing/culture; announced 11,600.8M pesos; aqueduct for the eight communities) (confirmed, official transcripts). Against which stands the live contradiction: the **Acueducto Independencia** — opposed by the Yaqui, subject of a Supreme Court ruling ordering consultation that **was never completed** — continues operating "irregularly" as of April 2026, moving Río Yaqui water to a Hermosillo adding 7,000–8,000 water hookups a year. Apology as filing, extraction as practice.

## CILA, precisely

The Mexican Section of the boundary commission: seat **Ciudad Juárez**, field offices including **Nogales**, jurisdiction on the land line from Sásabe to Ojinaga. Commissioner since September 2021: **Adriana Reséndez Maldonado** — first woman in the post, 23 years of CILA engineering experience (confirmed, official announcements). The jurisdictional hook the corpus's walls-versus-water note needs, from the **1944 Water Treaty, Article 2**: the Commission "exercises jurisdiction... over the land boundary line between the two countries, and over works constructed in [the rivers] and on [the boundary]" — works *on the boundary*, not only in rivers — plus the 1970 treaty's monument-maintenance mandate and binding technical dictámenes over crossings (confirmed via UNISON academic paper). **No CILA statement on the 2025–26 wall was found — but CILA's own bulletin archive is currently bot-walled and could not be crawled**; its 2025–26 visible output is water/sanitation (Acta 333, Dec 2025). The follow-up is precise: a direct pull of CILA's Boletines de Prensa, and the Mexican-side mirror of FOIA-4 — a request to CILA/SRE for any record of review of the Santa Cruz crossings. Given the 60%-of-roots-in-Mexico fact (La Noria note), CILA's Article 2 jurisdiction over "works on the boundary" is the strongest unexplored Mexican channel.

## La Paz, and the silence

The **La Paz Agreement** (signed Aug 14, 1983; DOF March 1984): environmental cooperation across a **100-km zone each side of the line**, national coordinators (EPA; Mexican coordination now through SEMARNAT's lineage), six annex workgroups, institutional descendant Frontera 2025 (confirmed, official texts). **No instance was found of La Paz ever being invoked against border barrier construction** — an apparent never, stated as absence-in-search. And the state-level silence generalizes: **no SRE diplomatic note specific to the wall located; no statement from Sonora Governor Durazo on the wall** — his documented 2025–26 border activity is cooperation with U.S. security operations, including accepting U.S.-donated vehicles for a new state border patrol. The round's structural finding, stated plainly: **on the Mexican side, the documented opposition to the wall is entirely civil-society and Indigenous — the rancheros, the Santa Cruz residents at the álamo, the O'odham dancing at La Bota — while both states align**. The line's two governments are, on this project, one government; the binational camp at the cottonwood is the only genuinely binational institution the wall has produced.
