# Walls versus water: the treaty record and the unraised theory at Lochiel

Research pass 2026-08-22. The finding in one sentence: the flood/treaty theory that repeatedly forced border-barrier redesigns on the Rio Grande — including against **this same contractor** — has never been publicly raised for the San Rafael Valley wall's two crossings of the Santa Cruz River, and no evidence could be found that IBWC/CILA review of those crossings ever occurred.

## The framework

The 1970 Boundary Treaty (Art. IV) bars works in the floodplain of transboundary rivers that deflect or obstruct normal or flood flows; IBWC and its Mexican section (CILA) jointly review such structures. In practice IBWC applies numeric criteria — **maximum 5% flow deflection; maximum water-surface rise of 3 inches urban / 6 inches rural** — recurring identically across litigation records and sworn agency statements (the specific Minute codifying them was not located; the criteria's existence is multiply attested). A separate 1977 IBWC/CILA agreement governs the Tijuana River channel. Crucially, the §102 waiver regime waives *domestic* legal requirements; nothing found extends it to treaty obligations — an inference, stated as such, that no source contradicts.

## The record of the theory working

- **2008–2012, Rio Grande**: IBWC warned CBP in writing that floodplain fence segments near Rio Grande City would push floodwater into Mexico; formally denied support in 2010 ("we must again deny our support for the proposed alignment"); CBP's own contractor (Baker Engineering) confirmed the physics. CILA formally challenged Hidalgo/Starr designs in 2011 (the U.S. section ultimately overrode). The fight produced redesigns (levee-walls) and years of friction.
- **2020–2022, the Fisher precedent**: Fisher Industries' private 3.5-mile bollard wall at Mission, TX was found by IBWC to violate the treaty — **10.32% deflection, more than double the 5% threshold** — and DOJ sued on IBWC's behalf. Settled May 31, 2022: floodgate installed, **quarterly inspections, and a $3 million bond held for 15 years**. **Fisher Sand & Gravel is the San Rafael Valley contractor.** The company building the Santa Cruz crossings carries a settled federal enforcement record for treaty-noncompliant river-crossing construction. (It was also awarded a $1.2B Presidio County, TX contract in March 2026, already under suit.)
- **2025, Tijuana**: CBP built through the river channel; claimed compliance rests on gates being open — compliance as an operational practice, not a design property.

## The record of the physics winning

- **Organ Pipe, July 12, 2008**: a new $21.3M, 5.2-mile fence acted as a dam in a 90-minute storm — water 2–7 ft deep along the fence, undersized 6"×24" drainage grates clogged, flow running 200 ft laterally, the Lukeville port of entry flooded, damage on both sides. NPS had warned DHS of exactly this failure mode in **October 2007**; the environmental assessment dismissed it.
- **Organ Pipe, Aug 7, 2011**: the fix — 10 liftable flood gates installed in 2010 — failed operationally: nobody raised the gates before the storm; debris tore out a 40-foot fence section.
- **Nogales, July 27, 2014**: Border Patrol had installed, in January 2014 and **without notifying IBWC**, a 5-ft barrier inside the historic drainage tunnel under Nogales, cutting its capacity ~40%; the monsoon flood that followed did **~$8 million in damage in Nogales, Sonora**. Direct precedent: unilateral U.S. obstruction of shared drainage, unreviewed, causing measurable damage in Mexico.
- The mitigation policy that follows from this record — floodgates left open all monsoon season — is *documented for the San Rafael project itself* ("leave the gates open during monsoon season, when powerful flows could otherwise topple the wall") and is exactly the operationally fragile practice that failed in 2011.

## The water at Lochiel

The Santa Cruz at the crossings is not a dry wash: it runs perennial for roughly its first two miles below the source. Primary gauge data (USGS 09480000, "Santa Cruz River near Lochiel," 82.2 sq mi drainage, record 1949–2023): **flood of record 12,000 cfs, reached twice — Oct 9, 1977 and Aug 15, 1984** — with 4,800–5,800 cfs events in 1993, 1999, and as recently as **Aug 21, 2022**. (Regionally, Tropical Storm Octave in Oct 1983 remains the benchmark Santa Cruz-system disaster — 13 dead, ~10,000 displaced, 8 inches of rain at Nogales — though this headwaters gauge peaked higher in 1977/1984.) Whatever structure spans those crossings must pass 12,000 cfs of debris-laden monsoon flow without deflecting it into Sonora or backing it onto the valley.

And the interim condition is already an obstruction: **Sky Island Alliance documented federal crews damming the Santa Cruz with a dirt construction road in June 2026**, pending the permanent crossing structure. ## The opening

Nothing in the public record — IBWC's Minutes archive, press, litigation filings — shows the San Rafael crossings were ever submitted to, reviewed by, or approved through IBWC/CILA treaty review; and none of the project's public opponents (CBD, Sky Island Alliance, Sierra Club, the tribes) has publicly raised the treaty theory here. Either review occurred (and its hydraulic analysis is a record to obtain) or it did not (and the crossings stand outside the treaty process that bound even a private builder in Texas). A FOIA to IBWC — drafted in lochiel-corridor-hold-services — resolves which, and either answer is material. CILA and Mexican federal channels (the 2017 Rascón warning shows the institutional concern exists) remain the binational lever no domestic waiver reaches.
