# The strip, the Nation, and the Texas base rate

Research pass 2026-08-22. Two halves that turn out to be one argument.

## The strip versus the Nation

The chronology is the whole federal case: **Proclamation 758 (May 27, 1907)** reserved the 60-foot strip from *public lands* in CA/AZ/NM; the Papago/Tohono O'odham reservation came later (EO of Jan 14, 1916; **EO 2313, Feb 1, 1917**, delineating boundaries — and making, per multiple sources, *no mention of the strip at all*). The government's argument in the current case: the strip predates the reservation, so walling it touches no tribal land.

The case: ***Tohono O'odham Nation v. Mullin*, No. 1:26-cv-02127 (D.D.C.)** — defendant DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin (enrolled Cherokee; the first Native American to head DHS, a fact history will want recorded). Nation notified May 15, 2026, with no consent requested; complaint and PI motion June 16–17 (Nation's AG office plus Kanji & Katzen); hearing July 22; **18-page denial by Senior Judge Richard Leon, Aug 14, 2026**. The Nation's arguments: no congressional act authorizes diminishing reservation land; trespass on sovereign territory; irreparable harm to sacred sites and cross-border family and ceremony; and — the deep one — **aboriginal title predating 1907**, meaning the strip's land was never validly "public" for Roosevelt to reserve. Leon found the aboriginal-title theory "unlikely to succeed" *at the preliminary stage*; this is not a merits ruling, and no prior scholarship or litigation on whether the 1907 strip survives incorporation into a later reservation could be found — **a matter of first impression, being made now**. Appeal under consideration.

Context that indicts the "necessity" framing: from 2006–08 the Nation *cooperated* — vehicle barriers, patrol roads, tribally monitored crossings, later the sensor towers — and that cooperative model is credited with cutting apprehensions on Nation land by 84–95% *without a wall*. Through the entire first wall era, the Nation's own 62 miles were never walled (Monument Hill and Quitobaquito sit on adjacent federal lands, not the reservation). The 2026 "Tucson 5" project (SLSCO, $1B+, double 30-ft walls) is the first attempt.

## The Texas base rate

Texas kept its public lands at statehood, so no Roosevelt strip exists there and every wall mile requires acquiring private land — which makes Texas the natural experiment for the question: *what happens to wall schedules when property basis must be litigated parcel by parcel?* Three waves answer consistently:

- **2007–09 (Secure Fence Act)**: 300–334 condemnation suits; settled cases averaged **~3.5 years**; Eloisa Tamez's case ran **8 years** (2008–2016); the Nature Conservancy's Southmost Preserve strip took 4 years; **60–85 cases were still open a decade later**; DOJ condemned land without confirming ownership or property lines ("The Taking," Texas Tribune).
- **2019–21**: 193 suits (three-quarters in the final year); ~5,800 acres sought; **$2B+ in contracts awarded before the land was owned**, producing $15,000/day idle-contractor costs (~$9M across four projects; one contract grew $33M→$42M); of 110 planned Rio Grande Valley miles, **15 were built**; parcels with 30+ scattered heirs; **GAO-21-114**: the government's *own planning figure* for South Texas land acquisition is **21–30 months** before construction, versus 12 elsewhere.
- **2024–26**: state-funded Texas wall "fragmented," pushed to remote parcels, 50 miles by end-2024; a fresh 39 federal condemnation cases filed by mid-2026; DHS now waiving statutory requirements in the Big Bend Sector specifically to escape this pattern.

**Base rate: parcel-by-parcel property litigation makes multi-year slippage the norm across three administrations.**

## The synthesis

The Roosevelt strip is the device that *converts* a Texas-style parcel fight into a chronology argument — 1907 beats 1917 — resolvable, per Leon, without individualized title litigation. That is exactly why the government leans on it, and exactly why the two title theories in play are structurally the same theory:

- The **Nation's** theory: aboriginal title predates 1907, so the strip never validly attached on O'odham land.
- The **Zanja grant** theory (the scope memo): the grant was confirmed and patented by 1900 — *private* title predating 1907 — so the strip never attached on former grant land at Lochiel either.

Both say: **Proclamation 758 could only reserve what was actually public in 1907.** If either succeeds anywhere along the alignment, that stretch of the project falls out of the chronology shortcut and into the Texas base rate — 21–30 months of acquisition by the government's own numbers, 3.5-year litigation averages, contractor idle costs by the day — which is to say, out of the hold window entirely and past the end of Fisher's 900-day clock. The pending title research (1899 plat, patent, county parcels) determines whether the grant-side version of the argument has ground to stand on; the Nation's version is before the D.C. Circuit's door now.
