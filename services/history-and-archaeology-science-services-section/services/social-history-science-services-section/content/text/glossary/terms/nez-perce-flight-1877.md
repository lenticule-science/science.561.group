In 1877 the non-treaty Nez Perce bands — Chief Joseph (Hinmatóowyalahtq'it) the most remembered of several leaders, with Looking Glass, White Bird, and the war leaders carrying much of the fighting — refused removal from their homelands and fought a running retreat of roughly 1,170 miles (widely attested figure) across Idaho, Wyoming, and Montana, outmaneuvering multiple U.S. Army columns for nearly four months while traveling with families, elders, and horse herds. Their destination, after the route through Yellowstone closed, was the Medicine Line and Sitting Bull's camp beyond it.

They were caught at the Bear Paw Mountains, Montana. The battle and siege ran **September 30 – October 5, 1877**, and the surrender came **October 5 — roughly forty miles short of the Canadian border** (primary-verified against the round's cross-checked sources). Joseph's surrender speech ("From where the sun now stands, I will fight no more forever") is attested through Lt. C.E.S. Wood's transcription, whose verbatim accuracy is questioned in the scholarship — quote it as reported, not as recorded. White Bird and a few hundred people (sources vary on the count) slipped the siege and did reach Sitting Bull's camp in Grandmother's Land; the rest were shipped not to the promised Idaho reservation but to Kansas and Indian Territory — the malarial exile the Nez Perce called Eeikish Pah, "the hot place" — with survivors permitted back to the Northwest only in 1885, Joseph to Colville, where he died in 1904.

For this corpus, Bear Paw is the negative case that completes the [Medicine Line](medicine-line.md)'s demonstration: the line's power to stop the U.S. Army was real, but it was a property *of the crossing*, not a field extending southward — forty miles short conferred nothing. The same people split by five miles of geography received opposite fates: refuge at Wood Mountain or Eeikish Pah. Exclusion-powers are threshold-shaped; the corpus's dispositional analysis of borders rests partly on this case, and the [two lines note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-two-lines.md) carries the full argument.
