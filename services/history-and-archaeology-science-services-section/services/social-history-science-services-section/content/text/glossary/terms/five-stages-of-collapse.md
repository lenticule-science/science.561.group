The **five stages of collapse**, developed by [Dmitry Orlov](../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/dmitry-orlov.md) from his direct observation of the Soviet Union's collapse and its aftermath, names a sequence of five domains in which societal collapse can proceed, each defined by the loss of a specific form of faith:

1. **Financial collapse** — faith that "the market shall provide" is lost; financial institutions become insolvent and savings are wiped out.
2. **Commercial collapse** — faith that goods will remain available is lost; retail infrastructure and import supply chains fail, and commodities are hoarded.
3. **Political collapse** — faith that "the government will take care of you" is lost; government services and legitimacy fail.
4. **Social collapse** — faith that "your people will take care of you" is lost; social institutions and community groups fail to fill the resulting void.
5. **Cultural collapse** — faith in the basic decency of humanity is lost.

Orlov's comparative claim is that the stages need not proceed to the same depth in every society facing collapse, and that the United States, despite greater aggregate wealth, held structural vulnerabilities — weaker informal-economy skills, greater dependence on cars and weaker public transit, higher household debt, thinner social cohesion — that could in principle let the stages proceed further than they did in the Soviet case.

The model is read alongside [catabolic collapse](catabolic-collapse.md) as a second account of collapse's uneven character, staged explicitly by domain rather than by the capital-maintenance dynamics Greer and [Joseph Tainter](diminishing-returns-on-complexity.md) describe. Orlov's financial-collapse stage is also read against [long-term debt cycle](../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/long-term-debt-cycle.md) as one account of what an "ugly deleveraging" looks like from inside the society experiencing it.
