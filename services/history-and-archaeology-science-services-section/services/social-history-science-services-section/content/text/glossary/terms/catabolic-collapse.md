**Catabolic collapse**, the theory developed by [John Michael Greer](../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/john-michael-greer.md) in the 2005 essay "How Civilizations Fall: A Theory of Catabolic Collapse," holds that societies accumulate "capital" — infrastructure, population, institutions — that requires ongoing maintenance energy to sustain. When available resources fall short of what full maintenance requires, a society lets part of that capital decay or be scavenged for its resource content ("catabolized") in order to keep the remaining capital functioning. This produces an uneven, stepwise, lurching decline, in which reduced capacity in one part of a society temporarily frees resources for another part, rather than a single, clean collapse event.

Catabolic collapse builds directly on [Joseph Tainter](diminishing-returns-on-complexity.md)'s [diminishing returns on complexity](diminishing-returns-on-complexity.md), extending it to explain the observed texture of historical declines — their unevenness and duration — rather than only the threshold at which decline becomes likely. Greer is also known for coining the phrase "collapse now and avoid the rush," urging voluntary early adaptation over forced later adaptation under worse conditions; the house's own founder encountered this coinage directly on the forum where Greer first used it.

See [five stages of collapse](five-stages-of-collapse.md) for a second, differently structured account of collapse's unevenness, staged by social domain rather than by capital-maintenance dynamics.
