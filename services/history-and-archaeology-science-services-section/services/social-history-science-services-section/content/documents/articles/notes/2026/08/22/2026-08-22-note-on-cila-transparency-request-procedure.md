# The Mexican-side FOIA mirror: filing a transparency request on CILA, SRE, and SEMARNAT

Research pass 2026-08-22, Sonoran round, stream four. Statuses: primary-verified (directly fetched official/legal text) / attested-secondary (press or summarized-fetch, cross-checked or plausible but not read verbatim) / unpinned (single source, not cross-checked, or absence-in-search).

## The finding in one sentence

Mexico's access-to-information law still gives any person — no ID, no stated interest, no citizenship test — a free, deadline-bound right to demand records from **the Mexican Section of CILA itself**, which the government's own transparency index lists as **its own obligated subject distinct from SRE's central unit** (attested-secondary); this is a live, unused channel for exactly the question the walls-versus-water note left open — whether the San Rafael crossings were ever reviewed under the treaty — and it does not depend on CILA's bot-walled website answering anything.

## The law, and what changed under it (2025)

The instrument is the **Ley General de Transparencia y Acceso a la Información Pública (LGTAIP)** — not a request under a foreign-sounding name, this *is* Mexico's FOIA. A **new LGTAIP was published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación on March 20, 2025**, replacing the 2015/2016-era law (primary-verified — DOF publication located and its date corroborated across independent legal-summary sources). The same date marks a structural break: **INAI, the autonomous body that had run Mexican transparency for 22 years, stopped operating on March 20, 2025**; the Senate vote formalizing its extinction and the transfer of its functions passed 321–125 (attested-secondary, cross-checked across Infobae, El Informador, Expansión, IDC).

INAI's federal functions passed to a new **deconcentrated body called "Transparencia para el Pueblo,"** housed inside the newly created **Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno (SABG)** — i.e., inside the executive branch (attested-secondary, multiple outlets agree; official portal at transparencia.gob.mx confirms the name and function, primary-verified by direct fetch). This is a real loss of independence, not cosmetic: commentary from IDC, Este País, and Mexicanos Contra la Corrupción e Impunidad (MCCI) converges on the same concern — Transparencia para el Pueblo, unlike INAI, does not report to Congress, and the recurso de revisión (appeal) process is now decided by a **Dirección General de Recursos de Revisión y Desacuerdos whose head is named by the head of Transparencia para el Pueblo, a post the President decides** (attested-secondary). Flag this for any counsel relying on the appeal stage: the initial request right is intact and legally binding regardless; the escalation path is weaker than the old model.

## The portal

**Plataforma Nacional de Transparencia (PNT)**, at plataformadetransparencia.org.mx, remains the live federal portal in 2026 (attested-secondary — described as active/vigente in current search results; the domain itself was not independently fetched this pass). Its request-filing module, **SISAI 2.0**, lets one account file to any of dozens of obligated institutions — federal, state, or municipal — in a single form; SISAI 2.0 is a module *of* PNT, not a replacement for it, launched 2021 to retire the old INFOMEX systems (attested-secondary, cross-checked across INAI-era and current state-portal sources). Alternate channels remain available per Transparencia para el Pueblo's own page: in person at the target institution's Unidad de Transparencia, email, post, or telephone (attested-secondary, direct fetch of transparencia.gob.mx summarized this but the page's specifics were not quoted verbatim).

## What a request must contain (Art. 124 LGTAIP)

Minimal and deliberately low-friction (attested-secondary, cross-checked across legal-summary sources of the article's text, not a verbatim primary read of Art. 124 itself):

1. Name of the requester, or their representative's data — **optional**, cannot be made a condition of admissibility.
2. An address or means to receive notifications.
3. A description of the information requested.
4. Any other data that helps locate it.

No showing of legal interest, no statement of intended use, no proof of citizenship or residency is required. This matters directly for this operation: a request can in principle be filed by anyone, but for reliability of response and standing to appeal, it should be filed by a Mexican-side principal or their counsel — an observation about how the law's response and appeal machinery is built to engage a requester of record, not a strategic recommendation about what to file.

## Timelines and cost

- **Response deadline: 20 business days**, extendable by **10 more business days** if the obligated subject's Comité de Transparencia justifies the extension and notifies the requester before the original deadline expires (primary-verified — SCJN's own plazos guide and a gob.mx procedures document both state this figure; independently corroborated by current search summaries of PNT practice).
- **Cost**: exercising the right is free; **the first 20 simple sheets are provided at no charge**; beyond that, only material reproduction/shipping/certification costs may be charged, paid before delivery — **searching or processing time can never be billed** (primary-verified, gob.mx's own "Costos y Plazos para la Reproducción de la Información" document).
- **Appeal (recurso de revisión)**: filed with Transparencia para el Pueblo within **15 business days** of the notified response (or of the deadline passing with no response); Transparencia para el Pueblo must resolve within **40 business days** of admitting the appeal, extendable once by up to **20 more days** (attested-secondary — state-portal and specialized-firm explainers of the LGTAIP-standard rule, not a verbatim read of the federal implementing rule; treat the specific day-counts as well-attested but not primary-pinned).

## The specific target: CILA has its own transparency listing

This is the load-bearing new fact. SRE's own transparency-obligations index page (sre.gob.mx/transparencia, "Sistema de Portales de Obligaciones de Transparencia," fetched directly) lists the **Sección Mexicana de la Comisión Internacional de Límites y Aguas entre México y Estados Unidos as its own separate transparency portal/obligated-subject entry — distinct from SRE's central transparency unit** — on the same footing as the separately listed Mexico–Guatemala and Mexico–Belice CILA sections (attested-secondary: this is a WebFetch summarization of the SRE page's content, not a verbatim quote of the entry; **the entry's exact registered name should be confirmed by a human doing an institution search directly inside the PNT/SISAI form before filing**, both because it wasn't read verbatim and because the same SRE page still pointed at "consultapublicamx.inai.org.mx" — a defunct, pre-reform INAI portal address — meaning the government's own site has not been fully updated since the March 2025 reform).

This closes a gap the walls-versus-water and Mexican-Indigenous-and-legal-frame notes both left open: **CILA's own Boletines de Prensa archive is bot-walled** (confirmed again this pass — cila.sre.gob.mx/cilanorte returns a Radware/perfdrive bot challenge to automated fetch) and no public statement from CILA on the 2025–26 wall was found by search. A transparency request does not need the website to cooperate; it is a legal demand served on the institution through PNT/SISAI, independent of what CILA chooses to publish. SRE's general transparency contact is **canciller@sre.gob.mx** (attested-secondary) as a fallback if the CILA-specific portal entry proves hard to locate.

## Two more targets, and why

- **SRE generally** — for any diplomatic note or interagency correspondence specific to the San Rafael Valley wall. This directly tests the Mexican-Indigenous-and-legal-frame note's finding that **no SRE diplomatic note specific to the wall was located** — an absence-in-search, not a proven negative; a records request is the instrument that can actually resolve it.
- **SEMARNAT** — Mexico's **national coordinator for the Border 2025 program**, the institutional descendant of the 1983 La Paz Agreement, alongside the U.S. EPA (attested-secondary, EPA's and SEMARNAT's own program pages). The la-paz-and-the-silence note found **no instance of La Paz ever being invoked against border-barrier construction** and no SEMARNAT/EPA correspondence specific to this wall; SEMARNAT is the addressee if any such correspondence exists.

## What to actually ask for (records description, not argument)

Doctrine bars authoring strategy for Mexican-side principals; what follows is a factual list of record types grounded in the evidence notes already verified, offered as raw material for counsel to select from — not an argument for them to make:

- Any dictamen técnico, análisis hidráulico, or correspondence exchanged with the U.S. Section of IBWC concerning the San Rafael Valley border wall's crossings of the Santa Cruz River, under the Mexican Section's Article 2 jurisdiction (1944 Water Treaty) over "works constructed in [boundary rivers] and on [the boundary]."
- Any record showing whether the Santa Cruz crossings were submitted for, or exempted from, the joint IBWC/CILA treaty review that produced the Fisher Industries floodgate settlement (Mission, TX, 2022) and the Rio Grande City redesigns (2011–2012) — both already-verified precedents of the same review process working elsewhere, cited in the walls-versus-water note.
- Any SRE diplomatic note, memorandum, or interagency communication referencing the San Rafael Valley wall, 2024–2026.
- Any SEMARNAT correspondence with EPA or other U.S. counterparts under the Border 2025 program referencing the San Rafael Valley wall or Santa Cruz River construction, 2024–2026.

## Caveats and gaps carried forward

- The CILA-Mexico-US portal entry's exact name/ID was not verbatim-confirmed — resolve with a direct human search inside PNT/SISAI before filing; do not cite the entry's name as fixed until then.
- The recurso de revisión day-counts (15 to file, 40 to resolve, +20 possible extension) are attested-secondary, drawn from state-portal and firm explainers of the general LGTAIP rule rather than a verbatim read of the federal implementing regulation for Transparencia para el Pueblo specifically.
- Whether Transparencia para el Pueblo's appeal process is functioning at full capacity in mid-2026 is itself contested in the Mexican transparency-advocacy press (MCCI, Este País describe "improvisación y pendientes," pending appointments) — treat the appeal stage as slower and less predictable than the request stage, independent of anything about this particular request.
- No test has been run: this note verifies the *procedure*, not the *answer*. Whether CILA/SRE/SEMARNAT actually hold responsive records remains open — the walls-versus-water note's own framing (either review happened, or it didn't, and either answer is material) still governs.
