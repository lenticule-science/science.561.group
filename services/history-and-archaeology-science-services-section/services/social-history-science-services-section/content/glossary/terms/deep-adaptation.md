**Deep adaptation** is the framework developed by [Jem Bendell](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/jem-bendell.md) in "Deep Adaptation: A Map for Navigating Climate Tragedy" (IFLAS occasional paper, 2018), organized around four R's: **resilience** (which social and physical structures to keep), **relinquishment** (what to consciously let go of before it is lost involuntarily), **restoration** (what past practices or attitudes to bring back), and **reconciliation** (making peace with mortality, uncertainty, and each other under the conditions the paper describes).

The paper argues near-term societal collapse driven by climate change is likely to inevitable within the lifetime of much of the current population, given documented climate feedback loops and the inadequacy of policy responses to date. Bendell's own account states the paper was submitted to a peer-reviewed journal and returned for revision rather than accepted; he self-published it as an occasional paper instead, after which it produced the large "Deep Adaptation Forum" community and drew sharp published criticism from climate researchers challenging specific claims and arguing the near-term-inevitable framing was not supported by the cited science.

The house reads deep adaptation as the clearest statement of planning for a **rising**, rather than fixed, [absorbing barrier](../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/finance-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/absorbing-barrier.md): the house's own note on sizing without a bank states that "climate breakdown raises the water" and that a sizing rule assuming a fixed barrier is wrong in the direction that kills, the same structural concern Bendell's framework addresses at the level of a life or community rather than a financial sleeve.
