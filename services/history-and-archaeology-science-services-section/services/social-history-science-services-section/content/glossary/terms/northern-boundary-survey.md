The northern line's founding pair mirrors the southern one's: a treaty that drew a line through peoples who were not consulted, and a survey that placed it wrong. The **Convention of 1818** (signed London, October 20, 1818; ratifications exchanged January 30, 1819; negotiators Gallatin and Rush for the U.S., Robinson and Goulburn for Britain — primary-verified dates) set the 49th parallel as the boundary from Lake of the Woods to the Rocky Mountains, extended to the Pacific by the Oregon Treaty of 1846. Like the [Gadsden Purchase](./gadsden-purchase.md) thirty-five years later, it was a settlement between two empires allocating land that belonged, in living fact, to the Lakota, Dakota, Blackfoot Confederacy, Cree, Assiniboine, Métis, and others the text never mentions.

The **Northern Boundary Survey of 1872–74** — joint U.S. and British commissions working by astronomical station across the prairies, with Métis guides widely reported in the fieldwork — marked the parallel in earth mounds, timber posts, and iron markers, placing the final marker at Waterton Lake on **August 8, 1874**. And the markers are wrong: the monumented line deviates from the true 49th parallel by an **average of about 270 meters (up to ~810 meters)** — a documented, measured geodetic error (verified against the Digital Chart of the World figures) — and both governments ratified the monuments over the mathematics. The marked line, not the parallel, is the boundary.

That makes the northern line the second independent case of the corpus's finding at [Monument 127](../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/social-and-economic-geography-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/la-gran-linea.md) on the Gadsden line (4.41 miles off its treaty coordinates, grandfathered): a border's authority has never depended on its geographic accuracy, because its referent was never geographic. What the surveyed-wrong line did hold, and precisely, was its exclusion-powers — which on this line pointed, for four remarkable years, at the United States Army: see [Medicine Line](./medicine-line.md) and the [two lines note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-two-lines.md).
