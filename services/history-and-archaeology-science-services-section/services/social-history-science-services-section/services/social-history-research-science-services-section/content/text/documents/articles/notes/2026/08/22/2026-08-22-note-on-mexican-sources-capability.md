# Mexican-sources capability map

Compiled 2026-08-22 (Sonoran round, stream four). This is standing infrastructure: what exists, what it holds, how to reach it. Refresh access notes before relying on them.

## Scholars (scopes verified against reviews/publisher pages, not jacket copy)

- **Cynthia Radding, *Wandering Peoples*** (Duke, 1997) — ethnohistory of highland Sonoran **Ópatas, Eudeves, and Pimas** (not Yaqui/Mayo, not Arizona-side), 1700–1850: sedentary communities surviving Bourbon and Mexican state incursion through negotiated mobility. The colonial-to-national transition anchor.
- **Miguel Tinker Salas, *In the Shadow of the Eagles*** (UC, 1997) — Sonora 1876–1910: the Porfirian transformation into a U.S.-facing border economy (mining, labor, the Arizona connection). The corpus's bridge from Pesqueira to Cananea.
- **Raquel Padilla Ramos** (INAH Sonora; murdered Nov 7, 2019 — see the Indigenous-frame note) — the Yaqui deportations, missions, Mayo/Yaqui ethnohistory; posthumous INAH tribute volume; Academia.edu profile carries full texts.
- **Ana María Alonso, *Thread of Blood*** (Arizona, 1995) — Chihuahua's Namiquipa: frontier violence, gender, and the making of colonist identity that fed the Revolution. The comparative frontier text, and the scholarly frame for the scalp-bounty world.
- **Zulema Trejo Contreras** (COLSON/CEHRF) — 19th-century Sonoran political/fiscal history; *Redes, facciones y liberalismo. Sonora 1850–1876*; co-author of *La institución significada* on Indigenous pueblos in colonial/republican Sonora.
- **Ignacio Almada Bay** (COLSON) — *Historia breve de Sonora*; Revolution-era and 19th–20th c. northwest.
- **José Marcos Medina Bustos** (COLSON/UNISON) — colonial Sonora: vecindad, missions, antiguo régimen → liberalism, 1681–1821; 50+ papers open on Academia.edu.
- **Thomas Sheridan, *Where the Dove Calls*** (Arizona, 1988) — Cucurpe ethnography, corporate peasant communities; the English-language complement to Radding.

## Institutions and journals

- **El Colegio de Sonora (COLSON)**, Hermosillo — Centro de Estudios Históricos de Región y Frontera; annual Coloquio; ***región y sociedad*** (open access, SciELO/Redalyc/DOAJ).
- **COLEF** — ***Frontera Norte*** (bilingual, open access, no fees).
- **UNISON** Historia y Antropología — regional history degrees; faculty overlap with COLSON.
- **Centro INAH Sonora** — archaeology/heritage, Kino missions binational work, journal *Noroeste de México*; Padilla Ramos's institutional home.
- **Sociedad Sonorense de Historia** (Hermosillo, 1975) — independent society; tracked by *Crónica Sonora*.

## Archives

- **Archivo General del Estado de Sonora (AGES)**, Garmendia 157, Hermosillo — notarial fondo from 1783 (Arizpe), the Boletín Oficial run (where the 1948 Caborca "Heroica" decree lives), Plan de Agua Prieta original; public sala de consulta; requests a copy of resulting publications.
- **AGN**, Mexico City — **Ramo de Provincias Internas** (1617–1842) is the frontier-era record group; RDD-AGN digital repository works as a *finding tool* (fondo/legajo references) more than a full digitization portal; UNM holds microfilm.
- **CILA actas** — 331 bilingual actas (1889–2025) online; the boundary/water legal record, directly relevant to the Santa Cruz crossings. (CILA's press-bulletin archive currently bot-walled — retry directly.)
- **Santa Cruz municipio parish/municipal records** — no finding aid located; entry point is the Directorio Nacional de Archivos (Sonora) plus the diocese at Hermosillo/Magdalena. Open item.

## Press map for the live story

- **El Imparcial (Nogales desk)** — the highest-frequency, lowest-latency Spanish source on the San Rafael wall and the álamo; near-weekly coverage; the only outlet documenting the Sonoran side of the camp in detail (including facts absent from U.S. coverage: the 60%-roots figure, the 200-m pause, the 1984 garita closure).
- **Dossier Político** — policy/infrastructure frame; connected the San Rafael build to Quitobaquito.
- **Proyecto Puente** — binational-institutional beat (Comisión Sonora-Arizona: water, security, investment); not covering the camp directly.
- Cross-check against Tucson Sentinel, KJZZ Fronteras, Patagonia Regional Times, CBD/Sky Island Alliance releases — each side's press carries facts the other omits; the divergences are themselves data (e.g., the police-detention claim exists only in the U.S./activist lineage).

## Digital toolkit

| Resource | Use | Access |
|---|---|---|
| región y sociedad; Frontera Norte; SciELO MX; Redalyc | current Sonoran academic historiography, full text | free |
| RDD-AGN (repositorio.agn.gob.mx) | locate fondo/legajo references incl. Provincias Internas | free search; in-person for most images |
| HNDM (hndm.iib.unam.mx) | historical newspapers: 1722–1889 fully open online; **1890–2006 on-site only** (Mexico City) | split access — plan around the 1890 cutoff |
| CILA actas (cila.sre.gob.mx) | boundary/water legal record 1889–2025 | free; press archive bot-walled |
| Directorio Nacional de Archivos | locate parish/municipal archives | free directory |
| Academia.edu COLSON mirrors | Trejo, Medina Bustos, Padilla Ramos full texts | free |

## Practical rules learned this round

1. The two open-access journals plus SciELO/Redalyc cover current Sonoran historiography without any archive visit.
2. The HNDM 1890 cutoff means 20th-century newspaper work needs Mexico City on-site access or a state hemeroteca (UNISON library, Biblioteca Pública del Estado).
3. Official Mexican sites (CILA, some SRE pages) increasingly bot-wall automated fetches — build direct-visit/manual-capture steps into any round that needs them.
4. Read both presses on any binational event and record the divergences as findings, not noise.
