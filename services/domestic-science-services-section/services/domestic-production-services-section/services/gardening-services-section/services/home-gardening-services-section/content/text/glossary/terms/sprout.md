A sprout is a germinated seed harvested and eaten before true leaves develop, typically within three to seven days of soaking. In [home gardening](../index.md), sprouts are among the simplest crops to grow because they require no soil, no light (in most cases), and no specialized equipment beyond a jar and clean water.

The sprouting process begins with soaking seeds in water for several hours to initiate germination. After soaking, the seeds are drained and kept moist through regular [rinse cycles](./rinse-cycle.md) — typically two to three times daily. The seed absorbs water, the [seed hull](./seed-hull.md) splits, and a root and shoot emerge. Sprouts are harvested when the shoot has grown to one or two centimeters but before the [cotyledons](./cotyledon.md) fully expand. Common sprouting seeds include mung bean, alfalfa, radish, broccoli, and lentil.

Sprouts differ from [microgreens](../topics/microgreens/index.md) in harvest timing, growing method, and the part of the plant that is eaten. A sprout is consumed whole — root, seed, and shoot — while a microgreen is cut above the soil line and only the stem and leaves are eaten. Because sprouts are eaten whole and grown in warm, moist conditions without soil, they carry a higher food-safety risk than microgreens, which is why [sanitizing](./sanitize.md) equipment and maintaining consistent rinse cycles is important for home sprouting.
