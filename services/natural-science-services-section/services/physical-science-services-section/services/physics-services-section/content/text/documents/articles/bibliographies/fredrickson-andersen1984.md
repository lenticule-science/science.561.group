# Fredrickson & Andersen 1984 — Kinetic Ising Model of the Glass Transition

*Physical Review Letters* 53(13), 1984, pp. 1244–1247.

## Why this is central

Introduces the **Fredrickson–Andersen (FA) model**, the archetypal kinetically constrained
model: a kinetic Ising model whose equilibrium measure is a trivial, non-interacting
product measure, but whose transition rates are constrained — a spin may flip only if at
least a threshold number of its neighbors are already in the excited (mobile) state. The FA
model is the paper that opens the entire KCM research program later surveyed by Ritort &
Sollich, and the model whose facilitation rule (a threshold-count neighbor condition) is
structurally closest, of all the KCM family, to the bootstrap-percolation activation rule
this section's measurement instantiates as `bootstrap2`.

## What it does not settle

The paper introduces the model and its basic properties; it does not itself carry the full
later apparatus of dynamic heterogeneity (Garrahan & Chandler), the parabolic relaxation
law (Sollich & Evans, Elmatad, Chandler & Garrahan), or the sharp jamming transition of the
Spiral Model (Toninelli, Biroli & Fisher) — each a later, separate contribution the FA
model's own dynamics does not itself exhibit (the FA model, like the East model, has no
genuine jamming transition at any finite density).
