# Berthier & Biroli 2011 — Theoretical perspective on the glass transition and amorphous materials

*Reviews of Modern Physics* 83(2), 2011, pp. 587–645.

## Why this is central

The standard modern review of the glass transition problem: it states the phenomenon (a
ten-to-fifteen order of magnitude viscosity rise over a modest temperature window, with
essentially no accompanying change in static structure), surveys the field's competing
theoretical accounts — random first-order transition theory, mode-coupling theory,
dynamical facilitation, geometric frustration — and is explicit that whether the glass
transition marks a genuine underlying thermodynamic transition or is purely a kinetic,
out-of-equilibrium phenomenon remains open. It is the reference this section's own note
uses to state the problem honestly, as a live field with competing schools, rather than
picking a winner.

## What it does not settle

The review states the open question; it does not resolve it. Being a 2011 survey, it also
does not carry the field's subsequent decade of work (ultrastable glasses, the swap Monte
Carlo algorithm's push toward the Kauzmann temperature, more recent facilitation
simulations) — later results the section's own notes should engage directly as they are
brought in, not read off this review alone.
