# Hardy 1993 — Nonlocality for Two Particles without Inequalities for Almost All Entangled States

*Physical Review Letters* 71, 1993, pp. 1665–1668.

## Why this is central

This paper exhibits a non-locality proof of a different logical shape than Bell's: rather
than an inequality violated by an average over many runs, the **Hardy paradox** derives a
direct logical contradiction between local hidden-variable predictions and quantum
predictions on specific joint outcomes, for almost every entangled (non-maximally
entangled) two-particle state. It is a "nonlocality without inequalities" argument,
structurally simpler to state than Bell's theorem and later recognized as sitting at a
specific rung — logically contextual but not strongly contextual — of the
Abramsky–Brandenburger possibilistic contextuality hierarchy.

## What it does not settle

The paper is a specific proof for a specific family of states and does not itself develop
the general sheaf-theoretic contextuality hierarchy the Hardy model is later placed
within; that placement is Abramsky & Brandenburger's later work.
