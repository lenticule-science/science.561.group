# Thermal spacetime: finite settlement

**Thermal spacetime** is our name for the finite-settlement reading of a physical generation. After kinematic possibilities, quantum relations, and a stable causal geometry have been articulated, thermodynamic observables describe how the resulting physical field stores, exchanges, dissipates, and redistributes energy.

We conjecture that the **strict-finite closure regime** is the appropriate mathematical presentation of a completed *local* turn. The resulting settlement has a stabilized field of admissible observations. It need not be a cosmological heat death: a finite rule closure and thermodynamic equilibrium are distinct predicates and must be related by their actual dynamical construction.

Finite settlement completes the present [Turn](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/turn.md), not all generative existence. The canonical Turn record holds its regime–Pisot selection conjecture and the proposed residual re-entry mechanism.

[Lambek residuals](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/residuation.md) identify the constraints remaining after directed composition. We conjecture that these unabsorbed relations can generate the next kinematic domain without undoing the existing thermal history.

The **thermodynamic arrow of time** describes the evolution of energy and entropy under the assumptions of a physical system. The deeper [relational arrow](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/relational-arrow-of-time.md) is our conjecture about the directed and noninvertible formation of histories. Showing how the latter yields the former requires a physical entropy law and observables, not just a closure label.
