# Lanford 1975 — Time Evolution of Large Classical Systems

In J. Moser (ed.), *Dynamical Systems, Theory and Applications*, Lecture Notes in
Physics vol. 38, Springer, 1975, pp. 1–111.

## Why this is central

This is the paper that makes Boltzmann's *Stosszahlansatz* (molecular chaos) a theorem
rather than a hypothesis, for a specific and short time interval: starting from a
hard-sphere gas of $N$ particles in the Boltzmann–Grad limit ($N \to \infty$, sphere
diameter $\to 0$, mean free path fixed), Lanford proves the one-particle marginal
distribution converges to a solution of the Boltzmann equation, and that molecular chaos
— asymptotic independence of the particles' positions and velocities — is *propagated*
in time by the dynamics if it holds initially. This is the rigorous derivation the
BBGKY hierarchy's formal molecular-chaos closure had lacked.

## What it does not settle

The convergence is proved only for a time interval short compared to the mean free
time between collisions (a small fraction of the time the physical gas actually takes to
reach equilibrium); extending propagation of chaos to arbitrarily long times remains
open in general, decades after this paper.

## Bridges

- To [molecular chaos](../../../glossary/terms/molecular-chaos.md) — the closure hypothesis
