# Sollich & Evans 1999 — Glassy Time-Scale Divergence and Anomalous Coarsening in a Kinetically Constrained Spin Chain

*Physical Review Letters* 83(16), 1999, pp. 3238–3241.

## Why this is central

Derives the East model's relaxation-time divergence through a **hierarchical argument**: to
mobilize a domain of a given size, a facilitating neighbor at a coarser length scale must
first itself be mobilized, and so on down through nested length and time scales, giving the
model's characteristic scaling of relaxation time with inverse temperature. This hierarchical,
nested-facilitation argument is the specific mechanism this section's note reads (labeled
inference, §4) as nested acknowledgement chains: to acknowledge a facilitation event at
depth `n`, the chain first requires acknowledging its own facilitating predecessor at depth
`n − 1`. The paper is the origin of the argument the parabolic law (Elmatad, Chandler &
Garrahan 2009) later fits empirically across many glass formers.

## What it does not settle

The paper's hierarchical argument is about the East model specifically (1D, one-sided
facilitation); it does not itself establish the same mechanism for two- or
three-dimensional KCMs, nor does it address whether real structural glass formers actually
realize East-model-like hierarchical relaxation as opposed to some other facilitation
geometry — an empirical question the corresponding-states program (Elmatad, Chandler &
Garrahan) takes up.
