# Anderson 1972 — More Is Different

*Science* 177(4047), 1972, pp. 393–396.

## Why this is central

This paper argues against a naive reductionism in which the behavior of large,
complex aggregates of elementary particles can simply be extrapolated from the
properties of a few particles: at each level of complexity (particle physics,
chemistry, biology, psychology) genuinely new laws, concepts, and generalizations are
required, which are not less fundamental for being emergent. "More is different" names
the general phenomenon that behavior at a larger scale is not derivable, in practice or
sometimes in principle, from recombining the resources already available at a smaller
scale — the paper's chosen illustrations are broken symmetry and phase transitions.

## What it does not settle

The paper is a position piece rather than a theorem; it does not supply a formal
criterion for when emergence in this sense occurs, and the philosophical literature on
reduction, emergence, and levels of explanation it opened remains an active debate.
