# Kirkpatrick, Thirumalai & Wolynes 1989 — Scaling concepts for the dynamics of viscous liquids near an ideal glassy state

*Physical Review A* 40(2), 1989, pp. 1045–1054.

## Why this is central

A founding paper of **random first-order transition theory (RFOT)**: it argues, drawing on
the mean-field theory of spin glasses with quenched disorder-free frustration, that a
structural glass former's free-energy landscape becomes rugged at low temperature, with an
exponentially large number of metastable "inherent structures" separated by activated
barriers, and that the configurational entropy counting those structures should shrink
continuously as temperature falls, extrapolating to zero at a genuine **Kauzmann
temperature** `T_K` below the experimentally observed glass transition `T_g`. This is the
paper this section's note cites for RFOT's central claim: that an underlying thermodynamic
singularity exists, and the kinetic glass transition merely precedes and obscures it.

## What it does not settle

The paper is a scaling and mean-field argument; it does not itself prove that a genuine
`T_K > 0` singularity exists in any finite-dimensional, short-range interacting model —
the central open point this section's note names as a falsifier of the placement it
argues for (§5). The extrapolation of measured configurational entropy to `T_K` remains an
extrapolation, not a proven phase transition, in the decades of work since.
