# Bombelli, Lee, Meyer & Sorkin 1987 — Space-Time as a Causal Set

*Physical Review Letters* 59, 1987, pp. 521–524.

## Why this is central

This paper proposes that spacetime, at the fundamental level, is discrete: a **causal
set** — a locally finite partial order in which $x \prec y$ means $x$ causally precedes
$y$. The order alone is claimed to determine both the causal structure and, statistically
via the number of elements in a causal interval, the conformal geometry and dimension of
the continuum spacetime it approximates. This is the founding paper of causal set theory,
a discrete approach to quantum gravity, and the source of "causal set" as a technical term
for a locally finite causally-ordered point set.

## What it does not settle

The paper argues for the causal-set hypothesis and sketches how geometric quantities
(dimension, volume, proper time) can be recovered statistically from the order, but it
does not itself supply a complete dynamics for how causal sets grow or a full account of
matter fields on a causal set — those are the subject of the causal set program's later
literature (classical sequential growth, quantum causal histories).
