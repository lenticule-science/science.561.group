# Popescu & Rohrlich 1994 — Quantum Nonlocality as an Axiom

*Foundations of Physics* 24, 1994, pp. 379–385.

## Why this is central

This paper asks what constrains the strength of non-locality allowed by a physical
theory, and exhibits the maximally non-local correlations consistent with the
no-signalling principle alone: the **PR box**, a hypothetical bipartite device whose
outputs satisfy $a \oplus b = xy$ for binary inputs $x, y$ and outputs $a, b$, achieving
perfect correlation in a Bell-type test while still obeying no-signalling. It shows
quantum mechanics does not saturate the no-signalling bound, posing the still-open
question of what physical principle (beyond no-signalling) picks out quantum correlations
specifically. The PR box has since become the canonical extremal example in the study of
non-locality and contextuality.

## What it does not settle

The paper does not claim the PR box is physically realizable — no known theory,
including quantum mechanics, produces it — and it does not answer its own question (what
principle, short of the full quantum formalism, forbids PR-box-strength correlations);
that remains an active research question (information causality, Tsirelson's bound, and
related proposals are the later literature).
