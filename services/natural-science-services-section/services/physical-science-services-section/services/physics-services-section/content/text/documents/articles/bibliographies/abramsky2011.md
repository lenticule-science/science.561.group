# Abramsky & Brandenburger 2011 — The sheaf-theoretic structure of non-locality and contextuality

*New Journal of Physics* 13, 2011, 113036.

## Why this is central

This paper gives quantum non-locality and contextuality a unified structural home: a
**measurement scenario** is a cover of measurement contexts, an **empirical model** is a
presheaf of probability distributions (or, at the possibilistic level, of admissible
outcome supports) satisfying a no-signalling compatibility condition on overlaps, and a
model is (non-)contextual exactly according to whether it does or does not admit a
**global section**. It organizes the possibilistic hierarchy — non-contextual, logically
contextual (some local assignment extends to no global one), strongly contextual (no
global assignment restricts correctly anywhere) — as strictly increasing obstruction
strength to gluing, the same language sheaf theory uses for local-to-global obstruction.
This is the paper that makes "does a global section exist" the technical question
contextuality asks.

## What it does not settle

The paper's cohomological development (relating non-existence of a global section to a
non-vanishing Čech-type obstruction class) is developed most fully in the companion and
follow-up literature (Abramsky, Mansfield & Barbosa 2012; Abramsky, Barbosa, Kishida, Lal
& Mansfield 2015); this 2011 paper itself establishes the sheaf/presheaf framework and the
possibilistic hierarchy rather than a general cohomological obstruction theorem.
