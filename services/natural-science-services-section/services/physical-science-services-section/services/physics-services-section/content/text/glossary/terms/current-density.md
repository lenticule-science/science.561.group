Current density is [current](electric-current.md) divided by the area it passes through, in amperes per square metre, or in stimulation practice mA/cm² [@halliday2013]. The same 2 mA spread over a 25 cm² sponge electrode gives 0.08 mA/cm². Squeezed through a small ear clip it gives many times that. At the skin, high current density is what causes tingling, redness and, if charge builds up, burns. That is why electrode size and contact matter for safety [@bikson2016].

Inside the body, current density divided by conductivity gives the [electric field](electric-field.md). Current spreads out after entering the head, so density, and with it the field, is highest near the electrodes and falls with depth.
