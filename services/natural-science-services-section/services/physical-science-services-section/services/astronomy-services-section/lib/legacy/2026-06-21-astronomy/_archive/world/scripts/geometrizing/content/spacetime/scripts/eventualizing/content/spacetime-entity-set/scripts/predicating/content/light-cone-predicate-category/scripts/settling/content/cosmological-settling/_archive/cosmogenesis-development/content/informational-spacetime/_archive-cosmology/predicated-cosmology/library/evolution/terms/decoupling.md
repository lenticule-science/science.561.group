Decoupling is the moment at which a particle species in the early universe stops interacting frequently enough with another species to remain in thermal equilibrium with it, and thereafter evolves independently.
The two most important decoupling events in cosmic history are **photon decoupling** at $t \approx 380{,}000$ years and **neutrino decoupling** at $t \approx 1$ second.

The general criterion: a species decouples when its interaction rate $\Gamma$ drops below the cosmic expansion rate $H$.
As long as $\Gamma > H$, particles interact many times per Hubble time and remain in equilibrium with their interaction partners.
When $\Gamma < H$, they interact less than once per Hubble time, and the cosmic expansion dilutes them faster than interactions can maintain equilibrium.
They free-stream from then on.

**Photon decoupling** coincides with [recombination](./recombination.md).
Before recombination, the universe was a hot plasma of free electrons and atomic nuclei.
Photons scattered off free electrons via Thomson scattering at a high rate, keeping photons and matter in thermal equilibrium and making the universe opaque.
When the universe cooled below $\sim 3000$ K, electrons combined with nuclei into neutral atoms; the free-electron density dropped by orders of magnitude; the photon-electron scattering rate fell below $H$; photons decoupled and have been free-streaming ever since.
The free-streaming photons we observe today are the [cosmic microwave background](../../structure/terms/cosmic-microwave-background.md).

**Neutrino decoupling** happened much earlier, at $t \sim 1$ second and $T \sim 1$ MeV.
Neutrinos interacted with the surrounding plasma via the weak interaction, whose rate $\Gamma \propto T^5$ falls steeply with cooling.
When $\Gamma$ dropped below $H$, neutrinos decoupled.
They have been free-streaming since.
The cosmic neutrino background — the analog of the CMB for neutrinos — is predicted to have a temperature of about 1.95 K today and should be detectable in principle, though it has not yet been directly observed.

A subtle case is **matter-radiation decoupling** in the sense of "matter density growing faster than radiation density."
This is not an interaction-rate event but a kinematic one: matter dilutes as $a^{-3}$ and radiation as $a^{-4}$ (radiation has the extra factor from redshift), so as the universe expands matter eventually dominates.
This crossover happened at $t \sim 50{,}000$ years, before photon decoupling, and is the moment at which structure formation becomes possible (matter perturbations can grow on sub-horizon scales only in matter-dominated cosmology).

In a predicated cosmology, decoupling is the categorical signature of a sub-settling reaching [quiescence](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-quiescence.md): the closure rule binding two species together stops firing, and the species' predicates evolve independently thereafter.
