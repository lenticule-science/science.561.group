# Does this cosmology extend to a multiverse?

Predicated-cosmology models *the* universe as a single [cosmological settling](../objects/cosmological-settling.md). The sibling [predicated-multiverse](../../predicated-multiverse/_index.md) subtheory models populations of [worlds-as-nuclei](../../predicated-multiverse/objects/world-as-nucleus.md) connected by the [Wood Between the Worlds](../../predicated-multiverse/objects/wood-between-worlds.md). The composition question runs in this direction:

**Q.** Does *our* cosmological settling — the one this subtheory describes — instantiate as a single nucleus in a predicated-multiverse family of worlds? And if so, what additional structure on the settling is needed beyond what is currently specified here?

The reverse direction — *given* a predicated-multiverse world-as-nucleus, does it carry cosmological-settling data — is the dual question [does-cosmological-settling-induce-world-structure](../../predicated-multiverse/questions/does-cosmological-settling-induce-world-structure.md), recorded on the multiverse side.

## Why this matters

The two predicated subtheories of cosmology should compose. If they do not — if a cosmological settling is structurally *not* a world in the nucleus sense, or a world in the nucleus sense systematically lacks settling data — then we have two independent predicated cosmologies, not one. The composition question is the load-bearing one for the unification of the framework.

## Status

Open. Recorded so the relationship is not silently lost; analysis lives in the multiverse-side dual question.
