**Stellar classification** is the assignment of stars to discrete spectral types on the basis of their absorption-line patterns. The dominant scheme is the **Morgan–Keenan (MK) system**, in which each star receives a spectral type letter (O, B, A, F, G, K, M for the main sequence, plus L, T, Y for brown dwarfs and W, S, C for chemically peculiar branches), a numerical subtype 0–9 (refining temperature within the letter), and a luminosity class in Roman numerals (I supergiants through V main sequence through VII white dwarfs).

The letter sequence O–B–A–F–G–K–M is, in essence, a temperature ordering: O stars are hottest (>30,000 K), M stars are coolest (~3,000 K). The historical letters (originally A–Q ordered by hydrogen-line strength, before Cannon and Pickering reordered them by temperature) are an accident; the temperature interpretation came later and gave the sequence its physical meaning.

Stellar classification is the discrete projection of the continuous parameter space (effective temperature, surface gravity, metallicity, rotation, age) onto a coarse grid that observers can apply consistently from a low-resolution spectrum. Modern surveys with millions of spectra (Gaia, SDSS, LAMOST, APOGEE) extract the underlying continuous parameters directly, but the MK classes remain the lingua franca for first-pass identification and for connecting historical literature to current data.
