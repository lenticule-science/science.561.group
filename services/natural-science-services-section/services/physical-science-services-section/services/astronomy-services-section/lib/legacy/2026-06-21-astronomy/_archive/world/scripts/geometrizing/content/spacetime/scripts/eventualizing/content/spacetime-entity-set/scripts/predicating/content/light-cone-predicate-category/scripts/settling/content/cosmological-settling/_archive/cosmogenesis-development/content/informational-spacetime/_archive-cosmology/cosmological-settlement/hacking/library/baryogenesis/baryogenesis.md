Baryogenesis is the (still unknown) process in the early universe that produced a small excess of matter over antimatter -- approximately one extra baryon per $10^9$ photons -- which survived after most matter and antimatter annihilated, leaving the matter-dominated universe we observe.

The puzzle: the laws of physics, as currently understood, are nearly symmetric between matter and antimatter.
The [Big Bang](./big-bang.md) should have produced equal quantities of both.
Equal quantities would have annihilated almost completely, leaving a universe of pure radiation with no [galaxies](../../structure/terms/galaxy.md), no stars, and no observers.
The observed universe contains negligible antimatter and abundant matter.
Something tipped the balance.

Andrei Sakharov identified in 1967 the three necessary conditions -- the **Sakharov conditions** -- for any physical process to generate a baryon asymmetry from a symmetric initial state:

1. **Baryon number violation.** Some interaction must change the number of baryons.
  Without this, equal numbers of baryons and antibaryons cannot become unequal.
2. **C and CP violation.** The process must distinguish matter from antimatter (charge conjugation symmetry violated) *and* it must distinguish a process from its mirror-image with antiparticles (CP symmetry violated).
  Without both, every baryon-creating process is matched by an equally fast antibaryon-creating one.
3. **Departure from thermal equilibrium.** In thermal equilibrium, forward and reverse rates are equal -- any asymmetry generated by one process is undone by its inverse.
  The asymmetry must be locked in by a non-equilibrium event.

The Standard Model of particle physics technically satisfies all three: baryon number is violated by electroweak sphalerons, CP is violated in the weak interaction, and the electroweak phase transition was out of equilibrium.
But quantitatively, the Standard Model produces an asymmetry many orders of magnitude smaller than observed.
New physics is required.

Leading candidate mechanisms include:

- **Electroweak baryogenesis.** A first-order electroweak phase transition (which the Standard Model alone does not give, but extensions can) generating asymmetry at the bubble walls.
- **Leptogenesis.** Heavy right-handed neutrinos decay asymmetrically, producing a lepton asymmetry that sphalerons partially convert to a baryon asymmetry.
- **GUT baryogenesis.** A grand-unified-theory phase transition at very high energy generates baryon asymmetry directly.

None has been confirmed.
Baryogenesis is one of the [five deepest open questions in cosmology](../../texts/open-questions-in-cosmology.md), and progress depends on physics beyond the Standard Model.
