# Cosmic time as settling bound

The role borne by [cosmic time](../../theories/evolution/terms/cosmic-time.md) when interpreted as the ordinal along which the [cosmological settling](../objects/cosmological-settling.md) accumulates evidence.
Cosmic time plays in predicated cosmology the role that [settling bound](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/settling-bound.md) plays in the parent [predication categories](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/_index.md) program: it is the index over which the family of [predication valuations](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-valuation.md) constituting the universe's evolving state is parameterized.

This identification is more than analogy.
The [knowledge order](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md) imposes monotonicity on the family: evidence accumulates, never retracts.
Cosmic time imposes the same monotonicity on the universe: events in the past of $t$ have already happened and are determinate; events in the future of $t$ have not yet happened and are not.
The two monotonicities are the same monotonicity, viewed from different vocabularies.
This is the content of [arrow-of-time-from-knowledge-monotonicity](../theorems/arrow-of-time-from-knowledge-monotonicity.md).

## What this implies for foundational cosmology

If cosmic time is a settling bound, then the question "why does cosmic time have a direction?" reduces to "why does the cosmological settling have a direction?"
The answer in the predication-categorical setting is structural: a settling is a $\leq_k$-monotone family, and monotonicity is in its definition.
The arrow of time is not extra physical content.
It is the orientation of the $\leq_k$ order on the value bilattice.

This does **not** dissolve the puzzle entirely.
It transposes it: the question becomes "why does the universe's state-evolution take the form of a settling, rather than some non-monotone alternative?"
That is now a question about the *initial condition* and the *closure rules* rather than about the time coordinate per se.
The Big Bang's low-entropy initial condition becomes "the [cosmological settling](../objects/cosmological-settling.md) starts at the bottom of $\leq_k$"; the laws of physics' compatibility with the second law of thermodynamics becomes "the closure rules are $\leq_k$-monotone."
Both claims are sharper than their ordinary-language predecessors.

## Bound type

Cosmic time is, in [Lambda-CDM](../../../terms/lambda-cdm-model.md), an ordinal of length the (unbounded) future plus a finite (currently $\sim 13.8$ Gyr) past.
The settling is not yet at quiescence — observable cells are still updating — but it is asymptotic: the late-time accelerating expansion sends most cells toward fixed values as their causal contact with the observer is severed.
