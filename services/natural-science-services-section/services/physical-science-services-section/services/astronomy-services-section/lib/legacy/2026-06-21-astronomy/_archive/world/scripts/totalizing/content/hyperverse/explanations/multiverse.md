The branch of cosmology that treats *the universe* as one element of a larger population rather than as the totality of what is. Multiverse cosmology is the empirical and structural study of: what other [worlds](terms/world.md) (universes, branches, regions) there might be; how they relate to ours; what mechanisms generate populations of them; and which of their features are accessible to us, even in principle.

Multiverse cosmology is **observationally weaker** than standard single-universe cosmology — most of its claims do not have direct empirical witnesses, and some are in principle untestable from inside any one universe. It is **structurally stronger**: it forces cosmologists to be explicit about which of the universe's features are necessary, which are contingent, and which depend on the boundary or initial conditions our universe happens to have drawn.

## What lives here

This subtheory collects the *empirical / classificatory* multiverse content. The *structural* (categorical, predication-theoretic) reformulation lives in the sibling [`predicated-multiverse`](../predicated-multiverse/_index.md) subtheory, which treats worlds as nuclei in a relational topos and asks what universal structure mediates between them.

- `terms/` — core multiverse vocabulary: [multiverse](terms/multiverse.md), [world](terms/world.md), [hyperverse](terms/hyperverse.md), [eternal inflation](terms/eternal-inflation.md), [Tegmark classification](terms/tegmark-classification.md).
- `texts/` — short orienting essays: [what is a multiverse](texts/what-is-a-multiverse.md).

## Boundary with the rest of cosmology

Multiverse content sits at the edge of cosmology. The neighboring subdomains are:

- [`evolution/`](../evolution/_index.md) — single-universe history (Big Bang, inflation, structure formation). Inflation is the most-studied generative mechanism for a multiverse, but it lives in `evolution/` because most of its cosmological work is done within one universe; only the [eternal inflation](terms/eternal-inflation.md) refinement is essentially multiversal.
- [`structure/`](../structure/_index.md) — what is in *our* universe. Multiverse classifications cite this for what counts as "a universe like ours."
- [`observation/`](../observation/_index.md) — methods. Multiverse hypotheses are mostly outside its current empirical reach; the texts here say so explicitly rather than smuggle in support.

## Status

Draft, narrow scope. This subdomain does not attempt to survey every multiverse proposal in the literature; it provides the vocabulary that the predicated-multiverse research subtheory needs, plus enough surrounding orientation to keep the categorical work honest about what is empirical and what is speculative.
