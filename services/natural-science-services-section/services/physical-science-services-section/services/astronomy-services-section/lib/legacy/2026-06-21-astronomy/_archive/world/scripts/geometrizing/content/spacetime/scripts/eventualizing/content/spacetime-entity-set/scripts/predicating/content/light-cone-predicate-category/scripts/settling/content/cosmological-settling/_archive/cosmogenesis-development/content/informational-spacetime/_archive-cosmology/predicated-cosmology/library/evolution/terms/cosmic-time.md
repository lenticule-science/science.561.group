Cosmic time is the time coordinate $t$ measured by an observer at rest with respect to the average matter distribution -- a **comoving observer**.
It is the natural time coordinate of the [Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric](./friedmann-equations.md), which describes a universe satisfying the [cosmological principle](../../../terms/cosmological-principle.md).

Comoving observers are those whose spatial coordinates do not change as the universe expands -- they sit at fixed positions on the comoving grid while the [scale factor](./scale-factor.md) $a(t)$ stretches the proper distances between them.
All comoving observers share a common cosmic time: their clocks tick at the same rate and read the same value at corresponding moments of cosmic history.
This is what makes "the universe is 13.8 billion years old" a meaningful statement -- it is the value of cosmic time at our present epoch, agreed on by every comoving observer.

Cosmic time exists only because the cosmological principle holds.
In a generic relativistic spacetime, there is no preferred time coordinate; observers in relative motion measure different times for the same events.
But in FLRW spacetime, the homogeneity of the matter distribution at each instant defines a preferred slicing -- the comoving slices -- and cosmic time is the proper time orthogonal to those slices.
Different comoving observers at different positions agree on what time it is because the universe looks the same to all of them.

Earth is **not** exactly comoving -- the Solar System moves at about 370 km/s relative to the cosmic microwave background rest frame -- but the correction to cosmic time is small, and we report ages of the universe in the comoving frame.

In a predicated cosmology, cosmic time plays the role of a [settling bound](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/settling-bound.md): it is the ordinal along which the universe's [predication settling](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) accumulates evidence.
The [arrow of cosmic time](./big-bang.md) -- past more determinate than future -- is the same monotonicity the [knowledge order](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md) imposes on settlings.
