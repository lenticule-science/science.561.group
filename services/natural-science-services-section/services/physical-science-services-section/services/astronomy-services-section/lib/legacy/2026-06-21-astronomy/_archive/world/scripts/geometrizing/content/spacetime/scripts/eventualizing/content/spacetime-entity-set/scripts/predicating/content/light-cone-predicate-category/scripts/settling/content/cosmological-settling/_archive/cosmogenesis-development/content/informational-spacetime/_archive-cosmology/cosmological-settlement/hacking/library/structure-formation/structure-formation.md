Structure formation is the process by which the universe evolved from the nearly uniform plasma observed in the [cosmic microwave background](./cosmic-microwave-background.md) into the present-day distribution of [galaxies](./galaxy.md), [galaxy clusters](./galaxy-cluster.md), and the [cosmic web](./cosmic-web.md).
It is the bridge between the universe at [recombination](../../evolution/terms/recombination.md) -- smooth to one part in $10^5$ -- and the universe today, where matter is concentrated into structures spanning fifteen orders of magnitude in scale.

The process is **hierarchical** in [Lambda-CDM](../../../terms/lambda-cdm-model.md): small structures form first, then merge into larger ones.
Tiny density perturbations imprinted in the early universe -- visible as temperature fluctuations in the CMB -- collapse under gravity, with [dark matter](./dark-matter.md) doing the bulk of the gravitational work.
Dark matter does not interact with photons and therefore began to clump as soon as it was non-relativistic, well before recombination released the [baryons](./baryonic-matter.md).
When the baryons were freed, they fell into the dark-matter potential wells already in place.
Stars formed inside the wells, galaxies formed around the wells, and galaxy clusters formed where wells aggregated.

The phases of structure formation, by epoch:

1. **Inflation ($\sim 10^{-32}$ s).** Quantum fluctuations stretched to cosmological scales, seeding the primordial density spectrum.
2. **Radiation era (until $\sim 50{,}000$ years).** Density perturbations on sub-horizon scales cannot grow because radiation pressure overwhelms gravity.
  Dark matter perturbations grow only logarithmically.
3. **Matter era ($\sim 50{,}000$ years to present).** Dark matter perturbations grow linearly with the [scale factor](../../evolution/terms/scale-factor.md).
  Baryons join after recombination.
4. **Nonlinear collapse ($z \sim 30$ onward).** Overdense regions decouple from cosmic expansion, collapse, virialize, and become the seeds of galaxies.
  The first stars form by $z \sim 20$.
5. **Hierarchical assembly ($z \sim 10$ to present).** Galaxies merge to form larger galaxies; galaxy groups merge to form clusters; the [cosmic web](./cosmic-web.md) of filaments and voids takes its present shape.
6. **Dark-energy era ($z < 0.7$).** Accelerating expansion suppresses the growth of further large-scale structure; the cosmic web is largely frozen at present.

The agreement between the predicted growth and observed structure -- in the CMB power spectrum, in galaxy surveys (SDSS, DESI, Euclid), and in the Lyman-alpha forest -- is one of the strongest tests of the standard cosmological model and of the existence of cold dark matter.
