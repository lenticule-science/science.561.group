The **main sequence** is the population of stars in the [HR diagram](hr-diagram.md) that derive their luminosity from hydrogen fusion in a non-degenerate core. It is by far the largest stellar population: about 90% of stars at any moment are on the main sequence, because the main-sequence phase is the longest-lived phase of stellar evolution.

A star's location along the main sequence is set almost entirely by its mass. More massive stars are hotter, more luminous, and shorter-lived (a 30 $M_\odot$ O star burns its hydrogen in a few million years; a 0.5 $M_\odot$ M dwarf will burn for trillions). The mass-luminosity relation $L \propto M^{3.5}$ (approximate, for solar-type stars) is one of stellar astronomy's most cleanly verified empirical laws.

The main sequence is structurally a quasi-stable equilibrium between gravitational contraction and the radiation pressure of nuclear burning. As core hydrogen depletes, the star leaves the main sequence — first to the subgiant branch, then to the red giant branch, with subsequent trajectory determined by mass. The point on the HR diagram at which a coeval stellar population (a cluster) leaves the main sequence is the cluster's **turnoff**, and its location dates the cluster: older clusters have turnoffs at lower masses (cooler, fainter), younger clusters at higher masses.
