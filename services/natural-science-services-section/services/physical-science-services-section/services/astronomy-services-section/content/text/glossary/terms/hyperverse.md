A non-standard cosmology term used in some research programs (including the ones from which this library inherits its predicated-multiverse content) to denote *the totality of worlds together with the structural medium in which they are embedded*.
A hyperverse is the [multiverse](multiverse.md) plus whatever inter-world fabric supports relations among its worlds.

The distinction from "multiverse" is small but real:

- A **multiverse** is a population of worlds; the relations among them are part of the picture but the term emphasizes the population.
- A **hyperverse** is the population *together with the medium*; the term emphasizes that the inter-world structure is itself an object of study.

In the categorical setting of [predicated-multiverse](_index.md), this distinction has a precise content: the hyperverse is the ambient category whose objects include both worlds and the [Wood Between the Worlds](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/predicated-agentic-orchestration-platform/library/relational-hyperverse-repository/library/wood-between-worlds.md) mediating object; the multiverse is the subcategory of worlds.

## Status

Niche term, not standard in the published cosmology literature.
It is included here because the predicated-multiverse research subtheory uses it; readers approaching from mainstream cosmology should treat *hyperverse* and *multiverse* as nearly synonymous, with the distinction above as a refinement when the inter-world structure itself is at issue.
