The **Hertzsprung–Russell diagram** (HR diagram) is the empirical scatter plot of stars in luminosity (or absolute magnitude) versus surface temperature (or color, or spectral type). It was constructed independently by Ejnar Hertzsprung (1908–1911) and Henry Norris Russell (1913) from the same observational input — magnitudes and spectra of nearby stars — and immediately revealed structure that no prior framework predicted: stars do not fill the diagram uniformly but cluster on a few well-defined sequences.

The principal features:

- **The main sequence** — a diagonal band running from hot, luminous, blue stars (upper-left) to cool, faint, red stars (lower-right), where stars spend most of their lifetimes burning hydrogen in their cores.
- **The giant branch and red giant clump** — a population to the upper-right of the main sequence, of cool, luminous stars that have exhausted core hydrogen.
- **The horizontal branch** — a roughly horizontal feature in metal-poor populations, of helium-core-burning stars.
- **The white dwarf cooling sequence** — a population at the lower-left, of small, hot, faint stellar remnants.

The HR diagram is the principal organizing chart of stellar astronomy. Its structure is a structural prediction of the equations of stellar structure (Eddington 1926, and subsequent stellar-evolution codes): the main sequence's slope, the location of the giant branch turnoff, and the white-dwarf sequence are all derivable from physics. The HR diagram is also a powerful diagnostic — fitting an observed cluster's HR diagram to model isochrones gives the cluster's age, distance, and metallicity in one operation.
