The August 2017 detection of the binary neutron-star merger GW170817 — observed essentially simultaneously by LIGO/Virgo (gravitational waves), Fermi/INTEGRAL (gamma-ray burst), and dozens of optical/infrared/UV/X-ray/radio facilities (the kilonova counterpart) — was the founding event of multimessenger astronomy. Joint analysis of all these observations gave constraints on the binary's masses, the equation of state of nuclear matter, the speed of gravitational waves, the host galaxy distance, and the production of heavy elements that no single channel could provide.

The standard analysis pipeline for such joint observations is built ad hoc per event: each instrument's data is reduced in its native pipeline, results are combined in a hierarchical Bayesian framework with hand-crafted likelihood couplings, and consistency between channels is checked by chi-square or evidence-ratio tests.

The structural question this raises is:

> **Q.** Is multimessenger fusion structurally a [bilattice meet](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md) of [observation cells](teaching/explanations/observation-cell.md) across heterogeneous [instrument fibers](../../../../services/cosmology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/instrument-fiber.md), with [calibration links](teaching/explanations/calibration-link.md) translating between the fibers' admissible-value sub-bilattices?

The hypothesis is that:

- Each detector channel (gravitational-wave strain, gamma-ray photon counts, optical light curve, neutrino events) lives in its own instrument fiber with its own admissible-value sub-bilattice.
- The standard "joint constraint" is the bilattice meet of the corresponding observation cells, after each is transported to a common reference frame via calibration links.
- The hand-crafted likelihood couplings of standard hierarchical Bayesian analyses are the practical realization of those calibration links, but their structural form is residuation in a residuated bilattice — the same object that organizes the [residuated sufficiency gap $g$](../../../../services/cosmology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sufficiency-gap.md) in predicated cosmology, here adapted to instruments rather than redshift bins.

If this is the right structural account, multimessenger fusion has a uniform formalism, calibration-link composition becomes auditable, and inconsistencies between channels become predicated *residuation failures* in the same sense as the [Hubble-tension](../../../../services/cosmology-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/hubble-tension-as-residuation-failure.md) — calibration-link failures across instruments rather than across cosmic-distance-ladder rungs.

What needs to be done:

1. Identify the bilattices used by each major class of multimessenger detector, along with their admissible-value sub-bilattices for each shared predicate (sky position, distance, time of arrival, polarization).
2. Construct calibration links between these bilattices for the cleanest test case (the GW170817 EM counterpart's joint sky localization).
3. Verify that the standard joint posterior reproduces, structurally, as the bilattice meet of the per-channel observation cells transported via the calibration links.
4. If (3) succeeds, generalize: state and prove a multimessenger fusion theorem analogous to the [selection-effect-as-fiber-restriction theorem](../../../../services/cosmology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/selection-effect-as-fiber-restriction.md), for the case of several instrument fibers contributing evidence about a single event.

This question connects predicated astronomy to predicated cosmology through a shared residuation apparatus and to predicated computing through shared questions about evidence fusion across heterogeneous data sources.
