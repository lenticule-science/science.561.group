The **radial-velocity method** detects [exoplanets](exoplanet.md) by measuring the periodic Doppler shift of the host star's spectral lines as the star orbits the common centre of mass with its (unseen) planet. A planet of mass $M_p$ in an orbit of period $P$ around a star of mass $M_\star$ induces a stellar reflex velocity of order $K \sim (2\pi G/P)^{1/3} M_p \sin i / M_\star^{2/3}$; precisely measuring $K$ from the line shifts gives a lower bound $M_p \sin i$ on the planet's mass.

The method requires high-resolution [spectroscopy](spectroscopy.md) and exquisite wavelength calibration. The first exoplanet around a main-sequence star (51 Peg b, Mayor and Queloz 1995) was a radial-velocity detection; subsequent instruments (HARPS, HIRES, HARPS-N, ESPRESSO) have pushed precision from tens of m/s down to ~10 cm/s, the level required to detect Earth-mass planets in habitable-zone orbits around Sun-like stars.

Radial-velocity is complementary to the [transit method](transit-method.md): transit measurements constrain the planet's radius but only a lower bound on mass via the geometric inclination ambiguity; combining a transit detection with a radial-velocity follow-up resolves the inclination, gives the true mass, and from radius+mass yields a mean density — the principal observable for distinguishing rocky planets from gas envelopes.
