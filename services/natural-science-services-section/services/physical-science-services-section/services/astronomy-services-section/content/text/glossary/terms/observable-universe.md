The observable universe is the spherical region of the universe centered on a given observer from which [light](../../../../services/cosmology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/light.md) (or any signal) could have reached the observer since the [Big Bang](big-bang.md).
Its boundary is the [particle horizon](particle-horizon.md).

For observers on Earth at the present epoch, the observable universe has a radius of about 46.5 billion light-years and contains an estimated $2 \times 10^{11}$ to $2 \times 10^{12}$ [galaxies](galaxy.md).
It is the entirety of what cosmology, as an empirical science, has direct evidence about.

The observable universe is **not** the universe.
The universe may be much larger -- spatially flat, extending without observable bound -- or even spatially infinite.
The [cosmological principle](../../../../services/cosmology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/cosmological-principle.md) extrapolates the observed homogeneity and isotropy of the observable universe to the universe as a whole, but this extrapolation is a postulate, not a measurement.

Each observer has their own observable universe.
Two observers separated by less than the particle horizon share most of theirs -- the great majority of galaxies one sees, the other can see too.
Two observers separated by more than the particle horizon share none of theirs at all: their observable universes are disjoint, and no measurement either makes can constrain the other's.

In predicated terms, the observable universe is the support of any observer's evidence about the cosmos.
It is the maximal region from which a [predication cell](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/predication-cell.md) about the universe -- any predicate, any subject, any object -- could carry causal evidence reaching the observer.
Beyond it, the observer is in a state of $\bot$ in the [knowledge order](../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md): told nothing.
