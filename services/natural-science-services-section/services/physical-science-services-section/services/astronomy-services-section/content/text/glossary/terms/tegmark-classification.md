A four-level taxonomy of [multiverse](multiverse.md) proposals introduced by Max Tegmark (2003, *Scientific American*; 2007, *Universe or Multiverse?*).
The classification orders proposals by *how much they require to be different about other [worlds](world.md)* relative to our own.

## The four levels

- **Level I — Beyond our cosmological horizon.** Other regions of the same spacetime, beyond our observable universe, with the same physical laws and constants.
  Content differs only because of different initial conditions and finite observable volume.
  The least controversial: all that is required is that space extends beyond what we can see.

- **Level II — Other post-inflationary bubbles.** Worlds with the same fundamental laws but different effective laws — different vacuum states, different effective constants, different particle content.
  Eternal inflation produces this naturally.
  The string-theory landscape provides candidate vacua to populate the bubbles.

- **Level III — Many-worlds (Everettian) branches.** Worlds corresponding to different branches of a universal quantum wavefunction.
  Same laws, same constants, different measurement outcomes.
  Whether Level III is "really" a multiverse or a re-description of single-universe quantum mechanics is interpretive.

- **Level IV — Mathematical universe hypothesis.** All consistent mathematical structures correspond to physically realized worlds.
  The strongest claim: not just different content within physics, but different physics altogether.
  Defended by Tegmark, contested by most working cosmologists.

## Use in this library

The classification is empirical and informal — it groups proposals by what they require, not by their structural similarity.
The [predicated-multiverse](_index.md) subtheory provides a structural picture (worlds as nuclei, the [Wood Between the Worlds](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/predicated-agentic-orchestration-platform/library/relational-hyperverse-repository/library/wood-between-worlds.md) mediating among them) that is in principle applicable to any of the four levels but does not commit to any of them.
Which level the predicated framing best models — and whether the framing forces a choice — is open.

Standard taxonomy in the multiverse-cosmology literature.
Cited here so subsequent entries can refer to "a Level II–style multiverse" or "a Level IV–style claim" without re-introducing the vocabulary each time.
