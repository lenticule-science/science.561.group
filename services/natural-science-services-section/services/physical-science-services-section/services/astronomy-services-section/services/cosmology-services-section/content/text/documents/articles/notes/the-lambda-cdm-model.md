Modern cosmology has a standard model, and it is embarrassingly successful.

## What the model says

The [Lambda-CDM model](../../../glossary/terms/lambda-cdm-model.md) describes a universe that:

- Began in a hot, dense state approximately 13.8 billion years ago (the [Big Bang](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/big-bang.md))
- Has been [expanding](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/cosmic-expansion.md) ever since, with the expansion now [accelerating](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/accelerating-expansion.md)
- Is spatially flat (the spatial geometry is Euclidean)
- Contains roughly 5% [baryonic matter](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/baryonic-matter.md), 27% cold [dark matter](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/dark-matter.md), and 68% [dark energy](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/dark-energy.md) (the cosmological constant $\Lambda$)
- Satisfies the [cosmological principle](../../../glossary/terms/cosmological-principle.md): homogeneous and isotropic on large scales

The dynamics are governed by the [Friedmann equations](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/friedmann-equations.md), which follow from [general relativity](../../../glossary/terms/general-relativity.md) applied to a homogeneous, isotropic universe. The model has six free parameters. Given these six numbers, it predicts:

- The angular power spectrum of the [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/cosmic-microwave-background.md)
- The distribution and clustering of [galaxies](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/galaxy.md)
- The abundances of light elements from [nucleosynthesis](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/nucleosynthesis.md)
- The expansion history and age of the universe
- The growth of [large-scale structure](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/cosmic-web.md) over time

## How well it works

The base model fits the Planck cosmic-microwave-background spectrum, large-scale structure, primordial abundance constraints and Type Ia-supernova distance relations remarkably well **under its stated assumptions**. These are independent observational probes, not proof of exact concordance or uniqueness. [Planck's parameter analysis](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833910) reports the base-model assumptions and the Hubble-constant discrepancy. The [DESI DR2 BAO result](https://doi.org/10.1103/tr6y-kpc6) finds a good flat-ΛCDM fit to BAO distances while noting CMB-parameter tension and conditional improvement from time-evolving dark-energy parametrizations. Different tests and model extensions do **not** automatically measure exactly the same parameters with identical systematics.

## What it cannot explain

Lambda-CDM is a parametric model, not a derivation from first principles. It fits the data, but it does not explain why the data look that way. The model has several deep problems:

**What is dark matter?** The model requires a non-baryonic matter component that makes up 27% of the universe. It specifies the gravitational behavior of this component but not its particle identity. Decades of direct-detection experiments have found nothing.

**What is dark energy?** The cosmological constant $\Lambda$ can be interpreted as vacuum energy, but quantum field theory predicts a value roughly $10^{120}$ times too large. This is the cosmological constant problem. Why $\Lambda$ has the observed value -- small but nonzero -- is perhaps the most extreme fine-tuning problem in physics.

**What happened at the very beginning?** The model describes the universe from the first fraction of a second onward, but it says nothing about the first instant. [Cosmic inflation](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/cosmic-inflation.md) addresses some of the puzzles (horizon problem, flatness problem), but inflation itself is a hypothesis, not a confirmed mechanism.

**Why is there matter at all?** The laws of physics (as we understand them) treat matter and antimatter nearly symmetrically. The universe should contain equal amounts of both, which would have annihilated into radiation. The observed baryon asymmetry -- the fact that we exist at all -- is not explained by Lambda-CDM.

## The Hubble tension

The [Hubble parameter](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/hubble-parameter.md) inferred from Planck CMB observations under flat base-ΛCDM is about **67.4 km/s/Mpc**, while several direct distance-ladder determinations are near **73 km/s/Mpc**. This is a **model- and calibration-dependent discrepancy**, not evidence that physical space is mathematically inconsistent. Alternatives include unrecognized systematic effects or extensions to the cosmological model; neither explanation is presently compelled by these two numbers alone. See the [Planck analysis](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833910) and [DESI DR2 scientific results](https://data.desi.lbl.gov/doc/papers/dr2/).

## Why this matters

Lambda-CDM is the most successful scientific model of the universe ever constructed. Its success is real and should not be diminished. But its success is also shallow: it tells us *what* the universe does without telling us *why*. Ninety-five percent of the content is dark. The initial conditions are imposed, not derived. The model works, but it leaves the deepest questions unanswered.
