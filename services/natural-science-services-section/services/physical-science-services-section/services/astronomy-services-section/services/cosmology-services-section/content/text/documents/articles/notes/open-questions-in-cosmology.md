The [ΛCDM model](../../../glossary/terms/lambda-cdm-model.md) explains a wide range of measurements under its explicit parameter and geometric assumptions. The following six questions are **representative** active research problems, not a mathematically proved ranking of depth or a complete list. A difference between observations or model fits is not by itself evidence of a new physical dimension, a failure of mathematics, or an established replacement theory.

## 1. What is dark matter?

[Dark matter](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/dark-matter.md) constitutes about 27% of the universe's energy content. Its gravitational effects are observed at every scale from individual galaxies to the cosmic microwave background. But its particle identity is unknown.

Leading candidates include weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs), axions, and sterile neutrinos. Direct detection experiments (LUX, XENON, PandaX) have progressively excluded large regions of the WIMP parameter space without a detection. Indirect searches (gamma rays from dark matter annihilation) and collider searches (production at the LHC) have also yielded null results.

Alternative explanations -- modified gravity theories (MOND, TeVeS) -- can fit galaxy rotation curves but struggle with the CMB and large-scale structure data. Particle dark matter remains a leading class of hypotheses; no specific candidate has been established by a direct detection. Modified-gravity alternatives remain active but face different constraints, so the observational claim (missing gravitating mass/effective dynamics) must be separated from its proposed carrier.

## 2. What is dark energy?

[Dark energy](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/dark-energy.md) constitutes about 68% of the universe and drives the [accelerating expansion](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/accelerating-expansion.md). The simplest model is the cosmological constant $\Lambda$ -- a fixed energy density of the vacuum.

The cosmological constant problem asks why the effective vacuum contribution has its observed magnitude. Naive high-energy cutoff estimates can exceed it by an enormous factor (often popularly summarized as $10^{120}$), but that factor is **not a unique prediction of quantum field theory independent of cutoff, renormalization and gravitational interpretation**.

Alternative models include quintessence (a dynamical scalar field whose energy density evolves over time) and modifications to [general relativity](../../../glossary/terms/general-relativity.md) at cosmological scales. The [DESI DR2 BAO constraints](https://doi.org/10.1103/tr6y-kpc6) and combinations with CMB/supernova datasets have strengthened interest in models with evolving equation of state, but the preference depends on model choice and data combinations. This is **not a discovery** that $w(z)$ varies. See the [2026 survey discussion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dark.2026.102268) and [NASA's current dark-energy overview](https://science.nasa.gov/dark-energy/).

## 3. What happened at the very beginning?

The hot-[Big Bang](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/big-bang.md) framework is quantitatively constrained by primordial nucleosynthesis and later CMB observations. Extrapolating to much earlier epochs requires increasingly uncertain assumptions; [inflation](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/cosmic-inflation.md) is a leading proposed account of the early accelerated phase, with no uniquely identified inflaton or established reheating mechanism.

More fundamentally: what, if anything, happened at $t = 0$? General relativity predicts a [singularity](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/singularity.md), but this is universally understood as a breakdown of the theory. A theory of quantum gravity is needed to describe the Planck epoch ($t \lesssim 10^{-43}$ seconds), and no complete theory exists.

## 4. Why is there more matter than antimatter?

The laws of physics as currently understood are nearly symmetric between matter and antimatter. The Big Bang should have produced equal amounts of both, which would have annihilated into radiation, leaving an empty universe. Instead, a small excess of matter survived -- roughly one baryon per billion photons. This is the baryon asymmetry.

The conditions for generating a baryon asymmetry were identified by Andrei Sakharov in 1967: baryon number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from thermal equilibrium. The known Standard Model sources of CP violation and electroweak transition dynamics do not account for the observed asymmetry in conventional electroweak-baryogenesis scenarios. Candidate explanations include leptogenesis and new scalar/CP sectors; neither mechanism nor a universally necessary extension is established by the asymmetry observation alone.

## 5. How does gravity work at the quantum scale?

General relativity is a classical theory. It does not incorporate quantum mechanics. At ordinary scales this is fine -- quantum effects are negligible for planets and stars. But at the Planck scale ($\sim 10^{-35}$ meters, $\sim 10^{-43}$ seconds), quantum gravitational effects should dominate. This regime is relevant for the [singularities](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/singularity.md) inside [black holes](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/black-hole.md) and the very earliest moments of the universe.

The [black hole information paradox](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/black-hole-information-paradox.md) is the sharpest manifestation of this problem: general relativity and quantum mechanics make contradictory predictions about what happens to information that falls into a black hole. The [holographic principle](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/holographic-principle.md) and the AdS/CFT correspondence provide partial answers, but a complete theory of quantum gravity -- one that reduces to general relativity at large scales and to quantum mechanics at small scales -- does not yet exist.

String theory, loop quantum gravity and other approaches are under active development. Candidate observational implications exist, but there is no widely accepted, decisive experimental discrimination of a complete quantum-gravity theory.

## 6. Are cosmological-parameter discrepancies systematic or new physics?

**The Hubble tension** compares a late-time, distance-ladder measurement
near 73 km/s/Mpc with the [Planck 2018 inferred
\(H_0=67.4\pm0.5\)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833910)
km/s/Mpc **under base flat ΛCDM**. This is a disagreement between
model-dependent inferences and calibrations—not a direct observation
that the Universe violates its own logic. The [2026
A&A analysis of line-of-sight
inhomogeneities](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202660282)
did not find its tested inhomogeneity corrections resolved the
tension.

Other disagreements, such as the clustering amplitude \(S_8\)
between weak-lensing and CMB probes, must be indexed by the
**specific dataset and estimator**. A
[June 2026 review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dark.2026.102286)
finds that the significance changes across surveys and the combined
CMB baseline. DESI DR2 and ACT DR6 also affect tests of
early-dark-energy interpretations
([2026 study](https://doi.org/10.1103/bx25-1g5d)).
The scientifically admissible question is which **joint model,
measurement pipeline and stated uncertainties** explains the
independently reproduced discrepancies best; no preferred
mathematical cosmology is entitled to the answer in advance.

## Why these questions matter

These questions interact, but **no theorem makes them one problem**. A quantum-gravity proposal may leave dark matter, baryogenesis and calibrated late-time expansion unexplained. Obsmetrologia's proposed kinematic/kinetic or chromatic-Lorentzian interpretation is a falsifiable mathematical research direction; it does not inherit empirical acceptance from the existence of the Hubble tension. Progress requires explicit forward models, unit-correct observational readouts, likelihood/calibration assumptions, and discrimination against standard cosmology and competitor models.
