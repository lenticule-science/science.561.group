# Does the cosmological settling induce world structure?

The sibling [predicated-cosmology](../../predicated-cosmology/_index.md) subtheory describes a single universe as a [cosmological settling](../../predicated-cosmology/objects/cosmological-settling.md) — a settling-monotone family of predication valuations on a spacetime entity set.
The predicated-multiverse subtheory describes worlds as [nuclei](world-as-nucleus.md) carrying quasicrystalline structure $\mathrm{QC}(W)$ and an internal flow category $\mathrm{Flow}(W)$.

The bridge question:

**Q.** Given a cosmological settling $v_{\mathrm{cos}} = (v_t)_{t \geq 0}$, is there a canonical extraction of $\mathrm{QC}$ and $\mathrm{Flow}$ data such that the settling becomes a single world-as-nucleus in the predicated-multiverse sense?

Three candidate extractions, none yet worked out:

1. **$\mathrm{Flow}$ from cosmic-time evolution.** The [cosmic-time-flow](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/cosmic-time-flow.md) morphism is a natural candidate for the time-like endomorphism $T_W$ required by world-condition (W2).
  Whether the full $\mathrm{Flow}(W)$ category — encoding *all* allowable internal transitions, not just cosmic-time advancement — has a canonical reading from the settling is open.

2. **$\mathrm{QC}$ from the predicate algebra.** The light-cone predicate category and its bilattice-valued evidence at any cosmic time form a structure that *might* satisfy quasicrystalline conditions (finite local complexity, aperiodicity) for sensible choices of "local complexity" on the predicate algebra.
  No proof or refutation exists.

3. **(W3) non-degeneracy.** The cosmological settling has structure everywhere it has values; (W3) demands no global degeneration of $\mathrm{QC}(W)$.
  Whether settling-thinness regions (e.g. asymptotic-future quiescent settlings) violate (W3) and so disqualify those settlings from being worlds is open.

## Why the question matters

This is the load-bearing bridge between the two predicated subtheories of cosmology.
If the bridge holds, predicated-cosmology and predicated-multiverse compose: a cosmological settling is a world; a multiverse is a family of cosmological settlings; the Wood mediates among them.
If the bridge fails — if cosmological settlings are systematically *not* worlds in the nucleus sense — then predicated-multiverse describes a different kind of universe than predicated-cosmology does, and the two subtheories are independent rather than composable.
