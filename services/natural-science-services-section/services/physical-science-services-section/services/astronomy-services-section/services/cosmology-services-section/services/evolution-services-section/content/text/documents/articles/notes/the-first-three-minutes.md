Run the expansion backward. The universe gets hotter and denser. At some point, it was hot enough for nuclear fusion -- not inside stars, but everywhere, all at once. This text traces the first few minutes of the universe, when the light elements were forged.

## Before nucleosynthesis

The standard [Big Bang](../terms/big-bang.md) model describes the universe as beginning in an extremely hot, dense state. The model does not describe the very first instant -- the "singularity" at $t = 0$ is a signal that [general relativity](../../../terms/general-relativity.md) breaks down. But from about $10^{-6}$ seconds onward, the physics is well understood.

At one microsecond: the universe is a quark-gluon plasma at trillions of kelvin. Quarks combine into protons and neutrons as the universe cools. At one second: the temperature is about 10 billion kelvin. Protons and neutrons exist in roughly a 7:1 ratio (protons are lighter and therefore favored). Neutrinos decouple from matter -- they stream freely through the universe from this point on.

## Nucleosynthesis: minutes 1-20

At about one minute, the temperature drops to about 1 billion kelvin. Now conditions allow nuclear fusion. Protons and neutrons begin combining:

1. A proton and a neutron form deuterium (heavy hydrogen).
2. Two deuterium nuclei fuse to form helium-4.
3. Small amounts of helium-3, lithium-7, and beryllium-7 are produced as side reactions.

By about 20 minutes, the universe has cooled below the threshold for fusion. The nuclear reactions freeze out. The result: roughly 75% hydrogen, 25% helium-4 by mass, with trace amounts of deuterium (~0.003%) and lithium-7 (~$10^{-10}$).

These are the primordial abundances. Every element heavier than lithium was produced later, inside [stars](../../structure/terms/star.md).

## Why the predictions matter

[Nucleosynthesis](../terms/nucleosynthesis.md) is one of the most precise tests of the Big Bang model. The predicted abundances depend on a single parameter: the density of [baryonic matter](../../structure/terms/baryonic-matter.md). The observed primordial deuterium abundance -- measured in high-redshift gas clouds that have undergone minimal chemical processing -- matches the prediction. The helium-4 abundance matches. The lithium abundance is slightly discrepant (the "cosmological lithium problem"), but the overall agreement is strong.

The baryon density inferred from nucleosynthesis ($\Omega_b \approx 0.049$) agrees with the completely independent measurement from the [cosmic microwave background](../../structure/terms/cosmic-microwave-background.md). Two different windows into the early universe -- one from the first minutes, one from 380,000 years later -- give the same answer.

## Why this matters

The title is borrowed from Steven Weinberg's 1977 book of the same name. Weinberg's point was that the first three minutes are the most consequential: the nuclear reactions that occurred in that brief window set the chemical composition of the universe for the next 13.8 billion years. Everything that followed -- the first stars, galaxies, planets, chemistry, life -- was built from the materials those three minutes produced.
