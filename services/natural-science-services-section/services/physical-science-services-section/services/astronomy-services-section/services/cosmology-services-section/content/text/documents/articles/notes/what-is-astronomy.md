Astronomy is the science of *what is up there*: stars, planets, galaxies, the gas and dust between them, the transient explosions and collisions that punctuate cosmic history, and the radiation those bodies emit and absorb. It is the oldest of the empirical sciences — calendar systems, navigation, and ritual time-keeping all rest on observational astronomy that predates writing — and it remains an observational science in a stronger sense than most: astronomers cannot perform controlled experiments on stars, only watch them and compare what they see to what their physical models predict.

What astronomy can do is collect light. Almost everything that is known about the universe outside the solar system has been learned from photons (and, recently, neutrinos and gravitational waves) that arrived at telescopes on or near Earth. The intricate machinery of [photometry](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/photometry.md), [spectroscopy](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/spectroscopy.md), polarimetry, and timing is the practical art by which information is extracted from those photons; the [HR diagram](../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/hr-diagram.md) and the cosmic distance ladder are theoretical scaffolds built on a long history of doing that extraction carefully.

Astronomy and [cosmology](../../cosmology/_index.md) are intertwined but distinct. Cosmology asks about the universe as a single object — its geometry, composition, history, fate. Astronomy asks about the bodies that fill the universe and the methods by which we know them. The line is fuzzy: stellar nucleosynthesis is astronomical and cosmological at once; supernova surveys are astronomy in execution and cosmology in interpretation. This library splits the entries by what is the *primary* concern of each — bodies and instruments under astronomy, bulk properties of the universe under cosmology — and cross-links freely.

A third closely-related field, *astrophysics*, is the application of physics to astronomical objects: stellar structure equations, accretion disk theory, plasma astrophysics, relativistic jets. Astrophysics is the explanatory engine for what astronomy observes; in this library it currently lives inside individual stellar/galactic/planetary subdomains rather than as a separate theory.
