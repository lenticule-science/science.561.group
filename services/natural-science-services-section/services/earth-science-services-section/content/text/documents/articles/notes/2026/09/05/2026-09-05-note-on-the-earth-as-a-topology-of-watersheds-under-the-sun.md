## 1. The watershed, plainly

A watershed is the land that drains to one outlet.
Its edge is the drainage divide, the ridge from which water goes one way or the other; its interior is the basin; its one point of exit is the outlet, where every drop of surface water that fell inside the divide passes on its way out.
Watersheds nest: a creek's basin lies inside a river's, which lies inside a larger river's, which lies inside the basin of the sea or of a closed lake with no outlet.
The United States Geological Survey's Watershed Boundary Dataset writes this nesting as the hydrologic unit code, an even number of digits, two per level, so that a code is the prefix of every code nested inside it (documented; the term [watershed](../../../../../../../../services/physical-geography-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/watershed.md) in physical geography now states it).
Two watersheds that share a divide share no water until their confluence, the point downstream where their two flows become one.

Three facts the corpus already holds reframe what a watershed is made of:

- **The river is mostly the aquifer.** Groundwater is 59 percent plus or minus 7 of global streamflow across 15,567 basins, and 58 percent plus or minus 34 of snowmelt runoff itself reaches the stream by way of groundwater first ([springs and headwaters](../../../../../../../../services/hydrology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-springs-and-headwaters.md), documented at search-synthesis confidence [@jasechko2024]). The visible basin is the presented surface of a settled store; a spring is where that store instantiates at a point, at its own temperature rather than the weather's ([SPRING](../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/settled-percolation-regime-instantiating-neighborhood-germs.md), documented).
- **The cycle is a helix, not a loop.** Water carries part of its past through each cycle ([the reflexive water cycle](../../../../../../../../services/hydrology-science-services-section/content/text/articles/teaching-articles/explanations/the-reflexive-water-cycle.md)); the forest calls its own rain, the paved city sheds it, and what falls carries the imprint of where it has been. A watershed is therefore a memory, not a pipe.
- **The sun is the only lift.** Water descends by gravity and ascends by one thing: the sun paying its latent heat, 2,260 kilojoules per kilogram, to lift pure water off the sea and off every leaf and leave every dissolved ion behind ([the world of the Tao and the hydrological circuit](../../../../../../../../../../../../services/engineering-science-services-section/services/computer-science-services-section/services/natural-computing-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-world-of-the-tao-and-the-hydrological-circuit.md) §3, documented). The three forcings are solar at roughly 173,000 terawatts, geothermal at 47, tidal at 3.7 ([the three forcings and the planetary tier](../../../../../../../../services/climate-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-three-forcings-and-the-planetary-tier.md), documented, adversarial verification pending). Everything a watershed does above its outlet is the sun's work descending; the basin's topography is the potential landscape that work descends through.

So the Earth, read hydrologically, is a partition (the divides), an order (drains-to), and a forcing (the sun), before it is any metric map.
Earth can be modeled as nested watersheds under solar influence. The rest of this note develops consequences of that model.

## 2. The topology is a causal order

Drains-to is a strict partial order on watersheds: irreflexive, transitive, and acyclic wherever water obeys gravity.
Read a point on a river as an event.
Its past cone is its catchment, every basin whose water can reach it; its future cone is its downstream reach, every basin it can reach on the way to its sink.
Two basins that share a divide are spacelike separated: no water passes between them, and nothing that happens in one can affect the other until their confluence, where two pasts become one future.
The sink (the ocean, or a closed basin) is the order's terminal object.

This is not a metaphor laid over the physics; it is the same mathematical object the corpus already keeps for time.
The light-cone reports the house holds (2026-07-28, science-services-section notes) state the causal-set slogan "order plus number equals geometry" and the theorems behind it: for a sufficiently distinguishing spacetime the causal order alone determines the topology and the conformal structure, and a counting measure restores the scale [@malament1977; @hawking1976] (documented, from those reports).
Read onto the basin: the drains-to order determines the nesting and adjacency of watersheds up to scale, and the area (the count of the basin) restores the scale.
The Watershed Boundary Dataset is, in this exact sense, a causal set whose events are basins (conjecture, stated so it can be checked against a real dataset: the falsifier is a delta or a bifurcation such as the Casiquiare, where one basin drains two ways and the order is no longer a forest).

This is also, literally, the house's own settling site.

Nested basins are an incidence poset of objects and their unions; the hydrologic unit code is its addressing; a section over a basin (a flow, a chemistry, a name) must agree on overlaps to glue into a section over the river.
A watershed fits the MUD model directly, not merely by analogy. It also provides a physical instance for a four-dimensional semiotic simulation, with each axis measurable through existing instruments.
