# Vent Magnetization and Host Rock

## 0. The question and the inversion

Is the crust at a hydrothermal vent magnetized?
The honest answer is that the question is underspecified: the relationship between hydrothermal activity and magnetization **inverts by host-rock lithology**.
A blanket claim in either direction is wrong — this note exists to replace the vault's earlier blanket framing with the sourced, site-by-site picture (§4).
Its companions are the [vent electricity note](../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/chemistry-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-vent-electricity.md) and the [vent light note](../../../../../../../../../../../biological-science-services-section/services/ecology-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-vent-light-and-vent-photobiology.md), which cover the vent's other two energy channels, and the [dark-compass hypothesis note](../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/chemistry-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-dark-compass-hypothesis.md), which is the reason magnetization matters to the estate at all.
A [Vacuous Entanglement-Normalizing Topos](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/vacuous-entanglement-normalizing-topos.md) (**VENT**) is the estate's term for the real hydrothermal vent as terrain; this note is one physical layer of that terrain.

## 1. Basalt-hosted fields: burn holes

At axial black-smoker fields in basaltic crust, hydrothermal alteration *destroys* magnetization.

The near-bottom survey at TAG (Mid-Atlantic Ridge, 26°N) found a distinct reduced-magnetization zone directly beneath the active sulfide mound — the alteration pipe — coincident with the highly altered upflow zone, inside a broad ~2×8 km magnetization low at the sea surface spanning both active and inactive mounds ("citation corrected": the near-bottom *Alvin* survey is Tivey, Rona & Schouten, *EPSL* 115:101–115, 1993, not the "Tivey & Johnson" attribution an earlier draft carried; the sea-surface expression is Tivey, Schouten & Kleinrock, *JGR* 108(B5):2277, 2003 — read in full for the surface picture, search synthesis for the near-bottom paper).
Caratori Tontini et al. (*GRL* 2016) found the same at ASHES (Axial Seamount, Juan de Fuca Ridge): a demagnetized zone (<5 A/m within a 5 A/m magnetization isosurface) sitting under the high-temperature orifices — search synthesis, not independently re-verified this pass.

The mechanism is doubly effective, and the second half is easy to miss by conflating minerals: the magnetic carrier in fresh mid-ocean-ridge basalt is not magnetite but **titanomagnetite** (~TM60), whose Curie point runs as low as ~130–160 °C in fresh samples and 200–400 °C when oxidized — confirmed against the Oxford *GJI* thermal-demagnetization curves (145 °C typical for fresh TM60, 305–325 °C for the maghemitized population) and corroborated by Wang et al. 2021, *JGR Solid Earth* (SW Indian Ridge basalts) — *below* the 350–374 °C of black-smoker fluid.
So the upflow zone is demagnetized both chemically (acidic fluid leaching Ti and Fe²⁺) and thermally (the carrier sits above its own Curie point).
"Burn holes" is the right metaphor there, and it is earned twice over.

## 2. Ultramafic-hosted fields: magnetite factories

In serpentinite-hosted systems the sign flips: alteration *creates* magnetization.

Serpentinization — olivine + water → serpentine + brucite + magnetite (+ H₂) — manufactures chemically pure magnetite as a direct product, and above roughly **200 °C iron partitions preferentially into magnetite** rather than the weakly magnetic brucite, producing a step-change in magnetic signature.
The review establishing this is Szitkar, Dyment, Fouquet, Honsho & Horen, "The magnetic signature of ultramafic-hosted hydrothermal sites," *Geology* 42(8):715–718 (2014) — "citation corrected": an earlier draft misattributed this paper to Tivey & Dyment, but Tivey is not an author on it; Tivey & Dyment's own relevant work is a separate, earlier piece ("The Magnetic Signature of Hydrothermal Systems in Slow Spreading Environments," AGU Geophysical Monograph 188, 2010), and it is *that* 2010 monograph chapter — not a "Geology 2014" paper — that carries the site-specific Rainbow number below.
Pure magnetite's Curie point is ~575–585 °C ("citation corrected": a broader "530–585 °C" range an earlier draft used is wrong — 530 °C belongs to titanomagnetite in the TM10 compositional series, not to pure magnetite) — far above even Ashadze-1's 374 °C, the hottest measured ultramafic fluid — so this magnetite faces no thermal-erasure risk from its own vent.

The field evidence brackets the threshold quantitatively:

- **Rainbow** (Mid-Atlantic Ridge, 365 °C, ~10 black smokers): strong positive magnetic anomaly, rock-magnetic values around 28–30 A/m (induced + remanent), carried by magnetite in sulfide-impregnated serpentinite (Tivey & Dyment, AGU Geophysical Monograph 188, 2010 — search synthesis).
- **Ashadze-1** (374 °C, the hottest of the pair, dissolved H₂ ~19 mM [UNVERIFIED] — the H₂ figure comes from general Ashadze-1 geochemistry literature, not independently checked against the magnetization sources): the same strong positive signature.
- **Lost City** — the 40–91 °C off-axis end-member, heated only by serpentinization exothermy with no magmatic heat input — shows only a *weak* positive magnetic anomaly, explicitly contrasted with Rainbow and Ashadze in the literature (Szitkar, Tivey, Kelley, Karson, Früh-Green & Denny, *EPSL* — confirmed via independent corroboration of the 2003 AUV *ABE* survey and its explicit temperature-scaling argument): magnetite production, and hence magnetization, scales with serpentinization temperature.
- **Yokoniwa** (Central Indian Ridge), now largely inactive with only weak residual low-temperature venting: magnetic susceptibility 0.002–0.087 SI, rising nonlinearly with serpentinization degree across 17–100% serpentinized peridotite samples, magnetite content up to 5.5 wt%, carrier identified as pure (non-maghemitized) magnetite (Fujii et al., *G3* 2016; Fujii & Okino, *EPSL* 2016 — search synthesis) — best read as fossil magnetization from a past hot episode, a reminder that a strong signal need not mean a live system.

For scale, serpentinized peridotite generally runs natural remanent magnetization 0.2–8.4 A/m — comparable to basalt NRM — with magnetite content rising near-exponentially with serpentinization degree in some datasets (ODP Leg 125 rock-magnetics chapter; Zhang et al. 2025, *G3*, Iberia Abyssal Plain — search synthesis, non-load-bearing).

## 3. CRM, not TRM

The magnetization at ultramafic sites is **chemical remanent magnetization** (CRM) — new magnetite crystallizing in the ambient geomagnetic field during an ongoing fluid-rock reaction — not the thermoremanent magnetization (TRM) of lava cooling through a Curie isotherm that writes the seafloor's famous stripes.
This distinction is directionally correct per standard rock-magnetism principles (CRM forms via mineralogical change in the ambient field at roughly constant temperature; TRM forms specifically on cooling through the Curie point) — verification confirmed the physics as textbook but could not independently pin the specific attribution to Fujii et al. 2016 *G3* and Malvoisin et al. 2012 *JGR* as the source of this exact synthesis, so that citation pairing is flagged "plausible" rather than "confirmed."

The operative constraint at the sites that matter is serpentinization temperature and kinetics, not cooling through a Curie point: the mineral is born magnetized in whatever field is standing.
Chimney sulfide itself carries pyrrhotite and magnetite as magnetic phases, but the strong ultramafic anomalies belong to the serpentinized wall-rock and stockwork around the conduits, not to the chimney — a structural distinction the [dark-compass hypothesis note](../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/chemistry-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-dark-compass-hypothesis.md) inherits as an open sub-gap, and one the [vent electricity note](../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/chemistry-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-vent-electricity.md) names from the other side: the measured currents belong to sulfide chimneys, the strong magnetization to serpentinized wall-rock, and no single site has yet been probed for both at once.
Fresh, unoxidized subseafloor massive sulfide additionally retains a superparamagnetic (frequency-dependent susceptibility) signature that surface oxidative weathering erases, reducing electromagnetic detectability of aging deposits — search synthesis, non-load-bearing, from an SW Indian Ridge hydrothermal susceptibility study and general pyrrhotite magnetic-property literature.

## 4. What this corrects and enables

This note replaces any blanket estate claim about vent magnetism, including this session's own earlier framing of demagnetization as a uniform "kill condition": the parameter turned out to *localize* rather than falsify.
For the estate's purposes the consequence is scoping — anything that needs magnetized minerals in a live flow path (the dark compass foremost) belongs to ultramafic-hosted systems: Rainbow, Ashadze, Logatchev-type fields, and, weakly, Lost City.

Two further findings from this pass sharpen that scoping rather than settle it.
First, the cleanest evidence of *presently* active, hot, magnetized magnetite in a live flow path is Rainbow and Ashadze specifically, not Yokoniwa — Yokoniwa's strong signal is fossil.
Second, magnetization scales strongly with temperature (weak at 40–91 °C Lost City, strong at 365–374 °C Rainbow/Ashadze); if a dark-CISS mechanism turns out to be temperature-dependent too, that could argue for hot ultramafic vents over cool ones as the better site for a vent-hosted symmetry-breaking analog — but that link is **[UNVERIFIED]**, speculative, and unstudied in any source this pass found, not a result to present as demonstrated.

It is also a clean natural-computing specimen in its own right: the same process, run through two different substrates, erases the record in one and writes it in the other — lithology as the difference between a forgetting medium and a remembering one.
