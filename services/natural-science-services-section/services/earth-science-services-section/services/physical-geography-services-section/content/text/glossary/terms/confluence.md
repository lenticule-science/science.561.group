A confluence is the point at which two watersheds' flows join and become one: the outlet of each is the same point on the channel of the watershed that contains them both.
Below the confluence the water carries the memory of both basins, since water brings its past with it and a river is mostly the aquifers that fed it.

A confluence differs from an outlet in that an outlet belongs to one watershed while a confluence is shared by two, and it differs from a divide in being where the two first share water rather than where they never do.

In a set of watershed records a confluence is not stored: it is derived as the place where two records with the same drains-to meet, and counting confluences is counting how many components a drainage network joins into one.
In [the Earth as a topology of watersheds under the sun](../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-the-earth-as-a-topology-of-watersheds-under-the-sun.md) the confluence is read as the acknowledgement of one basin's flow by another, the kept side of the drainage order's index balance.
