# Two introduction pathways relevant to manoomin: introduced *Zizania latifolia* and the *Ustilago esculenta* food question

**Working paper v0.1, 11 October 2026. This is a targeted research synthesis, not a measured probability model, independent scientific receiving, peer review, or a current federal import ruling.** Companion to [the native-host evidence assessment](2026-10-11-ustilago-esculenta-host-records-working-paper.md) and the [source-owned evidence register](../../../manoomin-jiaobai-ustilago-esculenta-evidence.md).

## Abstract

**Purpose.** Debate over grocery-store jiaobai mixes two different biological hazards: invasion by the East Asian aquatic perennial *Zizania latifolia*, and movement of its associated fungus *Ustilago esculenta* into native North American wild rice. Their causal requirements, evidence, and preventive responses differ.

**Evidence.** The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's 2025 Ecological Risk Screening Summary classifies *Z. latifolia* as High Risk based on an established history of invasiveness and broad U.S. climate match. EPPO's 2024 synthesis documents established introduced populations and wetland effects. California's 1991 and 2017 accounts confirm *U. esculenta* associated with introduced *Z. latifolia* plants in the United States. Tu et al. (2019) recovered culturable fungus from five harvested edible galls. Zhang et al. (2017) compared strains from edible white and spore-rich gray galls and found MT-type isolates had reduced reproductive and infection capacities under specific experiments; the T-type isolates had a more complete life cycle. None of these studies identified an infected native North American wild-rice bed or evaluated viable infectious material in an actual imported retail consignment.

**Interpretation.** Introduced live plants or viable propagules and postharvest edible stems are different commodities with different exposure pathways. A high plant-invasion screening rank does not prove a fungal host switch, and cultured fungus in food tissue does not establish transmissibility after shipping. Commodity-specific APHIS requirements must be established from a current official source before a named seller's conduct can be called a violation. Indigenous stewardship already conducts real disease and ecological work; new scientific evidence should support, not displace, those institutions.

**Contribution.** We specify observable causal gates, distinguish the strongest supporting and limiting evidence at each gate, and identify information that would change decisions without inventing an aggregate numerical risk score.

## 1. What is being assessed?

North American wild rice (including *Z. palustris* and *Z. aquatica*) is a seed-producing aquatic annual and a culturally central resource for Anishinaabe and other nations. The Asian *Z. latifolia* is a different, perennial grass. Its swollen stem, harvested as jiaobai, is produced in association with *U. esculenta*. Calling both plants “wild rice” obscures different life histories, reproductive systems, distribution histories, and threats.

We distinguish **Path A, establishment of the introduced *plant***, from **Path B, infection arising from associated fungal material transported in an edible commodity**. A third question, **Path C, movement of already infected living plants or rhizomes**, couples A and B. Each pathway can be assessed separately and may require different plant-health rules. No source examined here establishes that the 2026 Reddit observations are correctly identified imports from China, let alone that the products contained a strain able to infect an American native host.

The research question is not whether all possibility can be excluded. It is **which parts of each pathway have actually been observed, and what evidence is required before the next causal claim follows**.

## 2. Methods and evidentiary classes

This is a **targeted source-critical desk synthesis** completed on 11 October 2026. It draws on first-party federal and California assessments, international plant-pest summaries, publicly available primary biological research, and current public descriptions of Tribal-directed manoomin disease work. It is not a systematic literature review. No new specimens, cultures, inoculations, field observations, commercial purchase or numerical model have been generated.

Sources were kept in five distinguishable classes: **direct experimental evidence** (with named isolates, strains, plant hosts and trial conditions), **agency occurrence accounts** (identified event, species and technique), **agency risk screening** (assessment, extrapolation and assumptions), **regulatory process guidance** (where a current rule is obtained), and **anecdotal reports** (observations not independently checked by this research). A publication or commentary citing a primary study is not counted as another independent event.

A source can be strong for one question but silent for another. Fungal culture from harvested galls is primary evidence for fungal recovery; it is not a transport-challenge experiment. A global invasion synthesis is relevant to potential plant spread; it cannot act as a diagnostic assay in a manoomin bed. Historical regulatory text informs earlier status but cannot automatically replace an APHIS commodity rule in October 2026. The supporting [JSON evidence extract and tests](../../../../../../../../../data/manoomin-evidence.json) keep direct evidence, secondary assessments, reported sightings and unresolved questions distinguishable for downstream editorial use. No machine-generated status constitutes independent scientific acceptance.

## 3. Path A — introduction and establishment of the Asian aquatic grass

### 3.1 Observed invasive history

The 2024 European and Mediterranean Plant Protection Organization (EPPO) account summarizes *Z. latifolia* populations established in parts of New Zealand and other non-native settings, including Hawaii and a British Columbia locality [EPPO 2024]. The plant can spread vegetatively and form persistent, dense stands, with reported effects on freshwater and riparian vegetation. These are effects of the **plant**. They do not imply that every introduced population has *U. esculenta* or that the fungus can infect native American annual wild rice.

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service independently published an **April 2025 ecological risk screen** classifying the species as **High Risk** to the contiguous United States [USFWS 2025]. The agency assessed a history of invasive behavior as high and described a medium-to-high climate match across much of the contiguous country. The screening category is a reason for competent preventional evaluation; it is not a claim of occurrence at a specific rice bed, measured annual invasion probability, or proof that food imports violate federal law.

The two agency syntheses overlap in their underlying occurrence literature. **Concordant summaries are not independent field replications.** Estimating probability or magnitude of loss at an individual wetland would require habitat, hydrology, propagule pressure, introductions and management context. This targeted review lacks those measurements.

### 3.2 Causal gates

An establishment case would require **a correct viable plant identification**, **a vector transporting seed/rhizome or another propagule**, **release to a suitable wetland**, **survival**, **persistence/spread**, and potentially **displacement or interference** with native *Zizania*. Living material for planting, aquatic ornamentals, discarded rhizomes and untreated vegetation move through quite different chains from a trimmed edible stem.

No reviewed record assigns a specific retailer's harvested jiaobai product to a viable plant-introduction event. It is inappropriate to infer that someone selling cut food is importing live propagules, just as it is inappropriate to infer that an imported aquatic ornamental poses no environmental hazard because its genus appears in a food store.

For immediate prevention, the relevant evidence includes **actual planted or growing *Z. latifolia*** independently identified by qualified observers and the ordinary regulatory/land-stewardship channels through which introduced aquatics are handled. Reports from outside an authorized Tribal monitoring program must not expose protected bed locations.

## 4. Path B — edible jiaobai as a possible fungal transport pathway

### 4.1 The original host association and food product

Cultivated jiaobai is not a seed crop. *U. esculenta* colonizes the Asian perennial and promotes gall development in swollen culms. Harvested white galls are food; gray or dark galls contain more mature fungal reproductive structures and are generally not desirable for consumption [Chigira 2023]. The distinction between white/gray galls matters biologically, but gross appearance is not a validated phytosanitary inspection method.

**There are at least three distinct meanings of “fungus is present”:** DNA or hyphae detected in tissue, live organisms recoverable in a culture, and a life stage competent to establish a new infection. None of those meanings is equivalent to identifying the host range after an entire food distribution chain.

Tu and colleagues (2019) sampled **five galls at different stages of swelling** and used culture, sequence identification and molecular profiling. They recovered several strains of *U. esculenta* along with other fungi and bacteria; the authors noted that their limited sample size precluded conclusive generalization. Their experiment supports viable recoverable fungus **at sampled harvest-stage tissue**. It does not measure prevalence across Chinese commodity lots, identity of organisms in a present U.S. supermarket, survival during refrigerated/frozen transport, or release into native-water ecosystems.

### 4.2 The crucial MT-type versus T-type distinction

Zhang and colleagues (2017) compared paired isolates from edible white (MT-type) and spore-rich gray (T-type) galls on the cultivar Longjiao 2 of Asian *Z. latifolia*. Under their laboratory/greenhouse assay conditions, the **MT isolates had reduced teliospore formation and germination, delayed or weaker growth, and an infection defect**. The tested T isolates retained a more complete infection cycle. The authors' genome comparisons support biological differentiation between the strain types [Zhang 2017]. Chigira's 2023 work reports differences in sorus formation between edible white and gray galls, consistent with this distinction.

These results cut against a simplistic claim that **every edible white jiaobai stem contains abundant competent infectious spores**. They do not justify the reverse categorical claim that edible white galls are sterile or categorically safe: hyphae can remain in edible tissue; strains vary; the tested isolates and Asian host are a restricted experimental domain. The 2017 study did **not** infect American *Z. palustris*, *Z. aquatica* or *Z. texana*, and no current U.S. food consignment was sampled.

The experiments therefore constrain the mechanism and the kind of evidence needed. An assay of fungal DNA alone is insufficient for a claim about viable infective stages. Culture of harvested tissue is stronger for viability, but it still cannot substitute for strain characterization, transport survival and a competent-host diagnosis. We have not located credible independent evidence that those later steps occurred through the currently alleged retail pathway.

### 4.3 Conditional chain, not a fabricated probability

Define events **M** (material is correctly identified as a particular edible commodity and origin), **V** (a competent viable fungal stage survives processing and travel), **X** (the material reaches an environment permitting exposure), **H** (the native host species is susceptible to the actual strain), and **D** (infection is correctly diagnosed in that native host).

An observation of viable cells at harvest supports neither **V** for a present import nor **H** in a North American native host. Formally, an inference from M to D would require evidence for each conditional relationship. Multiplying guessed proportions or quoting a single organism-positive sample as an outbreak rate would give a misleading quantitative risk estimate. Neither a point probability nor a confidence interval for native-bed infection is justified by this review.

The competing explanations also matter. Retail jiaobai may be correctly identified but processed in a way that eliminates infectivity; a domestic listing may not represent a foreign-origin import; a fungal culture may be a strain with little capacity to establish new infections; spores might persist but never reach a relevant wetland; native species may be incompatible; or a field symptom might arise from an entirely different fungus. These possibilities are not established explanations. They are alternatives that determine what evidence would genuinely discriminate the risk.

## 5. Path C — infected living plants and propagation

California officials documented an infected **introduced planting** of *Z. latifolia* near Modesto in 1991, with infected gall tissue and dark sori, and described eradication [Watson 1991]. A 2017 CDFA account described detection of *U. esculenta* in *Z. latifolia* obtained in Riverside County by molecular identification [CDFA 2017]. These are real U.S. records involving **introduced host plants**, not confirmed infection of native manoomin beds.

A live infected rhizome or planting can carry both the introduced plant and its fungus, making plant survival, vegetative spread and fungal persistence more tightly coupled than in a harvested retail vegetable. Yet the cited incidents themselves are historical. They do not establish that the same plants persisted through 2026 or that there is ongoing domestic spread.

Current U.S. requirements distinguish plants for planting from cut or processed food, and change by country of origin and pest conditions [APHIS 2026]. A 2018 California assessment stated that stems were prohibited at that time [CDFA 2018], but a state agency's historical remark is not enough to identify today's operative federal ACIR entry for each commodity. **The relevant research result here is that a ruling is missing from our record—not that retailers are violating an identified rule.**

## 6. Compare the pathways without collapsing them

| Question | Introduced plant (A) | Harvested-food fungus (B) | Infected living plant (C) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is there direct biological/agency evidence of the mechanism? | Yes: invasive populations and U.S. federal High Risk plant screening | Partial: viable culture in harvested galls, tested strain differences; post-shipment infectious stage unmeasured | Yes: historical introduced infected plant detections; ongoing spread not established |
| Are current U.S. retail reports independently confirmed? | Not as viable introductions in this review | No shipment-specific viable/pathogen determination | No linkage to live imported material shown |
| Does the mechanism depend on infecting native *Zizania*? | **No**; plant competes regardless of fungal host switching | **Yes**, for the stated native-host fungal disease outcome | **No** for plant invasion; **yes** for native fungal disease |
| What would change the conclusion? | Verified living introduced plant at a new site, with competent habitat assessment | Assayed imported consignment and qualified fungal/pathway/host evidence | Verified current propagative consignment and competent host/plant diagnosis |
| What claim is not justified? | That any retail edible stem establishes the perennial plant | That a harvested fungal culture proves native rice infection | That a 1991 or 2017 introduced-host detection proves a present outbreak |

### 6.1 Decisions under asymmetric uncertainty

A harmful introduction may have serious cultural and ecological consequences even before a measured probability is available. Avoiding unauthorized movement of suspect live material, documenting independently witnessed products without purchasing samples, and asking APHIS for current import terms are ordinary proportionate steps. Nothing about these precautions requires claiming an established native outbreak or naming sellers as lawbreakers.

This is not evidence that public alarm campaigns, mass reports, ad hoc culturing, or unsolicited field access are effective protective measures. Those actions can waste authority capacity, contaminate evidence, disclose private locations, or stigmatize communities selling familiar foods. The research supports **specific, accountable observations to the competent authority**, not a crowd-sourced guilt list.

### 6.2 Stewardship already exists

Wisconsin DATCP documents a 2025 manoomin disease survey carried out with the Wisconsin Tribal Conservation Advisory Council. Tribal participants submitted specimens and the state laboratory supported diagnosis of **named other pathogens**, including brown-spot disease. This account does not claim the survey tested for *U. esculenta*; it is neither a positive detection nor a relevant negative [DATCP]. The existing network demonstrates actual Tribal-led scientific capacity. An outside research team should not invent a parallel surveillance jurisdiction, imply tribal sponsorship, or publish protected rice-bed locations.

A source-critical literature paper can be written without access to Tribal ecological knowledge. Any future collection, diagnosis or community-facing research must be scoped with the appropriate nations and research stewards, honoring provenance, consent and restricted knowledge rather than treating public interest as blanket permission.

The table below distinguishes *what would be learned* from a future evidence source; it is not a prescription for uncredentialed experiments.

## 8. Limitations, scientific receiving and public interpretation

The investigation has not completed exhaustive primary-literature searches, independent source screening, field diagnostics, phylogenetic analysis, retail sampling, experimental transport viability tests, quantitative host-range estimates, or official federal trade adjudication. The two FWS/EPPO plant risk summaries may draw on overlapping source populations. The Tu 2019 study's five samples and Zhang 2017's selected strain pairs are **not representative probability samples of contemporary commerce or all fungal strains**. No frequency, total-risk number, or false-negative estimate is reported. The 1911 greenhouse specimen, addressed as a secondary question in the [companion paper](2026-10-11-ustilago-esculenta-host-records-working-paper.md), does not resolve these contemporary pathway gaps.

For a future formal evidence map, researchers must log databases, all dated query strings, search limits, eligibility criteria, screened records, duplicates and exclusions; retrieve complete experimental methods; assess internal validity and causal transportability; and bring findings to an independent, competent scientific receiver. Current source text and data are a **working paper** and must not be marketed as independently accepted scholarship, peer-reviewed evidence of native infection or a legal ruling.

Koios.News can investigate verified primary developments with its own editorial review, including the difference between tested MT/T strains and actual retail exposure. A Civics ASSIST feature could eventually route accurate firsthand observations to the correct existing regulators; accusations of illegality require the applicable legal rule and responsible determination. **A paper is evidence for such services, not a substitute for their acceptance or delivery.**
