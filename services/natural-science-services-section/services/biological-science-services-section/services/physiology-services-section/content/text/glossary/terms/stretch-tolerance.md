Stretch tolerance is the nervous system's adaptation to sustained or repeated stretching that gradually permits greater range of motion without increasing tissue extensibility in the mechanical sense. The phenomenon reflects a shift in the nervous system's perception of end-range position and a habituation of the protective reflex response that normally constrains muscle lengthening. When a muscle is held in a static stretch, nociceptors and proprioceptors in the muscle and connective tissue signal the nervous system that the tissue is at its perceived limit. The nervous system interprets this signal and produces protective responses: the stretch reflex (reflex contraction to resist lengthening) and a sensation of resistance or discomfort. However, with sustained stretching over seconds to minutes, the nervous system adapts: the reflex response diminishes, the perception of end-range shifts to greater length, and the sensation of resistance decreases. This is stretch tolerance—the body's acceptance of greater length, mediated primarily through nervous system adaptation rather than through tissue remodeling. Long-term stretching programs produce both nervous system adaptation (which accounts for rapid initial flexibility gains) and tissue-level changes (which account for flexibility gains that accumulate over weeks to months). Stretch tolerance is the acute and subacute component that allows the body to access greater length immediately and in the days following a stretching session. The distinction matters because stretch tolerance is reversible (the nervous system's adaptation can recede within days if stretching ceases) while tissue-level adaptation is more durable. Flexibility is the measurable range of motion around a joint. Stretch tolerance is the nervous system mechanism that permits accessing that range. A person may have adequate flexibility (passive range of motion is sufficient for their activities) but limited stretch tolerance (they cannot maintain stretched positions comfortably), or vice versa (their tissues are tight but they have learned to tolerate the sensations of stretching).
