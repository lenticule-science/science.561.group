Allostatic load is Bruce McEwen's term for the cumulative physiological cost of repeated or chronic allostatic adjustment — the wear produced when a system built to anticipate and respond to demand is made to run that response too often, too intensely, or for too long. McEwen and Eliot Stellar introduced the term in 1993 as the price side of the allostasis ledger: adaptive in the short run, allostatic responses become damaging when the environment keeps signaling demand that never resolves. When the mismatch between demand and the organism's coping capacity becomes severe, the state is sometimes further distinguished as allostatic overload. The concept has been operationalized as a composite biomarker score, typically summing indicators across the cardiovascular, metabolic, immune, and neuroendocrine systems — cortisol, blood pressure, waist-to-hip ratio, C-reactive protein, HbA1c, and similar measures — with a higher composite meant to indicate greater cumulative wear.

The construct's clinical usefulness is well established, but its measurement is currently in genuine dispute, and this should be stated plainly rather than folded into general enthusiasm for the concept. A 2021 systematic review by Jenny Guidi, Marcella Lucente, Nicoletta Sonino, and Giovanni Fava found allostatic load clinically relevant but documented wide inconsistency across studies in which biomarkers are selected and how they are scored — there is no single agreed instrument, and "allostatic load" in one paper is not always measuring the same thing as in another. A 2023 individual-participant-data meta-analysis spanning 13 cohorts, roughly 67,000 people, and 40 biomarkers across 12 physiological systems found that a minimal five-marker index — C-reactive protein, resting heart rate, HDL cholesterol, waist-to-hip ratio, and HbA1c — predicts mortality about as well as far more elaborate composites. That result cuts two ways at once: it rescues the construct's predictive validity while undermining the stronger claim that allostatic load measures one coherent, multi-system quantity rather than a small number of metabolic and inflammatory markers doing most of the work under an ambitious name.

The practical consequence is that allostatic load should be treated as a useful summary statistic with contested internal structure, not as a validated unified biomarker of "the stress tax." Claims built on allostatic load composites are on firmer ground when they specify which systems are implicated — glucocorticoid signaling to prefrontal cortex, inflammatory markers, metabolic dysregulation — than when they cite a single aggregate score as though it transparently measured cumulative harm.

For the estate's relational and attention-economic framework, this caveat matters directly: theories that describe chronic precarity or compulsory threat-simulation as levying a physiological "tax" gain real support from the allostasis literature, but should not lean on "allostatic load" as though it were a single validated meter reading that number out. The honest version of the claim names the specific systems under strain and reports that their unification into one score remains an open methodological problem, not a solved one.

## Related terms

- Allostasis — the predictive regulatory process whose cumulative cost allostatic load names
- Glucocorticoid — a hormone family whose chronic elevation is a common component of allostatic load composites
