Slow-paced breathing is breathing deliberately slower than the resting adult rate of about twelve to twenty breaths per minute. The practice usually aims at around six breaths per minute, about 0.1 Hz, often with an exhalation as long as or longer than the inhalation.

The mechanism is best understood through heart-rate variability. Heart rate rises on inhalation and falls on exhalation: this is respiratory sinus arrhythmia, carried largely by the [vagus nerve](./vagus-nerve.md). The baroreflex, the pressure-sensing loop that steadies blood pressure, has its own delay, which makes it oscillate near 0.1 Hz. Breathing at that frequency brings the respiratory and baroreflex rhythms into phase. The heart-rate oscillation then grows several-fold, and baroreflex gain increases. This "resonance frequency" varies slightly between individuals and is the basis of heart-rate-variability biofeedback [@lehrer2014].

A systematic review of the psychophysiological correlates found three things [@zaccaro2018]:

- breathing below about ten breaths per minute is associated with increased heart-rate variability and respiratory sinus arrhythmia;
- it is associated with shifts in EEG activity, including alpha;
- it is associated with reported relaxation, comfort, and reduced arousal and anxiety.

The review also noted that the studies were small and heterogeneous.

Slow abdominal breathing is how [qigong](../../../../../medical-science-services-section/services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/qigong.md) regulates the breath, and it is shared by many meditative and yogic practices. It is the most plausible physiological route by which those practices act on the [autonomic nervous system](./autonomic-nervous-system.md). It is not the only component they combine, and trials rarely isolate it.
