# The Physiology of Threat, Care, and Holding

## 0. What this audits

The critical-theory note "Hope, Care, and Fear; Attention, Grace, and Abandonment" (2026-08-23) and its companion "Freedom at the Edge of the Held" (2026-08-23, both Claude Fable, critical-theory section) build an attention-economic theory of threat, care, standing, and freedom, and along the way make four claims that are physiological rather than purely conceptual — claims this note exists to check against the actual stress-, care-, and social-buffering literatures rather than take on the strength of the analogy that produced them.

Stated as this note will treat them:

**C1.** Chronic, unresolvable threat acts as a standing tax on cognition and body. The political production of precarity is "compulsory threat-simulation," and its harm is done "before and regardless of any blow" — the computation, not the impact, is the cost.

**C2.** Care — attention committed to another over time — without cost-accounting predictably terminates in burnout. Burnout is "the predictable terminal state of an unpriced flow," not a resilience failure of the carer.

**C3.** Holding — being securely held in relationship or standing, without needing to be continuously attended to — has a physiology. Standing-conferrals (enforceable rights, unconditional provision, kinship recognition) "should alter a population's affective economy — measurably — without any campaign to change sentiment."

**C4.** The capacity for exploration, play, and freedom is built only inside reliable holding (the developmental logic behind Winnicott's capacity-to-be-alone and Burghardt's "relaxed field": stressed or threatened animals do not play), so provision manufactures freedom's preconditions rather than merely funding comfort.

Each is checked in turn (§§1-4) against current stress physiology, occupational-health and caregiving science, social-buffering neuroscience, and a set of natural experiments in structural security, with contested and null findings reported alongside the supporting ones. §5 states verdicts. §6 proposes revisions the evidence forces on the source notes. §7 gives directions. §8 lists sources.

Citations are working references verified via web search in this research session; re-verify against editions before public use (per §8's closing note).

## 1. C1 — chronic threat as a standing tax

The correct home for C1 is not "stress" loosely construed but the specific **predictive-regulation** reframing of stress physiology. Sterling and Eyer's original allostasis proposal (1988), developed by McEwen and Stellar (1993) and McEwen and Wingfield (2003), replaced the fixed-setpoint model of homeostasis with a brain-centered account: the organism does not wait for deviation and correct it, it *predicts* upcoming demand and adjusts physiological parameters in advance. Sterling's later work (2012) and Schulkin and Sterling (2019) make the predictive character explicit, and a 2022 reframing ("Allostasis Revisited: A Perception, Variation, and Risk Framework") together with Barrett's Theory of Constructed Emotion (Katsumi, Theriault, Quigley, and Barrett, 2022 and 2023) extends this into a full predictive-processing account in which affect itself is the felt signature of ongoing prediction-error management. This is good news for C1's central and most defensible content: if the cost is borne in prediction under uncertainty rather than in damage from a realized event, then "compulsory threat-simulation" is not a metaphor but a description of what the system is doing, and its cost is paid whether or not the modeled threat ever lands.

The mechanism for the cognitive half of the tax is well characterized. Arnsten's line of work (foundationally, 2009, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*) established that even mild, uncontrollable acute stress rapidly degrades prefrontal-cortex-dependent cognition — working memory, top-down inhibition — through catecholamine and glucocorticoid signaling cascades, and that more prolonged stress produces architectural dendritic retraction in PFC while amygdala circuits are spared or strengthened. Critically, this is triggered by *uncontrollability and unpredictability*, not by the magnitude of any realized harm, which is exactly C1's claim in miniature at the level of a single circuit. The purely anticipatory version of the tax is directly demonstrated in the threat-of-shock and uncertainty literature: unpredictable-threat paradigms show cortisol and autonomic activation scaling with subjective uncertainty rather than with outcome, and real-world anticipatory cortisol rises before exams, surgery, and competition are well replicated.

Two pieces of the supporting architecture are weaker than the theory's confident tone would suggest, and should be flagged rather than smoothed over. First, "allostatic load" as a unified biomarker construct is contested at the operationalization level: Guidi, Lucente, Sonino, and Fava's systematic review (2021) found the construct clinically useful but flagged wide inconsistency in which biomarkers are scored and how; a 2023 individual-participant-data meta-analysis across 13 cohorts and 40 biomarkers found that a minimal five-marker index (CRP, resting heart rate, HDL-C, waist-to-hip ratio, HbA1c) predicts mortality about as well as elaborate composites — rescuing predictive validity while undercutting the claim that "allostatic load" measures one coherent multi-system quantity rather than a few metabolic and inflammatory proxies doing most of the work. Second, the weathering hypothesis (Geronimus, most recently 2023) is well supported at the level of the health-disparity pattern it explains, but the specific molecular carriers usually cited for it — telomere length and epigenetic clocks as biomarkers of discrimination-driven aging — are inconsistently replicated: a meta-analysis of the Everyday Discrimination Scale against biomarker outcomes found discrimination associated with elevated CRP but not with cortisol, IL-6, or telomere length in adjusted models, and other reviews describe the discrimination-telomere link as weak as a main effect, visible mainly as an interaction with depression, socioeconomic status, or internalized bias. The mortality and morbidity pattern weathering explains is real; the molecular mechanism most often invoked to carry it is not settled science.

## 2. C2 — unpriced care terminates in burnout

This is where the source notes most need revision, and the revision needed is not cosmetic.

Two independent problems undercut C2 as literally stated. The first is that "burnout" is not, at the construct level, a clean natural kind distinguishable from depression. The WHO's ICD-11 (2019) frames burnout as an "occupational phenomenon" — explicitly not a medical diagnosis — with three dimensions (exhaustion, cynicism, reduced efficacy), and this tripartite structure is the field's working consensus. But a sustained empirical challenge from Bianchi, Schonfeld, Laurent, and colleagues (bifactor and network analyses, 2021, *Assessment* and *Clinical Psychological Science*; historical update, 2022) finds that burnout's core component, exhaustion, correlates *more strongly* with depression — including anhedonia and depressed mood — than with the other two burnout dimensions, that the tripartite structure lacks discriminant validity, and that no diagnostic criteria distinguish "burnout" from a depressive episode occurring in an occupational context. This matters directly for C2: if what gets labeled burnout is substantially depression under another name, then attributing it specifically to "unpriced care flow" assigns a single cause to an outcome that has many, better-established causes (genetics, prior trauma, current stressors of every kind), and the theory is borrowing more explanatory precision than the underlying construct can currently supply. Compounding this, the biomarker literature offers no consistent physiological signature to anchor "burnout" as a distinct terminal state: reviews of the HPA axis and autonomic nervous system in burnout (2021, *Psychoneuroendocrinology*) find no compelling evidence of consistent HPA or ANS dysfunction, with at best partial support for a staged pattern (early hyperactivity, later hyporeactivity and blunted cortisol in severe chronic cases), and hair-cortisol studies (Regensburg Burnout Project, 2023) are similarly inconsistent — plausibly because there is no stable case definition to sample consistently against in the first place.

The second problem is more direct: the caregiving-mortality literature does not support "predictably terminates" as a general law of unpriced attention. Schulz and Beach's landmark study (1999, *JAMA*) found that *strained* spousal caregivers had elevated mortality risk, and this finding is the one usually cited to license "care kills." But at least five to seven larger, more diverse population-based studies since (surveyed in Roth, Fredman, and Haley's stress-buffering analysis, *The Gerontologist*, 2018) have found the opposite average effect — caregivers, as a group, show *lower* mortality than matched non-caregiving controls — a pattern plausibly explained by healthy-caregiver selection, by meaning and purpose buffering allostatic cost, or simply by the fact that Schulz and Beach's own sample was 44% "no strain," so their finding was specific to a burdened subgroup rather than to caregiving as such. The dementia-caregiver biomarker literature does show real elevated cortisol and inflammatory dysregulation, but specifically under high-burden, unsupported, unrelieved caregiving (inflammation meta-analysis, *The Gerontologist*, 2019; PNAS, 2020) — the physiological cost is real but conditional, not a universal terminal attractor of care-as-such.

The closest existing, better-validated operationalization of what the source notes mean by "unpriced flow" is not burnout but Helgeson's **unmitigated communion** — excessive other-focus to the exclusion of self, providing support without receiving it, at the expense of reciprocity. This construct reliably predicts worse psychological and metabolic outcomes (documented in adolescents with diabetes and in daily-diary studies of interpersonal behavior) and, unlike burnout, is specified in exactly the terms C2 needs: a *relational structure* (unreciprocated, unaccounted flow) rather than a generic exhaustion syndrome that overlaps with mood disorder.

**The theory should adopt unmitigated communion, or equivalently "high-burden, low-support, no-exit caregiving strain," as its operational construct in place of burnout**, and should state the prediction conditionally: care predicts exhaustion and dysregulation when it is high-demand, socially unsupported or unreciprocated, and structurally inescapable — not when it is chosen, supported, reciprocated, or meaningful, conditions under which caregiving correlates with equal or better survival than not caregiving at all.

## 3. C3 — holding has a physiology; standing alters the affective economy

The strongest single pillar for this claim is Coan's social baseline theory. The original hand-holding fMRI studies (Coan, Schaefer, and Davidson, 2006, *Psychological Science*) found that spousal, and to a lesser extent stranger, hand-holding attenuated neural threat responses under shock threat, moderated by relationship quality, and this has replicated in a larger, more representative community sample, with hand-holding-related reductions in hypothalamic activation predicting better self-reported general health. Social baseline theory's central claim — that attuned social co-regulation lowers the *metabolic cost* of managing uncertainty, changing the allostatic budget rather than only the subjective mood — is a direct, replicated demonstration that being held changes measurable physiological threat processing without any change in the actual threat.

The theory should not, however, lean on oxytocin as the biochemical carrier of "holding," which is the mechanism the source notes' surrounding literature often reaches for by default. Intranasal oxytocin research has a serious, well-documented replication problem: early single-dose prosocial-effect findings were underpowered and subject to publication bias against nulls, and more recent large, pre-registered studies have failed to replicate key claimed effects — a registered report using pooled equivalence testing found no meaningful effect of intranasal oxytocin on trusting behavior, and meta-analyses in autism and social-cognition applications explicitly conclude the evidence is insufficient to confidently support or reject an effect. The better-supported mechanism for C3 is the Coan-style HPA and neural-threat-circuit buffering pathway, which has actually replicated, not a single "bonding hormone" story.

The most important test of C3 is whether structural security changes population-level outcomes without any campaign to change sentiment, and here the natural-experiment evidence is genuinely supportive but should be reported with its nulls intact. The Oregon Health Insurance Experiment found that Medicaid lottery access reduced undiagnosed depression by roughly half and untreated depression by more than 60%, and reduced unmet mental-health need by about 40% (Baicker, Finkelstein, and colleagues, *NEJM* 2013; Milbank Quarterly follow-up, 2018) — one of the cleanest randomized demonstrations that a standing-conferral (health coverage as enforceable entitlement) shifts population mental health with no messaging attached. Long-term Moving to Opportunity follow-up (10-15 years) found improved adult mental and physical health and reduced psychological distress among adults and female children in the low-poverty voucher group, alongside more mixed effects on some economic outcomes for older youth. Finland's 2017-2018 basic income trial (2,000 unemployed recipients, unconditional monthly payment) found no significant employment effect but significant gains in life satisfaction and reductions in mental strain, depression, and loneliness — an unusually clean case of standing-conferral moving affect with no behavior-change campaign attached — and Stockton's SEED guaranteed-income pilot similarly found reduced anxiety and depression alongside, contrary to stereotype, increased full-time employment. A 2022 meta-analysis of 45 cash-transfer studies (McGuire, Kaiser, and Bach-Mortensen, *Nature Human Behaviour*, roughly 117,000 people) found small but statistically significant positive effects on subjective wellbeing (d = 0.13) and mental health (d = 0.07), scaling with transfer size relative to prior income.

Against this, the direct physiological biomarker evidence is weaker and sometimes null. Haushofer and Shapiro's unconditional cash-transfer RCT in Kenya found large gains in psychological wellbeing and consumption but **no overall effect on cortisol** — a directly relevant null result the theory must report rather than omit, even though subgroup effects (transfer timing) existed. **C3 should therefore be downgraded from "physiology, measurably" to "affect and mental-health outcomes, measurably, with more mixed and sometimes null direct biomarker evidence"** — the mental-health-level claim is strongly supported across several independent designs; the strict cortisol-level claim is not yet.

## 4. C4 — holding as the precondition of exploration and play

This is the best-supported of the four claims. Winnicott's "capacity to be alone" (1958) — that reliable holding by another is the precondition for a child's later ability to relax into unsupervised, exploratory solitude — is consistent with, though not independently proven by, the well-replicated secure-base literature in attachment research: securely attached children explore novel environments more, and more confidently, a finding traceable to Ainsworth's Strange Situation work and its many subsequent replications.

Burghardt's five-criteria definition of play requires initiation in a "relaxed field" — the animal must be adequately fed, healthy, and free of acute stress or intense competing demands — and his surplus-resource theory holds that play both requires and generates behavioral and energetic surplus; it is well documented across species that play is suppressed under food scarcity, threat, or illness, and is typically the first behavior to vanish under adversity. Pellis and colleagues' recent rat work (2020-2024) moves this from correlational to causally mechanistic: juvenile social-play deprivation causally alters medial prefrontal cortex dendritic architecture and GABAergic inhibitory synapse development, producing adult deficits in impulse control, cognitive flexibility, and social competence. This is a striking structural echo of §1's Arnsten findings — the same prefrontal circuitry appears to be the shared bottleneck for both the cost of chronic threat and the cost of play deprivation, suggesting C1 and C4 may be two readings of one underlying mechanism rather than two separate claims.

## 5. Verdicts

**C1 — SUPPORTED-WITH-REVISION.** The predictive-regulation and allostasis framework, Arnsten's glucocorticoid-PFC mechanism, and the anticipatory-stress and uncertainty literature robustly support harm prior to and independent of any realized blow. Revision: stop treating "allostatic load" as a single validated biomarker composite — its construct validity is contested, and a five-marker inflammatory/metabolic index does most of the predictive work attributed to more elaborate scores; treat weathering's health-disparity pattern as well supported and its proposed telomere/epigenetic-clock mechanism as not reliably replicated.

**C2 — SUPPORTED-WITH-REVISION, bordering CONTRADICTED as literally stated.** "Burnout" lacks discriminant validity from depression, and has no consistent physiological signature across the HPA and autonomic literature. Modern population studies mostly find caregivers outlive non-caregivers on average, directly complicating "predictably terminates." Revision: replace "burnout" with unmitigated communion, or high-burden/low-support/no-exit caregiving strain, as the operational construct, and state the prediction as conditional on absence of support, reciprocity, and exit rather than as a universal law of unpriced attention.

**C3 — SUPPORTED-WITH-REVISION.** Coan's social-baseline findings replicate and show genuine neural and physiological buffering from being held. Natural experiments (Oregon, Moving to Opportunity, Finland, Stockton, and the cash-transfer meta-analysis) consistently show standing-conferrals move mental health and affect at small-to-moderate effect sizes with no sentiment campaign attached. Revision: drop oxytocin as the proposed biochemical carrier of holding, given its replication crisis, in favor of the replicated HPA/neural-threat-buffering pathway; state the affective-economy claim with high confidence and the strict biomarker claim (cortisol specifically) as an open target, since the best available cash-transfer RCT found a null there.

**C4 — SUPPORTED.** Winnicott's clinical theory is consistent with the well-replicated secure-base literature; Burghardt's relaxed-field theory and Pellis's causal rat work converge on a mechanistic account in which developmental play deprivation produces lasting prefrontal deficits, mirroring the mechanism found under chronic threat in C1.

## 6. Revisions the evidence forces

1. **Reframe the "tax" as a predictive-regulation or allostatic budget targeted at specific systems, not an undifferentiated composite load.** Given the construct-validity problems with unified allostatic-load scores, the source notes' bandwidth-tax language should specify which systems are taxed — glucocorticoid-PFC signaling for the cognitive cost, inflammatory and metabolic markers for the somatic cost — rather than invoking one unified "load."
2. **Split the care claim into a conditional model.** Cost accrues from unsupported, unreciprocated, inescapable care, not from care as such; the theory should adopt unmitigated communion or high-burden/low-support/no-exit strain as its working construct, with meaning, reciprocity, and relief treated as buffering variables rather than as afterthoughts, given that most large caregiving studies find lower average mortality among caregivers, not higher.
3. **Separate the affective claim from the biomarker claim for holding.** The natural-experiment evidence for "standing-conferrals move population mental health without a sentiment campaign" is strong; the direct physiological evidence (cortisol specifically) is inconsistent or null in the best-designed cash-transfer RCT. Claim victory at the mental-health and affect level with high confidence; treat "physiology, measurably" as an open empirical target, and drop oxytocin specifically as the proposed mechanism in favor of the Coan-style HPA/neural-threat-buffering pathway.

## 7. Directions

1. Re-derive the §8 cycle of "Hope, Care, and Fear" using unmitigated communion in place of burnout as the care-side collapse term, and check whether the cycle's cut-point argument (convert holding from attention to standing) survives the substitution unchanged.
2. Seek the shared-circuit hypothesis of §4 directly: a study designed to test whether the Arnsten glucocorticoid-PFC pathway and the Pellis play-deprivation PFC pathway converge on the same synaptic or dendritic markers, rather than merely producing parallel behavioral deficits.
3. Build a caregiving-strain instrument keyed to unmitigated communion (reciprocity, exit, support) rather than to burnout inventories, and test it against the same population data that produced the Schulz-and-Beach-versus-Roth-et-al. reversal, to see whether it discriminates the strained subgroup that the mortality literature keeps finding.
4. Commission a direct replication attempt of the Haushofer-and-Shapiro cortisol null using a design that fixes the timing confound they themselves flagged (measurement occurring after lump-sum transfers had already been partly spent), before treating that null as settled.
5. Track the intranasal-oxytocin literature for further large pre-registered trials; if the replication crisis deepens further, the source notes' broader affective-economy language should be audited for any other unstated dependence on the oxytocin mechanism.
