The lymphatic system is a network of vessels, lymph nodes, and lymphoid organs collecting fluid that has left the bloodstream and permeated tissues (lymph), filtering this fluid for pathogens and debris, and returning filtered lymph to the bloodstream. The system serves two primary functions: immune defense (lymph nodes filter lymph and present antigens to immune cells) and fluid homeostasis (the system drains approximately 2-3 liters of interstitial fluid per day, maintaining tissue fluid balance). Unlike the circulatory system driven by the heart's pump, the lymphatic system relies on passive mechanisms: capillary filtration creates a pressure gradient; osmotic forces drive fluid movement; skeletal muscle contraction compresses lymphatic vessels and propels lymph forward; respiratory movement assists lymph return; and one-way lymphatic valves ensure lymph flows toward the thoracic duct and venous return. Lymph travels through progressively larger vessels to regional lymph nodes (immune filtering), then to collecting ducts, and finally into the venous system at the thoracic duct. The system is low-pressure compared to the circulatory system, making it vulnerable to impairment from injury, surgery, or structural abnormality. Manual lymphatic drainage (MLD) is a manual therapy technique using gentle rhythmic pressure and massage following anatomical lymphatic pathways to facilitate lymph movement. MLD is used clinically for lymphedema (swelling from accumulated lymph after cancer surgery or radiation). Evidence from Ezzo et al. (2015) shows MLD reduces limb volume and improves symptoms in secondary lymphedema post-breast-cancer, though optimal parameters remain unclear. MLD is most effective combined with compression and exercise. The lymphatic system exists because the circulatory system alone cannot balance fluid exchange: arteries deliver blood to capillaries where fluid filters into tissues; venules reabsorb some fluid, but not all. Approximately 3 liters per day must be returned via lymphatics to prevent swelling.
