Cannabigerolic acid (CBGA) is a cannabinoid produced in the glandular trichomes of *Cannabis sativa*, formed by the enzymatic joining of two separate biosynthetic pathways. The first supplies olivetolic acid, a resorcinolic acid built from hexanoyl-CoA and three malonyl-CoA units by a type III polyketide synthase (historically called olivetol synthase) together with olivetolic acid cyclase, the enzyme that performs the specific ring-closing step needed to produce the correct cyclization product rather than an off-pathway byproduct. The second supplies geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP), an isoprenoid unit generated mainly through the plastidial methylerythritol phosphate (MEP) pathway. A prenyltransferase enzyme then attaches the geranyl group from GPP onto olivetolic acid, producing CBGA.

CBGA's significance in *Cannabis* chemistry is structural rather than pharmacological: it is the last common precursor before the pathway branches into the plant's major cannabinoid products. Three closely related oxidocyclase enzymes — THCA synthase, CBDA synthase, and CBCA synthase, all members of the berberine-bridge-enzyme-like gene family and, in the case of THCA synthase and CBCA synthase, roughly 92 percent identical at the amino acid level — each convert CBGA into a differently cyclized cannabinoid acid: tetrahydrocannabinolic acid (THCA), cannabidiolic acid (CBDA), and cannabichromenic acid (CBCA) respectively. Which of these enzymes a given plant expresses, and at what relative activity, is largely governed by a single genomic region (historically modeled as a two-allele "B" locus, though modern genomic work has shown the underlying architecture to involve tandem, partially duplicated gene arrays rather than two clean single-copy alleles), and this is what defines a plant's cannabinoid chemotype. A plant that expresses little or no functional THCA synthase or CBDA synthase activity accumulates CBGA itself as the dominant cannabinoid — this is the basis of the comparatively rare "chemotype IV," CBGA-dominant cannabis.

Like the other major cannabinoids, CBGA is produced and stored in its carboxylic acid form; it is not decarboxylated to the neutral compound cannabigerol (CBG) inside the living plant except as a minor, non-enzymatic side reaction. Bulk conversion of the acid forms to their neutral counterparts (CBGA to CBG, THCA to THC, CBDA to CBD) is a post-harvest, heat- or time-driven chemical event rather than a step in the plant's own metabolism.

For the estate's compartmentalization research line, CBGA is the single point in cannabinoid biosynthesis where two separately synthesized precursor pools — a polyketide-derived aromatic acid and an isoprenoid-derived prenyl donor — converge inside the same trichome secretory cell before the combined product is exported into the extracellular storage cavity. As the shared node feeding every downstream chemotype-defining branch, CBGA is the natural marker compound for tracing where, developmentally and enzymatically, a plant's chemotype identity is actually decided.
