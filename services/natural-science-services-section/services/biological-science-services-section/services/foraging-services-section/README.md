# Foraging Services Section

Foraging is the practice of gathering wild plants, fungi, and other wild
foods for food, medicine, or material use. It is modeled here as a
cross-disciplinary discipline of practice — analogous to how
`agricultural-science-services-section` sits as a sibling to botany,
mycology, and ecology rather than being folded into any one of them —
because foraging draws on botany (plant identification), mycology (fungal
identification and toxicology), ecology (phenology, regional ecosystems,
sustainable-harvest dynamics), and iNaturalist-style field observation
(citizen-science identification tooling) without being reducible to any
single one of those pure sciences. This section owns the practice-level
knowledge those sciences feed: edibility, poisonous-lookalike safety,
harvest ethics and sustainability, seasonality, and the legal landscape
governing where and how gathering may occur.

## Seating derivation

Per `science-services-section`'s ownership rule, a note has one canonical
owner at its most specific defensible service section, and cross-disciplinary
relevance is represented by relations rather than duplicate canonical
copies. A library inventory of the biological-science forest
(`biological-science-services-section`) found no dedicated foraging, wild-food,
or ethnobotany section anywhere under `science-services-section`: genuine
wild-food content existed only as scattered, incidental mentions inside a
handful of botany species pages and a single gardening-section term page,
overwhelmingly concentrated on one species (ramps, *Allium tricoccum*) plus
one invasive plant (Japanese knotweed). Mycology — the section that would
need to carry mushroom-foraging toxicology and lookalike content — had none
at all, despite holding 50 glossary terms on fungal biology. No section
among botany, mycology, or ecology is "more specific" than the union of the
practice; foraging is its own defensible discipline, seated here as a
sibling to `agricultural-science-services-section`, `botany-services-section`,
`mycology-services-section`, and `ecology-services-section` directly under
`biological-science-services-section/services/`.

subject matter (ramps, knotweed) is itself a foraged species; only the
practice-level, cross-species treatment (edibility criteria, lookalike
safety, harvest law, seasonality method) is canonically owned here.

## Scope

This section owns:

- The identification-safety problem of poisonous lookalikes across plant
  and fungal taxa (e.g., poison hemlock vs. wild carrot, *Amanita* species
  vs. edible mushrooms, death camas vs. wild onion).
- Edibility-testing methodology and its documented limitations.
- Sustainable and ethical harvest practice, including Indigenous
  reciprocity-based frameworks.
- Foraging law and land-access regulation in the United States.
- Foraging calendars and seasonality practice, relating to ecology's
  phenology concept.
- Ethnobotany as the academic discipline studying wild food use, bridging
  botany, nutrition science, and conservation biology.

It does not own plant or fungal taxonomy, invasive-species ecology, or
garden-cultivation guidance — those remain canonically owned by botany,
mycology, ecology, and agricultural science respectively, and are related
to from here.

## Plausible future children

No child sections exist yet. Based on the scope above and the existing
scattered content this section consolidates, plausible future children
include:

- `plant-foraging-services-section` — wild plant edibility, identification,
  and harvest practice specific to plants, absorbing the practice-level
  content currently split between botany and gardening.
- `mushroom-foraging-services-section` — wild fungal edibility,
  identification, and toxicology, a domain currently unrepresented anywhere
  in mycology.
- `foraging-ethics-services-section` — Indigenous harvesting protocols,
  the honorable-harvest framework, and the cultural-appropriation concerns
  already present but undeveloped in the gardening section's ramps content.
