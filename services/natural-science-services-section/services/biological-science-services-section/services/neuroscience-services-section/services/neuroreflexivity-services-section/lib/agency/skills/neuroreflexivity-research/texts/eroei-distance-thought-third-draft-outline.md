# Outline: EROEI and the Depth of Cognitive Reflexivity

## The claim

EROEI corresponds with the depth of cognitive reflexivity available to a population. The relationship has three regimes: positive surplus enables reflexivity, break-even starves it, negative return inverts it. We are in the third regime. The process is irreversible on human timescales.

---

## Structure

This is a book, not a paper. It makes one argument across deep history, formal theory, and converging empirical evidence. The structure is a logical dependency chain: each part depends on the ones before it. If any part fails, everything after it is unsupported.

### Part I — What reflexive cognition is and what it costs
Steps 1, 2, 3, 3b. Establishes that reflexive cognition is a distinct, expensive, institutionally scaffolded mode of thought. This is the foundation everything rests on.

### Part II — Energy and surplus
Steps 4, 4b. The net energy cliff, absolute decline, the growing maintenance overhead, the compounding structure, irreversibility. The material base.

### Part IV — Deep history
Step 5b (from deep-history-plan.md). Five energy transitions from fire to fossil fuels, showing the coupling operating across independent transitions. Introduces extractability as the mediating variable. Establishes that the coupling is a recurring pattern, not a one-time correlation.

Steps 6, 6b. The theoretical contribution. Positive, break-even, negative. Rumination as inverted reflexivity. The formal apparatus. The predictions.

### Part VI — Evidence
Steps 7, 8. Selectivity (fluid reasoning declining, crystallized stable). Acceleration (curves steepening across independent data streams). G-factor fragmentation.

## Logical dependency chain (detailed)

Each step depends on the ones above it. If any step fails, everything below it is unsupported.

### Step 1. Reflexive cognition is a distinct mode

Not "more thinking" or "better thinking." A structurally different operation: the capacity to hold a model of one's own cognitive process and operate on it. Thinking about thinking. This is what psychometricians measure as fluid intelligence (matrix reasoning, pattern completion, novel problem-solving) as opposed to crystallized intelligence (vocabulary, stored facts).

**Must establish:**
- The reflexive/reactive distinction is real, not a spectrum
- It maps onto identified neural architecture (dorsal vs ventral attention, default mode network for self-referential processing)
- It is separately measurable (fluid vs crystallized subtests)

**Sources:** Vossel et al. 2014 (dorsal/ventral), psychometric literature on Gf vs Gc

**Vulnerability:** Someone could argue this is just a continuum, not a qualitative distinction. Counter: the neural systems are anatomically distinct and compete for resources. The psychometric subtests diverge empirically (fluid declining, crystallized stable). The distinction does real work.

---

### Step 2. Reflexive cognition is expensive

More expensive than reactive cognition. It requires the organism to maintain two simultaneous representations (the object and the self-model) and sustain this doubled state long enough to modify something. Reactive cognition is cheap — detect, classify, respond. Reflexive cognition costs more metabolically, attentionally, and temporally.

**Must establish:**
- The metabolic cost difference is real (not just intuitive)
- The temporal cost is real — reflexive processing requires sustained engagement, not just bursts
- This cost is not borne by the individual brain alone

**Sources:** Godbersen et al. 2023 (comprehension is metabolically demanding initially, efficient only once coherence develops), attentional resource competition literature

**Vulnerability:** The cost difference could be small enough not to matter at civilizational scale. Counter: Step 3 shows the cost is institutional, not just metabolic, which makes it orders of magnitude larger.

---

### Step 3. The cost is institutional, not just neural

This is the extended mind move. Reflexive cognition at scale depends on scaffolding: education systems, libraries, archives, research networks, communications infrastructure, media that rewards sustained engagement, and discretionary time. These are not luxuries or supplements. They are enabling conditions — possibly constitutive conditions — of reflexive processing beyond what isolated brains can sustain.

**Must establish:**
- The extended/socially extended mind thesis (cognition is not skull-bound)
- Specific examples of institutional scaffolding for reflexive cognition
- That the scaffolding is energetically expensive (it requires buildings, labor, heating, transport, maintenance, salaries, time freed from subsistence)

**Sources:** Clark & Chalmers 1998, Gallagher 2013, Slors 2019, Schofer & Meyer 2005 (higher education expansion), BLS paid leave data

**Vulnerability:** The most serious objection to the whole paper. Someone could argue that digital infrastructure IS scaffolding — that it replaced libraries and universities with something cheaper and more accessible. Counter: this is exactly the question. Whether digital infrastructure functions as reflexive scaffolding or as something else is the empirical question the paper is asking. The psychometric evidence (Step 7) answers it: if digital infrastructure were scaffolding for reflexivity, fluid reasoning wouldn't be declining. It is declining. So either digital infrastructure doesn't scaffold reflexivity, or it does but the net effect is negative.

**Load-bearing:** This step carries the most weight. If cognition isn't institutionally scaffolded, the connection to EROEI is just a metaphor about glucose. Everything below depends on this step being solid.

---

### Step 3b. How surplus builds scaffolding: labor reorganization and distance

Step 3 says scaffolding is expensive. This step explains how surplus actually creates it, through two mechanisms.

**Mechanism 1: Labor reorganization.** Surplus didn't spontaneously produce schools and libraries. It reorganized labor into larger, more coordinated, more managerially legible systems (Chandler 1977). This created an administrative layer — clerks, supervisors, planners, accountants — and the discretionary time for some that followed from the intensified coordination of others (Thompson 1967 on the shift from task-time to clock-time). The scaffolding for reflexive cognition was built on top of this reorganization: universities, research institutes, paid leave, professional specialization. **Discretionary time is not a natural endowment. It is a product of how surplus reorganizes labor.**

**Mechanism 2: Distance.** High surplus creates separation between action and consequence. When EROEI is high, the energetic costs of a way of life can be externalized — geographically, ecologically, socially. This distance is what allows abstraction. A society that doesn't have to confront the immediate material cost of its own operation can sustain forms of thought that are decoupled from immediate survival. This has a dual character: it enables reflexive depth (thinking can range beyond the local and immediate), but it also enables buffered ideology (institutions can mistake subsidy for normality, distance for maturity, scale for rationality).

**Must establish:**
- That scaffolding doesn't just cost energy — it emerges from a specific social process (labor reorganization under surplus)
- That "distance" is a real consequence of surplus, not just a metaphor — externalization of cost is energetically subsidized
- That distance has a dual character: it enables reflexivity AND it enables ideological buffering

**Sources:** Chandler 1977, Thompson 1967, Scoones et al. 2020 (development discourse as buffered formation)

**Why this matters for the argument:** When surplus declines, both mechanisms reverse. Labor reorganization contracts (fewer administrative/professional layers, less discretionary time). Distance collapses (consequences become proximate again, externalization becomes more expensive). The scaffolding built on labor reorganization and distance degrades from both sides simultaneously. This explains why the decline can be so rapid — it's not one process but two converging.

**Key formulation from the second draft:** "What looked like elevation was often buffered distance."

### Step 4. The net energy cliff

This is math, not argument. EROEI determines the fraction of gross energy available to society: f = (EROEI - 1) / EROEI. This is a hyperbola. Nearly flat above 10:1. Falls steeply below 5:1. This means the social effects of EROEI decline are nonlinear: large declines at the top are nearly invisible, small declines at the bottom are catastrophic.

**Must establish:**
- The formula and the curve
- Key thresholds from the literature (Hall's 5:1, Murphy's 8:1, Lambert's 20-30:1 for well-being)
- Current position on the curve (global fossil at useful stage: 3.5:1 — already below the 5:1 threshold)

**Sources:** Hall & Klitgaard 2018, Murphy & Hall 2010, Brockway et al. 2024, Hall Lambert & Balogh 2014

**Vulnerability:** EROEI numbers are contested. System boundaries matter. Murphy et al. 2022 stress harmonization problems. Counter: the paper doesn't need precise numbers. It needs the qualitative fact that we're on the steep part of the curve, and this is established even under generous assumptions. Brockway et al. 2024 in Nature Energy is about as authoritative as it gets.

### Step 4b. Absolute decline and the growing overhead

Step 4 describes the net energy cliff — the nonlinear relationship between EROEI and available surplus. This step establishes that the situation is worse than declining EROEI alone: the absolute amount of usable energy is declining while the infrastructure overhead required to maintain the energy system is growing. The floor is dropping and the ceiling is falling.

**Net energy has peaked.** Delannoy et al. (2021): net energy from oil liquids peaked ~2024 at 415 PJ/day. Gross production peaks ~2034 at 551 PJ/day. The 10-year gap means usable energy is already declining while headline numbers still rise.

**The maintenance trap.** IEA (2025): 90% of upstream oil/gas investment since 2019 has gone to offsetting decline, not growth. Without investment, oil falls 8%/year, gas 9%/year. Decline rates have doubled since 2010. Tight oil/shale would collapse >35% within 12 months. The IEA calls this the "Red Queen Syndrome."

**The food system is already a net energy sink.** PNAS Nexus (2024): global agrifood EROEI was 0.91 in 2019. For every calorie delivered, more than a calorie was consumed. Peaked at 0.94 in 2014 and plateaued. Meanwhile: 50% of topsoil lost in 150 years, 33% of arable land degraded, yield growth at its lowest rate in six decades.

**Every substrate is degrading on one-way timescales.** Topsoil: centuries to regenerate, 75 billion tonnes/year eroded. Aquifers: centuries to recharge, drawn down orders of magnitude faster. Forests: 2 billion hectares lost since pre-industrial, primary forest loss at record levels. Mineral ore grades: copper from 5% (1900) to 0.62% (present), with mining costs up 30% in five years. Each degradation demands more energy to maintain current output, drawn from a system that itself requires more energy to stay level.

**The pressures compound.** Declining oil EROEI → more energy for oil → less for fertilizer → degraded soil needs more fertilizer → more gas consumed → faster EROEI decline. Water depletion → deeper wells or desalination → more energy → less for everything else. Ore grade decline → more energy per tonne → harder to build renewables → longer dependence on declining fossil system. Each pressure worsens the others. The structure is multiplicative, not additive.

**The renewable transition itself costs net energy.** Kikstra et al. (2022): 1.5C-compatible pathways cause 10-34% decline in net energy during the transition. Capellan-Perez et al. (2019): system EROEI could fall from ~12:1 to ~3:1 mid-century under fast transition. Building new infrastructure while maintaining old infrastructure doubles the overhead. Heinberg: renewables are adding to fossil fuels, not replacing them — emissions keep rising. The transition is additive, not substitutive.

**Must establish:**
- That the decline is in ABSOLUTE net energy, not just EROEI ratio
- That the maintenance overhead is growing (Red Queen)
- That the substrate degradation is irreversible on human timescales
- That the pressures compound multiplicatively

**Why this matters for the argument:** The outline previously assumed there IS some surplus to work with — just less of it. This step establishes that the surplus is not merely shrinking but being consumed from both sides: less total energy AND a larger fraction consumed by the system's own maintenance. The "distance" between extraction and return — the physical infrastructure of pipelines, refineries, grids, data centers, farms, water systems — IS the overhead. It must be maintained or the system collapses. And maintaining it leaves progressively less for everything else, including cognitive scaffolding.

**Sources:** Delannoy et al. 2021, IEA 2025 (Red Queen report), PNAS Nexus 2024, FAO/UNCCD soil data, Kikstra et al. 2022, Capellan-Perez et al. 2019, Michaux 2024 (GTK), Bardi 2014

### Step 5. The coupling

This is where Steps 1-4 come together. The argument:

- Reflexive cognition is a distinct, expensive mode (Steps 1-2)
- Its expense is primarily institutional, not neural (Step 3)
- Institutional scaffolding costs energy
- Available energy surplus is determined by EROEI, nonlinearly (Step 4)
- Therefore: EROEI constrains reflexive depth through institutional scaffolding

**Must establish:**
- That the connection is material and institutional, not metaphorical
- That this is the same kind of claim as "agricultural output constrains population density" — a constraint claim, not a deterministic one

**Vulnerability:** Correlation is not causation. EROEI is declining and reflexive capacity is declining, but maybe something else is causing both, or the cognitive decline has a different cause entirely. Counter: the paper doesn't need to prove EROEI is the sole cause. It needs to show that (a) the coupling is theoretically grounded, (b) the predictions it generates are specific and falsifiable, and (c) the data matches those predictions better than alternative framings. Steps 7-8 do this.

### Step 5b. Deep history — the coupling across five transitions

The coupling hypothesis is strengthened if it operates across multiple independent energy transitions, not just the fossil era. The deep history shows it does — and introduces a variable the modern argument alone would miss.

**Transition 1: Fire → Cooking (~1 Mya - 300 kya).** Cooking increased caloric availability 12-50% (starch gelatinization, protein denaturation, reduced digestion cost). The human brain consumes ~25% of the body's energy. Wrangham (2009) argues cooking established the metabolic base that makes reflexive cognition biologically possible. The timeline is contested (brain expansion precedes secure fire evidence), but the biological plausibility is strong: the brain's energy demands need explaining, and cooking is the best candidate for how those demands were met. Present as: the first energy transition established the metabolic precondition for reflexivity.

**Transition 2: Foraging → Agriculture (~10 kya).** The counterintuitive case. Agricultural EROEI was ~1.1-1.6, WORSE than foraging per capita. Health deteriorated (Cohen & Armelagos 1984: 19 of 21 societies). Stature declined. So how did agriculture produce cognitive infrastructure? Not through per-capita surplus but through EXTRACTABLE surplus. Scott (2017): "Only the cereal grains can serve as a basis for taxation." Writing literally emerged from grain accounting (earliest cuneiform: pictographs of grain, inventories, transactions). The "division between manual and intellectual labor" was funded by grain taxation.

**What this reveals: extractability.** The coupling is not just EROEI → surplus → scaffolding. It is EROEI → extractable surplus → scaffolding. A society can have high per-capita EROEI (foragers) and no cognitive infrastructure, or low per-capita EROEI (farmers) and writing, mathematics, and specialist knowledge classes — if the surplus is extractable. The extracting agent (state, market, platform) determines what KIND of scaffolding gets built. Agriculture's scribes served the state's need for accounting. Platforms serve engagement capture. Neither was designed for reflexivity.

**Transition 3: The 6,000-year plateau (5000 BC - 1700 AD).** Per-capita energy capture barely moved: ~17,000 kcal/cap/day (2000 BC) to ~31,000 at Rome's peak, still ~30,000 in 1700 (Morris 2010, 2013). Same energy converters (muscle, wood, water, wind) for six millennia. Cognitive infrastructure (Alexandria, the Islamic Golden Age, medieval scholasticism) was funded by scale and extraction efficiency, not per-capita EROEI gains. When the extraction apparatus collapsed (fall of Rome), cognitive infrastructure collapsed with it. Tainter (1988): "Societies collapse when their investments in social complexity and their energy subsidies reach a point of diminishing marginal returns."

**Transition 4: Wood → Coal (1500-1800).** England's timber crisis. Population doubled, demand exceeded supply. Elizabeth I's Parliament: 30+ bills on the fuel crisis. By 1800, coal energy equaled 11 million acres of woodland. Coal surplus directly funded: steam-powered printing, compulsory education, public libraries, mass literacy. The clearest historical example of an EROEI crisis resolved by a new energy source, with cognitive infrastructure expanding immediately after.

**Transition 5: Coal → Oil → Decline (1900-present).** Morris: per-capita energy went from 92,000 to 230,000 kcal/day in the 20th century alone — more than doubling in one century after being flat for six millennia. This funded universal education, research universities, mass media, the Flynn effect. Now declining: useful-stage fossil EROEI at 3.5:1.

**What's historically novel about the present.** Every previous EROEI decline was resolved by a transition to a higher-EROEI source (wood → coal, coal → oil). The current decline has no successor at comparable EROEI. AND — uniquely — the compensatory infrastructure (digital platforms) is not just failing to scaffold reflexivity but actively extracting it. Previous declines produced Regime II (scaffolding degradation, complexity collapse — Rome, dark ages). Regime III is new. Rome didn't have platforms harvesting cognitive capacity during its decline. Past collapses were failures of support. This is an potential inversion.

**Key scholars:** White 1943 (White's Law: culture evolves with energy per capita), Cottrell 1955 (*Energy and Society*), Smil 2017 (*Energy and Civilization*), Morris 2010/2013/2015 (energy capture index, Social Development Index), Tainter 1988 (collapse and diminishing returns), Wrangham 2009 (cooking hypothesis), Scott 2017 (*Against the Grain*), Hagens (The Great Simplification), LePoire (energy transitions as complexity thresholds)

**Honest gaps:** Fire-brain timeline doesn't line up cleanly. No EROEI estimates for ancient energy converters (only per-capita consumption data). Nobody has measured institutional complexity against wood EROEI decline in pre-coal Europe. The full EROEI-cognition coupling across deep history has not been published as a unified argument. The pieces are all in the literature. Nobody has assembled them.

### Step 6. Three regimes

This is the theoretical contribution. The coupling isn't monotonic — it has qualitatively distinct regimes.

**Regime I — Positive surplus:** EROEI high enough to fund expanding scaffolding. Reflexive capacity growing. This was the 20th century. Flynn effect rising.

**Regime II — Break-even:** Surplus consumed by maintenance. Scaffolding holds but doesn't expand. Reflexive capacity plateaus. Things work but with less slack. This is the brief plateau period in the IQ data (late 1970s-1990s, depending on country).

**Regime III — Negative cognitive EROEI:** Scaffolding has degraded. Compensatory infrastructure (digital platforms) has replaced it. But the compensatory infrastructure extracts more reflexive capacity per cycle than it returns. Each engagement cycle costs more (attention, emotional regulation, switching overhead) than it yields (comprehension, insight, integration). The deficit drives the next cycle. The system accelerates.

**Key features of Regime III:**
- Reflexivity doesn't disappear — it inverts. The machinery keeps running but consumptively.
- Rumination is the signature: self-observation that depletes rather than integrates.
- Acceleration, not steady decline — because negative EROEI produces positive feedback.
- The inversion is invisible from inside — the system still feels like thinking.

**Rumination as inverted reflexivity — the neuroscience is direct.** Rumination activates the same DMN regions (mPFC, PCC, ACC) as productive self-reflection, but with pathological hyperconnectivity between DMN and subgenual prefrontal cortex (Hamilton et al. 2015). The distinguishing feature is executive control failure: reduced dlPFC neural flexibility (Philippi et al. 2022), impaired attentional disengagement from negative self-referent material (Koster et al. 2011), inability to inhibit negative content from working memory (Joormann & Gotlib 2010). Berman et al. (2011) showed PCC-sgACC connectivity correlates with brooding (r=0.44) but NOT with productive reflection (r=0.19). The machinery is the same. The regulation is gone. This is not metaphor — it is the neural signature of reflexive inversion.

Nolen-Hoeksema et al. (2008) describe the self-reinforcing cycle formally: negative affect → rumination → enhanced negative cognition + impaired problem-solving + eroded social support + inhibited coping → worse circumstances → deeper negative affect → more rumination. Each cycle costs cognitive resources (Levens et al. 2009: r=0.75 correlation between brooding and executive impairment) without producing the resolution that would repay the cost.

**Formal apparatus for "negative cognitive EROEI."** The concept has not been named as such, but every component exists:
- **Information foraging theory** predicts when people should stop but don't. Wittels et al. (2023, PNAS) show people systematically overharvest — staying in depleted patches longer than the Marginal Value Theorem predicts. Asymmetric belief updating (positive prediction errors weighted more than negative) means people are slow to recognize returns have fallen below costs.
- **Dopamine receptor downregulation** provides the diminishing-returns mechanism. Repeated stimulation causes reduced receptor availability. Baseline hedonic tone drops below normal. The deficit drives further engagement to restore baseline, which deepens the deficit.
- **Attention residue** (Leroy 2009) compounds through the day. Each task-switch leaves trace cognitive load. By afternoon, you carry residue from every switch since morning. Recovery takes ~23 minutes per interruption. In a 40-second switching environment, recovery never completes.
- **Lapse-activated consumption** (Heatherton & Wagner 2011): a small self-regulation failure triggers disinhibition that escalates. The prefrontal-subcortical balance tips toward subcortical dominance, and each lapse makes the next more likely. A formally described positive feedback loop.
- **Cognitive fatigue as entropy externalization failure** (Ezzedini 2025): fatigue increases reliance on rigid schemas, which impairs adaptation, which deepens fatigue. Another self-reinforcing loop with explicit thermodynamic framing.
- **Resource-rational analysis** (Lieder & Griffiths 2020) provides formal vocabulary for modeling cognition in terms of costs and returns. This is the closest existing framework for defining a cognitive EROEI.

**Must establish:**
- That Regime III is a distinct prediction, not just "things get worse"
- That it generates specific, testable predictions different from a simple decline model
- That "negative cognitive EROEI" is a coherent concept supported by converging mechanisms from independent literatures, even though the integrated concept has not been named or directly measured

**Vulnerability:** "Negative cognitive EROEI" as an integrated concept is an original synthesis. The paper must be transparent that it is assembling components from separate literatures. Counter: the components are individually well-established and the integration generates novel predictions (Step 6b) that can be tested against existing data (Steps 7-8). The concept earns its keep if the predictions hold. The paper names the missing measurement (Step 9) as a direct test.

### Step 6b. Predictions

Derived from the three-regime model. These are what make the hypothesis falsifiable.

1. **Selectivity:** Decline should be concentrated in fluid reasoning (reflexive), not crystallized knowledge (stored). If the decline were caused by something general (nutrition, lead, pollution), it would affect all subtests. If it's caused by scaffolding loss, it should specifically hit the scaffolding-dependent mode.

2. **Population gradient:** Steepest declines in populations with least remaining scaffolding and most exposure to compensatory infrastructure (younger, lower education, lower SES).

3. **Acceleration:** Under Regime III, declines should steepen over time, not plateau. Because negative EROEI produces positive feedback: deficit → more engagement → more deficit.

4. **Phenomenology:** The subjective experience should be effortful engagement producing exhaustion rather than comprehension. Not "can't think" but "thinking hard and getting nowhere." Doomscrolling, compulsive checking, rumination.

5. **Temporal alignment:** Cognitive declines should track EROEI decline with a lag determined by institutional inertia (scaffolding degrades slower than surplus, because buildings and curricula persist after the funding conditions that created them have changed).

6. **Coherence fragmentation:** If reflexive integration is the most surplus-dependent cognitive operation, then the g-factor (the coherence among cognitive subtests) should weaken — not just scores declining, but the binding between abilities loosening. The mind should become less integrated before it becomes less capable on any single dimension.

---

### Step 7. Evidence — does the selectivity prediction hold?

This is the hardest test. If fluid reasoning is declining while crystallized knowledge is stable, the coupling hypothesis gains strong support. If both are declining equally, the hypothesis is in trouble.

**Data:**
- Norway: IQ declining ~3.3 points/decade since 1975 cohort. Within-family. Environmental cause.
- Finland: Abstract reasoning (Shapes) had largest gain AND steepest reversal.
- US: Matrix reasoning and number series declining. Verbal reasoning stable. Steepest in ages 18-22 and lower education.
- Germany (Pietschnig et al. 2024): figural reasoning declining 4.68-5.17 IQ points/decade in students (2012-2022). **AND the positive manifold (intercorrelation among subtests) is weakening.** Cognitive abilities are not just declining — they are becoming less coherent. The g-factor itself is fragmenting. This is a stronger finding than simple score decline: it suggests the integrative capacity that binds cognitive operations together is degrading.

**Assessment:** The selectivity prediction holds. The Pietschnig finding on g-factor fragmentation adds a dimension the outline didn't previously capture: it's not just that fluid reasoning drops while crystallized holds, but that the coherence between ALL cognitive abilities is loosening. Under the coupling hypothesis, this makes sense — reflexive integration (the capacity that binds subtests into a coherent g-factor) is the most surplus-dependent operation and would degrade first.

---

### Step 8. Evidence — does the acceleration prediction hold?

If we're in Regime III, the curves should steepen.

**Data:**
- Attention: 2.5 min (2004) → 75 sec (2012) → 40 sec (2020). Rate of decline increasing.
- PISA reading: gradual decline through 2010s, then 10-point drop in one cycle (2018-2022). Double the previous record.
- Adolescent depression: stable through 2000s, then steep rise 2011-2018. Steepening, not linear.
- Anxiety: accelerating through the 2010s, pre-COVID.

**Assessment:** The acceleration prediction holds across all four data streams. A simple decline model (things gradually get worse) predicts linear or decelerating trends as populations adapt. The observed pattern is the opposite.

### Step 8b. Alternative explanations — why EROEI adds explanatory power

A serious paper must address why the standard alternatives don't fully explain the pattern. The key test: any adequate explanation must account for all three features — (1) fluid reasoning declining while crystallized knowledge is stable, (2) steepest declines in young, low-education populations, (3) acceleration.

**Lead / environmental toxins:** Wrong temporal pattern. Lead exposure has DECREASED since the 1970s-80s. The reversal begins in the 1990s-2000s, precisely when lead was at historically low levels. Microplastics and endocrine disruptors are biologically plausible but the research is early-stage and animal-model-heavy. Neither explains the fluid/crystallized divergence (neurotoxins should damage cognition generally) or the education gradient.

**Educational changes:** Teaching-to-test and narrowed curricula are real (post-NCLB in the US). But the reversal appears FIRST in Scandinavia — countries with famously non-test-oriented education. Does not explain why crystallized knowledge is stable (if teaching got worse, facts should decline too). Does not explain the age-18-22 gradient (the problem appears during young adulthood, not childhood). **Compatible with EROEI: educational defunding is downstream of declining surplus. Societies cut expensive inputs (Socratic teaching, labs, small classes) and substitute cheap ones (standardized curricula, screens).**

**Nutrition:** Ultra-processed food (UPF) is associated with 28% faster cognitive decline (JAMA Neurology 2022). UPF is 50-60% of caloric intake in US/UK. Biologically plausible via omega-3 deficiency, micronutrient loss. But does not explain the fluid/crystallized divergence. **Strongly compatible with EROEI: UPF is cheaper per calorie than whole food. The shift to UPF is an energy economics story — declining surplus forces the food system to optimize for throughput over nutritional quality.**

**Screen time / digital media:** Temporal correlation is striking (reversal sharpens around 2010-2012, smartphone adoption). The cognitive mode shift argument is strong — screens favor skimming/scanning over sustained attention, which would selectively impair fluid reasoning. But: the reversal begins in the 1990s in Scandinavia, well before smartphones. Effect sizes in observational studies are small. Some screen activities (spatial games) actually IMPROVE performance. **Compatible with EROEI: screens are the CHEAPEST way to occupy a developing mind. Societies adopt them because the alternatives (teachers, books, unstructured play in safe environments) cost more surplus.**

**Social/cultural changes:** Reduced parental time, less reading at home, 71% decline in outdoor play in one generation, reduced Piagetian reasoning scores (54% → 17% correct on Volume and Heaviness test, 1976-2004). These are real observations. But they describe WHAT changed, not WHY. Why did families stop reading together? Why did play time collapse? **These are symptoms, not causes. EROEI supplies the upstream answer: both parents must work because a single income no longer covers the cost of living under declining surplus; childhood is institutionalized and screen-mediated because that's what's affordable.**

**Summary:** None of the alternatives is wrong. Each identifies a real proximate mechanism. But each is either temporally misaligned (lead), downstream of EROEI (education, nutrition, social/cultural), or a mechanism without an upstream cause (screens). The EROEI framing unifies them: declining net energy forces societies to substitute cheap inputs for expensive ones across ALL these domains simultaneously — cheap food, cheap education, cheap childcare, cheap cognitive infrastructure. The convergence is not coincidental. It's what declining surplus looks like when it hits every sector at once.

**Sources:** Bratsberg & Rogeberg 2018, Pietschnig et al. 2024, Dworak et al. 2023, UPF/JAMA Neurology 2022, Piagetian decline data, Gray on play deprivation

### Step 9. What's missing

Two measurements that would directly test the hypothesis:

1. **Cognitive EROEI of digital infrastructure.** The ratio of useful reflexive capacity produced to cognitive resource consumed, for the systems that now dominate cognitive life. Nobody has calculated this. It's the measurement that would determine empirically whether we're in Regime II or Regime III.

2. **Population-level metacognitive capacity over time.** Metacognition is the most direct expression of reflexive cognition. Nobody has tracked it across decades. The measurement doesn't exist.

**Note:** The absence of these measurements is itself predicted by the model. Generating demand for them is a reflexive operation. In Regime III, the capacity to demand the right measurements is part of what's being consumed.

### Step 10. What the data says

The paper should not prescribe responses. It should state what follows from the evidence.

**The process is irreversible on any timescale relevant to the populations experiencing it.** Each substrate that cognitive scaffolding depends on regenerates on geological timescales (centuries to millennia for topsoil, aquifers, old-growth forest, mineral ore grades) and is being consumed on industrial timescales (decades). Fossil fuels are non-renewable and the easy deposits are gone. These are one-way processes. They compound: you can't restore topsoil without water, can't pump water without energy, can't get energy without minerals, can't mine minerals without energy. The order doesn't matter — the pressures commute. The endpoint is the same.

**There is no energy source currently practicable that reproduces the surplus that built the cognitive infrastructure now degrading.** Renewables are real but their systemwide EROEI does not approach the 50-100:1 of peak fossil fuels. The transition itself costs 10-34% of net energy (Kikstra et al. 2022) and could temporarily drive system EROEI to ~3:1 (Capellan-Perez et al. 2019). There is no scenario in the literature where global net energy returns to the levels that funded 20th-century cognitive expansion.

**Regime III is not a temporary crisis. It is a description of the descent.** The paper describes the shape of the process: which cognitive capacities degrade first (fluid reasoning, then integrative coherence), which populations are hit first (young, low-education, low-SES), how the decline accelerates (positive feedback from negative cognitive EROEI), and what the subjective experience of the process feels like (effortful engagement producing exhaustion — rumination, doomscrolling, compulsive checking).

**The maintenance/commons/care literature describes what persists longest, not what reverses the decline.** Under lower surplus, the organizational forms most likely to sustain some reflexive capacity for the longest period are those with short feedback loops (Ostrom 1999, Agrawal 2003), maintenance orientation (Ureta 2014), care as infrastructure (Buser & Boyer 2021), bounded extraction (Brondizio et al. 2021), and low-throughput social reproduction (Kotiswaran 2023). These are not solutions. They are descriptions of what degrades last — the organizational equivalent of crystallized knowledge persisting after fluid reasoning has declined. Attention restoration research (Ohly et al. 2016) suggests that low-intensity cognitive environments can produce disproportionate reflexive returns, but this is a description of what's possible at small scale under constraint, not a program for civilizational recovery.

**The population's diminishing capacity to recognize the problem is itself a consequence of the problem.** Generating demand for the right measurements (cognitive EROEI of digital infrastructure, population-level metacognitive trends) requires reflexive capacity. That capacity is what's being consumed. This is not a rhetorical flourish. It is a prediction of the model, and the absence of these measurements is evidence for it.

## Structural vulnerabilities (honest assessment)

**Strongest part of the argument:** The selectivity of the cognitive decline. Fluid reasoning declining while crystallized knowledge is stable is a specific prediction that holds and is not easily explained by alternative framings.

**Second strongest:** The acceleration. Curves are steepening across independent data streams. This discriminates Regime III from Regime II.

**Third strongest (new):** The irreversibility evidence. Every substrate regenerates on geological timescales and is consumed on industrial ones. The compounding structure means the pressures are multiplicative. This is not a projection — it is observable in the current data (food EROEI 0.91, oil maintenance at 90%, topsoil loss at 75Bt/year, aquifer depletion orders of magnitude above recharge).

**Weakest part:** The causal mechanism from EROEI to cognition passes through institutional scaffolding, and there are many other variables affecting institutions. The paper should acknowledge this and argue that the EROEI framing adds explanatory power (via selectivity, acceleration, and the unification of alternatives as downstream effects) rather than claiming it is the sole cause. The claim is a constraint claim, not a deterministic one — but the constraint is tightening, and the evidence for the constraint's effects is converging across independent literatures.

**Second weakest:** "Negative cognitive EROEI" is not formalized. The components (information foraging overharvesting, dopamine downregulation, attention residue, lapse-activated consumption, entropy externalization failure) are individually well-established. The integration is an original synthesis. The paper should present it as such — a synthesis that generates predictions — and name the missing measurements (cognitive EROEI of digital infrastructure, population-level metacognitive trends) as direct tests.

**The deepest vulnerability:** The reflexive trap. The paper argues that the capacity to evaluate its own claims is being degraded by the process it describes. This is either a prediction of the model or a convenient dodge. The paper should state it plainly as a prediction and let the reader decide. What it should NOT do is use it rhetorically to preempt criticism.

## Notes on scale and drafting

This is a book. Comparable in ambition to Graeber & Wengrow's *The Dawn of Everything* — a single argument carried across deep history with evidence from multiple disciplines, arriving at a conclusion that reframes how we understand the present.

**Part I** (Steps 1-3b) is the theoretical foundation. Probably 3-4 chapters. The extended mind argument (Step 3) is the load-bearing section — every skeptic attacks here, so it needs full development with concrete institutional examples, not just philosophical citations. Step 3b (labor reorganization, distance, extractability) is where the book starts to feel like its own thing rather than a review of existing literature.

**Part II** (Steps 4-4b) is the material base. Probably 2 chapters. The net energy cliff is math; keep it clean. The absolute decline / compounding / irreversibility section (4b) is where the book stops hedging. Present the numbers. Let them speak.

**Part III** (Step 5) is the hinge. One chapter. Where Parts I and II meet: this is the coupling hypothesis stated formally.

**Part IV** (Step 5b) is the deep history. Probably 5 chapters — one per transition. This is the section that will make the book readable to a wide audience. Each transition is a story: fire and the brain, grain and the scribe, the 6,000-year plateau, coal and the printing press, oil and the university. Each story demonstrates the coupling in a new context and adds a variable (biological cost, extractability, scale vs per-capita surplus, transition dynamics, the anomaly of peak surplus). The reader should arrive at the present feeling the weight of the pattern.

**Part V** (Steps 6-6b) is the theoretical contribution. Probably 2-3 chapters. The three regimes. Regime III in detail — rumination neuroscience, information foraging, dopamine downregulation, attention residue, the formal apparatus. The predictions stated before the evidence.

**Part VI** (Steps 7-8) is the evidence. Probably 2-3 chapters. The IQ data, the attention data, the reading data, the psychiatric data. Presented against the predictions. The g-factor fragmentation finding. The acceleration pattern.

**Part VII** (Step 8b) is the alternative explanations. One chapter. Lead, education, nutrition, screens, social/cultural. Each addressed honestly. The EROEI framing's distinctive contribution: unification of the alternatives as downstream effects.

**Part VIII** (Steps 9-10) is the conclusion. Probably 2 chapters. The missing measurements. The irreversibility argument. What persists longest. The reflexive trap. No prescriptions — just what the evidence shows.

That's roughly 20-25 chapters. A real book.

The contribution is: the coupling hypothesis, the three-regime model, the deep historical evidence showing the coupling across five independent energy transitions, the convergence of evidence from six independent literatures (energy economics, psychometrics, attention research, psychiatric epidemiology, neuroscience, information foraging theory), and the honest conclusion that the process is irreversible on human timescales and has no exit within the current energy paradigm.
