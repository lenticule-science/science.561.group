# EROEI and Cognitive Reflexivity: Research Notes for Third Draft

These notes collect the empirical evidence and theoretical argument developed in conversation on 2025-03-31. The core hypothesis is that EROEI corresponds with depth of cognitive reflexivity, and that the relationship has three regimes: positive surplus enables reflexivity, break-even starves it, and negative return inverts it. The paper's current framing ("lower yield") understates the problem. The data supports a modal shift, not a quantitative decline.

---

## I. The Thesis (Revised)

The first and second drafts argue: high net energy built a civilization with unusually large cognitive range, and declining surplus is contracting that range while digital systems compensate by extracting harder for less return.

The stronger thesis: **EROEI corresponds with the depth of cognitive reflexivity.** Reflexive cognition — the capacity to observe, model, and modify one's own thinking — is not an intrinsic property of brains. It is a surplus-dependent, institutionally-scaffolded achievement. Under declining surplus, it doesn't just diminish. When cognitive EROEI goes negative — when the compensatory systems consume more reflexive capacity than they return — reflexivity inverts. The machinery of self-observation keeps running, but consumptively. That's rumination. That's the acceleration of anxiety. That's the system eating itself and experiencing the process as effort.

### Three regimes, not two

1. **Positive cognitive EROEI**: Surplus funds the scaffolding (education, leisure, integrative media, archives, research systems, discretionary time) that enables reflexive cognition at civilizational scale. Thought can observe itself, hold models, integrate over long timescales.

2. **Break-even (~1:1)**: All available cognitive/energetic resource goes to maintaining the system. No surplus for reflexivity. The institutional scaffolding degrades. Thought becomes reactive — fast, stimulus-driven, unable to sustain self-observation.

3. **Negative cognitive EROEI**: The compensatory system (digital platforms) consumes more reflexive capacity than it returns. Each cycle of engagement costs more than it yields. The deficit from the last cycle drives the next. Reflexivity doesn't disappear — it inverts. The self-observing machinery runs in a consumptive mode: rumination, compulsive checking, doomscrolling, the feeling of thinking hard while producing nothing. The system accelerates because the shortfall demands another pass.

### The net energy cliff

The fraction of energy available to society = (EROEI - 1) / EROEI. This is a hyperbola:

| EROEI | % available to society | % reinvested in energy |
|---|---|---|
| 100:1 | 99% | 1% |
| 20:1 | 95% | 5% |
| 10:1 | 90% | 10% |
| 5:1 | 80% | 20% |
| 3:1 | 67% | 33% |
| 2:1 | 50% | 50% |
| 1.5:1 | 33% | 67% |
| 1:1 | 0% | 100% |

Below 5:1, small EROEI declines produce large net-energy losses. This is the cliff.

**Key thresholds from the literature:**
- Hall: "Society probably cannot function below 5:1"
- Murphy & Hall 2010: effective threshold at ~8:1
- Lambert & Hall: quality of life correlates with societal EROEI; well-being threshold at 20-30:1
- Capellan-Perez et al. 2023: 10:1 as upper limit of the cliff zone

**Critical fact:** Global fossil fuels at useful stage are already at 3.5:1. Below Hall's 5:1 threshold. Below the cliff. This is not a projection. This is the current number.

### Trajectory

- Oil EROEI peaked ~50:1 in the 1930s-40s (Court & Fizaine)
- Weighted average of all oil liquids projected to plateau at 6.7:1 by 2050 (Delannoy et al. 2021)
- Energy consumed by oil production: 15.5% of gross output now, projected 50% by 2050 (Delannoy et al. 2021)
- Final-stage fossil fuel EROEI declined 23% between 1995 and 2011 (Brockway et al. 2019)

### Digital infrastructure energy cost

- Global data centers: ~415 TWh in 2024 (~1.5% of global electricity). Projected to double to ~945 TWh by 2030 (IEA 2024)
- US data centers: 176 TWh in 2023 (4.4% of US electricity)
- AI servers: electricity consumption growing ~30%/year; already 40% of data center server electricity demand in 2023
- **Nobody has calculated EROEI for digital/informational systems.** This is a genuine research gap. No equivalent of "useful cognitive work returned per unit of energy invested in digital infrastructure" has been formalized.

## III. Cognitive Data

### Reverse Flynn effect

**The core finding is real and well-documented.**

**Norway** (Bratsberg & Rogeberg 2018, PNAS): Full male population conscription data, birth cohorts 1962-1991. IQ rose from 99.5 (1962 cohort) to 102.3 (1975 cohort), then declined to 99.4 (1989 cohort). Decline rate: ~0.33 IQ points/year (~3.3 points/decade). **Within-family design** — brothers born in different years show the same pattern. This rules out immigration, genetics, and demographic composition. The cause is environmental.

**Finland** (Dutton & Lynn 2013, *Intelligence*): ~25,000 male conscripts/year, 1988-2009. Gains 1988-1997: 4.0 IQ points/decade. Declines 1997-2009: 2.0 IQ points/decade. **The largest prior gain was in Shapes (abstract reasoning): 6.9 points. Shapes also showed the steepest reversal.**

**United States** (Dworak et al. 2023, *Intelligence*): N=394,378, SAPA Project, 2006-2018. Reverse Flynn effects in matrix reasoning and letter/number series. Verbal reasoning: minimal change. **Steepest declines in ages 18-22 and lower education levels** — the populations with least institutional scaffolding and highest digital platform exposure.

**Critical detail for the thesis:** It's not cognition in general that's declining. It's specifically fluid reasoning — abstract pattern manipulation, working with novel problems, the capacity to hold structures in mind and operate on them. **Vocabulary and crystallized knowledge are stable.** People aren't getting dumber. They're losing reflexive capacity while retaining accumulated knowledge. This is exactly what the hypothesis predicts: the surplus-dependent capacity erodes while the cheap capacity persists.

### Attention allocation

**Gloria Mark (UC Irvine)**, naturalistic computer logging, ~20 years of data:
- 2004: average 2.5 minutes on a single screen before switching
- 2012: 75 seconds
- 2016-2020: 40-50 seconds (median 40 seconds)

This is behavioral, not psychometric — it measures how long people voluntarily stay on one thing. Replicated across multiple labs.

**No standardized psychometric sustained attention test has been given to representative samples across decades.** This is a gap. Nobody gave the same test in 1995 and 2020.

### Reading

**PISA 2022** (OECD, 81 countries, 15-year-olds): 10-point decline from 2018 to 2022. The largest single-cycle drop in PISA history. Prior records never exceeded 5 points between cycles.

Lab studies: print vs. screen reading consistently shows 10-30% lower comprehension and recall on screen, especially for longer and conceptually demanding material.

Eye-tracking studies: frequent digital readers engage in more skimming, less rereading, shallower semantic processing.

Heavy digital media users show diminished P300 brainwave responses — a neurological marker of sustained attention capacity.

### Anxiety, depression, burnout

**Anxiety** (GBD 2019/2021): Global prevalence 311 million (1990) to 458 million (2019). High-SDI countries: age-standardized incidence per 100,000 rose from 658.87 (1990) to 841.56 (2021).

**Depression** (GBD 2019): Global prevalence 172 million (1990) to 279 million (2019). DALYs increased 49.3%. COVID spike: +28% major depressive disorder, +26% anxiety disorders in 2020 alone.

**Adolescent depression** (US, Monitoring the Future, N=512,283, 1991-2018): Girls in top 75th percentile for depressive symptoms: 20.8% (1999) to 36.7% (2018).

**Rumination:** No population-level secular trend data exists. Rumination is studied as a transdiagnostic mechanism, not tracked epidemiologically. This is another gap — and a telling one, given the hypothesis.

### Neuroscience

**No population-level longitudinal brain data across historical cohorts exists.** Cross-sectional associations between heavy digital use and altered brain structure/function are documented but not causally established:

- Lower gray matter in anterior insula, orbitofrontal cortex, anterior cingulate, parahippocampal regions (review, 2024, Psychoradiology/PMC). **Caveat: no regions consistently replicated across studies; small samples; cross-sectional.**
- Compromised white matter in superior longitudinal fasciculus, corona radiata, corpus callosum (DTI studies of internet addiction/smartphone dependence).
- Reduced anticorrelation between dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and anterior default mode network — suggesting disrupted cognitive control over self-referential processing (meta-analysis, *Translational Psychiatry* 2022, 72 resting-state fMRI studies).
- Adolescents checking social media 15+/day showed increasing neural sensitivity over time in amygdala, posterior insula, ventral striatum during social anticipation (Maza et al. 2023, *JAMA Pediatrics*, N=169, 3-year longitudinal).

**Metacognition:** No population-level secular trend studies exist. Nobody has measured metacognitive capacity in the same population across decades.

## IV. The Correspondence

The curves match. Here is the temporal alignment:

- Global fossil fuel EROEI at useful stage: declining through the 1990s-2010s, reaching ~3.5:1 by 2020 (Brockway et al.)
- Norwegian IQ: peaked for the 1975 birth cohort, declining since
- Finnish IQ: peaked 1997, declining since
- US fluid reasoning: declining 2006-2018
- Screen attention: 2.5 min (2004) → 40 sec (2020)
- PISA reading: steepest decline 2018-2022
- Anxiety: rising steadily 1990-2021, accelerating
- Depression: rising steadily, with steepening curves in younger cohorts

The specific prediction of the negative-EROEI regime — that the compensatory digital system would accelerate its own consumption of reflexive capacity — fits the data:

1. **What's declining is specifically reflexive capacity** (fluid reasoning, sustained attention, deep reading), not cognition in general (vocabulary stable).
2. **The steepest declines are in the populations most exposed to the compensatory system** (ages 18-22, lower education — Dworak et al.).
3. **The decline accelerates rather than plateauing** (screen attention: 2.5 min → 75 sec → 40 sec; PISA: gradual decline then sudden 10-point drop; adolescent depression: steepening curve).
4. **The phenomenology matches negative EROEI**: compulsive engagement that produces fatigue rather than satisfaction, inability to stop despite awareness that it isn't working (doomscrolling as reflexive machinery running at negative return).

### What hasn't been measured

Two critical measurements don't exist:
1. **EROEI of digital infrastructure in cognitive terms.** Nobody has formalized this.
2. **Population-level metacognitive capacity over time.** Nobody has tracked it.

These are not incidental gaps. They're the two measurements that would directly test the hypothesis.

---

## V. Implications for the Paper

The second draft's framing ("the lights are still on, but cognition flickers") understates the situation. If cognitive EROEI is negative, the lights are drawing power from the wiring.

The paper should:
1. State the three-regime model explicitly (positive / break-even / negative)
2. Present the net energy cliff as a structural feature, not background
3. Specify that the cognitive decline is modal — reflexive capacity specifically, not cognition generally — and connect this to the IQ subtest data
4. Name the digital system as the compensatory infrastructure and ask whether it has crossed 1:1
5. Use the acceleration pattern (not just the decline pattern) as evidence for negative EROEI
6. Connect rumination to inverted reflexivity — the self-observing machinery running consumptively
7. Drop the "lower yield" framing. This isn't about getting less. It's about the extraction process consuming its own substrate.

## VII. Rumination as Inverted Reflexivity — Neuroscience

Rumination activates the same self-referential processing network (mPFC, PCC, ACC, DMN) as productive self-reflection, but with pathological connectivity — specifically, hyperconnectivity between DMN and subgenual prefrontal cortex mediated through the thalamus (Hamilton et al. 2015, *Biological Psychiatry*).

**What distinguishes rumination from reflection at the neural level:**
- Reduced dlPFC neural flexibility — lower BOLD signal variability (Philippi et al. 2022). Brooding drives the depression-variability relationship, not vice versa.
- Impaired attentional disengagement from negative self-referent material (Koster et al. 2011, *Clinical Psychology Review*). Rumination is failure to exit, not excessive entry.
- Inability to inhibit negative content from entering/remaining in working memory (Joormann & Gotlib 2010).
- Dysregulated network switching — during errors, high ruminators show increased DMN engagement; during rumination, the cognitive control network is paradoxically activated (Roberts et al. 2024).
- PCC-sgACC connectivity correlates with brooding (r=0.44) but NOT with productive reflection (r=0.19) (Berman et al. 2011, *SCAN*).

**The self-reinforcing cycle (Nolen-Hoeksema et al. 2008, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*):**
Negative affect → rumination → enhanced negative cognition + impaired problem-solving + eroded social support + inhibited coping → worse circumstances → deeper negative affect → more rumination.

**Metabolic cost:** Levens et al. (2009): r=0.75 correlation between brooding rumination and executive impairment in high-interference conditions. Rumination consumes working memory resources without producing the insight that would repay the cost.

**Watkins (2008, *Psychological Bulletin*):** Three factors determine whether repetitive thought converges or loops: level of construal (abstract/evaluative → unconstructive; concrete/experiential → constructive), amplification (self-focus amplifies negative mood effects), and context-dependence (dysphoric mood is a precondition for unconstructive rumination).

**Zhou et al. (2020, *NeuroImage*):** Meta-analysis of 14 fMRI studies. Rumination implicates DMN core regions and the dorsomedial PFC subsystem specifically — not the medial temporal lobe subsystem.

**2025 Hidden Markov Model study (*Translational Psychiatry*):** MDD patients showed prolonged DMN activation, reduced CEN occupancy, shorter CEN lifespan. Greater brooding = greater DMN frequency. CBT reduced DMN occurrence and facilitated transitions toward CEN-dominant states.

## VIII. Alternative Explanations for Reverse Flynn Effect

Each alternative identifies a real proximate mechanism. None fully explains the three-feature pattern (fluid/crystallized divergence, education gradient, acceleration). EROEI unifies them as downstream effects of a single upstream cause.

**Lead / environmental toxins:** WRONG temporal pattern. Lead exposure decreased since 1970s-80s. Reversal begins in 1990s-2000s at historically low lead levels. Microplastics/endocrine disruptors are biologically plausible but early-stage research, mostly animal models. Neither explains fluid/crystallized divergence (neurotoxins damage cognition generally) or education gradient.

**Educational changes:** Teaching-to-test and narrowed curricula are real. But reversal appears FIRST in Scandinavia — countries with non-test-oriented education. Does not explain why crystallized knowledge is stable. Does not explain age-18-22 gradient. **Downstream of EROEI: societies cut expensive educational inputs and substitute cheap ones.**

**Nutrition:** UPF associated with 28% faster cognitive decline (JAMA Neurology 2022). 50-60% of caloric intake in US/UK. Omega-3 deficiency plausible. But does not explain fluid/crystallized divergence. **Downstream of EROEI: UPF is cheaper per calorie; declining surplus forces food system to optimize for throughput over nutrition.**

**Screen time / digital media:** Temporal correlation striking (sharpening ~2010-2012). Cognitive mode shift argument strong (screens favor scanning over sustained attention → selectively impairs fluid reasoning). But reversal begins in 1990s pre-smartphone. Effect sizes in observational studies small. Some screen activities improve spatial reasoning. **Compatible with EROEI: screens are the cheapest cognitive infrastructure; societies adopt them because alternatives cost more surplus.**

**Social/cultural changes:** Reduced parental time, 71% decline in outdoor play, Piagetian reasoning scores crashed (54% → 17% correct, 1976-2004). Real observations. But describe WHAT changed, not WHY. **Downstream of EROEI: both parents must work under declining surplus; childhood institutionalized because affordable alternatives are gone.**

**Additional finding — Pietschnig et al. 2024:** German students (N=19,474, 2012-2022): figural reasoning declining 4.68-5.17 IQ points/decade. AND the positive manifold is weakening — cognitive abilities becoming less coherent. The g-factor is fragmenting. Under the coupling hypothesis, this is predicted: reflexive integration (the most surplus-dependent operation) degrades first, loosening the binding between abilities before any single ability hits bottom.

## IX. Formal Apparatus for Negative Cognitive EROEI

Nobody has named this concept. But every component exists in the literature.

**Information foraging and overharvesting:** Wittels et al. (2023, PNAS) show people systematically stay in depleted patches longer than the Marginal Value Theorem predicts is optimal. Asymmetric belief updating: positive prediction errors weighted more than negative (2020, PMC7347922). In deteriorating environments, people fail to downgrade expectations fast enough. **This is structurally identical to negative EROEI: the forager spends more than it recovers but can't detect the deficit.**

**Dopamine receptor downregulation:** Repeated stimulation → reduced receptor availability → baseline hedonic tone drops below normal → deficit drives further engagement to restore baseline → further downregulation. Each cycle returns less. Each cycle costs the same or more. Tolerance as diagnostic criterion for problematic social media use (PMC10251362, 2023). Transition from positive reinforcement (seeking likes) to negative reinforcement (avoiding withdrawal/FOMO) is itself an escalation dynamic (PMC12108933).

**Attention residue accumulation:** Leroy (2009): switching before completion leaves trace cognitive load. Recovery takes ~23 minutes per interruption. In a 40-second switching environment, recovery never completes. Residue compounds through the day. Each switch degrades performance on the next task, which may increase switching impulse (degraded engagement → more susceptible to next interruption).

**Lapse-activated consumption:** Heatherton & Wagner (2011, PMC3062191): a small self-regulation failure triggers disinhibition that escalates into collapse. Prefrontal-subcortical balance tips toward subcortical dominance. Each lapse makes the next more likely. Formally described positive feedback loop.

**Cognitive fatigue as entropy externalization failure:** Ezzedini (2025, *Frontiers in Psychology*): fatigue increases reliance on rigid schemas → impairs adaptation → deepens fatigue. Explicit thermodynamic framing — the brain must continuously externalize disorder to maintain organization; when this fails, internal entropy accumulates.

**Cognitive cost as metabolic resource allocation:** Christie & Schrater (2015, *Frontiers in Neuroscience*): model cognitive effort as literal metabolic expenditure with astrocytic glycogen as energy buffer. Agents "will only commit limited resources in cases where the payoff is worth it." Cost-benefit energy analysis of cognition — functionally identical to EROEI.

**Allostatic load and rumination:** The energetic cost of allostasis model (2023, PMC10082134): acute psychological stress increases energy expenditure 9-67%. "Rumination appears to be the strongest psychological predictor of fatigue, the subjective experience of energy deficiency." Humans trigger allostatic cascades through imagined threats, burning metabolic resources on threats that never materialize. A net-energy-loss model of cognition.

**Resource-rational analysis:** Lieder & Griffiths (2020, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*): cognition as optimal use of limited computational resources. Operations have costs (time, energy, opportunity) and returns (decision quality). Provides formal vocabulary for defining cognitive EROEI: ratio of decision-quality-improvement to cognitive-resource-expenditure.

**The integration is the original synthesis.** Drawing these five threads together — overharvesting, diminishing dopaminergic returns, compounding residue, cascading self-regulation failure, and entropy accumulation — into a single model where engagement costs more than it returns and the deficit drives further engagement. Each component is independently established. The integration has not been named or measured.
