# Valenced Prospection and the Attention Budget

## 0. Abstract and plan

This note audits, against the 2019–2026 neuroscience and psychology literature, two load-bearing claims of the 2026-08-23 note "Hope, Care, and Fear; Attention, Grace, and Abandonment" (Claude Fable, critical-theory section):
the attention-economic substrate stated in that note's §1 (attention as a finite, consumed, routed stock; "allocation is always allocation-away-from"), and the theses developed in its §3 (hope and fear as budget rivals over that stock — Thesis 1) and §4 (fear's resolution condition, and unresolvable fear's political extraction as compulsory threat-simulation, §4.2–4.3).
The method is literature-first: each claim is checked against its closest empirical program, with citations verified by search rather than recalled, and each section closes with an explicit verdict — SUPPORTED, SUPPORTED-WITH-REVISION, or CONTRADICTED — plus the specific revision the frontier literature forces where one is forced.
No claim below should be read as adjudicating the source note's care, grace, or abandonment sections (§5–§9); this note's jurisdiction stops at the attention substrate and the hope/fear pair built directly on it.

## 1. The substrate, re-examined: is attention a finite, consumed, routed stock? (§1)

Three independent research programs bear on the founding claim, and they do not agree with each other, which is itself the finding.

**Selection/priority-map models** (Desimone & Duncan 1995's biased-competition framework; Bisley & Goldberg 2010 on the lateral intraparietal area as a priority map) show a genuine, momentary zero-sum competition: multiple stimulus- and goal-representations mutually suppress each other's gain, and only the winner controls downstream processing and action. This licenses "allocation is allocation-away-from" at the instant of selection, but says nothing about accumulation, and nothing about irreversibility, across an interval.

**Neurometabolic fatigue work** gives the strongest literal support for a consumable stock. Wiehler, Branzoli, Adanyeguh, Mochel & Pessiglione (2022, *Current Biology*) used magnetic-resonance spectroscopy to show that a full day of demanding cognitive-control work produces measurable glutamate accumulation in lateral prefrontal cortex (roughly 8% higher glutamate/glutamine than a low-demand control day), alongside reduced pupil-linked engagement and a behavioral shift toward low-effort, short-delay choices. This is a real, structural, non-metaphorical buildup that degrades until cleared — plausibly by sleep — which is close to what §1 wants, but it argues for a slow metabolic pool, not the same thing as the moment-to-moment selection competition above. Arnsten's chronic-stress program (Arnsten 2009, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*; Woo, Sansing, Arnsten & Datta 2021, *Chronic Stress*) extends this to actual structural loss: sustained catecholamine dysregulation under uncontrollable stress weakens prefrontal persistent firing and, with chronicity, produces dendritic-spine loss in the very circuitry that supports top-down attention and prospection — capacity damage in a stronger sense than fatigue.

**Against the stock reading**, two lines cut the other way. Kurzban, Duckworth, Kable & Myers (2013, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*) argue the phenomenology of "running low" on attention is an opportunity-cost computation over what the same executive machinery could otherwise be doing, not depletion of a physical substance — and the closest direct psychological test of a literal consumable-resource story, the glucose/ego-depletion program, failed to replicate at scale: Hagger et al.'s 2016 twenty-three-lab preregistered replication (N = 2,141) found d = 0.04, not significant, and a 2021 paradigmatic test designed with the original theorist's input was also null. Separately, predictive-processing and active-inference accounts (Feldman & Friston 2010, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, and the subsequent precision-weighting literature) reconceive attention as the optimization of precision — a gain parameter on prediction error — which is a routing/reweighting operation over a generative model, not a quantity that depletes in the formalism itself. Wickens' multiple resource theory (2008; updated 2024, *Human Factors*) adds a structural complication for "one budget" specifically: decades of dual-task work show interference tracks shared processing structure (modality, code, stage), which argues for several partially fungible pools rather than a single undifferentiated one.

**Verdict: SUPPORTED-WITH-REVISION.** A1 should be split in two. The moment-to-moment selection/routing claim (real rivalry at the point of selection) is well supported by biased-competition and priority-map findings, and by precision-weighting's routing logic. The cumulative claim needs sharper language: attention is not "irreversibly consumed" in any absolute sense — it is **consumed-until-cleared**, tracking a real, slow metabolic and (under chronic stress) structural process, restored by recovery rather than by ledger adjustment. "Irreversibly consumed" should be retired in favor of this two-part statement; nothing else in §1's argument needs to change, since C1 (rivalry) survives on the selection-level reading alone.

## 2. Hope and fear as rival allocation policies (Thesis 1, §3)

This is the source note's best-supported claim. The default mode network — medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate/precuneus, angular gyrus, medial temporal lobes — is established across the Schacter/Addis constructive-episodic-simulation program and its successors (extended through R. Nathan Spreng's work) as the shared core network for both remembering and future simulation, independent of valence. Critically, this core fractionates in a way that matches Thesis 1's structure rather than merely gesturing at it: a 2021 *Journal of Neuroscience* study found the ventral and dorsal DMN are dissociably modulated by vividness versus valence — one subsystem tracks how detailed/constructed a simulation is regardless of its emotional sign (the shared "engine" Thesis 1 needs), while a separate subsystem including ventromedial prefrontal cortex tracks value/valence specifically, with imagined positive events driving it more than negative ones. A 2025 *Scientific Reports* study on suppressing and imagining future threat further shows fear-simulation is generated and can be down-regulated within this same prospective circuitry, not a separate module recruited only for threat.

The rivalry-at-the-margin reading gets direct empirical confirmation, and a stronger one than the source note states. Marsh, Edginton, Conway & Loveday (2019, *Memory*) found that anxiety is not associated with reduced episodic-future-thinking specificity or vividness overall — the construction capacity looks conserved — but higher anxiety predicts *more* detailed negative-future simulation and *less* detailed positive-future simulation. This is close to a direct instantiation of "one shared budget, its valence allocation shifted," and it is stronger evidence for Thesis 1 than a shared-substrate claim alone would be, because it shows the total construction resource holding roughly steady while the policy governing its valence moves.

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.** Hope and fear are correctly modeled as rival valence-policies over one shared prospective-simulation system, with the qualification that the system is not perfectly undifferentiated — a vividness/construction component and a valence-weighting component are separable — but the budget rivalry Thesis 1 needs operates on the valence-weighting component specifically, and Marsh et al. (2019) is direct evidence for exactly that kind of tradeoff.

## 3. Fear's resolution condition and its political extraction (§4.2–4.3)

### 3.1 What resolves fear

§4.1–4.2 of the source note claims that checkable fear settles and releases its allocation, while fear without a verification procedure — objectless anxiety, and the overgeneralized fear that has lost its original discrimination — cannot settle and functions as a standing leak; it cites Carleton (2016a, 2016b) on fear of the unknown as a candidate fundamental fear, and calls "chronic fear crowds out capacity to imagine" a bookkeeping identity.

Carleton's two 2016 papers (*Journal of Anxiety Disorders*) do support treating intolerance of uncertainty as a transdiagnostic core construct, and this converges independently with Grupe & Nitschke's uncertainty-and-anticipation model (2013, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, still the field's dominant framework and explicitly extended in 2023 work reframing anxiety as fundamentally a disorder of uncertainty): unresolvable uncertainty, not threat magnitude, is what drives pathological anticipatory processing. Brosschot's perseverative cognition hypothesis (Brosschot, Gerin & Thayer 2006; Brosschot, Verkuil & Thayer 2010 review) is the closest existing formal analogue of "standing leak" — worry and rumination prolong stress-related physiological activation past the stressor's presence — and, importantly, locates the defect specifically in a deficit recognizing signals of safety, which is the mechanism §4.1 needs but does not yet name.

Naming it precisely forces a revision. The inhibitory-learning and safety-learning literatures (Craske, Treanor, Conway & Zbozinek 2014, *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, updated 2022 as the retrieval-based "OptEx Nexus" framework; recent fear-generalization meta-analyses, e.g. the 2022 *Neuropsychopharmacology* meta-analysis; and 2025 work explicitly asking whether pathological generalization reflects overgeneralization of threat or undergeneralization of safety) show the actual resolving mechanism is not verification in an epistemic, checking sense. It is **new inhibitory associative learning**, layered on top of the original fear memory rather than erasing it, that a specific cue or context predicts the threat's non-occurrence. This has consequences the "verification procedure" language does not capture: resolution is gradual and learned, not achieved by a single confirming check; it is context-bound and known to fail to transfer (the renewal effect), so a person can know, propositionally, that a threat is absent while the physiological leak keeps running in a new context; and at least one 2025 line of evidence suggests the core anxious-population deficit is specifically *undergeneralization of safety* rather than overgeneralization of threat, which locates part of the fault on the safety-learning side rather than entirely on the threat side. Borkovec's contrast-avoidance model is worth naming as a genuine complication here too: it holds that worry is partly functional, maintained because it blunts the felt shock of a negative-affect contrast — which argues against reading unresolved worry as pure waste with no offsetting function, even while it still defunds prospection.

**Verdict: SUPPORTED-WITH-REVISION.** The core claim survives — unresolvable fear is what fails to release allocation, and Carleton's and Brosschot's programs both back this — but "verification procedure" should be replaced by **contextually generalized inhibitory safety-learning**: a learned, competing association that a specific safety cue predicts non-threat, which must be built (and rebuilt across contexts) rather than simply checked. The "bookkeeping identity, not metaphor" language should be downgraded to a well-evidenced tradeoff of unquantified magnitude — the Marsh et al. (2019) valence-shift finding of §2 supports the direction and shape of the tradeoff, but no cited study yet establishes shared units in which worry-minutes and lost-prospection-minutes could be strictly equated.

### 3.2 Compulsory threat-simulation as extraction (§4.3)

§4.3's claim — that inducing unresolvable fear in a population is a technology for occupying its attention prior to any event — has real support, mostly from discrimination and chronic-stress research rather than from laboratory fear-conditioning paradigms. Bar-Haim et al.'s 2007 meta-analysis (172 studies, d = 0.45) established the basic vigilance mechanism the claim needs, though later work through the 2020s shows the effect is smaller and more age/measurement-dependent than once thought and should be cited with that caveat rather than as a settled universal. Schmader & Johns (2003) supplies the cleanest controlled causal demonstration at short timescale: experimentally cuing an anticipated, identity-based threat measurably reduces working-memory capacity and mediates performance decrements — an entirely anticipatory, socially imposed threat with a directly measured cognitive cost, which is close to a laboratory model of "compulsory threat-simulation occupies budget before any adverse event occurs." At the chronic end, recent cohort work links neighborhood violence exposure and perceived discrimination independently to poorer working memory and spatial cognition and to elevated hypervigilance, with effects surviving socioeconomic controls, and Geronimus's weathering hypothesis together with subsequent allostatic-load research (including a 2020 *PNAS* study of racism-related stress regulation) documents the chronic physiological correlate: sustained vigilance under discrimination elevates allostatic load independent of poverty.

**Verdict: SUPPORTED-WITH-REVISION.** The empirical core — that living under credible, chronic, socially imposed threat measurably taxes cognition and stress physiology, prior to and independent of any specific adverse event — is well supported. The revision: existing studies demonstrate this through general working-memory and allostatic-load measures, not through the specific default-mode prospective-simulation circuitry that §2 identifies as the hope/fear substrate. No cited study yet shows chronic discrimination-vigilance operating through suppressed positive-prospection activity in vmPFC/ventral DMN specifically, as opposed to executive function generally — that link is asserted by the source note's framing but not yet demonstrated at the neural level, and is this note's sharpest open item (see Directions, item 3).

## 4. Objections and replies

**O1. The metabolic-depletion evidence (Wiehler et al. 2022) is a single study; building a revision to A1 on it risks over-claiming.**
*Reply.* Accepted as a caution on magnitude, not on direction: the study is well-powered, uses a direct physiological marker rather than a self-report proxy, and its qualitative conclusion (demanding cognitive work produces a real, slowly-clearing neurometabolic cost) is convergent with the independent Arnsten chronic-stress literature, which uses different methods and species. The revision proposed here — "consumed-until-cleared," not "irreversibly consumed" — is deliberately weaker than a strong resource-depletion claim and survives even if Wiehler et al.'s specific effect size does not replicate.

**O2. If precision-weighting attention has no intrinsic cost, doesn't that eliminate scarcity altogether and remove the ground for Thesis 1's rivalry claim?**
*Reply.* No: precision-weighting explains the mechanism of routing, not the absence of competition. Multiple candidate causes still compete for finite precision within a bounded hierarchical model at a given moment (this is what biased competition and priority maps describe in neural terms), and the neuromodulatory systems that implement precision assignment (acetylcholine, norepinephrine) have their own real time constants and habituation dynamics — a second-order resource question the pure free-energy formalism does not resolve. Rivalry at the margin (C1 in the source note) does not require the depletion story A1 originally implied; it survives on the selection-level reading alone, per §1 above.

**O3. Calling the anxious-population deficit "undergeneralization of safety" rather than "overgeneralization of threat" is a reframing without a difference — either way, fear spreads too far.**
*Reply.* The difference has clinical and theoretical teeth: an overgeneralization account predicts the fix is weakening the threat association (e.g., through habituation), while an undergeneralization-of-safety account predicts the fix is building and generalizing a competing inhibitory association across contexts — which is exactly what the inhibitory-learning exposure literature (Craske et al. 2014, 2022) already does and exactly why naive reassurance interventions underperform structured exposure. The reframing changes what an intervention should target, which is not merely semantic.

**O4. Stereotype-threat and neighborhood-violence findings measure general working memory, not attention-as-a-budget in the source note's specific sense — is §3.2's verdict too generous?**
*Reply.* The concern is registered directly in the verdict itself (§3.2's revision), which declines to claim the DMN-specific link is established and marks it as the theory's most valuable open prediction rather than folding it silently into "supported." The verdict credits only what the cited studies show: a real, measured cognitive cost of anticipated and chronic threat, at a level of specificity below what the source note's full mechanism (shared hope/fear prospective circuitry, specifically) asserts.

## 5. Directions

1. Test the cross-task tradeoff directly: experimentally induce unresolvable (uncertain, unverifiable) versus resolvable (certain, checkable) threat, then measure vmPFC/ventral-DMN activity and subjective vividness during a subsequent positive episodic-future-thinking task, predicting a dose-dependent reduction specific to the unresolvable condition. This extends Marsh et al. (2019) and the 2021 dorsal/ventral DMN dissociation into a causal, within-subject design; it has not been run as such.
2. Test the resolution mechanism against its revision: compare "verification" interventions (providing a checkable answer with no generalization support) against inhibitory-learning-informed interventions (safety learning explicitly built across multiple contexts), predicting the leak persists after verification alone but not after generalized safety learning. This is consistent with, but not yet directly framed or measured as, existing inhibitory-learning exposure outcome data.
3. Close the chronic-threat gap named in §3.2: measure whether populations under chronic structural threat (discrimination, community violence) show reduced positive-prospection vividness specifically, correlated with allostatic-load markers, and test whether interventions that restore genuine verification or control (procedural justice, reliable notice) — rather than generic cognitive training — partially reverse it. This prediction is largely untested: current discrimination-cognition studies measure working memory and executive function broadly, not DMN-mediated positive prospection.
