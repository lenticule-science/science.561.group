The threat-imminence continuum is a rodent behavior-systems account, formalized by Michael Fanselow, organizing defensive behavior into three qualitatively distinct modes keyed to spatial, temporal, and psychological distance from a predator or threat rather than to a single graded intensity of fear. Fanselow's 1994 statement of the model, in *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, describes it directly: "Low levels of fear promote pre-encounter defenses, such as meal-pattern reorganization. Moderate levels of fear activate post-encounter defenses. For the rat, freezing is the dominant post-encounter defensive response... Extremely high levels of fear, such as those provoked by physical contact, elicit the vigorous active defenses that compose the circa-strike mode." This is a theoretical, integrative paper — a synthesis of Fanselow's own and others' rat lesion, stimulation, and pharmacology data, not a report of one new experiment.

The switch between modes is proposed as a disinhibition event rather than a graded escalation of a single circuit: "Projections from the amygdala to the ventral periaqueductal gray activate freezing. Midbrain structures such as the dorsolateral periaqueductal gray and the superior colliculus play a crucial role in organizing this mode of defense. Inhibitory interactions between the structures mediating circa-strike and post-encounter defense allow for the rapid switching between defensive modes as the threatening situation varies." The model therefore holds forebrain-gated freezing circuitry (amygdala to ventral PAG) and midbrain circa-strike circuitry (dorsolateral PAG and superior colliculus) in a hierarchical, mutually inhibitory relationship, so that engaging one suppresses the other.

The continuum's naming (pre-encounter, post-encounter, circa-strike) traces to a 1988 book chapter by Fanselow and Lester in *Evolution and Learning*; that chapter is not PubMed- or DOI-indexed, and its content here is reported only as cited and summarized in later, PubMed-indexed papers such as Fanselow (1994) — it has not been independently verified against its own primary text. A parallel ethoexperimental tradition, associated with Robert and Caroline Blanchard, named and quantified the same rodent behaviors (flight, freezing, defensive threat, defensive attack, risk assessment) that Fanselow's continuum formalizes computationally; the Blanchards' foundational 1988 *Annual Review of Psychology* chapter is likewise not independently verified here against a primary abstract, though its existence and general content are corroborated by later citing work.

The distinction that must be held is that this is a rat behavior-systems model built from lesion, stimulation, and pharmacology data in rodents confronting live or model predators and shock. Fanselow explicitly frames the continuum in terms of predatory threat and shock or pain paradigms — not mood-state transitions, not psychiatric symptomatology, and not any claim about what triggers a switch in a human outside a live threat-detection context. Later human fMRI work (see periaqueductal-gray, circa-strike-defense) reports a correlated shift in relative forebrain-versus-midbrain activity as an approaching threat becomes more imminent, which is consistent with the continuum's architecture, but the continuum itself, as stated by its own author, is a rodent construct.

The consequence for research design is that because the continuum specifies qualitatively distinct modes rather than one continuous fear-intensity dimension, a study that scores all high-fear behavior on a single scale risks collapsing circuits the model predicts are separately, and mutually inhibitorily, organized. A behavioral measure intended to test the model should register mode-appropriate defensive topography — freezing versus active escape or attack — rather than a single undifferentiated "fear" score, and should specify which stage of imminence (pre-encounter, post-encounter, circa-strike) a given manipulation is meant to probe.

## Related terms

- Circa-strike defense — the terminal, most-imminent mode of this continuum
- Periaqueductal gray — the midbrain structure whose dorsolateral column organizes the circa-strike mode
- Defense cascade — a later clinical synthesis that extends amygdala-hypothalamus-PAG circuitry from this and related rodent literatures into a trauma framework
