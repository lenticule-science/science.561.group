Groups of neurons that fire in step produce rhythmic fluctuations in the brain's electrical activity: neural oscillations, or brain waves [@buzsaki2006]. They are grouped into bands by frequency. The boundaries are conventions, and sources differ by a hertz or so:
- **delta,** under 4 Hz: deep sleep;
- **theta,** 4–8 Hz: drowsiness, memory, navigation;
- **[alpha](alpha-rhythm.md),** 8–12 Hz: relaxed wakefulness with eyes closed;
- **beta,** 13–30 Hz: active, focused thought and motor holding;
- **gamma,** above 30 Hz: local processing and binding.

Oscillations are generated by loops of excitation and inhibition, both within the cortex and between the cortex and the thalamus. They coordinate when neurons fire, and so which signals get through. They can be recorded from the scalp by [EEG](electroencephalography.md). They can also, it is hypothesized, be nudged from outside, which is what [entrainment](neural-entrainment.md) by rhythmic stimulation claims to do.
