Entrainment is the synchronization of an oscillator to an external rhythm. Applied to the brain, a [neural oscillation](neural-oscillation.md) shifts its frequency and phase to follow a periodic input [@buzsaki2006]. It works best when the input frequency is close to the oscillation's own, as with pushing a swing in time with its natural period, so even a weak push can have an effect.

This is the proposed mechanism of [tACS](../../../../../medical-science-services-section/services/medical-technology-services-section/services/electrotherapy-devices-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transcranial-alternating-current-stimulation.md). An alternating [field](../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/physics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/electric-field.md) too weak to fire neurons can still bias *when* already-oscillating neurons fire, pulling the population's rhythm toward the stimulation frequency [@herrmann2013]. Flickering light and rhythmic sound can entrain sensory rhythms too, though the "binaural beats" and "brainwave entrainment" products sold for relaxation claim more than has been shown.
