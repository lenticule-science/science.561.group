Hyperbolic odor-space is the geometric model underlying [Combinatorial Scent Mereology](../../documents/articles/notes/combinatorial-scent-mereology.md) (CSM). The idea: human olfactory perception is structured by a hyperbolic manifold — a space of negative curvature — rather than by a Euclidean feature space.

Olfactory receptor binding patterns form branching similarity hierarchies that embed naturally in negatively curved space [@haddad2008]. Tree-like metric graphs embed quasi-isometrically in hyperbolic spaces, a consequence of [Mikhail Gromov](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/mikhail-gromov.md)'s work on hyperbolic groups [@gromov1987]. This means distances in the OR binding landscape grow exponentially from any reference point, consistent with psychophysical data showing non-metric similarity judgments and triangle inequality violations [@keller2017; @haddad2010].

See [Combinatorial Scent Mereology](../../documents/articles/notes/combinatorial-scent-mereology.md) in the [biology](../../biology/index.md) module for the full mathematical treatment.
