Perseverative cognition is repetitive, stressor-related thought — chiefly worry and rumination — that keeps a stress-related mental representation active in the absence of the stressor itself, and in doing so prolongs the physiological activation ordinarily triggered by the stressor's actual presence.

The concept was formalized as the perseverative cognition hypothesis by Jos Brosschot, Wendy Gerin, and Julian Thayer, who proposed that worry (future-oriented perseverative cognition) and rumination (past-oriented perseverative cognition) are functionally equivalent in this respect: both sustain cardiovascular, endocrine, immune, and neurovisceral activation well beyond the period in which any external threat is actually present, and both can even reactivate such activation after a stressor has already ended. A 2010 follow-up review specified the mechanism further, proposing that perseverative cognition is part of the default response to threat, novelty, and ambiguity, and identifying a particular deficit in people prone to chronic worry: difficulty recognizing signals of safety. This distinguishes perseverative cognition from ordinary, adaptive anticipatory or reflective thought — the pathology is not that the person thinks about a stressor, but that the thinking fails to be interrupted by evidence that the stressor is not, or is no longer, present.

Subsequent research has linked perseverative cognition to a range of health outcomes, including elevated health-risk behaviors such as substance use and unhealthy eating, largely mediated through rumination, and has connected the hypothesis to related clinical models such as the intolerance-of-uncertainty and uncertainty-and-anticipation frameworks in anxiety research, which locate the same failure to settle in the broader architecture of anxious cognition.

For the relational framework, perseverative cognition names a mode in which an organism's relation to a stressor is sustained entirely internally — the "threat" persists as an ongoing relation to a simulated future or replayed past, with no current external correlate to resolve it. This is precisely the mechanism by which an attention-economic account can locate a standing leak: cognitive and physiological resources remain committed to a relation that, having no live external counterpart, has no natural mechanism by which it can be checked and released, and can only be interrupted by actively building a competing relation — most directly, a learned association with a safety signal.
