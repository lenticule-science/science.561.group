Annotated bibliography for "Valenced Prospection and the Attention Budget." Each entry gives what the source establishes and a solidity note. Two items are flagged as contested or superseded on their own terms rather than simply cited: the ego-depletion/glucose replication failure, and the moderation of the Bar-Haim attentional-bias effect by later work.

## The substrate

**Desimone, R. & Duncan, J. (1995). Neural mechanisms of selective visual attention. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*.**
Establishes biased competition: co-present stimulus representations mutually suppress each other's neural gain, and top-down goals bias which representation wins. Foundational and well replicated; the field's baseline account of moment-to-moment selection.

**Bisley, J.W. & Goldberg, M.E. (2010). Attention, intention, and priority in the parietal lobe. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*.**
Establishes the priority-map account: activity in the lateral intraparietal area encodes a combined bottom-up/top-down priority signal whose peak drives both attention and saccade targeting. Solid, extensively cited, and extended by subsequent priority-map work through the 2020s.

**Wiehler, A., Branzoli, F., Adanyeguh, I., Mochel, F. & Pessiglione, M. (2022). A neuro-metabolic account of why daylong cognitive work alters the control of economic decisions. *Current Biology*.**
Establishes, via MR spectroscopy, that a day of demanding cognitive-control work produces measurable glutamate accumulation in lateral prefrontal cortex alongside a behavioral shift toward low-effort choices. A single strong study with a direct physiological marker rather than self-report; the strongest existing evidence for a literal, slowly-clearing metabolic cost of sustained attention/effort.

**Arnsten, A.F.T. (2009). Stress signalling pathways that impair prefrontal cortex structure and function. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*.**
Establishes the catecholamine signaling mechanisms by which acute and chronic stress degrade prefrontal persistent firing and working memory. A well-established, heavily cited synthesis of a multi-decade research program, mostly from animal models with converging human imaging support.

**Woo, E., Sansing, L.H., Arnsten, A.F.T. & Datta, D. (2021). Chronic stress weakens connectivity in the prefrontal cortex. *Chronic Stress*.**
Extends Arnsten's program to structural change: chronic uncontrollable stress produces architectural and molecular loss of prefrontal dendritic spines. Solid mechanistic extension; strongest in animal-model evidence, with human relevance inferred rather than directly imaged at this resolution.

**Kurzban, R., Duckworth, A., Kable, J.W. & Myers, J. (2013). An opportunity cost model of subjective effort and task performance. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*.**
Argues effort and attentional "depletion" are better modeled as opportunity-cost computations than as consumption of a physical resource. A theoretical target article with substantial critical commentary attached (as is the BBS format); influential but explicitly contested within the same publication, and it does not by itself explain why demanding tasks reliably feel harder over time — a criticism raised directly in the published commentaries.

**Hagger, M.S. et al. (2016). A multilab preregistered replication of the ego-depletion effect. *Perspectives on Psychological Science*.**
FLAGGED — CONTESTED/FAILED REPLICATION. Twenty-three independent labs, N = 2,141, tested the classic self-control-depletion paradigm and found d = 0.04, not significant; a 2021 follow-up designed with the original theorist's cooperation was also null. The glucose-depletion mechanism specifically has not survived scrutiny. This does not show cognitive fatigue is unreal (see Wiehler et al. above), only that the specific "willpower as depletable fuel, restored by glucose" story is not supported.

**Feldman, H. & Friston, K.J. (2010). Attention, uncertainty, and free-energy. *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*.**
Establishes attention as precision-optimization within hierarchical predictive coding, formalized via neuronal simulations of spatial attention and biased competition. Foundational within the active-inference research program; widely extended (Hohwy, Parr, and successors through the 2020s), though the framework's empirical falsifiability has itself been debated in the literature (e.g., "Attention is more than prediction precision," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*).

**Wickens, C.D. (2008; updated 2024). Multiple resources and mental workload; The multiple resource theory and model. *Human Factors*.**
Establishes that dual-task interference tracks shared processing structure (modality, code, processing stage) rather than a single undifferentiated capacity pool. A long-standing, empirically productive applied-psychology framework; the 2024 update itself addresses persistent misconceptions in how the model's predictions are interpreted, indicating the model is mature but still actively clarified.

## Hope and fear as rival policies

**Schacter, D.L. & Addis, D.R. (2007 and successors). The constructive episodic simulation hypothesis. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* and related work.**
Establishes that episodic memory and future simulation share a common constructive process and, largely, a common brain network. Foundational and extensively replicated; the field's dominant account of prospection.

**Spreng, R.N. and colleagues (2021). The ventral and dorsal default mode networks are dissociably modulated by the vividness and valence of imagined events. *Journal of Neuroscience*.**
Establishes that the default mode network fractionates into a vividness-tracking subsystem (valence-independent) and a valence/value-tracking subsystem (including vmPFC). Solid, well-designed fMRI study; the key evidence that hope and fear share a construction engine while differing in a separable valence-weighting component.

**Neural correlates of suppressing and imagining future threat (2025). *Scientific Reports*.**
Establishes that fear-simulation is generated within, and can be down-regulated within, the same prospective-imagination circuitry used for future thinking generally, rather than a dedicated separate module. Recent single study; consistent with but not yet as heavily replicated as the older DMN-prospection literature.

**Marsh, L., Edginton, T., Conway, M.A. & Loveday, C. (2019). Positivity bias in past and future episodic thinking. *Memory*.**
Establishes that anxiety is not associated with reduced episodic-future-thinking specificity overall, but is associated with more vivid negative-future simulation and less vivid positive-future simulation. A well-targeted individual-differences study; the strongest direct evidence in this bibliography for a valence-allocation tradeoff over a conserved construction resource.

## Fear's resolution condition

**Carleton, R.N. (2016a). Fear of the unknown: One fear to rule them all? (2016b). Into the unknown: A review and synthesis of contemporary models involving uncertainty. Both *Journal of Anxiety Disorders*.**
Establishes intolerance of uncertainty / fear of the unknown as a proposed transdiagnostic core construct across anxiety disorders. Influential and widely cited theoretical syntheses; treated in the field as a strong, though not universally accepted, unifying account rather than settled fact.

**Grupe, D.W. & Nitschke, J.B. (2013). Uncertainty and anticipation in anxiety: An integrated neurobiological and psychological perspective. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*.**
Establishes the uncertainty-and-anticipation model of anxiety, identifying five processes disrupted by uncertain future threat. The field's dominant integrative framework for over a decade, still being actively extended (e.g., 2023 work reframing anxiety explicitly as a disorder of uncertainty); solid and well-corroborated.

**Brosschot, J.F., Gerin, W. & Thayer, J.F. (2006). The perseverative cognition hypothesis. *Journal of Psychosomatic Research*; Brosschot, J.F., Verkuil, B. & Thayer, J.F. (2010) follow-up review.**
Establishes that worry and rumination prolong stress-related physiological activation beyond the stressor's actual presence, and that chronic worriers specifically show deficits recognizing safety signals. Well-supported, mechanistically specific, and directly relevant to naming what "resolution" requires.

**Craske, M.G., Treanor, M., Conway, C.C. & Zbozinek, T. (2014). Maximizing exposure therapy: An inhibitory learning approach. *Behaviour Research and Therapy*; updated 2022 as the OptEx Nexus framework.**
Establishes that exposure therapy works by building new inhibitory associations (safety learning) competing with, not erasing, the original fear association, and specifies concrete strategies (expectancy violation, removing safety behaviors, promoting generalization). Highly influential clinical-science framework with strong outcome-literature support; the 2022 update itself responds to intervening evidence.

**Meta-analysis of conditioned fear generalization in anxiety-related disorders (2022). *Neuropsychopharmacology*.**
Establishes a small-to-medium effect size for overgeneralization of fear across anxiety-disorder populations relative to controls. A methodologically strong, recent meta-analysis; the effect is real but modest in magnitude, not large.

**Trait anxiety and fear generalization: Overgeneralization of fear or undergeneralization of safety learning? (2025). *Behaviour Research and Therapy*.**
Argues the anxious-population deficit may be better characterized as failure to generalize safety learning than as excess generalization of threat. A recent, more specific reframing; not yet as well-replicated as the older overgeneralization literature it revises, but consistent with the inhibitory-learning mechanism above.

**Borkovec, T.D. (cognitive avoidance theory); Newman, M.G. & Llera, S.J. (2011). A novel theory of experiential avoidance in generalized anxiety disorder (contrast avoidance model). *Clinical Psychology Review*.**
Establishes worry as potentially functional — maintained to avoid a felt contrast between calm and sudden negative affect — rather than purely wasteful. One of several competing cognitive-behavioral models of GAD; well-regarded but not the field's sole accepted account, and it complicates any framing of unresolved worry as pure leak with no offsetting function.

## Compulsory threat-simulation

**Bar-Haim, Y., Lamy, D., Pergamin, L., Bakermans-Kranenburg, M.J. & van IJzendoorn, M.H. (2007). Threat-related attentional bias in anxious and nonanxious individuals: A meta-analytic study. *Psychological Bulletin*.**
FLAGGED — MODERATED BY LATER WORK. Established, across 172 studies (N = 2,263 anxious, N = 1,768 nonanxious), a reliable threat-related attentional bias in anxious populations (d = 0.45). Foundational and still widely cited, but subsequent meta-analyses and large-scale studies (through the 2020s) show the effect is smaller and more heavily moderated by age and measurement method than the original synthesis suggested, particularly in children and adolescents; cite as a real but not unconditional effect.

**Schmader, T. & Johns, M. (2003). Converging evidence that stereotype threat reduces working memory capacity. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*.**
Establishes, experimentally, that cuing an anticipated identity-based threat measurably reduces working-memory capacity and mediates subsequent performance decrements. A well-replicated, causally clean demonstration; the cleanest existing lab model of anticipatory, socially imposed threat taxing cognition before any adverse event occurs.

**Geronimus, A.T. et al. (2006). "Weathering" and age patterns of allostatic load scores among Blacks and Whites in the United States. *American Journal of Public Health*; Chae, D.H. et al. (2020). Race and ethnic variation in college students' allostatic regulation of racism-related stress. *PNAS*.**
Establishes that chronic exposure to discrimination and structural disadvantage is associated with elevated allostatic load independent of poverty. A long-running, well-corroborated public-health research program; the mechanism linking allostatic load specifically to prospective-simulation capacity (as opposed to physiological stress markers generally) remains an inference rather than a direct finding.

**Recent (2023–2025) cohort studies on neighborhood violence, discrimination, and cognition (e.g., studies using the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development cohort and Black urban-adult samples).**
Establish associations between neighborhood violence exposure, perceived discrimination, hypervigilance, and poorer working memory/spatial cognition, with effects surviving socioeconomic controls. Recent, generally well-powered cohort work; associational rather than causal, and measuring general executive function rather than default-mode prospective simulation specifically — the gap this note's Directions section flags as the theory's most valuable open test.
