Excitability is how readily a neuron, or a population of neurons, fires when input arrives. It depends on how far the [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md) sits below threshold and on the state of the neuron's ion channels. Nearer threshold means more excitable.

Weak transcranial stimulation is thought to act here rather than by firing neurons. In a landmark experiment, 1 mA of direct current applied for a few minutes over the motor cortex changed the size of muscle responses to a standard magnetic pulse, and the change outlasted the stimulation. The anodal electrode, the positive one, raised excitability; the cathodal one lowered it [@nitsche2000]. Such subthreshold shifts, summed across millions of neurons and repeated over sessions, are the proposed route by which [tDCS](../../../../../medical-science-services-section/services/medical-technology-services-section/services/electrotherapy-devices-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transcranial-direct-current-stimulation.md) could influence mood, pain or learning [@bikson2016].
