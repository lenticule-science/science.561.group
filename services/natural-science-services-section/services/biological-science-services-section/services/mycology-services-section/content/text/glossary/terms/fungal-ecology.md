## Assumed audience

General adult who has completed Fungal Biochemistry and Nutrition.

## Saprotrophs — the decomposers

[Saprotrophic](./saprotroph.md) fungi decompose dead organic matter — fallen leaves, dead wood, animal remains. They are the primary decomposers of [lignin](./lignin.md) (the structural polymer of wood), breaking down the [lignocellulose](./cellulose.md) that no other organism can fully dismantle. Without fungal decomposition, dead plant material would accumulate and nutrients would be locked away from living organisms. See [Decomposition as Relation](./decomposition-as-relation.md) for a deeper treatment.

## Mycorrhizal mutualists

Over 90% of plant species form [mycorrhizal](./mycorrhiza.md) associations with fungi. The fungus extends the plant's root network, providing enhanced access to phosphorus and nitrogen. The plant provides the fungus with photosynthetically fixed carbon. These partnerships are ancient (400+ million years) and foundational to terrestrial ecosystems.

## Lichens

[Lichens](./lichen.md) are composite organisms formed by fungi and photosynthetic partners (algae or cyanobacteria). They colonize bare rock and harsh environments where few other organisms survive. Lichens are important in soil formation and nitrogen fixation, gradually breaking down rock surfaces and creating substrate for other life.

## Pathogens and parasites

Some fungi cause disease in plants (rusts, smuts, mildews, blights) and animals (athlete's foot, ringworm, aspergillosis). Pathogenic fungi can devastate crops and forests. Chestnut blight and Dutch elm disease are well-known examples of fungal pathogens that reshaped entire landscapes.

## Nutrient cycling

Fungi are central to the carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus cycles (see [nutrient cycling](./nutrient-cycling.md)). Decomposition releases nutrients back into the soil; mycorrhizal networks redistribute them among living plants. [Arbuscules](./arbuscule.md) inside root cells are the sites where phosphorus is transferred from fungus to plant. [Root exudates](./root-exudate.md) — chemical signals secreted by plant roots — initiate and maintain these partnerships. Without fungi, terrestrial [ecosystems](../../../../../ecology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ecosystem.md) would lose the primary engine that keeps nutrients circulating between living and dead matter.

## Why this matters

Understanding fungal ecology explains why forests depend on fungi, why soil health requires fungal activity, and why fungal diseases matter for agriculture.
