## What this argues

The literature on myalgic encephalomyelitis reports findings in four
systems. The neuroendocrine literature reports blunted
hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal output — attenuated responses to
corticotropin-releasing hormone and adrenocorticotropic hormone
challenge, low-normal baseline free cortisol, a flattened cortisol
awakening response. The autonomic literature reports sympathetic
hyper-reactivity, orthostatic intolerance, and frequent comorbid
postural orthostatic tachycardia. The metabolic literature reports
impaired oxidative phosphorylation, a proposed inhibition of pyruvate
dehydrogenase, and a reduced ventilatory threshold on the second day of
a two-day cardiopulmonary exercise test. The immunological literature
reports cytokine abnormalities and, less consistently, evidence of
microglial activation.

Read as a list, this is four modest and individually unimpressive
findings, each with mixed replication, which is roughly how the illness
has been treated. This note argues for reading it differently: not as
four deficits that happen to co-occur, but as one failure of
coordination among systems that are supposed to fund each other. The
claim is that the *relations* between these systems are the pathology,
and that this is why the illness's defining feature is a symptom flare
that arrives a day or two late and then lasts for a week.

The argument is a synthesis of partially established findings. It is
not a demonstrated causal chain, and the note marks which is which
throughout.

## The healthy case: two arms, one response

The house's [physiology](../../../../../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal-axis.md)
carriers describe two arms of the mammalian stress response that answer
the same detected demand on different substrates and different clocks.
The sympathoadrenal arm is neural and immediate: preganglionic fibres
reach the adrenal medulla and catecholamines are circulating within
seconds, raising heart rate and constricting vessels. The
hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal arm is hormonal and sustained:
corticotropin-releasing hormone, then adrenocorticotropic hormone, then
cortisol, with a fifteen- to thirty-minute latency.

What matters is that these are not two parallel responses. They are one
response in which each arm depends on the other. The fast arm imposes
metabolic demand; only the slow arm's glucocorticoid output mobilizes
the substrate to meet it, through hepatic gluconeogenesis and lipolysis.
The fast arm's vasoconstriction depends on cortisol's permissive
upregulation of α1-adrenergic responsiveness — catecholamines without
glucocorticoid lose vascular traction. And the same cortisol that funds
the episode also ends it, both by feeding back on the hypothalamus to
stop further CRH synthesis and by transrepressing NF-κB to shut down the
inflammatory phase.

Cortisol, in other words, has three jobs in one molecule: it pays for
the response, it makes the response effective, and it terminates the
response. This is efficient and it is fragile, because a single failure
removes all three at once.

## The ME/CFS case: full demand, no funding

```
              exertion / orthostatic challenge
                      │
        ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
        ▼                           ▼
  sympathoadrenal arm         HPA arm
  fires at full output        blunted at every stage
  (catecholamine surge)       (low CRH drive, attenuated
        │                      ACTH, insufficient cortisol)
        │                           │
        └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                      ▼
        demand imposed, nothing funds it:
        no substrate mobilization
        no vascular tone support
        no inflammatory brake
```

The observed pattern is one arm at full output and the other arm
failing. This predicts something more specific than "hyperarousal," and
the specificity is what makes it worth stating.

**Orthostatic intolerance with normal blood pressure.** If catecholamines
are abundant but the vasculature is desensitized to them for want of
glucocorticoid support, the expected picture is erratic vasoconstriction
followed by venous pooling and cerebral hypoperfusion — symptoms of
standing that are severe while blood pressure readings stay
unremarkable. That is exactly the presentation that gets patients told
their orthostatic examination was normal. The prediction is not that
blood pressure falls; it is that cerebral perfusion fails while blood
pressure holds.

**Rapid arrival at the anaerobic threshold.** If oxidative
phosphorylation has a reduced rate ceiling, and if the hormonal
substrate mobilization that would ordinarily extend it is absent, cells
reach anaerobic metabolism at workloads that should be trivial. Aerobic
capacity is a rate, not a stock, so a lowered ceiling does not mean
running out later — it means failing at low intensity. This is a
different claim from deconditioning, and the two-day cardiopulmonary
exercise test is the instrument that distinguishes them, because
deconditioned people reproduce their day-one values and ME/CFS patients
characteristically do not.

**A compounding loop through the hypothalamus.** The paraventricular
nucleus is metabolically expensive. If systemic mitochondrial capacity
is impaired, the neurons that must synthesize corticotropin-releasing
hormone are themselves underfunded, which would deepen the central
blunting that started the sequence. This step is inference. It is
consistent with the rest and it is not established, and it should be
labelled as the weakest link in the chain rather than smoothed over.

## Why the flare is late, and why it lasts

The delayed onset is the part of post-exertional malaise that most
needs explaining, because nothing in the acute picture above is slow.
Catecholamine surges resolve in minutes. Lactate clears in hours. A
symptom flare arriving twenty-four to seventy-two hours later and
persisting for days is on a different timescale entirely, and any
account that cannot produce that timescale has not explained the
illness's defining feature.

The inflammatory arm supplies it. In the healthy case the cortisol
surge terminates the inflammatory phase within minutes to hours. Absent
an effective brake — whether because cortisol output is insufficient or
because chronic exposure has produced glucocorticoid resistance at the
receptor, which are different failures with the same downstream
result — the pro-inflammatory cascade triggered by the adrenergic surge
is not counter-regulated. Microglia and mast cells degranulate;
IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6 and reactive oxygen species enter parenchyma and
circulation and stay. Cytokine half-lives and the transcriptional
programs they induce operate on exactly the multi-day scale that
post-exertional malaise occupies.

So the temporal signature comes from the persistence rather than the
magnitude. It is not that the inflammatory response is unusually large;
it is that nothing turns it off, and an ordinary response that is never
terminated looks, from the outside, like a delayed catastrophic one.

This also closes a loop worth naming. Inflammatory cytokines impair
mitochondrial respiration — this is well established — and struggling
mitochondria generate reactive oxygen species that are themselves
inflammatory signals. Metabolic failure and inflammation are not
sequential here. They sustain one another, which is a reason a single
exertional trigger can produce a state that outlasts its cause.

## Where this connects to the house's existing carriers

This synthesis sits directly downstream of the 2026-08-26 work in
[biochemistry](../../../../../../../../../../../biochemistry-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/26/2026-08-26-note-on-inflammation-withdrawal-and-the-molecular-signature-of-abandonment.md).
That note reads sickness behaviour, following Hart and Dantzer, as
organized energy reallocation rather than as malfunction — the
withdrawal, the immobility, the loss of appetite are a coordinated
program, not a breakdown. Applied here, that framing changes what
post-exertional malaise is. The enforced rest is not merely a symptom
of the energy deficit; it is a response to it, and one that is
appropriate to a real shortfall. That reading has an obvious clinical
consequence and an obvious risk of being misread, so both should be
stated: it argues against interventions that override the withdrawal,
and it is not a claim that the illness is behavioural in origin.

The companion note on
[cytokine transcriptomics](../../../../../../../../../../../biochemistry-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/26/2026-08-26-note-on-the-cytokine-transcriptomic-machinery-of-social-standing.md)
supplies the machinery the missing brake acts on — the NF-κB and IRF3
antagonism and the myeloid bias that glucocorticoid transrepression
normally restrains.

The relationship to
[allostatic load](../../../../../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/allostatic-load.md)
is worth drawing precisely, because the two frameworks are easy to
conflate and are not the same. Allostatic load prices the cumulative
cost of running the stress response too often, too intensely, or for
too long — a response that works, made to work too much. The failure
described here is a response that does not work: full output on one arm,
insufficient output on the other, with the coordination between them
lost. A cumulative-wear composite would not detect it, and might well
read normal, because the individual markers can each sit within range
while their relations are deranged. These are different pathologies and
they call for different measurements.

## What would falsify this

The synthesis makes claims that can fail, and naming them is the point
of writing it down.

If HPA blunting in ME/CFS turns out to be secondary to deconditioning,
sleep disruption, or illness duration rather than primary — a live
possibility, and Papadopoulos and Cleare's reviews treat it as
unresolved — then the endocrine arm of the argument is an effect rather
than a cause, and the decoupling framing loses its starting point.

If the reduced day-two ventilatory threshold fails to replicate in
larger and better-controlled samples, the metabolic arm loses its only
provocable objective measure.

If the microglial-activation findings do not survive — Nakatomi and
colleagues' 2014 translocator-protein PET study is small, and the
interpretation of TSPO ligand binding is contested independently of
this literature — the neuroinflammatory arm rests on peripheral cytokine
data that has itself been inconsistent across studies.

And the whole synthesis is vulnerable to the case-definition problem.
Cohorts selected by Fukuda criteria, Canadian Consensus Criteria, and
the 2015 Institute of Medicine criteria are measurably different
populations, and findings that appear inconsistent across studies may
reflect that rather than any inconsistency in the underlying biology.
Until studies routinely report which definition selected their cohort,
the field's replication record is harder to read than it looks — which
is itself a reason to hold this note's argument as a frame for testing
rather than a conclusion reached.
