Persistent online conflict may contribute cognitive and emotional exertion in ME/CFS. Clinical evidence makes that a reasonable question, but it does not establish that Twitter triggers a particular biochemical pathway. Feeling engaged, performing a task, developing fatigue, and recovering from exertion are separate outcomes. Current spectroscopy findings do not identify a universal excess of brain glutamate or establish glutamate clearance failure as the cause of PEM.

The [companion on antagonistic Twitter](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/media-science-services-section/services/social-media-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/10/07/2026-10-07-note-on-the-cognitive-cost-of-antagonistic-twitter.md) describes the exposure: sustained disputes, interpretive and response demands, continued attention between encounters, and occasional pleasant interruptions. This note addresses the clinical and biochemical bridge. The individual’s actual diagnosis, symptom pattern, exposure, or mechanism cannot be inferred from these studies alone.

## Cognitive exertion and symptom recovery

Stussman and colleagues' 2020 *Frontiers in Neurology* study used nine focus groups with 43 people reporting an ME/CFS diagnosis. Participants described physical, cognitive, and emotional triggers, with variable onset, symptom patterns, and recovery. The study supports the clinical relevance of nonphysical exertion through patient accounts; it does not identify a molecular cause. Among 18 discussing exercise-test experiences, 17 described onset within 24 hours and a peak within 72 hours. That subgroup result should not become a fixed latency for every trigger or every person. [Study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2020.01025)

An experimental connection to cognition comes from Staud and colleagues' 2018 *Fatigue* study of 17 women meeting CDC criteria and 16 healthy controls. A demanding auditory arithmetic task increased fatigue in both groups. Global cerebral blood flow was similar between groups at rest and during the task; regional relationships between perfusion and fatigue recovery differed afterward. This was an acute cognitive challenge, not a Twitter exposure or demonstration of delayed PEM. The reported CDC selection also needs to remain distinct from cohorts requiring PEM. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1080/21641846.2018.1453919)

Together these sources justify attention to cognitive exertion and recovery. They do not license diagnosing a crash from fatigue during use, equating every unpleasant interaction with PEM, or inferring a universal failure of cerebral perfusion.

## Outcomes that must remain distinct

| Outcome | What it describes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subjective activation | Feeling alert, interested, angry, compelled, or able to initiate activity. | Sustainable capacity or recovery. |
| Cognitive performance | Accuracy, memory, processing speed, inhibition, or ability to carry out an intended task. | The effort required, symptom consequences, or absence of PEM. |
| Immediate fatigue | Feeling exhausted during or shortly after activity. | Delayed PEM or its mechanism. |
| Autonomic or vascular measurements | Particular changes in pulse, variability, blood pressure, or perfusion under specified conditions. | A general measure of total exertion or a specific diagnosis by themselves. |
| Delayed symptom exacerbation | A later worsening in symptoms and function considered in the clinical context of ME/CFS. | Which neurotransmitter or metabolic pathway caused it. |
| Regional MRS glutamate | A spectroscopic estimate of glutamate within a specified tissue volume. | Synaptic release, extracellular accumulation, receptor activation, or toxicity. |

These distinctions matter when someone can continue following conflict despite being unable to organize other work, or feels briefly better while the later symptom course worsens. Such patterns are possible observations to explain, not evidence of one predetermined mechanism.

## Cognitive fatigue and the interpretation of MRS

Wiehler and colleagues' 2022 *Current Biology* study compared demanding and easier cognitive work across an approximately daylong protocol. The demanding group showed greater lateral-prefrontal glutamate measures relative to the comparison group and visual reference region, together with shifts toward shorter-delay and lower-effort choices. The authors proposed a metabolic explanation involving glutamate handling. The participants were not an ME/CFS cohort. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2022.07.010)

Möller's 2023 *Journal of Cerebral Blood Flow and Metabolism* critique, published online in 2022, explains why these signals cannot identify physiological extracellular glutamate accumulation. MRS largely reflects intracellular pools; diffusion measures do not uniquely identify a compartment. There was no pre-task baseline, and the group contrast included declining glutamate in the easier condition. This is a methodological critique, not a replication. [Critique](https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X221139550)

Three claims must therefore remain separate: demanding work was associated with different glutamate measures; the authors proposed an explanation; extracellular clearance failure was not demonstrated. The [earlier glutamate and fear-settlement note](../../../../../../../../../../../biochemistry-services-section/services/neurochemistry-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/26/2026-08-26-note-on-the-glutamatergic-endocannabinoid-axis-of-fear-settlement.md) now states that distinction in its fatigue section.

Nor does the result show that sleep removes the observed signal through glymphatic flow, that antagonistic Twitter reproduces the effect, or that lowering glutamate would improve ME/CFS. Each requires additional evidence.

## Stress does not imply a measurable glutamate increase

Vinkers and colleagues' 2017 *NeuroImage Clinical* study randomized healthy men to a psychosocial stress or control condition. Glutamate data were available for 29 participants. Medial prefrontal spectroscopy before and approximately 30 minutes after the challenge found no significant stress effect on glutamate; available GABA measurements were also negative. This does not exclude transient, compartment-specific, or other regional changes. It does block treating psychological stress as sufficient evidence of an MRS-detectable glutamate increase. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2017.01.001)

Likewise, a BOLD activation change measures a hemodynamic response. It cannot by itself establish a particular neurotransmitter concentration or release rate. “Prefrontal cortex” also encompasses different regions and functions; lateral executive-control findings cannot simply be substituted for medial prefrontal or cingulate spectroscopy.

## Regional glutamate findings in ME/CFS

Thapaliya and colleagues' study, published online in April 2024 in *The American Journal of Medicine*, used 3T MRS in the posterior cingulate cortex of 17 ME/CFS participants, 17 long-COVID participants, and 10 controls. Glutamate was higher in both patient groups than controls, with no significant difference between patient groups. The small regional comparison supports an association, not general brain excess, excitotoxicity, or the response to cognitive exertion. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2024.04.007)

Godlewska and colleagues' 2025 *Molecular Psychiatry* study enrolled 24 ME/CFS participants, 25 with long COVID, and 24 controls for 7T spectroscopy. ME/CFS selection used Fukuda criteria. Table 2 showed nonsignificant glutamate comparisons in the pregenual and dorsal anterior cingulate, with omnibus p values of 0.104 and 0.226. Elevated lactate in ME/CFS was exploratory and not corrected for multiple testing; lactate did not correlate significantly with fatigue scores or cognitive measures. Valid metabolite counts varied. [Study and tables](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03108-8)

These studies sampled different regions and are not direct replications of each other. Anterior-cingulate null findings do not invalidate a posterior-cingulate association, and the posterior finding cannot establish anterior-cingulate or lateral-prefrontal excess. Lactate is also not a proxy for glutamate clearance failure. Long COVID and ME/CFS should remain distinct populations in this argument.

## A bounded hypothesis for antagonistic Twitter

The proposed sequence is repeated interpretation and response demands, sometimes accompanied by personally meaningful threat and continued attention between encounters, followed by a symptom course in someone with exertional intolerance. The social and clinical findings make that sequence worth investigating. The intermediate physiology is unresolved.

Competing explanations include cognitive demands without measurable transmitter accumulation, interference associated with threat, prolonged involvement in unresolved interactions, and other contributors to the later symptom course. Posture and orthostatic intolerance, disrupted sleep, other activity, baseline illness variation, and medication timing may overlap with feed exposure. Their relevance must be established rather than assumed.

The existing [decoupling synthesis](../../09/01/2026-09-01-note-on-decoupling-as-the-shape-of-post-exertional-malaise.md) proposes an account linking multiple physiological systems. It remains a proposed explanation and does not establish that insufficient hormonal support causes the cost of online conflict.

## Evidence that would change the conclusion

A specific glutamate mechanism would need evidence linking this exposure to a change in a named brain region or signaling compartment, then linking that change to function or the subsequent symptom course. A tissue-level MRS difference alone would still require interpretation. A causal account would also need temporal ordering and evidence capable of distinguishing the mechanism from alternatives.

A clinical exposure association can be studied without resolving that mechanism. It would need to distinguish antagonism and cognitive demands from total screen duration; describe participation and personal stakes; and examine both immediate experience and later symptoms. A short performance test cannot substitute for the person's sustained functioning or recovery.

No fixed safe session length, glutamate-directed treatment, or deliberate symptom-provocation protocol follows from the current evidence. The usable conclusion is narrower: sustained conflict is a plausible source of cognitive and emotional exertion, and its immediate attraction does not answer the ME/CFS recovery question.
