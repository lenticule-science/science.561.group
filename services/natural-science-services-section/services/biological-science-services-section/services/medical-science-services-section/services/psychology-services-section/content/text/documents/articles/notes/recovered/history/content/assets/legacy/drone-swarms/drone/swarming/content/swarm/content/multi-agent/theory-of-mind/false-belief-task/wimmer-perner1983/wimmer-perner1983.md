# Wimmer & Perner 1983 — False belief task

Wimmer, H., & Perner, J.
(1983).
Beliefs about beliefs: Representation and constraining function of wrong beliefs in young children's understanding of deception. *Cognition*, 13(1), 103-128.

The paper introducing the *false-belief task* — the diagnostic experimental paradigm for theory-of-mind.
The Maxi/chocolate scenario tests whether subjects can predict an agent's behavior given that the agent has a *false* belief about the world's state.

The methodological contribution was substantial: previous ToM research could be criticized for testing behavior-consistent-with-ToM rather than ToM itself (per the underdetermination concern raised in commentary on Premack-Woodruff).
The false-belief task *disambiguated* — passing it requires distinguishing the agent's belief from the world's actual state, which behavioral pattern matching alone cannot do.

The task and its variants (Sally-Anne by Baron-Cohen et al. 1985; unexpected-contents by Perner, Leekam, and Wimmer 1987; many others) became the standard probes for ToM in developmental psychology.
The age-4 transition to passing is one of the most replicated findings in cognitive development.

Adaptation of the false-belief task to LLM evaluation (Kosinski 2023, Ullman 2023, others) is the most direct inheritance from this work to contemporary machine ToM research.
