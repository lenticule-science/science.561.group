# Premack & Woodruff 1978 — Theory of mind

Premack, D., & Woodruff, G.
(1978).
Does the chimpanzee have a theory of mind? *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 1(4), 515-526.

The founding paper of theory-of-mind research.
Premack and Woodruff coined the term *theory of mind* to denote "a system of inferences" by which an agent attributes mental states to others.
They asked whether their chimpanzee Sarah had this capacity, using tasks where Sarah selected photographs depicting solutions to problems she observed a human actor confronting.

The paper's central methodological move — testing ToM through behavioral predictions about agents-with-mental- states — set the agenda for decades of subsequent research.
The accompanying open peer commentary (BBS-style) included responses from Dennett, Fodor, Searle, and many others, raising the *underdetermination* issue that remains central to contemporary machine ToM debates.

The paper is widely cited as the founding moment of modern ToM research.
The framework it proposed — mental-state attribution as a research-tractable cognitive capacity — has structured developmental psychology, comparative cognition, philosophy of mind, and now machine ToM.
