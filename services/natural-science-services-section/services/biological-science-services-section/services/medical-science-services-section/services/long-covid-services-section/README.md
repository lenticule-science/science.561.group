# Long COVID Services Section

The Long COVID Services Section maintains knowledge for the study of
long COVID / post-COVID-19 condition (post-acute sequelae of
SARS-CoV-2 infection, PASC): the definitional history that determines
what a given study is actually measuring, the prevalence estimates
with their real uncertainty, and an honest map of the mechanistic
hypotheses — which are established, which are mechanistic hypotheses
still being tested, which are not yet replicated, and which are
contested.

## The organizing problem: definition first

Long COVID is not one thing measured three different ways; it is three
different case definitions producing three different populations, and
a claim from one is not automatically a claim about the other two.

- **WHO clinical case definition (2021/2022).** A WHO-led Delphi
  consensus of 265 patients, clinicians, researchers, and WHO staff,
  evaluating 14 domains and 45 items across two rounds, arrived at:
  occurrence in a person with a history of probable or confirmed
  SARS-CoV-2 infection, usually three months from COVID-19 onset, with
  symptoms lasting at least two months that cannot be explained by an
  alternative diagnosis. Symptoms — commonly fatigue, shortness of
  breath, and cognitive dysfunction — generally impact everyday
  functioning and may be new-onset after initial recovery or persist
  from the initial illness. This is the clinical definition, built for
  diagnosis and international epidemiological comparison (Soriano et
  al., 2022, *Lancet Infectious Diseases* 22(4):e102-e107,
  doi:10.1016/S1473-3099(21)00703-9, PMID 34951953). Established
  finding as a consensus artifact: it is what the field agreed to
  call the condition, not a mechanistic claim about its cause.
- **RECOVER research definition (2023).** The NIH RECOVER initiative's
  empirically derived definition, built from 9,764 RECOVER-Adult
  participants (8,646 with SARS-CoV-2 infection, 1,118 uninfected
  controls). Forty-four participant-reported symptoms were screened at
  six months or more post-infection; 12 were retained in a weighted
  composite score — post-exertional malaise, fatigue, brain fog,
  dizziness, gastrointestinal symptoms, palpitations, changes in
  sexual desire or capacity, loss of or change in smell or taste,
  thirst, chronic cough, chest pain, and abnormal movements — with a
  higher score indicating greater likelihood of PASC. In the most
  methodologically rigorous subgroup (participants first infected on
  or after December 2021 and enrolled within 30 days of infection,
  n=2,231), 10% (95% CI 8.8-11%) were classified PASC-positive at six
  months (Thaweethai et al., 2023, *JAMA* 329(22):1934-1946,
  doi:10.1001/jama.2023.8823, PMID 37278994). This is a research
  instrument, not a clinical diagnosis — it exists to give cohort
  studies a consistent, symptom-weighted outcome variable, and its
  symptom list is itself evidence about which symptoms best
  distinguish infected from uninfected people, not a claim about
  mechanism.
- **NASEM definition (2024).** Commissioned by HHS (OASH/ASPR), the
  National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine issued a
  substantially broader definition on June 11, 2024: long COVID is an
  infection-associated chronic condition that occurs after SARS-CoV-2
  infection and is present for at least three months as a continuous,
  relapsing and remitting, or progressive disease state affecting one
  or more organ systems, with presentation ranging from a single
  symptom to multiple diagnosable conditions (interstitial lung
  disease, arrhythmias, POTS, ME/CFS, diabetes, autoimmune disorders,
  among others) (National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and
  Medicine, *A Long COVID Definition: A Chronic, Systemic Disease
  State with Profound Consequences*, The National Academies Press,
  2024). This definition deliberately widens the frame from a symptom
  cluster to an umbrella disease state, folding in entities (ME/CFS,
  POTS) that have their own case-definition histories — a widening
  that changes what any given prevalence or mechanism study can be
  read to say about "long COVID" as a whole.

These three are not competing errors to reconcile into one number;
they are different instruments built for different purposes (clinical
diagnosis, research outcome measurement, health-system disease-state
scoping), and any claim sourced from one must be read against which
one it used. This is the same definitional-heterogeneity problem
documented for myalgic encephalomyelitis, and it is why ME/CFS appears
inside the NASEM definition as one of the diagnosable conditions long
COVID can resolve into — the two illnesses' research literatures share
this validity problem structurally, not just by symptom overlap.

## Prevalence: real uncertainty, not one number

- **CDC Household Pulse Survey**, self-reported "ever had long COVID" /
  "currently has long COVID" among noninstitutionalized US adults:
  prevalence among all adults fell from 7.5% (June 2022) to 6.0% (June
  2023), and among adults who had ever had COVID-19, from 18.9% to
  11.0%, over the same interval; a March 2024 wave put current
  prevalence at 7% of all adults (roughly 17 million people) (MMWR
  72(32):866-870, 2023, PMID 37561665). This is a self-report survey
  with no clinical or research case-definition confirmation behind the
  "yes/no" — established as a tracking trend, not a validated case count.
- **Global Burden of Disease Long COVID Collaborators**, modeling three
  symptom clusters (fatigue, cognitive problems, respiratory
  symptoms) across ten cohort studies in ten countries: an estimated
  144.7 million people worldwide experienced at least one cluster in
  2020-2021 (95% uncertainty interval 54.8-312.9 million) (Wulf Hanson
  et al., 2022, *JAMA* 328(16):1604-1615, doi:10.1001/jama.2022.18931,
  PMID 36215063). The near-6-fold spread between the low and high
  bound of that interval is itself the finding: modeled global
  prevalence is not a settled number.
- **O'Mahoney et al. meta-analysis** (2023, *eClinicalMedicine* 55:
  101762, PMID 36474804) pooled studies with ≥100 participants at ≥28
  days post-infection and reported 45% of COVID-19 survivors
  experiencing at least one unresolved symptom at approximately four
  months, hospitalized and non-hospitalized combined. This figure is
  contested within the literature — published correspondence in the
  same journal ("Was the rate of Long Covid as high as 45%–a scary
  report with flaw" *eClinicalMedicine* 2023) argues the pooled
  estimate is inflated by uncontrolled studies (no uninfected
  comparator) and by heterogeneous, low symptom-duration thresholds
  across the included studies — precisely the definitional-instrument
  problem named above, propagated into a meta-analysis.

Read together: self-report surveys, modeled global burden estimates,
and pooled meta-analyses of heterogeneous case definitions give
different numbers for different questions, and none of the three is
a ground-truth prevalence of "long COVID" as a single validated
condition — because no single validated case definition of it yet
exists across all three literatures.

## Mechanistic hypotheses: an honest map

No hypothesis below is established as *the* cause of long COVID; long
COVID is very likely mechanistically heterogeneous across patients,
and these are the major candidate mechanisms with their current
evidence state, not a ranked list of confidence.

- **Viral persistence.** SARS-CoV-2 RNA or antigen persisting in
  tissue beyond acute infection, detected across multiple solid-organ
  tissue types for months post-infection, with persistent infection
  associated with over 50% higher odds of self-reported long COVID in
  a large community surveillance study of the UK population (Ghafari
  et al., 2024, *Nature* 626:1094-1101, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07029-4,
  PMID 38383783). **Mechanistic hypothesis, actively researched** —
  association with symptom persistence is documented; whether the
  persisting material is replication-competent and causally drives
  symptoms, versus being an inert correlate of the same underlying
  susceptibility, is not yet settled.
- **Immune dysregulation / autoimmunity.** Klein et al. (2023, *Nature*
  623(7985):139-148, doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06651-y, PMID 37756695)
  cross-sectionally immune-profiled 275 individuals and found long
  COVID associated with altered myeloid and lymphoid compartments
  (non-conventional monocytes, activated and double-negative B cells,
  exhausted CD4+ T cells), exaggerated humoral response against
  SARS-CoV-2, elevated antibody responses to non-SARS-CoV-2 pathogens
  (notably Epstein-Barr virus), and lower cortisol — but did **not**
  find elevated autoantibodies as a distinguishing feature in this
  cohort. Other groups (Iwasaki lab and others) have reported
  significant autoantibody elevation in long COVID cohorts. **Mixed
  evidence, actively contested**: immune dysregulation broadly is a
  well-supported association; autoimmunity specifically as a driver
  is asserted by some cohorts and not replicated in others, likely
  reflecting both cohort and case-definition heterogeneity.
- **Latent virus reactivation (Epstein-Barr virus).** Su et al. (2022,
  *Cell* 185(5):881-895.e20, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.01.014, PMID
  35216672), a longitudinal multi-omic study of 309 COVID-19 patients,
  found EBV viremia in 14% of patients at diagnosis and that early EBV
  viremia and early SARS-CoV-2 RNAemia were each significantly
  associated with development of specific long COVID symptom clusters
  months later, alongside type 2 diabetes and specific autoantibodies
  as the other two of four identified early risk factors. **Mechanistic
  hypothesis with prospective supporting evidence**: the association
  is longitudinal and prospective (measured at diagnosis, symptoms
  assessed months later), which is stronger than cross-sectional
  correlation, but a causal reactivation-drives-symptoms mechanism
  is not yet demonstrated.
- **Endothelial dysfunction / fibrin amyloid microclots.** Pretorius,
  Kell, and colleagues report anomalous amyloid-form fibrin clots
  resistant to normal fibrinolysis, together with platelet
  hyperactivation, in long COVID plasma samples (Pretorius et al.,
  2022, *Cardiovasc Diabetol* 21:148, PMID 35933347), proposed to
  impair capillary oxygen delivery. **Contested mechanistic
  hypothesis, not yet replicated at scale**: the finding comes
  substantially from one research group's methodology; independent
  large-cohort replication and a demonstrated causal link from
  microclot burden to specific symptoms are both still outstanding,
  and the framing is treated in the wider field as one candidate among
  several rather than a settled mechanism.
- **Skeletal muscle / mitochondrial dysfunction.** Appelman et al.
  (2024, *Nat Commun* 15:17, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-44432-3) showed
  that in long COVID patients with post-exertional malaise, a
  standardized exercise challenge produced measurably worse muscle
  abnormalities the following day: lower oxidative phosphorylation
  capacity, reduced succinate dehydrogenase activity, amyloid-containing
  deposits in muscle tissue, and a shift toward glycolytic metabolism.
  **Mechanistic hypothesis with direct structural/metabolic evidence in
  this cohort, not yet replicated at scale**: the finding is a direct
  structural/metabolic parallel to the post-exertional malaise mechanism
  under active investigation in myalgic encephalomyelitis (see that section),
  but independent large-cohort replication is still outstanding. Whether
  this is a cause of post-exertional malaise or a downstream consequence of the
  same upstream process driving it is not yet resolved.
- **Dysautonomia / autonomic dysfunction.** Cross-sectional studies
  report autonomic symptom burden (COMPASS-31) in a majority of long
  COVID patients, with postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome
  (POTS) the most frequently reported specific autonomic diagnosis
  (Eldokla et al., 2022, *Ann Clin Transl Neurol*, doi:10.1002/acn3.51557,
  PMID 35393771, n=320). A proposed anatomical substrate — vagus nerve
  inflammation — has been reported in COVID-19 (Woo et al., 2023,
  *Acta Neuropathol* 146:387-394, doi:10.1007/s00401-023-02612-x,
  PMID 37452829). **Mechanistic hypothesis with cross-sectional
  supporting evidence**: autonomic symptom burden is well-documented in
  long COVID cohorts, but the evidence comes from cross-sectional
  observation rather than prospective study, and vagus nerve
  inflammation as the causal substrate is not yet replicated across
  independent cohorts.
- **Gut microbiome dysbiosis.** Liu et al. (2022, *Gut* 71(3):544-552,
  doi:10.1136/gutjnl-2021-325989, PMID 35082169) followed a prospective
  cohort and found gut microbiota composition at hospital admission
  predicted post-acute COVID-19 syndrome status at six months, with
  dysbiosis correlating with persistent symptoms including fatigue and
  poor memory; other groups report dysbiosis persisting beyond one
  year post-clearance. **Mechanistic hypothesis with prospective
  correlational support**: prediction from admission microbiome
  composition is a notable temporal claim, but a causal
  gut-to-symptom pathway (versus dysbiosis as a shared downstream
  effect of illness severity) is not established.

Each hypothesis above is a candidate contributor in some subset of
patients, not a unified theory of long COVID; the section's own
synthesis work is to track which combinations of case definition,
cohort, and mechanism actually co-occur in the literature, rather than
treating any single mechanism paper as generalizing to "long COVID" as
defined by a different instrument.

## Upstream dependencies

- **Immunology** — immunology holds the cytokine biology, T-cell exhaustion,
  and humoral response concepts that the immune-dysregulation and
  EBV-reactivation hypotheses are built from.
- **Human Body** — [Human Body](../human-body-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/inflammation.md)
  holds inflammation itself as a carrier concept.
- **Pain** — [Pain](../pain-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/central-sensitization.md)
  holds central sensitization, relevant where long COVID pain and
  cognitive symptoms are read through a sensitization frame rather
  than a peripheral-pathology frame.
- **Myalgic Encephalomyelitis** — [Myalgic Encephalomyelitis](../myalgic-encephalomyelitis-services-section/README.md)
  holds post-exertional malaise, the HPA-axis/sympathoadrenal
  decoupling frame, and the case-definition-heterogeneity problem this
  section inherits structurally; a subset of long COVID patients meet
  ME/CFS case criteria, and the two sections' mechanistic maps
  (mitochondrial dysfunction, dysautonomia, neuroinflammation)
  substantially overlap without being asserted identical.

This section does not restate those carriers. It holds the
definitional history, prevalence evidence, and mechanistic map
specific to long COVID / post-COVID-19 condition / PASC.

Its material is organized by artifact role under `content/`.
