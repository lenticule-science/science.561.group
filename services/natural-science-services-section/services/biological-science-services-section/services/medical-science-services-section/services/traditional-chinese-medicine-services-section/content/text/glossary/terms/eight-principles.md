The Eight Principles (ba gang 八纲) are the most fundamental diagnostic framework in [Traditional Chinese Medicine](traditional-chinese-medicine.md).
They organize all clinical findings into four pairs of complementary opposites that locate a patient's condition within a diagnostic coordinate system:

| Pair | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Interior / Exterior** (li / biao 里/表) | Where is the problem? |
| **Hot / Cold** (re / han 热/寒) | What is its thermal character? |
| **Excess / Deficiency** (shi / xu 实/虚) | Is something present that shouldn't be, or absent that should be? |
| **[Yin / Yang](yin-yang.md)** (yin / yang 阴/阳) | What is the overall character? |

Every TCM diagnosis begins with the Eight Principles.
Before identifying which organ system is involved or which specific pathological products are present, the practitioner determines: is this interior or exterior?
Hot or cold?
Excess or deficient?
Yin or Yang?
These four determinations establish the basic treatment strategy — the wrong determination leads to the wrong treatment.

## The four pairs

**Interior / Exterior**: Exterior patterns are acute, surface-level, and caused by external invasion — fever, chills, body aches, floating pulse.
Interior patterns are chronic, deep, and involve the [organ-function systems](zang-fu.md) — organ-specific symptoms, deep pulse.
Treatment of exterior patterns "releases the exterior" (inducing sweating).
Treatment of interior patterns addresses the specific organ dysfunction.

**Hot / Cold**: Hot patterns show inflammation — redness, thirst for cold, dark urine, rapid pulse, red tongue with yellow coating.
Cold patterns show insufficient warmth — pallor, preference for warm, clear urine, slow pulse, pale tongue with white coating.
Hot patterns are treated with cooling methods; cold patterns with warming methods.
[Hu Zhang](hu-zhang.md), being slightly cold, treats hot patterns.

**Excess / Deficiency**: Excess means a pathogenic factor is present — stagnation, accumulation, invasion.
Symptoms are strong, forceful.
Deficiency means a vital substance is depleted — [Qi](qi.md), [Blood](xue.md), [Yin](yin-deficiency.md), [Yang](yang-deficiency.md).
Symptoms are weak, dull.
Excess is treated by draining; deficiency by tonifying.
Many presentations are mixed.

**Yin / Yang**: The master pair that summarizes the others.
Yin patterns are interior + cold + deficient.
Yang patterns are exterior + hot + excess.
This pair provides the highest-level diagnostic summary.

## Why the Eight Principles prevent harm

The Eight Principles exist to prevent the most dangerous prescribing errors: - Applying cold treatment to a cold pattern (deepens the cold) - Applying hot treatment to a hot pattern (inflames the heat) - Draining a deficient patient (further depletes) - Tonifying an excess pattern (strengthens the pathogen)

Each of these errors makes the patient worse.
The Eight Principles are the minimum diagnostic framework needed to avoid them.
