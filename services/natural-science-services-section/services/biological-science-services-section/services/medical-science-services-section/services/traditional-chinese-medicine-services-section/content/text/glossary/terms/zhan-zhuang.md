Zhan zhuang (站樁), "standing like a post", is still [qigong](./qigong.md). The practitioner holds one standing posture for minutes at a time, from a few minutes at first to half an hour or more, and does not move.

The best-known posture, "embracing the tree" (chēng bào zhuāng), goes like this. Feet shoulder-width apart, knees softly bent, pelvis dropped so the lower back lengthens, arms raised and rounded in front of the chest as if holding a large ball or tree trunk, shoulders and elbows sunk, gaze level. Breath is natural and gradually lengthens, and attention rests in the lower [dantian](./dantian.md) or on the body as a whole.

Standing practice belongs to both the martial and the health lineages. In twentieth-century martial arts it was central to Yiquan, developed by Wang Xiangzhai, where it trains structural alignment and relaxed strength. In health qigong it is taught as the simplest complete exercise of the [three regulations](./san-tiao.md). The body is regulated by alignment. The breath is regulated by letting it slow. The mind is regulated by staying with sensation.

The load is isometric and sustained, mostly in the thighs and shoulders. Beginners typically notice trembling, heat, and fatigue there long before anything else. Teachers treat these as expected and advise standing shorter and more often rather than forcing duration.
