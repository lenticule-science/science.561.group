A biochemistry note written the same day as this one — "The Cytokine–Transcriptomic Machinery of Social Standing," in the Biochemistry Services Section — lays out, in molecular detail, how chronic social stress in mammals reroutes the production of blood cells. Read next to it, three TCM claims that have sat in the tradition's textbooks for two thousand years look uncannily on-topic: the Spleen makes blood, worry damages the Spleen, and marrow belongs to the Kidney. This note takes that resonance seriously enough to test it against both literatures precisely, rather than gesture at it. The verdict is not "TCM knew this all along" and not "TCM was groping toward this." It is narrower and more useful: two systems, cutting the body along different lines for different purposes, produced structures that partially align — and the places where the alignment fails are as informative as the places where it holds. Throughout, a claim marked **[rhyme]** is a recorded structural coincidence, not a validation of either system by the other; a claim marked **[break]** is a place the resonance gives out under scrutiny.

## 1. The Biomedical Finding, Restated

Four independent lines of mouse and macaque work, converging over roughly a decade, established that chronic social-threat states physically relocate blood-cell production:

- **Heidt et al. (2014)**, *Nature Medicine* 20(7):754–758: in a chronic-variable-stress paradigm, sympathetic nerve fibers in the bone marrow release excess norepinephrine, which acts on β3-adrenergic receptors on marrow niche (stromal) cells to suppress their production of CXCL12 — the chemokine that normally holds hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) quiescent in the niche. Falling local CXCL12 releases HSPCs into cycling, raising monocyte and neutrophil output. β3-adrenergic-receptor-knockout mice show a blunted version of the whole cascade.
- **Powell et al. (2013)**, *PNAS* 110(41):16574–16579: repeated social defeat in mice drives inflammatory leukocyte gene expression via exactly this route — β-adrenergic-induced myelopoiesis, blockable with β-adrenergic antagonists and GM-CSF antagonism.
- **McKim, Yin, Wang, Cole, Godbout & Sheridan (2018)**, *Cell Reports* 25(9):2552–2562: repeated social defeat mobilizes HSPCs out of the bone marrow entirely. They engraft in the **spleen** and establish a persistent extramedullary hematopoietic depot, still producing monocytes, neutrophils, and erythrocytes at least 24 days after the stressor itself has ended. Repeated isoprenaline injection (a β-adrenergic agonist) reproduces the splenic mobilization on its own, without any social-defeat exposure.
- **Sloan et al. (2007)**, *J Neuroscience* 27(33):8857–8865: chronic social subordination in macaques increases the density of catecholaminergic nerve fibers inside lymph nodes — the sympathetic wiring into lymphoid tissue itself remodels under chronic threat, on a slower timescale than the hormonal signaling above.

The shape of the finding: an emotional/social state (inescapable, repeated defeat), acting through a well-characterized autonomic pathway (sympathetic norepinephrine → β-adrenergic receptors), changes *where in the body* blood is made, and the changed location persists well past the provoking event. This is squarely a finding about substances (HSPCs, and downstream, blood cells) moving to and lodging in the wrong compartment under sustained threat.

## 2. The Resonance, Read Literally

Three TCM claims map onto this with startling immediacy if read at face value.

### 2.1 脾為氣血生化之源 — the Spleen as the source of qi and blood

The phrase "脾胃為後天之本，氣血生化之源" (the Spleen-Stomach is the root of postnatal life, the source from which Qi and Blood are engendered and transformed) is Li Dongyuan's (李東垣) formulation in the *Pi Wei Lun* (《脾胃論》, *Treatise on the Spleen and Stomach*, Jin-Yuan dynasty). Its physiological mechanism traces to *Su Wen* Chapter 21, 經脈別論 (*Jing Mai Bie Lun*): "飲入於胃，游溢精氣，上輸於脾，脾氣散精，上歸於肺" — drink enters the stomach, its essential qi is extracted and transmitted up to the Spleen, and the Spleen's qi disperses that essence upward to the Lung. The Spleen's function of 運化 (transformation and transportation) is what takes ingested food and drink and turns it into Gu Qi (穀氣, grain qi), the raw material the Heart then transforms into Blood proper (see the glossary term "Blood (Xue)"). The Spleen is also said to 統血 (govern/hold blood in the vessels) — an idea given its clearest classical statement not in the *Nei Jing* but by the Ming physician Xue Ji (薛己) in his case records: "心主血，肝藏血，脾能統攝於血" (the Heart governs Blood, the Liver stores Blood, the Spleen is able to hold and govern Blood).

**[rhyme]** A functional system named "Spleen" is credited, in TCM's oldest and most durable formula, with being the origin point of Blood — and the biomedical literature above shows the anatomical spleen becoming, under exactly the triggering condition TCM would call excessive 思 (pensiveness, see below), a literal site of blood-cell manufacture. The word "spleen" sits at the crux of both claims. That is a genuine coincidence of vocabulary and role, not an illusion of translation — the character 脾 (pí) has referred to the same general abdominal region since antiquity (§3.2 below).

### 2.2 思傷脾 — pensiveness/rumination injures the Spleen

*Su Wen* Chapter 5, 陰陽應象大論 (*Yin Yang Ying Xiang Da Lun*), lays out the Five Phase correspondences between emotion and organ system, including: "在志為思，思傷脾，怒勝思" (in the emotions [the Earth phase] is Pensiveness; Pensiveness injures the Spleen; Anger overcomes Pensiveness — an entry in the restraining/counteracting cycle of the Five Phases, not merely a causal note). This is one clause in a larger scheme — 七情 (the Seven Affects), first collected and systematized as a diagnostic category of internal causation by the Song physician Chen Yan (陳言/陳無擇) in the *San Yin Ji Yi Bing Zheng Fang Lun* (《三因極一病證方論》, c. 1174 CE): 喜、怒、憂、思、悲、恐、驚 — joy, anger, worry, pensiveness, grief, fear, fright — each assigned to a Zang organ, each pathogenic specifically when excessive, prolonged, or suppressed rather than merely felt.

**[rhyme]** The mouse social-defeat paradigm is not "sadness" or "fear" in any simple sense — it is chronic, repeated, *unresolved* social threat: an animal defeated again and again with no means of escaping the relationship. TCM's diagnostic emphasis on prolonged and unreleased affect, rather than affect itself, as the pathogenic ingredient rhymes with the biomedical requirement that the stressor be *repeated* and *inescapable* (a single defeat does not establish the splenic depot; McKim's protocol runs ten cycles of social defeat over two weeks) before the myeloid bias becomes durable.

### 2.3 腎主骨生髓 — the Kidney governs the bones and engenders marrow

*Su Wen* Chapter 5 gives the Water-phase correspondence chain: "北方生寒，寒生水，水生鹹，鹹生腎，腎生骨髓，髓生肝，腎主耳" (the North engenders Cold; Cold engenders Water; Water engenders saltiness; saltiness engenders the Kidney; the Kidney engenders bone and marrow; marrow engenders the Liver; the Kidney governs the ears). Marrow (髓, sui) — along with the brain, bone, blood vessels, gallbladder, and uterus — is named in *Su Wen* Chapter 11, 五藏別論 (*Wu Zang Bie Lun*), as one of the 奇恆之腑 (the "extraordinary" or "curious" organs): "腦、髓、骨、脈、膽、女子胞，此六者，地氣之所生也，皆藏於陰而象於地，故藏而不瀉，名曰奇恆之府" — these six are engendered by earth-qi, store yin, resemble the earth in character, store without discharging, and are therefore named the extraordinary organs. *Ling Shu* Chapter 33, 海論 (*Hai Lun*), adds "腦為髓之海" (the brain is the sea of marrow) — 髓 in TCM spans bone marrow and the spinal cord/brain substance in a single category that biomedicine keeps separate.

**[rhyme]** The organ TCM makes responsible for marrow's *existence* (Kidney/Jing) is distinct from the organ TCM makes responsible for blood's *existence* (Spleen) — and the biomedical finding is precisely that marrow's hematopoietic *function* has been evicted from its own house and relocated into the Spleen's. If one insists on reading the three classical claims as a single connected story, the story is almost exactly the biomedical one: an organ that makes blood (Spleen) begins doing the job normally done by the organ that makes marrow (Kidney), under an etiology (思, chronic unresolved strain) that is Spleen's own named vulnerability.

## 3. Where the Resonance Breaks

Read that way, the three classical claims line up almost too neatly — which is the signal to slow down and check what "almost too neatly" is hiding.

### 3.1 Two different senses of "making blood"

TCM's claim that the Spleen "makes blood" describes a **nutritive-transformation** process: food and drink are broken down, their essence extracted (運化), and that essence supplies the raw material the Heart then works into Blood. It is, in the vocabulary this note develops in §4, a claim about *nourishment and transport of substrate*, not about *cellular manufacture*. Biomedical hematopoiesis is the clonal proliferation and differentiation of hematopoietic stem cells into blood cell lineages — a process with a defined cell of origin (the HSC), a defined niche architecture, and no meaningful analogue to "digesting food into blood's raw material." **[break]** When TCM says the Spleen makes Blood and biomedicine says the spleen (in stressed mice) makes blood cells, the word "makes" is doing two structurally different jobs — one about metabolism and transport of nutritive essence, the other about stem-cell fate commitment. The coincidence is lexical and thematic before it is mechanistic.

### 3.2 The TCM Spleen is a functional system with its own anatomical history — not simply "wrong" biomedical anatomy

It is a common but flattening move to treat TCM's Zang-Fu organs as purely metaphorical labels with no anatomical referent at all (see the glossary term "Zang-Fu" on this point generally). The historical record is more interesting than that. The *Nan Jing* (《難經》), Difficulty 42 (四十二難), gives the Spleen a physical description including "散膏半斤" — "half a jin of scattered fat/paste" — a phrase Chinese medical historians have long read as a description of the pancreas, distinguishable in classical anatomical language from the solid, blood-related organ described elsewhere as 脾 proper. Wang Bing's (王冰) Tang-dynasty commentary on the *Su Wen* describes the Spleen's shape as resembling a horse's hoof; other texts describe it as sickle-shaped or tongue-shaped — descriptions that read, to a modern eye, as more consistent with the pancreas than the spleen. The Qing physician Wang Qingren (王清任), working from direct dissection in the 1830 *Yi Lin Gai Cuo* (《醫林改錯》, *Correction of Errors in the Forest of Medicine*), drew and described the pancreas, its duct, and its relations to the bile and hepatic ducts in detail, terming the organ 總提. Modern scholarship on this history concludes that classical 脾 functionally *includes* what biomedicine separates into spleen and pancreas — which is why 脾's textbook function (運化, transformation of food into usable substance) reads, to a biomedically trained eye, far more like the pancreas's exocrine/endocrine digestive role than like the spleen's actual jobs (red-cell filtration, immune surveillance, blood reservoir). **[break]** The classical Chinese medical tradition had genuine anatomical knowledge of the abdominal viscera and used it; the mismatch between TCM's 脾 and biomedicine's spleen is not a case of TCM lacking anatomy, but of TCM's functional system spanning an anatomical territory (spleen *and* pancreas) that biomedicine later split in two along a different seam. The resonance in §2.1 borrows force from the fact that both traditions use the same organ *name*; it borrows almost no force from the two traditions meaning the same *structure* by that name.

### 3.3 Mouse-normal, human-pathological: extramedullary hematopoiesis is not one phenomenon

The McKim, Heidt, and Powell findings are mouse data. In mice, the spleen is a hematopoietic organ across the lifespan at low baseline levels and readily reactivates as a red-pulp hematopoietic site under stress, infection, or anemia — this is normal reserve physiology for the species. In adult humans, the spleen normally does *not* make blood cells; splenic extramedullary hematopoiesis in an adult human is a pathological or reactive finding — seen in myeloproliferative disease, thalassemia, myelofibrosis, or severe chronic hemolysis — not a normal stress response. **[break]** This is the single most consequential disanalogy in the whole comparison, and it must be stated plainly rather than smoothed over: the mouse mechanism is a beautifully characterized instance of the body doing something *routine* under threat; if the same relocation happened in a chronically stressed human, it would be read by any hematologist as disease, not adaptive physiology. Whether the milder human phenomena in this space — the stress-induced classical-monocyte expansion and CTRA signature documented in Cole's human work, which involve population-mixture shifts in circulating leukocytes without any claim of splenic engraftment — are a toned-down analogue of the mouse mechanism, or a mechanistically separate phenomenon that only shares an upstream trigger (chronic β-adrenergic tone), is an open question the current literature does not resolve. Extrapolating the mouse splenic-depot finding onto TCM's "Spleen makes blood" as though it were a demonstrated human mechanism would be an error independent of anything TCM says.

### 3.4 The organ-attribution mismatch: TCM's own causal chain for chronic threat does not run through the Spleen first

This is the break most specific to TCM's internal logic, as opposed to the biomedicine/TCM interface generally. Modern TCM clinical writing about chronic, unresolved, modern-life stress overwhelmingly centers the **Liver**, not the Spleen — see the glossary term "Qi Stagnation": the Liver's function of 疏泄 (ensuring smooth Qi flow) is what fails under sustained frustration, constraint, or an inability to resolve conflict, producing Liver Qi stagnation (肝氣鬱結). The Spleen enters this picture only downstream, via the Five Phase controlling cycle: Wood (Liver) restrains Earth (Spleen) — "肝木乘脾土," Liver overacting on Spleen — so that chronic Liver constraint eventually impairs Spleen digestive function. A socially defeated, repeatedly attacked, unable-to-escape mouse is, if anything, the textbook case for constrained, unexpressed reactive drive — which in TCM's own vocabulary is closer to thwarted anger than to pensiveness. **[break]** Read this way, TCM's own most natural diagnosis of the McKim/Powell/Heidt stress paradigm is Liver Qi stagnation overacting on Spleen — a two-step causal chain — not a direct 思傷脾 (worry directly injures Spleen) reading. The clean three-beat resonance in §2 is compelling partly because it selects the affect (思/pensiveness) that shares a organ target (Spleen) with the biomedical finding's *location*, while passing over the affect (怒/anger, or thwarted fight-or-flight more broadly) that TCM's own etiological literature would nominate first for an inescapable, repeated-defeat paradigm. Neither reading is uniquely "correct" — TCM patterns are not exclusive — but the tidiness of §2 depends on a selection the tradition's own diagnostic literature does not make for you.

There is a further, more structural break underneath this one. TCM's Kidney/marrow and Spleen/blood relationship is a **generative** one along the Five Phase 相生 (mutual-engendering) cycle in only one direction relevant here: postnatal Spleen Qi (from food) continuously replenishes prenatal Kidney Jing over a lifetime, not the reverse, and TCM has no clinical concept of the Kidney's marrow-making function being *taken over* by the Spleen, or of hematopoietic material relocating between the two systems' domains. **[break]** The biomedical finding's central drama — a function moving from one organ's jurisdiction to another's under stress — has no structural echo in Zang-Fu physiology at all. TCM's organs specialize and cooperate; they do not swap jobs. The resonance in §2.3 works only if you supply, from outside TCM, the very idea (functional relocation between organs) that the biomedical finding needs and that TCM's own framework does not offer.

## 4. Substances-in-Transport: The Deeper Rhyme

Set the marrow/spleen case aside for a moment. The estate's owner has observed, across domains, that *which proteins are in which fluids when and where* seems to be a critical variable everywhere it is looked for. That observation deserves its own treatment, because it is where TCM and current biomedicine converge on something closer to a shared **method**, not just a shared vocabulary word.

### 4.1 TCM as a theory of routing

TCM's four fundamental substances — Qi, Blood (Xue), Body Fluids (Jin Ye), and Jing — are defined less by what they *are* than by where they *travel*, through what channel, and what they do on arrival. Pathology in TCM is overwhelmingly a pathology of location and flow rather than of substance identity: Qi Stagnation is Qi that is not where its flow should carry it; Blood Stasis is Blood stuck rather than circulating; Jin Ye that fails to transform and disperse becomes Dampness and then Phlegm — the same water-derived substance, reclassified pathologically purely by virtue of where it has accumulated and how it now behaves there. The meridians are, in this reading, best understood as a *routing map*: the diagnostic question is almost never "is this substance present," it is "is this substance in the right place, moving at the right rate, in the right direction." TCM's clinical vocabulary treats compartment and flow as first-class diagnostic facts in a way that a purely substance-identity nosology (this disease is caused by X, this molecule) does not.

### 4.2 IL-6: the same cytokine, opposite valence, by compartment alone

Modern cytokine biology has, in the last fifteen years, converged on a structurally similar insight for one of its most-studied molecules. IL-6 signals through two distinct mechanisms that use the same ligand and produce opposite clinical valence:

- **Classic signaling** requires the target cell to express membrane-bound IL-6Rα. This expression is restricted to hepatocytes and some leukocyte subsets. Classic signaling drives IL-6's regenerative and acute-phase functions (including hepatic CRP induction).
- **Trans-signaling** occurs when IL-6 binds a *soluble* form of IL-6Rα (sIL-6R), shed proteolytically from cell membranes chiefly by the metalloprotease ADAM17. The IL-6/sIL-6R complex can then activate gp130 — which is expressed almost ubiquitously — on *any* cell, including cells (neurons, astrocytes) that never expressed membrane IL-6Rα themselves and would otherwise be invisible to IL-6 entirely.

Rose-John and colleagues (2014, *J Neuroscience* 34(7):2503–2512) demonstrated that IL-6's pathogenic, pro-inflammatory actions in brain — blood-brain-barrier disruption, neuroinflammatory angiogenesis — are substantially trans-signaling-dependent, not classic-signaling-dependent; sgp130Fc, an engineered soluble gp130 that neutralizes trans-signaling selectively while sparing classic signaling, is the standard pharmacological tool used to demonstrate this dissociation. The same molecule, in the same organism, at the same moment, can be doing regenerative work in the liver and pathogenic work in the brain — and the entirety of the difference is which receptor form it happens to encounter, in which fluid compartment, in what concentration of a shed soluble partner. A standard serum IL-6 ELISA — the workhorse assay of the entire social-genomics/inflammation literature — cannot distinguish these two modes at all; it reports total IL-6 mass with no information about which signaling arm that mass will engage once it reaches a given tissue.

### 4.3 CXCL12: a gradient, not a presence

The Heidt et al. (2014) mechanism described in §1 is, at bottom, a story about the *concentration* of one chemokine in one micro-compartment. HSPCs are not held in the bone marrow because CXCL12 is present in the body; they are held there because a *local concentration gradient*, maintained by niche stromal cells, is high enough at that specific location to retain them via CXCR4 signaling. Lower that local concentration — via β3-adrenergic suppression of the niche cells' CXCL12 output, without changing anything about CXCL12 elsewhere in the body — and the same cells that were anchored begin to circulate. The diagnostic and mechanistic fact is not "CXCL12 exists," it is "CXCL12 is here, at this concentration, right now." Position, and gradient, not presence, is the operative variable — precisely the shape of claim TCM's flow-and-location vocabulary is built to make and that a simple substance-identity biochemistry (does the molecule exist, yes/no) is not.

### 4.4 Extracellular vesicles as addressed transport

A third, more recently characterized example extends the same logic to non-soluble cargo. Extracellular vesicles (exosomes and related microvesicles) package proteins, RNA, and lipids inside a lipid-bilayer envelope, decorate that envelope with surface markers that determine which recipient cells will take the vesicle up, and deliver the cargo intact to a chosen destination rather than releasing it into the general extracellular space for any nearby receptor to sample. This is transport with an address on it — a mechanism whose entire clinical and physiological significance is which cell sent it, which cell is meant to receive it, and what compartment it traveled through, not merely what molecules it contains.

### 4.5 The shared grammar

What unites §4.1–4.4 is a structural claim, not a substantive one: **in both systems, the meaning of a substance is not fixed by its chemical identity but by its compartment, its concentration gradient, and the receptor partners available to it at the place it arrives.** TCM built an entire clinical nosology on this premise centuries before it had any molecular vocabulary to name the mechanism — because at the bedside, "where is this substance, and is it flowing," is often the only observable available. Modern biomedicine reached the same structural insight from the opposite direction: it built a substance-identity chemistry first (isolate the molecule, sequence the receptor), and only in the last two decades has it acquired the tools — compartment-specific proteomics, receptor-selective blocking reagents like sgp130Fc, single-cell spatial transcriptomics, vesicle-tracking methods — to ask "where, and in what form" as a routine experimental question rather than an afterthought to "what." **[rhyme]** Both are, at the structural level, theories of routing and compartment. Neither validates the other's specific claims (TCM's meridian map is not IL-6's receptor distribution, and no biomedical finding makes Qi Stagnation more or less real as a diagnostic category); what they share is a recognition that *asking about location and flow is often more diagnostically productive than asking about presence alone* — and that recognition is worth naming as shared method, independent of whether either system's substance ontology is correct.

## 5. What Each System Sees That the Other Does Not

**What TCM sees that biomedicine's molecular literature does not (yet) systematically offer:** a whole-person functional integration usable at the bedside today, without waiting on a mechanism. A practitioner observing fatigue, poor appetite, loose stools, and a pale tongue diagnoses Spleen Qi deficiency and treats it — coherently, reproducibly across practitioners trained in the same tradition — without needing to know whether a monocyte subset shift is occurring. TCM also carries, in its Seven Affects framework, a claim biomedicine has only recently begun to test directly: that the *specific quality* of chronic affect (worry versus anger versus fear), not merely its presence or its generic "stress" magnitude, might sort patients into physiologically distinguishable subgroups (see Hypothesis 3 below) — a hypothesis biomedicine's own literature (§1 here; the companion cytokine note's Hypothesis 2 on controllability-dependent priming) is independently approaching from the *controllability* angle rather than the *affect-quality* angle. TCM got there first, for reasons of clinical utility rather than experimental design, and its organ-affect specificity remains an underexploited hypothesis generator even where its own mechanism story (Qi, Five Phases) is not the biomedical mechanism.

**What biomedicine sees that TCM's framework has no native way to represent:** a cell of origin. The entire McKim/Heidt/Powell mechanism is legible only because biomedicine can identify a specific clonal population (the HSPC), track it with lineage markers, knock out a specific receptor (β3-adrenergic) in a specific cell type (marrow niche stromal cells) and watch a specific, quantifiable consequence (CXCL12 concentration, cells-per-microliter of circulating monocytes) fail to occur. TCM's Zang-Fu system, being defined by function rather than structure (see the glossary term "Zang-Fu"), has no mechanism for representing "a specific cell type, defined by lineage, relocates between two organs' anatomical territory" — because TCM's organs are not, and were never meant to be, exhaustive maps of cellular anatomy. This is not a defect relative to TCM's own purposes (it does not need cellular resolution to guide herbal or acupuncture treatment); it is simply outside the resolution the framework operates at.

**What neither system saw until recently:** a unified theory of substance-mislocation as a general diagnostic principle applied at the molecular/cellular scale with quantitative precision. Biomedicine had scattered, ad hoc versions — metastasis, ectopic pregnancy, heterotopic ossification, extramedullary hematopoiesis itself — each treated as a special case within its own subspecialty, without a general nosological category comparable to TCM's stagnation/deficiency/wrong-place framework that spans the entire body. TCM had the general category (substance in the wrong place, or not flowing, as a first-class diagnostic fact) for millennia but never the resolution to specify *which* substance, *which* cell, *which* receptor. The genuinely novel thing happening in the current biomedical moment — compartment-specific proteomics, receptor-selective pharmacology, single-cell spatial methods — is the acquisition of tools that let a substance-identity science finally ask location-and-flow questions at the resolution TCM's clinical vocabulary has always assumed mattered, without either science needing to borrow the other's ontology to do it.

## Hypotheses

**Hypothesis 1 — Spleen-Qi-deficiency (脾虛) pattern diagnosis correlates with a peripheral myeloid/monocyte skew.**
*(i) Prediction:* Patients pattern-diagnosed with Spleen Qi deficiency (fatigue, poor appetite, loose stools, pale tongue, weak pulse) by standardized TCM instruments will show, on blinded CBC differential and flow-cytometric monocyte subsetting, a shift toward classical (CD14++/CD16−) monocytes relative to matched controls without the pattern diagnosis, in a manner resembling the CTRA-associated monocyte skew documented in Cole's human isolation/loneliness work. *(ii) Test:* Recruit TCM-pattern-diagnosed Spleen-Qi-deficiency patients and matched controls (age, sex, BMI, smoking status); collect standardized pattern-severity scores and blood for CBC differential plus flow cytometry, with laboratory personnel blinded to pattern diagnosis. *(iii) Kill condition:* No significant monocyte-subset difference between groups, or a difference that is fully explained by confounders (age, chronic illness burden, corticosteroid use) once controlled for, rather than tracking pattern-severity score — either result means the pattern-diagnosis category is not capturing a myeloid-bias phenotype and the resonance in §2.1 has no measurable biomedical correlate in humans.

**Hypothesis 2 — TCM's own causal chain (Liver overacting on Spleen) predicts the biomedical signature better than a direct Spleen-only reading.**
*(i) Prediction:* Given §3.4's observation that TCM's own literature would nominate Liver Qi stagnation overacting on Spleen (肝木乘脾土), not isolated Spleen Qi deficiency, as the natural diagnosis for chronic, inescapable social threat, patients pattern-diagnosed with the combined Liver-overacting-on-Spleen pattern will show a larger CTRA/monocyte-skew signature than patients diagnosed with pure Spleen Qi deficiency (no Liver component) or pure Liver Qi stagnation (no Spleen component). *(ii) Test:* Recruit three pattern-diagnosed groups (pure Liver Qi stagnation, combined Liver-overacting-on-Spleen, pure Spleen Qi deficiency) using standardized differentiation criteria; compare CTRA transcriptomic score and monocyte-subset flow cytometry across groups. *(iii) Kill condition:* If pure Spleen Qi deficiency shows an equal or larger myeloid signature than the combined Liver-Spleen pattern, TCM's own more elaborate causal ordering does no better than the naive direct reading this note initially proposed in §2, and the refinement in §3.4 should be dropped as descriptively interesting but not predictively useful.

**Hypothesis 3 — Affect-specific (七情) organ targeting produces distinguishable multi-system biomarker profiles.**
*(i) Prediction:* If TCM's assignment of specific affects to specific organs is doing real physiological work rather than serving only as a mnemonic classification, chronic-stress patients whose dominant self-reported affect quality is worry/rumination (思, Spleen-associated) will cluster, on a multi-system biomarker panel (inflammatory markers, autonomic tone, HPA-axis output, and gut-permeability markers reflecting Spleen's digestive domain), separately from patients whose dominant affect is anger/frustration (怒, Liver-associated, expected to track more with sympathetic/cardiovascular markers) or fear (恐, Kidney-associated, expected to track more with HPA-axis/cortisol markers). *(ii) Test:* In a chronic-stress cohort, collect validated self-report affect-quality measures alongside a multi-system biomarker panel; run unsupervised clustering on the biomarker panel and test whether cluster membership predicts self-reported dominant affect quality above chance. *(iii) Kill condition:* If biomarker clustering does not segregate by self-reported affect quality at all — i.e., all chronic-stress phenotypes converge on one generic inflammatory profile regardless of whether the dominant affect is worry, anger, or fear — the Seven Affects framework's organ-specificity claim receives no support from this design (though a null result here could also simply indicate that self-report is the wrong instrument for the distinction TCM's pulse-and-tongue diagnostics are built to make, which the test cannot on its own distinguish).

**Hypothesis 4 — Jin Ye fluid-pathology subcategories predict compartment-specific proteomic signatures.**
*(i) Prediction:* TCM's fluid-pathology categories — Dampness (濕), Phlegm (痰), Edema (水腫), and Fluid Retention (飲, fluid accumulated in a body cavity) — if they are tracking real, distinguishable pathophysiological processes rather than a single generic "excess fluid" grouping, will correspond to statistically distinguishable proteomic signatures in compartment-matched samples (synovial fluid, pleural effusion, peritoneal ascites), beyond what is explained by the underlying biomedical diagnosis and compartment alone. *(ii) Test:* In patients with effusions/ascites pattern-diagnosed as Damp-Heat, Phlegm-Damp, or simple fluid retention, collect compartment-matched fluid for proteomic profiling; compare profiles across TCM pattern categories with biomedical diagnosis and anatomical compartment held constant or statistically controlled. *(iii) Kill condition:* If proteomic profiles are statistically indistinguishable across TCM fluid-pathology categories once compartment and biomedical diagnosis are controlled for, the Jin Ye subcategories add no discriminative information beyond what compartment and Western diagnosis already provide, and §4.1's claim that TCM's fluid taxonomy tracks something biomedically real at the compartment level is not supported.

**Hypothesis 5 — Kidney-Jing-deficiency severity, not Spleen-Qi-deficiency severity, tracks the slow decay of stress-induced myeloid bias.**
*(i) Prediction:* Given the fast-installation/slow-decay asymmetry documented in the companion biochemistry note (§2.4 there) — where the McKim splenic hematopoietic depot persists for weeks after stressor cessation while circulating hormonal signaling normalizes fast — TCM's Kidney Jing deficiency pattern (classically slow to develop and slow to recover, constitutional in character) will track the multi-week decay timescale of the biomedical myeloid bias more closely than TCM's Spleen Qi deficiency pattern (classically more rapidly reversible with rest and diet). *(ii) Test:* In a longitudinal cohort with a clearly defined stressor offset (job loss resolution, end of a caregiving period), collect serial TCM pattern-severity scores for both Spleen Qi deficiency and Kidney Jing deficiency alongside serial monocyte-subset flow cytometry; fit decay time constants for each pattern score and compare against the myeloid-bias decay curve. *(iii) Kill condition:* If Spleen-pattern and Kidney-pattern severity scores decay on statistically indistinguishable timescales, or if neither pattern's decay timescale resembles the myeloid-bias decay curve, TCM's own fast/slow organ distinction does not map onto the biomedical fast-install/slow-decay asymmetry, and the specific organ assignment (Kidney over Spleen) proposed here should be abandoned even if the general framework in §4 is retained.
