The Spleen (脾, pí) is one of the five yin organ-function systems of [zang-fu](./zang-fu.md) theory. It names a set of functions, not the anatomical spleen. In biomedical terms those functions sit closer to digestion and absorption as a whole than to any single organ.

Its central function is transformation and transportation (yùn huà). The Spleen extracts the refined essence of food and drink, sends it upward to the Lung and Heart, and so provides the material from which [qi](./qi.md) and [blood](./xue.md) are made. It is called the root of postnatal life, the source that replenishes what is spent after birth, as [jing](./jing.md) is the root of what was received before it [@maciocia2015].

The tradition assigns it further functions:
- it governs the blood, keeping it within the vessels (bleeding and bruising easily are read as Spleen qi failing to hold);
- it raises the clear qi, which holds the organs in place (prolapse is read as its failure);
- it controls the muscles and the four limbs;
- it opens into the mouth and shows in the lips.

It belongs to the earth phase of [wu xing](./wu-xing.md), is paired with the Stomach, and is said to dislike dampness: weak Spleen function lets fluids stagnate into damp and [phlegm](./phlegm.md). Tiredness after eating, loose stools, a poor appetite and heavy limbs are the classic signs of Spleen qi deficiency, one of the commonest patterns in [pattern diagnosis](./pattern-diagnosis.md).
