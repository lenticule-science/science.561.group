This is a quick-reference guide to the most commonly encountered patterns in TCM clinical practice. Each entry gives the pattern name, its key signs, the diagnostic tongue and pulse findings, and the treatment principle. For full understanding of how patterns work, see [Understanding TCM Patterns](understanding-tcm-patterns.md).

## Qi patterns

### Qi deficiency (qi xu 气虚)

**Key signs**: Fatigue, shortness of breath on exertion, weak voice, spontaneous sweating, poor appetite
**Tongue**: Pale, possibly with tooth marks
**Pulse**: Weak (xu) or empty (kong)
**Treatment**: Tonify Qi. Primary herb: Huang Qi (Astragalus)

### [Qi stagnation](../terms/qi-stagnation.md) (qi zhi 气滞)

**Key signs**: Distension and fullness (chest, ribs, abdomen), mood swings, irritability, sighing, symptoms worse with stress
**Tongue**: May be normal or slightly purple at sides
**Pulse**: Wiry (xian)
**Treatment**: Regulate Qi, soothe the Liver. Primary herb: Chai Hu (Bupleurum). Formula: Xiao Yao San

### Qi sinking (qi xian 气陷)

**Key signs**: Chronic diarrhea, organ prolapse (uterine, rectal, gastric), bearing-down sensation, extreme fatigue
**Tongue**: Pale
**Pulse**: Weak, especially at the right Guan position
**Treatment**: Tonify and raise Qi. Formula: Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang

## Blood patterns

### Blood deficiency (xue xu 血虚)

**Key signs**: Pale face, dizziness, blurred vision, insomnia, scanty menstruation, dry skin, brittle nails, numbness
**Tongue**: Pale, thin
**Pulse**: Thin (xi), choppy (se)
**Treatment**: Nourish Blood. Primary herbs: Dang Gui (Angelica), Shu Di Huang (Rehmannia). Formula: Si Wu Tang

### [Blood stasis](../terms/blood-stasis.md) (xue yu 血瘀)

**Key signs**: Fixed stabbing pain, dark complexion, masses/lumps, dark menstrual blood with clots
**Tongue**: Purple or dark with petechiae
**Pulse**: Choppy (se) or wiry
**Treatment**: Invigorate Blood, dispel stasis. Herbs include [Hu Zhang](../terms/hu-zhang.md), Dan Shen, Chuan Xiong. Formula: Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang

### Blood heat (xue re 血热)

**Key signs**: Reckless bleeding (nosebleeds, heavy periods, blood in stool), skin eruptions, agitation, feeling of heat
**Tongue**: Deep red
**Pulse**: Rapid, possibly overflowing
**Treatment**: Cool Blood, stop bleeding. Primary herbs: Sheng Di (raw Rehmannia), Mu Dan Pi (Moutan bark)

## Yin and Yang patterns

### [Yin deficiency](../terms/yin-deficiency.md) (yin xu 阴虚)

**Key signs**: Night sweats, five-palm heat, afternoon fever, dry mouth/throat, malar flush
**Tongue**: Red, little or no coating
**Pulse**: Thin and rapid
**Treatment**: Nourish Yin. Formula: Liu Wei Di Huang Wan (Kidney Yin); Mai Men Dong Tang (Lung Yin)

### [Yang deficiency](../terms/yang-deficiency.md) (yang xu 阳虚)

**Key signs**: Cold limbs, pale face, fatigue (worse mornings), edema, copious clear urine, loose stools, low back pain
**Tongue**: Pale, swollen, tooth-marked, white moist coating
**Pulse**: Deep and slow
**Treatment**: Warm Yang. Formula: Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan (Kidney Yang); Li Zhong Wan (Spleen Yang)

## Combined and compound patterns

### Liver Qi stagnation with Spleen deficiency

**Key signs**: Rib-side distension + abdominal bloating, irritability + fatigue, alternating constipation and loose stools, symptoms worse with stress and improved with rest
**Tongue**: Pale body with slightly red sides
**Pulse**: Wiry at left Guan, weak at right Guan
**Treatment**: Soothe Liver, tonify Spleen. Formula: Xiao Yao San

### [Damp-heat](../terms/damp-heat.md) in the Liver/Gallbladder

**Key signs**: Jaundice, bitter taste, nausea, rib-side fullness, dark urine, irritability
**Tongue**: Red with thick yellow greasy coating
**Pulse**: Wiry, slippery, rapid
**Treatment**: Clear heat, resolve dampness. Herbs include [Hu Zhang](../terms/hu-zhang.md), Yin Chen Hao, Zhi Zi. Formula: Yin Chen Hao Tang

### [Phlegm](../terms/phlegm.md) misting the Heart

**Key signs**: Mental confusion, muddled thinking, incoherent speech, possible loss of consciousness, rattling sound in throat
**Tongue**: Swollen with thick greasy coating
**Pulse**: Slippery
**Treatment**: Resolve Phlegm, open the orifices. Emergency herbs: Shi Chang Pu (Acorus), Yuan Zhi (Polygala)

### Kidney Yin and Yang both deficient

**Key signs**: Low back pain, weak knees, tinnitus, PLUS cold limbs and nocturia — signs of both Yin AND Yang depletion. Common in advanced aging and severe chronic illness
**Tongue**: Pale-red, thin, with partial coating
**Pulse**: Deep, thin, may be slow or rapid depending on which deficiency predominates
**Treatment**: Nourish Yin and warm Yang simultaneously. Formula: modified Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan

## How to use this reference

1. Identify the patient's dominant symptoms
2. Match to the pattern whose key signs best fit
3. Check tongue and pulse for confirmation
4. Apply the treatment principle

In practice, patients often present with compound patterns (two or more simultaneous patterns). The skilled practitioner identifies the primary pattern and any secondary patterns, then designs treatment that addresses both — treating the root while managing the branch.
