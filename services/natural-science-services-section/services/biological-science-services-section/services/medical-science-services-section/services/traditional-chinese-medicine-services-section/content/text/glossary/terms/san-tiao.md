San tiao (三調), the three regulations, is how modern teaching of [qigong](./qigong.md) states what every form has in common:

- **Regulating the body** (tiáo shēn, 調身): posture, alignment, and relaxation in stillness, and the path and rhythm of each movement in motion.
- **Regulating the breath** (tiáo xī, 調息): the breath is first allowed to be natural, then made slow, even, fine, and long, usually abdominal. Some methods coordinate it with movement or add sounds; Liuzijue, for example, pairs six exhaled sounds with the organ-function systems.
- **Regulating the mind** (tiáo xīn, 調心): attention is gathered, often in the lower [dantian](./dantian.md), and intent (yì) accompanies the movement instead of wandering.

The three are not steps done in order. A practice counts as qigong, not as calisthenics, breathing drill, or meditation, when the three are regulated together. Chinese texts describe the goal as the three merging into one (sān tiáo hé yī).

The formulation is a twentieth-century systematization from the clinics that built modern qigong. Older daoyin and neidan texts speak of the same elements in their own vocabularies. Because it names the components an intervention combines, it is also a useful frame for research. A trial that compares a form with ordinary exercise or with breathing alone is asking which of the three regulations carries the effect.
