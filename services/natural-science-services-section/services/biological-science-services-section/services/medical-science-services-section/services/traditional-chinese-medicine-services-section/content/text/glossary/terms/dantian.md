Dantian (丹田) means "elixir field" or "cinnabar field". The name is taken from alchemy: it is the field in which the elixir is cultivated. It names a region of the body, not an organ, where [qigong](./qigong.md) and [inner alchemy](./neidan.md) gather attention and, in their own account, qi.

Most systems name three:

- **The lower dantian**, in the lower abdomen a few finger-widths below the navel and inward toward the spine. It is associated with [jing](./jing.md). When a teacher says "the dantian" without qualification, this is the one meant, and it is the usual place to rest attention in standing and breathing practice.
- **The middle dantian**, in the chest at the level of the heart. It is associated with [qi](./qi.md).
- **The upper dantian**, between the eyebrows. It is associated with [shen](./shen.md).

Inner alchemy's refinement of jing into qi and qi into shen is often told as a movement through these three fields [@pregadio2008]. The number, placement, and correspondences vary by lineage.

The dantian are fields where practice is placed. The [meridians](./meridians.md) are the channels along which qi is said to circulate. Acupuncture works on the channels and their points. Qigong more often works on the fields.

The lower dantian sits over the diaphragm's excursion and the abdominal wall, so resting attention there tends to produce slower, deeper abdominal breathing, the physiological process described in [slow-paced breathing](../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/slow-paced-breathing.md). That coincidence is recorded as a coincidence. It does not reduce the tradition's account to it.
