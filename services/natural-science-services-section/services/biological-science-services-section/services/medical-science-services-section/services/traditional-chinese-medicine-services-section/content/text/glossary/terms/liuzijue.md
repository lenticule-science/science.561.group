Liuzijue (六字訣), the "six-character formula" or "Six Healing Sounds", is [qigong](./qigong.md) built on the exhalation. Six sounds are breathed out slowly, sometimes silently, and each is paired with an organ-function system of [zang-fu](./zang-fu.md) theory:
- **xū (噓):** the liver;
- **hē (呵):** the heart;
- **hū (呼):** the spleen;
- **sī (呬):** the lung;
- **chuī (吹):** the kidney;
- **xī (嘻):** the [san jiao](./san-jiao.md).

Its earliest known description is in the *Yangxing yanming lu* ("Record of Nourishing Nature and Extending Life"), associated with the fifth-to-sixth-century Daoist master Tao Hongjing. There, the six exhalations expel illness and are drawn in against one intake breath [@kohn2008].

Of the four standardized forms of 2003, Liuzijue is the one where regulating the breath, the second of the [three regulations](./san-tiao.md), carries most of the practice. Each sound shapes a long, controlled exhalation, and gentle movements accompany it. In physiological terms it is a structured form of [slow-paced breathing](../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/slow-paced-breathing.md) with prolonged exhalation. That is one reason it has been trialled in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
