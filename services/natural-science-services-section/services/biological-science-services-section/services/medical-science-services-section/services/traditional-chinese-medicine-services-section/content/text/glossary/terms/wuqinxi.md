Wuqinxi (五禽戲), the "Five Animal Frolics", is a moving [qigong](./qigong.md) sequence that imitates five animals: the tiger, deer, bear, monkey, and bird (usually the crane).

The Han-dynasty histories attribute it to Hua Tuo, the second-century physician. They quote him saying that the body needs movement as a door-hinge does, which does not rot because it turns, and that the frolics were devised to move it. It belongs to the older tradition of animal postures in [daoyin](./daoyin.md): the "bear-hangings and bird-stretches" the *Zhuangzi* already names [@kohn2008].

Each animal pairs a manner of movement with an intended effect in the practice's own account:
- **the tiger:** power, with clawing and pouncing;
- **the deer:** stretching and turning the spine;
- **the bear:** heavy, rooted swaying that massages the abdomen;
- **the monkey:** light, quick agility;
- **the bird:** balance on one leg with outspread arms.

The Chinese Health Qigong Association standardized a ten-movement version, two per animal, in 2003. Its playfulness and variety make it the most expressive of the four standard forms. As with all of them, the imitation is carried by the [three regulations](./san-tiao.md) of body, breath, and mind.
