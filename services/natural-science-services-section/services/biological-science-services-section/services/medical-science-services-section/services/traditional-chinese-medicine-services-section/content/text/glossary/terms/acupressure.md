Acupressure (zhi ya, literally 'finger pressure') is manual stimulation of acupuncture points without needles. Within TCM, acupressure operates through the same meridian system and point functions as needle acupuncture. It is distinct from the broader therapeutic massage of tui na, though both are manual methods within TCM. Acupressure serves as an accessible tool for self-treatment and for patients unable or unwilling to receive needling. In traditional practice, acupressure techniques form part of the broader tui na repertoire, using finger pressure (an rou: pressing and rolling), pinching (nie), and percussion (pai) at specific points and along meridian pathways. The sustained pressure technique at acupoints is zhi ya—maintained pressure, usually with the thumb or index finger, held at one point for a specified duration. The key distinction is that acupressure targets specific points for their regulated functions, while tui na's broader massage techniques work along fascial lines and muscle groups. A tui na practitioner applying finger pressure to P6 (Neiguan) with the intention of treating nausea is performing acupressure; the same practitioner working along the forearm's flexor surface to release tension is performing broader tui na. P6 (Pericardium 6, Neiguan, literally 'inner gate') lies on the ventral forearm between palmaris longus and flexor carpi radialis tendons, approximately two cun above the wrist crease. In TCM terms, P6 governs the Pericardium's protective function and regulates the Heart's Shen (spirit/consciousness). Clinically, P6 is one of the most widely used points for nausea from all causes: motion sickness, medication-induced nausea, nausea from cancer treatment, post-operative nausea, pregnancy-related nausea, and functional nausea. Biomedical research on P6 acupressure and acupuncture for nausea found consistent effects through the Cochrane systematic review, showing reduced nausea incidence and severity. The mechanism involves vagal afferent stimulation altering brainstem centers regulating nausea and vomiting. From the TCM perspective, the point's traditional function—calming the Heart-Pericardium and restoring harmony—produces its clinical effect. Acupressure at P6 is readily self-administered for patients with chronic or recurrent nausea. The Kuznetsov applicator (a Soviet-era spiked mat) is sometimes marketed as an acupressure mat but is not part of TCM. It applies undifferentiated pressure to large skin areas without point-specific targeting, functioning as a reflex stimulus tool rather than TCM acupressure.
