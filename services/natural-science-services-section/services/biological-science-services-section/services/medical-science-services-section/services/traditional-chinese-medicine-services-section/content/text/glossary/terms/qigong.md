Qigong (氣功, "qi work" or "the skill of qi") names a family of practices that cultivate the body's functional activity, [qi](./qi.md), by coordinating three things at once: the body's posture or movement, the breath, and the mind's attention. Chinese teaching calls this coordination the [three regulations](./san-tiao.md): regulating the body, the breath, and the mind.

The word is modern. Its practices are old. Therapeutic stretching and breathing, *daoyin* (導引, "guiding and pulling"), is depicted in a silk chart buried at Mawangdui in 168 BCE, and the *Zhuangzi* already mocks practitioners of "bear hangings and bird stretches" [@kohn2008]. "Qigong" became the umbrella term only in the 1950s, when state clinics gathered [daoyin](./daoyin.md), breath cultivation, and simplified [inner alchemy](./neidan.md) under one secular, medical name. The mass "qigong fever" of the 1980s and 1990s followed [@palmer2007]. The history is traced in the [2026-09-24 note on qigong as a practice system](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/24/2026-09-24-note-on-qigong-as-a-practice-system.md).

Practices divide along three lines, which cross-cut:

- **Moving and still.** Moving forms (dong gong) such as [Baduanjin](./baduanjin.md) sequence postures with breath. Still forms (jing gong) such as [standing post](./zhan-zhuang.md) and seated breath-and-attention practice hold one posture.
- **Medical, martial, and contemplative.** Medical qigong treats or maintains health within [traditional Chinese medicine](./traditional-chinese-medicine.md). Martial qigong conditions the body for combat arts; its movement overlaps with [taijiquan](../../../../../medical-services-section/services/health-services-section/services/fitness-services-section/services/martial-arts-services-section/services/tai-chi-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/taijiquan.md). Contemplative and Daoist qigong aims at transformation of the person, not treatment.
- **Self-practice and emitted qi.** Most qigong is done by the practitioner for themself. "External qi" healing, where a master directs qi into a patient, is a distinct and far more contested claim.

Within Chinese medicine's own terms, qigong works on the [Three Treasures](./san-bao.md). It conserves [jing](./jing.md), keeps qi circulating instead of stagnating, and settles [shen](./shen.md). Attention is often gathered in the lower [dantian](./dantian.md). This section records that account as the tradition's own theory of the practice.

Biomedical research treats qigong as low-to-moderate intensity mind-body exercise with slowed breathing. Across dozens of randomized trials it reports benefits for balance, physical function, blood pressure, mood, and quality of life [@jahnke2010]. The trials' strength and limits are weighed in the [2026-09-24 note on the clinical evidence for qigong and taiji](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/24/2026-09-24-note-on-the-clinical-evidence-for-qigong-and-taiji.md). The breathing component has its own physiology in [slow-paced breathing](../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/slow-paced-breathing.md).
