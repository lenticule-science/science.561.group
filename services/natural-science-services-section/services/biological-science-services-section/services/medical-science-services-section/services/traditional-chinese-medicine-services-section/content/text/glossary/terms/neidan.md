Neidan (內丹), inner alchemy, is the Daoist discipline that recasts the language of laboratory alchemy as a practice within the body. Laboratory alchemy is *waidan*, "outer elixir": its practitioners compounded mineral elixirs of immortality in furnaces. In neidan, the practitioner's own body is the furnace and cauldron. The ingredients are the [Three Treasures](./san-bao.md), and the "elixir" is formed through breath, attention, and stillness [@pregadio2008].

It took mature form in the Tang and Song dynasties. Its canonical stages are usually told as a sequence of refinements:

1. Refining [jing](./jing.md) into [qi](./qi.md) (liàn jīng huà qì).
2. Refining qi into [shen](./shen.md) (liàn qì huà shén).
3. Refining shen and returning to emptiness (liàn shén huán xū).

Practices along the way include circulating attention along the governing and conception vessels (the "microcosmic orbit") and gathering in the three [dantian](./dantian.md).

Neidan is a path of transformation, not a therapy. It supplied the modern secular [qigong](./qigong.md) of the 1950s with its still, seated, attention-and-breath methods, stripped of their soteriology. The structural reading of neidan as a reversal, returning along the path of generation, is taken up in the philosophy section's [note on Daoism as a second grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/23/2026-08-23-note-on-daoism-as-a-second-grammar.md).
