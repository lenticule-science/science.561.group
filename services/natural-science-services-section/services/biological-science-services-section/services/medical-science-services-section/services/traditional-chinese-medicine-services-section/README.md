# Traditional Chinese Medicine Services Section

The Traditional Chinese Medicine Services Section maintains knowledge and
practices for Chinese medicine as a coherent system of diagnosis and
treatment: the substance theory of Qi, Blood (Xue), Body Fluids (Jin Ye),
and Essence (Jing); the Zang-Fu organ-function systems and the channels
(meridians) that connect them; the Yin-Yang and Wu Xing (Five Phase)
frameworks that organize clinical reasoning; pattern diagnosis and its
supporting arts (pulse, tongue); and the therapeutic modalities —
acupuncture, moxibustion, herbal medicine — through which the system
acts.

Its material is organized by artifact role under `content/`: glossary
terms for individual concepts (`content/text/glossary/terms/`), topic
overviews (`content/text/glossary/topics/`), teaching and reference notes
(`content/text/documents/articles/notes/`), and annotated bibliographies
(`content/text/documents/articles/references/`). Terms cite their
classical provenance where it can be verified — chapter and section of
the *Huang Di Nei Jing* (*Su Wen* and *Ling Shu*), the *Nan Jing*, and
the named physicians of later schools — rather than treating "TCM says"
as a citation in itself.

This section studies Chinese medicine as a knowledge system in its own
terms: a functional, pattern-based physiology with its own internal
logic, its own diagnostic categories, and its own criteria of clinical
success, not as a folk-anticipation of biomedicine awaiting confirmation.
Where its concepts and biomedical findings coincide, the coincidence is
recorded as a coincidence — a convergence between two independent
carvings of the body, interesting and worth stating precisely — and not
as proof that either system derives from or reduces to the other.
Authority over the tradition's own claims remains with its practitioners
and textual lineage; this section's biomedical comparisons are read as
external commentary, not as adjudication.
