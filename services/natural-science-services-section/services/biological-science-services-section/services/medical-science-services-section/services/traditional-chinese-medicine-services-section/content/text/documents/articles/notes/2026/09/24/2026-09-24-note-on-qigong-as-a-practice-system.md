[Qigong](../../../../../../glossary/terms/qigong.md) is an old practice under a young name. Studying it well means keeping the two apart. The practices are ancient: stretching and breathing to guide qi, and stillness and attention to refine it. The system is modern: one secular, medical category with standard forms, a theory of its common components, and institutions that certify it. This note traces how the one became the other.

## Three ancestors

**[Daoyin](../../../../../../glossary/terms/daoyin.md), therapeutic movement.** Three sources show that exercise-as-medicine was established by the early Han:
- the Mawangdui silk chart (168 BCE), with its labelled exercise figures;
- the *Yinshu* from Zhangjiashan [@harper1998];
- the *Zhuangzi*'s passing contempt for those who "blow and breathe, spit out the old and draw in the new, do the bear-hang and the bird-stretch" to lengthen life.

Medieval medicine kept the practice. The *Zhubing yuanhou lun* (610 CE) attaches daoyin prescriptions to its disease entries [@kohn2008].

**Breath cultivation**, xíngqì ("moving the qi") and tǔnà ("expelling and taking in"). Controlled, slowed, sometimes retained breathing ran through both medical and religious texts. It was aimed at nourishing life (yǎngshēng), the Chinese tradition of longevity practice.

**[Neidan](../../../../../../glossary/terms/neidan.md), inner alchemy.** Laboratory alchemy, [waidan](../../../../../../glossary/terms/waidan.md), sought elixirs in the furnace. From the Tang and Song dynasties, Daoist practitioners recast elixir-making as refinement within the body. Jing is refined into qi, and qi into shen. Attention is gathered in the [dantian](../../../../../../glossary/terms/dantian.md) and circulated along the governing and conception vessels [@pregadio2008].

Beside these ran the martial lineages. Standing practice ([zhan zhuang](../../../../../../glossary/terms/zhan-zhuang.md)) and the slow forms of taijiquan conditioned the body for combat and, increasingly, for health.

## The naming

The word *qigong* occurs sporadically in earlier texts. It became the name of a field in the 1950s, when Liu Guizhen and colleagues in state-run clinics taught breath-and-attention methods to patients under that label [@palmer2007]. The new category did three things:

1. **It secularized.** It dropped neidan's religious aim, transcendence, and kept its methods as therapy.
2. **It medicalized.** It placed the practice inside the new institutional [traditional Chinese medicine](../../../../../../glossary/terms/traditional-chinese-medicine.md), as a modality beside acupuncture and herbs.
3. **It unified.** It gathered daoyin, breath cultivation, and meditative stillness under one account of what they share. That account was later stated as the [three regulations](../../../../../../glossary/terms/san-tiao.md) of body, breath, and mind.

Suppressed during the Cultural Revolution, qigong returned in the late 1970s and 1980s as a mass movement, the "qigong fever". It brought millions of practitioners, charismatic masters, claims of emitted qi and extraordinary powers, and state-backed research into them. It ended in 1999, when the state turned against the largest qigong-derived movement, Falun Gong, and brought the field under regulation [@palmer2007]. The Chinese Health Qigong Association (founded 2001) then issued standardized forms in 2003: [Baduanjin](../../../../../../glossary/terms/baduanjin.md), [Yijinjing](../../../../../../glossary/terms/yijinjing.md), [Wuqinxi](../../../../../../glossary/terms/wuqinxi.md), and [Liuzijue](../../../../../../glossary/terms/liuzijue.md). These were fixed, short, and teachable. They are now the forms most practised and most studied.

## What the history means for study

**Name the practice, not "qigong".** "Qigong" covers still meditation and brisk calisthenics, and self-practice and emitted-qi healing. A claim about "qigong" that does not name the form, the dose, and which of the three regulations it emphasizes cannot be checked. Resources in this section should link the specific term (Baduanjin, zhan zhuang, slow-paced breathing) and not only the umbrella.

**Keep the traditions' own accounts and the biomedical account side by side, as two readings.** In the tradition's own terms, Baduanjin's third movement "regulates the spleen and stomach". In biomedical terms, it is a bilateral overhead reach with trunk extension, done while breathing slowly. Both statements are true in their own frames. This section's charter keeps them separate, and the [evidence note](./2026-09-24-note-on-the-clinical-evidence-for-qigong-and-taiji.md) reads trials as commentary on the second, not as verdicts on the first.

**Treat the modern standard forms as artifacts.** The 2003 forms are good instruments for research precisely because they are fixed. They are also institutional products of the post-1999 settlement. They represent the older, plural lineages only partly.
