# Traditional Chinese Medicine

Traditional Chinese Medicine is represented here as a coherent historical and contemporary knowledge system rather than as a collection of isolated techniques. Its categories describe patterns of bodily function, relation, transformation, and illness through concepts including Qi, Blood, Body Fluids, Essence, Zang-Fu organ-functions, channels, Yin-Yang, Five Phases, and diagnostic patterns.

Therapeutic modalities include acupuncture, moxibustion, herbal medicine, tui na and acupressure, cupping, gua sha, and movement or cultivation systems such as qigong.

[Gua sha](../terms/gua-sha.md) is especially useful for the section's comparative method because one treatment event can simultaneously have a traditional interpretation, a visible surface sign ([sha](../terms/sha.md)), measurable vascular and tissue effects, a clinical-outcomes literature, household and cultural meanings, and modern commercial claims. Those kinds of statement should remain distinguishable rather than being treated as translations of one another.
