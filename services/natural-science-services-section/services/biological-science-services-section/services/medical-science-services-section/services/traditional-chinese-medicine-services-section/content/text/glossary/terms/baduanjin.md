Baduanjin (八段錦), the "Eight Pieces of Brocade", is a moving [qigong](./qigong.md) sequence of eight exercises. Brocade is a fine, many-coloured silk, and the name praises the set's value. The set appears in Song-dynasty sources, and a seated version and a standing version have both been transmitted since [@kohn2008].

The eight movements of the standing set, in the common modern order and naming:

1. Holding up the heavens with both hands, to regulate the [san jiao](./san-jiao.md).
2. Drawing the bow, left and right, as if shooting a vulture.
3. Separating heaven and earth, raising one hand and pressing down the other, to regulate the spleen and stomach.
4. The wise owl gazes backward, turning the head, to relieve the five strains and seven injuries.
5. Swaying the head and swinging the tail, to calm heart-fire.
6. Two hands reach down to the feet, to strengthen the kidneys and waist.
7. Clenching the fists with angry eyes, to increase strength.
8. Rising and dropping onto the heels, seven times, to dispel the hundred illnesses.

Each movement's traditional name states its intended action on the organ-function systems of [zang-fu](./zang-fu.md) theory. The set is a daoyin sequence in the strict sense: posture, breath, and attention are coordinated in every movement, as the [three regulations](./san-tiao.md) require.

In 2003 the Chinese Health Qigong Association issued a standardized Baduanjin together with three other forms: Yijinjing, Wuqinxi, and Liuzijue. Because it is short, low in intensity, and fixed in form, it is the qigong most often used as the intervention in clinical trials.
