Auricular acupuncture treats points on the outer ear, the auricle, which it reads as a map of the whole body. In the French physician Paul Nogier's 1950s account, the ear resembles an inverted fetus, with the head at the lobe and the feet toward the top. Chinese practitioners adopted and extended the map, and today both a Chinese and a Western system of ear points are in use [@oleson2014]. Points are needled, pressed with seeds or pellets, or stimulated electrically, as in [electroacupuncture](electroacupuncture.md).

Its best-known standardized use is the five-point NADA protocol (sympathetic, Shen Men, kidney, liver and lung points), widely used alongside addiction and trauma care.

The ear is also where other methods converge. [taVNS](../../../../../medical-technology-services-section/services/electrotherapy-devices-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transcutaneous-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation.md) targets the cymba conchae and the tragus because the [vagus nerve](../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/vagus-nerve.md)'s auricular branch supplies them. [Cranial electrotherapy](../../../../../medical-technology-services-section/services/electrotherapy-devices-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/cranial-electrotherapy-stimulation.md) clips its electrodes to the earlobes. Whether ear points act through a body map, as the tradition holds, or through the ear's rich sensory innervation, as a physiological reading proposes, the two accounts meet at the same anatomy and can be studied side by side without either settling the other.
