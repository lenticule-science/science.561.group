The Stomach (胃, wèi) is a yang, or fu, organ of [zang-fu](./zang-fu.md) theory, paired with the [Spleen](./spleen.md) in the earth phase of [wu xing](./wu-xing.md). It receives food and drink and "rots and ripens" them, the first stage of digestion, from which the Spleen extracts the refined essence that becomes [qi](./qi.md) and [blood](./xue.md) [@maciocia2015].

Its qi moves downward. When it rises instead, the result is nausea, belching, hiccup or vomiting (rebellious Stomach qi). It is also called the origin of fluids, and a dry mouth and tongue are among the signs of Stomach yin deficiency. The Spleen moves upward and prefers dryness; the Stomach moves downward and prefers moisture. Much of Chinese dietetics is written for keeping the two in balance.
