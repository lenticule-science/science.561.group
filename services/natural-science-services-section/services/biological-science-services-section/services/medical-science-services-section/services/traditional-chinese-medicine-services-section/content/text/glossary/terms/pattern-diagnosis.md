Pattern diagnosis (辨證, biàn zhèng, "differentiating the pattern") is how [traditional Chinese medicine](./traditional-chinese-medicine.md) reasons from a patient to a treatment. The practitioner gathers signs through the four examinations: looking (including the tongue), listening and smelling, asking, and touching (above all the pulse). The practitioner then reads them together as one *pattern* (證, zhèng) of disharmony: a configuration of excess or deficiency, heat or cold, and of the [substances](./qi.md) and [organ-function systems](./zang-fu.md) involved [@kaptchuk2000].

The pattern, not the named disease, decides the treatment. This principle is called "treatment follows pattern differentiation" (biàn zhèng lùn zhì). Two consequences follow, and clinical teaching states them as maxims: one disease may be treated differently in different patients (tóng bìng yì zhì), and different diseases may be treated alike when they present the same pattern (yì bìng tóng zhì).

Several frames organize the reading, each suited to different conditions [@maciocia2015]:

- the [eight principles](./eight-principles.md), the first and most general: interior or exterior, cold or heat, deficiency or excess, yin or yang;
- the organ-function systems ([zang-fu](./zang-fu.md)) and the substances: [qi](./qi.md), [blood](./xue.md) and [fluids](./jin-ye.md);
- the six stages of the *Shānghán lùn*, for disorders from external cold;
- the four levels of defensive, qi, nutritive and blood, from the warm-disease school, for febrile disorders;
- the [san jiao](./san-jiao.md).

The practice is explained at length in the [pattern diagnosis note](../../documents/articles/notes/pattern-diagnosis.md) and applied in [common TCM patterns](../../documents/articles/notes/common-tcm-patterns.md).
