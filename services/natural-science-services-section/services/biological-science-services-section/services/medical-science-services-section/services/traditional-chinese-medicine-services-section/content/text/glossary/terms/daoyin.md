Daoyin (導引) is usually glossed "guiding and pulling": guiding the qi and pulling, or stretching, the body. It names the classical Chinese practice of therapeutic movement, in which postures, stretches, and self-massage are done with coordinated breathing to prevent and treat illness.

It is among the best-attested body practices of early China. A painted silk chart from Mawangdui tomb 3, sealed in 168 BCE, shows over forty figures in exercise postures, several labelled with the ailment they treat. A text on the practice, the *Yinshu* ("Pulling Book"), was recovered from Zhangjiashan [@harper1998]. The *Zhubing yuanhou lun* of 610 CE, a medical compendium of disease causes, prescribes daoyin exercises for specific disorders in place of drugs [@kohn2008]. The Five Animal Frolics (wuqinxi), traditionally attributed to the Han physician Hua Tuo, belongs to this lineage.

Daoyin is the main ancestor of modern moving [qigong](./qigong.md). Forms such as [Baduanjin](./baduanjin.md) are daoyin sequences that the twentieth century renamed and standardized.
