The Lung (肺, fèi) is one of the five yin organ-function systems of [zang-fu](./zang-fu.md) theory. Like every zang it names a set of functions, which overlap with but are not identical to the anatomical lungs.

It governs [qi](./qi.md) and respiration. Breathing draws in the "clear qi" of the air, which combines with the food essence sent up by the [Spleen](./spleen.md) to form the gathering qi (zōng qì) of the chest. Its movements are twofold [@maciocia2015]:
- **Spreading** (xuān fā): it disperses [defensive qi](./wei-qi.md) and fluids to the surface, where they warm the skin and guard against external pathogens.
- **Descending** (sù jiàng): it sends qi and fluids downward, toward the Kidney and the lower body, and so is said to regulate the water passages.

Because it spreads defensive qi to the surface, the Lung rules the skin and body hair. It opens into the nose, belongs to the metal phase of [wu xing](./wu-xing.md), is paired with the Large Intestine, and houses the pò, the corporeal soul. As the uppermost organ, the first to meet wind and cold, it is called the "delicate organ".

In [pattern diagnosis](./pattern-diagnosis.md), a cough, shortness of breath, a weak voice, catching colds easily and sweating on slight exertion are read as Lung qi deficiency. The breath-centred practices of [qigong](./qigong.md), [Liuzijue](./liuzijue.md) above all, are traditionally prescribed to strengthen it.
