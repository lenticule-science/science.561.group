Yijinjing (易筋經), the "Sinew-Changing Classic", is a moving [qigong](./qigong.md) set. Its exercises alternate sustained effort against the body's own resistance with stretching, and they aim, in their own account, to transform the sinews: weak and slack into strong and supple.

Its legend credits Bodhidharma, the Indian monk said to have brought Chan Buddhism to the Shaolin monastery. The attribution does not survive historical study. The text is a Ming-dynasty composition, dated to the 1620s, that joins Daoist [daoyin](./daoyin.md) and inner-alchemical ideas to the Shaolin martial tradition's growing prestige [@shahar2008].

The Chinese Health Qigong Association's 2003 standard form has twelve movements. Examples are "Wei Tuo presenting the pestle", "plucking a star", and "nine ghosts drawing sabres". It is practised slowly, with sustained stretches and twists of the spine, and like every form it regulates body, breath, and mind together, as the [three regulations](./san-tiao.md) describe. Of the four standard forms it is the most muscular.
