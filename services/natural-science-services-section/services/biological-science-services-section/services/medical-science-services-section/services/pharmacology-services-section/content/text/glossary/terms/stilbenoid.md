Stilbenoids are a class of [polyphenolic](polyphenol.md) compounds based on a 1,2-diphenylethylene (stilbene) backbone — two phenyl rings connected by a two-carbon ethylene bridge. They are produced by plants primarily as [phytoalexins](phytoalexin.md) — defensive compounds synthesized in response to pathogen attack, UV radiation, or tissue injury. Stilbenoids are synthesized via the phenylpropanoid pathway from phenylalanine, with stilbene synthase catalyzing the final cyclization step.

## Key stilbenoid compounds

**[Resveratrol](resveratrol.md)** (*trans*-3,5,4'-trihydroxystilbene): The most studied stilbenoid. Produced in large quantities by [Japanese knotweed](../../../../../../../botany-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/japanese-knotweed.md) rhizome, grape skins, peanuts, and blueberries. Inhibits platelet aggregation, NF-κB, COX-2, and activates AMPK and SIRT1 pathways.

**Polydatin** (piceid): The glucoside form of resveratrol — resveratrol bonded to a glucose molecule. The predominant stilbenoid in Japanese knotweed rhizome. Better oral [bioavailability](bioavailability.md) than free resveratrol due to potential intestinal transport via SGLT1.

**Piceatannol**: A hydroxylated derivative of resveratrol with stronger antioxidant and immunomodulatory activity. Found in grapes, passion fruit, and knotweed.

**Pterostilbene**: A dimethylated analogue of resveratrol found primarily in blueberries. Higher oral bioavailability than resveratrol due to reduced susceptibility to glucuronidation.

## Distribution

Stilbenoid production is concentrated in a few plant families: Vitaceae (grapes), [Polygonaceae](../../../../../../../botany-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/polygonaceae.md) (knotweeds, rhubarb), Fabaceae (peanuts), and Dipterocarpaceae (tropical timber trees). Japanese knotweed produces stilbenoids at concentrations 50–100 times higher than grape skins, making it the primary commercial source for resveratrol supplements.
