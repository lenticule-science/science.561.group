"Microdosing" Adderall means taking a fraction of a clinical dose, typically 2.5–5 mg of mixed amphetamine salts instead of the 10–40 mg/day range used for ADHD, to get focus or drive without the "high", the appetite loss or the crash. The term is borrowed from psychedelic microdosing, but the pharmacology does not carry over: a psychedelic microdose sits below the perceptual threshold, while 5 mg of amphetamine is a pharmacologically active dose with measurable central and cardiovascular effects. Amphetamine microdosing is low-dose stimulant use under another name.

## The drug

Adderall is a 3:1 mixture of dextro- and levo-amphetamine salts. Amphetamine enters monoamine neurons through the dopamine and norepinephrine transporters, disrupts vesicular storage via VMAT2, activates TAAR1, and drives transporter reversal, so dopamine and norepinephrine flow *out* into the synapse. It releases transmitter rather than only blocking reuptake (methylphenidate's mechanism), which is why its effect curve is steeper and its misuse liability higher. See [how drugs work](how-drugs-work.md).

Pharmacokinetics that matter for a low-dose regime:

- **Onset and peak**: immediate-release peaks at about 3 hours; XR at about 7 hours.
- **Half-life**: about 10 hours for d-amphetamine and 13 hours for l-amphetamine in adults. A morning 5 mg dose still leaves active drug at bedtime; daily dosing accumulates toward a steady state over 2–3 days. See [drug metabolism and clearance](drug-metabolism-and-clearance.md).
- **Urinary pH controls elimination**: acidic urine (vitamin C, citrus juice) speeds excretion and shortens the effect; alkaline urine (antacids, sodium bicarbonate) slows it and prolongs exposure. At low doses this swing is a large fraction of the total effect.

## The dose-response shape

Prefrontal catecholamine signaling follows an inverted U (Arnsten 2011): too little and too much both degrade working memory and control. A person's baseline position on that curve determines what a dose does. People with low baseline performance or ADHD sit on the rising limb and gain; people already near the peak are pushed past it and lose. This is the mechanism behind the trial results below, and it applies at low doses exactly as at high ones — a small dose moves a well-functioning brain a small distance over the top. See [dose-response relationships](dose-response-relationships.md).

## What the trials show in people without ADHD

- **Weyandt et al. 2018** (*Pharmacy*), 30 mg Adderall vs placebo, double-blind crossover, healthy college students: improved self-rated attention and focus, large autonomic and mood effects, and small, mixed effects on actual tasks — no gain in reading comprehension, fluency or short-term memory.
- **Ilieva, Boland & Farah 2013** (*Neuropharmacology*), a single dose of Adderall: no enhancement on any of 13 cognitive measures for participants in general, but participants *believed* they had performed better. The subjective sense of enhancement is the most reliable effect of the drug.
- **Ilieva, Hook & Farah 2015** (meta-analysis, *J Cogn Neurosci*): small effects on inhibitory control and delayed episodic memory; working-memory effects small and inconsistent.
- **Roberts et al. 2020** (meta-analysis, *Eur Neuropsychopharmacol*), 47 placebo-controlled studies of modafinil, methylphenidate and d-amphetamine in healthy, rested adults: modafinil and methylphenidate showed small overall effects; d-amphetamine showed no significant overall effect.
- **Bowman et al. 2023** (*Science Advances*), dextroamphetamine, methylphenidate and modafinil vs placebo on knapsack optimization problems: all three increased effort (time and moves spent) and decreased the quality of solutions. Participants worked harder and produced worse answers.

Expectancy alone reproduces the felt part: in **Looby et al. 2026** (n = 183 college students), a placebo presented as 10 mg Adderall produced medium-to-large subjective effects and no objective cognitive effect.

Every one of the drug studies used doses at or above the "microdose" range. Given that full doses produce small-to-null cognitive effects, smaller doses are expected to produce smaller ones, leaving mainly the motivational and mood shift: tasks feel more engaging and the person sticks with them. That shift is real and is what microdosers are actually reporting. It is not the same as thinking better, and on complex problems it trades accuracy for persistence.

## 2.5 mg and 5 mg specifically

### Converting the doses

Adderall milligrams are milligrams of four mixed *salts*, not of amphetamine. Each Adderall tablet is one quarter each of dextroamphetamine saccharate, amphetamine aspartate, dextroamphetamine sulfate and amphetamine sulfate, so a 5 mg tablet holds about 3.1 mg amphetamine base, roughly three quarters of it the dextro isomer. The research literature doses d-amphetamine sulfate. The conversion used in the psychiatric literature (Moran 2024: 40 mg Adderall ≈ 30 mg dextroamphetamine) gives:

| Adderall | ≈ d-amphetamine equivalent | Where it sits in the literature |
|---|---|---|
| 2.5 mg | ≈ 1.9 mg | Below every dose ever tested in healthy adults |
| 5 mg | ≈ 3.75 mg | Below the lowest tested dose (5 mg d-amphetamine) |
| 10 mg | ≈ 7.5 mg | Between the 5 and 10 mg arms |
| 30 mg | ≈ 22.5 mg | The Weyandt dose; above the 20 mg "reliably euphoric" dose |

So Adderall 5 mg is slightly *under* the floor of the healthy-volunteer literature, and Adderall 2.5 mg is well under it. No published placebo-controlled study has given 2.5 mg Adderall, or its ≈2 mg d-amphetamine equivalent, to healthy adults and measured anything.

### What 5 mg d-amphetamine does in healthy adults

The 5 mg dose appears as the bottom arm of dose-response studies, almost all from US human-pharmacology labs:

- **Wardle, Yang & de Wit 2012** (*Psychopharmacology*, n = 110, Chicago; placebo, 5, 10, 20 mg crossover): 5 mg had no significant effect on any outcome, including N-back performance and error monitoring. Only 20 mg changed behavior (reduced post-error slowing, i.e. less reaction to one's own mistakes).
- **McCloskey, Palmer & de Wit 2010** (*Psychopharmacology*, n = 99, same design): 5 mg produced near-zero "feel drug" ratings (adjusted mean 0.10). The authors state that neither 5 nor 10 mg "produced appreciable levels of liking or euphoria."
- **Weafer & de Wit 2013** (*Drug Alcohol Depend*, the pooled Chicago cohort, n = 165, 0/5/10/20 mg): attention lapses predict less subjective response and impulsive action more, and both associations strengthen as the dose rises.
- **Murray et al. 2021** (*Pharmacol Biochem Behav*, n = 112): given 20 mg as four 5 mg capsules and then a free choice of 1–4 capsules or placebo, 81 of 112 chose amphetamine; those who chose it were the ones with more elation and a larger systolic rise.
- **Kassim 2023** (*Hum Psychopharmacol*, systematic review): tabulates one more 5 mg study, from 1988, with no main effect on digit span, digit-symbol substitution, memory or simple reaction time.
- **Ward et al. 1997** (*Exp Clin Psychopharmacol*, Columbia residential laboratory, n = 6, 5 and 10 mg/70 kg over 11 days): increased response *rate* on work tasks without changing accuracy; no change in self-reported drug effects. People worked faster, not better, and could not tell.
- **Kelly, Foltin & Fischman 1991** (*Pharmacol Biochem Behav*, residential laboratory, n = 6, 10 mg/70 kg twice daily — 20 mg/day in divided doses): reduced food intake, improved accuracy on some work tasks, increased talking and smoking. The behavioral effects did not fade across the dosing blocks; the subjective "stimulated", "liking" and "potency" ratings faded rapidly.

### What this means for 2.5 and 5 mg Adderall

1. At 5 mg d-amphetamine — more drug than Adderall 5 mg — healthy adults show no measurable cognitive gain and barely perceive the drug. The one robust signal is faster responding at equal accuracy.
2. Adderall 2.5 mg sits at roughly half the dose that already shows no measurable cognitive effect. Any effect a person reports at that dose is pharmacologically indistinguishable from expectancy (placebo) on the evidence that exists; Looby et al. 2026 measured that expectancy directly: believing one took Adderall produces the subjective lift and no objective change.
3. Cardiovascular effects are the effect most likely to survive at these doses. Across 56 placebo-controlled trials (Chan, Chan & Wright 2025, Cochrane), daily oral amphetamines raised systolic pressure 1.93 mmHg, diastolic 1.84 mmHg and heart rate 3.71 beats per minute, unchanged at eight weeks and beyond. The Adderall prescribing information's healthy-volunteer pharmacokinetics (10 mg d-amphetamine → peak ≈ 33 ng/mL, half-life ≈ 11.75 h) put 2.5–5 mg Adderall at a peak of roughly 6–12 ng/mL, still with a full-day tail.
4. Low doses work for some people *with* ADHD. Horrigan & Barnhill 2000 (*J Clin Psychiatry*, open-label, n = 24 adults with ADHD) started at 5 mg twice daily; 54% responded, at a mean final dose of 10.8 mg/day. A person who feels a strong, clear benefit from 2.5–5 mg — clearer than the blinded literature predicts for healthy adults — is giving evidence about their own baseline, and that is a reason to get assessed.

### Repeated dosing at low doses

The tolerance picture is split. In Kelly, Foltin & Fischman 1991, *subjective* effects (liking, stimulation) faded within days while *behavioral* effects (appetite, work accuracy) persisted. Strakowski et al. 2001 (*Neuropsychopharmacology*, n = 59, 0.25 mg/kg — about 17 mg for a 70 kg adult — given three times 48 hours apart) found the opposite for mood: vigor and euphoria *grew* with each dose (sensitization), mainly in women, while drug liking peaked at the first dose. Neither design matches daily microdosing for weeks. Together they show that repeated amphetamine changes its own effect, in both directions, within a week.

### Where the research comes from

Nearly all healthy-volunteer amphetamine dose-response work is from the US (Chicago, Columbia, Penn, Rhode Island, Cincinnati). Outside the US the work exists but uses challenge doses, not low ones:

- **Switzerland** (Basel, Liechti group: Dolder et al. 2018): 40 mg d-amphetamine vs 100 mg lisdexamfetamine; improved go-accuracy and vigilance at that high dose.
- **Austria** (Pfoser-Poschacher et al. 2026, *Fundam Clin Pharmacol*, n = 60 men): 30 mg dexamphetamine; modest effect at 3 h, fewer attention omissions at 8 h, and no correlation between plasma level and performance.
- **Australia**: Silber et al. 2005 (Swinburne): 0.42 mg/kg (≈ 30 mg) worsened simulated daytime driving — more incorrect signalling, more red-light failures. Bowman et al. 2023 (Melbourne) is the knapsack study above. Poulton et al. 2024 (n = 52 adults with overweight or obesity) titrated dexamphetamine over six weeks against placebo and found no effect on executive function.
- **UK**: Roberts et al. 2020 (Liverpool) is the meta-analysis above.

Non-US labs study amphetamine as a challenge drug, a driving hazard or an obesity treatment, not as a low-dose enhancer. The low-dose question is a US question because Adderall is a US product.

## Risks at low dose

Low dose shrinks some risks and leaves others intact.

- **Cardiovascular**: at therapeutic doses amphetamine adds about 2 mmHg of blood pressure and 4 beats per minute, sustained for as long as it is taken (Chan, Chan & Wright 2025). At microdoses the rise is smaller, and it is present every dosing day. Undiagnosed structural heart disease, arrhythmia or hypertension is the main contraindication.
- **Sleep**: with a 10–13 hour half-life, even a morning microdose shortens and lightens sleep. Lost sleep impairs cognition more than the drug enhances it — a common loop is taking more to cover the fatigue the last dose caused.
- **Tolerance and creep**: daily use produces tolerance to the subjective lift (Kelly, Foltin & Fischman 1991), and the subjective lift is the main effect. The predictable trajectory is dose escalation, which is how "microdosing" becomes ordinary stimulant use. Fixed dose, scheduled off days and a written ceiling counter this.
- **Psychosis and mania**: Moran et al. 2019 (*NEJM*) found new-onset psychosis in about 1 in 660 adolescents and young adults starting prescription amphetamine, twice the rate with methylphenidate. Moran et al. 2024 (*Am J Psychiatry*) found past-month prescription amphetamine raised odds of first psychosis or mania 2.7-fold overall and 5.3-fold above 30 mg dextroamphetamine-equivalent (40 mg Adderall). Risk is dose-dependent and lowest at microdoses, but personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder is a hard stop at any dose.
- **Anxiety and mood**: in anxious people the noradrenergic push often reads as agitation rather than focus.
- **Interactions**: MAO inhibitors (contraindicated — hypertensive crisis), other stimulants including high caffeine intake, serotonergic drugs (serotonin syndrome risk), and urinary alkalinizers (prolonged exposure).
- **Supply**: diverted or street "Adderall" pills are frequently counterfeit and have contained methamphetamine and fentanyl. A pill not dispensed by a pharmacy is an unknown substance at an unknown dose; fentanyl test strips and drug checking apply. See [pharmacology and harm reduction](pharmacology-and-harm-reduction.md).

## Legal status

Amphetamine is Schedule II in the US. Possession without a prescription and sharing or selling a prescription are federal offenses. Using one's own prescription at a lower dose than written is legal and is a conversation to have with the prescriber, who can write a 5 mg immediate-release tablet directly instead of having the patient split larger ones.

## Synthesis

1. Low-dose amphetamine reliably changes how work *feels*: more engagement, more persistence, better mood.
2. It does not reliably improve how well people without ADHD think, and on hard problems it makes output worse while making the person feel more productive.
3. The gap between felt and measured benefit is the central fact; it is also what drives tolerance and escalation.
4. For someone with undiagnosed ADHD — whose baseline sits on the rising limb of the inverted U — the picture reverses, and the right path is an evaluation, not self-titration.
5. Adderall 5 mg (≈ 3.75 mg d-amphetamine) is below the lowest dose studied in healthy adults, and the lowest studied dose (5 mg) shows no cognitive effect and is barely felt. Adderall 2.5 mg has never been studied in healthy adults anywhere: an abstract search of Europe PMC for 2.5 mg amphetamine against placebo in healthy subjects returns one hit, a 1983 bupropion study. The searches are rows in [the measurement record](../../../../../data/records/2026-10-07-low-dose-amphetamine-literature.json).
6. The research that does not exist yet: a pre-registered, double-blind crossover of 2.5 mg and 5 mg Adderall vs placebo in healthy adults over several weeks, measuring task quality (not only speed or self-report), sleep, cardiovascular load and dose drift, with an active-placebo arm to separate expectancy. That study is what would settle the microdosing claim directly.
