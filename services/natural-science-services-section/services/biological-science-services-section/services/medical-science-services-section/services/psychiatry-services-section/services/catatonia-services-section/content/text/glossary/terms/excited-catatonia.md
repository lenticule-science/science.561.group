Excited catatonia — also called hyperkinetic catatonia — names the hyperkinetic pole of the catatonic syndrome: purposeless, excessive motor activity, expressed through signs such as agitation not influenced by external stimuli, mannerism, stereotypy, echolalia, or echopraxia, occurring within the same three-of-twelve-sign DSM-5-TR threshold used for the syndrome as a whole. As with the retarded pole, DSM-5-TR does not code excited catatonia as a separate diagnostic entity; it names which signs predominate in a given presentation, not a distinct disorder.

The excited and retarded poles are distinguished by the direction of the motor abnormality, not by the presence or absence of catatonic signs: both draw on the same twelve-item sign list and the same diagnostic threshold. Co-occurrence data make the point concretely. In a cohort of 1,456 patients across 2,130 catatonic episodes, 196 patients showed excitement, and of those, 105 (53.6%) — a slight majority — also showed stupor/immobility in the same episode, a markedly higher overlap than the reverse direction, where only 11.4% of stuporous patients also showed excitement. The study's authors describe stupor and excitement as "seemingly opposite states" that nonetheless frequently coexist, supporting a single-syndrome "alternation" model over two mutually exclusive subtypes — consistent with Kahlbaum's 1874 description of a cyclic course moving through both melancholic/retarded and manic/excited phases in the same patients.

No primary study located in the literature reviewed for this term measures the transition into or out of excited catatonia directly: no imaging or biomarker study observes a patient during a retarded-to-excited or excited-to-retarded switch, and the malignant-catatonia literature's frequently cited pattern of an excited prodrome preceding rigidity is descriptive clinical teaching, not a quantified, prospectively validated transition finding. Because a majority of patients showing excited signs in the cohort above also showed stuporous signs within the same episode, an assessment protocol that samples only once, or that treats excited and retarded presentations as mutually exclusive intake categories, will misclassify a substantial share of catatonic presentations.

## Related terms

- Catatonia — the syndrome of which this is one motor pole
- Retarded catatonia — the hypokinetic pole, which co-occurs with this pole in a slight majority of excited-catatonia patients
- Malignant catatonia — a severe variant whose literature describes an excited prodrome preceding rigidity, as descriptive teaching rather than a validated finding
- Bush-Francis catatonia rating scale — the instrument used to rate hypokinetic and hyperkinetic signs alike
