Agitated depression names a depressive syndrome combining depressed mood with prominent psychomotor agitation. The clinical lineage runs from Kraepelin, who described "excited depression" as one of his mixed states of manic-depressive insanity, through his student Weygandt, who coined "agitated depression" for a mixed state combining depressed mood, psychomotor agitation, and flight of ideas. Athanasios Koukopoulos revived and extended this lineage in his own later nosological scheme.

The distinction that matters is internal to Koukopoulos's own usage, not a distinction from an outside rival: in his later scheme, Koukopoulos distinguished "agitated depression" — mixed depression with psychomotor agitation present — from "mixed depression" proper, defined by the absence of psychomotor agitation, rather than treating the two labels as synonyms. This split places agitated depression on the side of exactly the symptom (psychomotor agitation) that the DSM-5 mixed-features specifier deliberately excludes from its own criteria, which is why agitated depression sits outside the DSM-5 specifier's admitted symptom set even though it is described in the same excitatory-depression literature. Different authors have used "agitated depression" and "mixed depression" inconsistently across the wider literature, a terminological variability this dossier flags rather than resolves.

For clinical observation, the practical stakes of the distinction are treatment-relevant: agitated depression, framed by Koukopoulos as a bipolar mixed state, is proposed to warrant mood-stabilizer rather than antidepressant-only treatment, on the reasoning that antidepressant monotherapy in a state already carrying an excitatory (agitated) process risks worsening rather than resolving it. The primary validation papers for this reframing are conceptual and clinical-validation papers proposing and defending a nosological distinction rather than large epidemiological studies, and their full numeric results were not independently verified beyond what secondary review (Verdolini et al. 2015) reports.
