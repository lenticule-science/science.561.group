Waxy flexibility is one of the twelve psychomotor signs named in the DSM-5-TR's three-of-twelve-sign diagnostic threshold for catatonia, listed alongside catalepsy, stupor, mutism, negativism, posturing, mannerism, stereotypy, agitation not influenced by external stimuli, grimacing, echolalia, and echopraxia. It belongs to the hypokinetic cluster of signs most associated with the retarded pole of the syndrome, though DSM-5-TR does not require it specifically for diagnosis — any three of the twelve signs suffice.

Waxy flexibility, together with catalepsy, features in the clinical pattern that reviews describe as distinguishing malignant catatonia from neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS): malignant catatonia tends to show waxy flexibility and catalepsy along with a preceding prodrome of excitement or agitation before rigidity develops, while NMS classically begins with "lead-pipe" rigidity — a different quality of resistance, without the yielding, maintained-posture quality of waxy flexibility — and no preceding excited prodrome, tied temporally to antipsychotic exposure. This differential pattern is stated consistently across clinical reviews, but no single primary study establishing it with reported sensitivity or specificity was located, so it is best treated as clinical teaching rather than a validated instrument.

Because waxy flexibility is a qualitative, examiner-elicited sign — present or absent on passive manipulation of a limb — reliable use requires an operationalized definition and trained rating rather than unaided impression. The Bush-Francis Catatonia Rating Scale was developed and validated to give catatonic signs operationalized definitions and a standardized examination technique, achieving high interrater reliability in its validation study: a 23-item total-score reliability of 0.93 (mean per-item agreement 88.2%) and a 14-item screening-instrument reliability of 0.95 (mean per-item agreement 92.7%). Absent that structure, waxy flexibility's presence, and its role in the malignant-catatonia/NMS differential, remain matters of individual clinical judgment rather than a validated, reproducible measurement.
