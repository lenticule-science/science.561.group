The Bush-Francis Catatonia Rating Scale (BFCRS) is a 1996 instrument developed to give catatonic signs operationalized definitions and a standardized examination procedure, so that presence and severity can be rated systematically rather than impressionistically. It comprises a 23-item rating scale, used to describe severity and track treatment response, and an embedded 14-item screening instrument (the Bush-Francis Catatonia Screening Instrument), used with a cutoff of two or more of the fourteen signs present to flag a patient as catatonic.

The BFCRS is a measurement instrument, not a diagnostic nosology: it operationalizes and rates the presence and severity of individual catatonic signs — including hypokinetic items such as stupor and waxy flexibility and hyperkinetic items such as agitation — but it was not designed or validated as a discriminator between "retarded" and "excited" catatonia as diagnostic categories; a single patient can score highly on hypokinetic and hyperkinetic items at once. In its validation study — 44 simultaneous ratings of 28 cases, each meeting the two-of-fourteen screening threshold — the 23-item total score had an interrater reliability of 0.93 (mean per-item agreement 88.2%, SD 9.9), and the 14-item screening instrument's total score had a reliability of 0.95 (mean per-item agreement 92.7%, SD 4.9), indicating close agreement among trained independent examiners using the scale's operationalized definitions.

The consequence for research design is that a study wanting to characterize a patient's pole — retarded, excited, or mixed — must derive that classification itself from the scale's item-level scores, since the instrument does not output a pole label, and the high reported reliability applies to the scale as validated (single-timepoint ratings by trained raters against its own item definitions), not to any downstream pole-classification scheme built on top of it. The 23-item total score is also the instrument used in the companion literature to track treatment response, including the graded, non-universal response to lorazepam and electroconvulsive therapy: in the study that formalized this treatment protocol, 16 of 21 patients (76%) who completed a full lorazepam trial had resolution of catatonic signs, tracked using this same scale.

## Related terms

- Catatonia — the syndrome this instrument rates

- Excited catatonia — the hyperkinetic pole, described through others
- Lorazepam challenge test — a diagnostic-and-therapeutic use of catatonia response commonly tracked with this scale
