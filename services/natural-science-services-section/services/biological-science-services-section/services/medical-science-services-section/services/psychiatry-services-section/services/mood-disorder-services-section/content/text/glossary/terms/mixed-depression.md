Mixed depression is Athanasios Koukopoulos's non-DSM diagnostic construct, first described in 1992: a full major depressive syndrome co-occurring with an excitatory process — psychic agitation or inner tension, marked irritability, mood lability or reactivity, and rage or anger. Koukopoulos's central claim is that this excitatory process, not the depressive process itself, is what generates the suicidal ideation, anxiety, and agitation observed in these episodes.

Mixed depression must be kept distinct from the DSM-5 "with mixed features" specifier, which is a different, narrower construct built around the opposite symptom set. Koukopoulos's criteria explicitly include psychomotor agitation, irritability, and mood lability as core, defining symptoms. The DSM-5 specifier's Criterion B explicitly excludes exactly these three symptoms — psychomotor agitation, irritability, and distractibility — from its list of admitted manic/hypomanic symptoms, because the DSM-5 task force judged them to overlap with core depressive/anxiety symptoms and unable to reliably signal manic rather than depressive polarity. The two frameworks are therefore not two names for the same phenomenon but two different symptom sets built on opposite decisions about the same three overlapping symptoms.

A validation study of Koukopoulos's proposed criteria in 435 clinic-referred patients with a major depressive episode (Sani et al. 2014, International Mood Network sites) found the most prevalent individual criteria were absence of psychomotor retardation (84%), mood lability or marked reactivity (78%), and psychic agitation or inner tension (75%); the overall diagnostic model achieved 76.4% sensitivity and 86.3% specificity against clinical correlates used as validators (CGI severity, rapid cycling, substance-abuse history) — concurrent/construct validity against clinical correlates, not diagnostic accuracy against an independent structured-interview gold standard. A head-to-head comparison in 300 patients with a major depressive episode (155 unipolar, 145 bipolar; Mineo et al. 2022) found Koukopoulos's criteria (KMxD) and the DSM-5 mixed-features specifier (MFS) achieved similar overall discrimination of bipolar from unipolar depression by ROC-AUC (KMxD 71.9%, 95% CI 66.2-77.7%; DSM-5 MFS 77.0%, 95% CI 72.0-82.1%), with KMxD more sensitive and DSM-5 MFS more specific — neither framework dominates the other in this single Italian clinical sample.

For measurement, Koukopoulos's criteria have been operationalized as the Koukopoulos Mixed Depression Rating Scale (KMDRS), validated in 350 patients (Sani et al. 2018) with internal consistency Cronbach's α = 0.76, inter-rater reliability κ = 0.73, a two-factor structure (anger, psychomotor excitation) accounting for 80% of variance, and predictive-validity C-statistic 0.82 — an instrument built on a rival, non-DSM construct, whose own authors flag that the validation was cross-sectional. For clinical observation and research design, this means a study or clinician reporting "mixed depression" must specify which framework — Koukopoulos's criteria, the KMDRS, or the DSM-5 specifier — was used, since the two constructs are built on opposite decisions about which symptoms count and yield materially different sensitivity/specificity trade-offs on the same patients.
