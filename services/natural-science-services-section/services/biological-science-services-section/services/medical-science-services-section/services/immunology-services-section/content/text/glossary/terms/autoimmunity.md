Autoimmunity is the immune system's regulatory apparatus failing on its own constituency: T cells or antibodies that escaped central and peripheral tolerance mechanisms mount a sustained response against a self-antigen, and the body's own tissue is treated as though it required elimination. See [the immune system](../../../../../human-body-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/the-immune-system.md) for the clinical presentation of specific autoimmune diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, type 1 diabetes, multiple sclerosis, lupus); this entry focuses on how tolerance fails.

## Mechanisms of failure

- **Escape from central tolerance** — thymic negative selection is imperfect; some self-reactive T cell clones are never presented with the self-antigen they target (because it is expressed only in a specific peripheral tissue, not broadly enough to appear during thymic selection) and so are never deleted.
- **Failure of peripheral tolerance** — regulatory T cell insufficiency, or costimulatory signals reaching an otherwise-anergic self-reactive cell (inflammation from an unrelated infection can supply the missing costimulation).
- **Molecular mimicry** — a pathogen antigen structurally resembles a self-antigen closely enough that antibodies or T cells raised against the pathogen cross-react with host tissue after the infection resolves. Rheumatic fever following streptococcal infection (cross-reactivity between streptococcal M protein and cardiac myosin) is a classic case.
- **Epitope spreading** — an immune response that begins narrowly targeted (one self-antigen, one epitope) broadens over time to target additional epitopes on the same or nearby proteins, as tissue damage from the initial response releases more self-antigen for presentation.
- **Genetic susceptibility** — particular HLA (MHC) alleles are strongly associated with specific autoimmune diseases, because the allele shapes which self-peptides get displayed during thymic selection and which get presented to potentially self-reactive T cells in the periphery.

## Treatment logic

Because autoimmunity is excess or misdirected immune activity rather than immune insufficiency, treatment runs the opposite direction from treating infection: immunosuppression (corticosteroids, biologic agents targeting specific inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α or IL-6) rather than immune stimulation.

## A framing from outside immunology

Autoimmunity has a separate, older sense in political philosophy: Roberto Esposito's use of the biological metaphor to describe communities whose protective mechanisms turn on their own members (see [immunitas](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/immunitas.md), and the vault's philosophical [auto-immunity](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/auto-immunity.md) entry). This entry is the clinical/mechanistic sense; that entry is the political-philosophical extension of the same figure.

The founding note for this section's theory arc offers a third, narrower reading, proposing autoimmunity be understood specifically as "false conflict-naming" — a classifier that should route a self-antigen signal into [tolerance](./immunological-tolerance.md)'s residual-uncertainty bucket instead promotes it to a named refutation.
