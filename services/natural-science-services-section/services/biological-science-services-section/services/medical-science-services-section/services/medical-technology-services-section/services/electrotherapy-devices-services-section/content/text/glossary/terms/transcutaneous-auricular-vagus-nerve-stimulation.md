Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) is electrical stimulation applied to the auricular branch of the vagus nerve via ear-placed electrodes. The auricular branch is a documented cutaneous sensory pathway [@butt2019] that innervates the ear. taVNS devices deliver electrical current at frequencies intended to activate this nerve branch, which sends afferent (sensory) signals into the brainstem via the vagus nerve nucleus [@butt2019].

Vagal afferent signaling has reproducible effects on autonomic nervous system tone (increased parasympathetic activity), anti-inflammatory cytokine release, and pain modulation [@butt2019]. These effects are well-documented with implanted vagus nerve stimulation in clinical settings. Stimulation parameters (frequency, intensity, pulse width) determine the magnitude and nature of vagal activation [@kaniusas2019], and anatomical targeting of the auricular branch increases likelihood of afferent vagal recruitment compared to non-targeted stimulation [@kaniusas2019].

Evidence for taVNS effectiveness is emerging. Anatomical studies confirm that transcutaneous stimulation at identified auricular landmarks reliably activates the vagus nerve [@butt2019]. Clinical trials for specific taVNS indications (depression, anxiety, pain, seizures) are ongoing but limited in number and scale; reported effect sizes are modest and study quality varies. (Cranial electrotherapy stimulation, a distinct diffuse-current technology reviewed by Shekelle et al. (2018) [@shekelle2018] for chronic pain, depression, anxiety, and insomnia, found insufficient evidence for any indication; that finding does not transfer to taVNS's targeted peripheral-nerve mechanism.)

Mechanistic studies demonstrate that appropriately parameterized taVNS increases parasympathetic tone (measured by heart rate variability), reduces inflammatory markers (TNF-α, IL-6), and produces modest analgesic effects in laboratory pain models. These effects align with known vagal physiology.

Safety is generally good. Mild skin irritation at the electrode site can occur. No serious adverse effects are well-documented. Users with implanted pacemakers or other implanted electrical devices should consult a physician before use.
