## Scope

This section covers consumer-grade electrical stimulation devices used for self-care and wellness. Electrotherapy devices deliver electrical current to skin or peripheral nerves to modulate pain perception, mood, sleep, or autonomic function. This section distinguishes between:

- **Point-targeted electrical stimulation** (electroacupuncture simulators): devices that claim to activate traditional acupuncture points via electrical current
- **Diffuse transcranial stimulation** (cranial electrotherapy): devices applying weak current across the skull
- **Peripheral nerve-targeted stimulation** (auricular vagus nerve stimulation): devices targeting the vagus nerve's cutaneous branch in the ear

## Research spine

The section grounds itself in the neural mechanisms of electrical stimulation:

- **Pain modulation pathways:** How low-frequency electrical stimulation (2–10 Hz) activates endogenous opioid systems and spinal pain-gating circuits, regardless of anatomical point-specificity (Han 2003, Zhao 2008)
- **Vagal neurobiology:** How the auricular branch of the vagus nerve provides a documented afferent pathway for neuromodulation, and the plausibility of transcutaneous activation (Butt 2020, Kaniusas 2019)
- **Regulatory classification:** FDA's December 2019 final order (effective 2020) that reclassified CES to Class II for anxiety/insomnia indications (with special controls) and Class III for depression indication (requiring premarket approval); transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation devices lack specific FDA classification
- **Clinical evidence:** Meta-analyses and systematic reviews assessing efficacy for pain, mood, anxiety, and sleep across device types, with attention to study quality and publication bias
