Neuromodulation is the deliberate alteration of nervous-system activity by delivering energy or drugs to targeted sites. The electrical and magnetic methods range along two lines: how invasive they are, and whether they fire neurons outright or only bias them.

- **Implanted:** deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's disease, vagus nerve stimulation for epilepsy and depression, spinal cord stimulation for pain. These deliver pulses strong enough to drive neurons.
- **Suprathreshold, non-invasive:** transcranial magnetic stimulation and electroconvulsive therapy fire cortical neurons from outside the skull. At the skin, [TENS](../../../../../../../pain-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transcutaneous-electrical-nerve-stimulation.md) fires peripheral sensory nerves to mask pain.
- **Subthreshold, non-invasive:** [transcranial electrical stimulation](transcranial-electrical-stimulation.md), including [cranial electrotherapy stimulation](cranial-electrotherapy-stimulation.md), applies weak currents that shift [excitability](../../../../../../../../../neuroscience-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neuronal-excitability.md) without firing neurons directly [@bikson2016].
- **Peripheral routes to the brain:** [taVNS](transcutaneous-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation.md) stimulates a skin branch of the [vagus nerve](../../../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/vagus-nerve.md) in the ear, reaching the brainstem through the nerve instead of through the skull.

The further down this list, the weaker the physical effect and the harder it is to separate a device's action from the [placebo effect](../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/placebo-effect.md).
