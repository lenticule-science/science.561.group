Electrosleep was the attempt to induce sleep, or a restful sleep-like state, by passing weak pulsed current through electrodes on the head, commonly over the eyelids and behind the ears. Experiments with electrically induced sleep in animals date from the early twentieth century. The method was developed clinically on a large scale in the Soviet Union from the 1950s and spread to Eastern and Western Europe for insomnia and anxiety.

Patients rarely actually slept, and the effect was reframed as relaxation. The American successor devices of the 1970s adopted the name [cranial electrotherapy stimulation](cranial-electrotherapy-stimulation.md) and moved the electrodes to the ears, while keeping the pulsed-current approach [@datta2013].
