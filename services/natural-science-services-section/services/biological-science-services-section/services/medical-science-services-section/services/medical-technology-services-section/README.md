## Scope

Medical technology (MT) is the study of devices, instruments, and materials used in medical and wellness applications—evaluated against standards, clinical evidence, and regulatory frameworks rather than marketing claims. This section examines consumer wellness devices purchased for the 561 Group's medical program, distinguishing between:

- Devices with mechanistic bases supported by reproducible science
- Marketing claims exceeding the evidence base
- Regulatory status (FDA medical device classification, IEC safety standards)
- Safety and contraindication profiles

The goal is not to endorse devices but to understand what they do, what the evidence says, and whether they can enter a non-regulated medical kit according to the Group's Inflammation Station gate (no regulated medical devices).

## Research spine

The section is grounded in:

- **Mechanism-first evaluation:** Understanding the physics and physiology underlying each device (electrical stimulation, mechanical pressure, photon absorption, heat transfer)
- **Evidence hierarchy:** Distinguishing between mechanistic plausibility, clinical trial data, systematic reviews, and marketing extrapolation
- **Regulatory realism:** Understanding FDA classification, international medical device frameworks, and how devices evade or trigger regulation through labeling choices
- **Safety and contraindication:** Documenting adverse effects, absolute contraindications, and safe-use parameters
- **The 561 Group's medical paradigm:** Identifying which devices can enter a non-regulated wellness kit and what evidence-labeled claims are honest

## Device families

1. **Electrotherapy devices:** Consumer electrical stimulation for pain, mood, and neuromodulation
   - Electroacupuncture point stimulators
   - Cranial electrotherapy stimulation (CES)
   - Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS)

2. **Manual therapy devices:** Handheld mechanical and thermomechanical stimulation
   - Percussion massage devices
   - Acupressure mats
   - Vacuum cupping devices
   - Multi-modal massagers (heat, vibration, phototherapy)
   - Resistance stretching straps

3. **Air purification:** Environmental air quality devices (existing child section)

## Distinction from product marketing

Consumer wellness devices are marketed with:

- Proprietary terminology ("breaks up fascia," "activates meridians," "detoxifies")
- Before-and-after imagery
- Celebrity or athlete endorsement
- Vague health claims skirting FDA regulations
- Price points justified by claimed scientific basis

Medical technology evaluation replaces these with:

- Mechanistic description (what physical principle operates the device)
- Cited evidence (which published trials support which claims)
- Regulatory status (what the FDA actually classified or approved)
- Honest scope (what evidence supports, what remains unclear)
