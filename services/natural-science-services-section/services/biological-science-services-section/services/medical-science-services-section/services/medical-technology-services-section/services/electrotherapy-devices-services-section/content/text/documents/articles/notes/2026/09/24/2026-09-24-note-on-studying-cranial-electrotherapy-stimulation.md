[Cranial electrotherapy stimulation](../../../../../../glossary/terms/cranial-electrotherapy-stimulation.md) (CES) sounds simple: clip two electrodes to the earlobes and let a small current run for half an hour. Judging it well takes three kinds of knowledge. The first is physics: how much current reaches the brain. The second is neuroscience: what a weak field can do to neurons and their rhythms. The third is clinical method: how to tell a device's effect from the effect of using a device. This note sets them out in the order they build on each other.

## 1. Current, voltage and the path through the head

Start with [current](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/physics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/electric-current.md), [voltage](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/physics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/voltage.md) and [impedance](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/physics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/electrical-impedance.md), and Ohm's law, *V = I × R* [@halliday2013]. A stimulator sets the current, typically a fraction of a milliampere to a few milliamperes. It raises or lowers the voltage to push that current through whatever impedance it meets. Dry skin can call for tens of volts; wet electrodes need far less.

Then follow the current into the head. [Alternating](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/physics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/alternating-current.md), biphasic pulses keep the net charge at zero, which protects the skin. [Current density](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/physics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/current-density.md) at the electrode decides whether it stings. The head is layered: conductive scalp, resistive skull, very conductive cerebrospinal fluid. So most current runs around the head in the scalp. Direct recordings in rats and humans found only a small fraction reaches the brain as an [electric field](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../physical-science-services-section/services/physics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/electric-field.md), of fractions of a volt per metre at common intensities [@voroslakos2018]. Modelling of ear-clip montages specifically finds that some current does reach cortical and deeper structures [@datta2013].

*Check your understanding:* why does the same device feel stronger on dry skin, and why does that not mean more current reaches the brain?

## 2. What a weak field does to a neuron

A neuron keeps a [membrane potential](../../../../../../../../../../../../../neuroscience-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/membrane-potential.md) of about −70 mV and fires an [action potential](../../../../../../../../../../../human-body-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/action-potential.md) past threshold. A field of a volt per metre shifts that potential by well under a millivolt. It is nowhere near firing, but it moves [excitability](../../../../../../../../../../../../../neuroscience-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neuronal-excitability.md). The key experiment is Nitsche and Paulus's: a few minutes of 1 mA direct current changed motor-cortex excitability beyond the stimulation period itself [@nitsche2000]. Weak stimulation works by bias, not by command.

## 3. Rhythms, and pushing them

The brain's activity is rhythmic. [Neural oscillations](../../../../../../../../../../../../../neuroscience-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neural-oscillation.md) in the delta-to-gamma bands track sleep, rest and attention. The [alpha rhythm](../../../../../../../../../../../../../neuroscience-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/alpha-rhythm.md) of relaxed wakefulness is the one CES was first claimed to increase. [EEG](../../../../../../../../../../../../../neuroscience-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/electroencephalography.md) measures these rhythms, though not easily during stimulation. [Entrainment](../../../../../../../../../../../../../neuroscience-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neural-entrainment.md) explains how a weak but rhythmic push can matter: it acts on an oscillation that is already running, the way small, well-timed pushes build up a swing [@herrmann2013].

## 4. The family CES belongs to

[Neuromodulation](../../../../../../glossary/terms/neuromodulation.md) ranges from implanted stimulators to skin electrodes. CES belongs to [transcranial electrical stimulation](../../../../../../glossary/terms/transcranial-electrical-stimulation.md), alongside [tDCS](../../../../../../glossary/terms/transcranial-direct-current-stimulation.md) (direct current, for excitability) and [tACS](../../../../../../glossary/terms/transcranial-alternating-current-stimulation.md) (sinusoidal current, for entrainment). It descends from Soviet-era [electrosleep](../../../../../../glossary/terms/electrosleep.md). Two neighbours share its ear electrodes but work differently:
- **[taVNS](../../../../../../glossary/terms/transcutaneous-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation.md)** stimulates the [vagus nerve](../../../../../../../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/vagus-nerve.md)'s skin branch.
- **[TENS](../../../../../../../../../../../pain-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transcutaneous-electrical-nerve-stimulation.md)** fires sensory nerves to mask pain.

Part of CES's current passes through those same ear nerves. So "CES works through the brain" and "CES works through the vagus" are rival hypotheses, and both need to stay on the table.

## 5. Reading the evidence

CES is used for anxiety, insomnia and depression, symptoms a person reports and where the [placebo effect](../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/placebo-effect.md) is large [@kaptchuk2015]. Whether a trial means anything depends on its [sham](../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/sham-stimulation.md) and its [blinding](../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/blinding.md). Two reviews summarize what exists:
- A systematic review of 26 randomized trials found low-strength evidence of modest benefit in patients with anxiety and depression, and insufficient evidence for other uses [@shekelle2018].
- A Cochrane review found no adequate evidence for acute depression [@kavirajan2014].

Read them for what they are. "Insufficient evidence" means the trials cannot tell, not that a benefit has been ruled out. "Modest, low-certainty benefit" is a real signal that larger, well-blinded trials would be needed to confirm. This section's earlier [device note](../04/2026-09-04-note-on-cranial-electrotherapy-and-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation-devices.md) originally overstated both the physics and this evidence; it has been corrected.

## 6. Where it meets this forest's other practices

- **Qigong and slow breathing.** They reach for the same states by another road. [Slow-paced breathing](../../../../../../../../../../../../../physiology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/slow-paced-breathing.md) reliably raises heart-rate variability through the baroreflex and the vagus. [Qigong](../../../../../../../../../../../traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/qigong.md) and meditation are associated with increased alpha. The [evidence for qigong](../../../../../../../../../../../traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/24/2026-09-24-note-on-the-clinical-evidence-for-qigong-and-taiji.md) faces the same problems of placebo and blinding, from the other side: there, no sham is possible at all.
- **The ear.** [Auricular acupuncture](../../../../../../../../../../../traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/auricular-acupuncture.md) and [electroacupuncture](../../../../../../../../../../../traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/electroacupuncture.md) use the same ear anatomy as CES and taVNS, with a different account of why it matters.

## Open questions

1. How large are the brain fields from each commercial ear-clip montage and dose, measured rather than modelled?
2. Does CES still work when the ear's cutaneous nerves are blocked? That test would separate the transcranial route from the vagal one.
3. Do subsensory devices with truly indistinguishable shams show the same benefits as perceptible ones?
4. Does CES change the EEG in the same way that slow breathing or qigong practice does, and does any EEG change predict symptom relief?
