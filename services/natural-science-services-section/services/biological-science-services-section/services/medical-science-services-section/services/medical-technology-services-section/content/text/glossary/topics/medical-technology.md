Medical technology is the science of devices, instruments, and materials used in medical and wellness applications. The 561 Group's medical technology section evaluates consumer wellness devices used in care and self-care against standards, clinical evidence, and regulatory frameworks rather than marketing claims. This involves understanding device design and the physics or physiology underlying its operation; assessing what the clinical evidence supports versus what marketing extrapolates; determining regulatory status under FDA and international frameworks; and documenting safety profiles and contraindications.

Medical technology distinguishes between consumer wellness devices and medical devices as classified by the FDA, which has implications for which devices can enter the Group's medical program under the Inflammation Station gate (non-regulated device requirement). The evaluation is mechanism-first: understanding electrical stimulation principles, mechanical pressure and vibration, photon absorption and cellular signaling, and heat transfer allows the assessor to determine what claims are plausible, which evidence sources are load-bearing, and what represents overstatement or marketing fiction. Consumer wellness devices often claim effects that exceed their mechanistic basis or the available clinical evidence, and distinguish between honest claims and overreach is a core duty of medical technology evaluation.
