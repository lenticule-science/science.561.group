Blinding, also called masking, withholds from people in a trial which group each participant is in. In a single-blind trial only the participants are unaware. In a double-blind trial the clinicians and assessors are too. Blinding exists because knowing shapes both what participants report and what observers see, especially for subjective outcomes [@kaptchuk2015].

Drug trials blind with identical pills. Practice and device trials often cannot. No one can be given a sham [qigong](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/qigong.md) without knowing it, so movement trials fall back on comparing active controls (the evidence in the qigong note is read that way). Stimulation trials depend on a convincing [sham](sham-stimulation.md). When blinding fails, [placebo effects](placebo-effect.md) and a device's specific effect cannot be told apart.
