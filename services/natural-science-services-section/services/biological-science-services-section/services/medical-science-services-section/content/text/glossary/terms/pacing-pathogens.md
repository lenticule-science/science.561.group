**Pacing pathogens** is a house term coined by emsenn on 2026-09-02. Pacing pathogens are the pathogens whose acute infection is documented to precipitate the chronic symptom family that infection-associated chronic illness names: post-exertional malaise, unrefreshing sleep, cognitive impairment, orthostatic intolerance, and fatigue that rest does not repair. The name states what the illnesses they leave behind have in common in practice: the one management strategy with consistent patient and guideline support is [pacing](../../../../services/myalgic-encephalomyelitis-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/pacing.md), activity kept inside the limit that avoids [post-exertional malaise](../../../../services/myalgic-encephalomyelitis-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/post-exertional-malaise.md).

## Which pathogens

Membership is evidential, not taxonomic. A pathogen belongs when prospective or well-characterized cohorts show a persistent post-infective syndrome with this symptom shape in a fraction of those infected. Documented members, with the strongest evidence first:

- **Epstein-Barr virus, Ross River virus, and Coxiella burnetii** — the Dubbo prospective cohort followed acute infections with all three and found a post-infective fatigue syndrome in about eleven percent at six months, with a stable case rate independent of which pathogen precipitated it [hickie2006]. That single finding is the empirical basis for grouping the pathogens at all. Infectious mononucleosis in college students has since been followed prospectively into myalgic encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome [jason2021].
- **SARS-CoV-2** — long COVID, the condition that supplied the statistical power behind the umbrella; see [infection-associated chronic illness](infection-associated-chronic-illness.md) and the long COVID section.
- **Giardia lamblia** — a protozoan, not a virus: the Bergen waterborne outbreak produced a well-characterized cohort with chronic fatigue syndrome after enteritis [naess2012].
- **Borrelia burgdorferi, Ebola virus, dengue virus, and SARS-CoV-1** — the remaining conditions the National Academies grouped as infection-associated chronic illness, each with its own post-infective syndrome literature cited on the umbrella term's carrier.
- Choutka and colleagues' review of unexplained post-acute infection syndromes is the field's own survey of this set and of the many further pathogens with weaker or single-cohort evidence [choutka2022].

The set is open at its edge. A pathogen with one small cohort belongs provisionally, and the carrier that adds it names the cohort.

## What the term is not

The term names the triggers, not the illnesses: infection-associated chronic illness groups the conditions people are left with, while pacing pathogens groups the agents that start them, across viruses, bacteria, and protozoa alike, which is why it is not a synonym for post-viral. It also makes no mechanistic claim. Whether the pathogens share one pathway or converge on a common downstream state is exactly what the field has not settled, and the term holds that open the way the umbrella term does.

## Consequence

For observation and engineering the precipitating pathogen is one attribute on a person's record, never the key the software is organized around: a pacing measure, a record schema, or a public explainer addresses the symptom family, and a new pathogen joining the set changes a list, not a system. The same holds for the public products: a channel, a newsletter, or a kit built for people with these illnesses serves everyone the set covers, not one virus's patients.
