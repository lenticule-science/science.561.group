Access intimacy is a concept developed by [Mia Mingus](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/mia-mingus.md) within the [disability justice](./disability-justice.md) framework. It names the feeling of ease that arises when someone understands your access needs and moves to meet them without requiring you to explain, justify, or prove those needs. Access intimacy is not accommodation in the institutional sense — not the filing of paperwork, the requesting of exceptions, or the provision of services after formal evaluation. It is the trust that access will be anticipated and shared as part of the relationship itself.

Mingus distinguishes access intimacy from other forms of intimacy because it emerges specifically from the experience of navigating a world not built for your body or mind. It can exist between strangers who share an understanding of inaccessibility, or it can be absent in close relationships where access needs go unrecognized. The concept foregrounds the [labor](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md) that disabled people perform to secure access — [labor](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md) that access intimacy relieves not by eliminating the need but by distributing it across a relationship rather than concentrating it in the person who needs access.

emsenn references access intimacy in "On white-supremacist covid-eugenicist queers" (2025-09-23), connecting it to [Leah Lakshmi Piepzna-Samarasinha](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/leah-lakshmi-piepzna-samarasinha.md)'s work in *Care Work*. Collective care infrastructure — the kind that disability justice movements build — depends on access intimacy as its sustaining condition. Without the trust that others will share in access [labor](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md), communal care becomes exhausting negotiation rather than mutual support. Access intimacy is what makes collective care sustainable.
