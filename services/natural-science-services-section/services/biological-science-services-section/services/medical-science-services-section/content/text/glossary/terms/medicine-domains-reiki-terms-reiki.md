A Japanese healing practice developed by Mikao Usui in which a practitioner channels energy through their hands to support healing

**Reiki** (Japanese: 霊気, literally &ldquo;spiritual energy&rdquo; or &ldquo;universal life energy&rdquo;) is a healing practice developed by Mikao Usui in Japan in the early 1920s. It centers on the idea that a [practitioner](../../../../services/reiki-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/practitioner.md) can channel energy through their hands — by direct touch or proximity — to support healing in the recipient.

A reiki session typically involves the recipient lying down, clothed, while the practitioner places their hands on or near a series of positions on the body, each held for several minutes. The practitioner does not manipulate tissue (as in massage) or apply pressure (as in acupressure); the practice is based on energy transfer rather than physical intervention.

Reiki is organized into levels or degrees. First Degree introduces the basic hand positions and self-treatment. Second Degree introduces symbols and the concept of distance healing. Third Degree (Master level) qualifies the practitioner to teach and attune others. The attunement process — in which a Reiki master performs a ritual to &ldquo;open&rdquo; a student&rsquo;s capacity to channel energy — is central to the tradition&rsquo;s transmission model.

From a biomedical perspective, there is limited evidence for reiki&rsquo;s efficacy beyond placebo effects and the general benefits of relaxation and compassionate attention. From the perspective of those who practice it, reiki operates on a dimension of experience that biomedical measurement does not capture. The practice continues to be widely used in integrative medicine settings, hospice care, and personal wellness.
