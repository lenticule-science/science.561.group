The Eight Principles (ba gang 八纲) are the most fundamental diagnostic framework in [Traditional Chinese Medicine](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/applied-sciences/medicine/_index.md). They organize all clinical findings into four pairs of complementary opposites that locate a patient&rsquo;s condition within a diagnostic coordinate system:

Pair
Question it answers

**Interior / Exterior** (li / biao 里/表)
Where is the problem?

**Hot / Cold** (re / han 热/寒)
What is its thermal character?

**Excess / Deficiency** (shi / xu 实/虚)
Is something present that shouldn&rsquo;t be, or absent that should be?

**[Yin / Yang](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/yin-yang.md)** (yin / yang 阴/阳)
What is the overall character?

Every TCM diagnosis begins with the Eight Principles. Before identifying which organ system is involved or which specific pathological products are present, the practitioner determines: is this interior or exterior? Hot or cold? Excess or deficient? Yin or Yang? These four determinations establish the basic treatment strategy — the wrong determination leads to the wrong treatment.

## The four pairs

**Interior / Exterior**: Exterior patterns are acute, surface-level, and caused by external invasion — fever, chills, body aches, floating pulse. Interior patterns are chronic, deep, and involve the [organ-function systems](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/zang-fu.md) — organ-specific symptoms, deep pulse. Treatment of exterior patterns &ldquo;releases the exterior&rdquo; (inducing sweating). Treatment of interior patterns addresses the specific organ dysfunction.

**Hot / Cold**: Hot patterns show inflammation — redness, thirst for cold, dark urine, rapid pulse, red tongue with yellow coating. Cold patterns show insufficient warmth — pallor, preference for warm, clear urine, slow pulse, pale tongue with white coating. Hot patterns are treated with cooling methods; cold patterns with warming methods. [Hu Zhang](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hu-zhang.md), being slightly cold, treats hot patterns.

**Excess / Deficiency**: Excess means a pathogenic factor is present — stagnation, accumulation, invasion. Symptoms are strong, forceful. Deficiency means a vital substance is depleted — [Qi](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/qi.md), [Blood](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/xue.md), [Yin](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/yin-deficiency.md), [Yang](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/yang-deficiency.md). Symptoms are weak, dull. Excess is treated by draining; deficiency by tonifying. Many presentations are mixed.

**Yin / Yang**: The master pair that summarizes the others. Yin patterns are interior + cold + deficient. Yang patterns are exterior + hot + excess. This pair provides the highest-level diagnostic summary.

## Why the Eight Principles prevent harm

The Eight Principles exist to prevent the most dangerous prescribing errors:

- Applying cold treatment to a cold pattern (deepens the cold)

- Applying hot treatment to a hot pattern (inflames the heat)

- Draining a deficient patient (further depletes)

- Tonifying an excess pattern (strengthens the pathogen)

Each of these errors makes the patient worse. The Eight Principles are the minimum diagnostic framework needed to avoid them.
