In a device trial the sham is the placebo: the same headset, electrodes, session length and ritual, but no effective stimulation. A good sham is indistinguishable from the real thing to the participant, and ideally to the staff as well.

That is hard for anything that can be felt. In [tDCS](../../../../services/medical-technology-services-section/services/electrotherapy-devices-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transcranial-direct-current-stimulation.md) the usual sham ramps the current up for 30 seconds to produce the tingle, then switches it off. Experienced participants can often tell [@bikson2016]. [Cranial electrotherapy](../../../../services/medical-technology-services-section/services/electrotherapy-devices-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/cranial-electrotherapy-stimulation.md) devices that run below the threshold of sensation have the opposite property: the sham is truly indistinguishable, but there is then no way to confirm the active device was delivering anything noticeable either. How well a sham works determines how far [placebo effects](placebo-effect.md) can be separated from the device's own action, which is why good trials check whether participants could guess their group.
