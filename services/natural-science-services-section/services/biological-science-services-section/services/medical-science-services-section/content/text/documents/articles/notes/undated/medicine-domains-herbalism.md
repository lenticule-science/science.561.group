Herbalism is the study and practice of using plants and plant extracts for therapeutic purposes. It spans multiple traditions — Western herbalism, [Traditional Chinese Medicine](../../../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/traditional-chinese-medicine.md), Ayurveda, Indigenous plant medicine practices worldwide — and interfaces with modern [pharmacology](../../../../../../services/pharmacology-services-section/content/text/glossary/topics/pharmacology.md) through pharmacognosy (the study of drugs derived from natural sources).

Herbalism differs from pharmacology not in its subject matter but in its unit of analysis. Pharmacology isolates active compounds and studies them individually. Herbalism works with whole plants and plant combinations, treating the plant as an integrated chemical system whose therapeutic effects emerge from the interaction of multiple compounds — not from a single active ingredient.
