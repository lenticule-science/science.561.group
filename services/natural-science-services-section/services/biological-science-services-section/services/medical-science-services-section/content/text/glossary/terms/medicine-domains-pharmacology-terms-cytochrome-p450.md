A superfamily of heme-containing enzymes — primarily in the liver — that metabolize the majority of pharmaceutical drugs, dietary compounds, and environmental toxins through oxidation reactions

The cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes are a superfamily of heme-containing monooxygenases found primarily in the endoplasmic reticulum of hepatocytes (liver cells) and enterocytes (intestinal cells). They catalyze the oxidation of a vast range of substrates — pharmaceutical drugs, dietary compounds, endogenous hormones, and environmental toxins — and are responsible for the metabolism of approximately 75% of all clinically used drugs.

The name reflects their biochemistry: &ldquo;cytochrome&rdquo; (colored cellular protein), &ldquo;P450&rdquo; (the protein absorbs light at 450 nm wavelength when bound to carbon monoxide). Humans have 57 CYP genes organized into 18 families and 44 subfamilies.

## Drug interactions

CYP enzymes are the primary mechanism of pharmacokinetic drug interactions. A compound that inhibits a CYP enzyme slows the metabolism of all other drugs that enzyme processes, effectively increasing their blood levels — sometimes to toxic concentrations. A compound that induces a CYP enzyme accelerates metabolism of other drugs, reducing their efficacy.

This is clinically relevant for herbal-drug interactions. [Resveratrol](../../../../services/pharmacology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/resveratrol.md) inhibits CYP3A4 and CYP1A2, meaning Japanese knotweed preparations can increase blood levels of drugs metabolized by these enzymes. [Emodin](../../../../services/pharmacology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/emodin.md) also affects CYP-mediated metabolism. These interactions are the pharmacological basis for the contraindication of [Hu Zhang](../../../../services/traditional-chinese-medicine-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hu-zhang.md) with certain pharmaceutical medications.

## Genetic variation

CYP genes are highly polymorphic — different individuals carry different versions of CYP genes, producing enzymes with different metabolic capacity. CYP2D6 polymorphism is particularly well studied: &ldquo;poor metabolizers&rdquo; (lacking functional CYP2D6) accumulate drugs that the enzyme normally clears, while &ldquo;ultra-rapid metabolizers&rdquo; (with duplicated CYP2D6 genes) clear drugs so quickly they may not achieve therapeutic levels at standard doses.
