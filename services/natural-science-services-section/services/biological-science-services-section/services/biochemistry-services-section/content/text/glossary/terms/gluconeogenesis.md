Gluconeogenesis is the synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors — lactate, glycerol, and glucogenic amino acids — carried out chiefly by the liver and to a lesser extent the kidney, sustaining blood glucose once hepatic glycogen is depleted [rui2014]. Lactate returned from working muscle is a principal substrate, which is the **acts-on** relation asserted here (**established**).

It is not the reverse of glycolysis run backwards: three glycolytic steps are effectively irreversible and are bypassed by distinct enzymes, which is what allows the two pathways to be regulated in opposite directions in the same cell [rui2014]. **Established.**

The **controlled-by** relation to cortisol is sourced in Kuo and colleagues' review of glucocorticoid regulation of glucose homeostasis, which sets out the transcriptional upregulation of gluconeogenic enzymes by the glucocorticoid receptor and the resulting rise in hepatic glucose output [kuo2015] (**established**).

Because glucocorticoids upregulate the pathway's enzymes transcriptionally, an insufficient cortisol response leaves an exertional demand unfunded at the substrate level rather than merely unregulated.
