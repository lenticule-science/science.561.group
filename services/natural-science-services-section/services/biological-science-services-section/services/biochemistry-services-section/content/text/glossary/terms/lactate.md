Lactate is the product of pyruvate reduction by lactate dehydrogenase, formed whenever glycolytic flux exceeds the rate at which pyruvate enters mitochondrial oxidation, and it is produced continuously in fully oxygenated tissue as well as under oxygen limitation [gladden2004; brooks2018]. The **produced-by** relation to anaerobic glycolysis names its formation pathway; the qualifier that the same reaction runs under full oxygenation is part of the definition, not an exception to it.

It is not a dead-end waste product of anaerobic metabolism: it is oxidized as a fuel by heart, muscle, and brain, and returned to the liver as a gluconeogenic substrate, so its concentration reflects the balance of production and clearance rather than a shortfall of oxygen [brooks2018]. **Established**: the lactate-shuttle account has displaced the oxygen-debt account in exercise physiology, and Gladden's review sets out the correction in detail [gladden2004]. The **acts-on** relation to gluconeogenesis is this hepatic return.

A blood lactate value is therefore a statement about two rates at once, and rising lactate during exercise cannot be read as evidence that a tissue has run out of oxygen. For any measurement built on exertion, the consequence is that a lactate threshold marks a change in the balance between production and clearance, which is a property of the whole system rather than of the muscle alone.
