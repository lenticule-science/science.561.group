Reactive oxygen species are partially reduced derivatives of molecular oxygen — principally superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, and the hydroxyl radical — generated by mitochondrial electron transport and by dedicated enzymes such as the NADPH oxidases [sies2020]. The **produced-by** relation to the mitochondrion is sourced in Zorov, Juhaszova and Sollott's review, which sets out mitochondrial production and the ROS-induced-ROS-release amplification in which oxidants from one mitochondrion trigger release from its neighbours [zorov2014] (**established**).

They are not simply damage products: at low flux hydrogen peroxide is a specific second messenger that oxidizes particular cysteine residues, so their role is set by flux, species, and site rather than by presence or absence [sies2020]. **Established**, and it is the correction the modern literature makes to the older oxidative-stress framing.

The **enables** relation to nuclear factor kappa B is sourced in Morgan and Liu's review of the crosstalk, which documents oxidant activation of the pathway while stating plainly that the relationship is bidirectional and context-dependent — the pathway also controls oxidant levels, and oxidants inhibit it in some settings [morgan2010]; Mittal and colleagues review the same species as effectors in inflammation and tissue injury [mittal2014]. **Well-supported and explicitly context-dependent, not a simple one-way activation.**

A measurement of oxidative stress in a person is therefore only interpretable against that person's own antioxidant capacity and the compartment measured, and a raised marker does not by itself indicate damage rather than signalling.
