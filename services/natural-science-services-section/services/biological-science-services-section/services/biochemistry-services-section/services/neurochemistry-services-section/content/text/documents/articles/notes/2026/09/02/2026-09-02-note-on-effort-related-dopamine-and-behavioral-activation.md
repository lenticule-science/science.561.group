## What this argues

The chemistry of getting moving is not the chemistry of enjoying the
destination, and the literature that established this says so in its own
titles. Mesolimbic dopamine gates the exertion of effort and the
activation of behavior; it does not mediate primary appetite or the
hedonic response to reward. That finding is rodent-causal, three decades
old, and routinely misdescribed. What is far less settled is everything
downstream of it: whether human effort-based decision-making tasks show
a stable deficit in depression, whether psychomotor retardation predicts
treatment response, and — the question this corpus's research line
actually asks — whether an acute arousal signal can transiently restore
activation in an organism already in a low-activation state. On the last
of these, the honest answer is that no located primary study tests it.

## Effort, not pleasure: what accumbens dopamine depletion does

The dissociation was built to be unambiguous.
[Salamone](../../../../../../glossary/terms/dopamine.md), Cousins and
Bucher trained rats on a T-maze where one arm delivered four 45-milligram
pellets and the other two, then placed a 44-centimetre barrier in the
high-reward arm so that the better outcome cost more effort. Haloperidol
and 6-hydroxydopamine depletion of nucleus accumbens dopamine shifted
choice away from the high-effort, high-reward arm toward the low-effort,
low-reward one — while the same animals consumed the same food normally
once the barrier was removed. The paper's title asks the question
directly: anhedonia or anergia?

The companion study sharpened it. Rats chose between lever-pressing for
preferred pellets and free-feeding on less-preferred chow. Accumbens
dopamine depletion and haloperidol decreased lever pressing for the
preferred food and increased free consumption of the less-preferred
chow. Total intake was not suppressed. Preference for the better food,
when it was free, was intact. What the manipulation removed was the
willingness to work, not the capacity to want or to enjoy.

Salamone and Correa state the position for the whole programme in the
abstract of their 2012 *Neuron* review: it has become traditional to
label dopamine neurons as reward neurons, but this is an
overgeneralization, and accumbens dopamine does not mediate primary food
motivation or appetite, but is involved in appetitive and aversive
motivational processes including behavioral activation, exertion of
effort, approach behavior, sustained task engagement, Pavlovian
processes, and instrumental learning.

This is rodent-causal evidence — lesions and receptor antagonists, not
correlations. It does not establish that any human clinical state of
anergia arises from accumbens dopamine depletion. That inference is an
extrapolation from an animal model, and no study reviewed here closes
the gap between the rodent neurochemistry and the human behavioral
measures below.

## The human effort literature is splitting, not converging

The Effort Expenditure for Rewards Task asks a person to choose
repeatedly between an easy task for a small reward and a hard task for a
larger one, with reward magnitude and probability varied. Treadway and
colleagues introduced it in a nonclinical sample of 61 undergraduates
and found that trait anhedonia predicted reduced willingness to choose
the harder, higher-paying option. A later clinical study reported that
patients with major depressive disorder chose the hard option less often
and used magnitude and probability information less effectively, with
longer current-episode duration predicting worse performance; that
study's exact sample size was not confirmed in the research pass behind
this note.

The replication picture is genuinely mixed. A transdiagnostic study of
effort-cost decision-making across psychotic and mood disorders found
that the major-depressive and bipolar groups did not differ from
controls, with the schizophrenia group driving the effect. A separate
computational study using a different task — an apple-gathering
paradigm, not the Effort Expenditure for Rewards Task — found a group
difference between combined depressed and remitted participants (n=87)
and combined relatives and controls (n=93) of d = 0.38, p = 0.004, but
attributed it to a trait-like acceptance bias rather than to altered
reward or effort sensitivity. A pooled transdiagnostic meta-analysis
covering 886 participants with major depressive disorder, 1,776 with
schizophrenia-spectrum diagnoses and 1,848 controls across 50 studies
and 71 effect sizes reports anhedonia severity as one of two significant
moderators of effort-based choice on high-reward trials.

Read together, these do not support a uniform, large, replicated
depression-specific effort deficit. They support a real but
heterogeneous effect whose size depends on task, population and
measurement, and which may be larger in schizophrenia-spectrum illness
than in depression. A tool or a study that assumes the strong version is
powered against a quantity the aggregate evidence does not yet supply.

## Psychomotor retardation: measurable, prognostically unsettled

Psychomotor retardation is measurable beyond clinician rating scales —
by actigraphy, kinematic analysis and ground-reaction-force platforms —
and a systematic review synthesizing 116 papers associates it with
prefrontal and dopaminergic abnormalities. In a cohort of 122 patients
with unipolar psychotic depression, baseline prevalence of psychomotor
retardation was 73%, falling to 35% after six weeks of treatment, and
its presence predicted lower remission and greater delusion persistence.

Its value as a treatment-response predictor is not settled and appears
to differ by drug class: baseline retardation predicted response to
milnacipran, a serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor, in at least
one cited trial, while similarly designed studies found it did not
predict selective-serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor response. The largest
pooled analysis located — a secondary analysis of nine randomized
controlled trials, still a preprint and not peer-reviewed — states
plainly that it remains unclear whether psychomotor retardation is
independently associated with poorer prognosis. Any claim that
retardation predicts poor antidepressant response must therefore carry
its drug class with it.

## Anhedonia and anergia are separable, and co-occur anyway

The conceptual separation of consummatory pleasure deficit from
effort-allocation deficit is what made the dopamine literature legible;
before it, both sat under one word and dopamine's role looked
paradoxical. The separation is argued in review rather than demonstrated
in a single decisive experiment — both of the standard sources are
narrative syntheses of the rodent causal work and the human behavioral
and imaging literature, not primary studies.

Separable does not mean independent. The same meta-analysis that
moderates effort-based choice by anhedonia severity shows the two
travelling together in clinical samples. The distinction earns its place
by telling a researcher which measurement answers which question, not by
sorting patients into two kinds.

## The switch question, answered honestly

The question this research line asks of this literature is whether acute
arousal — a stressor, a noradrenergic surge — transiently restores
effortful, mobilized behavior in an organism already in a low-activation
state. No primary study located in this research pass tests that
hypothesis.

What exists is adjacent and does not substitute. Yohimbine, an
alpha-2 adrenoceptor antagonist used as a pharmacological model of acute
stress, increased preference for a risky large-reward option over a safe
small-reward one in male rats (n=20 and n=28 across two experiments),
an effect attenuated by blocking prefrontal dopamine D1 receptors. That
is a change in risk preference, not in effort-cost allocation, and not
an exit from immobility. The rodent forced swim test, long read as
modelling behavioral despair reversible by antidepressants, has been
substantially reinterpreted as measuring a stress-coping strategy rather
than a depression-like state — which cuts against, rather than for, any
reading in which stress restores activation, since on that reinterpretation
the immobility is itself the acute-stress response.

So the transition this corpus is trying to characterize has no
neurochemical account here that a primary source supports. Stating that
plainly is the finding. It also names the experiment the field has not
run: a within-subject design that induces or identifies a low-activation
state, applies an arousal manipulation, and measures effort allocation
before and after.
