## 0. Why this note exists

The program's working claims are: exploratory and play behavior require a "relaxed field" and are the first casualties of threat; "holding" — secure social relation — has a physiology that measurably lowers the cost of managing threat; and periods of heightened plasticity are valence-neutral, resettling well or badly depending on the state they occur in. Oxytocin was the program's first candidate for holding's biochemical carrier. It was retired, not because oxytocin does nothing, but because the intranasal-oxytocin literature that made it legible as a *mechanism* — trust games, single-dose social-cognition effects — turned out to rest on small, underpowered, rarely preregistered studies with unresolved pharmacokinetics (oxytocin's ability to reach brain parenchyma at behaviorally relevant concentrations after intranasal dosing remains contested), and headline effects have not reliably survived high-powered replication attempts (Walum et al. 2016; Leng & Ludwig 2016, "Intranasal Oxytocin: Myths and Delusions"; Mierop et al. 2020 systematic review of interactive effects). This is not a claim that oxytocin is inert — it is a claim that oxytocin, taken as a stand-alone explanatory unit for holding, does not bear the weight the program was putting on it.

This note argues the endocannabinoid (eCB) system is a stronger candidate, not as a wholesale replacement for oxytocin but as a way of relocating the mechanism: oxytocin may be an upstream trigger, and the eCB system — anandamide (AEA), 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG), CB1 and CB2 receptors, and the synthesis/degradation enzymes (NAPE-PLD, DAGL, FAAH, MAGL) — the better-characterized effector arm that actually does the holding: it carries social reward, it gates rough-and-tumble play, it marks safe exertion, it is depleted by isolation and defeat, and — the section this note leans on hardest — it sits on both the synaptic and the glial side of the neuro-immune boundary that decides how a plastic window resettles. A companion note already covers the eCB system's role in fear extinction (Marsicano et al. 2002 CB1-knockout extinction deficits; the FAAH C385A variant; Hill/Patel's tonic-AEA/HPA-gating work; Marcus et al. 2019/2020 on stress-induced 2-AG collapse and amygdalo-cortical strengthening) and is not repeated here. This note is about the eCB system as the chemistry of social reward, play, and immune modulation — the "positive" and homeostatic arm of the same signaling system, not its fear-off switch.

## 1. Social reward and oxytocin's downstream arm

Wei, Allen, Piomelli, and colleagues (PNAS 2015, "Endocannabinoid signaling mediates oxytocin-driven social reward") found in mice that social contact — but not isolation — mobilizes anandamide in the nucleus accumbens (NAc), and that AEA/CB1 signaling there is both necessary and sufficient for the rewarding properties of social interaction, measured by socially conditioned place preference. The causal chain they establish is: oxytocin released from paraventricular-nucleus neurons acts on oxytocin receptors (Gq-coupled OxtR) in NAc; receptor blockade abolishes AEA mobilization, while chemogenetic activation of PVN oxytocin neurons reproduces it. The receptor-to-lipid step runs through the canonical Gq → phospholipase-C-linked cascade that produces diacylglycerol, substrate for DAG lipase (DAGL) and thus 2-AG, alongside NAPE-PLD-dependent AEA synthesis — both eCB synthetic enzymes are expressed, along with CB1 and FAAH, on medium spiny neurons and fast-spiking interneurons in NAc (mapped in detail in non-human-primate NAc by Zhou et al., localizing CB1R/NAPE-PLD/FAAH). The resulting AEA acts retrogradely at presynaptic CB1 receptors, suppressing an upstream (likely glutamatergic) input and reshaping the gain of the accumbal circuit during social contact.

Two things follow from taking this seriously. First, it reframes oxytocin's causal status: it is upstream trigger, not final common pathway. The "holding" feeling — if it is anything at the level of synapses — may be constituted by what CB1 signaling does to NAc microcircuitry, with oxytocin release being one (not necessarily the only) way of turning that signaling on. Second, this is a place where oxytocin's fragile literature and the eCB literature can be decoupled without losing the finding: even if intranasal-oxytocin behavioral effects in humans are unreliable, the mouse circuit finding that endogenous, centrally-released oxytocin drives AEA/CB1-dependent reward is a separate and more mechanistically anchored claim, built on genetic and pharmacological manipulation of CB1 rather than on exogenous peptide dosing in humans.

Status and caveats. The 2015 PNAS finding has been extended by the same group — most notably to a translational claim that boosting AEA signaling (via FAAH inhibition or MAGL blockade) corrects social-approach deficits in a mouse model relevant to autism (Wei, Dinh, Lee, Li, Anguren, Moreno-Sanz, Gall & Piomelli, Cannabis and Cannabinoid Research 2016), and folded into a broader review (Wei, Allsop, Tye & Piomelli, "Endocannabinoid Signaling in the Control of Social Behavior," Trends in Neurosciences 2017) arguing eCB signaling sits at multiple nodes of social-circuit regulation, not just NAc reward. What this note did not find in the 2015–2026 window is an independent, non-Piomelli-lab replication or disconfirmation of the specific oxytocin→AEA→CB1→social-reward chain in NAc. That is a real gap for a program trying to build on this: the finding is well-powered and mechanistically deep (multiple converging manipulations within one paper — pharmacological CB1 blockade, CB1 knockout, oxytocin receptor blockade, chemogenetic oxytocin activation) but it has not yet been stress-tested by outside labs the way, say, Marsicano 2002 has been. Treat it as a strong single-lab mechanistic case, not yet a field-consensus fact in the way CB1's role in fear extinction now is.

## 2. Social play: anandamide as the chemistry of rough-and-tumble

Trezza, Vanderschuren, and colleagues have built the most granular eCB account of any single social behavior using juvenile/adolescent rat social play (pinning, pouncing, wrestling — the classic "rough-and-tumble" ethogram). Social play itself raises AEA levels in amygdala and NAc (not in prefrontal cortex or hippocampus) in 4–5-week-old male Wistar rats (Marco et al./Trezza et al., J. Neurosci. 2012, "Endocannabinoids in Amygdala and Nucleus Accumbens Mediate Social Play Reward in Adolescent Rats"). Systemic URB597 (a FAAH inhibitor that raises AEA by blocking its hydrolysis) increases play and amplifies the amygdalar AEA rise; site-specific infusion into basolateral amygdala reproduces the play-enhancing effect, and CB1 blockade within BLA — but not within NAc — abolishes it, indicating a regionally dissociable, amygdala-dependent hedonic/CB1 component versus other, non-CB1-mediated contributions from NAc. Systemic or intracranial CB1 antagonism (SR141716A/rimonabant) reliably *decreases* play, in both directions bracketing a dose-dependent, bidirectional CB1 involvement rather than a simple "more AEA, more play" linearity.

A later dissociation (Manduca, Servadio, Damsteegt, Campolongo, Vanderschuren & Trezza, 2016, "Dissociating the role of endocannabinoids in the pleasurable and motivational properties of social play") separates hedonic ("liking") from motivational ("wanting") components: eCB and opioid manipulations affect these somewhat independently, and dopaminergic transmission in NAc is required for both opioid and eCB modulation of play to manifest behaviorally (Manduca et al., Neuropsychopharmacology, "Dopaminergic Neurotransmission in the Nucleus Accumbens Modulates Social Play Behavior in Rats," 2016; and "Interacting Cannabinoid and Opioid Receptors in the Nucleus Accumbens Core Control Adolescent Social Play"). The picture that emerges is a three-system interaction — opioid, eCB, dopaminergic — converging on NAc, with the eCB arm doing much of the region-specific tuning (amygdala for affect, NAc for the dopamine-dependent expression of that affect as behavior).

Developmental timing matters and cuts against treating this as a generic reward system: CB1 receptor density in rodent prefrontal/limbic structures peaks during the juvenile/adolescent period and declines toward adulthood, and social play itself is a developmentally windowed behavior (peaking pre-puberty, declining after). Disrupting eCB signaling specifically in early adolescence — genetically or pharmacologically — impairs later sociability (Zamberletti/Rubino-adjacent work; "Disrupting the endocannabinoid system in early adolescence negatively impacts sociability," Prog. Neuropsychopharmacol. Biol. Psychiatry) and chronic adolescent THC exposure decreases social play behavior in a manner separable from adult exposure (F1000Research 2021). This is consistent with a model in which the eCB system is not merely a modulator recruited during an already-existing play behavior but part of what keeps the developmental *window* for play open and properly tuned — jamming it early has consequences beyond the acute play episode.

## 3. Exercise and "runner's high": eCB as exertion-is-safe signaling

Fuss et al. (PNAS 2015, "A runner's high depends on cannabinoid receptors in mice") is the cleanest dissociation available between the eCB and opioid accounts of exercise-induced state change. Voluntary running in mice raises both plasma anandamide and β-endorphin. Using combined pharmacological and genetic (CB1-knockout) manipulation, the authors show that post-run anxiolysis requires intact CB1 receptors specifically on forebrain GABAergic neurons, and post-run analgesia requires peripheral CB1 and CB2 receptor activation — while opioid receptor blockade (naloxone) does neither. Sedation was unaffected by blocking either system, and euphoria is definitionally unmeasurable in mice, so "runner's high" in the full human sense was not directly tested — but the two components that were operationalized (anxiolysis, analgesia) were eCB-dependent and opioid-independent.

Human data corroborate the AEA-rise-with-exercise piece but are noisier on everything downstream of it. A registered systematic review/meta-analysis protocol (2022) and subsequent primary studies confirm that acute exercise reliably raises circulating AEA, with much more variable 2-AG responses across studies (Siebers et al. and related outdoor-running studies through 2024). Complementing Fuss et al., a human study using naltrexone (opioid blockade) found exercise-induced euphoria and anxiolysis persist despite opioid blockade (Sciencedirect 2021, "Exercise-induced euphoria and anxiolysis do not depend on endogenous opioids in humans"), consistent with — though not proof of — an eCB-led account in humans too. The limit to be honest about: peripheral (plasma) AEA elevation is not the same claim as central AEA/CB1 action, and human causal manipulation of CB1 during exercise (the human analog of Fuss et al.'s knockout/antagonist design) is understandably not available. The strongest claim the current literature supports is "eCB tone tracks and appears causally necessary (in mice) for the physiological signature of safe, self-paced exertion" — not yet "eCB tone is the full explanation of human runner's high."

For the program's purposes, this section matters less as an exercise-physiology fact and more as a second, independent behavioral domain (alongside social reward and social play) in which the eCB system marks a state characterized by *voluntary, self-terminable, non-threatening exertion* with reduced anxiety and pain — i.e., a physiological signature with the same shape as "relaxed field," produced without any social partner at all. That convergence is evidence the eCB system's role is not narrowly social but general to states of low-perceived-threat engagement, of which social holding is one instance and safe exercise is another.

## 4. Isolation and defeat as eCB lesions

If the eCB system carries holding, chronic removal of holding should show up as eCB lesions, not just downstream behavioral deficits. The isolation-rearing and social-defeat literatures broadly support this, though with more heterogeneity across paradigms, ages, and brain regions than the tidy single-direction story would like.

Straight social isolation rearing in rats produces a *decrease* in CB1 receptor expression in amygdala, alongside changes in FAAH and D2 receptor expression (early foundational work summarized in "Effect of social isolation on CB1 and D2 receptor and fatty acid amide hydrolase expression in rats," and companion gene-expression work, "The effect of social isolation on rat brain expression of genes associated with endocannabinoid signaling"). By contrast, a different social-adversity paradigm — adolescent peer rejection rather than full isolation — produces the *opposite* direction in amygdala: increased CB1 receptor protein, increased AEA, and decreased FAAH (Sciolino/Bortolato-adjacent work reported in "Adverse Social Experiences in Adolescent Rats Result in Enduring Effects on Social Competence, Pain Sensitivity and Endocannabinoid Signaling," 2016). Social isolation combined with handling manipulations in adulthood likewise shows paradigm-dependent shifts in eCB tone and behavioral reactivity to context (Sciolino et al., Neuroscience 2010).

The honest read: different flavors of social deprivation (total isolation vs. inadequate/hostile conspecifics vs. adult vs. adolescent onset) do not converge on one direction of CB1 change, but they converge on the claim that eCB tone is *not stable* under chronic social adversity — it moves, and it moves in ways that track the specific social lesion rather than social contact per se. This matters for the program's "relaxed field" claim: it is not that isolation simply silences eCB signaling (a clean "holding chemistry off" story); different adversities appear to dysregulate the CB1/AEA system in region- and direction-specific ways, some of which (elevated amygdalar AEA + reduced FAAH in peer-rejected animals) look more like a stress-adaptation than a simple deficit. A CB1-knockout mouse subjected to chronic social defeat stress shows markedly increased susceptibility — including elevated mortality in an initially standard protocol, requiring the experimenters to use a milder defeat protocol just to get CB1-KO animals through it — with dysregulated glucocorticoid responses and altered microglial activation-marker expression and morphology that tracked behavioral severity (Bura, Guegan et al./Häring-adjacent, Translational Psychiatry 2021, "Cannabinoid receptor 1 signalling modulates stress susceptibility and microglial responses to chronic social defeat stress"). This paper is the single best bridge in the literature between the isolation/defeat-as-eCB-lesion story and the CB1/CB2-neuroimmune story taken up next: without CB1, social defeat becomes not just behaviorally worse but physically dangerous, and the tissue-level correlate of that danger is a microglial phenotype shift.

## 5. CB2 and the immune arm: both sides of the neuro-immune bridge

This is the section most load-bearing for the program's theory, and it deserves to be walked through carefully because the story is genuinely elegant and only partially proven in vivo/in human tissue.

**Anatomy of the split.** CB1 is the abundant, classical, largely-neuronal cannabinoid receptor: presynaptic, Gi/o-coupled, retrograde-messenger receptor that suppresses neurotransmitter release and mediates most of the behaviors covered in Sections 1–3. CB2 has a nearly complementary distribution: comparatively sparse and low-abundance in healthy CNS neurons, but constitutively and often highly expressed on peripheral immune cells — B lymphocytes express the highest levels among leukocyte subtypes, followed by NK cells, macrophages, and T cells in that rank order (Frontiers in Pharmacology 2022, "Expression and Functions of the CB2 Receptor in Human Leukocytes"; classic B-cell-differentiation work by Carayon et al., Blood 1998) — and on microglia, the CNS's resident immune cell.

**CB2 upregulation on activated microglia.** The key fact that makes CB2 theoretically interesting rather than just another peripheral immune receptor: resting, surveillant microglia express CB2 at low levels, but CB2 is markedly induced during microglial activation — particularly in the "primed," proliferating, and migrating states rather than in either fully quiescent or fully/terminally activated microglia (this graded, state-dependent induction is reviewed in "CB2 Receptor in Microglia: The Guardian of Self-Control," and "The Dynamic Role of Microglia and the Endocannabinoid System in Neuroinflammation," 2022). This gives CB2 the profile of an inducible brake: largely absent until needed, appearing specifically on the immune cells that are in the process of responding to a challenge, at the point in that response where its activation still has leverage over the outcome.

**What CB2 agonism does to microglial phenotype.** In vitro and in rodent models, CB2 agonism on microglia is broadly anti-inflammatory: it suppresses microglial activation and lowers production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) — demonstrated across multiple agonists (endogenous AEA acting via CB2 to blunt LPS-induced neuroinflammation in primary microglial cultures; synthetic agonists WIN 55,212-2, JWH-015, JWH-133, GW405833, and HU-308 all showing convergent anti-inflammatory and, in several stress models, antidepressant-like/anxiolytic-like effects). Mechanistically this runs partly through ERK1/2 and through inhibition of microglial aerobic glycolysis, and functionally through inhibition of the phagocytic/engulfment program in some contexts. CB2-knockout mice, conversely, show heightened vulnerability to stress and altered microglial reactivity, and CB2 gene–environment interaction has been proposed as a vulnerability factor for stress-linked psychiatric disorders (Ishiguro, Onaivi et al., "Cannabinoid CB2 Receptor Gene and Environmental Interaction in the Development of Psychiatric Disorders").

**The theoretical payoff — eCB on both sides of the bridge.** Put Sections 1–4 and this section together and the eCB system is positioned uniquely: CB1 gates the *synaptic* side of plasticity (retrograde suppression of glutamate/GABA release, shaping which connections strengthen or weaken during a plastic window — exactly the mechanism the companion fear-extinction note develops for threat learning), while CB2 gates the *glial* side (whether microglia stay surveillant, become primed/reactive, or resolve back to quiescence, and how much pro-inflammatory signal they emit while doing so). Both receptors are driven by the same upstream ligands (AEA and 2-AG), synthesized on demand by the same enzymatic machinery (NAPE-PLD, DAGL), and both are sensitive to the same upstream social/environmental inputs described in Sections 1–4 (oxytocin release, play, safe exertion, isolation, defeat). That is what licenses the claim that the eCB system is not just *a* correlate of holding but a plausible *single chemical system spanning the neuron-glia interface*, positioned to determine, in one coordinated signal, both how a synapse changes and how the surrounding immune tissue respond to that change. Microglia are now well established (independent of cannabinoids) as active pruners of synapses via the complement cascade (C1q/C3 tagging synapses for CR3-mediated microglial engulfment — Stevens et al.-lineage work, reviewed in "The Role of Complement in Synaptic Pruning and Neurodegeneration," 2021) — the mechanistic partner CB2-mediated microglial quieting would need to act on if it is to gate pruning outcomes.

**What is established versus what is preclinical extrapolation — stated plainly.** Well-established: CB2 expression on immune cells and its state-dependent induction on activated microglia; CB2 agonism's broad anti-inflammatory/cytokine-suppressive effect in vitro and in rodent neuroinflammation models; CB1's role in synaptic plasticity generally and CB1-knockout's specific link to exaggerated microglial reactivity under social defeat (Section 4). Preclinical-only and NOT yet established: that CB2-mediated microglial modulation causally determines whether a social-stress-induced plastic window resolves into adaptive versus maladaptive synaptic pruning; that this operates as a coordinated dual-receptor "bridge" system in vivo during social — as opposed to purely inflammatory/injury — challenges; any human causal (as opposed to correlational or receptor-mapping) evidence for the CB2-microglia-pruning link at all. This is the theoretically richest section of the note and also the one furthest from direct proof — the program should treat the "CB1 synapse / CB2 glia, one system, two gates" framing as a strong, testable hypothesis (formalized in Hypothesis 4 below), not as an established mechanism.

## 6. Developmental/critical-period eCB signaling

The eCB system is not just a modulator recruited by adult social/threat circuits — it is constitutively involved in building those circuits. eCB signaling (largely CB1-mediated, but also involving CB2 in specific developmental niches) participates in neuronal migration, axon guidance, and synaptic circuit refinement from embryogenesis through adolescence (reviewed in "Cannabis, Endocannabinoids and Brain Development: From Embryogenesis to Adolescence," 2024). CB1 receptor density in cortical and limbic regions rises through childhood/juvenile life and peaks in adolescence before pruning back toward the adult pattern — placing peak CB1 expression directly inside the developmental window during which social play (Section 2) is also at its behavioral peak, which is unlikely to be coincidental given the play-dependence on CB1 signaling documented there.

A separate, genuinely striking finding ties CB1 and CB2 together developmentally rather than as parallel systems: both CB1 and CB2 receptor activation are required for normal masculinization of the developing medial amygdala and for juvenile social play behavior in a sexually dimorphic developmental model (PMC5272923, "Activation of Both CB1 and CB2 Endocannabinoid Receptors Is Critical for Masculinization of the Developing Medial Amygdala and Juvenile Social Play Behavior") — an early, direct empirical instance of CB1 and CB2 acting jointly on a single social-circuit outcome, which is exactly the kind of dual-receptor coordination Section 5's "bridge" argument needs more of.

The consequence side is the well-replicated cannabis-and-adolescence literature: because the endogenous system is actively used as a *tuning* signal during this window (not merely as an always-on suppressor), exogenous cannabinoid exposure during adolescence does not simply add extra suppression — it desynchronizes the tuning process itself. Chronic adolescent THC transiently disrupts the endogenous control of glutamate/GABA release, and produces long-lasting changes to prefrontal cortex structure, gene expression, and (in some designs) reelin-pathway synaptic phenotypes that are not seen with equivalent adult exposure (reviewed across "Adolescent brain maturation, the endogenous cannabinoid system and the neurobiology of cannabis-induced schizophrenia," "Long-term consequences of adolescent cannabinoid exposure in adult psychopathology," and a 2026 systematic review, "How adolescent cannabis use reshapes the developing brain"). This is the clearest existing empirical anchor, anywhere in the eCB literature, for the program's abstract claim that plastic windows are valence-neutral and resettle according to the state they occur in: the *same receptor system*, activated at the *same developmental stage*, produces normal sociosexual circuit maturation when driven by endogenous, socially-triggered ligand release (Sections 1–2, this section) and produces durable circuit disruption when driven by exogenous, socially-decoupled pharmacological activation (this paragraph). The plasticity itself is not the variable; what's driving the receptor, and in what behavioral/social context, is.

## Hypotheses

1. **eCB tone mediates the play-requires-safety relationship (strong form).**
   (i) *Prediction:* social play's dependence on a "relaxed field" is not merely correlated with eCB signaling but is *causally routed through* CB1 — i.e., systemic or BLA-targeted CB1 blockade should not just reduce play under safe conditions, it should *abolish the difference* between play rates under safe versus threat-primed conditions, because the mechanism that lets safety license play is the same mechanism being blocked.
   (ii) *Discriminating test:* in juvenile rats, cross a safety manipulation (familiar arena + familiar partner + no prior stressor vs. novel arena + unfamiliar partner + acute mild stressor) with a CB1 manipulation (vehicle vs. systemic rimonabant vs. BLA-infused rimonabant) in a full factorial design, scoring pins/pounces per unit time. The strong-form prediction is a significant safety × drug interaction where CB1 blockade collapses the safe-vs-threat play-rate gap toward zero (both conditions equally low), not merely a main effect of drug that lowers play in both conditions proportionally.
   (iii) *Kill condition:* if CB1 blockade reduces play by a similar proportion in both safe and threat-primed conditions (main effect only, no interaction), the strong form is falsified — CB1 signaling would be a general facilitator of play output, not the specific carrier of safety's *license* for play, and the program should describe eCB tone as necessary-but-generic rather than as the safety-gating mechanism.

2. **Social buffering of threat physiology is CB1-dependent.**
   (i) *Prediction:* the well-documented phenomenon of social buffering (presence of a conspecific/partner attenuating HPA-axis and autonomic responses to an acute stressor) requires intact CB1 signaling — CB1 antagonism or CB1 knockout should selectively abolish the buffering effect of a partner's presence while leaving the baseline (no-partner) stress response intact or minimally changed.
   (ii) *Discriminating test:* expose animals to an acute stressor (e.g., restraint, predator odor) alone versus with a familiar cage-mate present, under vehicle versus CB1-antagonist pretreatment, measuring corticosterone and heart-rate/HRV. Prediction: partner-present/vehicle shows blunted corticosterone versus alone/vehicle (buffering); partner-present/CB1-antagonist should look like alone/vehicle (buffering erased) while alone/CB1-antagonist should not be dramatically worse than alone/vehicle (ruling out a generic anxiogenic confound of the antagonist).
   (iii) *Kill condition:* if CB1 blockade raises stress hormones equally in both partner-present and alone conditions (i.e., it has a generic anxiogenic/stress-sensitizing effect rather than a buffering-specific one — plausible given CB1-KO's known baseline stress hypersensitivity, Section 4), the hypothesis is falsified as stated; social buffering would need a different or additional carrier (oxytocin acting through a non-eCB route, vagal/parasympathetic mechanisms, etc.), and CB1's role would be reinterpreted as general stress-buffering rather than specifically social-buffering.

3. **Runner's-high-type eCB anxiolysis and social-reward eCB signaling are the same systemic "safety" signal, not two coincidentally similar ones.**
   (i) *Prediction:* if eCB tone is a general state-of-safety signal rather than a social-specific one, pharmacologically or behaviorally elevating peripheral/central AEA via a non-social route (voluntary exercise) should produce measurable cross-over facilitation of a social-reward readout (e.g., conditioned place preference for a social partner, or social play rate) tested shortly afterward, and this cross-over should be CB1-dependent.
   (ii) *Discriminating test:* give rats a bout of voluntary wheel-running (vs. equivalent-duration forced non-exertive control, e.g., passive treadmill restraint) prior to a social-play or social-CPP test, under vehicle vs. CB1 antagonist, measuring AEA in NAc/amygdala alongside behavior. Prediction: exercise raises AEA and increases subsequent social reward/play measures relative to control, and CB1 blockade abolishes the exercise-conferred boost specifically (not just play/CPP in general).
   (iii) *Kill condition:* if exercise raises AEA (replicating Fuss et al.) but produces no measurable change in subsequent social-reward/play behavior, or if any boost persists under CB1 blockade, the "single general safety signal" framing is falsified — the social and exertion-linked eCB effects would need to be treated as parallel, region- or circuit-specific uses of the same molecule rather than one unified state variable, which is the more conservative and currently better-supported reading of the existing literature.

4. **CB2-mediated microglial modulation gates whether social stress produces synaptic pruning (the neuro-immune bridge hypothesis).**
   (i) *Prediction:* during and after chronic social defeat or isolation, the degree of complement-tagged (C1q/C3) microglial synaptic engulfment in limbic circuits (PFC, hippocampus, amygdala) is inversely modulated by CB2 tone — CB2 agonism should suppress stress-induced pruning/engulfment markers, and CB2 antagonism or knockout should exaggerate them, independent of CB1 status.
   (ii) *Discriminating test:* subject mice to chronic social defeat with concurrent CB2 agonist (e.g., HU-308/JWH-133), CB2 antagonist (AM630), or vehicle; quantify microglial C1q/C3 colocalization with synaptic markers (e.g., PSD-95, synaptophysin) via imaging/engulfment assays in PFC and hippocampus, alongside microglial morphology (process retraction, soma size) and behavioral outcome (social avoidance, anhedonia). Prediction: CB2 agonism reduces engulfment markers and improves behavioral resilience relative to vehicle; CB2 antagonism/knockout increases both engulfment and susceptibility, and this effect should be dissociable from CB1 manipulation (i.e., not simply reproducing the CB1-KO phenotype from Bura et al. 2021).
   (iii) *Kill condition:* if CB2 manipulation has no detectable effect on complement-tagged pruning markers under social defeat (even though it changes cytokine levels or microglial morphology, per the well-established anti-inflammatory literature), the specific "gates pruning" claim is falsified — CB2 would remain a validated anti-inflammatory lever without being the mechanism that determines synaptic outcome, and the "both sides of the bridge" framing in Section 5 would need to be scaled back to "CB1 and CB2 both respond to social stress" rather than "CB1 and CB2 jointly determine its structural consequences."

5. **Adolescent-onset CB1/CB2 co-activation is necessary for social-circuit maturation, and its developmental timing (not just its presence) determines whether the same signal builds or disrupts circuitry.**
   (i) *Prediction:* the same magnitude and duration of combined CB1/CB2 agonism will produce normal (or enhancing) effects on juvenile social-play circuitry (e.g., medial amygdala masculinization/social-play readouts, per PMC5272923) when delivered endogenously/pulsatile during the natural adolescent CB1-expression peak, but will produce disruptive, reelin-pathway-type structural deficits when delivered exogenously/tonically at the same developmental stage — i.e., the deficit is a property of exogenous, non-socially-gated activation, not of receptor activation per se.
   (ii) *Discriminating test:* compare adolescent rats given (a) intermittent, socially-triggered eCB elevation via normal social play access, (b) matched-dose exogenous CB1/CB2 agonist on a fixed schedule uncoupled from social context, and (c) no manipulation (isolation-reared, no play), assessing adult medial amygdala structure, adult social competence, and PFC synaptic/reelin markers. Prediction: (a) shows normal/optimal maturation, (b) shows THC-like disruption despite comparable receptor occupancy/AEA-equivalent exposure, (c) shows the isolation-lesion pattern from Section 4.
   (iii) *Kill condition:* if exogenous, socially-uncoupled agonist exposure (b) produces outcomes statistically indistinguishable from natural social-play exposure (a), the "context/timing, not just presence of signal" claim is falsified — it would indicate that eCB receptor activation is sufficient for normal maturation regardless of whether it is socially gated, undermining the valence-neutral-plasticity argument this hypothesis was built to support for this specific circuit.
