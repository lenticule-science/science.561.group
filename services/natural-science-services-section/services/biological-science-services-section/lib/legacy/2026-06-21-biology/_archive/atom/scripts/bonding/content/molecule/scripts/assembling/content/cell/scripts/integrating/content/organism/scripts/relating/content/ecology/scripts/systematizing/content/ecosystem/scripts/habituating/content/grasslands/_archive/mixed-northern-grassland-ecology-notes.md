Acknowledging that National Parks are a vector of land theft, Indigenous erasure and recreational extraction (as I heard of the concept from Benally, interview with subMedia), we can take back the knowledge they are holding onto: https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/prairie-ecology-badl.htm

Of particular use is the species list cataloging our nonhuman relatives present in Badland National Park, adjacent to and NW of where we will be stewarding: https://irma.nps.gov/NPSpecies/Reports/SpeciesList/Species%20Full%20List/BADL/5,2,3,1,4,11/true
Yarrow is native and uncommon, Echinacea augustifolia is both native and rare (opportunity for repopulation of this medicine), yucca glauca is native and uncommon, many native artemesia, many native sunflowers (food), several native amaranth (starch), Erigeron pumilus and strigosus (common names fleabane) are uncommon and native (do they treat or prevent fleas?), several native goldenrods, common salsify (tragopogon dubius) is non-native and common and good food both root and leaves, [[Cryptantha celosioides]] (miner's candle or buttecandle) is native and uncommon wildflower, common name is interesting (hints to resinous properties unless simply refering to appearance of infloresence), one native mustard, western tansy mustard ([[Descurainia pinnata]]) that is uncommon, several non-native mustards that are common (so could be harvested as food and grown in gardens without intensive labor), several native prickly pears, many native plants with saltbush as a common name (could be a source of salt or good for gray water processing), yellow dock ([[Rumex crispus]]) is common and non-native, [[Mentha arvensis]] is the native mint, [[Monarda fistulosa]] is the beebalm, native milkweeds and dogbane (fibers/cordage) -- ran out of capacity/ concentration to continue taking notes on this, stopped in the mint family, could revisit even earlier in the list
