# Analyze Mycorrhizal Network

Mycorrhizal networks are the fungal infrastructure that links plants belowground. Analysis means characterizing the network's type, who its hubs are, what flows along it, and what perturbs it.

## When to use

You are examining plant-fungal connectivity in a forest or plant community and want to predict network behavior — flows, resilience, response to management interventions.

## How it works

1. **Identify the mycorrhizal type.** Ectomycorrhizal or arbuscular, based on the dominant plant species. Different types form structurally different networks.
2. **Describe network structure.** Which trees are likely hub nodes (older, larger, mycorrhizally well-connected)? Which are peripheral?
3. **Identify resources exchanged.** Carbon, phosphorus, nitrogen, water, signaling molecules — and which flow in which direction across the network.
4. **Assess directionality.** Which organisms are likely net providers? Which are net receivers? Hub trees often subsidize seedlings and stressed neighbors.
5. **Evaluate resilience.** What happens if hub trees are removed (logging, disease)? Networks with few hubs degrade fast; well-distributed networks degrade more gracefully.
6. **Connect to management.** How do forestry practices (clear-cutting, selective harvest, replanting) affect network integrity? How fast do networks rebuild?
