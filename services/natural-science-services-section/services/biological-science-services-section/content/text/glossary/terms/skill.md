# Identify Fungal Structures

Fungal identification at the structural level — what's visible, what substrate, what role the fungus plays in its environment.

## When to use

You have a description or image of a fungal organism and need to characterize it: what structure is visible, what it's growing on, and what ecological role it likely has.

## How it works

1. Determine whether the visible structure is a **fruiting body** or **exposed mycelium**.
2. If a fruiting body, identify the type:
   - Gilled mushroom, bracket, puffball, cup, coral, jelly, etc.
   - Note cap, stem, and the spore-bearing surface (gills, pores, teeth, smooth, etc.).
3. Identify the likely **substrate**: soil, wood (and what kind), dung, living host.
4. Infer the **ecological role**:
   - Saprotroph (on dead matter).
   - Mycorrhizal (near living plant roots).
   - Pathogen (on living tissue).
5. Note features relevant to identification: color, bruising reaction, spore print color, habitat, season.

## Related skills

- `assess-fungal-ecological-role` — given the role identified here, characterize the fungus's place in its ecosystem.
- `analyze-mycorrhizal-network` — for mycorrhizal fungi specifically, characterize the network they participate in.
