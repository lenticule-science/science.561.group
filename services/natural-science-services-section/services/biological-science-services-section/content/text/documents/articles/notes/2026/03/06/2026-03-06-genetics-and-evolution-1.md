## Assumed audience

General adult who has completed [Cells and Metabolism](../../../cells-and-metabolism.md).

## DNA structure

DNA is a double helix of nucleotide bases (A, T, G, C) ([DNA](../../../../../../../../services/genetics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/dna.md)). The sequence of bases encodes genetic information. DNA replication copies this information before cell division.

## Genes and proteins

A [gene](../../../../../../../../services/genetics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/gene.md) is a segment of DNA that encodes a functional product — usually a protein. The flow of information goes: DNA → RNA → protein (the "central dogma"). Proteins carry out most of the work in cells: as enzymes, structural components, signals, transporters.

## Heredity

When organisms reproduce, they pass copies of their DNA to their offspring. Sexual reproduction combines DNA from two parents, producing genetically unique offspring. This is the basis of heredity — why offspring resemble but are not identical to their parents.

## Mutation and variation

Errors in DNA replication produce mutations — changes to the DNA sequence. Most mutations are neutral or harmful, but some produce beneficial new traits. Mutation is the ultimate source of all genetic variation.

## Natural selection

Organisms with traits better suited to their environment tend to survive and reproduce more successfully ([natural selection](../../../../../../../../services/adaptation-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/natural-selection.md)). Over generations, this differential reproduction changes the genetic composition of populations. Beneficial traits become more common; harmful traits become less common.

## Evolution

The change in the genetic composition of populations over generations ([evolution](../../../../../../../../services/adaptation-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/evolution.md)). Natural selection is the primary mechanism, but genetic drift, gene flow, and mutation also contribute. Evolution produced all the diversity of life from common ancestors over roughly 3.8 billion years.

## Adaptation

A trait that has been shaped by natural selection ([adaptation](../../../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/_archive/atom/scripts/bonding/content/molecule/scripts/assembling/content/cell/scripts/integrating/content/organism/scripts/inheriting/content/genetics/explanations/_archive/theories/adaptation/terms/adaptation.md)). The woodpecker's skull, the cactus's water-storing tissue, the Arctic fox's white winter coat — all adaptations to specific environments.

## Why this matters

Genetics and evolution explain both the unity and the diversity of life — why all organisms share basic molecular machinery, and why they differ in form and function.
