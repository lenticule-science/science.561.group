# 2026 Minnesota DFL Gubernatorial Primary: Ballot Analysis

Written the morning of the primary (2026-08-11), Duluth, MN. This note
records the candidate field, a preference ranking matched against my
documented values, and the reasoning — partly so the method is reusable
for future ballots.

## Context

Incumbent governor Tim Walz ended his re-election campaign in January
2026. U.S. Senator Amy Klobuchar entered the race, won the DFL
convention endorsement (~72% in the straw poll), and is the
overwhelming favorite. Seven tickets appear on the DFL primary ballot.
Note: Minnesota state primaries are single-choice, not ranked-choice
(Duluth rejected RCV by referendum in 2015) — the "ranking" below is a
preference ordering, and only the top of it gets marked.

## Values used for the ranking

Inferred from my own writing across this repository:

- Anti-carceral, anti-surveillance; sympathy for mutual aid and bail
  funds; distrust of "public safety" framing.
- Pro-tenant and housing-precarity-aware; hostility to landlordism.
- Disability justice and healthcare access as first-order lenses;
  covid-cautious.
- Indigenous sovereignty and anti-colonial commitments as central, not
  peripheral.
- Trans-affirming.
- Climate politics judged by material investment (funded infrastructure,
  not symbolic declarations) — per "Municipal Decisions Follow Capital,
  Not Ideology."
- Structural skepticism of centrist pragmatism and performative
  progressivism alike.

## The field, ranked

1. **Kobey J Layne / Paul Ference** — the vote. Layne is a 26-year-old
   trans woman (St. Paul; MPP, University of Minnesota; former
   legislative aide; former Republican until 2022) running on:
   single-payer universal healthcare, a 3% cap on rent increases, a 7%
   income cap on childcare costs, a wealth tax and progressive
   taxation, a $25/hr minimum wage at publicly traded companies,
   reduced criminal penalties for small drug amounts, 2SLGBTQIA+ and
   immigrant protection, and Palestine solidarity. Closest available
   match on tenant protection, healthcare, anti-carceral policy, and
   trans rights. The recent party switch is a caveat, but an outsider
   with a concrete redistributive platform beats a career politician
   with none.

2. **Mohammad Wazwaz / Murad Alshlool** — business owner and community
   leader; platform is vague (healthcare access, small business,
   immigrant protection) but credibly better than the centrist options
   on protecting vulnerable people.

3. **Amy Klobuchar / Ben Schierer** — will win. Former prosecutor
   campaigning on pragmatism, affordability, "public safety,"
   bipartisanship — nearly the archetype my own writing warns against.
   Ranked here on competence: she would sign progressive legislation a
   friendly legislature sends her. Running mate is former Fergus Falls
   mayor Ben Schierer.

4. **Ole "Viking" Savior / Ashley "Skol" Johnson** — perennial novelty
   candidate since the mid-1980s; no platform; harmless.

5. **Bill E. Gates Jr. / Leah Harris** — self-described centrist
   pastor; 78-page platform without actionable specifics; made
   anti-abortion commitments to MCCL. Disqualifying.

6. **Po Vang / Mark Frascone** — surface overlap (disabled, anti-ICE,
   healthcare-focused) but reportedly promotes antivax rhetoric and
   shares right-wing commentary, which cuts directly against
   covid-caution. The overlap is a trap.

7. **Thomas Evenstad / Jason Haarsager** — both have serious
   sex-offense convictions (late 1990s/early 2000s). Unrankable.

## Assessment

Given Klobuchar's near-certain win, the Layne vote functions as a
values vote — signaling the size of the party's left flank rather than
selecting a nominee. Consistent with the "capital, not ideology"
analysis: the primary result was structurally determined the moment
the endorsement consolidated; what remains contestable is the margin.
