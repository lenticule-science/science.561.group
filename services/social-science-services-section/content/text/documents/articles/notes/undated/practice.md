---
title: Practice
description: "A patterned, socially reproducible form of action — not a one-off behavior but a way of doing something that can be recognized, transmitted, and repeated within a social group. The material from which norms, institutions, and culture are built."
---

A practice is a patterned form of action that is socially recognizable and reproducible. Not every action is a practice. A practice requires that the action be one that can be identified as an instance of a type — a way of doing something that is shared or shareable across participants and across time. Practices are what social relations look like when they stabilize into form.

The concept comes from several traditions converging on a common intuition. Bourdieu's *habitus* is the internalized system of dispositions that generates practice: people act in patterned ways because of the structured field they move in and the history they carry in their bodies. Giddens' structuration theory holds that practices are the medium through which social structures are both produced and reproduced. Schatzki's practice theory treats practices as the fundamental unit of social life — prior to individuals and structures, which are both constituted through practices.

What practices produce: repeated practices generate *norms* (standards for what counts as doing it correctly or incorrectly), *roles* (positions defined by distinctive bundles of practices), *institutions* (stable systems of interconnected practices), and *culture* (the system of meaning within which practices are intelligible). These formations are not separate from practice but emergent from it — they exist only insofar as practices continue to be performed.
