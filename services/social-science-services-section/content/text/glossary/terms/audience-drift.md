Audience drift is what happens when an artifact's declared addressee and its real addressee diverge. The artifact says it is for a wide audience but its closures presuppose a narrow one. The artifact says it is for beginners but uses apparatus only experts have been trained to close. The artifact says it is a public-facing document but its frame-vocabulary is intelligible only to insiders.

The audience the artifact declares is a commitment about what closures the reader will be expected to perform. The audience the artifact's gaps actually require is the audience the artifact is, in fact, addressing. When these two come apart, the artifact has produced a kind of communicative double-bind: anyone who reads it will either be the wrong audience for some of its closures (and will fail them) or will be the wrong audience for what the artifact claims to be (and will be misled about what the document is).

## How drift accumulates

Audience drift is rarely a single decision. It accumulates: a writer expands an example for one expert reader, then leaves it in. A revision adds a citation that requires apparatus most readers do not have. A review process patches in disciplinary gaps that the original draft made explicit. By the end, the artifact is two documents bundled together — its declared one and its actual one — and the gap between them is paid by every reader who finds themselves in the wrong one.

## How it is repaired

Repair is a choice between two real options. **Narrow the declared audience.** Acknowledge in the artifact's framing that it is for the audience its closures actually serve, and let the broader audience know that this is not for them yet. **Re-license the closures.** Expand the gaps that exceed the broad audience's competence, transport the apparatus into a register the broader audience can engage with, and produce an artifact that actually serves the audience it claims.

The wrong repair — common in practice — is to leave the declared audience as it stands and add a disclaimer that some readers may find parts of the document challenging. The disclaimer does not pay down the [legibility-debt](legibility-debt.md). It only formalizes the artifact's refusal to address its declared audience.

## Why audience honesty matters

The audience the artifact declares is a commitment to readers about what they will and will not have to do. Honoring the commitment is what makes [audience](../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/audience.md) a load-bearing parameter and not a piece of cosmetic framing. An artifact that honors its audience commitment is one that, on inspection, asks for the closures the declared audience can perform — no more and not noticeably fewer. Audience drift is the systemic erosion of that commitment.
