***Style*** is the characteristic manner of a piece of writing — the sum of its [diction](diction.md), [syntax](syntax.md), [tone](tone.md), rhythm, and formal choices. Style is not decoration added to content; it is inseparable from content. How something is said shapes what it means.

The plain style — short sentences, common words, active [voice](voice.md) — communicates directness and clarity. The ornate style — complex sentences, unusual vocabulary, layered clauses — communicates nuance and deliberation. Neither is inherently better; each serves different purposes and [audiences](../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/audience.md). The mistake is applying one where the other belongs: writing a safety warning in ornate prose, or writing philosophy in primer sentences.

[Joseph Williams](../../general/people/joseph-williams.md) distinguished between style as choice and style as habit [@williams2006]. Writers who have studied style can shift registers deliberately — formal for an academic paper, conversational for a letter, spare for a news report. Writers who haven't studied it tend to write in a single default register, usually the one they've absorbed from reading. The [paramedic method](./paramedic-method.md) and [revision](./revision.md) techniques in this vault's [style guide](./style-guide.md) are tools for making style conscious and deliberate.

[Richard Lanham](../../general/people/richard-lanham.md) identified the [official style](./official-style.md) — nominalized, passive, abstract — as the dominant bad style of institutional writing, arguing that it persists not because it's natural but because it conceals agency and avoids accountability [@lanham2006].

## Related terms

- [voice](voice.md) — the distinctive quality of a writer's style across their work
- [tone](tone.md) — the attitude expressed within a given style
- [diction](diction.md) — word choice, a primary component of style
- [syntax](syntax.md) — sentence structure, the other primary component
- [official style](./official-style.md) — the institutional style that plain language opposes
- [style guide](./style-guide.md) — a document that codifies style decisions for consistency
