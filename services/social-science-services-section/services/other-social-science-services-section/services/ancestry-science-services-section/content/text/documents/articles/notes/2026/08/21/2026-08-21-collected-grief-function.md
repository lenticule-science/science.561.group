# Grief function

The ***grief function*** (Γ) is induced by compassion when convergence fails
and stabilization is no longer possible.

When a system modulates across a collapsing difference and still maintains resonance,
grief emerges.

> Γ(x) := Κ(x, y) ∧ Φ(y) ∧ Μ(y) ∈ S

That is, grief is the stabilization of vanished constraint through its enduring echo.

## Relation to this section's founding note

The founding note ([Ancestors as Settled Sections](../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/21/2026-08-21-note-on-ancestors-as-settled-sections.md))
frames grief as **re-gluing**: the survivors' sheaf must re-settle around a
removed open, overlap by overlap, with the settled section held witnessed
throughout — the bond continues because the section persists. This
collapse-dynamics fragment frames grief instead as **stabilization through
enduring echo**: resonance (Μ(y)) with something whose constraint (Φ(y))
has collapsed, held stable (∈ S) via compassion (Κ(x,y)) even though
ordinary convergence is no longer available.

The two are not obviously the same claim. "Re-gluing" describes a *process*
— repeated small acts of resettlement at each shared overlap. "Enduring
echo" describes a *state* — a stabilized trace of something that can no
longer converge by the system's normal means. Whether the echo is what
re-gluing produces, or whether echo and re-gluing name two different
mechanisms that happen to agree on grief's basic shape (persistence rather
than detachment), is an open question for this section rather than one
this collected fragment answers on its own.
