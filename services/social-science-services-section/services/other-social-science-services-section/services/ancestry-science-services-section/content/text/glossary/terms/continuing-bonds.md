**Continuing bonds** names a shift in grief research away from the earlier
"detachment" model — the assumption that healthy mourning requires
severing emotional ties to the deceased and reinvesting that energy
elsewhere — toward evidence that most bereaved people maintain an ongoing,
adapted relationship with the dead, and that doing so is associated with
better adjustment rather than pathological "stuck" grief. The bond
changes form (internalized presence, ritual remembrance, an internal
sense of what the deceased would say or want) but is not simply ended.

The concept originates in bereavement research (notably Klass, Silverman
& Nickman's 1996 edited volume of the same name) as a corrective to
earlier stage-based and detachment-based grief models; it has since been
extended and qualified — continuing bonds can be adaptive or maladaptive
depending on how they are held, and the research literature is not a
single settled consensus.

This section's [grief as re-gluing](./grief-as-re-gluing.md) term treats
continuing bonds as a prediction of its own account: if grief is the
survivors' sheaf re-settling around a removed open *while the settled
section stays witnessed*, then healthy resolution should look exactly
like continuing bonds — an ongoing, adapted relationship — rather than
like detachment. This term states the general research finding on its
own terms, independent of that framing, so the two can be checked against
each other rather than treated as automatically the same claim.
