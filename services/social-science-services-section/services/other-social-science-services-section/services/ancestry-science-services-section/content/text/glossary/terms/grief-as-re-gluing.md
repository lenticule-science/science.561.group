[Grief](./grief-as-re-gluing.md), in this section's founding sense, is
**re-gluing**: when a section of the community sheaf is removed by death,
the survivors' sheaf must re-settle around the resulting open. Every
overlap the lost section shared with the living — every shared routine,
place, phrase, standing appointment — presents its own small gluing
problem that must be redone without them.

This predicts grief's actual phenomenology in a specific way: grief does
not arrive as one event at the funeral and then recede. It ambushes at
the *small* shared regions, one at a time, whenever daily life crosses an
overlap that used to glue against the lost section and now has to glue
against its absence instead — the reflexive reach for the phone, the
empty chair at a particular table, the recipe that was always made
together. Each of these is a separate small re-gluing, not a subset of
one larger event.

This aligns with the real continuing-bonds literature in grief research:
healthy grief resolution is not detachment from the deceased but an
ongoing bond maintained in a new form. On this section's account, that is
exactly what re-gluing predicts — the survivors' sheaf re-settles *with
the lost section still held witnessed*, not by severing the relation. The
bond continues because the settled section persists; see
[ancestor as settled section](./ancestor-as-settled-section.md). The
founding note's falsifier condition follows directly: continuing-bonds
outcomes should correlate with measurable witness-set maintenance
(naming frequency, story circulation — see
[veneration as witness-set maintenance](./veneration-as-witness-set-maintenance.md))
rather than with detachment metrics. If grief resolution instead tracks
forgetting better than re-witnessed integration, this framing fails.

A structurally adjacent but independently-derived formal account — grief
as the *stabilization of vanished constraint through its enduring echo*
— was found in the relationality corpus and is collected at
[Grief as the stabilization of vanished constraint](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/21/2026-08-21-collected-grief-function.md),
with a discussion of how "re-gluing" (a process) and "enduring echo" (a
state) may or may not be the same claim.
