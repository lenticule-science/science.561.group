An [ancestor](./ancestor-as-settled-section.md), in this section's founding
sense, is a **fully-settled section** of the community sheaf: a person who
has died and so stopped emitting new arrows, but whose witnessed
contributions to the sheaf's structure — including its H¹ — persist
exactly as they were left. Death changes a section's tense, not its
membership.

This is a narrower, formally-loaded claim than the ordinary sense of
"ancestor" as a prior relative. It does not deny that ordinary sense; it
gives it a specific mechanism. The claim is that whatever a person's
charges wove into the community's structure while they were living stays
woven in after they stop living — the sheaf does not automatically retract
around their absence. What changes is whether those charges stay
**witnessed**: read, cited, spoken, kept in active circulation — or decay
toward unread. An ancestor forgotten is not structurally gone; they are
unwitnessed.

This makes an ancestor's ongoing presence a maintenance question rather
than a metaphysical question settled once at death. See
[veneration as witness-set maintenance](./veneration-as-witness-set-maintenance.md)
for the corresponding practice, and
[grief as re-gluing](./grief-as-re-gluing.md) for what the survivors' side
of that persistence looks like.

The full argument — including the phytolith-at-human-scale claim (a
witnessed life mineralizes into the sheaf it helped glue) and the
falsifier condition — is in this section's founding note; see the seed
topic's ["founding note" section](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/21/2026-08-21-seed-topic-ancestry-science.md#the-founding-note-read-this-first)
for the pointer.
