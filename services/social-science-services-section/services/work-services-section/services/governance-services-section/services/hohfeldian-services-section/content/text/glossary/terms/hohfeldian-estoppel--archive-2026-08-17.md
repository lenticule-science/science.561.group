## Formal definition

An **Estoppel** is a quadruple $\mathcal{E} = (Q, R, \rho, \delta)$:

$$\mathcal{E} = (Q : \mathrm{EstoppedParty},\; R : \mathrm{RelyingParty},\; \rho \in H_{t_\rho},\; \delta \in H_{t_\delta})$$

where:
- $Q$ is the **estopped party** — the party whose representation or conduct gave rise to the estoppel; after estoppel is established, $Q$ cannot assert a position inconsistent with $\rho$ in the current forum
- $R$ is the **relying party** — the party who relied on $Q$'s representation $\rho$ and changed their position as a result; $R$ is the equitable beneficiary of the estoppel
- $\rho \in H_{t_\rho}$ is the **representation** — the proposition that $Q$ asserted, represented, or stood by at time $t_\rho$; may be an explicit statement, a course of conduct, or a silence where there was a duty to speak
- $\delta \in H_{t_\delta}$ is the **detriment** — the harm that $R$ would suffer if $Q$ were now permitted to assert a position inconsistent with $\rho$; the detriment must be real and caused by $R$'s reliance on $\rho$

The **estoppel condition**: the forum $J$'s σ-nucleus refuses to settle $Q$'s inconsistent claim $C$ while the estoppel holds. In formal terms: the forum's site topology $J$ does not contain any covering sieve $S \in J(t)$ that would make $C$ locally valid — the local sieve generated by $Q$'s inconsistent claim fails to cover, because $J$ treats the combination of $\rho$ and $C$ as incompatible.

**Four invariants.** $\mathcal{E}$ is a valid estoppel iff:

1. **Representation**: $Q$ made a representation $\rho$ — a statement of fact, a course of conduct, a silence where there was a duty to speak, or a taking of a position in prior proceedings. The representation is a proposition $\rho \in H_{t_\rho}$ that $Q$ effectively placed in the relational context of the interaction with $R$.

2. **Reliance**: $R$ acted on $\rho$ — $R$ changed their position (entered a contract, incurred expenses, abstained from acquiring alternate protection) in reasonable reliance on $\rho$. The reliance must be reasonable: $R$ cannot claim estoppel if $R$ knew or should have known that $\rho$ was incorrect.

3. **Detriment**: $\delta \neq 0$ — if $Q$ is now permitted to contradict $\rho$, $R$ will suffer a detriment that would not have occurred but for $R$'s reliance on $\rho$. Detriment is the causal link between reliance and harm: $R$ relied, and because of that reliance, $R$ would be worse off if $Q$'s contradiction is allowed.

4. **Inequity**: it would be inequitable — inconsistent with the forum's $J$-topology — to permit $Q$ to assert a position inconsistent with $\rho$ given $R$'s reliance and detriment. The inequity condition is the forum-relative judgment: whether $J$ recognizes this combination of representation, reliance, and detriment as sufficient to block $Q$'s claim.

## Estoppel vs. waiver: the key distinction

Estoppel and waiver both result in a party being unable to assert a right, but they are structurally different:

| Dimension | Waiver | Estoppel |
|---|---|---|
| What happens to the right | $C$ is driven to $\bot$ (extinguished) | $C$ remains in $H_{t^*}$; only the path to $H^*_{t^*}$ is blocked |
| Initiator | $Q$ (right-holder) voluntarily relinquishes | Forum blocks $Q$'s assertion on equitable grounds |
| Third-party reliance | Not required | Required |
| Revocability | Generally irrevocable once made | Lapses if $R$'s detriment is remedied |
| Intent | $Q$ must intend to relinquish | $Q$'s intent is irrelevant; focus is on $R$'s reliance |
| Math | Section retraction ($C \to \bot$) | Nucleus blocking ($J$ refuses to cover $C$) |

The formal difference: waiver retracts $C$ from the fiber entirely. Estoppel does not touch $C$'s position in the fiber — $C$ remains in $H_{t^*}$; it is the forum's $J$-topology that refuses to process $C$'s claim as a covering event in this context. In another forum, $C$ might still be settled. Estoppel is forum-relative; waiver is not.

This distinction matters: if an estoppel is later remedied (the detriment is compensated), the estoppel may be lifted and $Q$ can once again assert $C$. A waiver cannot typically be reversed. This asymmetry follows from the formal structure: restoring $J$-coverage of $C$ is possible (the topology can be updated); restoring a retracted section requires a new act of creation.

## The three main forms

### Promissory estoppel

$Q$ makes a promise $\rho$ (a future-directed proposition: "I will do X") that $R$ reasonably relies upon. Even without consideration (making it unenforceable as a contract), $Q$ is estopped from denying the promise. *Restatement (Second) of Contracts* §90 (ALI, 1981):

> "A promise which the promisor should reasonably expect to induce action or forbearance on the part of the promisee or a third person and which does induce such action or forbearance is binding if injustice can be avoided only by enforcement of the promise."

In the formal framework: the promise $\rho$ is placed in the relational context ($\rho \in H_{t_\rho}$ as a forward-directed proposition); $R$'s reliance changes $R$'s section (moves $R$ from one fiber position to another based on $\rho$); the forum's $J$-topology refuses to allow $Q$ to contradict $\rho$ because doing so would cause $\delta$.

### Equitable estoppel (estoppel by representation)

$Q$ makes a representation of present fact $\rho$ (not a promise but a factual statement); $R$ relies; $Q$ is estopped from asserting the true facts. Classic case: $Q$ represents that $X$ belongs to $Q$; $R$ purchases $X$ from $Q$ in reliance; $Q$ is estopped from asserting that $X$ actually belongs to a third party (*Freeman v. Cooke*, 2 Ex. 654, 1848).

In the formal framework: $\rho$ is a proposition about the present state of the fiber ($\rho \in H_{t_\rho}$, not a prospective section); $R$ settles their own fiber position based on $\rho$; the forum blocks $Q$'s assertion of $\neg\rho$ because that would collapse $R$'s settled position.

### Estoppel by conduct (estoppel in pais)

$Q$'s conduct (not explicit statement) constitutes the representation $\rho$. Silence where there was a duty to speak is a classic case: if $Q$ watches $R$ make expenditures on $Q$'s land believing it to be $R$'s own, and $Q$ says nothing, $Q$ may be estopped from asserting ownership. *Pickard v. Sears*, 6 A&E 469 (1837).

In the formal framework: $\rho$ is not a verbal proposition but a section of $Q$'s behavioral fiber — the aggregate of $Q$'s acts and omissions at $t_\rho$ constitutes a proposition that $R$ reasonably reads as settling a certain state. The forum's $J$-topology treats this behavioral section as equivalent to a verbal representation.

### Estoppel by record (res judicata)

A prior judicial determination binds the parties in subsequent proceedings. $Q$ cannot re-litigate an issue already decided against $Q$ in a prior proceeding where $Q$ had a full and fair opportunity to be heard. This is estoppel by the record — the prior judgment $\phi \in H^{*(k)}_{t_\phi}$ blocks re-settlement of the same issue.

In the formal framework: the prior judgment $\phi$ is already in $H^{*(k)}$ (doubly settled at depth $k$ in the prior proceeding). The current forum's $J$-topology treats the prior settled judgment as a sieve that covers the current dispute — any attempt by $Q$ to re-litigate the same issue fails to produce new coverage because the issue is already covered by $\phi$. The prior nucleus-settled proposition acts as a blocking sieve in the current forum.

## Math grounding

Estoppel operates at the level of the **site topology** $J$, not the fiber $H_{t^*}$. The forum's Grothendieck topology $J$ determines which covering families are recognized. An estoppel is a **constraint on $J$** at the specific context of $Q$'s claim:

For the estopped claim $C$: the local sieve $S_Q(C)$ generated by $Q$'s assertion of $C$ does not cover — there is no $J$-covering refinement of $S_Q(C)$ that is in $J(t)$ — because the cultural/equitable topology $J$ treats the combination $\{\rho, \delta, C\}$ (prior representation + detriment + inconsistent claim) as a non-covering triple.

Formally: estoppel adds a condition to $J$. The normal site topology $J$ might allow $Q$'s claim $C$ to cover. The estoppel-modified topology $J_\varepsilon$ excludes $C$-covering when $Q$'s prior representation $\rho$ is in the context:

$$J_\varepsilon(t) = J(t) \setminus \{S \in J(t) \mid S \text{ includes } Q\text{'s } C\text{-assertion and } (\rho, \delta) \text{ are established}\}$$

This is a Lawvere–Tierney topology modification: the estoppel acts as a local nucleus $j_\varepsilon : \Omega \to \Omega$ that closes off the subobject corresponding to $Q$'s inconsistent claim. The modified nucleus $j_\varepsilon$ is exactly what blocks $Q$'s claim from propagating to $H^*_{t^*}$ in this forum.

The contrast with waiver: waiver retracts $C$ from the fiber (uses the $\Delta_{t^*}$-retraction morphism). Estoppel modifies the topology (uses the Lawvere–Tierney nucleus on the subobject classifier $\Omega$). Different morphisms; same practical effect for $Q$ in the current forum.

## Open questions

- Whether estoppel-by-record (res judicata) has the structure of a Grothendieck topology condition: whether the prior judgment $\phi$ generates a covering sieve that explicitly includes all attempts to re-litigate the same issue, making those attempts non-covering by the maximality axiom of the topology (the maximal sieve covers everything, so a sieve that includes the prior judgment covers the current dispute).
- Whether promissory estoppel (the promise is binding without consideration) corresponds to the cultural topology $J$ treating forward-directed propositions (promises) as binding sieve-generators — whether the culture's background topology includes promise-as-covering as a standard sieve, bypassing the contractual consideration requirement.
- Whether the four forms of estoppel (promissory, equitable, conduct, record) correspond to the four sources of the sieve that blocks $Q$: future-directed proposition ($\rho$ as promise), present-fact proposition ($\rho$ as representation), behavioral section ($\rho$ as conduct), and prior settled proposition ($\rho$ as prior judgment) — and whether these four sources exhaust the morphisms that can generate a blocking sieve in $J$.
