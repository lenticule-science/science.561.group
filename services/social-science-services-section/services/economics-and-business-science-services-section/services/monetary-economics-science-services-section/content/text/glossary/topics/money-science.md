Money science studies money as a witnessing technology, not a neutral
medium of exchange. Its founding claim: money is the witness-forgetting
functor applied to obligation. An obligation, natively, is a charged
relation — who owes what to whom, witnessed by the parties and their
community, consumed exactly once when discharged; its logic is linear. A
bearer instrument (cash) is that obligation with the provenance deleted:
it owes anyone, from anyone, for anything. Bearer instruments are
provenance deletion; gift economies, where the witness set stays attached
to the obligation, are the witnessed control group.

**Teleology:** To explain money's power and its hollowing effect as one
mechanism — frictionless circulation purchased by forgetting who said what
to whom — and to test that mechanism against comparative data: witnessed-
exchange systems should outperform bearer systems on relationship-
maintenance metrics and underperform on stranger-transaction throughput.

**Method:** Read monetary history and ethnography (Graeber's debt-first
anthropology; Indigenous gift-economy formalisms; the Union's own
`wit`/`tally` denomination work) as case studies in witnessing and
forgetting, and build out the compatibility between this framing and the
Landauer-entropy-as-currency line of thought already in the estate: if
money IS erasure (witness-deletion), Landauer's principle says erasure has
an irreducible cost, and the two may be the same claim in two
vocabularies.
