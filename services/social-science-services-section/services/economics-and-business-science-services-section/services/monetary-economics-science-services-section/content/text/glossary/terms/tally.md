A tally is the border-reconciled form of the wit: not a unit held at a position but a matched
pair of instruments held by two counterparties — a release receipt on one side and an
admission receipt on the other — that agree on the same case, the same exact resource set,
the same paths, and the same content digests. Transfer settles when the halves match and
holds on every omission or mismatch. The Union already operates this mechanically: the
reconciling-a-resource-repatriation skill is a tally match, and the assay contract's
border invariant (price forms only at a counterparty border) says where tallies, and only
tallies, come into existence.

The relation to the wit is coin and cleared check: wits are produced and spent inside a
settlement's own ledger; a tally is what a wit's crossing of a sovereign border looks like
when it is lawful — two-sided, content-addressed, matched rather than asserted. A single
half-tally is not currency; it is a claim awaiting its counterpart, and a mismatched pair is
a dispute for arbitration, not a transfer.

The name is the historical instrument, not an analogy to it: the split hazelwood tally,
grain matched across both halves at settlement so neither party could alter the record
alone, circulated as money in the English Exchequer for six centuries. A content-addressed
receipt pair matched on digests is the same design executed in bytes.
