This note records what the energy theories of money and value establish, which numbers they carry, and what a pooling instrument can take from them. ## Soddy: wealth is energy, money is debt

Soddy 1926 (an Internet Archive scan of the book, read in full text) defines wealth physically: "a form or product of energy or work which enables or empowers life". Money, credit and legal claims to wealth are, in his words, debts rather than wealth. Real wealth is subject to conservation and decay; debt is not, which is why claims can grow without limit while the wealth behind them cannot. Interest is legitimate as hire of the organs of production making perishable wealth; Soddy locates the failure of the capitalistic era in extending perennial interest to the non-repayment of any debt, and says capital multiplies the efficiency of human time but not human time itself. His "virtual wealth" argument is that a community acts as if it held wealth equal to the purchasing power of its money, so the quantity of money is arbitrary and its value is measured by virtual wealth. The passages read propose an index-number currency, not an energy-denominated unit.

## Technocracy and energy certificates

Oh 2024 (abstract only) traces Howard Scott's Technocracy movement of the 1930s as resting on scientific management and Veblen's institutional economics, aimed at maximizing productive efficiency and minimizing waste. It presented an ecological vision in the idea of energy certificates, and failed through political disorganization and a productivist premise. The mechanics of the certificate (issuance, accounting, expiry) are in Technocracy's own texts, which were not read.

## Minimum EROI and the saturation point

Hall et al. 2009 (abstract) calculate a "Law of Minimum EROI" of about 3:1 at the mine-mouth or farm-gate for oil and corn ethanol, and note most biofuels fall below 3:1 and must be subsidized by fossil fuels. Lambert et al. 2013 (abstract) compare societal EROI and per-capita energy use with HDI, child underweight, health expenditure, the Gender Inequality Index, literacy and improved water, and find energy indices highly correlated with standard of living, saturating above 150 GJ per capita or EROI above 20:1. Jackson & Jackson 2021 (abstract) simulate a transition with falling EROI in a stock-flow-consistent input-output model: an initial rise in output followed by recession, stagflation, rising inequality and stranded assets, worse as green energy is more capital-intensive.

## The energy-economy ratio

Garrett et al. 2020 (full text) find that over 1980 to 2017, world primary energy consumption E stayed in constant proportion to the time integral W of past inflation-adjusted world production: E/W = 5.9 plus or minus 0.1 gigawatts per trillion 2010 US dollars (standard deviation 2 percent; 5.2 or 6.2 if the starting cumulative production is doubled or halved), while both E and W more than doubled. The authors read this as current energy demand being fixed by past innovation and say, with no further efficiency gains, stabilising emissions needs at least one gigawatt of renewable or nuclear capacity a day or a steady-state economy that devotes production to maintenance.

## What a pooling instrument can do with this

- Soddy's distinction gives a design rule: the claims a pool issues are debts against future wealth, so total outstanding claims should be capped by a measured flow of real production, not by deposits alone.
- A 3:1 floor and a 20:1 saturation point give a net-energy band to monitor; pool distributions could be indexed to a net-energy or production proxy rather than to a nominal yield.
- Garrett's constant ratio treats cumulative production as the stock that energy demand follows; it suggests the pool's reserves be sized against cumulative member production, not current income.
- The energy-certificate idea remains a design reference for a unit of account that expires or is non-hoardable; the sources give no measured operation of one.
