The content-to-app funnel is a monetization architecture common among creator-led businesses: free, high-reach content across multiple social platforms functions as the top of a funnel whose terminal, monetized product is a single app, with older or more diffuse monetization channels (Patreon, ad revenue, a knowledge-base website) persisting only as secondary or legacy layers. It is a specific, evidence-checkable pattern, not just a general observation that "creators sell things" — this section's method is to trace the funnel's actual mechanics, not assume them.

## Why this belongs in competitive intelligence

The creator economy — sized by Goldman Sachs Research at roughly $250 billion as of its 2023 analysis, with a projected roughly-doubling to about $480 billion by 2027 at an estimated 10–20% compound annual growth rate — increasingly organizes monetization around owned channels (a site, a newsletter, a community, a product) built on top of reach generated by free content, rather than around ad revenue or one-off sponsorships alone. For a business built this way, understanding the funnel's shape — what channel is top-of-funnel, what is mid-funnel nurture, what is the terminal paid product, and how explicitly the creator routes traffic between them — is itself competitive intelligence: it reveals the real unit economics and distribution strategy behind an app that, viewed only through its store listing, looks like any other indie app with organic growth.

## What to trace, concretely

This section's founding application — the Feral Foraging / Gather app research — established the checklist for tracing a content-to-app funnel from public evidence alone:

1. **Channel footprint and follower counts across every platform** (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Patreon, Discord, a newsletter), each figure tagged with its confidence and source (a store's own structured data is higher confidence than a search-engine-aggregated subscriber count).
2. **Explicit calls-to-action in bios and captions** — a social bio's literal link-in-bio text ("Download my foraging app") and a tracked/affiliate-tagged link structure are direct, citable evidence of funnel intent, not inference.
3. **Messaging that redirects between channels** — where a legacy monetization channel's own copy explicitly tells its audience to move to the new paid product (in the founding case, Patreon's about page stating the founder "encourages supporters to subscribe to the Gather app as the primary way to support the work"), that is a first-person admission of the funnel's shape, not a researcher's inference.
4. **Conversion-rate signals where disclosed** — a platform that discloses both total membership and paid-member counts (Patreon's public member/patron split) yields an actual, citable conversion rate, useful for judging how much of a creator's reach translates to revenue independent of the terminal app's own numbers.
5. **Product-community infrastructure separate from the creator's general community** — a Discord server or Facebook group branded around the *product* rather than the creator's general content brand is evidence the funnel has matured into a standalone product operation, not just a monetized fan base.

## Relationship to app-store intelligence

The content-to-app funnel's top and middle stages (social reach, community, legacy tools) sit outside the app store; its terminal stage is exactly what [`app-store-intelligence`](./app-store-intelligence.md) measures (install bands, ratings, pricing, roadmap). A complete profile in this section traces both: where the audience came from, and what the store surface shows once they convert.
