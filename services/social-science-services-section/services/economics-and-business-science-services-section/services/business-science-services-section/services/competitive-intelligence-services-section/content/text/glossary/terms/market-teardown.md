A market teardown is not a single fact but a **document shape**: a repeatable structure for packaging open-source [competitive intelligence](./competitive-intelligence.md) research into something a reader — human or agent — can act on directly, rather than a narrative report that has to be re-mined for decisions.

## The pattern

As practiced in this house, a market teardown has, in order:

1. **Research provenance** — who compiled it, when, and by what method (sourced with inline URLs), and what larger decision or document it is evidence for.
2. **Headline findings** — a short numbered list of the strategic takeaways the research actually supports: the open differentiator, the most-repeated complaint, structural market gaps, near-unclaimed features, an incumbent weakness worth not repeating.
3. **Per-subject profiles** — one section per competitor (app, product, or business), each built from its own store listing, documented user complaints with cited forum/review-aggregator URLs, and direct links to its App Store, Google Play, Trustpilot, or equivalent public pages.
4. **A feature-parity or claim table** — subjects as columns, dimensions as rows, cells rated for presence/quality, with a classification column (must-have / differentiator / skip) translating the raw comparison into a decision.
5. **Evidenced weaknesses** — a numbered, cited list of cross-subject patterns worth learning from or avoiding.
6. **Net positioning** — a closing synthesis: what is confirmed open, what table stakes must still be cleared, what is worth taking early.

## Origin

The pattern was established by **Lenticule Science's Ilinx Research program**, the game-sciences discipline seated under `game-science-services-section` (named for the play-category of vertigo per Caillois; its own `package.json` description names "ludics, regime taxonomy, market teardowns, and theory bindings" as its four pillars). Its fullest worked instance is the [Weiqi Market Teardown](../../../../../../../../../agent-services-section/services/play-services-section/services/game-science-services-section/services/board-games-services-section/services/weiqi-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/weiqi-market-teardown.md), a 2026-08-19 competitive teardown of eight mobile and browser Go apps (BadukPop, GoMagic, Tsumego Pro, OGS, Fox/Yike, AI Sensei, EasyGo, PlayGo.gg) that built per-app profiles from App Store/Google Play listings and cited [online-go.com forum](https://forums.online-go.com/) complaint threads into a feature-parity checklist for a browser-first weiqi platform, closing with a stated differentiator (live in-game concept recognition) that no incumbent had claimed. Sibling teardowns exist elsewhere in the `561-group` tree following the same `*-market-teardown.md` naming convention, confirming this is an established, repeatable template across the library rather than a one-off document.

## Generalization to businesses and founders

This section (`competitive-intelligence-services-section`) generalizes the pattern from games to businesses generally, adding one profile dimension a game teardown does not usually need — the [founder's public persona](./founder-public-persona.md) — on the reasoning that a founder-led business's public credibility is itself a competitive asset, researchable and evaluable by the same open-source method as its pricing page or its app-store rating. The house's own founding applications of this generalized form profiled Feral Foraging / the Gather foraging app and its founder Jesse Akozbek across three linked documents: a product teardown (store presence, ratings, review-mined complaints, positioning against iNaturalist/PictureThis/Falling Fruit), a business/channel-footprint analysis (monetization model, social-channel reach, business-entity facts from a self-disclosed privacy policy), and a founder-persona research document (verified identity, public bio timeline, and persona-as-business-asset analysis) — see [`app-store-intelligence`](./app-store-intelligence.md), [`founder-public-persona`](./founder-public-persona.md), and [`content-to-app-funnel`](./content-to-app-funnel.md) for the evidence classes and patterns those documents established, and the method note below for how the pass was actually run.
