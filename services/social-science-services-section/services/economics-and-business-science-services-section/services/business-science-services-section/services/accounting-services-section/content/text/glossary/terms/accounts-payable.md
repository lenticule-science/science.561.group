Accounts payable is the money an entity owes to its suppliers for goods or services received but not yet paid for.

It is a liability because the entity has a present obligation to pay. Trade
payables are commonly current, but classification follows the governing
current/non-current criteria rather than a universal 30-to-90-day term. Receipt
of goods or services can recognize inventory, another asset, or an expense; it
does not always create an immediate expense. Accounts payable captures the
unsettled obligation separately from that debit-side classification.

Accounts payable is the mirror image of [accounts receivable](./accounts-receivable.md). What's receivable from the seller's perspective is payable from the buyer's. The same transaction creates an asset on one company's books and a liability on another's. This symmetry is a natural consequence of [accrual accounting](./accrual-accounting.md) — both parties record the economic event when it happens, then settle the cash separately.

Managing accounts payable means preserving the evidence for each obligation,
tracking its due date and payment terms, and settling it without duplicate or
late payment. Paying earlier than required trades away liquidity and is useful
only when a discount, relationship, or other financing consideration justifies
that cost. The timing of payables relative to receivable collection is one
component of the entity's cash conversion cycle and working-capital need.
