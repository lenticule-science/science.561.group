Information is material when omitting, misstating, or obscuring it could
reasonably be expected to influence decisions made by the primary users of the
relevant [financial statements](./financial-statements.md). Materiality is an
entity-specific aspect of relevance, not one reusable monetary threshold.

Magnitude, nature, context, aggregation, and the affected disclosure all matter.
An individually small related-party item, covenant effect, change in trend, or
management-compensation effect can be material by nature or circumstance. A
percentage benchmark can begin analysis, but cannot replace the user and
decision context.

Auditors use overall materiality and lower performance-materiality amounts to
design work and reduce aggregation risk. Evaluation of detected misstatements
still considers qualitative factors and uncorrected effects individually and in
aggregate. An unmodified opinion is not obtained by comparing one total with one
threshold; it follows the applicable assurance standard and the evidence as a
whole.

Materiality affects recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure
under both [GAAP](./gaap.md) and [IFRS](./ifrs.md). It is not permission to depart
from a requirement merely because each individual error is small; obscuring
information through aggregation and the cumulative effect of errors also need
evaluation.
