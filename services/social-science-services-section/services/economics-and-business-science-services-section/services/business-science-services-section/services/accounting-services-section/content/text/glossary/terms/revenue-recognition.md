Revenue recognition is the principle that determines when revenue is recorded in the accounting system.

Under [accrual accounting](./accrual-accounting.md), revenue recognition depends
on the governing framework and the substance of the arrangement, not merely on
invoice date or cash receipt. Recognition timing directly affects reported
performance and contract assets, receivables, or contract liabilities.

Topic 606 under [GAAP](./gaap.md) and
[IFRS 15](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-15-revenue-from-contracts-with-customers/)
use a converged five-step model for revenue from contracts with customers:

1. **Identify the contract** with the customer.
2. **Identify the performance obligations** in the contract.
3. **Determine the transaction price** the entity expects to receive.
4. **Allocate the transaction price** to each performance obligation.
5. **Recognize revenue** when (or as) each performance obligation is satisfied.

If a company receives \$120,000 in advance for one performance obligation
satisfied evenly over a twelve-month service period, the simplified pattern is
\$10,000 of revenue per month. The receipt initially creates cash and a contract
liability; revenue reduces that liability as performance occurs. Different
performance obligations, variable consideration, significant financing,
contract modifications, or a non-even transfer pattern can change the result.
