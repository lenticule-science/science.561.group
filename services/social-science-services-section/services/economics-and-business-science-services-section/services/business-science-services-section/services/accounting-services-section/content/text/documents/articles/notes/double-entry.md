Double-entry accounting represents each recognized event through balanced debit
and credit effects in a reporting entity's accounts. An entry normally affects
at least two accounts and may contain many postings or commodities.

## Core ideas

- Accounts classify assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses, and their
  contra balances according to the governing reporting basis.
- Each journal entry preserves equal debit and credit totals in its balanced
  unit or units.
- The statement-of-financial-position relation is assets = liabilities +
  equity.
- Balance is an arithmetic property. It does not prove source completeness,
  ownership, correct cutoff, classification, or measurement.

## Example

If an entity acquires qualifying equipment for 3,000 USD cash and recognizes
3,000 USD as its cost, it debits Equipment and credits Cash for 3,000 USD. The
entry records the supported cost; it does not assert that the equipment's market
value is 3,000 USD at every later date.
