A trial balance lists [ledger](./ledger.md) accounts with debit or credit
balances and tests whether the totals agree. A mismatch establishes a one-sided,
unequal, aggregation, or mapping defect somewhere in the reported population.
Omitting an entire balanced entry or both sides of a posting can leave the trial
balance equal, so “a posting was missed” is not by itself an explanation the
report can prove.

A trial balance that balances doesn't guarantee accuracy. It confirms only that the [double-entry](./double-entry-bookkeeping.md) mechanism was applied consistently — that every debit had a credit. Errors that preserve equality go undetected: a transaction posted to the wrong account (but with correct debits and credits), a transaction recorded at the wrong amount in both accounts, or a transaction omitted entirely. The trial balance catches mechanical errors, not conceptual ones.

A trial balance can be produced whenever ledger control is needed, including
before and after period adjustments. An adjusted trial balance is an important
statement input, but notes, cash-flow classification, consolidations,
eliminations, roll-forwards, and other disclosures can require evidence and
transformations not contained in its account totals.
