A budget is a governed plan for a defined entity, horizon, and set of resources.
Maintaining it means preserving the assumptions behind the plan, comparing them
with compatible actuals, and making changes prospectively rather than rewriting
the original baseline.

## Establish the baseline

1. State the planning horizon, reporting units, responsibility boundaries, and
   version of the baseline.
2. Separate accrual income and expense from cash receipts and payments. A plan
   can be profitable while failing its liquidity constraints.
3. Record opening cash, committed obligations, debt service, restricted funds,
   minimum reserves, and expected timing—not only category totals.
4. Distinguish fixed commitments, volume-sensitive costs, discretionary uses,
   and one-time items. Use explicit scenarios for uncertain prices, volumes,
   collections, and financing rather than hiding uncertainty in one point
   estimate.
5. Confirm internal coherence: the operating plan, cash plan, capital plan, and
   financing plan must reconcile with one another.

## Compare budget with actuals

Use the same entity, period, account mapping, and measurement basis on both
sides. Decompose material variance where evidence allows:

- timing: the event moved between periods;
- volume: actual activity differed from planned activity;
- price or rate: the unit amount differed;
- mix: the composition of activity changed;
- scope or classification: the compared populations differ; or
- execution: activity occurred differently from plan.

A favorable label is not a conclusion. Lower maintenance expense may represent
efficiency, deferred work, or an omitted invoice. Higher revenue may arrive with
unplanned collection risk or fulfillment obligations.

## Reforecast without erasing

Retain the approved baseline, record forecast changes as new versions, and
explain assumption changes. Reforecast when new evidence materially changes the
remaining plan; do not revise merely to make past variance disappear. Decisions
should respond to cumulative evidence, liquidity, commitments, and changed
conditions rather than to an isolated favorable or unfavorable month.
