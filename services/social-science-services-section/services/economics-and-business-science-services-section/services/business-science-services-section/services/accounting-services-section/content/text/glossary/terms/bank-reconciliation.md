A bank reconciliation compares the entity's book balance for a bank account
with independent bank evidence at the same cutoff. It explains differences such
as outstanding payments, deposits in transit, bank-originated fees or interest,
errors, and transactions missing from one record.

Reconciling items are not all journal entries. Timing items may already be
correct in both systems and reverse through settlement; bank-originated or book
errors may require entries or correction. Agreement after identified
adjustments supports the recorded balance, but does not prove ownership,
completeness, authorization, or absence of fraud. Frequency should follow
transaction volume, risk, and reporting needs; month-end reconciliation is a
common control, not a universal sufficient cadence.
