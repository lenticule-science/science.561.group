FIFO (First In, First Out) names two related but distinct practices. In physical
rotation, older [inventory](../../../../../../../../../../../operations-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/inventory.md)
is issued before newer inventory. For perishable stock, first-expired,
first-out may be the stronger control when expiry dates do not follow receipt
dates. In accounting, FIFO is a cost-flow assumption: the earliest eligible
cost layers are relieved first, whether or not the identical physical units
were the ones shipped.

In practice, FIFO requires labeling every item with its receipt date and organizing storage so that older items are in front (or on top) and newer items go behind (or beneath). When a new delivery arrives, it goes to the back; existing stock moves forward. This is simple but requires daily discipline — the natural tendency is to grab whatever is most accessible, which is usually the newest delivery.

Poor physical rotation can create preventable waste in food businesses. A case
buried behind newer deliveries may expire unused, causing physical counts and
actual food cost to diverge from expectation. The loss should be measured as
shrinkage, spoilage, or waste under the entity's inventory policy rather than
assumed from the rotation failure alone.

Under accounting FIFO, a sale relieves the oldest remaining eligible layer. If
flour costs \$0.50/lb in January and \$0.55/lb in February, the January layer is
assigned to cost of sales first, subject to quantities and the applicable
inventory rules. The method affects cost of sales and ending inventory; it does
not assert the actual bag selected. See [Food Costing and Waste Reduction](../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/food-costing-and-waste-reduction.md).
