Gross margin is gross profit divided by revenue, expressed as a percentage. If a restaurant generates \$50,000 in monthly revenue and classifies \$17,500 of food, labor, and other costs in cost of sales, gross profit is \$32,500 and gross margin is 65%.

Gross margin describes the relationship between reported revenue and the costs
classified as cost of sales. A 65% gross margin leaves \$0.65 per revenue dollar
before operating expenses and other items, but does not by itself identify
efficiency, pricing power, cash generation, or viability. Changes can arise from
product mix, costing and allocation policy, write-downs, currency, acquisition
accounting, or classification as well as operational performance.

Comparisons require consistent definitions and a genuinely comparable business
model. Industry ranges age quickly and can conceal differences in labor
classification, hosting costs, franchise structure, inventory policy, and net
versus gross revenue presentation. Trend analysis should bridge price, volume,
mix, unit input cost, waste, and accounting-policy effects rather than assign a
decline to one cause from the ratio alone.
