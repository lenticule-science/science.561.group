A [journal entry](../../../glossary/terms/journal.md) records an accounting
interpretation of one event or related batch using
[double-entry bookkeeping](../../../glossary/terms/double-entry-bookkeeping.md).
It should preserve a path back to source evidence; the entry is not itself the
source event.

## Steps

1. Identify the source, reporting entity, event, and relevant dates.
2. Decide whether the event is recognizable under the books' accounting basis
   and which period contains it.
3. Determine which [accounts](../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/03/06/2026-03-06-account.md) express the
   economic effects. A transfer between the entity's own accounts is not
   revenue or expense.
4. Measure each effect in its native commodity and any required cost or
   reporting currency. Keep quantity, historical cost, and market price
   distinct.
5. Assign [debits and credits](../../../glossary/terms/debit-and-credit.md) and
   confirm that the entry balances in every required unit.
6. Record a precise narration and stable source identifier. Include lot,
   counterparty, project, or settlement metadata when those facts affect later
   reconciliation or reporting.
7. Check for duplicate import, account constraints, cutoff, and consistency
   with subsidiary records. Arithmetic balance alone does not validate the
   entry.

**Example — purchased office supplies for \$300 cash:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Office Supplies Expense | \$300 | |
| Cash | | \$300 |

**Example — received \$5,000 from a client on account:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | \$5,000 | |
| [Accounts Receivable](../../../glossary/terms/accounts-receivable.md) | | \$5,000 |

## Common mistakes

- Treating every cash receipt as revenue or every cash payment as expense.
- Posting a related party's resource as though the reporting entity owned it.
- Using settlement date when the governing basis requires another recognition
  date, or silently moving an event across a closed cutoff.
- Replacing an unknown acquisition cost with a current price.
- Omitting fees, taxes, noncash consideration, or one side of a compound event.
- Correcting history by editing the old entry instead of posting a dated
  correcting entry with an audit trail.
