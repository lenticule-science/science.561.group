Operating expenses (OpEx) are expenses classified within operations but outside
cost of sales under the entity's presentation policy. The boundary is not
simply indirect versus direct cost: depreciation, labor, software, occupancy,
and fulfillment costs can appear in different functions depending on what they
support and what the reporting basis requires.

The presentation boundary between [cost of goods sold](./cost-of-goods-sold.md)
and other operating expenses matters for interpreting subtotals:

- **Revenue − COGS = Gross profit** under a gross-profit presentation.
- **Gross profit − operating expenses and other operating effects = operating
  income** under that presentation.

A business can have a high [gross margin](./gross-margin.md) and still report an
operating loss. That result does not by itself prove which expense is “too high”;
the account detail, activity drivers, capacity, and expected benefits are needed
for that judgment.

For managerial analysis, operating expenses can be modeled as fixed, variable,
mixed, step, committed, or discretionary over a stated range. Financial-
statement classification does not determine that behavior. Debt principal is a
balance-sheet settlement rather than an operating expense, while interest
classification follows the reporting basis. See [cost behavior](./cost-behavior.md)
and [break-even analysis](../../../../../../../finance-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/break-even-analysis.md).
