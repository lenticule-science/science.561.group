Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) is a context-dependent label for
authoritative accounting requirements in the United States. For nongovernmental
entities, the FASB Accounting Standards Codification is the principal source of
authoritative US GAAP, within a legal and regulatory system that also includes
the SEC for issuers. State and local governmental accounting has a distinct
GASB standard-setting structure. A bare claim that an entity “uses GAAP” should
therefore identify the entity and reporting authority.

General-purpose US GAAP reporting uses accrual recognition and supplies
definitions, recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure
requirements. Comparability is important, but consistent rules do not make
unlike entities identical or remove estimates and policy choices. Materiality
affects application; it is not a command to disclose every fact someone might
find interesting.

The slogan that GAAP is “rules-based” while [IFRS](./ifrs.md) is
“principles-based” is not a decision rule. Both contain broad principles,
detailed scope provisions, exceptions, and application guidance. Differences
must be established topic by topic and for the relevant effective period; the
substantially converged revenue models do not imply that inventory, leases,
impairment, presentation, or every other topic is aligned.
