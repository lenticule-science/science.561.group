## What this lesson covers

This lesson covers recurring
[bookkeeping](../../../../../../glossary/terms/bookkeeping.md): setting up
accounts, recording supported transactions, reconciling sources, maintaining
receivables and payables, and organizing records for reporting. Bookkeeping can
support solvency and compliance decisions; it does not guarantee either one.

## Prerequisites

[Reading Small-Business Financial Statements](./2026-03-06-note-on-reading-small-business-financial-statements.md). You need to understand the [income statement](../../../../../../glossary/terms/income-statement.md), [cash flow statement](../../../../../../glossary/terms/cash-flow-statement.md), and [balance sheet](../../../../../../glossary/terms/balance-sheet.md) to understand what bookkeeping produces.

## Separate business and personal finances

The reporting boundary must remain explicit.

- Use dedicated entity accounts where available and appropriate.
- Identify every account and card included in the reporting boundary.
- When a person pays an entity obligation or the entity pays a personal item,
  preserve the evidence and record the supported contribution, distribution,
  reimbursement, or related-party balance.

Unclassified commingling obscures ownership, authorization, tax treatment, and
performance. Its legal consequences depend on jurisdiction, entity form,
governing documents, creditor facts, and conduct; it does not automatically
“pierce the corporate veil.”

## Choose your bookkeeping method

### Cash basis vs. accrual basis

**Cash basis**: Record revenue when cash is received, expenses when cash is paid. A catering invoice sent in March but paid in April is March revenue under accrual but April revenue under cash.

**Accrual basis**: Record revenue when earned (regardless of when paid) and expenses when incurred (regardless of when paid).

| | Cash basis | Accrual basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Simplicity** | Simpler — matches bank account | More complex — requires tracking receivables and payables |
| **Period effects** | Can separate economic activity from the period in which it is shown | Recognizes assets, liabilities, income, and expense under accrual criteria |
| **Tax timing** | Follows the applicable cash-method tax rules and exceptions | Follows the applicable accrual-method tax rules and exceptions |
| **Eligibility** | Depends on jurisdiction, taxpayer, activity, elections, and current thresholds | Depends on the same governing facts |

Method selection depends on the purpose of the books and governing requirements.
Internal accrual records can be useful even when a tax return uses a permitted
cash method. Invoicing, inventory, prepayments, credit, and deferred performance
make the limits of cash-only reporting especially visible.

## Set up a chart of accounts

The chart of accounts supplies the ledger's durable classifications. Statement,
tax, budget, project, and managerial views may require explicit mappings rather
than a one-to-one account relationship.

### Basic chart of accounts for a small food business

**Assets** (what the business owns)
- 1000 — Checking account
- 1010 — Savings account
- 1100 — Accounts receivable
- 1200 — Inventory
- 1500 — Equipment
- 1510 — Accumulated depreciation (contra-asset)

**Liabilities** (what the business owes)
- 2000 — Accounts payable
- 2100 — Credit card payable
- 2200 — Sales tax payable
- 2300 — Payroll liabilities (taxes withheld but not yet remitted)
- 2500 — Loan payable

**Equity** (owner's stake)
- 3000 — Owner's equity / capital contributions
- 3100 — Owner's draws / distributions
- 3900 — Retained earnings

**Revenue**
- 4000 — Food sales
- 4010 — Beverage sales
- 4020 — Catering revenue
- 4030 — Event revenue
- 4100 — Other income

**Cost of goods sold**
- 5000 — Food cost
- 5010 — Beverage cost
- 5020 — Packaging and supplies

**Operating expenses**
- 6000 — Wages and salaries
- 6010 — Payroll taxes
- 6100 — Rent
- 6110 — Utilities
- 6120 — Insurance
- 6200 — Marketing and advertising
- 6300 — Repairs and maintenance
- 6400 — Office and administrative
- 6500 — Professional fees (accounting, legal)
- 6600 — Depreciation
- 6700 — Interest expense
- 6800 — Licenses and permits
- 6900 — Miscellaneous

Default charts are starting templates, not evidence of fit. Preserve every
distinction needed for independent reconciliation, restrictions, related
parties, measurement, and reporting while using metadata or subsidiary records
for dimensions that do not belong in account hierarchy.

## Recording transactions

### At transaction intake

- **Record all sales**: Reconcile
  [point-of-sale](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../operations-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/point-of-sale.md)
  orders, discounts, refunds, taxes, tips, fees, tenders, and settlement deposits.
  An integration transports data; it does not prove complete or correct mapping.
- **Record cash deposits**: Record transfers from cash on hand to the bank and
  match the deposit to the source cash population without recording the sale a
  second time.
- **Record purchases paid by card or cash**: Capture the complete purchase
  population at the level supported by the accounting policy, distinguish
  assets from period expenses, and retain the required source evidence.

### On the exception-review cadence

- **Enter supplier obligations**: Match purchase orders, receipts, invoices,
  credits, and disputes as applicable. Recognize the payable when the governing
  basis requires it; an invoice's arrival date does not by itself determine the
  recognition date. Classify acquired items according to their substance.
- **Record bill payments**: Apply each payment and supplier credit to the
  supported payable rather than merely reducing a vendor total.
- **Review intake exceptions**: Resolve uncategorized, rejected, duplicated, and
  unmatched bank-feed items on a cadence that supports the required cutoff and
  detection time.

### At each operational or reporting review

- **Reconcile bank accounts**: See below.
- **Reconcile credit card statements**: Match purchases, fees, interest,
  credits, payments, disputes, and the liability balance to the statement at a
  common cutoff. Card payments settle a liability; they are not a second
  expense.
- **Review accounts receivable**: Reconcile customer balances, due dates,
  disputes, subsequent receipts, and collection evidence. Collection action
  should follow contractual terms, risk, and policy rather than a universal age
  threshold.
- **Review accounts payable**: Reconcile supplier balances, due dates, credits,
  disputes, cash constraints, and payment authorization before scheduling
  settlement.
- **Generate financial statements**: Run your [income statement](../../../../../../glossary/terms/income-statement.md) and [balance sheet](../../../../../../glossary/terms/balance-sheet.md). Compare to your [financial projections](../../../../../../../../../../../finance-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/financial-projections.md) and to the prior month. Investigate significant variances.

## Bank reconciliation

Bank reconciliation compares the book and bank populations at a common cutoff.
Perform it when statements arrive and at required reporting cutoffs, with a
cadence responsive to volume and loss exposure.

### Process

1. Print or download your bank statement for the month.
2. Open the maintained reconciliation record or software workflow.
3. Enter the statement ending balance.
4. Check off each transaction in your books that appears on the statement. Mark deposits and withdrawals.
5. Identify discrepancies:
   - **In your books but not on the statement**: Candidate outstanding payments
     and deposits in transit. Validate each item against its initiation date,
     payee or deposit support, clearing evidence, and the common cutoff; age
     alone does not establish that it is a valid timing difference.
   - **On the statement but not in your books**: Candidate fees, interest, or
     initiated payments omitted from the ledger. Identify and classify each
     item from evidence before posting it.
   - **Different amounts**: Differences requiring investigation in the book
     record, source record, population, timing, currency, or amount. Correct the
     record shown by evidence to be wrong.
6. Retain the matched population, explain every difference, post supported book
   adjustments, and carry unresolved timing items forward. Equal totals alone
   do not complete the reconciliation.

### Why it matters

Reconciliation can expose transposition errors, unauthorized activity,
omissions, duplicate entries, and stale timing items. Detection speed depends
on the evidence source and review cadence; a monthly statement review is common
but is not sufficient for every volume or loss exposure.

## Managing accounts receivable

Accounts receivable (AR) represents recognized claims against customers. For
businesses that sell on credit, its control population must connect performance
and invoice evidence to customer balances, credits, receipts, disputes, and the
general ledger.

- **Invoice under the contract and recognition policy**: Connect each invoice to
  performance, tax, amount, counterparty, and due-date evidence. Invoice date
  does not by itself determine revenue recognition.
- **State terms clearly**: Preserve due date, dispute procedure, accepted payment
  rails, discounts, penalties, and governing agreement.
- **Follow up systematically**: Define reminder and escalation timing from
  materiality, dispute status, customer risk, contract, collection policy, and
  applicable law rather than universal day counts.
- **Use deposits when the contract and risk allocation support them**: Record
  cash received before performance as a liability until the recognition criteria
  for revenue are met.

## Managing accounts payable

Accounts payable (AP) is money you owe suppliers.

- **Track due dates**: Enter every invoice with its due date. Most accounting software shows upcoming payables in a dashboard.
- **Pay according to authorized terms and liquidity constraints**: Consider due
  date, disputes, service continuity, discounts, fraud controls, cash forecasts,
  and legal obligations.
- **Evaluate early payment rather than forbidding it**: Compare any discount,
  supplier relationship, operational risk, financing cost, and cash alternative.

## Tax preparation

Maintained books reduce tax-preparation reconstruction, but book accounting does
not by itself determine the tax return. Tax work still requires the applicable
entity, jurisdiction, filing period, tax basis, elections, limitations, and
supporting schedules.

### What your accountant needs

- supported [income statement](../../../../../../glossary/terms/income-statement.md)
  for the applicable reporting period
- reconciled [balance sheet](../../../../../../glossary/terms/balance-sheet.md) at
  the applicable year-end or filing cutoff
- information returns and payroll records required for the taxpayer,
  jurisdiction, payee, payment type, and current filing year
- records supporting asset acquisitions, capitalization, depreciation, and
  disposal under the applicable book and tax rules
- records supporting claimed business use of vehicles or travel, where
  applicable
- records supporting claimed business use of a home or other mixed-use
  property, where applicable

### Estimated tax payments

Estimated-payment duties depend on taxpayer, jurisdiction, expected liability,
withholding, safe harbors, and current filing-year rules. Use the applicable tax
authority's current instructions and qualified advice; a durable accounting note
must not encode universal dates or thresholds as though they applied to every
reader.

### Sales tax

Transaction-tax duties depend on nexus, jurisdiction, product or service,
customer, exemptions, marketplace rules, and filing registration. A
[point-of-sale](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../operations-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/point-of-sale.md)
calculation is an input to reconciliation, not proof that tax was correctly
determined, filed, or remitted.

## Choosing bookkeeping software

Select the exact product and configuration by testing entity, currencies,
inventory, payroll, invoicing, bank and payment integrations, roles, approvals,
audit history, reconciliation, reporting, export, retention, and migration.
Prices and plan boundaries are dated market evidence, not accounting theory.
Consistency matters only after the system is shown to preserve the required
record and controls.

## Guidance

- Set up a chart of accounts for a business you're planning. Map each account to the line it would appear on in your [financial statements](./2026-03-06-note-on-reading-small-business-financial-statements.md).
- Record one week of a hypothetical business's transactions: daily sales, two supplier invoices, a utility payment, and payroll. Categorize each one.
- If you already have a business, reconcile your bank account for last month. How many discrepancies did you find?
