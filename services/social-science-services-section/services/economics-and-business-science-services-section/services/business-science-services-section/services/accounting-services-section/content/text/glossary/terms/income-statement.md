An income statement is a [financial statement](./financial-statements.md) that
reports recognized financial performance over a period. Depending on the
framework and presentation, it includes revenue, expenses, gains, losses, tax,
and subtotals such as operating profit or net income. Comprehensive-income
items may require an additional statement or section.

“Revenue minus expenses” is a useful introductory identity, not a complete
recognition model. Revenue and expense are defined elements, and gains or losses
can arise outside ordinary sales. Classification and aggregation determine what
the statement communicates; a bottom-line total can be arithmetically correct
while its components are materially misleading.

Period performance changes equity through the entity's applicable accumulated
results account; a corporation commonly uses retained earnings. Distributions,
contributions, prior-period corrections, and other comprehensive income have
different paths. Because accrual recognition is not cash settlement, reported
profit can coexist with declining liquidity. The
[cash flow statement](./cash-flow-statement.md) explains cash movement, while the
[balance sheet](./balance-sheet.md) carries the closing resources, obligations,
and residual claims.
