Net income is a period-performance total under a specified reporting framework,
after the income and expense items included in profit or loss. It is often the
last line of the [income statement](./income-statement.md), but it is not “revenue
minus all costs”: owner distributions, many capital transactions, and items
reported in other comprehensive income are outside that description.

For one common presentation:

- Revenue − cost of sales = gross profit.
- Gross profit − operating expenses and other operating effects = operating
  income.
- Operating income plus or minus non-operating items and tax = net income.

The exact subtotals depend on the entity and framework; this bridge is not a
recognition rule.

Net income summarizes recognized accrual performance, not whether “everything”
has been accounted for or whether cash receipts exceeded cash payments. It must
be read with the statement's period, policies, estimates, unusual items, and
other statements.

Net income is not the same as cash. [Depreciation](./depreciation.md) reduces net income but doesn't consume cash. [Accounts receivable](./accounts-receivable.md) increases revenue (and net income) before cash is collected. Capital expenditures consume cash but don't appear on the income statement (they appear on the [balance sheet](./balance-sheet.md) and are depreciated over time). This is why a profitable business can run out of cash, and why the [cash flow statement](./cash-flow-statement.md) exists as a separate document. See [Reading Financial Statements](../../documents/articles/notes/reading-financial-statements.md) and [Managing Cash Flow](../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/managing-cash-flow.md).
