The choice between [accrual accounting](./accrual-accounting.md) and cash-basis
accounting changes when revenue and expense effects appear. It does not replace
the separate judgments about entity boundary, recognition, measurement,
classification, or evidence.

**Cash accounting** records transactions when cash moves. Revenue is recorded when payment is received, and expenses are recorded when payment is made. This is how most individuals think about money — if you haven't been paid yet, it doesn't count.

**Accrual accounting** records effects when the governing recognition criteria
are met, regardless of when cash moves. The exact criteria are more specific
than “the work is done” or “the benefit is received” and depend on the item and
reporting framework.

**Why the difference matters — a worked example:**

A consulting firm completes a \$10,000 project in March and sends the invoice. The client pays in April. Meanwhile, the firm's employees earned \$6,000 in wages during March, paid on April 5.

Under cash accounting, March shows: Revenue \$0, Expenses \$0, Net Income \$0. April shows: Revenue \$10,000, Expenses \$6,000, Net Income \$4,000. March looks like nothing happened; April looks like everything happened.

Under the simplified accrual assumptions, March shows: Revenue \$10,000,
Expenses \$6,000, Net Income \$4,000. April shows settlement of the related cash,
receivable, and payable balances rather than new project revenue or wage expense.
The example illustrates period assignment; an actual contract and payroll still
require their own recognition evidence.

**Why accrual accounting is used for general-purpose reporting:** It can show
rights, obligations, resources consumed, and performance before or after the
related cash movement. [Revenue recognition](./revenue-recognition.md) and
expense recognition have their own requirements; “matching” is not permission
to recognize an item that otherwise fails them.

**Why cash accounting still exists:** It is simpler and directly describes cash
receipts and payments. Tax eligibility is jurisdiction-, entity-, activity-,
and period-specific, so a changing gross-receipts threshold does not belong in
the timeless definition. Cash-basis records also do not replace cash planning:
even accrual-basis entities need a [cash flow statement](./cash-flow-statement.md)
and liquidity controls.

**The connection to the accounting cycle:** [Adjusting entries](./adjusting-entry.md)
implement accruals, deferrals, allocations, and estimates at a reporting cutoff.
They are the mechanism that brings continuous records to the selected reporting
basis, not evidence that every accrual estimate is automatically more accurate.
