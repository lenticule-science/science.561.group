A variance is a defined difference between a baseline and an observed or revised
amount. Budget-to-actual is common, but price, quantity, mix, volume, yield,
rate, efficiency, and forecast-update variances answer different questions.
Their sign depends on the chosen convention and bridge, so every report should
state the formula and comparable population.

“Favorable” and “unfavorable” ordinarily describe the modeled effect on the
selected objective, not management quality or long-run value. Lower maintenance
spend can be favorable to current expense while damaging capacity; an adverse
sales-volume variance can follow a deliberate exit from an unprofitable line.
The variance identifies where explanation is needed. It does not identify the
cause by itself.
