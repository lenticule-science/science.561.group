A balance sheet is a [financial statement](./financial-statements.md) that reports an entity's assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time.

It presents the accounting equation—Assets = Liabilities + Equity—in report
form. Assets are present economic resources controlled by the entity under the
applicable framework, not merely things it “owns.” Liabilities are present
obligations, and equity is the residual interest after liabilities. A statement
that fails the equation contains a recording, aggregation, mapping, or
presentation defect; equality alone does not establish that the reported items
are complete, owned, recognized, or correctly measured.

Presentation may order items by liquidity or separate current from non-current
items, depending on the framework and entity. Equity carries contributed and
accumulated residual claims through the categories appropriate to the entity.
[Double-entry bookkeeping](./double-entry-bookkeeping.md) preserves equality
across the complete account system; it does not require every entry to touch a
balance-sheet account directly, and it does not validate the entry's economic
substance.

Equity is not a separate pool of assets and is not automatically the entity's market value. The note [When Equity Is a Claim and an Equity Is an Asset](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-equity-as-claim-and-asset.md) distinguishes owners' equity from an equity security held as an investment asset and shows how the classification changes with the reporting entity.
