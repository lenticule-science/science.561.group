Payroll refers both to employee compensation and to the controlled process that
calculates, authorizes, distributes, reports, and remits it. Inputs can include
approved time, salary terms, leave, bonuses, benefits, garnishments, employee
classifications, and jurisdiction-specific withholding elections. Gross pay,
employee deductions, employer taxes or contributions, net pay, and remittances
are different accounting amounts and should reconcile to their source systems.

Tax rates, wage bases, filing rules, and employer obligations change by
jurisdiction and period; timeless theory should not encode one current US rate
as the definition of payroll. Employer cost can also include benefits, paid
leave, workers' compensation, payroll-provider fees, equipment, and other
commitments, so no general percentage of gross wages is defensible without a
defined scope and date.

Payroll controls include segregation between employee-master changes,
time approval, payroll calculation, payment release, and reconciliation;
restricted access to personal data; review of exception reports; and
reconciliation among the payroll register, bank settlement, tax filings, and
general ledger. Software or a service provider performs tasks but does not
transfer the employer's responsibility for accurate inputs, authorization, and
oversight.
