Accounts receivable is the money owed to an entity by its customers for goods or services delivered but not yet paid for.

It is an asset when the entity has an enforceable right to consideration and the
recognition criteria of its reporting basis are met. A receivable is current
when collection is expected within the applicable current-classification
horizon; stated customer terms alone do not determine presentation. Under
[accrual accounting](./accrual-accounting.md), a receivable can bridge the time
between satisfying the revenue-recognition conditions and collecting cash.

Not all receivables are collected. When the applicable measurement model calls
for an allowance, the allowance is a contra-asset that reduces the gross
receivable to the amount expected to be collected. The estimate depends on the
reporting framework and relevant evidence, which can include historical loss
experience, current customer conditions, subsequent receipts, and supportable
forecasts. Aging is evidence for that estimate, not the estimate itself.

A receivable is not cash. An entity can report earned revenue and a large
receivable balance while lacking the liquid funds needed to settle current
obligations. Operational management therefore includes prompt invoicing,
explicit payment terms, collection follow-up, reconciliation of customer
balances to the control account, and deposits or other credit controls when the
risk and size of an order warrant them.
