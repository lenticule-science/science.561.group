A budget is a quantitative plan for a defined future period, decision, or
responsibility area. It may forecast revenue, expense, cash, capacity, headcount,
or capital commitments. Its structure should support the decision being
controlled; it need not reproduce the structure or measurement basis of
[financial statements](./financial-statements.md).

Comparison with actual results produces a [variance](./variance.md), but the
arithmetic difference does not explain its cause. Useful control preserves the
budget version, assumptions, volume basis, responsible decision rights, and a
bridge from managerial measures to recorded actuals. Budgets support planning,
coordination, liquidity management, and evaluation, while also creating risks
of gaming, stale targets, and misleading accountability when uncontrollable
effects are assigned to a manager.
