An adjusting entry is a [journal entry](./journal.md) made at a reporting cutoff
to bring recorded balances to the recognition and measurement basis required
before [financial statements](./financial-statements.md) are prepared.

Adjusting entries handle five situations:

- **Accrued revenues** — revenue that's been earned but not yet recorded (e.g., services performed but not yet billed)
- **Accrued expenses** — expenses that have been incurred but not yet recorded (e.g., wages owed but not yet paid)
- **Deferred revenues** — cash received before it's earned (e.g., a subscription payment collected in advance)
- **Deferred expenses** — cash paid before the expense is incurred (e.g., prepaid rent or insurance)
- **[Depreciation](./depreciation.md)** — the allocation of a long-lived asset's cost over its useful life

Timing is one common thread, but measurement is another. Under
[accrual accounting](./accrual-accounting.md), recognition does not wait for
cash; estimates and allocations also update carrying amounts at the cutoff.
Routine accrual and deferral adjustments normally do not record a new cash
movement, because that movement belongs to its source transaction. Corrections
discovered during close must still be classified by their substance rather
than forced into a supposed no-cash rule.

## Earlier material

An adjusting entry is a [journal entry](journal.md) made at the end of an accounting period to update account balances before [financial statements](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/adjusting-entry/financial-statements.md) are prepared.

The common thread is timing.
Under [accrual accounting](accrual-accounting.md), revenue and expenses must land in the period they belong to, not the period when cash happens to move.
Adjusting entries close that gap.
They never involve the cash account — if cash moved, it was already recorded in an earlier transaction.
What adjusting entries do is reallocate amounts between balance sheet and income statement accounts so the numbers reflect economic reality at period-end.
