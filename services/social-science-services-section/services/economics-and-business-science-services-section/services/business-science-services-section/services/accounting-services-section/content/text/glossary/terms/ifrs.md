International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS Accounting Standards) are
issued by the International Accounting Standards Board. Jurisdictions decide
which entities may or must use them, often with local endorsement, effective
dates, or modifications. “Uses IFRS” therefore needs an entity and jurisdiction;
it is not a single global legal status.

IFRS and US [GAAP](./gaap.md) are distinct bodies of requirements. The slogan
that IFRS is “principles-based” and GAAP “rules-based” is too coarse to decide a
transaction: both contain principles, definitions, scope rules, exceptions, and
detailed application guidance. Differences must be analyzed standard by
standard, including scope and transition, rather than inferred from the slogan.

The IASB and FASB produced substantially converged revenue standards, IFRS 15
and Topic 606, but other requirements—including parts of lease accounting—are
not identical. A filer must establish which framework and regulator govern it
before selecting recognition, measurement, presentation, or disclosure rules.
