An advance reader copy (ARC) is a copy of a book given free to chosen readers before its on-sale date, so that honest reviews and word of mouth exist when the book can first be bought. Trade publishing calls the printed form a galley or bound proof; independent publishing mostly sends ebooks. In both, the copy is marked "advance reader copy — uncorrected proof — not for sale", names the on-sale date, and is often watermarked so a leaked file can be traced to the person it was given to.

**Why it exists.** A new book has no reviews, and readers and retail ranking both discount a book with none. Recommendation and rank on retail stores reward sales and reviews clustered near release, so a publisher wants reviews ready to post on release day. An ARC buys that by handing the book to readers early. Independent authors usually send ARCs two to eight weeks before release, to an *ARC team* recruited from their newsletter or through a distribution service (BookFunnel, StoryOrigin, Booksprout, NetGalley). Practitioner reports put the share of ARC readers who post a review at roughly a fifth to two fifths, higher when the campaign sets a date and sends reminders.

**What it is not.** An ARC is not payment for a review. Amazon allows publishers to give free or discounted copies to readers only if no review is required and no attempt is made to influence it. The US Federal Trade Commission's rule on consumer reviews (16 CFR 465, in force from 21 October 2024) forbids reviews conditioned on sentiment and undisclosed insider reviews. Its Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255) treat a free copy as a *material connection* the reviewer should disclose. Goodreads has required pre-publication reviewers to say how they got the book since December 2025. So an honest ARC invitation says that a review is welcome but not required. It names no rating, and it tells the reader to disclose the free copy. The author, the author's staff and relatives, and any persona the publisher operates do not review the book.

**Pre-orders.** ARCs usually run alongside a pre-order. The book is listed with a future on-sale date, so early readers can buy, and reviewers can point to a listing. Retailers set deadlines for the final file (KDP: 72 hours before release; Google Play: 3 days; Apple Books: 10 business days) and punish a missed deadline by cancelling orders.

**AI-generated books.** Reviewers weigh AI authorship, and NetGalley encourages publishers to disclose AI use in the book's description. An ARC of an AI-written book says so on the advance-reader page, before the reader decides whether to read and review it.
