**Personal branding** as a popular concept originates in trade business commentary, not scholarship: Tom Peters's ["The Brand Called You"](https://www.fastcompany.com/28905/brand-called-you) (*Fast Company*, Issue 10, August/September 1997, p. 83) argues that individuals should manage themselves as "Me Inc.," a company with its own brand, and is the concept's trade-canon origin point.

The peer-reviewed critical response is Alison Hearn's ["'Meat, Mask, Burden': Probing the contours of the branded 'self'"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1469540508090086) (*Journal of Consumer Culture* 8(2), 2008, pp. 197–217), which argues that the "reflexive project of the self" becomes, under post-Fordist capital, an explicit form of labor — **self-branding** — in which the self is worked up as image ("meat"), presented as persona ("mask"), and carried as an ongoing burden of continuous self-promotion.
This is a critical-cultural-studies account, methodologically distinct from Peters's trade advocacy, and should be cited as the peer-reviewed counterweight to it, not as an empirical test of Peters's claims.

Susie Khamis, Lawrence Ang, and Raymond Welling's ["Self-branding, 'micro-celebrity' and the rise of Social Media Influencers"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19392397.2016.1218292) (*Celebrity Studies* 8(2), 2017, pp. 191–208) extends Hearn's self-branding-as-labor account to social-media influencers specifically, arguing self-branding's growth parallels the growth of digital/social technology within a broader climate of neoliberal individualism, and gives "micro-celebrity" its academic grounding as distinct from mass-media celebrity — the bridge between self-branding theory and [influencer marketing](./influencer-marketing.md) practice.

Personal branding, in this scholarly sense, should be distinguished from [brand personality](./brand-personality.md), a separate construct concerning the human-trait profile consumers attribute to a product/corporate brand rather than the branding of an actual person.

See the [study of brand and marketing science](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-brand-and-marketing-science.md) for the fuller literature review and its treatment of the trade/peer-reviewed tier distinction in this sub-literature.
