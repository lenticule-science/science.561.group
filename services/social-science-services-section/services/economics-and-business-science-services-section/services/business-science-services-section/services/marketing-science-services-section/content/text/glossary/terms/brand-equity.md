Brand equity, as a managerial concept, is anchored by David A. Aaker's *[Managing Brand Equity: Capitalizing on the Value of a Brand Name](https://archive.org/details/managingbrandequ00aake)* (Free Press, 1991), which structures it around five underlying asset categories: brand loyalty, name awareness, perceived quality, brand associations, and other proprietary assets (patents, trademarks, channel relationships).
Aaker's book is a practitioner-facing strategy monograph, not a peer-reviewed journal article, and should be cited as foundational textbook canon rather than as an empirical study.

The peer-reviewed complement is Kevin Lane Keller's ["Conceptualizing, Measuring, and Managing Customer-Based Brand Equity"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224299305700101) (*Journal of Marketing* 57(1), 1993, pp. 1–22), which formalizes **customer-based brand equity (CBBE)** as "the differential effect of brand knowledge on consumer response to the marketing of the brand," built on associative-network memory theory and decomposing brand knowledge into brand awareness and brand image.
Where Aaker (1991) supplies the managerial asset taxonomy, Keller (1993) supplies the peer-reviewed psychological mechanism explaining why those assets translate into differential consumer response; the two are complementary rather than competing accounts, and neither substitutes for the other's evidentiary tier.

Brand equity, in Aaker's and Keller's shared sense, should be distinguished from [brand personality](./brand-personality.md), which concerns a brand's perceived human-trait profile rather than its overall differential value, and from the Ehrenberg-Bass tradition's [mental and physical availability](./mental-and-physical-availability.md), which explains brand growth through purchase-behavior patterns rather than through a knowledge-based equity construct — the two traditions are treated in this section as distinct, only partially reconciled schools rather than as a single unified theory.

See the [study of brand and marketing science](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-brand-and-marketing-science.md) for the fuller literature review, including how this section distinguishes the Aaker/Keller equity tradition from the Ehrenberg-Bass empirical-generalization tradition.
