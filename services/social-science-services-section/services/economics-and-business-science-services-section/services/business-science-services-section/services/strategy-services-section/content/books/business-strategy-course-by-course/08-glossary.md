# Glossary {.unnumbered}

The chapter in brackets is where each term is taught.

**Backward induction.** Solving a sequential game from the last move back.
[5]

**Build, borrow, buy.** Organic growth; contracts and alliances; mergers and
acquisitions. [3]

**Commitment.** A move that limits your own options to make a threat or
promise credible. [5]

**Complementor.** A player whose product makes yours more valuable. [1]

**Credible threat.** A threat the threatener would want to carry out when
the time comes. [5]

**Disruptive innovation.** An entrant's product that starts worse on the
market's key measures but cheaper or simpler, with overlooked customers, and
improves until it takes the mainstream. [2]

**Dominant strategy.** A strategy best whatever others do. [5]

**Envelopment.** A larger platform bundling another platform's function
into its own. [6]

**Five forces.** Rivalry, new entrants, buyer power, supplier power,
substitutes. [1]

**Game tree; payoff table.** Pictures of sequential and simultaneous games.
[5]

**Hold-up problem.** A partner exploiting another's relation-specific
investment. [3, 4]

**Multi-homing.** Using more than one platform at once. [6]

**Nash equilibrium.** Strategies from which no player gains by changing its
own alone. [5]

**Network effect.** A product becoming more valuable as more people use it;
same-side or cross-side. [6]

**PARTS.** Players, added values, rules, tactics, scope. [5]

**Prisoner's dilemma.** A game in which each player's dominant strategy
leaves both worse off than cooperating. [5]

**Relational contract.** A shared, largely unwritten understanding sustained
by the value of the relationship's future. [4]

**Resource-based view.** Advantage from resources that are valuable, rare,
inimitable and non-substitutable. [1]

**Scenario planning.** Testing strategies against a few plausible
futures. [2]

**Signpost.** An early sign of which scenario is unfolding. [2]

**Synergy.** Extra value from combining two businesses: cost, revenue or
capability. [3]

**Tipping.** A market moving toward one dominant platform. [6]

**Two-sided market.** A platform serving two groups linked by cross-side
network effects. [6]

**Unfreeze, change, refreeze.** Lewin's three movements of change. [2]

**Value created.** Willingness to pay minus supplier opportunity cost. [1]

**Value net.** A business among its customers, suppliers, competitors and
complementors. [1]

**Willingness to pay.** The most a customer would pay. [1]
