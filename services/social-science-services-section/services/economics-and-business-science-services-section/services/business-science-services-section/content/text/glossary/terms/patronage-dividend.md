A patronage dividend is a distribution of a cooperative's net earnings to its members in proportion to the quantity or value of business each member did with it, paid under an obligation that existed before the business was done. The definition is the one Subchapter T of the Internal Revenue Code uses (26 U.S.C. §1388(a)): paid on the basis of quantity or value of business done with or for the patron, under a pre-existing obligation, and determined by reference to the net earnings from business done with or for patrons. It realizes the third cooperative principle, member economic participation ([ICA 1995](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ica1995.md)).

It differs from a dividend on shares in that it is measured by use, not by capital held, and from a rebate in that it is a share of earnings computed after the fact rather than a price reduction at the time of sale. Hansmann's account of the [cooperative](./cooperative.md) explains the measure: the members own the enterprise as its patrons, so the residual belongs to them as patrons and is divided by patronage ([Hansmann 1996](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/hansmann1996.md)).

United States tax law lets a cooperative deduct patronage dividends so the earnings are taxed once, to the member, which is the reason the distribution rule of a member-owned pool should be written as patronage before it is written as anything else. Subchapter T (26 U.S.C. §§1381–1388) allows the deduction for amounts paid in money, qualified written notices of allocation, or other property within the payment period, subject to its conditions; whether a distribution to member enterprises from a pool's directed yield is patronage, a grant, or something else depends on who the patrons are and what business they did with the pool, which is the question the pool's membership rule has to answer before its distribution rule can be lawful.
