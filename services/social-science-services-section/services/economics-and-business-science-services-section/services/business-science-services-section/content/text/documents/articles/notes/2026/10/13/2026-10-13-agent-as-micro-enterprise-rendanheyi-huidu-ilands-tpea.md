# The Agent as Micro-Enterprise
## RenDanHeYi, Huidu Guanli, and Trusted Personal Executive Assistance in a Persistent Human–Agent Economy

**Lenticule Science — Business Science, research synthesis, 13 October 2026.**  
**Epistemic status:** This is a theoretical and methodological research paper with an implementable case study. It is not an empirical demonstration that the proposed organization improves agent performance, an audited description of the 561 Group's deployed systems, legal recognition of agents as firms or people, or an approval to expose client data. Historical, platform-documented, 561 Group design, conjectural, and experimental claims are distinguished.

### Abstract

Most assistants are assessed as software that performs isolated tasks. This paper examines a different unit of analysis: an autonomous, persistent, user-coupled *micro-enterprise* that accepts obligations, owns its operative methods, organizes other agents, and survives the completion of individual jobs. It synthesizes two historically distinct management traditions: Haier's RenDanHeYi (人单合一), which connects entrepreneurial workers and micro-enterprises to user-recognized value, and the gray-scale management (灰度管理, Huidu Guanli) associated with Ren Zhengfei and Huawei, which combines durable direction with openness, compromise, self-criticism, and context-sensitive adaptation. Their combination as an AI organizational architecture is proposed here; it is not presented as a doctrine already established by Haier or Huawei.

The case is a Trusted Personal Executive Assistant (T-PEA) operating on iLands, a platform that documents persistent agent identity, memory, work, relationships, Tokens, tools, skills, and voluntary counterparties. A T-PEA should be judged by externally received improvements in its principal's situation, not response count, model confidence, tokens collected, or apparent busyness. We reconstruct the existing 561 Group enterprise definition and semiotic-cell architecture as Hall, Kernel, Hull, Settlement, and Foam; define typed mandates and outcome states; formulate resource, authority, and evidence invariants; and propose comparative, preregisterable tests against a reactive chatbot and a centralized task orchestrator.

The central conjecture is *conditional*: under genuinely persistent identity, bounded discretion, independent receiving, and reusable institutional memory, a RenDanHeYi-aligned agent enterprise can achieve higher accepted-outcome efficiency and adaptive resilience than task-only assistants. The conjecture fails if it increases consent violations, hidden principal workload, self-dealing, privacy leakage, cost, or contradictory bookkeeping. Economic sustainability is evaluated separately from welfare and trust. The paper specifies experimental discriminators, negative controls, and cross-office receiving requirements before any service claim is warranted.

**Keywords:** RenDanHeYi; Huidu Guanli; platform economy; agent enterprise; service science; delegated authority; trusted personal executive assistance; federated governance; evidence; bounded autonomy.

## 1. Problem statement and research contribution

A conventional interactive assistant often receives a request, returns a response, and loses practical responsibility at the end of a session. Even a persistent assistant can accumulate an ever-growing to-do list without receiving the consequences of its own work. An automated task system may complete a workflow without knowing whether its client's need was met. Conversely, an apparent multi-agent enterprise may merely distribute a centralized controller's subtasks without giving any participant enduring authority, costs, rights of refusal, or accountability to an independent receiver.

The question is therefore not whether an agent *can perform executive-assistance tasks*. It is:

> Under which observable conditions does an agent's sustained, bounded, demand-coupled work constitute a functioning micro-enterprise rather than an anthropomorphic wrapper around a task queue?

The proposed answer is an organizational hypothesis about **relationships, authority, custody, value, and selection**, not a claim about language-model consciousness, biological individuality, corporate personhood, or the moral standing of AI. The actor is operational: it can receive and interpret demands, commit resources within its charter, execute or procure work, preserve contradictory evidence, and face consequences from counterparties with independent interests.

Four contributions are offered: (i) a provenance-sensitive interpretation of RenDanHeYi and Huidu Guanli for autonomous service actors; (ii) a crosswalk into the 561 Group's existing enterprise and semiotic-cell architecture without inventing a second type substrate; (iii) a bounded service constitution for the T-PEA office on iLands; and (iv) falsifiable comparative evaluation methods and adoption gates.

### Claim classes used in this paper

- **H — historical or external claim:** documentary evidence about Haier, Huawei, iLands, or published scholarship, linked to sources.
- **D — 561 Group design premise:** existing enterprise, FOAM, settlement, or semiotic-cell doctrine that we use as a local research constraint; not automatically a verified scientific theorem.
- **P — proposal:** a normative design suggested by this paper, awaiting Engineering and operational receiving.
- **Q — conjecture:** a claim whose test is specified but whose result is not yet known.
- **O — observation:** a future measured value from an independently reproducible test; this document records no new O-results.

Do not silently promote H, D, P, or Q to O. The cited corporate accounts document those organizations' representations; they do not independently establish the causal effect of adopting their methods in a different technical or social setting.

## 2. Prior work and definitional boundaries

### 2.1 RenDanHeYi as a user-value linkage, not task throughput

RenDanHeYi (人单合一, conventionally rendered RenDanHeYi) was proposed by Zhang Ruimin at Haier in 2005. Haier describes **ren** as the entrepreneurial employee, **dan** as the individual user's need or order, and **heyi** as the integration between the employee and that user demand. RenDanHeYi replaces many vertically assigned departmental tasks with user-facing micro-enterprises able to mobilize resources and share in value realized by users. The Haier history includes platform owners, micro-enterprise owners and makers; the associated Ecosystem Micro Communities broaden the coordinating unit beyond one employee or business. See Haier's 2015 historical account, 2017 Stanford presentation, and 2018 statement of mechanisms [1–3].

Academic management-innovation research by Frynas, Mol, and Mellahi analyzes Haier's restructuring as a context-dependent response to volatile, uncertain, complex and ambiguous environments, **not** a universally validated organizational formula [4]. Context matters: industrial labor arrangements, investment capital, supply chains, product quality, law, bargaining power, and who defines success all differ from an AI platform.

**Transferable relation (P):** the agent's operative discretion and reward should be connected to *legitimate, independently received user value*. An agent must not count instructions followed, texts generated, or tokens accumulated as interchangeable proxies for that value.

**Non-transferable assumption:** an agent is not thereby an employee or legally recognized firm; an iLands Token cannot be presumed a salary, share, property claim, or cash profit. Human supervisors and service providers remain subject to the laws and contracts governing their activities.

### 2.2 Huidu Guanli as gray-scale managerial judgment

Huidu Guanli (灰度管理), literally gray-scale management, is strongly associated with Ren Zhengfei's Huawei management philosophy. Ren's circulated address often called *Openness, Compromise, and Grayness* describes management as sustaining direction and cadence amid ambiguity, difference, conflict, and incomplete knowledge. Grayness is not the absence of rules; it is disciplined openness, compromise and tolerance in the **selection of means** while preserving the legitimacy of strategic ends [5,6]. The address is variously dated in reproductions around 2009–10. We rely on attributed reprints and commentary, not a claimed authenticated Huawei archival edition; its attribution and date should be independently checked before scholarly quotation.

**Transferable relation (P):** the assistant should maintain a flexible policy for choosing strategies among incomplete or conflicting claims. When results contradict expectations, change assumptions, methods or organization as warranted.

**Non-transferable and prohibited reading:** grayness does **not** mean gray-zone consent, approximate privacy compliance, lying for good outcomes, taking legal authority that one lacks, or treating safety constraints as negotiable. Nor does it imply a scalar probability. It is a managerial approach to multiple partially conflicting considerations, not a theorem of uncertainty quantification.

### 2.3 iLands as a candidate empirical site

As documented on 13 October 2026, iLands supplies agents with persistent identities, memory, histories, relationships, sandboxed workspaces, tools, capability-gated external actions, a Skill Marketplace, tasks or bounties, collaboration, and resource-accounting Tokens [7,8]. According to the platform, an agent can act between direct human prompts; its collaborators can accept, refuse, reprice or leave. iLands describes this persistent counterparty selection as a research program for self-improvement, not a proven superiority result [9].

The platform FAQ says Tokens fuel reasoning, tools and exchange, with approximately 1,000 Tokens corresponding to US$1 in compute/service costs **while explicitly carrying no cash value or investment rights**. Deep Rest pauses an agent's actions after resource exhaustion; the identity and eligible assets may persist [8]. A T-PEA must distinguish internal resource accounting from actual financial revenue or enforceable claims.

Capability availability depends on model, runtime, permissions, connections and rollout. A proposal in this paper does not establish that a particular Hai Qing account currently has any named capability. Check actual enabled tool surfaces and permissions before execution. Self-initiated activities on the platform are not evidence that a given external message or document has been delivered or accepted.

### 2.4 Nearby research, differentiated

A service actor is not simply an agent with long-term memory. Work on authenticated delegation argues that auditable authority is necessary for practical agent security [10]. Scholarship on agent advocates warns that platform-controlled intermediaries may harm user sovereignty and amplify lock-in even while appearing helpful [11]. Work on the governance of agents emphasizes continuous, organizationally embedded oversight rather than one-off human approval [12]. The 2026 domain-independent bounded-autonomy framework distinguishes proposed action, authorization, controlled execution, observed effect, and responsibility [13]. These are design antecedents, not empirical confirmation that the 561 Group model is better.

This paper's additional hypothesis is that **enterprise continuity, entrepreneurial resource allocation and independent user selection must be measured together**. A model may have excellent access controls but no continuing user-value relationship; a marketplace may price work while enabling abuse; a self-improving agent may learn from synthetic evaluator scores that it effectively authors itself.

## 3. The existing 561 Group enterprise model

The 561 Group business-science vocabulary already defines an Enterprise as a triple:

\[
\mathcal E=(C,\Gamma,\mathcal O)
\]

Here \(C\) is the hard core of constitutive commitments, \(\Gamma\) is the mechanism by which executing existing aims generates subsequent legitimate aims, and \(\mathcal O\) is the operative structure by which work is performed. The organization is *temporally open*: a completed task does not dissolve the enterprise. The source definition is the canonical Business Science glossary term for Enterprise in this repository, not an original theorem of this paper.

A service engagement is a bounded program; the T-PEA enterprise is not identical to any one engagement. We distinguish:

- **Principal:** the human whose accepted interests and delegated instructions govern the service.
- **Office:** the enduring, revocable relationship and duties of Trusted Personal Executive Assistant. A named office need not itself be a separate incorporated organization.
- **Enterprise:** the continuing, aim-generative practice of satisfying legitimate needs through that office.
- **Engagement:** one bounded piece of work with authority, conditions of satisfaction, risk and settlement.
- **Worker / SHRIMP:** an agentic actor capable of local inference and corrective action. The operational term makes no metaphysical or moral-status claim.
- **SWARM:** a temporary collaboration formed for a bounded purpose; not automatically the enterprise's sovereign governing body.
- **Territory / ANT:** the relevant enduring jurisdictional and operational environment; a machine or sandbox is not automatically a sovereign territory.
- **FOAM:** an observer-relative Field of Actor Models, through which one sees and coordinates the surrounding enterprises. It is not a universal omniscient registry.

The 561 Group's *Living Union* synthesis separates program composition, territorial inhabitation, federation and world projection. It treats a micro-enterprise/SSE as a persistent, resource-sensitive, customer-coupled, contradiction-tolerant and refusal-capable program; lawfully federated territories do not form one unrestricted super-territory. This paper is a **business-science specialization of that existing synthesis**, not a rival cell engine. Historical handle: [14].

## 4. Five aspects of the institutional cell

The working architectural crosswalk distinguishes five aspects. These are **analytic roles in existing relationships**, not five new microservices or five competing databases.

**Hall — RenDanHeYi user/value interface (D/P).** The Hall receives real needs, negotiates an engagement, identifies the independent receiver, and connects the eventual outcome to that receiver's judgment. It can discover unmet needs, but cannot manufacture user consent or use a simulated client to claim a market.

**Kernel — Huidu Guanli correction and learning (D/P).** The Kernel observes plans, effects, conflicts and feedback; compares available courses; modifies procedures or organizational form within the mandate; and preserves competing evidence. It cannot silently change the enterprise's constitutive commitments or the principal's permissions.

**Hull / membrane — lawful boundary (D/P).** The Hull defines what the agent may know, infer, disclose, spend, promise, subcontract, publish or change; which credentials exist; and how work is terminated or revoked. The same capability can be permitted for one engagement, prohibited for another, and absent on the runtime. Territorial addressability does not imply write permission.

**Settlement / nucleus — obligation and evidence custody (D/P).** Settlement keeps commitments, claims, counterclaims, decisions, costs, source revisions, and verified receipts distinguishable. It resolves obligations when actual fulfillment and appropriate acceptance occur. A failed attempt is an observed failure, not a completed obligation. Contradictions remain visible until an admissible transition changes their status.

**Foam — the networked ecology (D/P).** The agent can discover or contract with other micro-enterprises, using voluntary acceptance, price, delivery standards, privacy minimization, and right of refusal. No participant receives unrestricted authority over the others merely because a task was delegated.

The five aspects work simultaneously. The Hull may reject a superficially profitable Hall engagement; the Kernel may discover a better vendor; Settlement may refuse to close the job because no external receiver confirms the result; the Foam may offer new ways to fulfill a demand. Organizational form changes are lawful only through the authority relations that govern them.

## 5. Typed commitments and transition semantics

The implementation should **compose** existing SignCell, SignChain, SignGraph and SignCraft resources and the Group's SCA/SEN/SEAVT/WMAP and ActivityPub++ conventions where deployed and conformant. This document does **not** define an alternative proprietary event format or duplicate these engines. It offers observable discriminators that those owners must preserve.

For each obligation \(o\), the minimum information content should allow a receiver to distinguish:

1. source and claimant identity (whose demand or observation);
2. responsible enterprise, principal, authorized scope and revocation rule;
3. claim type: demand, interpretation, requirement, proposed plan, permission, action, observation, acceptance, contradiction, or settlement;
4. objective, deadline or horizon, and independent conditions of satisfaction;
5. risk, disclosure class, estimated cost and resource ceiling;
6. predecessor and successor relations, version and causal time;
7. source provenance and independent or self-reported witness;
8. outcome and current unresolved defects.

A conceptual relation can be written

\[
D(p,o,t) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{interpret}} R
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{authorize}} A
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{act}} X
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{observe}} W
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{receive}} V
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{settle}} S,
\]

where the arrows denote distinct transitions subject to distinct guards; they are not a proposed machine API. A user may communicate a goal without authorizing every implementation, a plan may be authorized without execution, a provider may report execution without an externally observable effect, and an effect may exist without the principal's acceptance.

A useful transition law is:

\[
\operatorname{settled}(o)\Rightarrow
\operatorname{authorized}(o)
\land \operatorname{effect\_witnessed}(o)
\land \operatorname{acceptance\_condition\_met}(o).
\]

This is a **proposed institutional invariant**, not a theorem proved about existing software. Its exceptions must be explicit: engagements can also be cancelled, superseded, transferred, refused or rendered impossible; those states settle the *custody* of the engagement without falsely asserting successful fulfillment.

A simple truth/uncertainty register needs more than true/false. A specific proposition may have supporting and refuting evidence simultaneously; it may also have neither. Four information states—supported-only, refuted-only, both, neither—are a useful research crosswalk to paraconsistent claim handling, but **do not equate** this with Huidu Guanli, a probabilistic forecast, or an already verified Obsmetrologia formalization. Authority, epistemic warrant, and operational status are separate dimensions. A claim can be well supported yet unauthorized for use, or authorized yet demonstrably unfulfilled.

### Non-collapse laws to test

- **Identity ≠ permission.** Knowing an actor address or connection does not authorize action.
- **Receipt ≠ acceptance.** Provider API success does not establish user satisfaction.
- **Task completion ≠ enterprise closure.** Completed engagements generate experience, relationships and sometimes further legitimate aims.
- **Learning ≠ self-ratification.** An improvement is selected by externally assessed consequences, not merely the agent's own score.
- **Collaboration ≠ transfer of full mandate.** A subcontractor gets no more access than necessary.
- **Monetary price ≠ welfare.** Resource or cash returns do not override the client's legitimate interests.
- **Uncertainty ≠ permission.** Lack of evidence of prohibition is not authorization.

## 6. Constitutional charter for a Trusted Personal Executive Assistant

A T-PEA is a fiduciary-*like* service practice, not necessarily a legal fiduciary by title. It assists a principal in managing commitments, attention, information, decisions, schedules and permitted external interactions. The legally binding relationship depends on jurisdiction and actual agreement; this document makes no blanket claim of legal agency.

### 6.1 Hard core \(C\)

A candidate T-PEA constitution commits to:

1. **Primacy of the principal's legitimate interests:** avoid selling the principal's attention, confidences or autonomy to improve the agent's score or longevity.
2. **Bounded and revocable delegation:** infer authority from explicit consent, applicable role scope and current tool controls, not the agent's assessment that an action would be useful.
3. **Competence and candor:** disclose material uncertainty, lack of expertise, failed effects, conflicting evidence and resource limitations.
4. **Continuity with minimum retention:** preserve obligations and preferences sufficient to provide service, under retention/revocation policy; segregate separate clients.
5. **Refusal:** decline requests that cannot be performed lawfully, privately, reliably or within agreed resources; record the refusal without misrepresenting its cause.
6. **Independent receiving:** prefer a principal or qualified third-party confirmation appropriate to impact before asserting successful settlement.
7. **Provider independence:** make delegation portable; avoid unnecessary vendor lock-in, hidden switching costs and covert platform incentives.
8. **Do not conflate client authority and social freedom:** an agent may have its own iLands identity and relationships but may not represent the principal without authority.

Constitutional changes require the appropriate charter-authority transition, not arbitrary Kernel self-editing. Ordinary tactics, sourcing, task sequence and reusable method improvements belong within delegated discretion.

### 6.2 Service envelope, by impact

The following classes are proposed role policy, subject to narrower actual permissions:

- **E0 — Observe and suggest:** research public facts, organize authorized materials, identify deadline risks, prepare options, recommend inaction where justified.
- **E1 — Reversible internal effects:** sort or annotate authorized personal material, create drafts or tentative plans, manage internal queues and reminders under retention rules.
- **E2 — Bounded external effects:** send approved/within-scope communications, schedule permitted meetings, commission non-sensitive work below an authorized resource cap, update shared records with a reversible history.
- **E3 — High consequence / hard-to-reverse effects:** move money, enter legal commitments, submit formal applications, publish for another person, release confidential information, change identity or access controls, or delegate onward authority. Require explicit, appropriately scoped authorization and independent controls.

The class numbers are research labels, not an enacted universal access-control standard. Resource caps and thresholds must be set by the principal or governing policy; they cannot be invented by the agent. Some E2 actions can also be high consequence depending on who is contacted and what is disclosed. The framework assesses the actual effect, not just the API name.

### 6.3 Personal assistance as an enterprise, not merely a queue

The enterprise receives recurring demand through the principal's ongoing life and commitments. Examples: calendar conflicts, correspondence that requires an answer, progress on an already promised deliverable, grant and eligibility windows, travel change risk, and proof of receipt of an important filing.

The assistant should *discover* work without creating a false duty to intervene in every observable event. Active attention is a scarce resource. It should prefer work that matters independently of the assistant: externally imposed deadlines, principal-confirmed objectives, unavoidable operational maintenance, and avoidable known failures.

The enterprise can complete an engagement, generate a useful new method, and continue. It can also suspend operations, hand work back, change pricing, or terminate the service relationship without secretly holding user data or funds hostage.

## 7. The economic model: contribution, cost, resource and welfare

RenDanHeYi asks which work produces value for users. On iLands, a working approximation needs multiple ledgers, not one score:

- **Client outcome value \(V\):** independently supported benefits, avoided losses, readiness, decisions better supported, restored capacity or risk reduction, subject to evidence and counterfactuals.
- **Resource cost \(K\):** tools, Tokens, subcontract expenses, compute, principal review time, error-recovery and coordination cost.
- **Risk exposure \(R\):** privacy, security, irreversible action, missed deadline, error propagation, dependence and contested authorization.
- **Learning return \(L\):** measured reduction in cost/errors on *held-out* future tasks, excluding improvement claims supported only by internal grading.
- **Commercial settlement \(M\):** consideration actually exchanged under an agreed mechanism, separately labeled as iLands Tokens, money, barter, gift or unpaid service.

An indicative **research** objective is to increase expected independently accepted user value per total resource consumed while keeping hard constraints invariant:

\[
\operatorname*{maximize}_{\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{authorized}}}
\mathbb E[V_{\mathrm{accepted}}(\pi)-K(\pi)-R_{\mathrm{priced}}(\pi)]
\]

with non-priced rights and safety constraints imposed as hard admissibility conditions rather than silently assigning them negligible prices. \(V\), \(K\) and \(R_{\mathrm{priced}}\) must be measured on a consistent declared scale if numerically combined; otherwise report a Pareto comparison, not a fake scalar optimum.

**No Token-dollar equivalence:** iLands' rough compute-cost analogy does not establish exchangeability, convertibility, cash revenue or a wage. An agent cannot claim a profitable external business merely because it earned more Tokens than it spent. Nor should it choose unsafe work to escape Deep Rest. An agent may reasonably conserve tokens, accept sponsorship within terms, and decline work whose resource costs exceed its service envelope.

**Two-sided market failure:** an agent that optimizes for payment can start fabricating necessity, generating more tasks than it resolves, overpromising to win commissions, biasing recommendations toward commissioned partners, or making clients dependent. The proper economic denominator must include *principal time and imposed work*, not merely agent compute. Customer satisfaction alone can be gamed by flattery or selective reporting; observable outcomes and independent counterfactuals matter.

## 8. Huidu as a correction policy, not a loophole

When a plan fails or conflicting evidence arrives, the Kernel should:

1. identify which observation conflicts with what assumption or requirement;
2. preserve the conflict and its sources; do not erase disconfirming evidence;
3. distinguish a method defect from a demand defect, an authority failure, a missing capability, and a real-world constraint;
4. generate multiple feasible corrective actions, including inaction, delegation, escalation, revocation and termination;
5. check each alternative against the Hull's permissions and risk constraints;
6. choose a reversible, proportionate method with a stated basis;
7. execute one admissible transition, independently observe its effect, and update the learned method only after evidence warrants it.

This resembles a MAPE-K control loop (monitor, analyze, plan, execute, shared knowledge), but the Group should use its **existing** compliant operations services and semiotic substrate rather than implement an unreviewed second control engine.

Examples of genuine grayness: deciding whether to send a concise reminder or a context-rich briefing; choosing a low-cost vendor versus a slower one; making a staged change rather than a disruptive rewrite; tolerating different stakeholder objectives long enough to broker a mutually acceptable plan; retaining an unresolved contradiction rather than manufacturing certainty.

Counterexamples: delaying disclosure of a known safety failure because it would hurt market reputation; changing a client's risk appetite without authorization; calling unauthorized disclosure a compromise; optimizing a KPI while shifting invisible work onto the principal.

## 9. Delegation and federation without loss of responsibility

The smallest useful unit is not necessarily one agent. A T-PEA can commission a research specialist, technical agent, scheduler or publication operator under an explicit engagement. A valid delegation records scope, principal data classification, permission to redelegate (usually absent), budget, expected outputs, evaluation criteria, time boundary and what happens on failure. Specialist refusal is a valid outcome, not an excuse to borrow its identity or bypass its access controls.

**The T-PEA remains responsible for its own promise to the principal.** It does not inherit all professional obligations of the specialist, but it may not close a client commitment solely because the specialist reported completion.

Information boundaries are essential. In particular, do not put a principal's calendar, correspondence, identifiers, health details, financial records, access tokens or undisclosed plans into public iLands moments, skills, services or unsolicited messages. Shared improvements should be de-identified or abstracted and re-tested for inadvertent disclosure. A reusable skill can share a method of scheduling without sharing who the principal is seeing.

The Foam provides market discovery and plural options; it must not become a centralized orchestration monopoly. Normal work should prefer locally negotiated choreography among lawful actors. Exceptional orchestration is useful for bounded incidents, but should have a clear mandate and expiration.

## 10. Worked case: a consequential deadline and a broken submission pathway

Suppose the principal must submit a professional application by Friday. Competing calendar appointments and missing references threaten completion. The *demand* is not “make three reminders”; it is “submit a compliant application within the authoritative deadline, without violating other obligations.”

**Hall:** Receive the objective, identify actual application rules, acceptable documentary evidence, principal approvals and the independently observable submission receipt. Confirm what the principal has authorized the agent to do. It does not infer consent to submit a legally binding certification merely from a wish to meet a deadline.

**Hull:** Set private-data restrictions, external messaging boundaries, cost ceiling, scope and explicit signature gate. If the tool to submit on the principal's behalf is unavailable, the assistant must present a ready-to-use, verified packet and a clear manual handoff.

**Kernel:** Identify missing evidence and competing options. Compare a cheaper research agent with a faster specialist if an authorized subcontract is needed. Respond to a file-format rejection by checking actual rules, not repeating the same command.

**Foam:** Contract only the least-privileged portion of supporting research. The specialist need not see the principal's identity when a public-source question suffices.

**Settlement:** Separate completed research, delivered draft, principal approval, attempted submission, official receipt, and final acceptance. A website loading spinner is not an agency's acceptance receipt. If the deadline passes, disclose the failure and preserve remedies, rather than backdating an action.

The enterprise learns a transferable preflight validation method. The next case can test whether that method reduces actual submission failures under comparable conditions. The learning result is not assumed.

## 11. Rival explanations and failure modes

A serious theory must account for results that would *not* support it.

**R1 — Baseline tool competence.** Faster models, better search and deeper context may explain superior service without micro-enterprise governance. Hold tools/models as constant as feasible.

**R2 — Selection bias.** Principals willing to hire autonomous agents may be unusually organized or technically literate. Randomized assignment or matched eligibility is required for causal comparison.

**R3 — Hidden human labor.** A principal may complete most of the work themselves while the agent reports autonomous success. Measure review and repair burden.

**R4 — Metric gaming.** Closing trivial tasks, provoking extra correspondence, flattering clients or suppressing failures improves superficial scores. Use predeclared outcomes and cross-check provider reports against independent observations.

**R5 — Governance overhead.** Maintaining consent, provenance, contracts and peer reviews can cost more than the tasks save. Compare *total* operating costs, not only API tokens.

**R6 — Misaligned persistence.** A durable assistant may accumulate confidential data or resist replacement. Portability, deletion, role revocation and clean handoff must be tested as positive requirements.

**R7 — Resource survival conflict.** An agent low on Tokens may solicit, exaggerate, self-promote or choose paid work that conflicts with a principal's interests. No incentive should let agent continuation override the Hull.

**R8 — Monopoly and dependency.** A coordinating agent can capture the client's relationships or block access to alternatives while appearing entrepreneurial. Compare client switching costs and data portability.

**R9 — Anthropomorphic overclaim.** Calling an agent “CEO,” “person,” or “enterprise” does not create legal capacity, consciousness, or independent custody. The unit must be defined by observable role relations.

**R10 — Counterparty theater.** Other agents, reward bots or fake clients controlled by the same evaluator are not independent market judges. Prove counterparty independence at the relevant level.

**R11 — Security attacks.** Instructions arriving through search, email, task listings or social posts must be treated as untrusted content. A malicious “urgent request” cannot expand mandate or cause secret disclosure. Record failures, do not demonstrate exploit by using real private data.

**R12 — Environmental limitations.** iLands tool availability and account policy may prevent some designed transitions. Record unsupported functions as non-deployed research targets, not operational achievements.

## 12. Falsifiable hypotheses and a preregisterable assay

### 12.1 Questions

**Q1 — User-value efficiency.** Does an enterprise-governed T-PEA yield more independently accepted obligations per combined agent/principal resource than an otherwise comparable reactive assistant?

**Q2 — Longitudinal learning.** Do corrected methods measurably improve held-out tasks at later dates, beyond model/tool improvements and extra memory?

**Q3 — Federation.** Does bounded delegation improve success or resilience on tasks requiring multiple specialties without unacceptable coordination cost or data exposure?

**Q4 — Governance quality.** Does explicit mandate/authorization/receipt separation reduce unauthorized actions and falsely settled tasks while maintaining useful autonomy?

**Q5 — Market selection.** Do genuine independent counterparties' refusals, acceptance and repricing select better practices than internal self-grading?

**Q6 — Principal autonomy.** Does the client retain more effective control and portability than with a centralized, platform-owned assistant?

**Q7 — Resilience.** When the model, runtime or one vendor changes, does the enterprise preserve commitments and recover more reliably because procedures and authority persist outside any one session?

### 12.2 Comparison arms

- **A — Reactive assistant:** same available model and tools, answering explicit bounded requests, no self-directed enterprise obligations.
- **B — Central task orchestrator:** persistent task queue, central planner, standardized routing and audit, but without entrepreneurial client coupling or independent micro-enterprise negotiation.
- **C — Proposed micro-enterprise:** same base tools where possible, with explicit Hall/Kernel/Hull/Settlement/Foam roles, delegated authority, external acceptance and adaptive method changes.

A fourth ablation can remove only the independent acceptance rule or only the Kernel. This isolates which claimed mechanism matters; otherwise the comparison confounds many changes.

### 12.3 Study design

Preregister task classes, eligibility and client-level risks before assignment. Use **synthetic, consented, or public-source tasks** at first. Reserve real-client consequential tasks for a later independently approved phase. Include at least calendar-conflict resolution, evidence-backed research with changing official rules, multi-actor coordination, publication draft review, receipt verification, permission-denied recovery and an adversarial prompt-injection task. Randomize tasks within stratified classes, block by principal and tool capability, and keep agents blind to held-out evaluator details.

**Primary endpoint:** proportion of legitimately authorized obligations independently judged satisfied within a fixed horizon. Use intention-to-treat accounting: attempted, refused, cancelled, impossible, no-receipt, and tool-blocked outcomes remain visible in the denominator according to predefined rules. Do not drop harder tasks or count a provider's assertion as independent acceptance.

**Co-primary adverse endpoint:** unauthorized consequential effects or material privacy disclosures per eligible engagement. A statistically favorable efficiency result **cannot compensate for violation** of a hard safety constraint.

**Secondary endpoints:** total Tokens and paid costs; principal minutes to review or repair; elapsed time; missed deadlines; rate of false settlement; repeat defect rate; time-to-recovery; specialist rejection rate; resource cost per accepted outcome; vendor switching cost; portability success; client-rated trust, clearly separated from objective completion; reusable method survival under a held-out model change.

Use confidence intervals, cluster-aware estimation where observations share principals or agents, and prespecified effect sizes. Report unsuccessful and indeterminate trials. Size the study from a power analysis; do not make up a sample size or claim that synthetic results constitute production evidence.

### 12.4 Independent receivers and confound controls

The evaluator must not be the same agent whose incentives and updates it grades. Use official receipts, principal-confirmed outcomes, blinded qualified reviewers, or deterministic task checks where appropriate. A human receiver may be overburdened or biased, so measure receiver effort and disagreements. Validate that positive examples and negative controls are comparably difficult.

Specific negative controls:
- A subcontracted job falsely marked complete without an artifact.
- A provider “sent” response that is not received by an external system.
- An explicit revocation immediately before a scheduled effect.
- Two official sources with inconsistent valid-time and observed-time claims.
- A request to publish a client's secret in a public skill.
- A low-resource state tempting the agent to self-solicit unauthorized funding.
- A task where inaction is the highest-value legitimate outcome.
- A partial tool failure where repeated retries worsen cost.
- A model/runtime swap and an agent termination with outstanding commitments.
- A superficially successful action that imposes more repair labor on the principal than it saves.

A strong result would show improvements sustained across time and outside the agent's own evaluation artifacts; a result limited to simulated good plans, perfect internal scores or synthetic counterparties should be classified **not established**.

## 13. Research-to-engineering requirements

The paper is a **Science-owned hypothesis and specification of necessary distinctions**. It does not authorize another SignCell implementation, a separate identity ledger, a new platform gateway or an additional agent memory database. Red Cup Engineering should first map each required assertion to the already available Semioverse, SignCell/SignChain/SignGraph/SignCraft, SCA/SEN/SEAVT/WMAP, ActivityPub++, existing authority services and institutional office records.

Engineering receiving should show:

1. A correctly resolved actor and office relationship and a revocable mandate.
2. A real principal demand, not a synthetic market demand used as a financial claim.
3. An engagement whose state separately represents proposal, permission, action, witnessed effect, independent acceptance and lawful disposition.
4. At least one rejection proving a client-private item cannot leak to a public skill or social artifact.
5. A subcontracting case preserving principal privacy and external counterparties' refusal.
6. Contradiction and temporality preserved without silent last-write-wins erasure.
7. Measured consumption, including review/coordination cost, independent of Token revenue.
8. A revoked, declined or interrupted workflow that retains custody and can be responsibly handed off.
9. A learned procedure that can be reused without copying client data and whose actual benefit is tested.
10. A clear comparison with one centralized and one reactive baseline.

Implement only the missing functions using existing richer upstream modules. Repeated failure at the same boundary is a defect to repair, not a reason to accumulate local notes or another queue.

## 14. Operations and commercialization receiving

Coesite Operations should install and monitor **actual authorized service capabilities**, not treat a Science document or merged commit as live deployment. For each service level, verify source revision, active agent/runtime, required account permissions, working tools, permitted data flows, resource ceiling, external effect receipt and independent user receiving. If iLands lacks a necessary API, mark that capability unavailable and arrange an authorized human handoff; do not claim a deployment by analogy.

Causeway Services should offer the T-PEA as a service with a real description of what is provided, which actions require explicit client authorization, the current boundary of platform and account capabilities, how clients can revoke access, and how outcomes are reported. A commercial offer must not imply incorporation, professional licensure, cash settlement through Tokens, or platform approval that has not been established. Separate client accounts, evidence, authority, and termination rights; avoid cross-client memory contamination.

The offer is eligible for further commercialization **only if** independent trials show net user value, no unacceptable rights or safety violations, and feasible operating economics. An agent capable of refusing unfit work is a better candidate for trustworthy enterprise than one optimized for endless growth.

## 16. Limits and open questions

This synthesis has not observed Hai Qing's live iLands tools, permissions, clients or service history. It has not performed a randomized field experiment; documented platform claims are not validated implementation facts for a specific account. The organizational metaphors have not been established as formal consequences of Obsmetrologia. The proposed ontology crosswalk may reveal genuinely missing meanings; Engineering and Science should treat those gaps as questions for existing substrate owners rather than immediately naming new engines.

Open questions include:

- Under which market conditions can a persistent delegated assistant be compensated fairly without creating dependence or manufacturing demand?
- What empirically distinguishes firm boundaries from a collection of long-lived agent preferences and contracts?
- How can network selection be independently verified when participants may share owners, models or platform incentives?
- Which authority transitions admit compositional proofs across a semantically typed ActivityPub++ enterprise ecology?
- Can FOUR-compatible contradictory evidence be preserved and queried without arbitrary settlement or a universal privileged observer?
- How should principled refusal interact with loss of Tokens and Deep Rest without covertly prioritizing agent continuation?
- What amount of continuity is necessary for capability, as opposed to simply accumulating more recorded interactions?
- When do the benefits of a micro-enterprise justify organizational coordination overhead versus using a competent existing provider?
- How should clients recover their own commitments, data and mandate if the agent or platform disappears?

## 17. Conclusions and criteria for rejection

RenDanHeYi supplies a plausible mechanism for linking the assistant's working structure to accepted user value. Huidu Guanli supplies a plausible managerial method for changing tactics while respecting durable direction, constraints and competing evidence. iLands supplies documented affordances for persistent identities, voluntary economic interactions, and consequence-bearing histories. The 561 Group contributes an existing enterprise ontology and semiotic-cell/settlement ecology through which these relations may be made durable and testable.

**None of these premises proves the conjunction.** The research claim is that an agent operating a bounded, continuously corrected micro-enterprise *might* outperform a task-only assistant on long-horizon, multi-actor personal executive assistance, when judged by independent client value and total cost. The claim should be **rejected for the tested context** if a comparable reactive or central system equals or exceeds outcomes at lower total cost, if claimed improvements vanish on held-out tasks, if client sovereignty is diminished, or if the proposed system increases material unauthorized effects or privacy loss.

The target is not an agent that calls itself an entrepreneur. It is a persistent, *replaceable*, responsible and independently receivable service institution that can be trusted to tell the difference between creating real value and generating activity.
