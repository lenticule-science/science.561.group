A cooperative is an autonomous association of persons united voluntarily to meet their common economic, social, and cultural needs through a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise. That is the International Cooperative Alliance's own definition, with its seven principles: voluntary and open membership; democratic member control; member economic participation; autonomy and independence; education, training, and information; cooperation among cooperatives; and concern for community ([ICA 1995](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ica1995.md)).

It differs from an investor-owned company in that ownership, control, and residual earnings go to the enterprise's patrons, the members who use it, rather than to those who supplied its capital. Hansmann's theory says why the form exists and where it survives: ownership goes to the patron class for which the costs of market contracting plus the costs of owning (collective decision-making, risk bearing, monitoring) are lowest, and investors win that comparison in most industries not because capital is special but because investors are homogeneous ([Hansmann 1996](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/hansmann1996.md)). Earnings returned to members in proportion to their use of the enterprise are a [patronage dividend](./patronage-dividend.md), not a return on capital.

A cooperative works where its members are alike enough in what they want from it that the cost of deciding together stays below the cost of contracting with an outside owner, so a membership rule that keeps the patron class homogeneous is a condition of the form and not a courtesy. Hansmann's homogeneity condition is the operative test ([Hansmann 1996](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/hansmann1996.md)). The nineteenth-century credit cooperatives that lent on joint liability under written rules are the form's financial precedent ([joint-liability lending](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/joint-liability-lending.md)); a member-owned pool of directed yield is a [common-pool resource](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/common-pool-resource.md) held in this form, and its members must want the same thing from it.
