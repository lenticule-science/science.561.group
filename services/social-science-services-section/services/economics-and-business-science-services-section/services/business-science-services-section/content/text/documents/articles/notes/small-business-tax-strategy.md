## What this lesson covers

How United States federal taxes commonly work for small businesses, with state and local taxes identified separately. This lesson supplies the concepts and records needed to make informed decisions throughout the year. It does not determine the treatment of a particular transaction or replace advice based on the entity's actual facts. It covers how common [corporate structures](../../../../../services/strategy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/corporate-structure.md) are taxed, how deductions and estimated payments work, and how operational decisions affect tax reporting.

[Small Business Bookkeeping](../../../../../services/accounting-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/03/06/2026-03-06-note-on-small-business-bookkeeping.md) and [Choosing a Corporate Structure](./choosing-a-corporate-structure.md). ## How small business income is taxed

### Sole proprietorship / single-member LLC

Business profit generally flows to the owner's individual return through Schedule C. Relevant federal taxes can include:
- **Federal income tax** at the rates applicable to the owner's total taxable income.
- **Self-employment tax** on net earnings from self-employment, subject to the Social Security wage base and Medicare rules. The calculation is not simply 15.3% multiplied by Schedule C profit; Schedule SE applies the statutory net-earnings adjustment and applicable limits.
- **State income tax** (if your state has one)

### Partnership / multi-member LLC

Same as sole proprietorship, but profits are split among partners according to the operating agreement. Each partner receives a Schedule K-1 reporting their share and pays taxes on their personal return. Each partner pays self-employment tax on their share (with some exceptions for limited partners).

### S-Corporation

Income, deductions, and credits generally pass through to shareholders. A shareholder who performs services for the corporation is also an employee and must receive **reasonable compensation** subject to [payroll](../../../../../services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/payroll.md) taxes before receiving non-wage distributions in place of compensation. The IRS can reclassify distributions as wages when the facts show that they paid for services.

There is no universal salary percentage or profit threshold that makes an S-corporation election beneficial. The analysis includes reasonable compensation for the work actually performed, payroll administration, state taxes and fees, eligibility rules, retirement-plan effects, and the owner's full income-tax position. The IRS identifies duties, time and effort, training, comparable pay, and the source of gross receipts among the relevant compensation factors. See the IRS guidance on [S-corporation compensation](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues).

### C-Corporation

The corporation pays federal and any applicable state corporate income tax on its taxable income. When after-tax earnings are distributed as dividends, shareholders may also recognize dividend income. This two-level structure is commonly called "double taxation." Rates and dividend treatment depend on the tax year and shareholder's facts.

Entity choice cannot be made from the corporate rate alone. Retained earnings, distributions, shareholder eligibility, financing plans, state taxes, payroll, and exit treatment can outweigh the apparent rate comparison.

## Common deductions

A deduction reduces taxable income; it is not a dollar-for-dollar tax credit. Its effect depends on the taxpayer, entity, accounting method, limitations, and the tax to which the deduction applies. A business expense generally must be ordinary and necessary, properly classified, and adequately substantiated.

### Deductions every small business should know

| Deduction | What qualifies | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost of goods sold** | Ingredients, materials, direct [labor](../../../../../../../../sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md) | Subtracted from revenue to calculate gross profit — often a major reduction for product businesses |
| **Rent** | [Commercial lease](../../../glossary/terms/commercial-lease.md) payments | Generally deductible when paid or incurred for business property, subject to classification and related-party rules |
| **Wages and salaries** | Employee compensation | Includes employer's share of [payroll](../../../../../services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/payroll.md) taxes |
| **[Insurance](../../../glossary/terms/business-insurance.md)** | Business insurance premiums | General liability, property, workers' comp, etc. |
| **Supplies** | Office supplies, cleaning supplies, smallwares | Items used up within the year |
| **Utilities** | Electric, gas, water, internet, phone | Business portion only |
| **Professional fees** | Accountant, attorney, consultant | Must be ordinary and necessary for the business |
| **Advertising and marketing** | Ads, signage, website hosting, printed materials | Current deduction or capitalization depends on the expenditure and facts |
| **Interest** | Interest on business loans and credit | The interest portion of loan payments — not principal |
| **[Depreciation](../../../glossary/terms/depreciation.md)** | Equipment, vehicles, leasehold improvements | Spread over useful life, or accelerated via Section 179 |

### Section 179 and bonus depreciation

The cost of qualifying property is ordinarily recovered through [depreciation](../../../glossary/terms/depreciation.md) over the applicable recovery period. Section 179 and the additional first-year depreciation allowance can accelerate some deductions, but they have different eligibility rules, limits, phaseouts, ordering rules, and business-income consequences. The applicable amounts and percentages can change by tax year. Confirm them in the current [IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946) before modeling a purchase.

An accelerated deduction changes timing; it does not make the equipment free and may not be optimal when the deduction cannot be used currently or a later-year deduction is more valuable.

### Commonly missed deductions

| Deduction | What's missed |
|---|---|
| **Vehicle expenses** | Business use of a vehicle may qualify under the standard-mileage or actual-expense method. Keep contemporaneous business-purpose and mileage records, and check method-election restrictions before comparing results. |
| **Home office** | A qualifying space must satisfy the applicable exclusive-use, regular-use, and business-use rules. The simplified method and its limits should be checked for the tax year. |
| **Startup costs** | Eligible startup and organizational costs have special current-deduction, phaseout, and amortization rules; classify them separately from costs incurred after operations begin. |
| **Education and training** | Conferences, courses, books, and certifications related to the business |
| **Bank and credit card fees** | Monthly account fees, processing fees, merchant service charges |
| **Software and subscriptions** | POS software, accounting software, scheduling tools, industry subscriptions |
| **Meals** | Business-meal deductions and exclusions depend on purpose, participants, substantiation, and specific statutory limits; do not infer deductibility from a business card charge alone. |

## Quarterly estimated taxes

Individuals, including sole proprietors, partners, and S-corporation shareholders, generally make estimated payments when they expect to owe at least the applicable threshold after withholding and credits. Corporations use a different threshold and computation. Underpayment can produce a penalty even when the return balance is paid by the filing deadline. Confirm the current rules and dates through the IRS [estimated-tax guidance](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax).

### Due dates

| Quarter | Period covered | Due date |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | January 1 – March 31 | April 15 |
| Q2 | April 1 – May 31 | June 15 |
| Q3 | June 1 – August 31 | September 15 |
| Q4 | September 1 – December 31 | January 15 (following year) |

### How to calculate

Two common individual safe-harbor computations are:

1. **Prior-year safe harbor**: base required annual payments on the applicable percentage of prior-year tax.
2. **Current-year safe harbor**: base them on the applicable percentage of current-year tax.

Thresholds, higher-income rules, annualized-income treatment, withholding, and payment timing affect the calculation. Use Form 1040-ES or the corresponding corporate form for the tax year rather than treating four equal transfers as a universal rule.

### Practical approach

Ask your accountant to calculate your quarterly amounts after the first full quarter of operation. Once established, set calendar reminders and transfer the payment amount to a separate savings account each month (one-third of the quarterly amount) so the money is available when due.

## Sales tax

If your state has sales tax and you sell taxable goods, you are a tax collector — collecting from customers and remitting to the state.

### What you need to know

- **What's taxable**: Varies by state. Most states tax prepared food; some tax groceries; a few tax neither. Beverages, alcohol, and packaged goods may have different rates.
- **Collection**: Your [POS](../../../../../../../../../../operations-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/point-of-sale.md) should calculate sales tax automatically based on your location and applicable rates.
- **Remittance**: File and pay on the schedule your state assigns (monthly, quarterly, or annually based on sales volume). Late filings incur penalties and interest.
- **Trust fund liability**: Sales tax collected from customers is not your money. It's held in trust for the state. Using collected sales tax to cover operating expenses is a serious legal violation.

### Keeping sales tax separate

Some business owners keep collected sales tax in a separate bank account, transferring it out only when remitting to the state. This prevents accidentally spending money that belongs to the government.

## Tax planning throughout the year

Tax strategy is not an April activity. Decisions made throughout the year determine the tax bill.

### Timing income and expenses

Under cash-method [bookkeeping](../../../../../services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/bookkeeping.md), income is generally recognized when actually or constructively received and expenses when paid. Delaying an invoice does not defer income already received or made available without substantial restriction. A taxpayer cannot postpone recognition merely by declining to take possession of available income. See [IRS Publication 538](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p538) for accounting-method and constructive-receipt rules.

Payment timing, when property is placed in service, capitalization rules, inventory rules, advance payments, and the entity's accounting method can all change the result. Record the actual event first; make a timing decision only after identifying the rule that governs it.

### Entity review

Review the [corporate structure](../../../../../services/strategy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/corporate-structure.md) when ownership, activities, payroll, state presence, financing, or expected earnings materially change. No fixed profit threshold proves that an S-corporation election is advantageous; compare the complete compliance cost and tax treatment under the entity's actual facts.

### Retirement contributions

Self-employed individuals and small businesses may be eligible for SEP, SIMPLE, or qualified plans, including one-participant 401(k) plans. Eligibility, employee coverage, compensation definitions, deadlines, and contribution limits differ and change over time. Use the current IRS [retirement-plans guidance](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans) for plan-year limits.

Do not assume that every deductible retirement contribution also reduces self-employment tax. For an S-corporation shareholder-employee, retirement contributions are based on eligible compensation; shareholder distributions are not earned income for retirement-plan purposes.

## Guidance

- Calculate your effective tax rate: total taxes paid (income + self-employment + state) ÷ net business income. Is it what you expected?
- Model an S-corporation election only from supportable reasonable compensation, the entity's state and federal obligations, administrative cost, retirement-plan effects, and the owner's full tax position.
- Set up a system for quarterly estimated taxes: a recurring calendar event, a separate savings account funded monthly, and a relationship with an accountant who calculates the amounts.
