Social collateral is the value of a borrower's relations with other people, pledged against a loan in the sense that those people can and will impose costs on the borrower for not repaying. Besley and Coate named it in a model of group lending with joint liability: where members can sanction each other, the group's willingness to repay exceeds the sum of its members', and the sanction, not any asset, is what secures the loan ([Besley 1995](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/besley1995.md)). Stiglitz had shown the mechanism's economics: joint liability moves monitoring from a lender who cannot see how a loan is used to peers who can, at the price of risk borne by those peers ([Stiglitz 1990](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/stiglitz1990.md)).

It differs from physical collateral in that nothing is seized on default; what the lender relies on is the sanction the borrower's peers impose, and the same relations that secure the loan also carry default from one borrower to another. Besley and Coate show both faces: a member who could repay may repay for a partner who cannot, and a member who could repay may default because the partner's default makes the group's repayment pointless; which face shows depends on how severe the sanctions are ([Besley 1995](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/besley1995.md)). Ardener's catalog of rotating-association sanctions, from loss of reputation to seizure of goods, is the ethnographic record of the same collateral ([Ardener 1964](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ardener1964.md)).

A lender who cannot observe the strength of a group's sanctions cannot price social collateral, so an instrument that records the group's own crossings makes the collateral legible and priceable where the group-lending literature left it exogenous. That is the house's [tally credit](../../../../../monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tally-credit.md): the limit is a function of the counterparty's matched crossings, the record of circulation the books cannot otherwise see, rather than of a sanction the lender has to guess at; a default is held as a recorded mismatch rather than punished through the borrower's peers, so the collateral is kept without the contagion. See also [joint-liability lending](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/joint-liability-lending.md).
