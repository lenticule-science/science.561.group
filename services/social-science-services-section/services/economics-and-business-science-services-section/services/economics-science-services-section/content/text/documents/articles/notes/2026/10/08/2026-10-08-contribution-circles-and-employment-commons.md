Three organisations sit next to credit and yield protocols: one allocates a budget by peer gifting, one employs its members through a cooperative that issues a work token, one hosts other people's money. Only their front pages were read, so this note states what those pages carry and what they leave out.

## Coordinape

The welcome page says Coordinape is "a platform for DAOs to easily and fairly distribute resources to contributors", where contributors reward the value they see created and no committee votes on it ([Coordinape Docs 2026](https://docs.coordinape.com)). The epoch length, how GIVE is allocated and how it converts to funds were not on the page, and the gift-circles guide was not opened, so the allocation rule is not stated here.

## Opolis

Opolis is a member-owned employment cooperative: members hold ownership, earn $WORK tokens, receive dividends when declared and nominate candidates to the Board of Stewards; an Employment Commons LCA is employer of record for employee members, who get W-2 payroll and benefits; community membership costs a one-time 97 USD. The page states 210 million USD or more of payroll processed from 2020 to 2025 and more than 1,150 W-2s issued ([Opolis 2026](https://opolis.co)). A member count and the token's chain were not on the page. It is the one example read of a cooperative that sits between members and a regulated employer role, which is the position a Tidepool micro-enterprise account would hold toward its members.

## Open Collective

The documentation landing page describes an online funding platform for open and transparent communities and sends groups without a legal entity or bank account to a fiscal host ([Open Collective Docs 2026](https://docs.opencollective.com)). Fiscal-host fees, counts of collectives and the dissolution of the Open Collective Foundation were not on the page read. Colony and Aragon were not searched.
