## Why this is central

Fisher supplies the house's citation for the mechanism by which the [long-term debt cycle](../../../glossary/terms/long-term-debt-cycle.md)'s "ugly deleveraging" turns into a self-reinforcing spiral: the [debt deflation](../../../glossary/terms/debt-deflation.md) term rests on Fisher's chain from over-indebtedness through distress selling, monetary contraction, falling prices, and the rising real burden of fixed nominal debt, which is the mechanism that makes deflation-driven liquidation capable of worsening rather than resolving an over-indebtedness problem.

## What it does not settle

Fisher wrote in 1933 without the later apparatus of formal general-equilibrium or agent-based modeling, and his account is a verbal, sequential causal chain rather than a fully specified mathematical model with testable parameter estimates; later economists (notably Minsky, building explicitly on Fisher) extended it, and the debate over how much of a modern depression is attributable to debt-deflation dynamics specifically, versus other channels, remains open. Fisher's own proposed remedy (reflation to the price level at the crisis's onset) is a policy claim the 1933 paper does not empirically test against the depression it was written to explain.
