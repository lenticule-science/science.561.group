**Limits to Growth** names both the 1972 book by [Donella and Dennis Meadows](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/donella-and-dennis-meadows.md), Jørgen Randers, and William W. Behrens III, and the World3 system-dynamics model it was built on. World3 modeled the interaction of population, industrial output, food production, resource depletion, and pollution under a range of policy scenarios; its "standard run," commonly called the business-as-usual scenario, projected industrial output and population growing past the level the underlying resources and pollution sinks could sustain — **overshoot** — and then declining, rather than approaching a stable plateau.

The 1972 book was widely criticized on release, including by economists who argued it underestimated technological substitution and price-driven resource discovery. Independent empirical comparisons since — Graham Turner's 2008 and 2014 papers, and Gaya Herrington's 2021 update using data through 2019 — have found the observed global trajectory tracking closer to the original business-as-usual scenario than the criticism anticipated, with Herrington's update projecting a growth slowdown beginning around the 2020s to 2040s on the trend as measured.

The house reads World3's overshoot dynamic as a formal, quantitative predecessor to the declining-EROEI position held by [Nate Hagens](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/nate-hagens.md), [Richard Heinberg](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/richard-heinberg.md), and [Joseph Tainter](../../../../../../../../../history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/diminishing-returns-on-complexity.md), and alongside the [2026-09-04 note on sizing without a bank](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/04/2026-09-04-note-on-sizing-without-a-bank.md)'s concern with a rising absorbing barrier under climate breakdown, arrived at independently through decades-old, repeatedly re-tested systems modeling rather than through energy accounting.
