A **financial cascade**, in David Korowicz's sense (the 2012 Feasta / Risk-Resilience Network report *Trade Off: Financial System Supply-Chain Cross-Contagion*), is a self-reinforcing failure that propagates between two systems the 2008 crisis response treated as separable: the financial system (banks, credit, payments) and the physical, globalized supply chain (just-in-time inventory, trade finance, letters of credit, cross-border logistics). Korowicz's argument is that a severe enough financial shock does not stay contained to balance sheets: a banking-system freeze that stops trade finance and payment clearing can halt the flow of goods within days, given how thin just-in-time inventories run; halted goods flow then produces business failures, unemployment, and further loan defaults that feed back into the financial system, deepening the original shock. Because both systems are now global and tightly coupled, a shock large enough to break the loop in one place has no clean firewall to the other.

Korowicz's report argues this makes the 2008 recovery path — central banks and states backstopping liquidity until confidence and trade resumed — a poor guide to a future, more severe event: 2008's crisis did not sever the physical supply-chain links themselves, so once liquidity returned, the movement of actual goods restarted largely intact; a cascade that does break those physical links (through prolonged trade-finance freeze, cross-border logistics failure, or coordinated bank failures) would need the links themselves reconstructed, not just confidence restored, and reconstruction is a physical and organizational problem money alone cannot solve quickly.

The house reads financial cascade as one mechanism by which a [debt deflation](./debt-deflation.md) or [Minsky moment](./minsky-moment.md) confined to the financial sphere could cross into the physical economy the house's other collapse citations describe, and as a structural reason the [long-term debt cycle](./long-term-debt-cycle.md)'s "ugly deleveraging" case deserves to be modeled with supply-chain and trade-finance channels included rather than as a purely monetary event.
