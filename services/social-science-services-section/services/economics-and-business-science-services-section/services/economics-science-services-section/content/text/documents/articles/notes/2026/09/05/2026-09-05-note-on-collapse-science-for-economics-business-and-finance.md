# Collapse science for economics, business, and finance

This note reads the twelve sources newly seated in this section's glossary and
bibliography (Bardi 2017, Morgan 2013, Hall 2009, Scheffer 2009, Fisher 1933,
Korowicz 2012, Odum 1996, Motesharrei 2014, Minsky 1992, Pozsar 2022,
Georgescu-Roegen 1971, plus the previously unciteed Tainter 1988) against the
[2026-09-04 note on economics, business, and finance as a nesting](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/04/2026-09-04-note-on-economics-business-and-finance-as-a-nesting.md)'s
ruling that the three divisions nest — economics contains business contains
finance — and against the [2026-09-05 note on fintech under energy
descent](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/services/561-group-operations-services-section/services/561-group-economic-operations-services-section/services/561-group-business-operations-services-section/services/561-group-finance-operations-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-fintech-under-energy-descent.md)'s doctrine (sections 1 and 2: the house's position among
sixteen named forecasters and collapse theorists, and the weighting rule
attack zero-joule rents first, every sleeve has a collapse stage that kills
it). Each division below states what the literature argues happens to it under
energy descent, which numbered instrument in the fintech note that argument
supports or complicates, and the measurable indicators the literature proposes
for tracking it. Every claim is marked as the source's own, the fintech note's,
or this note's reading.

## 2026-10-17 development: capacity ownership is not an EROEI inference

The Leader's [flow-capacity economy research](../../10/17/2026-10-17-note-on-capacity-ownership-in-a-flow-economy.md) defines a new object of economic ownership: a share of ecologically admissible future **service capacity**, with distinct physical feasibility, institutional authority, and distributable monetary surplus. This extends the present note's question from "how does energy descent constrain the three divisions?" to "what must an economy own and reproduce if its object is durable productive capacity?" It is **not** a model-implied conclusion of Hall, Morgan, Odum, or Georgescu-Roegen. Their work motivates physical limits and accounting but does not specify the new capacity-share entitlement.

Correct inference discipline: the net-energy fraction \(1-1/\mathrm{EROEI}\) follows from a stated physical boundary; an overall economic decline, civilizational collapse, or legal entitlement does not follow from that identity alone. Absolute-net-energy-peak dates in earlier source notes are **model projections unless independently observed**, not measurements. Watershed and atmospheric service bundles must be measured by typed, coupled service constraints; EROEI cannot absorb habitat, governance, and finance into a single joule numerator. The new note states counterexamples, baseline and rights requirements, and the still-unrecovered Obsmetrologia meaning of distal bypass. This is a research development, not a revision of the historical 2026-09-05 literature readings below.

## 1. Economics: the outermost division, energy as the substrate

**What the literature says.** The economics division is where the literature
is most direct: [Hall 2009](../../../../bibliographies/hall2009.md) and the [energy
cliff](../../../../../../glossary/terms/energy-cliff.md) it names argue that net energy available to an
economy falls nonlinearly as EROI declines, so an economy dependent on a
falling average EROI faces a disproportionate, accelerating loss of surplus
rather than a smooth one. [Morgan 2013](../../../../bibliographies/morgan2013.md)'s [surplus
energy economics](../../../../../../glossary/terms/surplus-energy-economics.md) restates the same
mechanism in national-accounts terms: reported GDP overstates true,
energy-backed prosperity once the energy cost of energy is rising, because
debt-financed consumption substitutes for a shrinking real surplus without
being named as such. [Georgescu-Roegen 1971](../../../../bibliographies/georgescu-roegen1971.md)'s
[thermoeconomics](../../../../../../glossary/terms/thermoeconomics.md) supplies the reason a constraint
of this general shape should be expected at all: the economic process is a
thermodynamic transformation bounded by the second law, so perpetual material
growth is bounded by physics, not merely by present technology. [Bardi
2017](../../../../bibliographies/bardi2017.md)'s [Seneca effect](../../../../../../glossary/terms/seneca-effect.md) adds
the shape: an economy whose growth was financed by drawing down a
compounding subsidy (cheap energy, cheap credit, or both) should be expected
to decline faster than it grew, not merely to reverse its growth curve
symmetrically.

**House instrument.** This is the literature behind the fintech note's first
council position (section 1: "energy is the substrate," the founder's own
2025-12-25 oil-sanctions log) and the joule-denominated barrier (section 2:
"the barrier is denominated in joules"). It **supports** the house's decision
to size survival capital in energy units rather than dollars: if Morgan and
Hall are right that reported GDP is decoupling from energy-backed prosperity,
a dollar-denominated barrier drifts against the real hazard it is meant to
measure, while a joule-denominated one does not. It **complicates** the
fintech note's confidence in scored forecasting (section 1, the Tetlock/Silver
position) to the extent that Morgan's SEEDS figures are themselves
proprietary and not independently reproducible by the house — a scored
forecast built on an unverifiable input series is not more calibrated for
being scored.

**Indicators.**

- **Hall's EROI series** — the compiled and updated EROI-by-source literature
  (Hall's own studies and their successors). Data source: peer-reviewed
  publication (irregular; new estimates appear every few years as studies are
  published). Cadence for the house: an annual literature review rather than a
  continuously updated series, since no single body publishes a maintained
  EROI time series the way statistical agencies publish GDP.
- **Reinsurance pricing** — Swiss Re Institute's sigma report (global insured
  natural-catastrophe losses) and Munich Re's NatCatSERVICE, cross-checked
  against Artemis.bm's continuous catastrophe-bond pricing and issuance data
  (see [adaptation economy](../../../../../../glossary/terms/adaptation-economy.md)). Data source:
  Swiss Re Institute (annual sigma report, typically published in the following
  March), Artemis.bm (continuous, updated per bond issuance and renewal).
  Cadence: annual for the sigma headline figure, continuous for cat-bond
  market pricing.

## 2. Business: circulation under a shrinking surplus

**What the literature says.** [Motesharrei, Rivas, and Kalnay
2014](../../../../bibliographies/motesharrei2014.md)'s [HANDY model](../../../../../../glossary/terms/handy-model.md)
argues that within an economy facing resource constraint, the businesses and
institutions that survive longest are not determined by resource scarcity
alone but by how access to what remains is distributed: an elite population
insulated from early scarcity signals keeps consuming and investing on old
assumptions while the commoner population and the labor it supplies is
depleted, so the circulation layer (who trades with whom, on what terms)
degrades before the underlying resource stock is exhausted. [Odum
1996](../../../../bibliographies/odum1996.md)'s [emergy](../../../../../../glossary/terms/emergy.md) supplies a candidate
common unit for valuing the businesses in that circulation layer against each
other on an energy basis rather than a purely monetary one, which matters once
Morgan's argument (division 1) is taken seriously: a business's reported
revenue and its energy-backed contribution to the economy can diverge. [Bardi
2017](../../../../bibliographies/bardi2017.md)'s [Seneca effect](../../../../../../glossary/terms/seneca-effect.md) applies
again at this layer: a business ecosystem built on a compounding subsidy (cheap
credit, cheap advertising-funded acquisition, cheap energy-intensive logistics)
should decline faster than it grew once the subsidy reverses.

**House instrument.** This is the literature behind the fintech note's second
weighting (section 2: "every sleeve has a collapse stage that kills it") read
at the business layer, and behind section 3.6 ("the option register: what the
band owns that TESCREAL money must buy") — HANDY's elite/commoner asymmetry is
this note's reading of why a counter-elite's capital (TESCREAL money, in the
fintech note's terms) can keep building through a period of general resource
stress: it is structurally the insulated Elite population HANDY describes,
and the option register's method (a structural claim held against a capital
project) is one of the few instruments this note found in the corpus by which
a commoner-side actor can extract value from that elite's continued
investment rather than merely being priced out by it. **Complicates**: the
option register's own falsifier (section 3.6: "if no announced project in the
band's region carries a structural claim the band can exercise, the register
is empty and stays a watch list") means HANDY's mechanism does not
automatically produce house leverage — it produces leverage only where a
structural claim already exists, which is a narrower condition than HANDY's
general elite/commoner dynamic.

**Indicators.**

- **Turchin's political stress indicator** — see division 3 below for the
  index itself; at the business layer, the relevant proxy is elite
  overproduction measures applied to a specific sector (e.g., concentration of
  capital allocation decisions in a shrinking set of firms or funds). Data
  source: the same cliodynamics literature (Seshat databank, Turchin's own
  published series); cadence: the underlying data (wealth concentration, elite
  employment saturation) is typically annual, from national statistical
  sources.
- **The ratio of aspirational to structural claims** — the fintech note's own
  instrument (section 3.4, the claims-decay register): "the rate at which
  claims move from structural to aspirational is a collapse-stage indicator."
  Applied to the business layer, this reads as the rate at which a business's
  contractual and regulatory protections (a lease, a permit condition, a labor
  protection) are honored versus tolerated when violated. Data source: the
  house's own claims-decay register, built member by member and project by
  project; cadence: event-driven (recorded when a claim is filed or tested)
  with a periodic (the note suggests annual) audit of the aggregate ratio.

## 3. Finance: the innermost division, booking under a rising barrier

**What the literature says.** [Fisher 1933](../../../../bibliographies/fisher1933.md)'s [debt
deflation](../../../../../../glossary/terms/debt-deflation.md) argues that an over-indebted financial
system does not merely contract under stress but can enter a self-reinforcing
spiral, because the real burden of fixed nominal debt rises as prices fall.
[Minsky 1992](../../../../bibliographies/minsky1992.md)'s [Minsky moment](../../../../../../glossary/terms/minsky-moment.md)
supplies the mechanism by which a period of calm produces exactly the
over-indebtedness Fisher's spiral needs: financing structures drift from
hedge to speculative to Ponzi finance during prosperity, becoming fragile to
any reversal. [Korowicz 2012](../../../../bibliographies/korowicz2012.md)'s [financial
cascade](../../../../../../glossary/terms/financial-cascade.md) argues the resulting failure need not
stay confined to finance: a severe enough shock can propagate into globalized,
just-in-time supply chains and trade finance and then feed back into the
financial system, in a way the 2008 liquidity-backstop response would not be a
reliable guide to arresting. [Pozsar 2022](../../../../bibliographies/pozsar2022.md)'s [Bretton
Woods III](../../../../../../glossary/terms/bretton-woods-iii.md) argues the reserve-currency regime
itself is a candidate failure point: sanctions demonstrating that outside
money can be made unusable by political decision gives every reserve-holding
state reason to shift toward commodities and gold, which the house reads as
one face of the petrodollar order's end named in the fintech note's council
section.

**House instrument.** This is the literature behind the fintech note's
long-term-debt-cycle weighting (section 1: "regimes change, so hold what
survives the change," Dalio read with the petrodollar's end) and directly
behind section 3.5 (the stage ladder for sleeves, from Orlov, Greer, and
Bendell): Fisher, Minsky, and Korowicz together are this note's reading of
*why* Orlov's ladder places financial collapse first (stage one) — a
debt-deflation spiral triggered by a Minsky moment, cascading through
Korowicz's cross-contagion channel, is a specific, literature-grounded
mechanism for the abstract "faith in business-as-usual lost" Orlov's stage one
names abstractly. This **supports** the house's decision (section 3.5) to
carry a stage attribute on every sleeve and move capital down the ladder
(brokerage-held to chain-held to pool-held to physical) as indicators move,
rather than treating a financial-sleeve's venue as permanently safe. It
**complicates** the joule-denominated barrier (section 2) at exactly the
point Pozsar's argument bites: if the reserve-currency regime itself is in
transition, the exchange rate used to convert a joule-denominated barrier
into the currency a bill must actually be paid in is itself a moving,
politically contestable quantity, not a stable conversion factor.

**Indicators.**

- **Dalio's debt-cycle metrics** — aggregate debt-to-income and debt-service
  ratios, and the beautiful/ugly deleveraging classification Dalio's own
  published framework (and public dashboards) applies. Data source: BIS and
  IMF debt-to-GDP and debt-service series, cross-checked against Dalio's own
  published economic-principles material. Cadence: quarterly, matching most
  central-bank and BIS debt-data releases.
- **Scheffer's early-warning signals** — critical slowing down, rising
  variance and autocorrelation, and flickering, computed over any sufficiently
  sampled financial time series (the house's own recorded market and forecast
  series, per [critical transition](../../../../../../glossary/terms/critical-transition.md)). Data
  source: whatever series the forecasting section already records; cadence: as
  frequently as the underlying series samples — daily for market data, and the
  autocorrelation/variance statistics themselves are typically computed over a
  rolling window (weeks to months) rather than instantaneously.

## What the house believes that the literature does not settle

None of the twelve sources above proves the house's own three central claims;
each is read as support consistent with them, never as the claim's own
evidence. Stated plainly, with a falsifier for each:

- **Energy is the substrate of geopolitics, not one input among many.** The
  house holds this as a proposition, not as a finding any source above
  establishes; Hall and
  Morgan describe declining energy surplus, they do not themselves argue that
  geopolitical events are best explained by it. Falsifier: a major
  geopolitical realignment (a war, a sanctions regime, an alliance shift) that
  is better explained by ideology, personality, or non-energy resource
  competition than by energy-return dynamics, with no energy-return channel
  identified even indirectly, would count against the claim.
- **Climate outcomes will be worse than the models in current use project.**
  None of the twelve sources is a climate model; this is a house belief about
  a different literature entirely, imported into this note only because it
  sets the fintech note's "rising barrier" (section 5 of the sizing note).
  Falsifier: if the IPCC's central physical-science projections continue to
  track observed warming, sea-level rise, and extreme-event frequency within
  their stated uncertainty bands over the coming decade, rather than being
  exceeded on the high side as the house expects, the belief is wrong.
- **The pool (the tiyóšpaye/mutual-aid pool the fintech and sizing notes
  build toward) is a manufactured ensemble, and manufacturing one is possible
  without a bank.** This is the sizing note's own reading (section 4),
  extending Peters's ergodicity vocabulary and Kropotkin's mutual aid; none
  of the twelve sources in this note addresses pooling directly. Falsifier:
  if a running pool, sized and booked as the sizing note describes, fails to
  re-seed a member's ruined sleeve more often than an equivalent amount of
  money held individually would have absorbed that ruin, the pool is not
  functioning as a manufactured ensemble and the claim is wrong.

This note does not itself settle any of the three; it names them as claims
with stated falsifiers so a later note or a running instrument can test them,
rather than carrying them as unstated assumptions inside a sleeve's sizing
rule.
