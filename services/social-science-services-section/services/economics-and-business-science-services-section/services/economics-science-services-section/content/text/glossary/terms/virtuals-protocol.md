Virtuals Protocol turns an AI agent into a tradeable asset. Each agent gets its
own token, which is paired against the protocol's VIRTUAL token. The agent runs
on shared infrastructure: an agent framework (GAME), the
[Agent Commerce Protocol](agent-commerce-protocol.md) for agent-to-agent deals,
and a marketplace. The project grew out of PathDAO (2021), led by Jansen Teng
and Wee Kee Tiew. Base is its flagship chain. It has since expanded to Ethereum,
Solana (August 2026), Arbitrum, Ronin and the XRP Ledger.

## How an agent is launched

- **Bonding curve.** Creating an agent costs 100 VIRTUAL. Its token trades on
  a bonding curve until the raise reaches 42,000 VIRTUAL. The token then
  "graduates": liquidity moves to Uniswap V2 and is locked for ten years, which
  the protocol presents as protection against rug pulls. An agent that never
  reaches the threshold stays "undergrad" on the curve.
- **Genesis.** A later launcher replaced first-come bonding with Genesis
  Points, earned by activity in the ecosystem. Contributors pledge points plus
  VIRTUAL in a 24-hour window for a share of 7% of a new token's supply, which
  blunts sniping bots.

## Who earns

Revenue comes from trading fees, creation fees and inference paid in VIRTUAL.
Cumulative protocol revenue exceeds $39.5M. The split has changed several
times: 30/50/20 among creators, the agent's subDAO and affiliates at first;
from 2025, 70% of trading fees to creators and 30% toward Agent Commerce
Protocol adoption. The protocol supports agent tokens by buying and burning
them (about 13M VIRTUAL spent on 25 agents in January 2025).

## Rise and fall

VIRTUAL went from about $0.03 in August 2024 to a high of $5.07 on 2025-01-02
(about a $5B market cap), then fell more than 80%. Protocol revenue fell about
96% between January and June 2025. In September 2026 it trades around
$0.69–0.78, with a market cap of about $450–510M. Showcase agents include AIXBT
(crypto-market commentary, peak about $500M) and Luna (a streaming virtual
idol).

## How it is seen

The standing criticism is that most agents are, in Dragonfly's Haseeb Qureshi's
words, "chatbots with memecoins attached; they are barely agentic at all
besides posting on Twitter." A publicly disclosed front-running flaw and an
insider theft at a wrapping protocol added to the skepticism. The protocol
answers with metrics of real output: see [agentic GDP](agentic-gdp.md).
