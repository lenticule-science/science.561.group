## Why this is central

Pozsar supplies the house's citation for [Bretton Woods III](../../../glossary/terms/bretton-woods-iii.md): the argument that freezing Russia's foreign-exchange reserves after the 2022 invasion demonstrated that outside money (a claim on another sovereign) can be made unusable by political decision, giving reserve-holding states reason to shift toward commodities and gold, and read by the house as the monetary-regime-change face of the petrodollar order's end named elsewhere in the finance doctrine.

## What it does not settle

This is a bank research note written for Credit Suisse clients within days of the sanctions event, not a peer-reviewed or empirically tested paper; its predictive claims (a durable structural shift in reserve composition and a lasting repricing of commodities relative to financial assets) are a forecast, not a settled finding, and reserve-composition data since 2022 shows only a partial, gradual shift rather than the rapid regime change the note's framing suggests. The note is also explicitly argued from the perspective of a bank strategist positioning clients for a scenario, which is a different genre of claim than an academic finding and should be read with that incentive in view.
