## Why this is central

Minsky supplies the house's citation for [Minsky moment](../../../glossary/terms/minsky-moment.md): the argument that stability itself is destabilizing, as financing structures drift from hedge to speculative to Ponzi finance during a sustained prosperous period, becoming fragile to interest-rate rises or asset-price reversals exactly because the earlier calm removed the caution that would have prevented the drift.

## What it does not settle

The working paper is a theoretical and historically illustrated argument, not a paper with a formal, estimable statistical model that produces testable predictions about the timing of a given "Minsky moment" (a term Paul McCulley coined later, in 1998, not Minsky's own); the hypothesis has been influential but remains, by Minsky's own presentation, a qualitative account of financing-structure dynamics rather than a quantitative forecasting tool, and distinguishing hedge, speculative, and Ponzi finance in real time for a specific sector or economy is an interpretive judgment rather than a mechanically computed classification.
