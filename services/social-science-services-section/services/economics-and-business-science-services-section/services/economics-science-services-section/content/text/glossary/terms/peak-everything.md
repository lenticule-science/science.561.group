**Peak everything**, the term coined by [Richard Heinberg](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/richard-heinberg.md), generalizes peak-oil analysis beyond a single commodity: not only conventional oil, but coal, key minerals, topsoil, fresh water, and eventually global population are argued to face near-term production or availability peaks within a broadly overlapping period, making the twenty-first century better understood as a century of declines across several resource dimensions at once rather than a single-commodity transition problem.

The thesis depends on treating declining net energy return — see [energy return on investment](./energy-return-on-investment.md) — as the deeper mechanism, since a shrinking energy surplus undermines the technological-substitution and efficiency-gain arguments typically raised against single-resource depletion concerns: substitution and efficiency improvements themselves require energy surplus to fund, so a general decline in that surplus constrains the response to any one resource's depletion as well as the resource itself.

Peak everything sits alongside [limits to growth](./limits-to-growth.md) as a qualitative and a quantitative statement, respectively, of the same overshoot concern, and is read by the house as one of its three central citations — together with [Nate Hagens](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/nate-hagens.md) and [Joseph Tainter](../../../../../../../../../history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/glossary/terms/diminishing-returns-on-complexity.md) — for its own declining-EROEI position.
