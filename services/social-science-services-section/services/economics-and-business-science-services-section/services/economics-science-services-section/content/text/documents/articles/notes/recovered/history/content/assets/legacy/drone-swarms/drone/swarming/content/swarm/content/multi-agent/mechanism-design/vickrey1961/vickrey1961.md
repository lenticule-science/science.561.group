# Vickrey 1961 — Auctions

Vickrey, W.
(1961).
Counterspeculation, Auctions, and Competitive Sealed Tenders. *Journal of Finance*, 16(1), 8-37.

The paper introducing the second-price sealed-bid auction (now called the *Vickrey auction*) and proving its central property: truthful bidding is a *dominant strategy* — the bidder optimum regardless of others' bids.

The result was foundational for mechanism design:

- It demonstrated that *truthful* mechanisms are achievable in non-trivial settings.
- It seeded the *Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG)* family of mechanisms generalizing the result to combinatorial settings.
- It established the *strategic-equivalence* analysis pattern (English ascending and Vickrey have equivalent outcomes under standard assumptions).

Vickrey received the Nobel Prize in Economics (1996), shared with James Mirrlees, partly for this work.

The paper's formal apparatus is straightforward; its significance is conceptual — establishing that mechanism design could produce rules with strong incentive properties, not just describe equilibria of given games.
