Joint-liability lending is lending to a group whose members are each liable for the others' loans, so that the group's repayment, not the individual's, is what the lender collects on. Ghatak and Guinnane organize its economics by the four problems a lender faces: adverse selection, which the group answers by sorting into like-risk groups; moral hazard, which peers answer by monitoring; costly state verification, which peers answer because they know whether a partner truly failed; and enforcement, which peers answer with pressure ([Ghatak 1999](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ghatak1999.md)). The nineteenth-century German credit cooperatives did all four under written rules, so the mechanism does not depend on village life ([Ghatak 1999](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ghatak1999.md)). Stiglitz's peer-monitoring model gives the price: the risk the lender would otherwise bear is moved onto the peers who can see ([Stiglitz 1990](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/stiglitz1990.md)).

It differs from individual lending in that the borrowers, not the lender, select, monitor, verify, and enforce on one another, and from a rotating association in that an outside lender supplies the capital. The [rotating savings and credit association](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rotating-savings-and-credit-association.md) needs no lender because the members are the fund; joint liability exists to let an outside lender use the same [social collateral](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/social-collateral.md). The empirical record on whether joint liability outperforms individual liability once meetings, dynamic incentives, and public repayment are separated out is mixed ([Ghatak 1999](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ghatak1999.md)).

The four jobs joint liability gives the group, selecting members, monitoring effort, verifying failure, and enforcing repayment, are the jobs any pool's draw rule must do, and the house pool does them with records rather than with punishment of a defaulter's peers. In the house pool a default is a mismatch held in FOUR: both witnesses are recorded, the line closes, and the unmatched half stays on the worklist ([tally credit](../../../../../monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tally-credit.md)); no other member's line is reduced by it, which removes the contagion Besley and Coate identified ([Besley 1995](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/besley1995.md)) at the cost of the peer pressure Ghatak and Guinnane count as enforcement.
