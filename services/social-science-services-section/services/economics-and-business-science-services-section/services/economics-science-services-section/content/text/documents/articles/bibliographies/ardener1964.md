## Why this is central

Ardener supplies the definition the house uses for the institution: "an association formed upon a core of participants who agree to make regular contributions to a fund which is given, in whole or in part, to each contributor in rotation." Her comparative survey catalogs the variations the definition admits (the order fixed, drawn by lot, or auctioned; the fund given whole or split; interest charged or not; a fixed or an open membership; the organizer paid or not) and the sanctions that hold members to their contributions (loss of reputation, exclusion from future rounds, seizure of goods, and in some societies legal action). It is the carrier for the fact that the design space of a pool is already mapped and that every variant has a documented enforcement mechanism.

## What it does not settle

The survey is a typology, not a theory: it does not say which variant is chosen under which conditions, or why some associations fail. Its enforcement catalog is of social sanctions among people who know each other; it cannot speak to a membership whose relations are recorded on a ledger rather than lived, so the house's choice to hold a default as a recorded mismatch rather than to sanction is outside its evidence.
