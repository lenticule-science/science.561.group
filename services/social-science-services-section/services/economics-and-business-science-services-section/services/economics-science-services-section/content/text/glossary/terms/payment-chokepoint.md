A payment chokepoint is a position within a payment or banking network through which a disproportionate share of transactions must route, such that an actor able to influence the operator of that position can deny network access to any other actor who depends on it, without needing to control every node in the network. [Farrell and Newman](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/farrellnewman2019.md) name this the chokepoint effect: network structures are typically asymmetric rather than evenly distributed, correspondent-banking and card-clearing networks being their central illustration, and a state with jurisdiction over a hub, the United States over dollar clearing and the major card networks concretely, can exercise or threaten to exercise chokepoint leverage without transacting with the ultimate target at all.

A payment chokepoint differs from financial deplatforming as a structural position differs from an action taken from that position: the same chokepoint, once documented, can be exercised against many different targets over time, while a single deplatforming event affects one target at one moment. It differs from ordinary market concentration in that market concentration is a competitive-structure fact about who serves the most customers, while a chokepoint is specifically a position whose value to a coercive actor is the power to exclude, independent of the excluding actor's own market share; see [financial-deplatforming](./financial-deplatforming.md).

A donation-funded communication collective that depends on one payment processor, one card network, and one bank for the near-totality of its donation intake has, whether or not it has named it this way, concentrated its funding through a small number of chokepoints, each independently sufficient to cut off its funding if that intermediary chooses or is induced to withdraw service. Diversifying across payment rails, multiple processors, multiple banking jurisdictions, non-custodial or cash-adjacent channels, is the direct mitigation this structural fact implies, at the cost of the operational complexity each additional rail adds; see [donation-funded-infrastructure](./donation-funded-infrastructure.md).
