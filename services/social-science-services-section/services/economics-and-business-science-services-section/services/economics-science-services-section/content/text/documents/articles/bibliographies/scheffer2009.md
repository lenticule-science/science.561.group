## Why this is central

Scheffer et al. supply the house's citation for [critical transition](../../../glossary/terms/critical-transition.md): the argument that critical slowing down, rising variance and autocorrelation, and flickering are generic mathematical signatures of an approaching regime shift, detectable without a full mechanistic model of the specific system. This is the house's proposed instrument for detecting that a collapse sequence named elsewhere in this bibliography (catabolic collapse, structural-demographic cycles, five stages of collapse) has begun before its later, more visible stages arrive.

## What it does not settle

Early-warning signals are generic diagnostics, not predictions of timing or magnitude: a rising-autocorrelation signal indicates weakening stability but not when a transition will occur, how severe it will be, or which of several possible alternative states the system will move to. The signals can also be confounded by noise characteristics unrelated to an approaching transition, and detecting them reliably in short or noisy real-world time series (financial, social, or political data, as opposed to the controlled ecological systems much of the empirical validation comes from) remains an active methodological problem the paper does not resolve.
