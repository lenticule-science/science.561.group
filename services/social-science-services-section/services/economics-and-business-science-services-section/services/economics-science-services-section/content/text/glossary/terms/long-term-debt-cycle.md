The **long-term debt cycle**, in [Ray Dalio](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/ray-dalio.md)'s framework, is a cycle of debt accumulation relative to income lasting roughly fifty to seventy-five years — much longer than the familiar short-term business cycle of roughly five to eight years, which is governed primarily by central-bank interest-rate policy. Over the long cycle, an economy's aggregate debt grows faster than incomes for a sustained period, financed by rising asset values and increasingly easy credit, until debt-service burdens become unsustainable relative to income and the cycle ends in a deleveraging event.

Dalio distinguishes two deleveraging types by how the associated pain is distributed and paced: a **beautiful deleveraging** balances austerity, debt restructuring, wealth transfer through taxation, and money printing so that nominal growth stays positive while debt burdens fall relative to income; an **ugly deleveraging** fails to balance these levers and produces depression (if debt restructuring and printing are too limited) or hyperinflation (if printing dominates).

The house reads the long-term debt cycle as the institutional-finance version of the pattern the [2025-12-25 oil-sanctions log](../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/logs/personal-logs/2025/12/25/2025-12-25-emsenn-personal-log-about-latest-oil-sanctions.md) reads out of energy sanctions: that finance converts real shortages and declining returns into deferrable, legible risk that can be "carried on balance sheets instead of resolved at the level of production or consumption." An ugly deleveraging is the point at which that deferral stops working; see [risk of ruin](../../../../../finance-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/risk-of-ruin.md) and [absorbing barrier](../../../../../finance-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/absorbing-barrier.md) for the house's vocabulary for the same failure mode at the scale of an individual or a strategy rather than a whole economy.
