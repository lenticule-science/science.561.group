A common-pool resource is a resource from which it is costly to exclude users and whose units, once taken by one user, are not available to another. Fisheries, groundwater basins, grazing meadows, and irrigation systems are the classic cases; a fund that members can draw from is one too, because a dollar drawn is a dollar no other member can draw. Hardin's essay is the claim that such a resource, left to its users, is overused by each acting rationally, so "freedom in a commons brings ruin to all" ([Hardin 1968](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/hardin1968.md)).

It differs from a public good in that its units are subtractable, and from open access in that a common-pool resource may be governed by rules its users make and enforce. Ostrom's field cases are the rebuttal: Hardin described open access, and the users of alpine meadows, Japanese village commons, Spanish irrigation, and Californian groundwater basins governed their resources for centuries by rules of their own making ([Ostrom 1990](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ostrom1990.md)).

The eight design principles Ostrom found in long-enduring cases are the checklist a pool's governance is measured against: bounded membership, rules fit to conditions, member-made rules, accountable monitoring, graduated sanctions, cheap dispute resolution, recognized autonomy, and nesting. Ostrom states them as regularities of the surviving cases, not as a guarantee ([Ostrom 1990](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ostrom1990.md)), and her Nobel lecture names the general form the eighth principle points at, polycentric governance: many formally independent centers of decision, nested, each making rules close to the resource it uses ([Ostrom 2010](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ostrom2010.md)). For the house pool, membership admission is the boundary, the barrier series is the rule fit to conditions, the two-witness books are the accountable monitoring, and the open question the principles leave is whether a default held as a recorded contradiction ([tally credit](../../../../../monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tally-credit.md)) can stand in for graduated sanctions. A shared pot allocated among projects by contribution is the related problem [quadratic funding](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/quadratic-funding.md) addresses.
