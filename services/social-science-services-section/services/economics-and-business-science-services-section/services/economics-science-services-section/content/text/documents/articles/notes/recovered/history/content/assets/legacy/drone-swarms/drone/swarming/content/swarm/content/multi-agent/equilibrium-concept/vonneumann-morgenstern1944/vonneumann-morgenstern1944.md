# Von Neumann & Morgenstern 1944 — Theory of Games

Von Neumann, J., & Morgenstern, O.
(1944). *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*.
Princeton University Press.

The founding work of modern game theory.
Von Neumann & Morgenstern established:

- **Expected utility theory.** Axiomatic derivation of utility functions from preference orderings under uncertainty (the VNM utility theorem).
- **Two-player zero-sum games.** The minimax theorem showing existence of optimal mixed strategies.
- **Cooperative game theory.** Formal treatment of coalitions, characteristic functions, and active imputations.
- **The framing.** Strategic interaction among rational agents as a *mathematical* subject distinct from decision theory and economics.

The book reframed economics as a strategic-interaction discipline, enabling the formal multi-agent reasoning that subsequent game theory built on.
Nash, Selten, Harsanyi, and many others built on this substrate.

For multi-agent systems specifically, this work is the *deepest* substrate: the analytical vocabulary of utility, strategy, equilibrium, and rationality that all subsequent multi-agent analysis presupposes.
