An **industrial prototype** is the first artifact built to a design in a firm that produces commodities for sale by a series process. It exists to answer questions about the design before the firm commits fixed capital to tooling: dies, molds, jigs, fixtures and the line itself. It is made outside the production line, by hand or in a toolroom, and its unit cost is high because no series amortizes it. It is not sold as the commodity.

## What the prototype does
- **It tests the design while change is cheap.** The cost of changing a design rises as the work moves toward tooling and series output, because tooling is fixed capital sunk in the design as it stood. The prototype is the cheapest place to find that the design is wrong.
- **It demonstrates the design to the holders of capital.** The prototype is shown to the investor, the buyer's engineers or the client so that capital is committed to the series. Its exchange value derives from the series it promises. It is a [capability demo](./capability-demo.md) of the design.
- **It is followed by a chain it does not replace.** In the stage-gate model of product development (Cooper, 1990), the prototype sits between the business case and the validated, tooled product. The stages after it are design verification and validation, a pilot run, first-article inspection and release to series. Standards write the chain down: ISO 9001:2015, clause 8.3, requires design and development outputs to be verified and validated before release, and US FDA design controls (21 CFR 820.30) require the same for medical devices.
- **It is discarded, kept as evidence, or derived from.** Its disposition is part of its definition. A prototype never becomes the product by being shipped.

## Why industrial capital separates it
Under [real subsumption](./real-subsumption.md), capital reorganizes the [labor process](./labor-process.md) as a machine system, and the design of work is separated from its execution ([scientific management](./scientific-management.md); Braverman, 1974). The prototype is the artifact of the design side. The tooled line is the execution side. The separation is what lets the firm learn from a cheap object before it spends on an expensive one, and it is why a product that skips the line and reaches the buyer as its own prototype is treated by the industry as a failure of the production system, not as an early release.
