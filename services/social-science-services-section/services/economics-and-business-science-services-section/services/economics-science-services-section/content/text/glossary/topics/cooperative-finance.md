Cooperative finance gathers the instruments in which the members are the fund: a group that pools fixed contributions and hands the pot around in turn, the accumulating savings group with its ledger and social fund, the lending group whose members are liable for each other, the pool whose draws are limited by a member's record of settled crossings, and the cooperative that owns all of it as its patrons and returns the surplus by patronage. It also gathers what such a fund must decide once it holds a surplus (a reserve, a float, a split among projects, a prize) and the governance a pot held in common needs, read against Ostrom's design principles and Hardin's objection. The poverty premium sits here because it is the return these instruments earn: the fee a member pays for lacking a float is the fee the fund keeps when it supplies one.

The topic sits in economics rather than in finance because its subject is the allocation of a shared resource among people in economic relation, and the finance section's instruments (sizing, ruin, venue mechanics) apply within each sleeve rather than to the pool as a whole. Its theory runs from the ethnography of rotating associations through the group-lending models of the 1990s to public-goods mechanism design, and its practice, for the house, is the pool: Tidepool's engineering rests on these terms, the mutual-aid and emergent-strategy carriers in sociology supply the register it is said in, and the monetary-economics section's tally and wit supply the unit its draw rule is priced in.
