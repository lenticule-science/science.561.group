An overseas warehouse (海外仓) is a warehouse in the destination country from which a cross-border seller fulfills orders locally. The seller ships stock in bulk, it is entered through customs once as an ordinary import, and each customer order then ships domestically from the warehouse. The alternative it replaces is shipping each order as its own parcel from the home country.

Chinese sources price the service as a stack: inbound receiving (入库费), SKU measurement, outbound handling (出库费), sortation, storage (仓储费) and last-mile postage ([Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/678327501)). In the United States the same work is sold by third-party logistics firms (3PLs) at, by 2026 search synthesis, $2–15 an order for pick-and-pack depending on volume, $18–25 a pallet a month for storage, and monthly minimums of $500 or more ([Fulfill.com](https://www.fulfill.com/3pl-pricing)).

Two changes made it the default for Chinese sellers into the US. The US ended duty-free entry for low-value parcels (for China and Hong Kong on 2025-05-02, for every country on 2025-08-29), so per-parcel shipping lost its cost advantage. And China began refunding export VAT when goods leave for an overseas warehouse (from 2025-01-27), rather than only once sold ([chukou1](https://www.chukou1.com/News/NewsDetails.aspx?id=1840)). Temu's US Local Seller Program and Shein's US distribution centers are the large-platform forms of the same move.

An overseas warehouse moves [inventory risk](./inventory-risk.md) into the destination market: the seller now owns unsold stock abroad and needs someone there to receive returns, count stock and deal with the warehouse in its language.
