## Why this is central

A cluster-randomized trial of village savings and loan associations across Ghana, Malawi, and Uganda, the largest experimental evidence on savings groups: membership raised savings and access to credit, improved business outcomes and households' resilience to shocks, and shifted decision-making within households, while effects on income and consumption were small. The house cites it as the measured ceiling on what a pool of small contributions does for its members: it makes them more resilient and more liquid, and it does not by itself make them richer, which is why the house pool's return is stated as fees avoided and floats supplied rather than as growth.

## What it does not settle

The groups studied are facilitated, meeting weekly, in rural low-income settings; the trial says nothing about a dispersed membership on a ledger, about groups whose pot is yield, or about high-income-country fee schedules. Its outcomes were measured about three years after formation, so long-run persistence is unknown.
