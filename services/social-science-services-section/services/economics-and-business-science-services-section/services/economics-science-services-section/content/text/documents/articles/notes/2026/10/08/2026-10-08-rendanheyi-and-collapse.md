This note records what the sources establish about Haier's Rendanheyi micro-enterprise model and what was looked for but not reached on collapse and complexity (Tainter). ## What Rendanheyi is, from Haier's own text

Haier Smart Home Annual Report 2025 (the report for financial year 2024, full text) defines the model: "Ren" refers to creators, "Dan" to user value, "Heyi" to the integration of values realised by employees and the values created for users; employees become creators and implementers become entrepreneurs, and the enterprise becomes an open ecosystem platform. The report says Rendanheyi is applied to acquired companies as a value-added sharing mechanism for the whole-process team under a common goal. Financials in the same report: 2024 revenue of RMB 285.971 billion, up 4.3 percent from RMB 274.198 billion (restated) in 2023; profit attributable to owners RMB 18.741 billion, up 12.9 percent; 122,733 employees at the end of 2024 against 112,458 a year before. The report contains no count of micro-enterprises or ecosystem micro-enterprise communities.

Haier Group 2015 is Haier's press release at the model's tenth anniversary: the model was adopted in 2005; almost 200 internal micro-enterprises had been established, 77 percent of which realised more than CNY 100 million in annual revenue in 2014; 2014 profit was twelvefold the pre-transformation level, a 28 percent compound annual growth rate over nine years. Phase 2.0 turns Haier into a co-creation platform of micro-enterprises in which the company is a shareholder of its micro-enterprises and employees are entrepreneurs with decision-making authority who can distribute benefits. Haier Global News 2016, a Haier news item on Gary Hamel's visit, says that after eliminating bureaucracy the platform had accumulated more than 3,800 nodes of micro-enterprise, and reports Hamel's view that decisions taken only at the top of a hierarchy make failure inevitable. All three are self-reported by Haier; no independent audit of the micro-enterprise revenue figures was read.

Frynas et al. 2018 (abstract) describe Rendanheyi as a platform of management practices through which Haier transformed from a hierarchical manufacturer to a responsive online entrepreneurial company, and argue that organizational, competitive, institutional and technological contexts shaped it, so such innovations are context-dependent.

## Looked for and not found

Hamel and Zanini's own texts (an SSRN paper, a London Business School Review piece and an MIT Innovations article) are listed in the register but the PDFs returned a bot check and the records carry no abstract, so Hamel is read here only through Haier's account of his visit. Tainter's The Collapse of Complex Societies (1988) appears in the register only as book reviews; his 2000, 2006 and 2011 articles are closed (no abstract at the publisher, Semantic Scholar or Unpaywall); Tainter and Patzek, Drilling Down, is listed but not readable; Turchin and Nefedov, Secular Cycles, is listed but not read. A web search for an open copy of the mid-1990s chapter reached no text. No empirical test of declining marginal returns to complexity was readable. No independent measurement of micro-enterprise performance (survival, failure rate) at Haier was found.

## What a pooling instrument can do with this

- The only quantitative Haier evidence is the company's own: 200 micro-enterprises in 2015, 3,800 nodes by 2016, with the group's headcount still rising to 122,733 in 2024. A micro-enterprise unit with its own revenue, a shared platform and a shareholder relation between platform and unit is a described structure; its success rates are unmeasured in the sources read.
- Haier's phase 2.0 relation, the platform as shareholder in its micro-enterprises, maps to a pool holding a stake in member units; the sources give no terms.
- Tainter's marginal-return argument could not be read, so no claim about complexity costs of a pool's governance layers is drawn from it here.
