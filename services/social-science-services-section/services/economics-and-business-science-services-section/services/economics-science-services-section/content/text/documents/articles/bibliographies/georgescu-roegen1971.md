## Why this is central

Georgescu-Roegen supplies the house's citation for [thermoeconomics](../../../glossary/terms/thermoeconomics.md): the argument that the economic process is a thermodynamic transformation of low-entropy matter and energy into high-entropy waste, bounded by the second law rather than only by current technology or policy, which the house reads as the theoretical root beneath its energy-return-on-investment and emergy citations.

## What it does not settle

Georgescu-Roegen's proposed "fourth law" — that matter, like energy, cannot be recycled with perfect efficiency — is contested by physicists as an overextension of the entropy concept, since entropy as conventionally defined applies to energy and its availability for work, not directly to the dispersal of matter in the way his fourth law asserts; mainstream physics does not recognize a matter-entropy law of the kind he proposed. The book's broader claim that continuous material growth is thermodynamically bounded is separate from and does not depend on the contested fourth law, but the two are sometimes conflated in secondary discussion, and the house treats the fourth law as the weaker, contested part of the argument.
