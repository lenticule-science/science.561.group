## Why this is central

This is the paper that gives the rotating savings and credit association a model. A group of individuals who each want an indivisible durable good contribute a fixed sum each period to a pot that one member takes; under autarkic saving each would wait the full saving time, while in the association every member but the last receives the good sooner and no one later, so the association is a Pareto improvement made from nothing but the agreement to pool. The paper distinguishes the random association (order drawn by lot) from the bidding association (order sold to the member who bids the most, so the pot goes to the member who values earliness most) and compares both with a credit market, showing when the association is preferred. It is the house's source for the claim that pooling creates value from the timing of indivisible needs, which is the timing float's economics stated in a model, and for the fact that the draw order is itself an allocation problem with at least two lawful solutions.

## What it does not settle

The model has no default: members are assumed to keep contributing after they have taken the pot, and the paper says explicitly that enforcement is left to social sanction outside the model. It has no risk, no shocks to income, and no barrier below which a member is ruined, so it cannot say how a pool should size draws for members who face those. It also models a single indivisible good of common value; a pool whose members' needs differ in kind (rent, a repair, a deposit) is outside the model, which is exactly where a priced draw rule has to do work the fixed rotation cannot.
