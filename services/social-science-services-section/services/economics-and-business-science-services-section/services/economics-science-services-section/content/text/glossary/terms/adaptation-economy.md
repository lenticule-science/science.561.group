The **adaptation economy** names businesses whose growth is driven directly by physical and financial decline rather than by growth in the underlying real economy: insurers repricing or withdrawing from catastrophe-exposed markets, reinsurers and capital markets pricing and trading that repriced risk, and publishers and consultancies selling literacy in collapse itself. Confirmed first-party cases:

- **Insurance retreat.** On May 26, 2023, State Farm General Insurance Company announced it would stop accepting new applications for all business and personal-lines property insurance in California, effective the next day, citing (in the company's own words) "historic increases in construction costs outpacing inflation, rapidly growing catastrophe exposure, and a challenging reinsurance market." Existing homeowners policies were not cancelled by this action and personal auto was unaffected; the withdrawal was from new business only, but it marked one of the largest US insurers ceasing new underwriting in the country's most populous state over wildfire-driven cost.
- **Reinsurance repricing.** Swiss Re Institute's sigma research estimated global insured natural-catastrophe losses at roughly USD 135–137 billion for 2024 (Artemis.bm reporting of the Swiss Re estimate, December 2024); a later report placed the fuller 2024 figure at USD 146 billion. The house has not read the sigma report itself directly and states the range rather than a single figure for that reason. Reinsurers price and hold this rising loss experience, and repriced reinsurance cost is itself the reinsurance-market pressure State Farm cited.
- **Catastrophe bonds.** The outstanding catastrophe-bond market reached an all-time high of approximately USD 49.44 billion at year-end 2024, up roughly 10% on the year, on record full-year new issuance of USD 17.7 billion (Artemis.bm, December 2024) — capital markets, not insurers' own balance sheets, absorbing an increasing share of the priced catastrophe risk.
- **Weather derivatives, water-rights markets, and collapse-literacy publishing** are named here as the same structural pattern — a market created or enlarged by increasing climate and resource volatility — without a first-party case confirmed to house standard in this pass; a specific instrument, price series, or publisher figure for each is left to a later note rather than asserted here.

The house reads the adaptation economy as the business-layer instrument the [2026-09-05 note on fintech under energy descent](../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-fintech-under-energy-descent.md)'s weighting rule (section 2) sorts by energy cost: reinsurance and catastrophe-bond pricing are close to pure rent on volatility (a spread collected for bearing a risk, at near-zero energy cost to the collector) and so, on that rule, survive energy descent better than the physical margins (repair, rebuilding, propane delivery) an insurer's payout ultimately funds. [Energy cliff](./energy-cliff.md) and [critical transition](./critical-transition.md) name the physical mechanism the priced risk is a claim on; [debt deflation](./debt-deflation.md) and [financial cascade](./financial-cascade.md) name what happens if the pricing itself fails faster than the physical risk it is meant to track.
