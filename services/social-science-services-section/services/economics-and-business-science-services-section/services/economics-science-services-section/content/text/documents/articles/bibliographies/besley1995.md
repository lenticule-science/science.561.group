## Why this is central

This is the paper that names social collateral and shows both of its faces. Under group lending with joint liability a borrower who could repay may repay on behalf of a partner who cannot, so the group repays where individuals would default; but a borrower who could repay may also default because the partner's default makes the group's repayment pointless, so joint liability can also lower repayment. Which face shows depends on the social sanctions members can impose on each other: where sanctions are strong the group's willingness to pay exceeds the sum of its members', and the sanction, not the loan, is the collateral. The house cites it for the exact claim that a relation among members can secure credit the way an asset does, and for the warning that the same relation transmits default.

## What it does not settle

The model treats social sanctions as an exogenous penalty of given severity; it does not say where the sanction comes from or how a lender could observe its strength, which is the gap a ledger of matched crossings is meant to fill. It is a two-borrower model with a single project each, and its welfare comparisons hold only under its distributional assumptions. Nothing in it prices the collateral: it establishes that social collateral exists, not how large a line it supports.
