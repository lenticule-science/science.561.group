Chuhai (出海, literally "going out to sea") is the Chinese business term for a firm taking its products, brand or operations out of China into foreign markets. It is broader than exporting. It covers a factory selling abroad under its own brand instead of through a foreign buyer, an e-commerce seller opening stores on foreign marketplaces, an app or game publishing abroad, and a firm setting up foreign entities, warehouses and staff.

The term also names a policy. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology opened a special service action for small and medium firms going abroad in January 2025. More than twenty provinces and cities run their own support programs, and the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) lists going-global support for small firms among its priorities ([Beijing Review, 2026-09-21](http://www.bjreview.com/Latest_Headlines/202609/t20260921_800447225.html)). China's tax authority has refunded export VAT on goods shipped to an [overseas warehouse](./overseas-warehouse.md) since 2025-01-27.

What a firm going abroad needs from the destination market, and cannot supply from China, is the subject of [the 2026-09-26 note on what Chinese firms going abroad buy from US residents](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-what-chinese-firms-going-abroad-buy-from-us-residents.md): people and entities located in the market who can hold legal roles, import, warehouse, sell, speak and sign there.

The concept's boundaries: chuhai is not 走出去 (zǒuchūqù, "going out"), the older state strategy (from about 2000) of outward direct investment by large, mostly state-owned firms; chuhai is used above all for private firms, often small, reaching foreign customers. It is not export processing, where a Chinese factory makes goods to a foreign buyer's order and the buyer does the selling; a firm going abroad sells for itself.
