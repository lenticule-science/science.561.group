A **capacity share** is a fractional, bounded claim on a specified portion of a system's ability to deliver a *typed service* over a period while satisfying physical, ecological and legitimate governance constraints. It does not convey ownership or control of the underlying river, watershed, atmospheric circulation, land title, Indigenous jurisdiction, equipment or extraction license. A legally enforceable contractual share may itself count as a form of property; the distinction is from **underlying asset ownership**, not from every kind of legal property.

**Proposed model:** A tranche identifies a source network, period, service, unit, feasible capacity envelope, shared dependencies, rights and consent, expiry, monitoring, payment source and receivers. For each nonoverlapping issued tranche, holder fractions obey \(0\le s_i\le1\) and \(\sum_i s_i\le1\). Cross-tranche shared constraints still need a joint feasibility test; the sum rule alone does not prevent double issuance. Services such as firm electrical energy, flood attenuation and habitat protection cannot be added as if they were the same measured quantity.

The realized service **flow** need not equal the capacity available beforehand. Theoretical machine **capability** is not feasible system capacity. A trading price for a share is not a measured change in capacity. A share's revenue distribution may depend on a verified ecological floor and surplus after maintenance, reserves and liabilities; a positive payout is neither guaranteed nor sufficient evidence of stewardship.

**Scientific status: proposed, not a settled legal instrument.** The model must demonstrate independent data, accounting boundaries, local authority, lawful transfers, additionality where claimed, and non-domination. The statement that energy underlies production does not mathematically derive an entitlement.
