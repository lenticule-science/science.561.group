## Why this is central

This paper supplies the house's citation for the nonlinear shape of net energy loss as EROI falls, which is the mechanism underlying both [energy return on investment](../../../glossary/terms/energy-return-on-investment.md)'s "net energy cliff" and the dedicated [energy cliff](../../../glossary/terms/energy-cliff.md) term: because the net energy fraction available to society is 1 minus the reciprocal of EROI, the same absolute drop in the ratio removes disproportionately more usable surplus once EROI is already low, which is exactly the range many alternative and late-stage conventional energy sources occupy.

## What it does not settle

EROI figures vary substantially by system boundary: whether a study counts only wellhead energy, extraction to point-of-use, or full societal delivery cost changes the ratio for the same underlying resource by a large factor, and the paper's own minimum-EROI threshold for "a sustainable society" is a modeled estimate under stated assumptions about what a modern society needs, not a directly measured universal constant. The paper does not settle which boundary convention is correct, and comparisons across studies that use different boundaries are not directly comparable without adjustment.
