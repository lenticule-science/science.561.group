Donation-funded infrastructure is digital infrastructure — software, hosting, or a communication service — that is sustained primarily by voluntary financial contributions from users or sympathetic third parties rather than by a market price charged for the service or a wage paid to those maintaining it. Eghbal's 2016 report for the Ford Foundation documents that the software libraries underlying most commercial and public digital infrastructure are frequently produced and maintained by small numbers of unpaid or under-resourced volunteers, and her 2020 study of open-source maintenance finds the same pattern holds structurally: a small number of maintainers manage a much larger number of users and occasional contributors.

Donation-funded infrastructure differs from commons-based peer production in what each names: commons-based peer production names the mode of coordinating labor to build the good, while donation-funded infrastructure names one financing arrangement for sustaining that good once built, and the two commonly but not necessarily co-occur. It differs from advertising-funded or subscription-funded infrastructure in that its revenue is voluntary and unlinked to any specific transaction or feature, which is also what makes its revenue structurally unpredictable in a way a metered subscription is not; see [commons-based-peer-production](./commons-based-peer-production.md).

Eghbal's maintainer-to-user ratio finding states the operational risk directly: a small number of people carry disproportionate responsibility for infrastructure whose failure would affect a much larger population, so donation-funded infrastructure's central fragility is not usually the total amount of money raised but the small number of people whose departure, burnout, or incapacity the funding model has not planned to absorb. A donation-funded communication collective additionally depends on the payment rails, the bank accounts and card processors, through which its donations arrive, which is the dependency [financial-deplatforming](./financial-deplatforming.md) and [payment-chokepoint](./payment-chokepoint.md) name as a distinct point of failure from the labor-supply fragility Eghbal documents.
