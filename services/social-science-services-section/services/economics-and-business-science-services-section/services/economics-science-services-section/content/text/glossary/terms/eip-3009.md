EIP-3009 — its own specification titles it "ERC-3009: Transfer With Authorization," and the two names circulate interchangeably in the wild — is an Ethereum standards-track proposal, read directly from `eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-3009` this pass, authored by Peter Jihoon Kim, Kevin Britz, and David Knott, and still formally in **Draft** status as of this read despite years of production use by major token contracts.
Its stated purpose is to enable "meta-transactions and atomic interactions with ERC-20 token contracts via signatures conforming to the EIP-712 typed message signing specification": a token holder signs an authorization off-chain, and any third party — critically, one that need not be the holder and need not know the holder's ETH balance — can submit that signature on-chain to execute the transfer, paying the gas itself.
Three capabilities fall out of this in the specification's own framing: delegating gas payment away from the token holder entirely, paying transaction fees in the token itself rather than in ETH, and executing multiple authorized transfers *atomically and out of order*, without the sequential-nonce ordering constraint an ordinary Ethereum transaction imposes.

The nonce design is the standard's most consequential departure from ordinary Ethereum transactions, and the specification argues for it explicitly rather than treating it as incidental: EIP-3009 uses a **random 32-byte nonce** chosen by the signer, not a sequential counter, on the stated reasoning that "sequential nonces do not actually help achieve transaction ordering for meta transactions in practice" — failed submissions waste gas, and a relayer or miner can reorder submissions regardless of what order the nonces were assigned in, so a scheme that requires strict ordering only adds friction without adding a real guarantee. This is what [transfer-with-authorization](./transfer-with-authorization.md) is: the specific function-level mechanic built on top of that nonce design, including the exact `transferWithAuthorization`/`receiveWithAuthorization` signatures, the `AuthorizationUsed` event, and how replay protection actually works across chains — that term carries the mechanic in full, this term carries the standard it comes from.

EIP-3009 is not itself an x402-specific invention; it predates x402 and is USDC's own transfer-authorization mechanism (Circle's FiatTokenV2 contracts implement it, though the specification page read this pass names no early adopters directly — that association is carried forward here as background knowledge, not verified against a primary Circle source in this session, and should be graded **[UNVERIFIED]** as a specific claim about USDC's own contract lineage until a Circle or FiatToken source is read directly). What matters for this section's purposes is narrower and confirmed: [x402](./x402.md)'s `exact` payment scheme settles a payment by having the buyer's client sign an EIP-3009 authorization and having the seller's [facilitator](./x402-facilitator.md) submit it on-chain, which makes EIP-3009 the concrete [settlement primitive](./settlement-primitive.md) underneath most current x402 activity on EVM chains — and, because EIP-3009 is general-purpose rather than x402-exclusive, on-chain measurement of EIP-3009 activity is an upper bound on x402 volume, not a direct count of it; see [the note on the Bazaar undercounting settlement](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-x402-bazaar-undercounts-settlement-by-an-order-of-magnitude.md) for why that distinction is load-bearing.
