## Why this is central

Bouman draws the line the house's two terms rest on: the rotating association (ROSCA), whose fund is handed whole to one member per period and which therefore holds no money between meetings, against the accumulating association (ASCA), whose fund is kept, lent to members at interest, and shared out at the end of a cycle, and which therefore needs a treasurer, a ledger, and a rule for who may borrow how much. He reads both as durable financial institutions of the poor rather than as stages toward banking, records their scale (in some countries a majority of adults belong to one), and lists the design variables an organizer sets: size, period, contribution, order, interest, and the sanctions for default. The house cites it for the ROSCA/ASCA distinction and for the observation that the accumulating form is where the draw limit first becomes a rule someone has to write.

## What it does not settle

The paper is a survey and a classification, not a model or a trial; it does not price the draw limit or measure default, and its scale figures are country estimates of uneven provenance. It predates the village savings and loan association programs and the randomized evidence on them ([Karlan 2017](./karlan2017.md)).
