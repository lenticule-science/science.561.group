A rotating savings and credit association is a fixed group whose members each contribute a fixed sum every period to a fund that is handed, whole, to one member per period until every member has received it once. Ardener's comparative definition is "an association formed upon a core of participants who agree to make regular contributions to a fund which is given, in whole or in part, to each contributor in rotation" ([Ardener 1964](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/ardener1964.md)); Geertz found the same institution independently in Java, West Africa, and the Caribbean and read it as a "middle rung" between kin obligation and banking ([Geertz 1962](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/geertz1962.md)). The order in which members receive the fund is fixed, drawn by lot, or auctioned to the member who values earliness most; the last two are the random and bidding forms Besley, Coate, and Loury model ([Besley 1993](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/besley1993.md)).

It differs from a savings account in that the fund is never held by an institution and never earns interest, and from a loan in that no member borrows from anyone but the group itself. Its economics is the economics of timing: when each member wants an indivisible good, every member but the last gets it sooner than by saving alone and none gets it later, so the association improves on autarky without any outside capital, and the bidding form allocates the earliest draws to those who value them most ([Besley 1993](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/besley1993.md)). Its enforcement is social: a member who has taken the fund and stops contributing loses standing, future rounds, and sometimes goods, and the model leaves that sanction outside itself. Bouman classifies it against the accumulating association, which keeps and lends its fund instead of handing it round ([Bouman 1995](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/bouman1995.md)).

A pool that prices each draw instead of fixing the rotation keeps the association's gain from timing and drops its one rule, so the draw rule is where a pool's design departs from the association. The house pool ([tally credit](../../../../../monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tally-credit.md)) is that departure: a member's draw is limited by the record of their matched crossings rather than by their turn, and the fund is a standing float rather than a pot emptied each period. The modern development literature calls the accumulating variants [savings groups](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/savings-group.md); the credit variants with joint liability are [joint-liability lending](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/joint-liability-lending.md).
