## What brokerage is

**Brokerage** is [intermediation](../../../../../../glossary/terms/intermediation.md) paid on [contingency](../../../../../../glossary/terms/contingency-fee.md): a broker is paid only when a match completes, and never takes ownership of the thing matched, at any point in the transaction.
This is the mechanism named in the commission that produced this note: a solo operator with abundant parallel agent labour and almost no capital, for whom exhaustive search is cheap and inventory ownership is not an option, is structurally suited to the search-and-matching half of a transaction and structurally unsuited to the capital-holding half — brokerage is the market form that monetizes exactly the first half and requires none of the second.
See [the `brokerage` term](../../../../../../glossary/terms/brokerage.md), [the `intermediation` term](../../../../../../glossary/terms/intermediation.md), and [the `search-and-matching` term](../../../../../../glossary/terms/search-and-matching.md) for the full theoretical treatment; this note does not repeat that grounding and instead carries the applied research the terms were founded from.

The research behind this note is recorded in full in [the 2026-08-28 brokerage-mechanism record](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-brokerage-mechanism/brokerage-survey.md), a single-agent, WebSearch-only pass across four candidate forms.
Every claim in that record, and carried forward here, is graded "search synthesis" unless stated otherwise — no primary source was fetched and read directly in this pass, which is a real limitation of the evidence base this note and the operations service it feeds are built on, not smoothed over.

## The four forms, screened

### Contingency recruiting

**The mechanism holds up as claimed.** Contingency fees run 15-30% of first-year salary (2026 median cited around 20-22%), scaling with role seniority — 15-18% entry-level, 20-22% mid-level, 25-31% executive.
A $200,000-salary executive placement at 20% nets $40,000 in a single transaction: confirmed, and as claimed, the largest single transaction size plausible anywhere in this estate's current activity.
Licensing is a narrow, state-specific compliance question rather than a categorical bar: the standard employer-paid contingency model is largely exempt from "employment agency" licensing regimes, which target agencies that charge job seekers (illegal or against norms across the US, UK, and EU for permanent placement in the first place); New York is the clearest documented exception, requiring a lighter "Employer Fee Paid Agency" compliance filing even for the exempt model.
No source located names a capital floor for entry — the barrier described throughout is relationship-building and niche selection (first clients via direct outreach, freelance platforms as a proving ground), which is exactly the terrain agent-labor-driven exhaustive search plus a human founder's own relationship work can address together.

**The AI-sourcing question, corrected.** The commission asked whether AI-sourced candidates are accepted or resented by employers in 2026.
The evidence found answers a related but different question: employers' own AI-driven applicant-screening pipelines are generating record candidate resentment (25% in tech/finance hiring, offer-acceptance rates fallen from 74% in 2023 to 51% in 2025) because candidates distrust being evaluated by AI and are frequently not told AI was involved at all.
That is evidence the *candidate market* a recruiter would sell into is increasingly distrustful — a real headwind — but it is not evidence that *hiring managers reject candidates a recruiting firm found using an AI agent*; the dominant described 2026 model is hybrid (AI-assisted sourcing, human relationship and closing layer), with AI-powered sourcing platforms reporting acceptance rates up to 83%, and 85% of recruiters retaining final say over AI recommendations rather than employers refusing AI involvement outright.
This is flagged in the record as an open lead: no source in this pass directly surveyed hiring managers on the specific question asked, and this note does not claim more certainty than that gap allows.

**Mission screen: neutral.** Contingency recruiting, run generically, does not advance the pursuit of Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty; it is a fee-for-match service to whichever employer and candidate happen to be matched, with no inherent orientation toward or against that mission.
It also does not fail the mission the way real-estate wholesaling below does — no documented pattern of predatory targeting of Black or Indigenous people was found in this pass, and the underlying labor-market service (helping a person get hired) is not extractive by its nature.
A deliberately niched version — placement work specifically serving Black or Indigenous candidates, or serving employers whose own mission overlaps this estate's — would move this from neutral toward mission-serving, but that is a choice this estate would have to make explicitly; the generic mechanism does not make it automatically.

### Real-estate wholesaling (contract assignment)

**The mechanism is real, capital-light, and the highest per-deal fee found.** [Contract assignment](../../../../../../glossary/terms/contract-assignment.md) requires no purchase capital beyond an earnest-money deposit; assignment fees average roughly $13,000 (search-synthesis figure from a 1,000-plus-wholesaler industry survey, not independently verified), with a documented range from $2,500 to $40,000-plus.
Legally, no state has banned it outright as of this research, and Florida — plausibly this estate's operating jurisdiction, though that inference from the 561 area code was not independently confirmed — currently permits unlicensed assignment under a specific and mechanically strict marketing constraint: advertise the contractual interest, never the property itself, or the activity becomes unlicensed brokerage under Florida Statute Chapter 475.
The regulatory trend nationally is toward tightening, not loosening — ten-plus states now require licensing after one or two assignments, and six states added disclosure requirements in 2025 alone — which matters for any plan to operate this form beyond a single state.

**Mission screen: this form fails the mission binding as typically practiced, and this note states that plainly rather than treating the capital-light mechanism as a green light.**
Predatory wholesaling — unsolicited cash offers targeting financially distressed or elderly homeowners for below-market prices — is separately and specifically documented as disproportionately targeting Black and Brown communities, and as a mechanism of displacement and lost intergenerational wealth in predominantly Black neighborhoods, where houses already sell for roughly 12% less than comparable properties in majority-white neighborhoods.
This is not an incidental risk sitting alongside the mechanism; it is close to definitional to how deal sourcing actually works in this form, since a seller with full market options and no urgency has little reason to sell below market value to a wholesaler rather than list conventionally — sourcing sellers is, structurally, sourcing distress.
It is also not a fringe complaint: it is the explicit, stated rationale behind Oklahoma's Predatory Real Estate Wholesaler Prohibition Act, Seattle's 2025 homeowner-protection ordinance, and Philadelphia's homeowner-protection law (framed explicitly as an anti-gentrification, anti-displacement measure), and an AARP model act recommends non-waivable appraisal requirements and anti-solicitation rules specifically because of this pattern.
A version of contract assignment that did not source from financial distress — commercial property, or assignment used deliberately to route land into Black or Indigenous community land trusts rather than out of Black homeowners' hands — would not carry this same failure, but that is a different, much narrower business than "real-estate wholesaling" as the mechanism is actually practiced and priced in the sources found, and this estate has not designed that narrower version.
As surveyed, this form is graded a mission failure, not a neutral one, and this note does not recommend its pursuit in the generic form the research describes.

### Freight brokerage

**Licensing is federal and mechanical; capital is the real barrier, and it is large.** Every freight broker needs FMCSA operating authority and a $75,000 surety bond (BMC-84) or trust fund.
The $75,000 is a bond face amount, not cash required up front — but the annual premium a brand-new, no-credit-history applicant (this estate's realistic starting position) would actually pay runs 5-15% of the bond, i.e. roughly $3,750-$11,250 per year, commonly alongside a minimum $25,000 collateral deposit against the bond, since "bad credit, no collateral" bond programs are reported largely unavailable in the current market.
This is graded, in the source record, as the single hardest capital barrier found across all four forms — not merely inconvenient, but genuinely capital-barred for an operator with "almost no capital," before even accounting for load-board subscriptions and the carrier/shipper relationships needed to move a single load.

**Mission screen: neutral, and moot.** Nothing about freight matching advances or opposes the mission binding either way; the question does not reach the mission screen because the capital barrier rules the form out first.

### Insurance brokerage

**Licensing is real but lightening; the actual barrier is carrier appointment, which licensing does not grant.** State-controlled producer licensing is remotely obtainable (online pre-licensing, remote-proctored exams are standard), pre-licensing hour requirements are trending down nationally, and most states offer non-resident reciprocity.
But a license alone does not let anyone sell anything: Florida law, cited as representative, requires a carrier appointment to make a license valid at all, and a license lapses if unappointed for 48 months.
Appointment is a business relationship negotiated directly with each carrier, not a state filing — a brand-new solo operator with no book of business faces a cold-start problem structurally similar to contingency recruiting's "first client" problem, except gated by carrier underwriting criteria rather than an employer's hiring need, and the fastest documented path around building direct carrier relationships from zero — affiliating with an existing aggregator or FMO that appoints new sub-agents under its own contracts — was flagged in the source record as an open lead, not independently researched.

**Mission screen: neutral, contingent on unresolved access.** Nothing about matching an insurance buyer to a carrier's policy advances or opposes the mission either way, the same as freight brokerage; unlike freight brokerage, though, the barrier here (carrier appointment access) is a relationship problem rather than a settled capital wall, and is not yet resolved enough by this research to call the form either viable or barred.
