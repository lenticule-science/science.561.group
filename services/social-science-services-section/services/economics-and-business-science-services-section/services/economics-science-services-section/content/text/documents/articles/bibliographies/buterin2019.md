## Why this is central

The paper derives quadratic funding: a public good's funding equals the square of the sum of the square roots of individual contributions, so many small contributors raise more matching than one large contributor of the same total, and the difference between that amount and the sum of contributions is paid from a matching pool. Under the paper's assumptions this is the unique mechanism that funds each good at the level where the marginal benefit summed across contributors equals marginal cost, so it is the efficient allocation of a shared pot among competing projects, which is exactly the decision a pool faces each cycle. The house cites it as the principled alternative to Breadchain's fixed-plus-voted split and to one-member-one-vote, and as the reason a distribution rule needs a membership boundary: the mechanism is only as sound as its identities.

## What it does not settle

The mechanism is vulnerable to collusion and to one person contributing under many identities, and the paper says so; every deployed instance has had to add identity or reputation constraints the paper does not supply. Its optimality holds under quasilinear utility and independent private values, and the matching pool's size is exogenous. Nothing in it addresses a pool whose pot is yield rather than contributions, or a membership that also draws on the pot.
