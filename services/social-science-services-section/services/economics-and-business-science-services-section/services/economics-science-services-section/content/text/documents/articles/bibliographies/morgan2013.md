## Why this is central

Morgan supplies the house's citation for stating declining energy surplus directly in the units national accounts already use. The [surplus energy economics](../../../glossary/terms/surplus-energy-economics.md) term rests on Morgan's energy-cost-of-energy (ECoE) accounting: the argument that reported GDP has been decoupling from energy-backed prosperity because a rising share of gross energy has to be reinvested in obtaining energy itself, and that debt-financed consumption has been substituted for the shrinking real surplus without being named as such.

## What it does not settle

Morgan's SEEDS model and its specific ECoE and "clean GDP" figures are proprietary and not independently peer-reviewed in the way an academic EROI series is; the book itself is a trade publication aimed at a general audience, not a refereed journal article, and its numerical claims about the gap between reported and energy-backed prosperity in specific countries have not been independently reproduced by the house from Morgan's underlying data. The house treats the ECoE framing as a useful accounting lens rather than as an independently verified set of national figures.
