## Why this is central

Ostrom's Nobel lecture restates the program of Governing the Commons twenty years on and names its general form: polycentric governance, many centers of decision-making that are formally independent but take each other into account, nested from the household through the village to the region, with rules made close to the resource and enforced by those who use it. The lecture summarizes the field and experimental evidence that communication, monitoring, and graduated sanctions by users themselves sustain a commons where neither a single central authority nor privatization does, and it is the cleanest statement of the eighth design principle, nested enterprises, as a theory of scale. The house cites it beside the book because a pool of pools, each with its own barrier and its own members, is a polycentric system in exactly this sense, and because the lecture's evidence base includes laboratory experiments the book did not have.

## What it does not settle

The lecture is a synthesis and reports the experiments at the level of findings, not designs; polycentricity is presented as a description of what works and not as a mechanism with predictions about when nesting fails. It says nothing about resources held as ledger entries or about a membership that never meets.
