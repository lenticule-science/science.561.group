*International Security* 44(1), Summer 2019.

## Why this is central

Farrell and Newman's structural claim grounds both
`payment-chokepoint` and `financial-deplatforming` in this section:
global economic and information networks are asymmetric, concentrating
disproportionate traffic through a small number of hub nodes, and a
state with jurisdiction over a hub can weaponize that position through
two named mechanisms — the panopticon effect (surveilling flows
through the hub) and the chokepoint effect (denying network access to
a targeted actor, either directly or by coercing third parties who
route through the hub to sever access themselves). Financial clearing
and correspondent banking are the paper's central illustration of a
chokepoint-susceptible network, which is exactly the network layer a
donation-funded communication collective's payment processor and bank
sit inside.

## What it does not settle

The paper is international-relations theory about state coercive
capacity, not a case study of any one designation or de-banking event,
and it does not itself analyze non-state communication
infrastructure; the application to a hosting collective's payment
rails is this section's own extension of the paper's general
mechanism, not a claim the paper makes.
