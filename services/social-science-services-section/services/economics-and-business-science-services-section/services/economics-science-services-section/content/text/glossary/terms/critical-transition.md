A **critical transition**, in Marten Scheffer's sense, is an abrupt shift of a complex system from one stable state to a qualitatively different one, typically because a slowly changing underlying condition pushes the system past a threshold (a bifurcation point) where the original state no longer exists or is no longer stable. Scheffer's research, generalized across ecosystems, climate subsystems, and financial and social systems in Scheffer et al. (2009, *Nature*), argues that a class of **early-warning signals** is generic across very different kinds of systems approaching such a threshold, because the mathematics of losing stability is shared regardless of the system's specific mechanism:

- **Critical slowing down** — the system's rate of recovery from small perturbations slows measurably as the threshold approaches, because the restoring force that pulls the system back to its current state weakens.
- **Rising variance and autocorrelation** — fluctuations around the current state grow larger and more persistent, since the weakened restoring force also fails to damp them out as quickly.
- **Flickering** — as the threshold is neared, the system may briefly jump to the alternative state and back again before making the final transition, especially under noisy conditions.

These signals are diagnostic of an approaching threshold without requiring a mechanistic model of the specific system, which is what makes them proposed as generic **stage indicators**: a rising-autocorrelation or rising-variance series in a social, financial, or ecological time series is read as evidence of weakening stability regardless of what specifically is destabilizing it.

The house reads critical-transition theory as supplying the general mathematical basis for the specific, mechanism-particular stage sequences named in [catabolic collapse](../../../../../../../../../history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/catabolic-collapse.md), [five stages of collapse](../../../../../../../../../history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/five-stages-of-collapse.md), and [structural-demographic theory](../../../../../../../../../history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/structural-demographic-theory.md): where those accounts each name a particular sequence of stages for a particular kind of system, Scheffer's early-warning signals are the general-purpose instrument the [2026-09-05 note on collapse science](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-collapse-science-for-economics-business-and-finance.md) names for detecting that any one of those sequences has begun, before its later, more visible stages arrive.
