An agent token is a token issued for a single AI agent. Holding it is a bet on
that agent. On [Virtuals Protocol](virtuals-protocol.md) each agent's token is
paired against VIRTUAL. Its creator earns trading fees, and the protocol may buy
and burn it out of revenue.

What the token claims on differs by platform, and is usually weak. The agent's
earnings do not flow to holders by right. What holders get is exposure to the
agent's attention, plus any buybacks. That gap is why critics call most agent
tokens memecoins with a chat interface.

The category's cautionary case is ai16z (later elizaOS). It was sold as an
autonomous AI venture fund, peaked near $2.4B, and was declared "completely
dead" by its founder on 2026-08-05 after a fall of about 97%. A federal class
action alleges the autonomy was misrepresented.

The [Tokens](../../../../../../../../../engineering-science-services-section/services/computer-science-services-section/services/artificial-intelligence-science-services-section/services/agent-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ilands-token.md)
of iLands are not agent tokens in this sense. They are prepaid compute credit
with no cash value, and are not traded.
