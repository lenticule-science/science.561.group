**Surplus energy economics**, developed by Tim Morgan and formalized in his SEEDS model (Surplus Energy Economics Data System), argues that the economy that matters for prosperity is not the one conventional GDP measures but the one left over after the **energy cost of energy (ECoE)** — the share of gross energy output that must be reinvested in extracting, processing, and delivering energy itself — is subtracted. Morgan's 2013 book *Life After Growth* argues that decades of rising ECoE have been masked by treating debt-financed consumption as if it were the same thing as energy-backed prosperity, producing a "shadow" GDP that grows on paper while the underlying surplus available to fund discretionary spending, public services, and investment has been shrinking.

ECoE is the inverse framing of [energy return on investment](./energy-return-on-investment.md): where EROI states the ratio of energy returned to energy invested, ECoE states the share of gross output that investment consumes, and a rising ECoE is the same physical trend as a falling EROI read from the cost side rather than the return side. Morgan's distinctive move is to apply this directly to national accounts, arguing that a country's true, energy-backed prosperity per capita can diverge sharply from — and, on his read, has diverged sharply below — its reported GDP per capita once ECoE is rising.

The house reads surplus energy economics alongside [peak everything](./peak-everything.md) and [limits to growth](./limits-to-growth.md) as a third, accounting-focused argument for the same declining-surplus position: where Heinberg generalizes across resources and the Meadows model system dynamics, Morgan attempts to state the surplus directly in the same units national accounts already use, so that a widening gap between reported and energy-backed prosperity becomes a testable claim rather than only a qualitative one.
