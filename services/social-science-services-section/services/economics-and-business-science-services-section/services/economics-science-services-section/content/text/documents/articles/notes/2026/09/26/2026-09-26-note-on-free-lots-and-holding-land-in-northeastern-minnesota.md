# Free lots and holding land in northeastern Minnesota

The [Minnesota property-resale market note](2026-09-26-note-on-the-minnesota-property-resale-market.md)
measures land to be sold to others. This note measures land to be had and
kept: city lots given to people who build on them, and rural land bought and
held. The law is in the
[note on Minnesota law for acquiring and holding land](../../../../../../../../../../../law-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-minnesota-law-for-acquiring-and-holding-land.md).
Figures from a government page, statute or program's own document are marked
**primary**; news and aggregator figures **secondary**; the Group's own
queries **measured**.

## 1. Supply (measured)

From the land business's pull of St. Louis County's parcel layer
(2026-09-27):

- **Duluth** holds 3,569 tax-forfeited platted city lots ("Tax Forfeited
  Platted City Lots/non-PILT"), with a median county land estimate of $1,000.
  2,941 of them are 3,000 square feet or more (a standard 25-by-125 lot is
  3,125), 1,901 are 4,000 or more, and 988 are 3,000 square feet or more with
  a land estimate of $5,000 or more. Rebuild Duluth's second-round sites were
  valued at $5,000 to $15,000. Another 207 forfeited parcels (1,850 acres,
  median estimate $13,700) are county-administered.
- **Rural St. Louis County** (the county's towns and unorganized land) has
  54,615 parcels, 27,076 of them rural vacant or seasonal. Rural vacant land of
  five acres or more carries a county land estimate of $805 an acre at the
  25th percentile, $1,060 at the median and $1,437 at the 75th. 4,281 parcels
  of ten acres or more are estimated at $25,000 or less and held by an owner
  who can sell; 83 of those owe back taxes. 9,153 forfeited parcels cover
  880,122 acres; the county says it manages "nearly 900,000 acres of
  tax-forfeited rural land and 13,000 urban parcels" (primary).
- Examples of owner-held rural land behind on taxes: 41.5 acres in the Town of
  Alborn estimated at $22,100 owing $348; 39.7 acres in Ault at $24,100 owing
  $596; 43.1 acres in Balkan at $23,900 owing $531.

## 2. Free and nominal-price lots

### Duluth

- **Rebuild Duluth (2019–2020)** gave lots valued at $5,000 to $15,000
  "at no cost to individuals or organizations that can provide a viable plan
  for constructing new housing" (primary). The 2019 round placed lots with ten
  developers (Star Tribune, secondary). Every executed 2020 agreement found
  went to a builder company. Terms: start within a year, finish with a
  certificate of occupancy by a set date, or the lot reverts (law note,
  section 2).
- **The Housing Trust Fund** has absorbed it (ENTER magazine, secondary). Its
  2025 guidelines (primary) lend "to developers": up to $300,000 for a
  single-family detached home and up to $1,000,000 a project, for hard
  construction, utility infrastructure and blight removal (not land purchase
  or soft costs), for 18 months (24 with demolition), at 0–3% in the
  affordable fund or 0–5% in the general fund. Up to $10,000 is forgivable on
  an affordable for-sale home and up to $50,000 on affordable rentals, with the
  review committee's recommendation. Rentals must be affordable at 80% of area
  median income or less, for-sale homes at 100% or less. A development
  agreement is recorded for fifteen years; no entity may hold more than two
  open loans or $1,000,000; approved projects must start within nine months.
- **DEDA as facilitator (2025).** DEDA bought forfeited parcels from the
  county (up to $132,230.07) and conveyed them to Dirt, Inc. for infill
  housing (primary). This is the current path from a forfeited lot to a
  builder.
- **No side-lot program** was found in Duluth (a secondary search found no
  program).

No active free-lot program was found in Wisconsin or North Dakota (Grafton,
ND replaced its lots with a $20,000 new-construction grant). Lincoln, Kansas
has ended its program.

Across all of them "developing" means the same thing: a plan and proof the
builder can pay for it, a permit and a start by one deadline, a finished house
with a certificate of occupancy by a second, and the lot back to the town if
either is missed.

## 4. What a house costs to permit in Duluth (primary)

- **Zoning (R-1, UDC August 2025):** minimum lot area is "the smaller of 4,000
  sq. ft. or average of developed 1-family lots on the block face," and
  frontage the smaller of 40 feet or the block-face average. Side yards are 6
  feet, or 3 feet on lots with 30 feet of frontage or less. So a 25-foot lot
  builds where its block is already built at that width, and otherwise needs a
  variance (one 2020 Rebuild Duluth corner lot did).
- **Tiny houses** fixed on a foundation are allowed wherever one-family homes
  are, with "no minimum width requirement," if they meet the state building
  code and connect to sewer and water; houses on wheels are not dwellings.
  Accessory dwelling units may be up to 800 square feet.
- **Fees (2026):** a $150,000 house pays a $1,818.50 permit fee, plan review at
  65% of it (about $1,182) and a state surcharge of about $75, roughly
  $3,075 in all. The sewer capacity availability fee (Resource Renew) is $940
  per single-family unit. The water connection fee was not found.
- **Construction cost** (secondary, lead-generation estimates, to be replaced
  by bids): manufactured homes $55–85 a square foot, modular around $120.

## 5. Forty acres of forest: buying, holding, earning

A worked example at the county median, 40 acres at $1,060 an acre, $42,400,
bought from the county on terms (10% down, ten annual installments, 7%):

| item | amount |
|---|---|
| down payment | $4,240 |
| first-year installment ($3,816 principal plus 7% on $38,160) | $6,487 |
| one-time assurance fee (3%), deed fee, recording, deed tax (0.33%) | about $1,480 |
| property tax, class 2b (1% class rate times local rates; an estimate to check against a tax statement) | about $200–$400 a year |
| property tax, class 2c managed forest (0.65%) | about two-thirds of that |
| forest management plan (DNR forester, $300 + $10 an acre), 50–75% cost-share available | $700 |
| SFIA payment, 8-year covenant ($12.13 × 40) | +$485 a year |
| SFIA payment, 50-year covenant ($21.46 × 40) | +$858 a year |

Enrolled in SFIA, the forest pays its own property tax with money left over.
Land bought over the counter or from an owner below the county estimate (the
Group's blind-offer range is 25% to 45% of it) costs proportionally less, and
the SFIA payment does not fall with the price.

What else the land can earn:

- **Timber.** Public agencies received $30.73 a cord for aspen stumpage in
  2021 (DNR Stumpage Price Review, primary); a stand's yield in cords an acre
  is measured by the forester's plan. On land bought on terms, timber cannot
  be cut until its appraised value is paid.
- **Carbon.** The Family Forest Carbon Program takes 30 or more forested acres
  and pays $200–300 an acre over a 20-year contract; its Minnesota practice
  requires hardwood (maple-beech-birch or oak-hickory) to be at least half the
  basal area (primary). NCX's harvest-deferral market is winding down.
- **Sap.** A sugarbush carries 50 to 55 taps an acre (secondary, extension
  services outside Minnesota); birch sap and syrup are the same work on birch.
- **Leases.** Hunting leases in northern Minnesota run a few dollars to about
  $15 an acre (secondary, weak).
- **Resale on terms.** Land investors resell rural land on contracts for deed
  at 10–20% down and a higher interest rate than a bank's (secondary); the
  payments are income, and the notes can be sold.
