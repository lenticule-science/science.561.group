*Fibreculture Journal* 5, 2005 (FCJ-025).

## Why this is central

Kücklich coins "playbour" for labor that presents itself as play and so evades recognition, and payment, as work: his case is game modders whose unpaid modifications generate value later captured by publishers. This note applies the same lens to attention-reward and lightning-game surfaces (ZBD's polls and ads, THNDR-style Lightning games): the platform frames the activity as play or a small treat, which is precisely what licenses it to pay far below any wage floor and to gate it behind streaks and captchas that a genuine wage-labor relationship would not require. Playbour is this note's warrant for treating "is this activity framed as play" as diagnostic of low or zero effective pay, alongside the anti-bot design question of whether a human is required by the surface's own terms.

## What it does not settle

Kücklich's field is pre-2010 PC game modding communities; he does not address blockchain, Lightning Network payments, or app-based micro-reward schemes, all of which post-date the paper by over a decade. The paper also does not quantify an hourly rate for any activity it discusses — this note's own measurement rows supply that where a first-party rate is published.
