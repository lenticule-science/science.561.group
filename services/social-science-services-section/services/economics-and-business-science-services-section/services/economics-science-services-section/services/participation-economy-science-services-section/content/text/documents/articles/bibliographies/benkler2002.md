*Yale Law Journal* 112, 2002.

## Why this is central

Benkler coins "commons-based peer production" here: a mode of production in which large numbers of people coordinate, usually over the internet, on a non-market, non-hierarchical basis to build a shared, openly-licensed good (his running example is Linux). It is the closest-sounding named field to a naive reading of "participation economy," and this note uses it to draw a sharp line: commons-based peer production is about jointly building a shared good with no individual payout expected, while every surface this note measures (ZBD, Hive, free AI inference, faucets) pays the individual participant directly. No Buy is not commons-based peer production in Benkler's sense, even though some of its lanes (Hive posting, open-source AI tools) sit adjacent to peer-production communities.

## What it does not settle

Benkler's paper is legal-economic theory about firm boundaries and information production; it names no attention-reward, faucet, or learn-to-earn program and does not address individual monetary payout at all, which is the precise feature this note's surfaces have and Benkler's peer-production commons does not.
