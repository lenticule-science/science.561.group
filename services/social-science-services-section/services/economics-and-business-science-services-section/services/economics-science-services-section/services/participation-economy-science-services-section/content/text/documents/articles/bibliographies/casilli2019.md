Éditions du Seuil, coll. "La couleur des idées," 2019, 394p. (English-language commentary cites it as *Waiting for Robots: An Inquiry into Digital Labor*.)

## Why this is central

Casilli's investigation of "click work" gives a three-part typology this note uses directly to classify No Buy Crypto's surfaces: **digital labor on demand** (task-assigned gig work, e.g. Uber-style dispatch), **microwork** (piece-rate platform tasks, e.g. Amazon Mechanical Turk — the same territory Gray and Suri 2019 cover), and **networked labor** (unpaid or nominally-rewarded activity performed by ordinary platform users — "produsers" — such as posting, reacting, and curating on social platforms). Hive posting and curation, and attention-reward apps like ZBD's polls and ads, sit in Casilli's third category, networked labor, not the first two; this is the basis for this note's claim that No Buy Crypto's core surfaces are a different literature than gig/microwork platforms, closer to prosumption (Ritzer and Jurgenson 2010) than to Ghost Work-style piecework.

## What it does not settle

The book is a French-language sociological investigation with a French and European regulatory frame; it does not measure US-specific platforms named in this note's rows (ZBD, Stacker News, Fountain, THNDR) and predates most crypto-native earn programs' current terms of service. Figures and platform specifics in this note's rows are measured independently from each platform's own first-party surface, not derived from Casilli's fieldwork.
