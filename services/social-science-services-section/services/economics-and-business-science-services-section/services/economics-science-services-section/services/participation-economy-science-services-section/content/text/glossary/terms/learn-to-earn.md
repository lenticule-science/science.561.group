Learn-to-earn is the term the crypto industry uses for its own quiz-for-crypto reward programs: a person watches a short educational video or reads a short lesson about a cryptocurrency, answers a quiz confirming they absorbed the content, and receives a small amount of that cryptocurrency as a reward. Coinbase's own version of this program, Coinbase Learning Rewards (also called Coinbase Earn), is measured directly in the accompanying incumbent-and-provider measurement record rather than restated here, because its current live status and mechanics are a first-party platform fact, not a literature fact.

Learn-to-earn is distinguished from a crypto faucet by the presence of a task: a faucet pays for clearing an anti-bot check with no content consumed, while learn-to-earn requires watching or reading specific material and answering questions about it before payment. It is distinguished from an attention-economy reward (a poll, an ad) by its stated purpose: a learn-to-earn program's content is about the token being distributed, and the program functions as an onboarding and marketing instrument for that specific asset, whereas a poll or an ad reward is typically unconnected to any asset the platform itself issues.

The engineering consequence is that a learn-to-earn program is usually a limited-run marketing campaign for a specific token launch rather than a recurring, indefinitely-available surface, so a no-buy catalog entry for a learn-to-earn program should record whether it is currently live as a distinct, separately-dated fact from its general existence as a program type — a learn-to-earn program that once existed and has since been discontinued is not the same catalog entry as one currently accepting new participants.
