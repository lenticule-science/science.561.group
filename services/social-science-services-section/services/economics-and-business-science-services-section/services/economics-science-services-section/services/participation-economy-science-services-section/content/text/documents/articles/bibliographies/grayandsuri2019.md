Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2019.

## Why this is central

This is the founding full-length treatment of "ghost work": an anthropologist and a computer scientist document, across large-scale field research, the on-demand microwork (content moderation, transcription, labeling, identity confirmation) that hides behind seemingly-automated platform services. It is the paired empirical anchor, alongside Casilli (2019), for distinguishing platform *microwork* — a person paid piece-rate for a discrete task assigned by a platform — from the attention-reward and zero-price surfaces (Goldhaber 1997; Shampanier, Mazar and Ariely 2007) that this note also covers: a ZBD poll or a Coinbase learn-to-earn quiz is not ghost work in Gray and Suri's sense because no output is being produced for a paying third-party requester, which is this note's basis for the anti-bot and human-labor-gated classification.

## What it does not settle

The book's field sites are Amazon Mechanical Turk and similar labor-marketplace platforms; it does not cover crypto-native earn surfaces, attention/ad-reward apps, or learn-to-earn programs, none of which pay a wage for a specified deliverable. It also does not itself propose policy for platforms that pay in cryptocurrency rather than fiat.
