The participation economy is the economy in which a person earns a payout by participating — through attention, engagement, play, movement, learning, curation, resource-sharing, quest-completion, or supplying training data — rather than by wage, salary, contract, or gig, with the payout taking the form of sats, tokens, credits, or inference rather than dollars paid for a specified deliverable or a scheduled shift. This is not a term any existing literature uses for itself (the closest-sounding literatures, commons-based peer production and participatory economics, are both, on inspection, a different thing); it is the house's own founded term, ruled by the founder on 2026-09-05, who named it as one of five economies the American economy holds side by side: participation, gig, wage, salary, and contract.

The participation economy is distinguished from the gig economy by what is being paid for: gig work is a platform-dispatched task or trip with a specified deliverable (a completed ride, a delivered package), while participation-economy activity (a poll answered, a vote cast, a quest completed) has no specified deliverable a requester is paying for — it is Casilli's "networked labor" category, not task-assigned work. It is distinguished from the wage, salary, and contract economies even more sharply: those three are studied by labor economics and the economics of contracting and pay in dollars for hours, a period, or an agreed deliverable, while the participation economy pays in the platform's own denomination (a token, a credit, a unit of inference) for the act of participating itself.

The engineering consequence is that free-tier AI access is this economy's zero-price side: a provider giving away inference for free is not acting out of charity, it is being paid in a different currency — the person's own usage, data, and lock-in, exactly as Google's own Gemini API terms state for its free (Unpaid Services) tier, where submitted content and generated responses may be used to improve Google's products and read by human reviewers, a posture the same terms explicitly reverse once billing is enabled. This is the same zero-price mechanism Shampanier, Mazar, and Ariely document from the consumer's side [@shampaniermazarariely2007], read here from the provider's side: free is a price, paid in a different currency, not an absence of price.

The house's own theory places the participation economy more sharply than the outside literatures do. [Money science](../../../../../../../monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/topics/money-science.md) defines money as witness-deletion: a bearer instrument is an obligation with its provenance erased, so that it owes anyone, from anyone, for anything, and witnessed exchange, where the record of who did what stays attached to the value, is the control group. The participation economy is the economy in which the money stays witnessed: a Hive reward carries the post and the votes that produced it, a curation payout is attached to the vote, a ZBD payout is tagged to the poll, an inference credit is bound to the account that participated. The payout is closer to a [wit](../../../../../../../monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wit.md), one witnessed distinction-step, than to a dollar. Money science predicts what the measurement rows show: witnessed systems hold relationships and lose stranger throughput, so participation payouts are small, sticky, and slow to exit into bearer money, and the exit into dollars (a KYC gate, a custodian's withdrawal fee, an exchange listing) is exactly the point where the witness is deleted and the payout becomes ordinary money. This is a reading of the house's carriers, not a finding of any outside literature.
