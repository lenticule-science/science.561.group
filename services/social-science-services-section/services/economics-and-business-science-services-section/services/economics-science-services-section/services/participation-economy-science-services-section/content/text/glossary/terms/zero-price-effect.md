The zero-price effect is the experimental finding, established by Shampanier, Mazar, and Ariely's controlled demand studies, that pricing a product at exactly zero produces a shift in demand out of proportion to the discount an equivalent price cut at any positive price would produce; the authors found the effect is best explained by an affective response to the word and fact of "free" rather than by a purely rational recalculation of cost against benefit [@shampaniermazarariely2007].

The zero-price effect is distinguished from ordinary price elasticity by its discontinuity: price elasticity predicts demand rises smoothly as price falls, while the zero-price effect predicts an extra jump in demand specifically at the point where price reaches exactly zero, a jump larger than the smooth elasticity curve alone would produce. It is distinguished from the attention economy (a different, adjacent literature) by level of analysis: the zero-price effect is a finding about individual consumer psychology facing a priced choice, while the attention economy is a claim about what an entire economic system allocates and monetizes.

The engineering consequence is that a provider offering a literal free tier (as opposed to a merely cheap one) should expect disproportionately higher signup and usage than the tier's nominal value would predict, which is part of why free AI-inference tiers and free-to-join crypto surfaces are structured as onboarding and marketing instruments: the zero-price effect is doing real acquisition work for the provider, not merely giving something away.
