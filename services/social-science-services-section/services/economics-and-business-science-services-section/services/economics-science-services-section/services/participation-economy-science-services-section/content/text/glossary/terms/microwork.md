Microwork is small, discrete, piece-rate digital work — labeling images, transcribing audio, moderating content, confirming an identity match — assigned by a platform to a distributed, often anonymous human workforce and paid per completed task. Gray and Suri's field research names this "ghost work": on-demand human labor that hides behind services marketed as fully automated, with Amazon Mechanical Turk as the paradigm platform [@grayandsuri2019]. Casilli's typology places microwork as one of three digital-labor categories, alongside digital labor on demand (task-assigned gig dispatch, e.g. ride-hailing) and networked labor (unpaid or nominally-rewarded activity by ordinary platform users) [@casilli2019].

Microwork is distinguished from the attention-economy and networked-labor surfaces this program measures (ZBD's polls and ads, Hive posting and curation) by the presence of a paying requester with a specific deliverable in mind: a microwork task exists because some third party needs that exact labeled image, transcript, or moderation decision, and would otherwise have to pay a full employee or a more expensive contractor to produce it. A poll answer or an ad view produces no deliverable any requester needed; it is Casilli's networked labor, not microwork, even though both are piece-scale digital activity.

The engineering consequence is that no surface currently measured for this program's arenas (no-purchase crypto earning, free AI inference) is microwork in Gray and Suri's or Casilli's sense, because none of them assigns a task with a specific external requester and deliverable; this term is admitted to the glossary to state that boundary explicitly and to keep this program's ground distinct from the house's separate gig-work science section, which already covers task-assigned platform labor.
