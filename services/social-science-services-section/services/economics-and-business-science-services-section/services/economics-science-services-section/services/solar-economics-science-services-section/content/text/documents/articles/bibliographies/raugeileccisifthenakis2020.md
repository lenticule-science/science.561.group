## Why this is central

This is the house's primary citation for a verified, quantified figure on how much adding battery storage lowers a PV system's own net-energy performance. Its own abstract, read directly from the paper's own PDF (retrieved 2026-09-05 from Oxford Brookes University's institutional repository), states that a life-cycle assessment of a 100 MW ground-mounted PV system paired with 60 MW of lithium-manganese-oxide battery storage finds "energy pay-back time and life-cycle global warming potential increase by 7% to 30% (depending on storage duration scenarios), with respect to those of PV without storage." Because energy payback time and EROI move inversely for a fixed system lifetime, this is the house's basis for stating that a PV-plus-storage system's EROI sits measurably below PV-alone's own already-disputed figure, not above it.

## What it does not settle

The 7-30% figure is specific to a 100 MW PV / 60 MW LIB configuration under the paper's own irradiation and storage-duration scenarios; it is not a universal ratio for all PV-plus-battery pairings, chemistries, or scales (the paper's own residential-scale companion literature, Davidsson Kurland and Benson 2019, was not independently verified for its own numbers in this pass — see that carrier). The paper studies lithium-manganese-oxide chemistry specifically, not lithium-iron-phosphate or other chemistries now common in residential storage.
