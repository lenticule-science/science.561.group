## Why this is central

This is the house's citation for the live, first-party-documented boundary dispute over how photovoltaic EROI should be harmonized against fossil fuels. De Castro and Capellán-Pérez computed their own "standard," "point of use," and "extended" EROI values for wind, solar, and hydro from comprehensive material-requirement data; their own public comment (GEEDS research group, observed 2026-09-05) states that this paper was excluded from Murphy, Raugei, Carbajales-Dale, and Rubio Estrada's 2022 harmonization review despite meeting every criterion that review itself states for inclusion, and that the review's specific harmonization choice (crediting renewable electricity with distribution costs the way fossil fuels are credited with refining costs) produces results the excluded authors call "not robust" for cross-technology comparison. The house treats this as evidence that "harmonized EROI" is not a settled, neutral computation but a live methodological choice with real first-party disagreement behind it.

## What it does not settle

This note read the authors' own comment on their exclusion, not the full text of the 2020 paper itself; its own numeric EROI values for photovoltaics under each of its three boundary definitions were not independently extracted in this pass. The dispute is stated here as a documented disagreement between two groups of specialists, not adjudicated by this note in either direction.
