## Why this is central

This is the IRS's own first-party statement that the federal residential clean-energy tax credit (26 U.S.C. § 25D) is terminated: "The credit will not be allowed for any expenditures made after December 31, 2025." The same page states an expenditure "is treated as made when the original installation of the item is completed," so "if installation is completed after December 31, 2025, the expenditure will be treated as made after December 31, 2025, which will prevent the taxpayer from claiming the section 25D credit" — a rooftop solar installation completed in 2026 does not qualify, with no step-down rate. This is confirmed against a second, independent IRS page, the agency's own "Residential Clean Energy Credit" page (`https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit`, last reviewed 2026-07-04, observed 2026-09-06), which states "The credit is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025," and against the IRS's own 2025 Instructions for Form 5695 (`https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5695`, observed 2026-09-06), which states "You can't claim residential clean energy credits for expenditures made after December 31, 2025." The termination follows Public Law 119-21 (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed 2025-07-04).

## What it does not settle

The "Residential Clean Energy Credit" landing page (`https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit`) also carries older, uncorrected phase-out language ("you can claim the annual credit every year that you install eligible property until the credit begins to phase out in 2033") alongside its own termination sentence; this carrier treats the FAQ's and the Form 5695 instructions' explicit expenditures-after-2025-12-31 language, corroborated across three separate IRS pages, as controlling, and does not attempt to reconcile the landing page's own internal inconsistency.
