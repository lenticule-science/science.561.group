## Why this is central

This is the canonical scholarly recording of the wiwáŋyaŋg wačhípi (sun dance) cited by the house's own Lakota philosophy carrier, `sun-dance-as-polity-renewal.md`, which reads the ceremony as "the polity's annual confederacy-scope substrate-renewal morphism." Walker compiled the monograph from interviews with Oglala medicine men and other participants after the federal ban (1881-1934) had already stopped the ceremony from being performed, making it a reconstruction from living memory rather than direct observation — a fact this note states plainly because it bears on how the record should be read, not as a mark against its authority. The house's own carrier names Walker's 1917 monograph and his 1980 *Lakota Belief and Ritual* alongside it as its own sources.

## What it does not settle

This note did not independently re-read the monograph's full text in this pass; it is cited here as the first-party bibliographic record (title, publication, pagination, and the American Museum of Natural History's own digital-library holding) that the house's philosophy carrier already draws on, not as a fresh reading of its ethnographic content. Walker's early-20th-century ethnographic method and his own informants' individual perspectives are a standing methodological consideration for any reconstruction of a banned ceremony from memory; this note does not adjudicate that consideration.
