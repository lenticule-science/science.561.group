## Why this is central

This is the house's citation for the coinage of **energy cannibalism**: the finding that during a period of rapid exponential growth in a technology's deployment, the energy invested in building the next wave of capacity can consume most or all of the energy delivered by the currently installed base, so a fast-growing industry can deliver little or no net energy to the rest of the economy even though each individual unit has a perfectly respectable lifetime EROI. This is the basis for the accompanying note's own care to keep energy cannibalism (a growth-rate, industry-aggregate claim) distinct from any single installation's EROI (a per-unit, lifetime claim) — the two are frequently conflated in popular treatment and the house does not conflate them.

## What it does not settle

This note did not independently retrieve the original Climate 2008/Klima 2008 conference proceedings page in this pass; the citation is corroborated via a ResearchGate listing and Wikipedia's "Energy cannibalism" article rather than a first-party conference-proceedings URL, and is stated here plainly as such. The paper's own numeric thresholds (what growth rate, at what technology-specific EPBT, actually triggers net-negative aggregate delivery) were not independently extracted in this pass; the accompanying note cites the concept and its distinction from per-unit EROI, not a specific numeric result from the paper.
