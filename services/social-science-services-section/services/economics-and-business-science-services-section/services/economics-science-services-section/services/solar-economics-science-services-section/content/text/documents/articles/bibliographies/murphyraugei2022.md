## Why this is central

This is the house's citation for the most recent attempt to make EROI values for photovoltaics, wind, hydropower, and thermal fuels comparable across studies rather than resolve which single number is correct. Its own abstract states plainly that "PV, wind and hydropower have EROIs at or above ten while the EROIs for thermal fuels vary significantly, with that for petroleum oil notably below ten." The paper's own two harmonization moves — converting all figures to a consistent point-of-use boundary, and to a consistent "primary energy equivalent" basis via an assumed grid efficiency (η_G, tested at 0.3 and 0.7) — are read directly from the published PDF (retrieved 2026-09-05) in the accompanying note, including its own worked numerical example showing a factor-of-four point-of-extraction EROI difference collapsing to "only a very marginal difference at the point of use."

## What it does not settle

The paper's own harmonization choices are themselves a live, first-party-documented dispute: Carlos de Castro and Iñigo Capellán-Pérez state in a public comment (GEEDS research group) that their own peer-reviewed 2020 paper computing standard, point-of-use, and extended EROI for wind, solar, and hydro was excluded from this review despite meeting its stated inclusion criteria, and that the review's specific requirement (crediting renewable electricity with distribution costs the way fossil fuels are credited with refining costs) is a substantive methodological choice, not a neutral convention. The review's own Figure 7 also shows a wide spread in harmonized PV EROI that the paper attributes to real technological and irradiance differences, and states outright that "it is unreasonable to expect to arrive at a single value" for PV. This paper does not address battery or storage EROI at all.
