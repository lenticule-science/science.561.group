## Why this is central

This is the house's citation for the first complete, real-world (rather than laboratory or manufacturer-datasheet) energy analysis of a large-scale grid-connected photovoltaic deployment: 3.5 GW installed across Spain. Prieto and Hall calculate an EROI of 2.45:1 for the fleet as of 2008, at the point the electricity reaches the distribution system, assuming a 25-year lifetime. Their method — following all money spent per installed GW and converting it to energy via energy-intensity-of-money accounting for over twenty activities most EROI studies exclude (permitting, land, security, insurance, financing, labor, grid-connection infrastructure, O&M) — is the house's anchor for the low end of the published PV EROI range, and the reference this note's own EROI boundary-dispute reading (§1.2-1.3 of the accompanying note) is built around.

## What it does not settle

The 2.45:1 figure is specific to Spain's 2008 fleet and its assumed 25-year panel lifetime; it is not a universal PV constant, and later researchers (Ferroni and Hopkirk, per Hall's own 2016 public restatement of the book) argue actual panel lifetimes run closer to 18 years, which would lower the figure further. The book's method (energy-intensity-of-money) is itself a modeled conversion, not a direct physical measurement, and carries its own uncertainty. This note did not independently re-read the book cover-to-cover; its account of the method and figure rests on Hall's own 2016 public restatement of it (cited in the accompanying note), not on this note's own extraction of the book's full text.
