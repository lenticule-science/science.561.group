## Why this is central

This is the house's citation for **ESOI** (Energy Stored on Invested), the storage-specific analogue of EROI: the lifetime energy a storage technology can cycle through, divided by the energy required to build it. Barnhart and Benson's finding — that electrochemical batteries (lithium-ion, sodium-sulfur, lead-acid) have markedly lower ESOI than pumped hydro storage or compressed air energy storage — is the house's citation for why "adding storage" is not one uniform EROI penalty across technologies, and is the basis for the new glossary term this note admits, energy-storage-return-on-investment.

## What it does not settle

The Royal Society of Chemistry's own abstract page for this paper returned an access-blocked (403) response when this note attempted to retrieve it directly; the paper's exact numeric ESOI values per battery chemistry were not independently verified against the paper's own tables in this pass. The finding above rests on two independent secondary science-press accounts of the paper (Green Car Congress and ScienceDaily, both dated 2013-03-08) rather than the paper's own text, and is stated here plainly as such — no specific ESOI number is asserted from this source in the accompanying note.
