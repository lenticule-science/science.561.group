Net metering is the arrangement under which a utility measures the net difference between the electricity a customer-owned generator (most commonly a rooftop or small ground-mounted solar array) exports to the grid and the electricity the customer draws from the grid, and bills or credits the customer for that net amount rather than for gross consumption alone. Where net metering applies, exported electricity is typically credited at or close to the utility's own retail energy rate rather than at a lower wholesale or avoided-cost rate, which is what makes small-scale, behind-the-meter solar economically attractive to a household in a way a wholesale-priced buyback arrangement would not be.

Net-metering rules are set state by state (and, for cooperative or municipal utilities in some states, utility by utility) rather than by a single national standard, and typically specify an eligible system size, a compensation method, and whether the arrangement is available to investor-owned utilities, cooperatives, and municipal utilities alike or only to some of them. Minnesota's own statute (Minn. Stat. § 216B.164, implemented at Minn. Rules 7835.4015) is a first-party example measured for this section's founding field note: a system under 40 kW AC may elect compensation at "the average retail utility energy rate," a system under 1 MW AC (100 kW AC for a cooperative or municipal utility) may elect a "simultaneous purchase and sale" rate, and an eligible system's size is capped at 120% of the site's own energy use (Minnesota Public Utilities Commission, "Net Metering & Compensation," observed 2026-09-05). A rural electric cooperative may instead publish its own distributed-generation interconnection policy without a compensation rate stated on its public-facing page at all, as this section's field note found for both a South Dakota and a Minnesota cooperative it measured — a standing gap in what is publicly disclosed, not evidence that no such rate exists internally.

The house reads net metering as one of the concrete mechanisms, alongside [energy return on investment](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/energy-return-on-investment.md) and the [energy cliff](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/energy-cliff.md), through which a household's own solar generation is priced against the incumbent grid; Soft Dyson's tariff and yield instruments (the program carrier's instrument 3.1) will need to read a household's applicable net-metering rule, where one is published, as one of the inputs to what a household's own generation is actually worth to it.
