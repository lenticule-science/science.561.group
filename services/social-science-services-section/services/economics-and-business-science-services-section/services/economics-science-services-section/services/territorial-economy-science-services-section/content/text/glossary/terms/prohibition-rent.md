Prohibition rent is the share of an illicit good's retail price that compensates trafficking risk rather than production cost. Risk-pricing research finds it is the largest single share of a narcotic's price: "over 50% of cocaine's retail price is compensation for trafficking risk rather than production cost -- cocaine and heroin are worth more per gram than gold" (Jonathan P. Caulkins and Peter Reuter, *What Price Data Tell Us about Drug Markets*, Journal of Drug Issues, 1998; cited in "The economic, financial, and industrial technologies of narcotics empires," §2, Industrial technology). Markup concentrates at the point of highest legal exposure: Italian trafficker interviews found "121% markup at retail, 57% at wholesale, only 26% at high-level multi-kilo trafficking" (*Uncertainty and risk: A framework for understanding pricing in online drug markets*, International Journal of Drug Policy).

Prohibition rent is narcotic-specific, not a general feature of a [chain position](./chain-position.md)'s share. The executive's reading of the rows states the separation directly: "That premium is prohibition rent, narcotic-specific, and a licit territorial product has none of it, so what remains is the plain distribution of value along the ladder and the question of where refinement happens" ("What the narcotics empires teach a territorial economy," "Industrial"). A [territorial economy](./territorial-economy.md)'s product therefore carries a chain-position share but never a prohibition rent.
