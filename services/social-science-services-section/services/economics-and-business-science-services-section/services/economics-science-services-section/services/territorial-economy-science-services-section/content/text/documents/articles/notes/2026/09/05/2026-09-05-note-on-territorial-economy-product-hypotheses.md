# Territorial-economy product hypotheses

This is a September 2026 hypothesis note. Its evidence belongs in [the first
territory readout](2026-09-05-note-on-the-first-territory-readout-for-the-band-region.md)
and [the price-ladder note](2026-09-05-note-on-the-price-ladders-of-the-band-region.md),
not in an operations tree.

## Hypothesis

A holder should be able to see a price ladder—what a material sells for at
each step of a chain—and the share retained at every position before entering
an agreement. A territory catalog should never name, catalog, or sell a
holder's material or knowledge outside that holder's explicit consent scope.

## Findings worth retaining

- Development funds, producer grants, fair-trade arrangements, and
  benefit-sharing agreements can return value, but the surveyed tools do not
  compute a producer's share from a specific price ladder.
- Public access-and-benefit-sharing registries deliberately cannot hold
  confidential material [@cbd-abs]. A private consent record is therefore a
  distinct relationship, not a replacement for the public registry.
- The Hoodia agreement's published 6% royalty and 8% milestone share are
  reference figures, not a universal floor. No participant or territory is
  committed by this note.

## Smallest honest test

Take one published, non-controlled-substance price ladder already cited in
the science notes. Show the arithmetic, source dates, and unknowns; do not
infer a holder, price, or agreement. Any future catalog record needs a real
holder's explicit consent and a defined exit path before it contains a name
or a material.
