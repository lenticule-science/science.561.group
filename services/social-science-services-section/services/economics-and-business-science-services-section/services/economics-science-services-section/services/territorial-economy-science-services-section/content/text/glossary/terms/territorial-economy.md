A territorial economy begins from a territory's own materials and knowledge, reaches a distant population, and returns the value to the territory. The program carrier states this directly: "A territorial economy is one that begins from a territory's own materials and knowledge, reaches a distant population, and returns the value to the territory," and reads the case study against it: "A narcotics empire is a territorial economy with the sign flipped" (561 Group Territorial Economy Services Section README, "One value chain, two directions").

The measure of a territorial economy is not what grows in it but where it sits on the resulting value chain. The science seat's reading of the narcotics-empire rows states this as the governing finding: coca leaf sells for about $400-500 a ton and farmer-made paste for about $900/kg, while the same kilogram reaches $70,000-80,000/kg at United States wholesale, so "a territorial economy is measured by the chain position the territory holds, not by what grows there" ("What the narcotics empires teach a territorial economy," "The one finding that orders the rest").

A territorial economy is therefore the positive case the narcotics empires invert: value chains exist either way, but a territorial economy's chain returns its value home. Its yield, and what it could hold through knowledge rather than raw material alone, is read the same way an empire's chain is read: by [chain position](./chain-position.md).
