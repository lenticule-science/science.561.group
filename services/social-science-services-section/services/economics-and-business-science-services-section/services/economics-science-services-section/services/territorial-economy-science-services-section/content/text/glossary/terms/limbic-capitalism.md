Limbic capitalism is historian David Courtwright's term, grouping opioids, engineered food, and games as one category of brain-reward-targeting business design. The research note's heading on modern-era continuations and analogies states it as the cited author's claim: "Historian David Courtwright's 'limbic capitalism' thesis explicitly groups Purdue's opioids, McDonald's engineered food, and Tencent's video games as one category of brain-reward-targeting business design" (*The Age of Addiction*, discussed in *Limbic Capitalism Has Been Driving Addiction for Hundreds of Years*, After Babel; cited in "The economic, financial, and industrial technologies of narcotics empires," §7, Modern-era continuations and analogies (scholars' claims, not this research's conclusions)).

The same heading records the term's own further extension by other commentators, not by Courtwright: Chinese state media labeled online gaming "spiritual opium" in 2021, and commentators have since extended the framing to short-video platforms as "electronic fentanyl," "electronic opium," and "electronic cocaine." The research note is explicit that these are "reported here as the cited authors' own claims and metaphors, not as this research's independent assessment of whether social media functions like a narcotic" (same section). Limbic capitalism is therefore carried in this seat as a named scholarly claim to be measured, not as a house theory; the house's own reading of the same rows draws a narrower, mechanical fence rather than adopting the analogy (see [hopeium](./hopeium.md)).
