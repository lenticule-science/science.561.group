Benefit sharing is the return of value to the holders of a biological or knowledge material under an access and benefit-sharing agreement — the Nagoya Protocol's term — carrying a consent scope, a share, and a floor. The program carrier states the standard directly: "the standard the operations seat runs under for a territory's biological and knowledge materials is access and benefit-sharing (the Nagoya Protocol), never bioprospecting" (561 Group Territorial Economy Services Section README, "One value chain, two directions").

Its opposite is bioprospecting without benefit-sharing: taking a territory's knowledge or material out of the territory without such an agreement. The carrier's money section names this among what the program refuses to earn from: "selling a territory's knowledge or biological material out of the territory (bioprospecting without benefit-sharing) ... These are the incumbents' cells and the empires' cells, and the program's beat hypothesis, not its revenue" (same README, "Money is the receiver").

Benefit sharing sits beside, and is distinct from, the carrier's rule for what is never sold at all — ceremony, kinship, and knowledge that does not translate: "the Group sells position-valued products ... while keeping its circulation, the living relational practice, deliberately unbooked. Sell sections; never sell the whirl" (README, quoting Stringing Glass §3, "The theory the program stands on"). A territory's product is sold at the border under a benefit-sharing record; what does not translate stays unbooked and is never catalogued at all.
