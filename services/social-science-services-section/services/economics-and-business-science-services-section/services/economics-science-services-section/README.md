## Capacity ownership and energy economics (2026-10-17)

The Economics Science Section holds the [Capacity Ownership in a Flow Economy research note](content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/10/17/2026-10-17-note-on-capacity-ownership-in-a-flow-economy.md) and its two distinct glossary concepts: [capacity share](content/text/glossary/terms/capacity-share.md), the proposed conditional fractional right to a service-capacity tranche, and [flow-capacity economy](content/text/glossary/terms/flow-capacity-economy.md), the proposed economic order in which those shares become primary allocations. Both are **proposed**, not legislated titles, issued assets or established theorems. The model makes physically feasible energy-service delivery, ecological regeneration, law and Indigenous jurisdiction independent admissibility constraints. The existing [EROEI](content/text/glossary/terms/energy-return-on-investment.md), [surplus-energy economics](content/text/glossary/terms/surplus-energy-economics.md), and [Energy Commons](content/text/glossary/terms/energy-commons.md) definitions remain separate scientific and institutional objects, not alternate names for capacity ownership.

Downstream science uses Solar Economics for energy-production boundaries, ecological-science collaborators for watershed-health indicators, and Obsmetrologia for the mathematical foundation. The [DifferentialBypass source](https://github.com/lenticule-science/obsmetrologia/blob/main/Obsmetrologia/Cohomology/DifferentialBypass.lean) states a local theorem about closure-algebra/settling-coalgebra agreement and ordinal-regime alignment. Its identity with the Leader's phrase **distal bypass** is unresolved; so is any theorem transporting it to physical energy or capacity rights. The first recipient question is whether a share issuance and payout rule can be demonstrated not to double-allocate coupled services, reward ecological degradation, or override lawful governance.
