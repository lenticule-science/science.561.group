**Post-earnings-announcement drift** (**PEAD**) is an empirical tendency for a security's abnormal returns after an earnings announcement to continue in the direction of the measured earnings surprise rather than completing immediately.

The term does not specify one strategy. Researchers can define surprise from analyst forecasts, seasonal changes, standardized unexpected earnings, or market reactions; they can measure drift over different horizons and against different expected-return models. Those choices affect the result.

Proposed explanations include gradual information processing, investor underreaction, limits to arbitrage, transaction costs, and risk omitted from the benchmark. Announcement timing, stale forecasts, restatements, survivorship, data availability, and look-ahead bias are material implementation concerns.

A documented average drift is not a guaranteed post-announcement return. Evidence can weaken after publication or differ by period and market, and a tradable strategy must include spreads, price gaps, borrow, portfolio breadth, and the time at which the surprise data became available. Dated effect estimates belong in research records tied to their samples.
