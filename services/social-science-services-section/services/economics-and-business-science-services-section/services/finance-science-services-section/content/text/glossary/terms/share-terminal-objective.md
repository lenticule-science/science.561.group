A **share terminal objective** is a house term for maximizing the number of units of a designated asset held at a specified horizon. The objective differs from maximizing the portfolio's market value unless all comparisons use the same terminal asset price and no other assets or liabilities remain.

A usable objective must define the target asset, horizon, external cash contributions, treatment of income and taxes, admissible risks, and whether temporary sales or borrowing are allowed. It must also normalize stock splits, token redenominations, mergers, distributions, and other changes in the unit itself; otherwise an issuer could multiply the measured “success” without creating economic value.

For a fixed amount converted once, units acquired equal money spent divided by execution price. Spreading purchases through time, trading around a position, or selling derivatives changes the distribution of possible units but does not guarantee more units. Each method introduces opportunity cost, market risk, execution costs, taxes, or contingent obligations.

The objective can be legitimate when the asset itself is the desired terminal good or unit of account. It should still be evaluated against alternatives: maximizing raw unit count can reward uncompensated risk, ignore liabilities, and conceal loss of purchasing power. Reports should therefore show both normalized units and their economic value, along with cash flows and risks used to obtain them.
