**Risk parity** allocates a portfolio's capital so each included asset class or strategy contributes an equal share of total portfolio risk, rather than an equal share of capital. Because assets with lower volatility (bonds, historically) contribute less risk per dollar than assets with higher volatility (equities), a risk-parity portfolio typically holds more capital in the lower-volatility assets — and, to reach a target overall return, often applies leverage to the lower-volatility side rather than concentrating unlevered capital in the higher-volatility one.

Bridgewater Associates' "All Weather" strategy is the approach's most cited institutional application: a portfolio designed to hold roughly equal risk across assets that perform differently under the four combinations of rising or falling growth and rising or falling inflation, rather than one weighted toward equities' historical average performance in only one of those regimes.

Risk parity is a specific instance of the broader risk-budgeting idea within [portfolio-construction](./portfolio-construction.md), and it depends on the same [risk-model](./risk-model.md) inputs (a covariance estimate) any construction rule needs; a risk-parity allocation is only as reliable as the volatility and correlation estimates it is built from, and leveraging a low-volatility asset to match another asset's risk contribution reintroduces the leverage and financing considerations any levered position carries.
