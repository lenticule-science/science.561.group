## Who is taxed on yield a member directs away

The Lucas Opinion 1930 states that the tax reaches salaries "to those who earned them" despite "anticipatory arrangements and contracts however skilfully devised", for "the fruits [cannot be] attributed to a different tree from that on which they grew". The Horst Opinion 1940 adds that "the power to dispose of income is the equivalent of ownership of it" and "the exercise of that power to procure the payment of income to another is the enjoyment and hence the realization of the income by him who exercises it." Read plainly, a member who owns the asset that earns yield and directs the yield to a cooperative realizes the yield as income. The Staking Revenue Ruling 2023 adds the timing rule for staking: a cash-method taxpayer includes the fair market value of validation rewards "in the taxable year in which the taxpayer gains dominion and control over the validation rewards." The ruling addresses validation rewards, not lending-vault yield. No ruling, notice or case on a member directing vault yield to a cooperative was found.

## What Subchapter T gives a cooperative

Part I of Subchapter T applies to "any corporation operating on a cooperative basis" (Subchapter T Scope 2017, section 1381(a)(2)). A cooperative so covered does not count, in taxable income, patronage dividends paid in money, qualified written notices of allocation or other property for patronage of the year (Subchapter T Taxable Income 2026, section 1382(b)). A patronage dividend is an amount paid "on the basis of quantity or value of business done with or for such patron", "under an obligation of such organization to pay such amount, which obligation existed before the organization received the amount so paid", and "determined by reference to the net earnings of the organization from business done with or for its patrons"; it excludes amounts out of earnings from business with other patrons "to whom no amounts are paid, or to whom smaller amounts are paid, with respect to substantially identical transactions" (Subchapter T Definitions 2026, section 1388(a)). A written notice of allocation counts as qualified if redeemable in cash for at least 90 days or consented to, and is excluded unless at least 20 percent of the dividend is paid in money (section 1388(c)). The pre-existing obligation must be in the bylaws before the yield lands.

An LCA with at least two members is an eligible entity that can elect association (corporate) classification on Form 8832 (Entity Classification Rule 2026, 26 CFR 301.7701-3). Whether a Colorado LCA is an eligible entity or a per se corporation was not read. Nothing read establishes that member-directed yield is "business done with" the cooperative, which is the question the draft articles leave to counsel.

## Broker reporting

Public Law 119-5 was H.J. Res. 25, which became law April 10, 2025 and disapproved the IRS rule "Gross Proceeds Reporting by Brokers That Regularly Provide Services Effectuating Digital Asset Sales" (DeFi Broker Disapproval 2025). The disapproved rule text was not read.

## What this gives a design

The statute gives a definite form: a bylaw adopted before the yield cycle states the cooperative's obligation to pay patronage dividends, computed on each member's patronage under one rule for substantially identical transactions, from the net earnings of business with patrons, paid in money or qualified notices with the 20 percent cash floor. That form removes the cooperative's own tax on the allocated amount; it does not by its text remove the member's income.
