A **hedge fund fee structure** is the set of contractual terms determining what a fund manager is paid out of investor capital and investor returns. The traditional structure, often summarized as "two and twenty," charges a **management fee** — commonly around 2% of assets under management annually, paid regardless of performance, intended to cover the fund's operating costs — and a **performance fee** — commonly around 20% of profits, intended to align the manager's incentives with generating positive returns.

Two further terms shape what the performance fee is actually charged on. A **high-water mark** requires a fund to recover any previous losses before it can charge a new performance fee, so a manager cannot collect performance fees on the same capital's gains twice by charging on volatile round trips back up to a level already reached and paid for. A **hurdle rate** is a minimum return (often tied to a risk-free rate or a stated benchmark) the fund must clear before any performance fee applies at all, so the manager is compensated only for return above that minimum rather than for the minimum itself.

These terms interact with the strategies described elsewhere in this glossary in a specific, quantifiable way: a fee structure charged on gross returns can turn a strategy with a real but modest edge into one with a negative expected return net of fees for the investor, which is why an evaluation of whether a strategy is worth allocating to is properly a net-of-fees question, not only a question about the strategy's own gross [sharpe-ratio](./sharpe-ratio.md) or [drawdown](./drawdown.md).
