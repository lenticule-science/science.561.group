**Collateral** is property or financial value made available to support an obligation, commonly through a security interest, pledge, lien, custody arrangement, or margin agreement. If specified default or margin conditions occur, the secured party may have rights to control, liquidate, or apply the collateral according to the contract and governing law.

Collateral reduces some loss exposure but does not eliminate credit risk. Value can fall, title can be disputed, liquidation can be delayed, senior claims can take priority, and sale costs can reduce recovery. Lenders therefore apply eligibility rules, valuation haircuts, advance rates, covenants, monitoring, and concentration limits.

A **loan-to-value ratio** compares the obligation or advance with the collateral value under a stated valuation method. Both the numerator and denominator can change, so an initial ratio is not permanent protection.

A **personal guarantee** is distinct from collateral. It is a person's contractual promise to answer for an obligation; it may be secured by identified personal property or unsecured. Giving a guarantee does not generally erase an entity's limited-liability status for every debt—it creates personal exposure for the guaranteed obligation according to its terms.

Accounting records should distinguish the asset's ownership and carrying amount from liens, pledged status, restricted use, guarantees, and the lender's collateral valuation.
