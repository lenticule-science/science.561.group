**Net working capital** is current assets minus current liabilities at a reporting date. Analysts also use **working capital** to mean the operating investment in receivables and inventory net of operating payables; the intended definition must be stated.

Working capital is not the same as cash available. Current assets can include slow, disputed, pledged, or illiquid amounts, while current liabilities can have different due dates. Positive net working capital therefore does not prove that near-term obligations can be paid, and negative working capital does not by itself prove distress.

Useful analysis examines composition and timing: cash, [accounts receivable](../../../../../business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-receivable.md) aging, inventory quality, [accounts payable](../../../../../business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-payable.md) terms, accrued obligations, and short-term debt. Current ratio, quick ratio, cash forecasts, and the [cash conversion cycle](./cash-conversion-cycle.md) answer related but different questions.

An increase in operating working capital is generally a use of cash; a decrease is generally a source of cash, subject to classification and noncash changes. Forecasts should derive these balances from operational timing rather than insert one unsupported reserve amount.
