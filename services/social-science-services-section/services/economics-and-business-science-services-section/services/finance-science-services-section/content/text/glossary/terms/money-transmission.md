**Money transmission** is, in the federal regulation at 31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(5), "the acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and the transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another location or person by any means"; whether a person is a money transmitter is a matter of facts and circumstances. The criminal statute at 18 U.S.C. 1960 reaches operating an unlicensed money transmitting business.

FinCEN's 2019 guidance on convertible virtual currency tests an intermediary by four criteria: who owns the value, where it is stored, whether the owner interacts directly with the payment system, and whether the intermediary has total independent control over the value. A user of an unhosted single-signature wallet transacting on the user's own behalf is not a money transmitter; a provider holding value in its own accounts, or with total independent control, is, whatever it calls itself. State statutes define the term separately; Wyoming's exempts virtual currency transmission, Colorado's contains no such exemption.
