**Debt service** is the principal, interest, and any other financing payments included by a contract or analysis for a stated period. It is a cash-obligation measure; principal repayment reduces a liability rather than creating an accounting expense.

A **debt service coverage ratio** (**DSCR**) compares a defined earnings or cash-flow numerator with defined debt service:

**DSCR = cash flow available for debt service ÷ required debt service**

There is no universal numerator or denominator. Commercial real-estate, project-finance, corporate, and small-business lenders can use net operating income, EBITDA adjusted for specified items, or cash flow after taxes and maintenance expenditure. They can also differ on leases, balloon payments, owner compensation, and unused facilities.

A ratio above one means the defined numerator exceeds the defined payments for that period; it does not guarantee payment because timing, cash restrictions, working-capital changes, forecast error, and other claims matter. A ratio below one identifies a modeled shortfall that must be funded, refinanced, waived, or otherwise resolved.

Reports should reproduce the governing definition, reconcile it to the books and cash forecast, and test timing and downside scenarios rather than relying on a generic lender threshold.
