**Account limiting** is a counterparty's restriction of an account's permitted size, products, promotions, funding, withdrawals, or continued access. Betting-industry slang sometimes calls promotion or stake restriction **gubbing**.

A provider can limit an account for credit, fraud, compliance, responsible-use, market-integrity, operational, or commercial reasons. Consistently informed or promotion-focused activity can be commercially unattractive, but a restriction alone does not prove why the provider acted.

Account limiting is a form of counterparty and access risk. A strategy that depends on discretionary promotions, leverage, APIs, high limits, or continuous quoting should model the possibility that those facilities change or disappear. Expected value should be measured over the finite authorized opportunity set, not as though the same terms repeat indefinitely.

Controls include complying with identity and account terms, avoiding dependence on one provider, retaining transaction and communication records, reconciling restricted funds, and maintaining an exit plan. Evading a restriction through false identities or unauthorized accounts is a separate act that can create forfeiture, fraud, and legal risk; it is not a scaling technique within the original strategy.
