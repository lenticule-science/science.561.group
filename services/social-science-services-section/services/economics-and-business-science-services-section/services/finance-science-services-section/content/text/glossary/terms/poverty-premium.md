The poverty premium is the extra amount a low-income household pays for the same goods and services that a higher-income household buys more cheaply. Davies, Finney, and Hartfree reconstructed it item by item for Britain in 2016: energy paid by prepayment meter or off the best tariff, insurance priced by postcode, higher-cost credit, rent-to-own goods, fees for cash access and money transfer, and the cost of living far from cheap shops, summing to about £490 a year for an average low-income household with a wide range around it ([Davies 2016](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/davies2016.md)). The same method, a fee schedule of what the households actually use, is how any other country's premium is measured.

It is not a higher price for a worse product; it is the same energy, insurance, credit, and cash access bought through the only channels a household without a float, a good postcode, or a credit history can use. The largest items are timing costs: an overdraft or payday fee on a shortfall of days, a deposit held at no interest, a late fee that stacks ([Davies 2016](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/davies2016.md)). Karlan, Ratan, and Zinman record the mirror image among the poor of low-income countries, who pay to hold money safely ([Karlan 2014](../../../../../economics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/bibliographies/karlan2014.md)).

A fee that exists only because a household lacks a float is a return available to whoever supplies the float, so a pool that fronts a member's shortfall earns the premium the member would otherwise have paid. That is the house pool's timing float: a draw priced at the fee it avoids, repaid at the member's inflow, booked with two witnesses; the premium is the pool's income and the member's saving at once, and the fee schedule is the measurement the price rests on. Sizing that float is a [line of credit](./line-of-credit.md) problem whose limit the pool sets by [tally credit](../../../../../monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tally-credit.md).
