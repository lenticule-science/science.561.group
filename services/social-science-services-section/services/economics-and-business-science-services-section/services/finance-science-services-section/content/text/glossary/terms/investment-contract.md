An **investment contract** is, in SEC v. W. J. Howey Co., 328 U.S. 293 (1946), "a contract, transaction or scheme whereby a person invests his money in a common enterprise and is led to expect profits solely from the efforts of the promoter or a third party." United Housing Foundation v. Forman, 421 U.S. 837 (1975), applied it to shares in a nonprofit housing cooperative bought to obtain a place to live and found no security because no profit of the traditional kinds was offered. Reves v. Ernst & Young, 494 U.S. 56 (1990), applies a different, family-resemblance test to notes, including a cooperative's demand notes.

In March 2026 the SEC and CFTC stated that a non-security crypto asset becomes subject to an investment contract when an issuer induces an investment of money in a common enterprise with representations or promises of essential managerial efforts from which purchasers would reasonably expect profits.
