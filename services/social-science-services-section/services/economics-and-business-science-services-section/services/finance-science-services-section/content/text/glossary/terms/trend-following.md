**Trend following** takes a position in the direction of an asset's own recent price trend — long when the trend is up, short when it is down — rather than comparing the asset to other assets in a universe; it is the strategy family most directly built on time-series [momentum](./momentum.md) rather than cross-sectional momentum. Trend-following programs run across a broad range of futures markets (equity indices, rates, currencies, and commodities together) are commonly organized and marketed as **managed futures**, run by firms registered as **Commodity Trading Advisors (CTAs)**, a regulatory category in the United States for firms that advise on futures and derivatives trading.

Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen's study of time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012) is the standard academic anchor for the strategy family, documenting the pattern across 58 futures markets from 1985–2009 with a diversified portfolio achieving a Sharpe ratio near 1.0; see [the house's note on documented edges at retail scale](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-documented-edges-at-retail-scale.md) for how that specific result, being a futures-market result, translates imperfectly to a retail account without futures access.

A commonly cited feature of diversified trend-following portfolios is a return profile with low correlation to equity markets and, historically, positive performance during some of the sharpest equity drawdowns, which is the basis for their use as a portfolio diversifier rather than only a standalone return source; the same evidence base also documents that trend-following performance is not always-on and can go through extended multi-year periods of weak returns, particularly following a sharp market reversal the trend signal has not yet adapted to.
