A **non-controlling developer or provider** is defined in Section 604(b)(3) of H.R. 3633 as reported to the Senate as a developer or provider of a distributed ledger service that "in the regular course of operations, does not have the legal right or the unilateral and independent ability to control, initiate upon demand, or effectuate transactions involving digital assets to which users are entitled, without the approval, consent, or direction of any third party." Section 604(c) would provide that such a person is not a money transmitting business under 31 U.S.C. 5330 nor engaged in money transmitting under 18 U.S.C. 1960 solely for creating or publishing software, providing hardware or software for a customer's own custody, or infrastructure support. Section 604(d) preserves 1960(b)(1)(C) for specific intent to move criminal proceeds.

The bill failed cloture on the motion to proceed 49 to 50 on September 15, 2026 and is not law. The enacted analogue is Section 2(7)(B) of the GENIUS Act, which excludes protocol developers, immutable self-custodial interfaces and liquidity-pool participants from the definition of digital asset service provider.
