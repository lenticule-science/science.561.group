A **good faith violation** (**GFV**) generally occurs in a US cash-account workflow when a customer buys a security with proceeds from an earlier sale and then sells the newly purchased security before the earlier sale has settled.

For example, a customer sells Security A, uses its unsettled proceeds to buy Security B, and sells B before A's proceeds settle. The customer had a good-faith expectation that A's sale would pay for B, but B was sold before that payment became settled.

A GFV differs from [free-riding](./free-riding.md): free-riding relies on the sale of the newly purchased security itself to pay for its purchase, whereas a GFV begins with another sale expected to provide the funds. Broker terminology and transaction facts still control the classification.

Restrictions, counting windows, and exceptions are regulatory and broker-policy facts that can change. They should be checked against current primary rules and the account agreement rather than encoded as timeless numbers. Systems should separately track settled cash, unsettled proceeds by settlement date, buying power, and planned disposition dates.
