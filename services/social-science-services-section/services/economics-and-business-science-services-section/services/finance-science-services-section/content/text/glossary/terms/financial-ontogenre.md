The **financial ontogenre** is the recurring classificatory and narrative form
through which finance makes entities, claims, risks, and possible futures
recognizable as financial objects.

The term combines *ontology* and *genre*. The ontological side concerns what a
financial practice permits to exist in its model: an owner, issuer, asset,
liability, unit of account, price, claim, maturity, default, or risk. The genre
side concerns the expected sequence that makes those objects intelligible:
capital is contributed, deployed, valued, made productive, settled, distributed,
refinanced, impaired, or lost.

The concept is therefore not a synonym for finance. It names a way finance
formats relations and events so they can be recognized and acted on as finance.

## Financial domain and financial ontogenre

The **financial domain** is the world of practices, institutions, instruments,
relationships, events, and material conditions conventionally treated as
financial. It can include banks, households, exchanges, payment systems, debts,
securities, prices, laws, informal obligations, and failures.

The financial ontogenre is a particular schema and trajectory imposed within
that domain. It determines which participants appear as reporting entities,
which relations appear as claims, which events count as recognition or
settlement, which times become periods or maturities, and which differences can
be compared through a unit of account.

A domain can contain more than one ontogenre. A cooperative, a household, a
commercial lender, a securities exchange, and a mutual-aid network can inhabit
overlapping financial relations while making different entities, duties, and
futures legible. The concept should not be used to pretend that one financial
ontology exhausts the domain.

## Recurring financial arc

A common financial ontogenric arc has the following moments:

1. **Constitution:** a person, enterprise, account, fund, or instrument is
   recognized as a bounded financial entity.
2. **Initial position:** assets, liabilities, contributed claims, prices, or
   credit limits establish a baseline.
3. **Anticipation:** a possible future is represented as expected cash flow,
   return, loss, repayment, delivery, or default.
4. **Entitlement or obligation:** a contract or instrument assigns who may
   receive, demand, deliver, seize, vote, or bear loss.
5. **Measurement:** heterogeneous events are translated into quantities and
   values that the financial system can compare.
6. **Realization:** payment, delivery, exercise, liquidation, impairment, or
   default resolves part of the anticipated future.
7. **Continuation or closure:** the result is reinvested, rolled forward,
   refinanced, distributed, written off, or used to terminate the relation.

This is an analytic pattern, not a rule that every financial practice follows.
Its use is to expose assumptions that ordinary financial language can hide.

## Instruments organize possible futures

Financial instruments do not merely sit inside an already complete financial
world. Their terms help produce the financial objects and temporal sequences
that market participants then perceive.

- A loan organizes principal, interest, payment dates, default, and creditor
  remedies into an expected trajectory.
- An equity security organizes residual claim, voting or other governance
  rights, distributions, dilution, and exit.
- A future organizes a later delivery or cash settlement around a specified
  underlying, quantity, price convention, and date.
- An option organizes a contingent right, a decision deadline, an exercise
  condition, and an asymmetry between holder and writer.
- Collateral converts a resource into a governable response to a possible
  failure by specifying valuation, control, and seizure conditions.

In each case, the instrument makes a possible future actionable in the present.
It establishes roles and consequences before the future event is known.

## Accounting enacts a financial ontogenre

Accounting artifacts are not passive mirrors of financial reality. A reporting
entity boundary decides whose resources and obligations enter the statements. A
chart of accounts decides which differences remain visible. Recognition rules
decide when a relation becomes an asset, liability, income, or expense.
Measurement rules decide which quantities can be compared. Closing rules decide
how a period's activity becomes an accumulated position.

These decisions are necessary for bookkeeping and reporting. Calling them an
ontogenre emphasizes that they jointly produce a recognizable kind of financial
story: an entity begins with a position, undergoes classified events, and ends
with a changed position that becomes the next period's beginning.

The same source event can occupy different roles in different ledgers. Issuing
shares increases an issuer's cash and owners' equity; acquiring those shares can
create an investor's financial asset. The event does not carry one universal
classification independent of reporting perspective.

## Ontogenrism as the stronger claim

Within this corpus, **ontogenrism** names the stronger hypothesis that
ontologizing operates through genre: institutions format existence into legible,
governable subjects by imposing narratives of anticipation and entitlement, and
those narratives tend to assume that existence is coherently classifiable in
advance.

The financial ontogenre is a domain-specific site where that hypothesis can be
tested. Finance repeatedly turns uncertain relations into named entities,
priced claims, scheduled obligations, risk factors, and settlement states. The
test is critical rather than rhetorical: identify exactly what the model makes
legible, what it treats as residual, whose position defines the classification,
and what happens when events contradict the anticipated arc.

## Analytical use

Use *financial ontogenre* when the question concerns how financial categories
and temporal expectations constitute their objects. Use *financial domain* when
the question concerns the broader set of financial practices and relations.
Use *financial ontology* when only the inventory and relations among recognized
objects matter and no claim about their recurring narrative trajectory is
needed.

The concept is most useful for questions such as:

- How does a reporting boundary create the entity whose performance is measured?
- What kind of subject does a credit score or risk classification make legible?
- Which possible futures can a contract express, and which become residual?
- How do recognition, valuation, and settlement rules turn uncertain relations
  into present claims?
- Whose entitlement defines the normal arc, and whose loss or labor appears only
  as an external condition?
- How does the system record contradictions such as disputed ownership,
  nonperformance, illiquidity, or incompatible valuations?

The financial ontogenre is not the world of finance. It is one of the forms by
which finance makes a world describable, comparable, and governable.
