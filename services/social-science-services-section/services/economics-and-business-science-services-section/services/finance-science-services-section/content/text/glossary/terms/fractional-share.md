A **fractional share** is an interest representing less than one whole share of a security. Fractions can arise from dividend reinvestment, stock splits, mergers, or a broker's fractional-trading program.

Broker-provided fractional trading lets customers submit a partial-share quantity or a money-denominated order. The broker may aggregate orders, act as principal, or hold the whole-share position through its custody structure and record customers' beneficial fractions internally. Consequently, execution, transfer, voting, corporate-action, and liquidation treatment can differ from whole shares.

Fractional ownership lowers the minimum dollars needed to obtain exposure and can make target-weight diversification possible with small balances. It does not reduce percentage market risk, spreads, taxes, or the need to evaluate the security itself.

Eligibility, minimum size, precision, supported order types, trading hours, short-sale treatment, and portability are broker- and security-specific. They can change, so operational code should query current instrument metadata and account rules rather than relying on a static glossary list.
