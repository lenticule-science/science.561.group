**Prime brokerage** is the bundle of services a large bank or broker-dealer (a "prime broker") provides to a hedge fund or other institutional trading client, distinct from ordinary retail brokerage. The core services are **margin financing** (lending cash against a client's positions so it can hold larger positions than its own capital alone would support), **securities lending** (lending out shares so the client can borrow and sell them short, since a short sale requires delivering borrowed shares), **custody and clearing** (holding the client's assets and settling its trades), and **capital introduction** (connecting the fund to prospective investors, a relationship rather than a pure execution service).

A prime broker's financing and securities-lending terms directly shape which strategies a fund can run and at what cost: a [market-neutral](./market-neutral.md) or [statistical-arbitrage](./statistical-arbitrage.md) strategy that depends on shorting requires borrow to be available at all (some securities are difficult or impossible to borrow, or carry a high borrow fee reflecting scarce lendable supply), and a leveraged strategy's realized return depends on the financing rate the prime broker charges as much as on the strategy's own gross performance.

A hedge fund's use of prime brokerage is separate from a retail account's [pattern-day-trader](./pattern-day-trader.md) rules and [intraday-margin](./intraday-margin.md) limits described elsewhere in this glossary: prime-brokerage terms are individually negotiated per client and are not the standardized retail margin schedule a smaller account operates under.
