# Underwriting edges below their capital floor

Commissioned 2026-08-28: extend the 2026-08-27 small-account trading research to the mechanisms it explicitly did not cover — **risk underwriting** (taking the other side of a risk for a premium: options premium selling, peer-to-peer and crypto lending, staking and validating) and **known-edge betting** (betting where the edge is mathematical rather than predictive: market making, matched betting off bookmaker promotions).
Full sourced research, every claim graded inline, lives in [the underwriting-and-edge-betting research record](../../../../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-underwriting-and-edge-betting/underwriting-survey.md); this note carries that record's spine forward rather than compressing it, per the section's density standard.
The 2026-08-27 work this note continues rather than duplicates: [capital thresholds for perpetual and delta-neutral strategies](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-capital-thresholds-for-perpetual-and-delta-neutral-strategies.md), [documented edges at retail scale](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-documented-edges-at-retail-scale.md), [on-chain strategy feasibility at small capital](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-on-chain-strategy-feasibility-at-small-capital.md), and [growth-optimal sizing when ruin is cheap](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-growth-optimal-sizing-when-ruin-is-cheap.md).

## The finding, stated first

Four of the five mechanisms surveyed here — [options premium selling](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/premium-selling.md), peer-to-peer lending, crypto lending, and [staking](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/staking-yield.md) — reproduce, in their own specific arithmetic, the exact capital-threshold pattern the 2026-08-27 [capital-thresholds note](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-capital-thresholds-for-perpetual-and-delta-neutral-strategies.md) already established for delta-neutral funding farming: below a specific dollar figure, fixed costs and fixed risk-management practices dominate a small account's return, not because the underlying edge is fake, but because the practices that make it a *managed* edge rather than a concentrated bet have their own dollar floor.
[Market making](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/market-making.md) fails the same way at a different, much higher floor — true latency-sensitive market making needs millions of dollars in infrastructure regardless of trading capital, and its retail-accessible analogue is the [concentrated-liquidity LP work the 2026-08-27 on-chain note already did in full](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-on-chain-strategy-feasibility-at-small-capital.md#concentrated-liquidity-lp-gas-clears-rebalancing-doesnt), not re-derived here.
[Matched betting](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/matched-betting.md) is the single exception in this survey, and the exception is the actual finding this note exists to state precisely: its scarce resource is not capital at all, and its failure mode is not losing money — it is [account limiting](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/account-limiting.md), a counterparty response to being correctly identified as an informed, hedged bettor.

## Options premium selling: the same fixed-cost pattern, at a new number

A cash-secured put's collateral is mechanical: 100 × strike, in cash.
The 2026-08-27 [small-account trading mechanics note](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-small-account-trading-mechanics-after-the-pdt-retirement.md#options-mechanically-real-functionally-a-lottery-ticket-at-100) already established that at $100 on Alpaca, this makes cash-secured puts and covered calls mechanically unavailable outside essentially nonexistent sub-$1 underlyings, leaving Level 2 long calls/puts — full premium paid, no collateral, a single binary-payoff contract — as the only real avenue.
This note's contribution is the threshold above that mechanical floor: a single contract on a genuinely liquid, optionable low-priced stock needs **$500–$1,500** in collateral, but the risk-management practices that separate premium selling from a concentrated one-off bet — never risking more than 20–25% of the account on one position, sizing each trade at 1–2% of total balance, rolling a threatened assignment out in time or down in strike — all presuppose multiple simultaneous, rollable positions.
Practitioner sourcing converges on **$5,000–$10,000** as the practical minimum where that discipline becomes affordable (search synthesis, [the record's §1](../../../../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-underwriting-and-edge-betting/underwriting-survey.md#1-options-premium-selling--capital-reality)).
This is not a contradiction of the existing [volatility-risk-premium term](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/volatility-risk-premium.md), which documents the aggregate index/variance-swap version of the same edge at Sharpe ≈1.0–1.47; it is the single-name mechanics layer explaining why a small account cannot safely access that same aggregate edge — the Sharpe ratio presumes a diversified, rollable book of positions, and that book has its own dollar floor independent of whether the edge itself is real.

## Lending and staking: real percentages, trivial dollars at $150

Proof-of-stake real yield after inflation runs roughly **0–10%** depending on chain (Solana real yield as low as 0–3%, Ethereum under 2%, Tezos 5–10%, Cosmos 2–8% after a 21-day unbonding period, Polkadot 10–14% nominal behind a ~250 DOT minimum and a 28-day unbond) — search synthesis, full per-chain figures in [the record's §2](../../../../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-underwriting-and-edge-betting/underwriting-survey.md#2-lending-and-staking--realistic-yields-minimums-and-whether-150-clears-anything).
Applied directly to $150: **$6–$12/year**, the same order of magnitude the 2026-08-27 [on-chain strategy feasibility note](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-on-chain-strategy-feasibility-at-small-capital.md#yield-deposit-read-only-tooling-exists-and-the-protocol-is-mid-exploit-today) found for Moonwell yield deposit on $40 of ETH (**$1.60–$2.40/year**), scaled up.
Peer-to-peer lending shows the same diversification-floor pattern as options: platforms recommend spreading capital across 100+ loans at no more than 1% each, which at $150 implies a $1.50-per-loan ticket size below most platforms' own $25 minimum note.
**Slashing risk exists specifically for delegators, not only validator operators** — a real, underdiscussed detail this record surfaced: delegating to a validator that gets slashed for misbehavior or downtime slashes the delegator's stake too, and the standard mitigation (spreading delegation across multiple high-uptime validators) has the same per-position dilution problem as every other diversification discipline in this note.
Crypto lending on Aave/Compound runs 2–6% on majors/stablecoins, and Layer-2 gas is genuinely cheap enough (~10x cheaper than mainnet, consistent with the 2026-08-27 note's own **~$0.017** measured Base swap cost) that gas is not the binding constraint here either — the binding constraint is, again, that 4–6% of $150 is a few dollars a year, smaller than the value of the attention spent checking on it.

## Market making: infrastructure-gated, not capital-gated, at the professional tier

True market making's barrier is not a dollar figure a small account could eventually clear — it is colocated servers, direct market-data feeds, and FPGA hardware running into the millions of dollars a year, a barrier equally binding at $150 or $500,000 because it is capital expenditure on infrastructure, not trading capital (search synthesis, [the record's §3](../../../../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-underwriting-and-edge-betting/underwriting-survey.md#3-market-making--capital-latency-and-solo-operator-accessibility)).
The retail-accessible analogue this operator can actually reach is concentrated-liquidity LP on Base, and the 2026-08-27 on-chain note already worked that arithmetic in full from the operator's own capital position: gas clears trivially (**≈$0.06** per open/close round trip against a $40 position), but rebalance frequency does not — a single daily rebalance can consume an entire year's gross fee income at this scale.
This note does not reopen that finding; it places it in context as this survey's answer to "what does market making look like here," alongside a genuine open lead — no source located this pass gives a minimum-capital figure for prediction-market maker rebates on Kalshi or Polymarket specifically, and the repo's own Polymarket client is hard-coded to reject every order regardless.

## Matched betting: the exception, and why it does not simply fit an agent fleet

Matched betting backs a bookmaker's promotional bet and lays the opposite side on an exchange (or, in the US market where Betfair-style exchanges are not licensed, hedges across two competing sportsbooks instead), covering every outcome so the position is close to riskless and the profit comes from the promotion, not from predicting a sporting result.
This is the one mechanism in the whole survey — including the four above, and everything the 2026-08-27 growth-optimal-sizing note covered — that is not a probabilistic wager the [Kelly criterion](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/kelly-criterion.md) or the [subfair-game](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/subfair-game.md)/superfair framing applies to: a correctly matched pair has no meaningful variance to size a fraction against, because both outcomes are covered before the bet is placed.
Legal in the US wherever the underlying sports betting itself is legal — 39 states, D.C., and Puerto Rico as of 2026-08 — with UK-sourced bankroll figures (£500–£1,000 total from working every major sign-up offer once, £120–£250/month ongoing from reload offers) carried here as directional only, since the UK literature assumes exchange-based back/lay hedging and the US cross-book workaround is reported as structurally less efficient, with no comparable US-specific figure located this pass.

**The failure mode that actually kills it is account limiting, not losing bets** — a correctly hedged bet cannot lose in the way that matters, so the real risk is the bookmaker's own detection of the pattern.
[The account-limiting term](../../../../../../../text/glossary/terms/account-limiting.md) works the mechanism precisely: "gubbing" restricts a flagged account to promotion-free ordinary betting, which reduces its matched-betting value to roughly zero without technically closing it, and bookmakers are reported to trigger this specifically on arbitrage-shaped and consistently-winning patterns.
**Does this scale with monitoring capacity, the property that would make it fit an agent fleet?** Within one honest identity, yes — the binding constraint on a single account's matched-betting income is attention (tracking which offers are live, executing the hedge before a wagering window closes), not capital, which is the property that made this mechanism worth asking about for an agent fleet in the first place.
But that same property has a hard ceiling: past one identity's own offer inventory, the only way to scale further is running matched-betting activity across multiple accounts under false identities ("gnoming") — which is explicitly classified as fraud, not gray-area advantage play, distinct from and more severe than ordinary gubbing risk, and is exactly what device-fingerprinting, IP-correlation, and betting-pattern detection tooling is purpose-built to catch.
**The honest statement of the finding: matched betting is monitoring-scalable, not capital-scalable, but only up to a ceiling set by identity, and an agent fleet's structural advantage — running many parallel instances of the same task — is precisely the move that turns this from advantage play into fraud.**

## Screening against the mission binding

The root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../CLAUDE.md) states plainly that work not serving the pursuit of Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty does not belong in the 561 Group, and this section's own corpus has already drawn a real line on monetization on principle elsewhere — the 2026-08-20 content-farming and attention-arbitrage research memos in this same science-services-section document the Group's rejection of ad-driven engagement extraction as a monetization model, not merely as a bad business plan.
Matched betting deserves the same direct treatment rather than a quiet omission, and it does not land in the same place as content farming, for a specific, statable reason: the counterparty being extracted from is a bookmaker's own promotional marketing budget, deliberately overpriced to acquire customers who mostly do not hedge — not an identifiable vulnerable person, and not a shared pool of other bettors' losses.
That distinguishes it from predatory gambling in the mechanism, but not in the surrounding infrastructure: matched betting operates entirely inside the UX, brand presence, and account relationship of a sportsbook industry that has a real, documented history of predatory and regressive targeting, including of Black and poor communities specifically through state lotteries and saturation marketing — a fact this note states rather than elides, even though it does not, on its own, disqualify a hedged, non-predatory tactic executed against that industry's own promotional spend.
The practical conclusion follows from the mechanism analysis above rather than from the ethics paragraph alone: matched betting is a real, legal, positive-EV tactic worth the Group treating as opportunistic and bounded — never worth building multi-identity automation against, both because that specific move is fraud regardless of the ethics of the underlying tactic, and because an account or identity flagged for fraud is exactly the kind of reputational exposure the [561-group-revenue-operations-service](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/services/561-group-operations-services-section/services/561-group-revenue-operations-service/README.md)'s own operating constraints already name as the Group's binding constraint, ahead of capital.
