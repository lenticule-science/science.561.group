## The federal test

An investment contract "means a contract, transaction or scheme whereby a person invests his money in a common enterprise and is led to expect profits solely from the efforts of the promoter or a third party" (Howey Opinion 1946). Forman Opinion 1975 held shares in a nonprofit cooperative housing project not to be securities because the touchstone is "an investment in a common venture premised on a reasonable expectation of profits to be derived from the entrepreneurial or managerial efforts of others", and "neither of the kinds of profits traditionally associated with securities were offered", the focus being "the acquisition of a place to live". Reves Opinion 1990 shows the other side for a cooperative's paper: a farm co-op's demand notes, sold to raise capital and bought for interest, were securities under the family-resemblance test. Marine Bank Opinion 1982 held a bank certificate of deposit not a security because holders are protected by the federal banking laws, and a private profit-sharing agreement not of the kind that "comes to mind when the term 'security' is used".

## Commission positions in 2026

The SEC CFTC Interpretation 2026 states that a non-security crypto asset becomes subject to an investment contract "when an issuer offers it by inducing an investment of money in a common enterprise with representations or promises to undertake essential managerial efforts from which a purchaser would reasonably expect to derive profits", cites Forman's finding of no profit-seeking offer where the bulletin emphasized the nonprofit nature of the project, and treats a digital tool such as a membership with no passive yield or rights to future income as not a security. It does not discuss lending claims, vault shares or credit tokens. Regulation Crypto Assets 2026 proposes two Section 5 exemptions ($5 million over four years; $75 million per twelve months) and an investment-contract safe harbor, with comments due October 20, 2026; the word "cooperative" does not appear in it.

## What exists for cooperatives

Federal statute: Securities Act Section 3 paragraph (5) exempts securities issued by supervised savings and loan, building and loan, cooperative bank and similar institutions, and by farmers' cooperatives exempt under 26 USC 521; an LCA is neither (Securities Act Section 3 2026). Offering routes: Regulation Crowdfunding Rule 2026 allows up to $5,000,000 in twelve months; Regulation A Rule 2026 allows $20,000,000 (Tier 1) and $75,000,000 (Tier 2).

Staff precedent: the NCB No-Action Letter 2011 let a congressionally chartered member-owned cooperative bank issue Class C stock without registration on counsel's opinion that it was not a security, noting it was issued only as part of the patronage refund, evidenced membership, carried no ordinary dividend rights, was transferable only to eligible borrowers with approval and only at par, that NCB was subject to an alternative regulatory scheme, and that the position was on enforcement only. No later staff letter applying Howey to a cooperative membership bought to obtain services was found. The Cooperative Blockchain Coalition 2025 submission to the SEC Crypto Task Force states that the SEC has treated non-transferable cooperative memberships as non-securities but its rules contain no explicit safe harbor, and proposes one for memberships that are non-transferable except to the entity or members at a pre-approved price, pay dividends based on each member's own efforts, and are bought by verified users or suppliers; it is a stakeholder proposal and no SEC action on it was found.

State statute: Colorado exempts from the Colorado Securities Act any "security, patronage refund, per unit retain certificate, capital credit, evidence of membership, preferred equity certificate, or other equity instrument" that an LCA issues "as an investment in its stock or capital to the patron members", and relieves its directors, officers, members and salaried employees from broker-dealer registration for those issuances (Colorado LCA Securities 2024, C.R.S. 7-58-1009). Wyoming exempts a member's or owner's interest in, or a retention certificate or like security given in lieu of a cash patronage dividend by, a nonprofit membership cooperative organized under state cooperative laws, but not when sold to non-members (Wyoming Securities Act 2026, 17-4-201). Neither reaches the federal Securities Act.

## What this gives a design

A membership interest that is non-transferable except back to the entity at par, that carries no ordinary dividend, that is acquired as the means of using the service, and whose economic return arrives only as a patronage allocation tied to the member's own use, matches each feature the NCB No-Action Letter 2011 relied on and the Forman Opinion 1975 reasoning, and is covered by the Colorado exemption for the state securities law. It is not covered by a federal statutory exemption, so the federal position rests on the Howey analysis. Features that move toward a security: transferability at a market price, returns detached from the holder's own patronage, investor members whose return depends on the entity's managers, and promotional statements about profits. A tradable pool token whose price reflects yield earned by managers' efforts would have to be analyzed under the SEC CFTC Interpretation 2026 framework; that release is silent on it.
