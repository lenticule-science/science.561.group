**Strategy capacity** is the amount of capital a strategy can deploy before additional capital no longer earns an acceptable expected return at the margin. Capacity is not one universal assets-under-management threshold; it depends on the strategy, instruments, horizon, turnover, concentration, execution method, and required return.

Scale can reduce performance by moving prices, consuming available liquidity, worsening queue position, increasing time needed to trade, revealing information, or exhausting a finite set of opportunities. Financing, borrow, counterparty limits, regulation, mandates, and operational throughput can bind before market impact does.

Capacity analysis distinguishes total historical profit from **marginal** deployability. A strategy may remain profitable on its existing capital while a new dollar cannot be invested at the same expected return. Conversely, an account below a strategy's capacity is not automatically advantaged: fixed costs, minimum order sizes, diversification needs, data, and skill can create a lower feasible scale as well as an upper one.

A capacity estimate should state the assumed market volume, participation rate, impact model, turnover, holding period, signal decay, portfolio breadth, costs, and risk limits. Because those inputs change, capacity is an operating estimate to monitor rather than a permanent property of a named strategy.
