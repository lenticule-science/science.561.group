**Ludics** is Jean-Yves Girard's proof-theoretic framework that gives a central role to interaction rather than beginning with fixed formulas and inference rules. Its objects, called **designs**, can be read as proof-like strategies with polarized actions and addresses.

Two compatible designs interact through a normalization process. They are **orthogonal** when their interaction converges according to the framework's success criterion. A **behaviour** is a collection of designs closed under biorthogonality: it contains exactly the designs that pass all tests induced by its orthogonal collection.

The **daimon** is a special terminating action that can end an interaction. Its exact proof-theoretic interpretation should not be flattened into ordinary surrender, payoff, or market exit; those analogies omit the address, polarity, and normalization structure that makes the formalism distinctive.

Ludics is not the same as Caillois's classification of play. Nor does the existence of interaction and strategy vocabulary provide a quantitative model of prices. A financial application would need an explicit translation from market states, quantities, payoffs, and admissible actions into designs and orthogonality, plus results that survive the translation. Without that construction, a comparison is an analogy rather than financial theory.
