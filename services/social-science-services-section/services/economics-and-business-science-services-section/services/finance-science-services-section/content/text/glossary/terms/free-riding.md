In US securities-account usage, **free-riding** generally means purchasing a security without sufficient funds or credit to pay for it and then relying on proceeds from selling that same security to satisfy the purchase obligation.

The defining issue is payment for the purchase, not merely rapid trading. It differs from a [good faith violation](./good-faith-violation.md), which concerns selling a position bought with unsettled sale proceeds before the funds supporting that new purchase have settled.

Consequences depend on the account agreement, the broker's treatment of funds and credit, and applicable regulation. They can include a restriction requiring purchases to be fully paid with settled funds for a period. The exact trigger, duration, exceptions, and applicability should be taken from current primary rules and the broker's written policy.

Operational controls include maintaining a settled-cash balance, tracking settlement dates by asset and market, distinguishing buying power from withdrawable or settled cash, and rejecting orders whose payment source is not valid for the intended sale date.
