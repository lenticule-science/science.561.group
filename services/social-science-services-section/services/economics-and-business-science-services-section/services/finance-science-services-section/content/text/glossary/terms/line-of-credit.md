A **line of credit** is a revolving facility under which a borrower may draw up to an available commitment, repay principal, and reborrow during the facility's availability period, subject to its terms.

Interest is generally charged on outstanding draws, while commitment, unused-line, annual, origination, collateral-monitoring, or transaction fees may apply even when nothing is drawn. The rate can be fixed or floating. Availability may be a stated limit or a borrowing base calculated from eligible receivables, inventory, or other [collateral](./collateral.md).

A committed facility, an uncommitted facility, a demand line, and a credit card do not provide identical certainty. Covenants, representations, material-adverse-change provisions, defaults, lender discretion, and renewal dates can reduce or end access when funds are most needed.

Lines of credit often finance timing differences in [working capital](./working-capital.md), but repeated permanent borrowing can indicate a structural cash deficit better financed with longer-term capital or corrected operationally. Planning should model draws, interest, fees, covenant headroom, collateral availability, repayment sources, and a loss-of-access scenario.
