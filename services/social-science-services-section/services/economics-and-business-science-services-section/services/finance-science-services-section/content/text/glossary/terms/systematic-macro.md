**Systematic macro** trades global asset classes — currencies, interest rates, commodities, and equity indices — using signals and rules encoded in a model, executed according to that model's output rather than a portfolio manager's discretionary read of macroeconomic events. This distinguishes it from **discretionary global macro**, which trades the same broad asset classes based on a manager's judgment about economic conditions, policy, and geopolitics, without a fully codified rule generating the position.

Systematic macro strategies commonly combine several signal families across the traded markets: [trend-following](./trend-following.md) (positioning with an asset's own recent direction), carry (holding assets that pay a yield or roll advantage for holding them), and value (positioning based on a measured deviation from a fundamentals-based fair value), fused through a [portfolio-construction](./portfolio-construction.md) process into a single set of target positions across all the traded markets simultaneously.

Because a systematic macro model trades a small number of highly liquid futures and currency markets rather than thousands of individual securities, its breadth — the [fundamental-law-of-active-management](./fundamental-law-of-active-management.md)'s count of independent bets — comes primarily from diversifying across markets and signal types rather than from trading many names within one market, which shapes both its typical capacity (much higher than a small-cap equity strategy, per [strategy-capacity](./strategy-capacity.md)) and its typical instrument set (futures and forwards rather than cash equities).
