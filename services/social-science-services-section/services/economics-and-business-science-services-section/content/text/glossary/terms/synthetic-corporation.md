A synthetic corporation is an enterprise whose unified control and economic
identity is built out of a web of contracts, licenses, and equity-substitute
instruments rather than out of direct share ownership within one legal
entity. The company behaves, to outside investors and to the market, as one
integrated corporation — but the legal chain connecting the people who
control it, the entities that hold its assets and licenses, and the
shareholders who fund it runs through contract, not through stock.

The clearest documented example is the variable interest entity (VIE)
structure used by Chinese companies that list on foreign exchanges. Chinese
law restricts foreign ownership of businesses in sensitive sectors
(telecommunications, media, and other licensed industries). A company that
wants foreign capital anyway sets up a foreign holding entity (often in the
Cayman Islands), which cannot itself legally hold the restricted business
license. Instead, the license sits in a separate operating company owned by
local nationals, and a set of contracts — loan agreements, exclusive call
options, voting proxies, equity pledges, exclusive services agreements —
lets the foreign holding entity consolidate the operating company's
financial results and exercise practical control over it, without owning
its shares. Investors who buy stock in the foreign holding entity are
buying a contractual claim on a share of the operating company's profits,
not a direct ownership stake in its underlying assets. Alibaba is the
best-studied case: law professors Jesse M. Fried (Harvard Law School) and
Ehud Kamar (Tel Aviv University) describe this arrangement as "synthetic
control" — control "created synthetically with little or no equity
ownership via a web of employment and contractual arrangements" — in
"Alibaba: A Case Study of Synthetic Control," posted to the Harvard Law
School Forum on Corporate Governance on August 11, 2020, and published in
the Harvard Business Law Review. Their paper shows that Jack Ma, who by
2020 owned under 5% of Alibaba's equity, nonetheless controlled the
company by controlling Ant Group, which in turn controlled the VIE
entities holding Alibaba's Chinese operating licenses — control running
through a chain of contracts external to Alibaba's own capital structure
rather than through share ownership or a dual-class stock structure.

The word "synthetic" carries the same meaning across several adjacent
structures in corporate finance and tax law, which is why it generalizes
naturally to the corporation as a whole. A synthetic lease structures an
asset so that, for financial-accounting purposes, a special-purpose entity
owns it and leases it to the operating company, while for tax purposes the
operating company is treated as the owner and claims the depreciation and
interest deductions — the same underlying asset, two different legal
treatments assembled by contract. Synthetic equity, defined for U.S. S
corporations at 26 U.S.C. § 409(p)(6), is a stock option, warrant, or
similar right that gives its holder the economic benefit of owning stock
without an actual current ownership stake. A synthetic corporation applies
this same logic at the scale of the whole firm: the economic and
governance reality of a single, controlled corporation, assembled from
instruments and entities that are not, in strict legal form, one
corporation at all.

Firms build synthetic structures for the same handful of reasons in every
case: to route around a legal restriction (foreign-ownership rules, in the
VIE case), to separate accounting treatment from tax treatment (the
synthetic lease), or to grant an economic interest without transferring an
actual ownership stake (synthetic equity). The tradeoff is that the
contracts substituting for direct ownership are only as strong as their
enforceability — Fried and Kamar's paper flags that VIE contractual
arrangements have never been tested in a PRC court, meaning a synthetic
corporation's control can be less secure than the equivalent legal
ownership it stands in for, even while it looks identical from the
outside.
