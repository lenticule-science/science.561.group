# Scarcity-Bandwidth Replication Record

Companion bibliography to "The Bandwidth Tax, Audited" (2026-08-26). Each entry carries one status tag: **REPLICATED** (independently confirmed at comparable magnitude), **CONTESTED** (live methodological dispute, unresolved), **FAILED-REPLICATION** (a dedicated replication attempt returned null or reversed), **NULL** (a well-powered direct test found no effect), or **UNTESTED** (no direct test of the specific claim exists yet).

## Priming paradigm

**Shah, A. K., Mullainathan, S., & Shafir, E. (2012). Some consequences of having too little.** *Science*, 338(6107), 682–685. Foundational priming study: inducing thoughts of financial or time shortfall produces attentional "focusing"/tunneling and neglect-driven overborrowing in lab tasks. **CONTESTED** — the depletion sub-finding failed later replication (see O'Donnell et al. 2021 below); the focusing/tunneling result fares somewhat better but has not been independently re-tested at scale.

**Shah, A. K., Mullainathan, S., & Shafir, E. (2019). An exercise in self-replication: Replicating Shah, Mullainathan, and Shafir (2012).** *Journal of Economic Psychology*, 75(Part A). The original authors' own high-powered self-replication, prompted by an external failure to reproduce the "cognitive fatigue" (depletion) effect. Recovered some 2012 effects but not depletion. **FAILED-REPLICATION** (for the depletion component); **CONTESTED** (for the remainder).

**O'Donnell, M., Shah, A. K., Budiman, N., Goldstein, D. G., et al. (2021). Empirical audit and review and an assessment of evidentiary value in research on the psychological consequences of scarcity.** *PNAS*, 118(44), e2103313118. Preregistered high-powered replications of 20 studies sampled from the scarcity-priming citation network. Replication effect sizes smaller than originals in 80% of studies, directionally reversed in 30%, only 4 of 20 significant in the original direction. The single strongest surviving effect (recalled scarcity → reported back pain) has no clear mechanistic tie to bandwidth. **FAILED-REPLICATION** for the paradigm as a whole.

**Shah, A. K., Mullainathan, S., & Shafir, E. (2022). A scarcity literature mischaracterized with an empirical audit.** *PNAS*, 119(30), e2206054120. Rebuttal arguing several of the 20 sampled studies were not genuinely about resource scarcity, failed sampling criteria, or deviated procedurally from originals. **CONTESTED** — a dispute over which 20 studies should have been sampled, not a restoration of original-paper effect sizes.

**O'Donnell, M., et al. (2023). Reply to Shah et al. & Lynch et al.: In defense of replication.** *PNAS*, 120(26), e2304251120. Defends the 2021 audit's sampling and methodology against the 2022 rebuttal. **CONTESTED** — the exchange remains open at time of writing.

## Harvest-cycle (natural-experiment) paradigm

**Mani, A., Mullainathan, S., Shafir, E., & Zhao, J. (2013). Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function.** *Science*, 341(6149), 976–980. The flagship result: 464 Indian sugarcane farmers score worse on Raven's Matrices and a cognitive-control task pre-harvest (poor) than post-harvest (rich). Popular "~13 IQ points" framing is a rhetorical translation from the companion book, not a psychometric figure in the paper itself. **CONTESTED** — methodologically challenged at publication (see next entry) and not confirmed as bandwidth-mediated by the best subsequent field test (Fehr, Fink & Jack 2022, below).

**Wicherts, J. M., & Scholten, A. Z. (2013). Comment on "Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function."** *Science*, 342(6163), 1169. Raises three objections: dichotomizing continuous income can manufacture/inflate the interaction effect; possible ceiling effects on the cognitive-control task; pre/post design conflates poverty with retest/practice effects. **CONTESTED** — addressed but not fully resolved by the authors' reply.

**Mani, A., Mullainathan, S., Shafir, E., & Zhao, J. (2013). Response to Comment on "Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function."** *Science*, 342(6163), 1169. Defends the original design using continuous income and against ceiling/retest confounds; does not address the seasonal co-occurrence of the lean season with malaria burden, labor demand, nutrition, and sleep changes. **CONTESTED**.

**Fehr, D., Fink, G., & Jack, B. K. (2022). Poor and Rational: Decision-Making under Scarcity.** *Journal of Political Economy*, 130(11), 2862–2897. Zambian farmers (not Kenyan; not AER — a common misattribution). 5,842 trading decisions across the agricultural cycle: scarcity shifts behavior toward *more* rational trading, and this shift is explicitly not mediated by measured cognitive performance. **FAILED-REPLICATION** for the bandwidth-mediation mechanism specifically, in the closest comparable field design.

**Carvalho, L. S., Meier, S., & Wang, S. W. (2016). Poverty and Economic Decision-Making: Evidence from Changes in Financial Resources at Payday.** *American Economic Review*, 106(2), 260–284. RCT-style survey-timing design, 1,191 low-income US households. Found more present-biased monetary time preference pre-payday but no before/after difference in risk-taking, decision quality, cognitive-function-task performance, or heuristic/bias measures. **NULL** for the cognitive-bandwidth mechanism.

## Broader poverty-cognition correlational literature

**Schilbach, F., Schofield, H., & Mullainathan, S. (2016). The Psychological Lives of the Poor.** *American Economic Review: Papers & Proceedings*, 106(5), 435–440. Review essay proposing "bandwidth" as an organizing construct for poverty's psychological correlates (malnutrition, alcohol, sleep deprivation as bandwidth taxes). **UNTESTED** as a unified mechanism — a synthesis proposal, not a new empirical test.

**de Bruijn, E.-J., & Antonides, G. (2021/2022). Poverty and economic decision making: a review of scarcity theory.** *Theory and Decision*. Systematic review of scarcity theory's three core propositions (attentional focus → overborrowing; trade-off thinking → consistent consumption; reduced bandwidth → higher discounting/risk aversion). Finds the first two reasonably supported by the literature but the third — the proposition closest to the audited claim — **not conclusive**. **CONTESTED**.

## Cash transfers, cognition, and mental health

**Haushofer, J., & Shapiro, J. (2016). The Short-Term Impact of Unconditional Cash Transfers to the Poor: Experimental Evidence from Kenya.** *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 131(4), 1973–2042. GiveDirectly RCT: large increases in psychological wellbeing, strong consumption response ($158→$193 PPP/month at 9 months), investment in durable assets/livestock; no significant average cortisol effect. **REPLICATED** (wellbeing/consumption; later confirmed and extended by Egger et al. 2022 and the 2023–2024 Kenya UBI results, below).

**McGuire, J., Kaiser, C., & Bach-Mortensen, A. M. (2022). A systematic review and meta-analysis of the impact of cash transfers on subjective well-being and mental health in low- and middle-income countries.** *Nature Human Behaviour*, 6(3). Meta-analysis, 45 studies, ~117,000 people. Small but robust effects: subjective wellbeing d = 0.13 (95% CI 0.09–0.18), mental health d = 0.07 (95% CI 0.05–0.09) at ~2 years' average follow-up; transfer size predicts magnitude. **REPLICATED** — the single strongest meta-analytic result in this bibliography.

**Ridley, M., Rao, G., Schilbach, F., & Patel, V. (2020). Poverty, Depression, and Anxiety: Causal Evidence and Mechanisms.** *Science*, 370(6522). Review of bidirectional causal evidence: poorest are 1.5–3× more likely than richest to experience depression/anxiety within a location; income shocks cause mental illness; mental illness reduces income; cash transfers and psychological interventions each break the loop. **REPLICATED** (synthesis of a convergent multi-study literature).

## Cash transfers, venture, and investment

**Egger, D., Haushofer, J., Miguel, E., Niehaus, P., & Walker, M. (2022). General Equilibrium Effects of Cash Transfers: Experimental Evidence from Kenya.** *Econometrica*, 90(6) (Frisch Medal, 2024). $1,000/household to 10,500+ households across 653 villages (>15% of local GDP as fiscal shock). Large positive local-economy spillovers, minimal price inflation, local transfer multiplier ≈ 2.4. **REPLICATED**.

**Suri, T., Banerjee, A., Faye, M., Niehaus, P., et al. Kenya Universal Basic Income RCT (fielded from 2017; results reported 2023–2024).** ~23,000 people across two-year UBI, twelve-year UBI, and lump-sum arms. No clear labor-supply reduction, no compelling village-level inflation, lump-sum gains comparable to or exceeding the twelve-year stream (recipients used informal savings groups to convert streams into investment-financing lump sums). **REPLICATED** — the third independent large-scale confirmation of the no-idleness/investment-increase result.

## Procedural certainty (adjacent claim, no direct bandwidth test exists)

**Field, E. (2007). Entitled to Work: Urban Property Rights and Labor Supply in Peru.** *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 122(4), 1561–1602. Titling of 1.2 million urban squatter households raised household labor hours ~17%, cut in-home work 47%, reduced child labor 28%. **REPLICATED** for the labor-supply outcome; **UNTESTED** for any cognitive-bandwidth outcome (none measured).

**Hainmueller, J., Lawrence, D., Martén, L., Black, B., Figueroa, L., Hotard, M., Jiménez, T. R., Mendoza, F., Rodriguez, M. I., Swartz, J. J., & Laitin, D. D. (2017). Protecting unauthorized immigrant mothers improves their children's mental health.** *Science*, 357(6355). Natural experiment on DACA eligibility: mothers' eligibility significantly reduced children's adjustment and anxiety disorder diagnoses. **REPLICATED** for the mental-health outcome; **UNTESTED** for cognition specifically.

**Harknett, K., & Schneider, D. (2019). Consequences of Routine Work-Schedule Instability for Worker Health and Well-Being.** *American Sociological Review*, 84(1) (The Shift Project, ~60,000 hourly workers). Unpredictable scheduling associated with worse sleep, more psychological distress, lower happiness; schedule predictability outweighs wage level as a wellbeing predictor. **REPLICATED** for stress/wellbeing outcomes; **UNTESTED** for cognitive-bandwidth outcomes.
