# The Bandwidth Tax, Audited

## 0. Purpose

The note on hope, care, fear, attention, grace, and abandonment (2026-08-23) states, of Mullainathan and Shafir's scarcity-bandwidth findings: "This 'bandwidth tax' is the load-bearing empirical fact of the whole note." The companion note on freedom at the edge of the held builds its manufacture argument (§5) on the same footing, citing "the same farmers score markedly higher on cognitive measures post-harvest than pre-harvest" as part of its empirical case for provision-as-liberty-input.

A load-bearing claim carries the weight the corpus's grading convention exists for: an empirical premise this central should be graded, not merely asserted, and a claim left ungraded is a debt against everything built on it. This note pays that debt. It traces the replication record of the scarcity-bandwidth hypothesis from the original 2013 publication through the 2016–2026 literature — four evidentiary tracks, two adjacent claims the estate's notes also depend on — and states, as a blockquoted formal deliverable, the strongest version of the underlying empirical fact that survives. It then regrades the dependent passages in the anchor note and the freedom note against that survivor.

The verdict in one line: the specific psychometric story (scarcity taxes fluid intelligence by an IQ-scale amount) is fragile to failing; the general story (chronic insecurity taxes wellbeing and relief restores it, with cash unlocking investment rather than idleness) is robust. The estate's notes lean on both; only the second should bear weight going forward.

## 1. Track (a): the online/lab priming paradigm

The priming paradigm — inducing thoughts of financial shortfall, then measuring cognition or behavior — originates with Shah, Mullainathan & Shafir (2012), reporting that scarcity induces attentional "focusing" (tunneling) and neglect-driven overborrowing. The authors' own high-powered self-replication (Shah, Mullainathan & Shafir 2019) recovered some effects but not the "cognitive fatigue" (depletion) result, after Camerer's replication project failed to reproduce it.

The decisive test is O'Donnell, Shah, Budiman, Goldstein et al.'s "empirical audit and review" (PNAS, 2021): twenty studies sampled from the literature citing the seminal scarcity-priming paper, each given a high-powered preregistered replication. Result: replication effect sizes were smaller than the original for 80% of the twenty studies, directionally opposite for 30%, and only four of twenty reproduced a statistically significant effect in the original direction. One of the four surviving effects — that recalling scarcity elevates reported back pain — has no clear mechanistic tie to bandwidth at all.

The finding drew a public rebuttal, Shah, Mullainathan & Shafir's "A scarcity literature mischaracterized with an empirical audit" (PNAS, 2022), arguing several of the twenty sampled studies were mischaracterized, off-criterion, or procedurally altered; O'Donnell et al. answered in "Reply to Shah et al. & Lynch et al.: In defense of replication" (PNAS, 2023). The dispute concerns which twenty studies should have been sampled, not whether priming effects reach original-paper magnitude — even the rebuttal does not restore the pre-2021 standing of the paradigm.

**Grade: FAILED-REPLICATION, contested.** The mechanism most directly responsible for "provision returns expropriated attention" as an *online-priming* effect should not be cited as established.

## 2. Track (b): the harvest-cycle (natural-experiment) paradigm

The flagship result — Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir & Zhao, "Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function" (Science, 2013) — tested 464 Indian sugarcane farmers before and after harvest, finding worse Raven's Matrices and cognitive-control performance in the pre-harvest (poor) period than post-harvest (rich). The popular "~13 IQ points" figure comes from the book *Scarcity*'s rhetorical translation of this Raven's-score gap into an IQ-equivalent comparison to a night of lost sleep; it is not a psychometric result computed or reported as an IQ score in the Science paper itself, and it should be cited as an analogy, never as a measured quantity.

Wicherts & Scholten's contemporaneous comment (Science, 2013) raised three specific objections: dichotomizing continuous income into poor/rich manufactures or inflates interaction effects; the cognitive-control task may show ceiling effects; and a within-subject pre/post design conflates poverty with simple retest/practice effects. Mani et al.'s reply (2013) defended against all three but did not resolve the deeper confound that the pre-harvest lean season in this population co-occurs with the malaria season, elevated labor demand, and worse nutrition and sleep — so even a real effect need not isolate *money scarcity* from its seasonal companions.

The closest independent field test of the harvest-cycle logic is Fehr, Fink & Jack, "Poor and Rational: Decision-Making under Scarcity" — **published in the *Journal of Political Economy* (2022), among Zambian farmers, not the American Economic Review, and not Kenyan farmers**, a citation correction worth stating plainly since the misattribution circulates. Analyzing 5,842 trading decisions across the agricultural cycle, the authors find scarcity shifts farmers toward *more* rational trading (reduced exchange-asymmetry bias) — and this shift is explicitly **not mediated by any measured change in cognitive performance**. Working-paper versions of the same project found seasonal payday variation did not move executive-function scores except in the poorest sub-region, where income *uncertainty* (rainfall risk), not temporary liquidity, appeared responsible.

The cleanest real-world payday test in a different population, Carvalho, Meier & Wang, "Poverty and Economic Decision-Making: Evidence from Changes in Financial Resources at Payday" (American Economic Review, 2016; n = 1,191 low-income US households randomized to pre/post-payday survey timing), found more present-biased monetary intertemporal choice before payday but **no before/after difference in risk-taking, decision quality, cognitive-function-task performance, or heuristic/bias measures**.

**Grade: CONTESTED, non-generalizing.** The raw pre/post gap in the original study is real but its attribution to a bandwidth mechanism specifically (rather than confounded seasonal stressors or retest effects) has not held up; the two best subsequent field tests each come back null or non-mediating on cognition.

## 3. Track (c): the broader poverty-cognition correlational literature

Independent of both paradigms, poverty correlates with worse average performance on cognitive and executive-function measures — reviewed in Schilbach, Schofield & Mullainathan, "The Psychological Lives of the Poor" (AER P&P, 2016), and audited more skeptically in de Bruijn & Antonides, "Poverty and economic decision making: a review of scarcity theory" (*Theory and Decision*, 2021/2022). De Bruijn and Antonides evaluate scarcity theory's three core propositions — attentional focus causing overborrowing; trade-off thinking producing more consistent consumption; reduced bandwidth raising discounting and risk aversion — against the accumulated literature and find the first two reasonably supported, but **the third proposition, the one closest to the load-bearing claim under audit, "not conclusive."** A parallel meta-analysis of financial-scarcity-cue studies reports small, heterogeneous pooled effects, not an IQ-point-scale deficit.

**Grade: CONTESTED for the mechanism; NULL for effect-size claims at the scale popularly cited.** A correlation between poverty and cognitive-task performance is well established; a specific causal bandwidth channel, distinguished from chronic stress, nutrition, sleep, or reverse causation, is not.

## 4. Track (d): cash-transfer effects on cognition and mental health

This is the track that survives, though it licenses a mental-health claim, not a cognitive-restoration claim.

Haushofer & Shapiro's GiveDirectly Kenya RCT (*Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 2016) found large increases in psychological wellbeing from unconditional, concentrated transfers, alongside a strong consumption response (household consumption rising from roughly $158 to $193 PPP/month at nine months) and investment in durable assets and livestock; there was no significant average effect on cortisol, though effects appeared in some subgroups.

McGuire, Kaiser & Bach-Mortensen's meta-analysis (*Nature Human Behaviour*, 2022; 45 studies, ~117,000 people, LMICs) found cash transfers produce **small but statistically robust** improvements in subjective wellbeing (Cohen's d = 0.13, 95% CI 0.09–0.18) and mental health (d = 0.07, 95% CI 0.05–0.09) after roughly two years' average follow-up, with transfer size the strongest predictor of magnitude.

Ridley, Rao, Schilbach & Patel's review ("Poverty, Depression, and Anxiety: Causal Evidence and Mechanisms," Science, 2020) synthesizes bidirectional causality between poverty and common mental illness: within a given location, the poorest are typically **1.5–3× more likely** than the richest to experience depression or anxiety; negative income shocks cause mental illness, mental illness reduces income (closing a poverty-trap loop), and both cash transfers and psychological interventions independently break it.

**Grade: REPLICATED, at small-to-moderate effect sizes, for mental health and subjective wellbeing — not for fluid intelligence or working memory.** This is the strongest empirical leg of the whole family of claims, but it is a different leg than the one the anchor note names.

## 5. Adjacent claim (i): cash increases venture and investment, not idleness

The Kenya GiveDirectly evaluation and IPA/GiveDirectly follow-on work found no evidence of transfer-induced "laziness": recipients shifted occupational choice toward self-employment with no net reduction in total household labor supply, and no increase in alcohol spending. The general-equilibrium follow-up — Egger, Haushofer, Miguel, Niehaus & Walker (*Econometrica*, 2022; Frisch Medal, 2024), $1,000-per-household transfers to over 10,500 households across 653 villages, a fiscal shock exceeding 15% of local GDP — found large positive spillovers to non-recipients, minimal price inflation, and a local transfer multiplier of **~2.4**. The long-running Kenya UBI RCT (Suri, Banerjee, Faye, Niehaus and collaborators; ~23,000 people; 2023–2024 results) comparing two-year UBI, twelve-year UBI, and lump-sum arms found **no clear reduction in labor supply, no compelling village-level inflation**, and lump-sum transfers producing gains comparable to or exceeding the twelve-year stream, in part because recipients converted monthly income into lump sums through informal savings groups to finance larger investments.

**Grade: REPLICATED**, and robust across three independent RCT programs at increasing scale, over a decade.

## 6. Adjacent claim (ii): procedural certainty as an independent good

No study runs the Mani et al. cognitive battery before and after a tenure or status intervention. But three independent literatures converge on a stress/time-reallocation story that does not require a bandwidth mechanism:

Field, "Entitled to Work: Urban Property Rights and Labor Supply in Peru" (QJE, 2007): titling 1.2 million urban squatter households raised total household labor hours ~17%, cut in-home work 47%, and reduced child labor 28% — consistent with removing the need to physically guard untitled property, though this is a labor-supply outcome, not a cognition outcome.

Hainmueller, Lawrence, Martén et al., "Protecting unauthorized immigrant mothers improves their children's mental health" (Science, 2017): mothers' DACA eligibility, identified via a natural experiment, significantly reduced their US-citizen children's adjustment and anxiety disorder diagnoses — a clean causal demonstration that removing status uncertainty (not income) improves downstream psychological outcomes.

Harknett & Schneider's Shift Project ("Consequences of Routine Work-Schedule Instability for Worker Health and Well-Being," ASR 2019; ~60,000 hourly workers): unpredictable scheduling ("clopening," on-call shifts, short-notice changes) is associated with worse sleep, more psychological distress, and lower happiness, and schedule predictability is reported as a **stronger** predictor of worker wellbeing than wage level.

**Grade: REPLICATED for stress/mental-health/time-allocation outcomes; UNTESTED for cognitive-bandwidth outcomes specifically.** Procedural certainty is a supported independent good, but the estate's notes should stop calling on the bandwidth instrument to license it and cite the stress/mental-health instrument instead.

## 7. The strongest defensible formulation

> Chronic material and status insecurity — not momentary financial scarcity per se — robustly and causally worsens mental health (depression/anxiety prevalence 1.5–3× higher among the poor; Ridley et al. 2020), and relieving that insecurity — via cash (d ≈ 0.07–0.13 on mental health/wellbeing, McGuire et al. 2022; large self-reported wellbeing gains, Haushofer & Shapiro 2016), secure legal status (DACA/mental-health effects, Hainmueller et al. 2017), secure tenure (Field 2007), or predictable scheduling (Harknett & Schneider) — produces measurable, replicated improvements in stress, mental health, and time/attention reallocated toward productive investment (Egger et al. 2022; Suri/Banerjee 2023–2024; Field 2007). What has *not* survived independent replication at the claimed magnitude is the specific mechanistic story that acute scarcity taxes fluid intelligence/working memory by an amount comparable to a large IQ deficit, whether induced by priming (largely failed replication) or observed across a harvest cycle (confounded, contested, and not mediating behavior in the best subsequent field test).

## 8. What survives and what must be restated

**Anchor note, §1.1** ("Mullainathan and Shafir (2013) showed that scarcity itself... taxes cognitive bandwidth measurably and involuntarily, with effect sizes equivalent to substantial losses in fluid intelligence... This 'bandwidth tax' is the load-bearing empirical fact of the whole note.") **— must be restated.** The claim as written cites a contested psychometric-equivalence framing (§2 above) as settled fact and names it load-bearing for the whole structure. The load can be shifted to §7's formulation — a stress/attention-budget effect evidenced by mental-health and wellbeing instruments — without disturbing the note's C1–C3 structural consequences, which do not actually require the IQ-scale magnitude, only that scarcity/threat consume some cognitive-attentional resource involuntarily. That weaker, better-evidenced claim still supports rivalry (C1) and the impossibility-of-universal-attention argument (C2).

**Anchor note, §4.3** ("The bandwidth findings of Mullainathan and Shafir give this mechanism its empirical floor — scarcity and threat consume cognition involuntarily.") **— survives with restatement of the floor.** The terror-as-attention-extraction argument does not need fluid-intelligence loss specifically; chronic-stress and hypervigilance effects on cognition and mental health (Ridley et al. 2020, and the broader stress-and-cognition literature §3 above) supply an adequate floor. Replace "give this mechanism its empirical floor" with a citation to the chronic-stress/mental-health literature rather than the priming/harvest-cycle pair.

**Anchor note, §8** (the cycle's prediction: "standing-conferrals... should alter a population's *affective* economy — measurably, by the bandwidth instruments of §1.1"). **— the prediction survives; the named instrument must be restated.** Predict measurement by mental-health, stress-hormone, and subjective-wellbeing instruments (McGuire et al. 2022; Haushofer & Shapiro 2016), not by fluid-intelligence/working-memory batteries, which is where the mechanism has actually been tested and has come back null (Carvalho, Meier & Wang 2016) or non-mediating (Fehr, Fink & Jack 2022).

**Anchor note, §11, direction 2** ("Extend the bandwidth-tax paradigm to compulsory threat-simulation (§4.3): measure the attention returned by procedural-certainty interventions... in precarious populations.") **— survives as a research direction, already partially executed by literatures outside the estate's citation set.** §6 above shows the direction is not merely proposable but has independent supporting evidence (DACA, land titling, the Shift Project) — provided the measurement instrument specified is mental-health/time-allocation, not a cognitive-bandwidth battery, which is exactly the substitution this note argues for throughout.

**Freedom note, §5, P2 and C1** ("freedom from acute threat is, concretely, freedom from compulsory threat-simulation — an unburdened bandwidth budget, in the precise sense the scarcity research measures"; "the bandwidth tax of survival leaves nothing to fund the field"). **— the argument's shape survives; "in the precise sense the scarcity research measures" must be struck or softened.** The scarcity research, precisely, no longer measures what this clause claims it measures (§§1–3 above). The premise should instead read as an attention/stress-budget claim licensed by the chronic-poverty mental-health literature (§4, §7 above), which supports the same P1→C2 inference without borrowing a magnitude the priming and harvest-cycle literatures cannot support.

**Freedom note, §5, second strengthening** ("The same farmers score markedly higher on cognitive measures post-harvest than pre-harvest (the bandwidth-tax paradigm); unconditional cash-transfer trials find increased venture, investment, and risk-taking rather than the predicted idleness (Haushofer & Shapiro 2016; the Banerjee et al. 2017 review finding no work-reduction).") **— splits cleanly: retire the first clause, keep and strengthen the second.** The harvest-cycle clause should be dropped per §2 above. The cash-transfer/venture clause is the strongest-surviving empirical claim in this entire audit (§5 above) and can now cite three independent RCT programs across a decade (Haushofer & Shapiro 2016; Egger et al. 2022; Suri/Banerjee 2023–2024) rather than one review — if anything, this half of the argument should be given *more* prominence, not retired alongside its neighbor.

No downstream thesis in either note actually requires the retired magnitude; every structural argument that cited it can be re-grounded in the mental-health and cash-transfer literatures at the (smaller, but real) effect sizes reported in §4 and §5 above.
