Burnout is a condition of chronic exhaustion, depersonalization, or
reduced sense of accomplishment that can emerge when disaster response
stress is not adequately managed [@redcross2021].

Within emergent disaster response, the term matters because grassroots
response often depends on people working under intense pressure,
irregular schedules, traumatic exposure, and unstable resources.
Fernandes-Jesus and coauthors note that disaster communities often
decline as participants run out of energy and resources, while research
on emergency volunteers shows that emergency duties are associated with
higher emotional exhaustion and depersonalization [@fernandesjesus2021;
@redcross2021].

Burnout is not only an individual weakness. It is a sign that the labor
and care arrangements of a response effort are failing to sustain the
people carrying it.
