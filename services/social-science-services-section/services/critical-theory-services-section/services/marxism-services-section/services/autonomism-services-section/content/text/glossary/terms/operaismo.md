Operaismo (Italian: workerism) is the political and intellectual tendency that emerged in Italy in the early 1960s, associated with the journals *Quaderni Rossi* (1961–1965) and *Classe Operaia* (1964–1967) and with figures including Raniero Panzieri, [Mario Tronti](../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/mario-tronti.md), and Antonio Negri. Operaismo's foundational move is a Copernican inversion of the relationship between capital and [labor](../../../../../../../terms/labor.md): rather than studying capital's laws of motion and deriving the working class as a consequence, operaismo starts from working-[class struggle](../../../terms/class-struggle.md) and derives capital's development as a response.

This inversion has a concrete methodological form: *conricerca* (co-research or workers' inquiry), in which intellectuals and workers collaboratively investigate the conditions, practices, and spontaneous forms of resistance within the workplace. The aim is not to produce academic knowledge about workers but to produce knowledge with workers that serves their autonomous organization.

Operaismo's central theoretical contribution is the concept of [class composition](./class-composition.md): the specific technical arrangement of [labor](../../../../../../../terms/labor.md) (machinery, process, skill) determines the forms of resistance available, and working-[class struggle](../../../terms/class-struggle.md) forces capital to decompose and recompose [labor](../../../../../../../terms/labor.md) through technological restructuring. The automation of the factory is not progress but capital's response to workers' [refusal](../../../../anarchism/terms/refusal.md) to cooperate.

From operaismo emerged [autonomism](../schools/autonomism/index.md) and the broader politics of the [refusal of work](./refusal-of-work.md) — the claim that the most radical form of struggle is not the seizure of the means of production but the [refusal](../../../../anarchism/terms/refusal.md) to produce.
