Subrogation is the legal right by which an insurance carrier that has paid out a policyholder's covered loss "steps into the shoes" of that policyholder to pursue reimbursement from whoever actually caused the loss — a third party, or that party's own insurer.
The right is created by the insurance contract and by underlying tort or contract law, not by any market transaction between the recovering party and the party it recovers from, which is what distinguishes subrogation recovery from a brokered trade.

A 2026-08-28 research pass found insurance subrogation recovery structured almost entirely as **enterprise business-to-business** work: the named providers found — Cozen O'Connor, Crawford & Company, Sedgwick, Weightmans — are law firms and large claims-administration corporations serving insurance carriers recovering from other carriers or at-fault commercial parties, operating on [contingency](./contingency-recovery.md), and explicitly outside consumer-debt-collection protections because the accounts involved are commercial, not individual-consumer, relationships (search synthesis; see the [claims recovery mechanism record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-claims-recovery-mechanism/claims-recovery-survey.md)).
No solo-accessible path into this enterprise-scale market was found: it requires carrier claims-data access, adjuster relationships, and transaction volume a solo operator has no route into, independent of any licensing question.

A narrower, consumer-facing variant — **deductible recovery**, helping an individual policyholder recover their deductible from an at-fault party's insurer — was named in the sources found but not confirmed as operated by unlicensed solo actors.
Its closer legal analog is **public adjusting** — negotiating an insurance claim on a policyholder's behalf for a fee — which is licensed in nearly every U.S. state, with unlicensed practice a defined and enforced violation (the "Unauthorized Practice of Public Adjusting," per the National Association of Public Insurance Adjusters, read directly in substance): "you can't negotiate a claim with the insurance company on behalf of the owner without a license."
Whether deductible recovery specifically falls under public-adjuster licensing was not resolved in the 2026-08-28 pass and is flagged there as an open lead.
