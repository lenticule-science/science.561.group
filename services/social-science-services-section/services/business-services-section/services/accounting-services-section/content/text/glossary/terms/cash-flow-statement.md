A cash flow statement is a [financial statement](./financial-statements.md) that
explains the change in cash and cash equivalents over a period, ordinarily
classifying flows as operating, investing, or financing under the applicable
reporting framework.

Operating activities cover cash generated or consumed by the entity's core business — collecting payments from customers, paying suppliers, covering payroll. Investing activities track cash spent on or received from long-term assets like equipment, property, or securities. Financing activities capture cash raised from or returned to owners and creditors — issuing stock, borrowing, repaying debt, or paying dividends. Together, the three sections explain exactly why the entity's cash balance changed from the start of the period to the end.

The cash flow statement answers a different question from the
[income statement](./income-statement.md). Accrual income can include non-cash
items and recognized sales not yet collected. An entity can therefore report
profit while losing liquidity through slow collections, capital spending, or
debt service. Under the indirect presentation, operating cash flow reconciles
from accrual profit through non-cash items and working-capital changes; under a
direct presentation, major classes of operating cash receipts and payments are
shown. Neither presentation makes cash flow a complete measure of performance
or solvency.
