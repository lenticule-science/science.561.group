Reconciling [accounts receivable](../../../glossary/terms/accounts-receivable.md)
connects the general-ledger control account to a complete customer-level
population and to evidence that the recognized claims exist at the same cutoff.
Agreement between two internally derived totals is necessary but not sufficient.

**Steps:**

1. **Fix the subject and cutoff.** Identify the entity, control account,
   subsidiary ledger, currencies, and whether balances are gross, net of credit
   memos, or net of an allowance.
2. **Establish the population.** Include invoices, contract or performance
   evidence, receipts, credits, write-offs, disputes, and direct ledger postings
   through the cutoff. Test sequence gaps and subsequent activity for omitted
   or duplicated items.
3. **Sum customer balances.** Reconcile the subsidiary total to the
   [general ledger](../../../glossary/terms/ledger.md) and explain every
   difference. Common causes include payments recorded on one side, invoice or
   credit-memo mapping errors, foreign-exchange treatment, and direct postings
   that bypassed the subsidiary ledger.
4. **Test existence and rights.** Inspect support for material or unusual
   balances, related parties, negative customer balances, old items, and
   balances collected or disputed after cutoff. External confirmation may be
   appropriate when the assurance objective requires it.
5. **Prepare an aging schedule.** Age from the date required by the entity's
   policy—such as invoice or due date—and apply the buckets consistently. A
   common presentation is:

   - Current (0-30 days)
   - 31-60 days
   - 61-90 days
   - Over 90 days

6. **Assess collectability.** Apply the governing impairment policy.
   Age is evidence of collection risk, not an automatic write-off rule. Disputes,
   customer credit, collateral, subsequent receipts, and other available
   evidence also affect collectability.
7. **Post and retain supported adjustments.** Record omissions, applications,
   credits, write-offs, foreign-exchange effects, and allowances with source
   references. Retain the final control-account tie and unresolved items.

**Why aging matters:** A business might show \$100,000 in accounts receivable on
the [balance sheet](../../../glossary/terms/balance-sheet.md), but the face
amount alone does not establish existence, contractual rights, timing, or
collectability. The aging schedule supports those assessments; it does not make
them by itself.
