A ledger is a record of financial transactions organized by [account](./account.md), as opposed to the [journal](./journal.md), which organizes transactions chronologically. The process of transferring entries from the journal to the ledger is called *posting*. After posting, each account in the ledger shows all the [debits and credits](./debit-and-credit.md) that have affected it, along with a running or periodic balance.

The *general ledger* carries the posted accounts used to develop financial
reports. Some accounts are *control accounts* backed by subsidiary records: an
accounts-receivable control balance, for example, can be reconciled to customer
detail. “Authoritative” should refer to a governed record and its provenance,
not imply that a ledger total overrides contradictory bank, custody, physical,
or counterparty evidence.

The ledger makes account histories visible. Where the journal asks “what was
recorded together at this time,” the ledger asks “what recorded effects reached
this account.” Posting completeness can be tested in both directions between
journal and ledger, and balances should remain traceable to entries. That
cross-check proves neither that every source event entered the journal nor that
the accounting judgments were correct; source-to-book and book-to-source tests,
subsidiary reconciliation, and independent evidence address those questions.
