# Identify Evidentiary Formatting

Evidentiary formatting is often invisible at the moment of its effects. Institutions adopt community practices and call it engagement. This skill names the indicators that the adoption is driven by credentialing logic rather than genuine relation.

## When to use

You are observing institutional adoption of community practices and want to determine whether the institution is in a community relation or building an evidentiary artifact for external audiences.

## How it works

### Indicators

1. **Adoption without contact.** The institution produces the form (zine, garden, workshop) without participating in the community that originates it. The practice is adopted as an output, not as a relation.

2. **Documentation priority.** The institution emphasizes documenting the practice — photographing, counting participants, producing reports — more than the practice itself. Documentation signals evidentiary value (demonstrating something to external audiences) over functional value (serving the community).

3. **Standardization across the sector.** The practice becomes uniform across institutions. If every nonprofit in a region starts producing similar community publications at the same time, the adoption is driven by a shared template (legal, compliance, professional), not by independent community need.

4. **Originating community absent.** The institution claims proximity to a community through the practice but doesn't include community members in decision-making, design, or evaluation. The practice is performed *about* the community, not *with* it.

5. **Defensive framing.** Institutional representatives describe the practice in terms of what it *demonstrates* ("shows our commitment," "reflects our values") rather than what it *does* ("serves this specific community need," "responds to this specific condition"). Demonstrative language signals credential function.

### What to do when you identify formatting

- **Name it.** Use the vocabulary: evidentiary credentialing, legal formatting, compliance artifact. Naming makes the dynamic analyzable and communicable.
- **Trace the source.** Identify the legal precedent, compliance guidance, or professional template that initiated the adoption. Was there a specific proceeding? A sector-wide compliance update? A conference where the practice was recommended?
- **Assess the damage.** How has formatting affected the originating community's practice? Has meaning shifted? Has institutional adoption displaced community use? Or is the community practice continuing alongside without significant interference?
- **Share the analysis.** Communities whose practices are being formatted often experience the effects without having vocabulary for them. Sharing — in accessible language, not academic jargon — can help communities name what they are experiencing and consider their options.
