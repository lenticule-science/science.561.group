# Read a Case Sociologically

Reading a legal case sociologically means treating the proceeding as something that *produces* effects — formatted practices, propagating precedent, structural absences — rather than reducing it to its verdict. The verdict is the least interesting part; the structural effects are the analysis.

## When to use

You are analyzing the structural effects of a trial, prosecution, or civil suit on communities and cultural practices. The court has acted; you are reading what the action did, not whether it was right.

## How it works

1. **Identify parties and legal standing.** Who appears? Who has legal personhood in this proceeding? Who is affected but absent? The gap between participants and affected parties is analytically central.

2. **Catalog the evidence.** What cultural practices, institutional activities, or community relations were entered as evidence? For each, ask: what was this *in its social context* before it entered the courtroom? What proposition does it now support?

3. **Trace the formatting.** For each cultural practice entered as evidence, walk the four-step process:
   - **Selection** — what was selected, what was not?
   - **Decontextualization** — what social relations were stripped?
   - **Reattachment** — what legal proposition is the practice now attached to?
   - **Standardization** — can any institution reproduce it?

4. **Assess propagation potential.** Will the evidentiary moves propagate? Through which channels (direct citation, professional knowledge, institutional risk management)? How wide is the relevant institutional sector?

5. **Map the visibility gap.** Which communities are affected but have no legal standing? What perspective is absent from the legal record? How does the absence shape the court-produced facts?

6. **Predict structural effects.** From formatting and propagation, predict credentialing, homogenization, and displacement effects. What institutional behaviors will the precedent incentivize?

7. **State limits.** What community responses, adaptations, or resistances can the structural analysis not see? Where would ethnographic or community-centered inquiry add what this analysis lacks?

## Output

A reading should produce: the evidentiary moves and their formatting effects, propagation pathways and timescale, a visibility gap map, predicted structural effects, and an honest statement of analytic limits.
