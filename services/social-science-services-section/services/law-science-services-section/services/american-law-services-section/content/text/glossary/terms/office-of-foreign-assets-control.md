The Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) is the bureau within the US Department of the Treasury that administers and enforces economic and trade sanctions programs: designating parties to sanctions lists such as the Specially Designated Nationals List, promulgating and applying sanctions regulations such as the Global Terrorism Sanctions Regulations at 31 C.F.R. Part 594, and issuing the general and specific licenses that authorize otherwise-prohibited transactions.

OFAC's listing and enforcement role is distinct from the State Department's designation role: under Executive Order 13224 §1(b), the State Department makes the underlying terrorism determination in consultation with Treasury and Justice, while OFAC administers the resulting property-blocking and transaction-prohibition regime and controls licensing, so designation and listing/enforcement are sequential acts performed by different agencies rather than one agency's single decision.

Because OFAC alone controls licensing, a designated party's only lawful path to any continued US-nexus transaction runs through an OFAC-issued license, and the license's own scope and expiration date, not the designated party's preferences, set the boundary of what wind-down activity remains lawful. OFAC's issuance of Counter Terrorism General License 36 the same day it added Autistici/Inventati to the Specially Designated Nationals List illustrates both roles acting together; see [a research note on sanctions, designation, and hosting-provider law](../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/08/2026-09-08-note-on-sanctions-designation-and-hosting-provider-law.md) [@ofacgl2026].
