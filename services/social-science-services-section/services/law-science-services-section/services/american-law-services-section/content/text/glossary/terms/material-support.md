Material support is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. §2339A, which prohibits providing material support knowing or intending it will be used to commit an enumerated predicate offense, and 18 U.S.C. §2339B, which prohibits providing material support or resources to an organization the State Department has designated a Foreign Terrorist Organization, with "material support or resources" statutorily defined to include property, tangible or intangible, and services including training, expert advice or assistance, personnel, and communications equipment.

Section 2339A requires the government to show the support was linked to a specific predicate offense or that the defendant knew or intended it would be so used; Section 2339B requires no predicate-offense showing at all, criminalizing material support to a listed organization as such, which the Supreme Court confirmed in Holder v. Humanitarian Law Project, 561 U.S. 1 (2010), holding that even coordinated, nonviolent advocacy — training in international law, petitioning for peaceful dispute resolution — can constitute "training" or "expert advice or assistance" under §2339B without violating the First Amendment, so long as it is coordinated with the designated organization rather than wholly independent of it.

Holder v. Humanitarian Law Project reads "service" under §2339B broadly enough to reach coordinated, non-violent technical assistance, which bears directly on any organization supplying communications infrastructure to a party found to coordinate with a listed organization; but §2339B attaches specifically to the Foreign Terrorist Organization list (INA §219), not to the separate Specially Designated Global Terrorist list under Executive Order 13224, so providing infrastructure to a party designated only as an SDGT is a sanctions/IEEPA matter under 31 C.F.R. Part 594 rather than automatically a §2339B material-support offense, though a party could in principle appear on both lists. See [a research note on sanctions, designation, and hosting-provider law](../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/08/2026-09-08-note-on-sanctions-designation-and-hosting-provider-law.md) for the excerpted statutory text and the CRS overview this distinction rests on [@uscode2339a; @uscode2339b; @holderv2010].
