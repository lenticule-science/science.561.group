# Minnesota law for library materials policies

Two statutes set what a Minnesota library must have in writing about the
materials it holds. Both were read at the Revisor of Statutes on 2026-09-27.

## 1. Removal for viewpoint is prohibited (Minn. Stat. 134.51, subd. 1)

"A public library must not ban, remove, or otherwise restrict access to a book
or other material based solely on its viewpoint or the messages, ideas, or
opinions it conveys." Enacted by Laws 2024, chapter 109, article 7, section 2
**(primary: [Minn. Stat. 134.51](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/134.51))**.

"Public library" is wider than the word suggests (subd. 2(b)). It covers:

1. city and county public libraries organized under chapter 134;
2. libraries a city and a school district run jointly;
3. **every school district and charter school library or media center** under
   124D.991, including those of intermediate districts and cooperatives; and
4. public higher education libraries.

The "governing body" is the board or group that sets the library's policies:
a regional public library system's board, a multicounty multitype system, a
combination library, a school library's governing body, or any other public
library's (subd. 2(c)).

## 2. What a library may still decide (subd. 3)

A library may still decline to buy, lend, or shelve, or may remove or restrict,
material for legitimate reasons: practical ones (shelf space, rarity, damage,
obsolescence); legitimate pedagogical ones (whether a sensitive topic suits
the library's intended audience, curating a collection, or a likely material
and substantial disruption of school discipline); or compliance with state or
federal law. Parents', guardians', and adult students' rights under Minn.
Stat. 120B.20 (to review instructional materials and arrange alternatives) are
untouched.

## 3. The policy every governing body must adopt (subd. 5)

- **(a)** A policy establishing procedures for **selection of, challenges to,
  and reconsideration of** library materials, consistent with the section.
- **(b)** The policy must not limit the parental rights under 120B.20.
- **(c)** The policy must establish that selection and reconsideration are
  **administered by** one of:
  1. a licensed library media specialist (Minn. R. 8710.4550);
  2. **an individual with a master's degree in library science or library and
     information science**; or
  3. a professional librarian or a person trained in library collection
     management.
- **(d)** When a content challenge or reconsideration finishes, the governing
  body reports it to the commissioner of education: the title, author, and
  identifying information; the date, time, and place of any public hearing,
  with minutes or transcripts; the result; and a contact on the governing body.

Subdivision 4 protects employees: the governing body may not discriminate
against or discipline an employee for complying with the section.

The statute does not say the administrator must be an employee. A governing
body without one of the three qualified people on staff can name a contracted
one in its policy, which is the governing body's decision to make with its own
counsel.

## 4. School libraries: the collection development plan (Minn. Stat. 124D.991)

Since 2023 (Laws 2023, chapter 55, article 9, section 6) a school district or
charter school library or media center must, among other things, have **"a
collection development plan that includes but is not limited to materials
selection and deselection, a challenged materials procedure, and an
intellectual and academic freedom statement"**, and be "served by a licensed
school library media specialist or licensed school librarian"
**(primary: [Minn. Stat. 124D.991](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/124D.991))**.

Charter schools are generally exempt from statutes that do not name them
(Minn. Stat. 124E.03, subd. 1), but both 124D.991 and 134.51 name charter
school libraries expressly, so both reach them.

The two statutes fit together: 124D.991 requires the school's plan, and 134.51
fixes what the plan's challenge procedure may and may not do and who
administers it. A person with an MLS but no Minnesota school library licence
can administer reconsideration under 134.51, subd. 5(c)(2), but does not
satisfy 124D.991's staffing clause.

## 5. The situation the law meets

The 2024 Minnesota K-12 School Library Census found that 46 percent of
responding schools had no licensed library media specialist, and a quarter of
respondents could not find a qualified candidate (reported to the Senate
education finance committee on 2025-02-20:
[census report](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/fineduc/20250220/fineduc_20250220_MCMT-MN-School-Library-Report.pdf);
[Public News Service, 2025-05-06](https://www.publicnewsservice.org/2025-05-06/education/nearly-half-of-mn-school-libraries-lack-licensed-media-specialist/a96609-1)).
Every one of those schools still owes a 124D.991 plan and a 134.51 policy with a
qualified administrator. See the term
[library materials policy](../../../../../../glossary/terms/library-materials-policy.md).
