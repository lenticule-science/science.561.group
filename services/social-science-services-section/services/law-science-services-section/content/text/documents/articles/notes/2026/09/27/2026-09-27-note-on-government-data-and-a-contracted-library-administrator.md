# Government data and a contracted library administrator

A library board without a qualified person on staff can name a contracted one to
administer selection and reconsideration under Minn. Stat. 134.51, subd. 5(c)
(see [Minnesota law for library materials policies](2026-09-27-note-on-minnesota-law-for-library-materials-policies.md)).
That contractor then handles the library's government data. This note sets out
what follows, from the statutes as read at the Revisor on 2026-09-27.

## 1. The contractor is bound as the library is (13.05, subd. 11)

"If a government entity enters into a contract with a private person to perform
any of its functions, all of the data created, collected, received, stored,
used, maintained, or disseminated by the private person in performing those
functions is subject to the requirements of this chapter and the private person
must comply with those requirements as if it were a government entity. All
contracts entered into by a government entity must include a notice that the
requirements of this subdivision apply to the contract. Failure to include the
notice in the contract does not invalidate the application of this subdivision.
The remedies in section 13.08 apply to the private person under this
subdivision."
**(primary: [Minn. Stat. 13.05](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/13.05))**

So:

- The engagement letter must carry the subdivision 11 notice, and the obligation
  applies even if it does not.
- A request for data about a reconsideration goes through the library's
  responsible authority, and the contractor answers it as the library would.
- 13.08's remedies (damages, injunction, penalties) run against the contractor
  directly.

## 2. What 13.40 makes private, and what it does not

Libraries run by a government entity are administered under chapter 13
(13.40, subd. 1(b)). Private data on individuals (subd. 2(a)) are:

1. "data that link a library patron's name with materials requested or borrowed
   by the patron or that link a patron's name with a specific subject about
   which the patron has requested information or materials"; and
2. "data in applications for borrower cards, other than the name of the
   borrower."

**(primary: [Minn. Stat. 13.40](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/13.40))**

The section does not name the person who files a request for reconsideration.
Under 13.03, subd. 1, government data are public unless a statute or temporary
classification makes them otherwise
**(primary: [Minn. Stat. 13.03](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/13.03))**.
Two readings are possible, and the library's responsible authority, with its
counsel, chooses between them:

- **Public.** A reconsideration request is a filing with the governing body, not
  a request for materials, so the requester's name is public by default, and
  134.51, subd. 5(d)'s report to the commissioner already makes the title,
  hearing and result public.
- **Private.** A request that names a patron and the title they object to "links
  a patron's name with materials", so 13.40, subd. 2(a)(1) covers it.

Neither reading is settled by a court or an advisory opinion found on
2026-09-27. A policy should say which one the library applies, and the request
form should tell the requester before they sign. The house's policy templates
leave that sentence to the board.

## 3. What a contracted administrator keeps

A contractor who keeps the library's case files is holding government data. The
safer practice, and the one Binding Generations' pipeline follows, is to keep
case files in the library's own systems and keep in the contractor's records
only what the contract needs: dates, hours, titles, and outcomes, never a
requester's name, address or borrowing.

## 4. Employment, and the question still open

Subdivision 5(c) names qualifications (a licensed library media specialist; a
master's degree in library science or library and information science; a
professional librarian or a person trained in collection management) and says
nothing about employment. On its text a contractor who holds the qualification
satisfies it. No guidance from the Minnesota Department of Education's State
Library Services, the Minnesota Library Association or the school library
association addressing contractors was found on 2026-09-27. The question to put
to State Library Services (651-582-8200) is whether a governing body may name a
contracted individual as its administrator, and how the challenge report of
subd. 5(d) should name them. MDE publishes a "Reporting Form for Library
Materials Challenge" under State Library Services
([education.mn.gov/MDE/dse/Lib/sls](https://education.mn.gov/MDE/dse/Lib/sls/)).
Whether it is a form or a portal could not be confirmed because the page refused
automated reading.

## 5. Tribal libraries are outside 134.51

134.51, subd. 2(b) defines "public library" as four kinds: city and county
libraries organized under chapter 134; joint city and school district libraries;
school district and charter school libraries; and public higher education
libraries. A library a tribal nation operates is none of these, and a state
statute does not reach a tribal government's own institutions on the
reservation. Tribal libraries (Red Lake's Medweganoonind Library, White Earth's
Maamigin Achigaazo, Leech Lake Tribal College's library) set their own
policies.

One case is unclear. Fond du Lac Tribal and Community College is both a tribally
chartered college and a member of the Minnesota State system, so its Ruth A.
Myers Library may be a "public higher education institution" library under
clause (4). That is an inference from the text, not a ruling.
