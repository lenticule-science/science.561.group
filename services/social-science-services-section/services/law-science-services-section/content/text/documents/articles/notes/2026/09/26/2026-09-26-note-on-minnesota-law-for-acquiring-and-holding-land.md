# Minnesota law for acquiring and holding land

The [note on Minnesota law for assigning property contracts](2026-09-26-note-on-minnesota-law-for-assigning-property-contracts.md)
covers selling a right to buy land. This note covers the other side: getting
land for oneself, cheaply or free, and holding it. It serves two lines of the
561 Group's work, the Group buying rural land to keep and a Duluth resident
getting a city lot and a house on it (the
[note on land the Group keeps](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/public-economic-services-section/services/land-contracts-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-land-the-group-keeps.md)
and the
[note on a free Duluth lot and a house on it](../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-a-free-duluth-lot-and-a-house-on-it.md)).
The numbers and programs are in the
[economics note on free lots and holding land in northeastern Minnesota](../../../../../../../../../economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-free-lots-and-holding-land-in-northeastern-minnesota.md).

Sections read directly from the statute, regulation or agency page are marked
**primary**; the rest is marked **secondary**.

## 1. How a county sells tax-forfeited land (Minn. Stat. 282.01) — primary

[Tax-forfeited land](../../../../../../glossary/terms/tax-forfeited-land.md) is
held by the state in trust for the taxing districts and sold by the county.
Four routes out of that trust matter here.

**To anyone, at auction or over the counter.** Since 2024-07-01 every
forfeited parcel is offered at public auction (Minn. Stat. 282.005). St. Louis
County runs its auctions online only, through publicsurplus.com, in two
tracks: a *settlement sale* for parcels forfeited 2016 and later (first offered
with a residential-use restriction, then without), and a *new forfeiture*
auction whose minimum bid is the estimated market value. "Properties that do
not sell at auction may be available over the counter, first come, first
served on an Available List," and "offers to purchase for less than what is
listed cannot be accepted, pursuant to state statute" (St. Louis County,
Tax-Forfeited Land Sales page, read 2026-09-26). Lake, Itasca, Aitkin and
Carlton counties sell the same way with local differences (Lake runs an
estimated-market-value auction, then a minimum-bid auction at the tax debt;
Itasca takes a $500 bid deposit and a 5% buyer premium; Aitkin holds an oral
auction and lists unsold parcels over the counter for 30 days at the starting
bid; Carlton sells through MNBid and never below estimated market value;
county notices, 2025).

**On terms.** A county may sell on installments:

> "a payment of at least ten percent of the purchase price must be made at the
> time of purchase, and the balance must be paid in no more than ten equal
> annual installments, or (2) the payments must be made in accordance with
> county board policy, but in no event may the board require more than 12
> installments annually, and the contract term must not be for more than ten
> years." (282.01, subd. 4(a))

St. Louis County's terms for standard (non-settlement) sales are 10% of the
price or $500, whichever is greater, a contract of up to ten years, and 7%
interest; settlement sales "must be paid in full at the time of sale." The
buyer receives a certificate, not a deed, until the price is paid. Two
conditions bind a [terms sale](../../../../../../glossary/terms/terms-sale.md):

> "Standing timber or timber products must not be removed from these lands
> until an amount equal to the appraised value of all standing timber or
> timber products on the lands at the time of purchase has been paid by the
> purchaser." (subd. 4(a))

and missing an installment, the interest, "or the current taxes" is a default
on which the county board may cancel the certificate and resell (subd. 5).
Every sale also carries a 3% assurance fee (Minn. Stat. 282.221), a state deed
fee, recording, and the 0.33% deed tax (Minn. Stat. 287.21; none on deeds
under $3,000).

**To a city, below market, for affordable housing.** A county board may sell
nonconservation forfeited land to a governmental subdivision "for less than
their market value" when "the reduced price will lead to the development of
affordable housing" and the subdivision "has documented its specific plans
for ... developing affordable housing, and the specific law or laws that
empower it to acquire real property in furtherance of the plans" (282.01,
subd. 1a(d)). A city or state agency may also ask the county to withhold a
parcel from sale for six months while it decides (subd. 1a(a)). Under 1a(d),
the discount goes to a public body (a city, its housing and redevelopment
authority, or its economic development authority), never directly to a person
or company. A resident or builder receives the land from that body.

**To neighbors, for lots too small to build on.** City land "which cannot be
improved because of noncompliance with local ordinances regarding minimum
area, shape, frontage or access" may be sold by the auditor without a public
sale, restricted to adjoining owners, and "for less than its appraised value"
(subd. 7a). St. Louis County notifies adjacent landowners of sales but runs no
separate adjoining-owner program (county page; secondary for the absence).

## 2. What a free city lot obliges (the Rebuild Duluth contract) — primary

The Duluth Economic Development Authority (DEDA) is the body that takes
forfeited lots from the county under subd. 1a and passes them on. Its
2019–2020 program, Rebuild Duluth, stated its aim in its land sale agreements:
"to incentivize the construction of innovatively designed, affordable housing
units ... by providing vacant lots at no cost to qualified purchasers who have
been selected through an application process" (DEDA meeting packet,
2020-08-25). The city's call for the second round offered lots "at no cost to
individuals or organizations that can provide a viable plan for constructing
new housing on the site" (City of Duluth press release, 2020).

What "developing" meant in those contracts:

1. **Start.** "Within one year from the Closing Date, Buyer shall have devoted
   the Property to its intended use by commencing construction ... as
   evidenced by the issuance of a building permit and the filing of an
   affidavit ... to which are attached pictures of commenced construction."
2. **Finish.** By a stated date (for one 2020 lot, 2022-06-01 after an
   extension), "completing construction ... as evidenced by ... the issuance
   of the Certificate of Occupancy."
3. **Reversion.** "If Buyer fails to meet either the one-year deadline or the
   [completion] deadline ... DEDA may cancel the sale and title to the
   Property shall revert to DEDA." This is a
   [reverter](../../../../../../glossary/terms/reverter.md).
4. **Budget.** A cost overrun of 25% over the application's estimate let the
   buyer walk away or pay $5,500 for the lot instead.
5. **Affordability**, set per project: one builder agreed the finished house
   "will be sold for $150,000 or less" through One Roof's community land
   trust.

The contracts set no minimum house size and no owner-occupancy term. The
agreements executed in 2020 all went to builders (Ford Home Company, Hughes
Olsen Workshop LLC). The same shape recurs in small towns' free-lot programs
across the Upper Midwest: an application with a plan and proof of financing,
a start deadline, completion shown by a certificate of occupancy, a deposit or
reverter securing it, and sometimes a size floor or an occupancy term (the
economics note, section 2).

In 2025 DEDA used subd. 1a to buy forfeited parcels for the builder Dirt, Inc.,
"serving as the facilitator to allow the developer to acquire tax forfeit
parcels from the county for the purpose of infill housing development," with
the price paid to the county (DEDA special meeting minutes, 2025-06-30,
Resolutions 25D-29 and 25D-30).

## 3. Building it yourself (Minn. Stat. 326B.805) — primary

A residential building contractor license is not required of "an owner of
residential real estate who builds or improves residential real estate if the
owner occupies or will occupy the residential real estate for residential
purposes, or will retain ownership for rental purposes upon completion"
(326B.805, subd. 6). An owner who builds or improves more than one property in
24 months is presumed to be building to sell unless they keep the properties
as rentals. An LLC that builds a house to rent qualifies under the rental
branch. An LLC that builds to sell uses a licensed contractor. A building
permit is required either way.

## 4. What rural land costs to hold: tax classes and the forest incentive — primary

- **Class 2b rural vacant land** (Minn. Stat. 273.13): unplatted rural land not
  used for agriculture, "including land used for growing trees for timber,"
  with no structure on it, at a class rate of 1% of market value.
- **Class 2c [managed forest land](../../../../../../glossary/terms/managed-forest-land.md)**:
  at least 20 contiguous forested acres (up to 1,920 per taxpayer) under a
  forest management plan registered with the DNR and less than ten years old,
  applied for by May 1 for the next year's taxes, at 0.65%. It cannot be
  enrolled in SFIA at the same time (Minnesota Department of Revenue).
- **The [Sustainable Forest Incentive Act](../../../../../../glossary/terms/sustainable-forest-incentive-act.md)**
  (Minn. Stat. ch. 290C) pays owners of 20 or more contiguous forested acres
  who covenant not to develop them: in 2026, $12.13 an acre a year on an
  8-year covenant, $16.79 on 20 years and $21.46 on 50 years, for up to 1,920
  acres; payments are taxable income, and the Department of Revenue expects
  2027 payments to fall under 2025 legislation (Department of Revenue SFIA
  page). The land stays taxed in its own class (2b for vacant forest), so the
  payment is set against that tax.

## 5. Reselling vacant land on a contract for deed — primary

Land investors often resell rural land on a contract for deed: the buyer pays
over time and receives the deed at the end.

- The contract must be recorded by the buyer within four months (Minn. Stat.
  507.235).
- On default the seller cancels by a notice giving 60 days to cure for
  contracts made after 1985-08-01, or 90 days where the seller is an investor
  seller (Minn. Stat. 559.21).
- Chapter 559A's added protections (disclosures, churning limits) apply to
  investor sellers of *residential real property*, which it defines as "one to
  four family dwelling units, one of which is intended to be occupied as the
  principal place of residence" by the buyer (559A.01, subd. 9). A contract for
  deed on vacant rural land is outside chapter 559A.

## 6. Land and first-time-homebuyer standing — secondary

Minnesota Housing's first-time homebuyer has not had "an ownership interest in
a principal residence in the last three years" (Minnesota Housing, Start Up,
secondary summaries of the program manual). Owning vacant or rural land that
is nobody's residence does not end that standing, so one household member can
hold land while another keeps the standing that down-payment programs require.
The 561 Group's
[2024 planning statement](../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2024/12/27/2024-12-27-planning-statement.md)
already reasons this way ("Keep land owned by 1 person — meets first-time
home-buyer qualification").
