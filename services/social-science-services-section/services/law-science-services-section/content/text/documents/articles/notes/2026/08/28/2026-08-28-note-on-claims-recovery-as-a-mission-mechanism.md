# Note on claims recovery as a mission mechanism

**Claims recovery** is money already owed to someone who has not collected it: state unclaimed property, class action claim filing, medical bill auditing, and insurance subrogation and rebate/warranty capture are its four channels.
The full research behind this note lives at [`../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-claims-recovery-mechanism/claims-recovery-survey.md`](../../../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-claims-recovery-mechanism/claims-recovery-survey.md); this note carries its findings forward rather than summarizing them away.

## Seating derivation

A bounded `find` across `$SCIENCE/services/social-science-services-section/services/` turned up sixteen candidate sections: `ancestry-science`, `black-philosophy`, `business`, `critical-theory`, `education`, `human-geography`, `legalism`, `ludics`, `market-science`, `market`, `military-science`, `organization`, `public-history`, `social-history-science`, `sociology`, and `work`.
Two of those are live candidates for claims recovery on their names alone: `business-services-section` and `market-services-section`, and both have empty (zero-content) READMEs, meaning neither has an established convention to fork into.
A sibling agent's brokerage research is expected to seat under `market-services-section` in the same operating window this note was written, per the task instructions that produced this note — brokerage's core act is mediating a trade between a willing buyer and a willing seller, a genuine market relation.

Claims recovery has no market relation of that kind at its core.
In every one of the four channels this note treats, the claimant does not trade with anyone: they assert a pre-existing legal entitlement that already exists in their favor, created either by a state's unclaimed-property statute (the property already belongs to the owner; escheat did not extinguish that), by a certified class action settlement's own terms (membership in the class already qualifies the claimant), by the underlying billing or insurance contract (an audited error is a contract or coverage dispute, not a negotiation from scratch), or by a carrier's subrogated right against a third party (a right created by the policy and by tort or contract law, not by a market transaction).
The mechanism's crux question — is this the unauthorized practice of law, and where — is itself a legalism question, not a market-science or business-operations question: it asks what counts as "practicing law" and who may lawfully assert another person's legal claim for a fee, which is squarely the discipline `legalism-services-section` already holds.

This section already houses the closest available precedent.
The 2026-08-27 [note on mission-aligned service markets in land-tenure and probate records](../27/2026-08-27-note-on-mission-aligned-service-markets-in-land-tenure-and-probate-records.md) treats heirs'-property title-clearing — itself a claims-recovery-adjacent mechanism, reconstructing a claimant's chain of title to recover access to land they already co-own — and seats that finding directly under `legalism-services-section`, alongside the glossary terms [`heirs-property`](../../../../../../glossary/terms/heirs-property.md), [`partition-action`](../../../../../../glossary/terms/partition-action.md), and the pre-existing [`adjudication`](../../../../../../glossary/terms/adjudication.md) term, rather than pushing the material down into `american-law-services-section` despite the subject matter (USDA programs, the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act) being squarely U.S.-specific.
This note follows that same precedent for consistency: claims recovery seats directly under `legalism-services-section`, not under `american-law-services-section`, even though most of its concrete content — state unclaimed-property acts, state UPL bars, state public-adjuster licensing — is American law specifically.
That is a live boundary question, not a resolved one: if `american-law-services-section` develops a firmer convention for holding state-specific statutory material, this note's content is a reasonable future candidate to migrate there, and this note says so plainly rather than pretending the boundary is settled.

## The mechanism

A **finder**, **claims aggregator**, **bill auditor**, or **subrogation recovery firm** identifies money owed to a specific person or entity who has not collected it, and charges a fee — almost always contingent, a percentage of what is actually recovered — for the work of locating, documenting, and asserting the claim.
The mission fit is unusually direct for two structural reasons the [science-services-section STYLE.md](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/style.md)'s own authority ordering (theory and real research win over recency) asks every claim here to earn on its merits, not on the strength of the framing: the work is exhaustive document reading and name matching, which is exactly what parallel agent labour is suited to; and payment is contingent, so a failed attempt costs the claimant nothing and stakes no reputation, which matters directly for the mission-aligned-service-markets record's operating constraint (recorded in the sibling [revenue-operations service README](../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/services/561-group-operations-services-section/services/561-group-revenue-operations-service/README.md)) that reputation risk, not visibility, is this operator's binding constraint.

## Findings, channel by channel

**State unclaimed property** is the cleanest legal channel found.
Every U.S. state escheats abandoned financial property after a dormancy period and lets the rightful owner claim it back for free, directly, at any time; a finder identifies that property for a specific owner and charges a fee, typically a percentage of the recovered amount, for the service.
Four states were checked against primary or near-primary sources: **California** caps the fee at 10% (California Code of Civil Procedure §1582, search synthesis) and bars contact until the property is published or has sat one year with the Controller, whichever comes first; **Texas** caps at 10% (Texas Property Code §74.507, search synthesis for the cap itself) with a 24-month unenforceability window, but also requires a **Class A or Class C Investigations Company License** from the Texas Department of Public Safety's Private Security Bureau — read directly from [the agency's own page](https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/private-security/heir-finders-and-investigations-related-unclaimed-accounts), citing Texas Occupations Code §1702.104 — which converts Texas from a fee-compliance state into a licensing-barrier state; **Florida** tiers its cap at 15% (property held ≤24 months) rising to 25% (held longer), with total fees and costs capped at 30%, read directly from [the Florida Senate's own §717.135 text](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/717.135), and voids any agreement made within 90 days of the department's own owner-notification attempt or within 12 months of the property being reported, whichever is first (search synthesis for the 90-day/12-month clause); and **New York** caps at 15% of recoverable value, read directly from [New York Abandoned Property Law §1416 via the state Senate's own codified-laws page](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABP/1416), with no personal license requirement found in the section itself, only a written, witnessed, notarized, Comptroller-form-prescribed contract carrying a mandatory boldface disclosure that the owner can claim for free directly.
A secondary aggregator ([themissingmint.com](https://themissingmint.com/guides/state-finder-fee-cap-by-state), search synthesis, not independently verified row by row) reports the modal cap nationally is 10%, with a 15% cluster, and outliers as low as Washington's 5% and as high as Arizona's 30%.
No source found in this pass treats the core locate-and-file act itself as the unauthorized practice of law; the barrier where one exists is occupational licensing (Texas) or state registration of uncertain weight (Delaware and Pennsylvania, flagged **[UNVERIFIED]** in the source record), not UPL doctrine.

**Class action claim filing** on one's own behalf is unconditionally legal in every jurisdiction — asserting one's own legal rights is never the unauthorized practice of law.
Filing for others, for a fee, is a real operating industry (consumer apps named **Payout**, **ClaimHood**, **Settlemate**, and **DoNotPay**, charging 15-40% of payout or flat subscriptions around $15/month) that appears, by inference from who has and has not faced UPL litigation rather than from any stated rule, to survive by staying **ministerial** — matching a claimant's purchase or membership records against a settlement's own, already-adjudicated eligibility criteria, rather than exercising legal judgment about a contested claim.
Two real precedents bound this risk without deciding it: **LegalZoom** was sued in a certified class action in New Jersey over its online legal-document-preparation service, and **DoNotPay**, which markets itself as "the world's first robot lawyer" while being, in the plaintiff's words, "not actually a robot, a lawyer, nor a law firm," was sued in California state court on the same UPL theory.
The governing doctrine found — "the factor weighing most heavily toward a UPL finding is the collection of a fee for helping complete legal forms for someone else" — is real and cited (search synthesis, aggregated across bar-association ethics guidance), but this note's ministerial/judgment distinction is its own synthesis of the pattern, not a stated legal test, and is flagged **[UNVERIFIED]** as a rule even though the underlying industry pattern is real.

**Medical bill auditing** is functionally unregulated as a profession — no state license was found required to practice it (read directly in substance from [Wikipedia's Medical-bill advocacy article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical-bill_advocacy), itself a thinly-sourced article, so this absence-of-regulation finding is provisional) — with a real voluntary market-signaling credential, the **Board Certified Patient Advocate (BCPA)**, administered by the [Patient Advocate Certification Board](https://www.pacboard.org/eligibility/) (read directly), reachable via a bachelor's-degree or an equivalent-experience pathway, followed by a proctored biannual exam, renewable every three years.
Reported contingency rates cluster at 10-25% of documented savings (search synthesis), somewhat below this survey's own 20-35% starting anticipation, for reasons not resolved in this pass.
The channel's marketed error-rate claim does not survive scrutiny: the widely-repeated "75-80%" or "9 in 10 medical bills contain errors" figure traces, through a chain of secondary citation, to patient-advocacy organizations (**Medical Billing Advocates of America**, **CoPatient**) with a direct financial stake in a large number, and does not survive contact with the one rigorous, disinterested figure found — the **American Medical Association's own 2013 study, putting the error rate at 7.1%** of paid claims — or even the mid-range **2014 NerdWallet finding of 49% on Medicare claims specifically**.
One source traces the 75-80% figure further, to a 2011 Commonwealth Fund study whose actual finding was reportedly about the *percentage of doctor bills on people's credit reports*, from which an error-likelihood estimate was derived and multiplied through — a methodology, if accurately described, that measures bill-collection friction rather than audited billing-code error rate.
That provenance chain rests one level removed from primary (the Commonwealth Fund source itself was not located and read in this pass) and is flagged **[UNVERIFIED]**, but the pattern is clear enough to state plainly: this record found no primary evidence supporting the "8 or 9 in 10" figure, and it should never be repeated in 561 Group material as a factual claim without this caveat.

**Insurance subrogation and rebate/warranty capture** have no solo-accessible path found.
Subrogation is structured carrier-to-carrier or carrier-to-commercial-entity — the named providers (**Cozen O'Connor**, **Crawford & Company**, **Sedgwick**, **Weightmans**) are law firms and large claims-administration corporations serving insurance carriers, and "B2B recovery accounts... follow commercial collection rules," outside consumer-debt-collection protections (search synthesis).
Rebate and warranty capture is likewise enterprise-scale by its own market leader's description: **PRGX** states plainly that its "commercial recovery audit clients are typically Fortune 1000 companies... with multi-billion dollars of purchase transactions to be audited" (read directly in substance from [PRGX's own guide](https://www.prgx.com/guides/ap-recovery-audit-services-guide/)), and even the smaller-scale consultancy found (**Capture**, serving restaurant and retail clients) operates at a business-to-business, not individual-consumer, scale — no source found describes a viable solo, individual-consumer-facing rebate or warranty contingency practice, because per-claim dollar amounts at consumer scale are almost certainly too small to clear a viable percentage-of-recovery business model, a structural (market-size) barrier independent of any licensing question.
The consumer-adjacent edge of subrogation, **deductible recovery**, plausibly falls under **public-adjuster licensing** — a real, state-enforced regime distinct from UPL proper, under which "you can't negotiate a claim with the insurance company on behalf of the owner without a license" (read directly in substance, [NAPIA's Unauthorized Practice of Public Adjusting page](https://www.napia.com/uppa)) — though no source found names deductible-recovery-for-a-fee specifically as public adjusting, so this remains an open lead rather than a settled bar.

## Why this is not neutral with respect to the mission binding

The [root `CLAUDE.md`](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../CLAUDE.md) states the mission binding plainly: all work exists to serve the pursuit of Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty, and work that doesn't serve that binding doesn't belong here.
Claims recovery is not neutral with respect to that binding, and the mechanism explains why on its own terms, not by assertion: unclaimed property, uncollected class-action payouts, and unaudited medical overbilling are not randomly distributed.
They accumulate specifically wherever a person lacks the time, the paperwork literacy, the stable mailing address, or the institutional trust to chase a small, bureaucratically-gated claim through a state agency, a settlement administrator, or a hospital billing department — conditions that fall disproportionately on the same populations the 2026-08-27 heirs'-property record already found structurally dispossessed by the identical mechanism (intestate succession compounding into fractionated heirship, disproportionately among Black landowners in the U.S. South, per that record) and structurally analogous in Bureau of Indian Affairs trust-land probate.
Recovering money someone was never told they were owed is, in this specific and literal sense, recovering agency that bureaucratic friction — not any legitimate forfeiture — took from them; a claims-recovery practice built to serve Black and Indigenous claimants specifically, rather than the general public indiscriminately, would be doing the mission's work directly rather than merely operating without contradicting it.
This note does not resolve who the 561 Group's actual first clients would be — that belongs to the operations service, not to this note — but it records the finding that the *class of harm this mechanism reverses* is not evenly distributed, and that unevenness is itself mission-relevant, not incidental.
