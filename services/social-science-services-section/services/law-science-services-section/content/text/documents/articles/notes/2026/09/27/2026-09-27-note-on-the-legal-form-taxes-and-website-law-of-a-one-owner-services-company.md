# The legal form, taxes, and website law of a one-owner services company

This note answers the questions a person forming a small services company in
Minnesota asks after the ones the
[note on Minnesota law for forming and running an LLC](../26/2026-09-26-note-on-minnesota-law-for-forming-and-running-an-llc.md)
answers. It was written for Binding Generations, whose owner sells youth
library services, workshops, and some digital and physical goods, but it holds
for any one-owner services company.

## 1. Which legal form

People often say "corporation" for any company. Minnesota offers four forms to
one owner:

| Form | Filing | Governance the law asks for | Liability | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship, optionally with an assumed name (ch. 333) | $30 mail / $50 online for the name, plus newspaper publication | none | none: the owner answers for everything | owner's return, Schedule C |
| Limited liability company (ch. 322C) | $135 mail / $155 online | an operating agreement; no required meetings | the owner is not liable for the company's debts by being a member (322C.0304) | single member: disregarded, Schedule C; may elect S corporation |
| Business corporation (ch. 302A) | articles of incorporation | bylaws or statutory defaults, a board, officers, shareholder and board actions | shareholders shielded | C corporation unless it elects S |
| Nonprofit corporation (ch. 317A) | articles | a board of at least three directors; no private inurement | shielded | may seek 501(c)(3) exemption |

**(primary: [ch. 302A](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/302A),
[ch. 322C](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C),
[ch. 317A](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/317A))**

For one owner the LLC gives a corporation's liability shield with a fraction of
its formalities. That shield matters for a company that runs in-person
workshops with children and families. A C corporation taxes profit twice:
the federal 21 percent and Minnesota's franchise tax (9.05 percent for 2026,
[House Research](https://www.house.mn.gov/hrd/pubs/ss/sscorpft.pdf)), then the
owner's tax on dividends. A nonprofit cannot be owned or pay its founder its
profit, so it cannot be a company a person owns.

**The one place an LLC does not fit is grants.** Minnesota's Legacy Amendment
small grants go to nonprofits, educational organizations, government units, and
federally recognized Tribes ([Minnesota Historical Society](https://www.mnhs.org/preservation/legacy-grants)).
A for-profit company reaches that money as a **vendor** to an eligible
institution (a library, a regional library system, a school, a Tribe) that
holds the grant and contracts for the work, or through a fiscal sponsor for a
project of its own.

## 2. When an S corporation election pays

An LLC may elect to be taxed as an S corporation (IRS Form 2553). The owner
then takes a reasonable salary, on which payroll taxes are paid, and the rest
of the profit as distributions that owe no self-employment tax. The election
adds payroll and a separate corporate return, commonly $1,500–3,000 a year in
compliance cost; practitioners put the break-even at roughly $50,000–80,000 of
steady net profit. Below that, the default (Schedule C, 15.3 percent
self-employment tax on net earnings) costs less. The election is a tax choice
the company can make later; it does not change the form.

## 3. Minnesota sales tax by product line

| Line | Taxable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Consulting, policy drafting, collection audits | no | professional services are not among Minnesota's enumerated taxable services ([Revenue: taxable services](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/taxable-services-minnesota)) |
| Workshop and class fees | no | lesson and class fees are not taxable unless facility use is a significant part of the charge ([Revenue: nontaxable sales](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/guide/nontaxable-sales-12)) |
| Newsletters, blogs, digital booklists and guides | no | "periodicals, magazines, newspapers, blogs, or other news and information products" are excluded from digital books ([Revenue: digital products](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/digital-products)) |
| E-books and digital audiobooks sold directly | yes | "digital books or e-books" are specified digital products (same page) |
| Physical books, hand-bound books, zines, and other crafts sold | yes | tangible goods; "sales of arts and crafts, except for most clothing, are subject to sales tax" ([Revenue: arts and crafts](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/guide/arts-and-crafts)) |

A company that sells only services, workshops, and information products needs
no Minnesota sales tax account. It registers for one (free) before its first
taxable sale ([Revenue: registering](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/guide/registering-your-business)).

## 4. What the law asks of the website

- **Affiliate links.** A commission on a recommended product is a material
  connection the FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR part 255) require be
  disclosed clearly and next to the recommendation, not only on a separate
  page ([FTC: what people are asking](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)).
  A booklist with Bookshop.org links (10 percent to non-bookstore affiliates)
  carries its disclosure at the list.
- **Claims about the business's history.** Section 5 of the FTC Act
  (15 U.S.C. 45) prohibits representations likely to mislead a reasonable
  consumer on a material point ([FTC Policy Statement on Deception, 1983](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements/410531/831014deceptionstmt.pdf)).
  How long a business has operated and what it has done are material to a
  buyer choosing a consultant. Publication dates on a company's writing are
  such a representation, so a page carries the date it was actually first
  published. Work first published elsewhere keeps its original date and says
  where it first appeared.
- **Children's privacy.** COPPA applies to sites directed to children under 13
  or with actual knowledge of collecting their personal information
  ([FTC: COPPA six-step plan](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business)).
  A site that sells to libraries and families collects sign-ups from the adult:
  a registration form asks the parent or organizer, never the child.
- **Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act** (in force 2025-07-31) applies only to
  controllers processing data of 100,000 Minnesota consumers a year, or 25,000
  with a quarter of revenue from selling data; small businesses as the SBA
  defines them are mostly exempt ([Minnesota Attorney General](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Data-Privacy/Exemptions/)).
  A plain privacy statement is still good practice.
