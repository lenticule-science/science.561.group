# Mission-aligned service markets in land tenure and probate records

## Scope and canonical owner

This note's subject is **mission-aligned service markets**: work that serves Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty and has a real buyer with a real budget, in the specific frame of land-tenure and probate-records work.
It is not about agent labour marketplaces (a sibling 561 Group workstream) and not about security-bounty payout economics (another sibling workstream); neither is cited further here.

Land-tenure and probate-records work could plausibly sit in several children of `social-science-services-section/services/`: business, work, market, legalism, public-history, or human-geography.
This note lands in **legalism** (not its `american-law-services-section` child, whose existing material is a criminal-procedure and national-security-law curriculum — the USA PATRIOT Act, adversarial procedure, prosecutorial discretion — a different American-law subfield entirely, in a lesson-curriculum genre that does not match this section's own plain-glossary convention) for a specific reason: every deliverable this market actually sells is an **adjudicative work product**.
A title chain exists to be presented to and accepted by a court in a [partition action](../../../../../../glossary/terms/partition-action.md); a cultural-affiliation finding exists to be accepted or contested under a federal administrative process; a BIA heirship determination exists to be accepted by a probate judge.
That is squarely legalism's own stated territory — "how does a legal system decide what counts as a crime, what makes a contract enforceable, who has standing to bring a claim" — and legalism already carries directly adjacent vocabulary this note leans on: [evidentiary credentialing](../../../../../../../../../sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/evidentiary-credentialing.md), [adjudication](../../../../../../glossary/terms/adjudication.md), [due process](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/due-process.md), and burden of proof.

## The spine: heirs' property title-clearing

The mechanism, carried in full in the [source record](../../../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-27-mission-aligned-service-markets/service-market-survey.md): Black families lose land through fractured chains of title caused by generations of [intestate succession](../../../../../../glossary/terms/intestate-succession.md) — an owner dies without a will, the property passes to all descendants as undivided tenants in common, and each subsequent intestate death multiplies the number of co-owners, producing [fractionated heirship](../../../../../../glossary/terms/fractionated-heirship.md).
Clearing one parcel means reconstructing deed, probate, marriage, death, and census records across decades and often several counties, then building an heir tree that supports a full [chain of title](../../../../../../glossary/terms/chain-of-title.md) back to the original decedent.

This is the load-bearing claim for why the market is a genuine parallel-agent opportunity rather than a generic services idea: **exhaustive multi-county record reconstruction across decades of intestate succession is work no human does economically at volume.**
A human researcher can search one county's deed index, one probate docket, one set of vital records at a time, at a billed rate; the same task run across the dozens of counties a single fractionated Southern family's land history can touch, for the hundreds of thousands of parcels the literature describes as affected nationally, is precisely the kind of exhaustive, parallelizable, low-judgment-per-record retrieval-and-cross-reference task that a fleet of agents can attempt at a scale and cost no billed-hourly human researcher can match — while the judgment-bearing step (the title opinion itself) still requires the attorney sign-off noted below.

**This is an existing commercial category with published pricing, not a hypothesis** (search synthesis): named vendors **HeirSearch**, **Heir Pros**, **Morse Genealogical**, **Discovering Connections**, and **DavisDNA** sell heir search to attorneys and estates today; non-percentage firms charge roughly **$60 per year of research** plus roughly **$175 per person located**, while contingency-fee firms take **20–30% of the estate** recovered.

**Buyer-side funding is real and named**, not inferred: USDA's **Section 2501** program funded **31 grantees a combined $22.6 million in FY2024**, up to **$250,000** each, explicitly for land-title-issue resolution, and its grantee list names the **Center for Heirs' Property Preservation** — a real, operating organization built around this problem.
USDA's separate **Heirs' Property Relending Program** carries **$67 million**, later expanded past **$100 million**, with title searches, surveys, and document preparation named as eligible costs.
Roughly **twenty states** have adopted the **Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act**, which creates the [partition actions](../../../../../../glossary/terms/partition-action.md) that generate ongoing, court-facing demand for exactly this deliverable.

**The same capability serves the second mission pillar directly.**
Bureau of Indian Affairs trust-land probate presents the structurally identical problem — fractionated heirship under a different trust-law regime — and BIA's FY2024 budget added staff specifically for a probate backlog, a named agency signal rather than an assumed need.
One skill, both mission pillars: this dual applicability is why heirs'-property title-clearing anchors this note rather than sitting beside NAGPRA and interpreter work as a peer finding.

**Honest risks, carried rather than omitted.**
Many rural Southern county archives hold the underlying records only on paper, unindexed and undigitized — this reintroduces paid human document retrieval as a real marginal cost per county, meaning the work does not collapse to a pure database query even with full agent automation of the cross-referencing step.
Title *opinions* require a licensed attorney's sign-off in essentially every U.S. jurisdiction, which makes this a **paralegal-tier input** to a legal product rather than a standalone product sellable direct to a court or a title insurer.
Established incumbents (the five vendors named above) already occupy the niche, so entry means undercutting or displacing an existing priced competitor, not creating demand from nothing.
And **[UNVERIFIED]**: whether nonprofit intermediaries in this space hold *discretionary* procurement budget, as opposed to grant money earmarked for their own staff headcount, was not established in this research pass — this is the single fact that decides whether the nonprofit-buyer path is real or illusory (the attorney/estate commercial-market path does not depend on it), and it is the first thing to verify before committing further effort there.

## NAGPRA cultural-affiliation research — recorded, with the hazard as the main finding

The **January 2024 NAGPRA rule** set a hard deadline of **2029-01-10** for museums and agencies to complete consultation and file updated inventories; as of 2025 only **48%** of reported remains had been returned, and boutique NAGPRA consultancies already bill institutions for this work (search synthesis).
The hazard is the reason this market is recorded narrowly rather than pursued as a business line: the 2024 rule deliberately **privileges tribal oral history and tribal determination over documentary evidence**, closing the old "culturally unidentifiable" loophole that previously let institutions withhold items for lack of a documentary chain.
A tool skilled at exactly the documentary cross-referencing this note's spine relies on could as easily be hired to build a case for **withholding** an item — contesting a claimed affiliation on documentary grounds — as for repatriating one.
Mission alignment here is not a property of the skill at all; it depends entirely on who the buyer is, which shrinks the addressable pool toward tribes, tribal consortia, and pro-repatriation firms specifically, away from the larger population of museums and universities who hold most of the deadline-driven budget.
See [nagpra](../../../../../../glossary/terms/nagpra.md) for the term itself; this note states the hazard plainly so it is checked before any future engagement, not discovered after.

## Indigenous-language interpreter support — the product is the interpreters' tool

California immigration courts have a qualified-interpreter pool of only a few dozen people covering 24+ Indigenous language communities — Mixtec, Zapotec, Mam, K'iche', Triqui, and others — a documented, real 2026 due-process crisis (search synthesis).
Live courtroom interpretation is liability-critical work done by certified humans, and these languages are extremely low-resource for any model, so the product is a triage and terminology-support tool sold to the interpreters themselves and to the legal-aid organizations and courts that schedule them. It multiplies a scarce, paid human rather than replacing one.

## Other markets, and the way into each

- **Indigenous-rights ESG data feeds to Equator Principles banks** — RepRisk already screens ~90,000 sources daily across 125,000 companies and is Bloomberg-integrated; institutional ESG procurement runs through existing vendor relationships, so the way in is a specialist feed sold to RepRisk-class aggregators or to the indigenous-rights advocates who pressure the banks.
- **BIA individual Indian trust-account underpayment audits** — Cobell v. Salazar's $3.4B settlement already litigated this exact grievance; the ongoing work is individual allottees' account questions, reached through tribal realty offices and legal-aid programs.
- **Tribal water-rights adjudication monitoring** — no existing market found at all; unresolved whether this is a genuine unserved gap or simply too bespoke per adjudication to support a repeatable offering.
- **Satellite encroachment monitoring for EPA GAP-funded tribal environmental offices** — GAP funds tribes' own internal capacity, and this pass could not confirm GAP-funded offices hold independent vendor-purchasing authority at all.
- **Reparations claims verification** (California SB 518 / CAFAA; Evanston) — newly legislated with no open contract yet, and Evanston's existing documentation bar is shallow and staff-administered; the way in is the documentation service claimants need as programs open.
