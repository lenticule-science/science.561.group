# Purpose and relation to the saved research

The Leader requested that the complete pen-name, character, and legal research be put into the Group's systems. This dossier provides the evidential layer behind the already saved [comparative legal note](2026-09-29-note-on-fictional-ai-author-personas-and-publishing-law.md). It does not replace that synthesis, adopt Maren Rooke, or certify a launch. The companion character proposal preserves the full invented biography, scenes, interview, correction rehearsal, and portfolio rationale; those are creative proposals, not factual claims about a human author.

The subject is a publishing practice, not merely a chatbot. A novel's byline, the provenance of its text, an author-character's fictional history, a seller's identity, an advertising image, a testimonial, and a persistent interactive service can each raise a different question. Applying one label to all of them loses the distinction the research needs to preserve.

This is a bounded review of US federal materials, selected California and New York measures, EU frameworks, and relevant UK, Canadian, Australian, and mainland Chinese materials. It is not a fifty-state survey, an audit of national implementation throughout the EU, or a survey of all Sinophone jurisdictions. Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia, Japan, and other potential destinations remain separate research tasks where an actual distribution plan requires them. A jurisdiction not discussed is not a jurisdiction cleared.

# Research method and evidential status

Primary legislative, regulator, government, and platform pages were searched and opened on September 29, 2026. The source register supplies stable identifiers, URLs, document types, and relevant locators. Legal descriptions below are paraphrases rather than reproductions of statutes or third-party articles. The register does not represent a complete downloaded archive or claim preservation of source-byte hashes. A later evidentiary archive should record authorized source snapshots, retrieval timestamps, and content digests separately.

The following grades must remain distinct when this material is ingested into other services:

- **Law or enacted text:** a legal instrument, subject to scope, effective dates, amendments, and interpretation.
- **Official guidance:** an authority's explanation; useful evidence of interpretation, not a new statutory clause or universal safe harbor.
- **Platform policy:** a channel's admission or account conditions, not public legislation.
- **Application:** the researcher's reasoning about a proposed activity; conditional until the actual product and operator are known.
- **Proposal:** an editorial or operational choice offered to the Group, not an adopted requirement.
- **Unknown:** a missing fact or review; never silently promoted to permission.

# Source-by-source findings and their boundaries

## US-01 — Commercial factual claims and the overall impression

The FTC's policy statement explains its general Section 5 deception analysis: material representations or omissions likely to mislead reasonable consumers in context can be deceptive. Objective claims require substantiation; an advertisement's overall impression includes implied claims, images, and juxtaposition. Only that general analysis is used here, not the document's physical-product US-origin standard [@us01, section III].

**Application:** a retailer page should not use fictional ancestry, employment, or personal experience as factual evidence of a real writer's authority. This is not an assertion that every imagined biography is unlawful, or that every traditional pseudonym requires public disclosure of a human's private identity. The question depends on what the commercial presentation represents.

## US-02 — Virtual influencers, fictional dramatization, and customer evidence

FTC staff guidance expressly distinguishes virtual influencers from prohibited fabricated reviews. A visibly fictional dramatization is not automatically a consumer testimonial. A generated spokesperson does not, however, make a false underlying customer experience genuine. The guidance also addresses misleading claims that controlled review entities are independent. Staff answers are not a compliance safe harbor [@us02].

**Application:** the Group may develop several fictional authors with different tastes. It should not treat their existence as evidence that several independent customers bought, loved, or reviewed a title. A fictional interview is a performance, not a press clipping. Keep that distinction when a passage is reused in promotional material.

## US-03 — Endorsements and relationships between pen names

The Endorsement Guides FAQ explains that material relationships may need disclosure, that recommendations must reflect genuine experience where they purport to do so, and that disclosure placement depends on the presentation. It specifically discusses reciprocal author review arrangements. The Guides explain Section 5 principles rather than themselves imposing a separate penalty schedule [@us03].

**Application:** two house-owned names can appear in an openly identified house feature; changing names does not establish independent endorsement. A relationship disclosed on a profile may be absent when a post is encountered on its own. Review the actual use and excerpt, not only the account biography.

## CA-01 — The enacted California bot law has a particular trigger

SB 1001's chaptered text concerns online bot use intended to mislead people in California about artificial identity for specified knowingly deceptive commercial or electoral purposes. It supplies a clear-and-conspicuous disclosure route and definitions for bots and platforms. It does not simply classify every pseudonymous byline as a prohibited bot. The definitions and limits require careful reading; a platform-size definition is not automatically an exemption for every person using a bot [@ca01, sections 17940–17943].

**Application:** assess any automated public author account separately from the printed book. Recheck current codification and the precise service facts before relying on a narrow exception.

## CA-02 — Sustained social interaction is a separate product category

SB 243 defines companion chatbots through adaptive human-like interactions and capacity to meet social needs or sustain relationships, with specified exclusions. Its enacted provisions address nonhuman disclosure, self-harm prevention/referral protocols, notices, and additional measures for known minors, including periodic reminders during continuing interactions. Section 22603 expressly begins annual reporting July 1, 2027; that reporting date should not be confused with all substantive duties [@ca02, sections 22601–22605].

**Application:** persistent fan correspondence designed around attachment to Maren needs a separate review. Calling it fiction, a novelist, or customer service does not determine classification. A static author page is not, by that fact alone, this interactive service.

## NY-01 — Entirely invented apparent humans can be synthetic performers

The current New York section 396-b display defines synthetic performers to include certain digital assets appearing to be human performers without being recognizable as an identifiable natural performer. Covered commercial advertisements knowingly using them require conspicuous disclosure. The section includes expressive-work promotion, audio-advertising, translation, and advertising-medium provisions with specific boundaries [@ny01, subdivisions 1 and 3–8].

**Application:** avoiding a celebrity clone does not alone settle the treatment of a photorealistic author advertisement. Nor should the expressive-work provision be treated as an automatic exemption for every ad selling a novel. Review what the performer does in the work and in the advertisement. Existing publicity and other rights remain separate.

## EU-01 — Separate the Article 50 obligations

The official Article 50 presentation distinguishes provider duties for direct AI interaction, provider marking of generated or manipulated outputs, and deployer disclosures for deepfakes and qualifying public-interest informative text. Relevant creative works receive an adapted manner of deepfake disclosure, not an undifferentiated exemption. Article 50(5) concerns intelligibility, first exposure or interaction, and accessibility. The page's amendment warning limits reliance on it as a fully current consolidated text [@eu01].

**Application:** classify each proposed surface and whether the Group is acting as provider, deployer, or both. Do not derive a uniform novel-cover label or a universal fiction exemption from a provision addressing a different activity.

## EU-02 — Interpretation, timing, and review must not be simplified away

The Commission FAQ treats plausible synthetic subjects as relevant to its deepfake analysis, alongside resemblance and potentially false appearance of authenticity. It distinguishes public-interest text from other published text and substantive review/editorial control from superficial checks. It states August 2, 2026 applicability and describes a limited marking transition for certain pre-existing systems, not a general delay for deployers. Its explanations are interpretive guidance [@eu02].

**Application:** do not base this launch on an assumed transition exemption. Identify the actual editorial responsibility, service role, and asset context. A health or science post in the author's voice requires a different analysis from a scene in her invented port.

## EU-03 — A voluntary code is an implementation aid

The Commission describes the transparency code as a voluntary practical route for demonstrating compliance with relevant marking and labeling duties. Its legal and technical role should not be confused with either the statute itself or the separate general-purpose-AI code. This pass reviewed the official overview and FAQ, not every measure in the full code [@eu03].

**Application:** later engineering work may compare proposed labels and provenance handling with the code, recording the exact version and measures used. The present research does not certify an implementation against that code or claim that every code recommendation is independently mandatory.

## EU-04 — Consumer protection is not replaced by an AI label

The Commission's unfair-commercial-practices overview places consumer protection across commercial conduct before, during, and after transactions. It provides the framework reference for misleading sales presentation; this research did not audit every member state's implementation [@eu04].

**Application:** a disclosure mechanism should be assessed together with the sales page's overall claims and the actual identity of the seller. Technical compliance with a content-marking mechanism is not evidence that unrelated statements about a product or business are accurate.

## EU-05 — Reader correspondence is personal data, not free character memory

The EU business guide distinguishes controller and processor responsibilities, lawful processing bases, specified purposes, transparent information, transfers, and individual rights. It identifies additional conditions for sensitive categories, including health, sexual orientation, and beliefs. Consent is one legal basis, not a word to append to every processing activity [@eu05, relevant headings].

**Application:** decide whether letters are retained for replies, editorial feedback, research, or publication; those purposes are not interchangeable. Do not automatically copy intimate correspondence into a shared agent memory or fictional canon. Treat processor access and international transfer as separate questions from whether a reader agreed to receive a reply.

## UK-01 — The commercial-practices regime is not an AI-fiction ban

CMA207 explains the DMCC Act framework for commercial practices from April 6, 2025, including misleading actions, omissions, and specified prohibited practices such as fake reviews. Some assessments concern likely effects on transactional decisions; not every provision has an identical test [@uk01].

**Application:** review the total author and product presentation, including commercial intent, factual provenance, and reviews. This source does not establish a single UK rule requiring identical labels on all AI-written novels. Any launch assessment must identify the actual practice and applicable provision rather than generalize from the word AI.

## UK-02 — Computer-generated-work questions differ by jurisdiction

The government's 2026 report discusses section 9(3)'s arrangements-based treatment of computer-generated works and uncertainty about its interaction with originality. It also discusses policy options. A report proposing or evaluating reform is not proof that the reform has been enacted. The direct statutory endpoint was unavailable in this pass [@uk02].

**Application:** a US copyright conclusion should not silently become the whole international rights analysis. Equally, the UK discussion is not a promise of exclusive worldwide rights in every machine-generated paragraph. A licensing offer should state only the rights the actual contracting party can substantiate.

## CAN-01 — Material commercial relationships matter

Competition Bureau guidance applies deceptive-marketing principles to those promoting a business interest. It calls for prominent disclosure of relevant connections and accurate accounts of actual experience in reviews, with attention to how the message appears across devices and services [@can01].

**Application:** describe house-produced promotional features as such. The fictional author's literary independence, including disagreement with another character, is different from economic independence from the publisher. Do not let the former imply the latter where that would affect a recommendation's apparent credibility.

## CAN-02 — Literal truth does not exhaust the representation

The Bureau's false-representations guidance addresses material misleading claims and their general impression, including false affiliations. A presentation can therefore require examination beyond a technically accurate fragment in isolation [@can02].

**Application:** check combinations of portrait, biographical text, byline, and production note. Keeping each sentence defensible in isolation is not the same exercise as checking what those sentences and images ask a buyer to believe together. This is an applicability question, not a conclusion that any particular Maren page has already violated the Act.

## AU-01 — The cited enforcement example has a limited evidential status

On March 24, 2026 the ACCC announced that PhotobookShop paid penalties following infringement notices alleging misleading influencer reviews. Its account concerns undisclosed compensation and the presentation of review material. The announcement is not a court judgment, and this dossier does not treat payment as a judicial finding [@au01].

**Application:** the example illustrates why compensated or controlled recommendation practices warrant review. It does not establish a standalone Australian synthetic-author statute or give blanket clearance to AI material that carries a disclosure.

## CN-01 — Publication through covered services brings labeling questions

The Chinese measures distinguish visible and implicit labels, set requirements for specified providers and distribution services, and require users publishing generated material through covered services to declare it and use labeling functions. Article 10 restricts malicious removal or falsification of specified labels. Article 9's conditional unlabelled-output provision does not cancel separate publication duties. The measures took effect September 1, 2025 [@cn01].

**Application:** Chinese-drama influence alone is not the territorial test. Identify the distribution service and audience actually being served. Labeling is not authorization for mainland publishing, licensing, or adult-content distribution. The English account here is a paraphrase of the Chinese original.

## IP-01 — Human contribution, not the sound of the byline

The US Copyright Office's Part 2 summary ties copyrightability to human determination of sufficient expressive elements. It recognizes potentially protectable human selection, arrangement, and modification while distinguishing prompts alone from authorship of resulting expression [@ip01].

**Application:** the author-character may be a useful public attribution without resolving the legal authorship of each component. Keep the human contribution record separate from the simulated novelist's fictional memories and from the mere fact that a human ordered or approved production.

## IP-02 — Registration disclosure and correction are concrete processes

The published registration guidance requires applicants to describe human contributions and disclose generated material, explicitly excluding more-than-de-minimis AI material from the claim. It says not to list the AI technology or its supplier as author merely because it was used. It also explains correction of pending applications and supplementary registration for previously registered works [@ip02, section IV].

**Application:** do not infer that a pen name or corporate credit supplies missing human expression. If a registration exists, review its actual application before deciding whether correction is needed. This research did not inspect a registration for Ice Out and does not say that one exists.

## IP-03 — Pseudonyms, commissioned works, and inspiration are different issues

The Copyright Act's definitions recognize pseudonymous works and separately define derivative works and works made for hire. Sections 102–103 distinguish protected expression from ideas and address protection of derivative material without converting pre-existing material into the later author's property [@ip03].

**Application:** C-drama-inspired pacing or themes do not establish permission to reproduce a particular drama's dialogue, characters, images, or music. Commissioning work does not by itself prove that all work-made-for-hire conditions were met. Obtain a scoped rights analysis for any actual borrowed assets rather than assuming either that inspiration is forbidden or that a genre label licenses copying.

## PLATFORM-01 — KDP's disclosure classification follows production facts

KDP requires reporting generated text, images, and translations to the platform, including generated material substantially edited afterward. Its AI-assisted category instead concerns human-created content aided by specified AI processes. The policy places responsibility for compliance and rights on the publisher [@plat01, AI content section].

**Application:** fill the platform fields from element-level production evidence, not from whichever label is more commercially attractive. This is a platform reporting requirement; it is not itself a statute prescribing identical reader-facing disclosure everywhere.

## PLATFORM-02 — A pen name does not replace the account holder

KDP permits pen names but calls for the underlying real account information needed for payment and tax purposes. It also constrains changes to a published primary-author field and calls for consistency across author metadata and book assets [@plat02].

**Application:** the repository currently naming 561 Group does not prove an edition is live on KDP. Inventory actual listings before deciding how a proposed byline change would be handled. Preserve historical editions and provenance; do not silently convert an old production record into a new fictional human attribution.

## PLATFORM-03 — D2D eligibility is not satisfied by disclosure alone

Draft2Digital's guidelines reject entirely AI-generated content without extensive human editing and also address solely machine-produced mass content. They distinguish AI-assisted work from that category [@plat03].

**Application:** revision by further AI agents is not evidence that a human performed the editing the policy names. Conversely, the presence of some human editing is not an acceptance guarantee. Assess the actual work against the current channel and downstream partner rules, with uncertainty left visible.

## NAME-01 — Clearance requires more than an exact-name web search

USPTO guidance describes comprehensive clearance using similar marks and related goods or services, including common-law uses and additional resources beyond federal registrations [@name01].

**Application:** the limited exact-name and author-variant web searches for Maren Rooke did not establish an existing exact-match novelist, but no trademark, retailer, domain, or handle clearance was completed. Similar spelling and pronunciation also matter. Neither Aster Quay nor The Late Office has been commercially cleared by this work. No name or account was reserved.

# Proposed disclosure specimens and failure tests

These specimens are editorial proposals for testing and legal review, not prescribed statutory language or a safe harbor.

**Author introduction:** “Maren Rooke is a fictional author-character created and performed using AI by the 561 Group.” Add an accurate sentence about the publishing operator and a real contact route once those are established. Do not fill unresolved contracting-entity details with an invented company form.

**Fiction entry point:** “Maren's fictional life.” Introduce the ferry office and her relationships beneath that frame. When a scene is posted independently, carry enough context for that separate encounter to remain intelligible. The same invented scene may need different surrounding text in a book, a retailer panel, and an advertisement.

**Book production note:** keep Ice Out's true existing AI-agent provenance. A future edition can state its presentation under an approved author-character without claiming that a real human with that biography created the expression. Human drafting, editing, translation, and selection claims should describe actual contributions, not merely the existence of an approval button.

**House feature:** identify an interview, discussion, or recommendation as a publisher-produced feature where that is what it is. A fictional interviewer should not be credited as an independent news organisation; an exchange among house characters should not be displayed as unrelated customer reviews.

**Interactive entry:** plainly state the user is interacting with AI performing the character, identify the operator, and provide the relevant service/privacy information. This wording alone does not satisfy every duty of a companion or other regulated service; it is one part of a separate assessment.

Four useful failure tests are: would the presentation suggest a real human childhood; would it suggest human production not supported by records; would it imply independent endorsement; and would a reader know whom to contact about a purchase or correction? These are proposed tests of communication. They are not exhaustive legal definitions.

# Evidence packets to request before a launch decision

The following work products are proposed inputs to the existing publication-pipeline planning, not newly appointed offices or mandatory house-wide artistic rules.

**Identity and name packet.** Candidate spelling, pronunciation, intended genres and services, exact and similar-name searches, registries consulted, retailer collisions, domains and handles actually checked, unresolved rights, dated findings, and decision authority. Search evidence supports a decision; lack of hits is not exclusivity.

**Authorship and rights packet.** Exact manuscript and asset digests; human and model contributions at the level needed to explain each claim; commissioned or licensed inputs; permissions by use and territory; actual ownership basis; and any existing registration. It should be possible to answer what changed between editions without altering earlier evidence.

**Channel packet.** Dated policy observations, actual production classification, edition-specific eligibility reading, disclosures submitted, distributor response if any, and remaining partner restrictions. A research note is not proof the platform accepted the book.

**Presentation packet.** Final copy and assets as rendered on representative mobile and desktop surfaces, including excerpts encountered without a profile visit. Record disclosures, links, reader-comprehension observations, revisions, and the exact approved version. Store private testing data separately from public narrative history.

**Territory and interaction packet.** Contracting seller, markets intentionally served, relevant service roles, location of processing and providers, interaction capabilities, minors exposure, data purposes, and the applicable legal review. A static release can be evaluated without assuming a later fan-chat service has also been accepted.
