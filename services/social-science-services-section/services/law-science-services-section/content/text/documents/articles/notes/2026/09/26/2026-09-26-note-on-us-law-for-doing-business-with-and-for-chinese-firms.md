# US law for doing business with and for Chinese firms

The companion economics note,
[What Chinese firms going abroad buy from US residents](../../../../../../../../../economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-what-chinese-firms-going-abroad-buy-from-us-residents.md), maps
the services. This note records the law those services run under, as of
2026-09-26. Much of it is on timers (suspensions, truces, rules phasing in),
so every dated item below is a date to re-read. Items read from a primary
source (a statute, regulation, court opinion, agency notice or official data)
are marked **primary**; the rest is law-firm and trade-press synthesis.

## 1. Tariffs on Chinese goods

- **IEEPA tariffs are gone.** In *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*
  (consolidated with *Trump v. V.O.S. Selections*, No. 24-1287), decided
  2026-02-20, the Supreme Court held 6–3 that the International Emergency
  Economic Powers Act does not authorize tariffs **(primary:
  [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf))**.
  CBP stopped collecting the "fentanyl" and "reciprocal" tariffs on
  2026-02-24. Importers claim refunds through CBP's CAPE process, opened
  2026-04-20; the government has appealed the Court of International Trade's
  refund order ([Holland & Knight, 2026-06](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-government-appeals)).
- **Section 122 bridged, then lapsed.** A universal 10% surcharge under
  Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 ran from 2026-02-24 until its 150-day
  limit ended it on 2026-07-24.
- **Section 301 forced-labor tariffs replaced it.** From 2026-07-24, USTR
  imposes an additional duty on 60 economies judged to lack an enforced ban on
  forced-labor imports: 12.5% for China, the EU and Brazil, 10% for economies
  with a ban or a trade commitment **(primary:
  [USTR fact sheet](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/july/fact-sheet-ustr-section-301-action-response-failure-60-economies-ban-imports-produced-forced-labor);
  [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/07/actions-by-the-united-states-in-the-investigations-under-section-301-of-the-trade-act-of-1974-of-the-acts-policies-and-practices-of-60-economies-related-to-the-failure-of-each-economy-to-impose-and/))**.
  Sources differ on how it stacks: Holland & Knight and C.H. Robinson describe
  it as additional to most other duties, with exceptions for USMCA goods and
  products under Section 232; Zonos describes it as topping the combined rate
  up to 12.5% ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/and-the-tariff-beat-goes-on);
  [C.H. Robinson](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/blog/section-301-forced-labor-tariffs/);
  [Zonos](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/country-guides/united-states/section-301-forced-labor)).
  Read the Harmonized Tariff Schedule chapter 99 heading for the entry itself.
- **China's own Section 301 lists are untouched**: 7.5% to 100% by list, EVs at
  100%, with 178 product exclusions extended to 2026-11-09/10
  ([USTR, 2025-11](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2025/november/ustr-extends-exclusions-china-section-301-tariffs-related-forced-technology-transfer-investigation)).
- **Section 232 sector tariffs stand**: steel, aluminum and copper (up to
  50%), autos, a narrow semiconductor tariff (from 2026-01-15), and 100% on
  patented pharmaceuticals phased in 2026-07-31 and 2026-09-29.
- **The Busan truce holds.** The 2025-10-30 Trump–Xi arrangement (US fentanyl
  tariff cut, Chinese rare-earth controls paused, Chinese soybean purchases of
  25 million tons a year for 2026–2028) was extended on 2026-09-23 to
  **2027-01-10** ([White House fact sheet, 2025-11](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-strikes-deal-on-economic-and-trade-relations-with-china/);
  [NBC News, 2026-09-23](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/us-china-extend-trade-truce-trump-rcna599525)).

The general concept is the glossary's [tariff policy](../../../../../../../../../sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/governance-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tariff-policy.md).

## 2. De minimis is gone for every country

Duty-free entry under Section 321 ended for Chinese and Hong Kong goods on
2025-05-02 and for all countries on 2025-08-29. CBP made the suspension
indefinite by two interim final rules effective 2026-06-24, and moved mail to
a postal informal entry process under 19 CFR part 145 with full compliance by
2026-10-22 **(primary:
[Federal Register 2026-12670](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other);
[2026-12669](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry))**.
Postal entries may be filed only by owners, purchasers or licensed customs
brokers.

## 3. Importing for someone else

- **Customs business for another party needs a broker license.** Entry,
  classification, valuation and duty payment on another's behalf is "customs
  business" under 19 CFR part 111 and requires a licensed
  [customs broker](../../../../../../glossary/terms/customs-broker.md); an importer acting solely for its own
  account, and its own employees, does not (19 CFR 111.2(a)(2)(i))
  ([eCFR 19 CFR 111](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-111)).
  So the Group can import for itself (buying goods and reselling them) with no
  license, and can do everything around a Chinese seller's import (finding the
  broker, the bond, the warehouse; holding the FDA, FCC or CPSC role) without
  one; filing the entry for the seller is the broker's.
- **Foreign importers of record are being squeezed.** A Chinese company can be
  its own [importer of record](../../../../../../../../../economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/importer-of-record.md) through a US surety bond, but
  Executive Order 14411 (2026-06-03) directs CBP to raise bonding and domestic
  asset requirements, bars foreign importers from informal entries, and
  requires foreign importers on formal entries to be CTPAT-validated or file
  through a CTPAT-validated broker, phasing in over 180 days
  ([Brownstein](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/executive-order-tightens-cbp-requirements-for-u-s-importers-of-record/);
  [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/executive-order-14411-foreign-importers-record-rules.html)).
  This is the legal reason Chinese sellers now want a US company and a US
  person as importer.
- **Forced labor.** Goods made wholly or partly in Xinjiang, or by an entity on
  the UFLPA Entity List (last updated 2026-08-03), are presumed made with
  forced labor and denied entry unless the importer rebuts by clear and
  convincing evidence **(primary:
  [Federal Register 2026-15628](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/03/2026-15628/notice-regarding-the-uyghur-forced-labor-prevention-act-entity-list))**.
- **CPSC eFiling.** From 2026-07-08, certificate-of-compliance data for
  regulated consumer products is filed at entry, with no low-value exception
  ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2026/CPSC-Implements-Mandatory-eFiling-for-Certificates-of-Compliance-Targeting-Dangerous-Foreign-Imports)).
- **Chemicals.** TSCA import certification is filed at entry
  ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/tsca-import-export-requirements/tsca-requirements-importing-chemicals)).

## 4. Roles a US-located person must hold

- **FDA US agent.** A foreign food facility names a [US agent](../../../../../../glossary/terms/fda-us-agent.md)
  with a US street address, available in US business hours, who receives FDA
  communications for it (21 CFR 1.227; [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/85098/download)).
  Foreign medical device establishments do the same under 21 CFR 807.40, and
  each device registration names its agent in the FDA's public data
  **(primary: [openFDA](https://open.fda.gov/apis/device/registrationlisting/))**.
  Food facilities renew in the biennial window 2026-10-01 to 12-31; device
  establishments renew every year in the same October–December window. No
  license is needed to be a US agent.
- **FCC responsible party.** Under the Supplier's Declaration of Conformity,
  the responsible party must be located in the US and able to produce records
  and samples (47 CFR part 2, subpart J). Since 2025 the FCC has barred test
  labs tied to foreign adversaries from the authorization program, withdrawing
  recognition from 20+ Chinese labs ([FCC fact sheet](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-411206A1.pdf);
  [Cooley, 2025-11-05](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-11-05-fcc-acts-in-campaign-against-bad-equipment-test-labs)),
  so testing is routed to recognized labs.
- **Amazon insurance.** Mainland-China sellers in the enhanced-safety
  categories buy their $1M liability coverage through Amazon's Insurance
  Accelerator from 2026-11-02. Placing insurance for others needs a state
  producer license.

## 5. Who we may deal with: screening

Providing ordinary commercial services to a private Chinese company is lawful.
What is not is dealing with a blocked or restricted party, or "facilitating" a
transaction a US person could not do directly (OFAC), or supporting listed
military, intelligence or weapons end uses (EAR 15 CFR 744.6), and OFAC
liability for dealing with a blocked party is strict. So every counterparty,
and any owner named, is checked against the
[consolidated screening list](../../../../../../glossary/terms/consolidated-screening-list.md) before an engagement and before each
significant payment. The list is published free by the International Trade
Administration and merges the Treasury (SDN, sectoral, CAPTA and others),
Commerce (Entity List, Military End User List, Unverified List, Denied Persons)
and State lists **(primary:
[data.trade.gov consolidated.json](https://data.trade.gov/downloadable_consolidated_screening_list/v1/consolidated.json),
read 2026-09-26)**. Related terms: [secondary sanctions](../../../../../../glossary/terms/secondary-sanctions.md).

- **Entity List affiliates rule.** BIS's rule extending Entity List
  restrictions to firms 50% or more owned by listed parties took effect
  2025-09-29 and is suspended 2025-11-10 to **2026-11-09**, snapping back on
  2026-11-10 unless suspended again **(primary:
  [Federal Register 2025-19846](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/12/2025-19846/one-year-suspension-of-expansion-of-end-user-controls-for-affiliates-of-certain-listed-entities))**.
  Owners therefore get screened too, not only the counterparty's own name.
- **Investment, not services.** Treasury's outbound investment rules (31 CFR
  850, codified and extended by the COINS Act in the FY2026 NDAA) restrict US
  persons *investing* in Chinese semiconductor, AI, quantum, supercomputing and
  hypersonics businesses; they do not restrict selling services to Chinese
  firms ([Skadden, 2026-01](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/01/us-treasurys-reverse-cfius-authority)).
  OFAC's NS-CMIC list bars buying securities of listed Chinese military-industrial
  companies.
- **Products.** Connected-vehicle software with a China nexus is barred from
  model year 2027; new Chinese drone models cannot get FCC authorization.
  The Commerce drone rule itself was withdrawn on 2026-01-08
  ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/01/14/us-commerce-department-withdraws-proposed-restrictions-on-chinese-made-drones/)).

## 6. Acting for a Chinese company: FARA

The [Foreign Agents Registration Act](../../../../../../glossary/terms/foreign-agents-registration-act.md) requires registration by a
person acting in the US as an agent of a foreign principal in political
activities, public relations or representation before officials. Its
commercial exemption, 22 U.S.C. 613(d), exempts private, nonpolitical activity
in furtherance of a foreign principal's bona fide trade or commerce **(primary:
[22 U.S.C. 613](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A22+section%3A613+edition%3Aprelim%29))**.
Selling a Chinese maker's products, holding its FDA agent role, staffing its
booth or running its store is commercial; lobbying, political messaging or
public-opinion work for it, or for a Chinese state body, is what registers.
DOJ proposed in 2025 to narrow the "other activities" limb of the exemption to
activity predominantly serving a domestic interest; no final rule had been
found as of 2026. The Attorney General's 2025-02-05 memo moved criminal FARA
enforcement toward espionage-like conduct; civil registration enforcement
continues ([Skadden, 2025-02](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/attorney-general-announces-shift-in-foreign-agents)).
The engagement letter states the work's commercial scope, which is also what
the exemption turns on.

## 7. Minnesota

- **Land.** Minnesota's [alien farm law](../../../../../../glossary/terms/alien-farm-law.md) (Minn. Stat.
  500.221, 1977) bars a person who is neither a citizen nor a permanent
  resident, and an entity less than 80% owned by citizens or permanent
  residents, from acquiring agricultural land, with exemptions **(primary:
  [Revisor](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/500.221))**. It names no
  country; no China-specific Minnesota land statute was found as of
  2026-09-26, while Iowa, North Dakota and South Dakota have them and Wisconsin
  bills (SB219/AB218) would bar "foreign adversary" ownership of any real
  property ([Committee of 100 tracker](https://www.committee100.org/our-work/federal-and-state-bills-prohibiting-property-ownership-by-foreign-individuals-and-entities/)).
  For the land-contracts business, a Chinese buyer can take assignment of
  non-agricultural Minnesota land; agricultural land cannot go to one.
- **State contracting.** No Minnesota statute barring state contracts with
  Chinese firms was found; the FY2026 NDAA bars Defense Department contracts
  with Chinese military companies from 2026-06-30.
- **Export help.** DEED's STEP program reimburses half of export costs (trade
  missions, trade shows, translated websites) up to $7,500
  ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/business/exporting/export-financing)).

## 8. Getting paid, and paying

- **From China.** A Chinese company pays a US service provider as a documented
  service-fee remittance through its bank, which reviews the contract and
  invoice for SAFE compliance; a clean package takes about 5–10 business days
  ([MSA Asia](https://msadvisory.com/safe-registration-outbound-remittance-china/)).
  So the US side issues a written service contract and an invoice per payment.
  Chinese individuals face an annual $50,000 foreign-exchange quota, which is
  why payment should come from the company, not its owner.
- **Rails.** Wires; cross-border fintechs (Airwallex, PingPong, LianLian,
  Payoneer, WorldFirst, Wise); Alibaba Trade Assurance for paying suppliers;
  Alipay+ and WeChat Pay acceptance through Stripe, Adyen or Citcon at about
  2–2.5% with no Chinese entity needed ([Citcon](https://www.citcon.com/blog-posts/china-cross-border-payments-alipay-wechat-pay-unionpay));
  and USDC over x402, which Ant Group's Anvita Flow also settles in.
- **Data.** Personal data leaving China goes through a CAC security
  assessment, standard contract or, from 2026-01-01, certification
  ([Morgan Lewis, 2025-10](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/10/chinas-data-outbound-rules-update-measures-for-the-certification)).
  That is the Chinese client's filing; a US representative receiving its
  customer lists should expect it to ask for a standard contract.
