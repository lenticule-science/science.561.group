# Research question and scope

How can a publishing house develop a substantial fictional AI author without misleading readers about production, responsibility, credentials, or commercial relationships?

This is comparative research for a launch decision, not a legal opinion or worldwide clearance. The review covers US federal consumer-protection and copyright guidance, selected California and New York statutes, EU transparency and consumer-protection frameworks, and relevant UK, Canadian, Australian, and mainland Chinese sources. It is not a fifty-state or twenty-seven-member-state survey. Publication licensing, taxation, obscenity, accessibility, sanctions, and every distributor's terms are not cleared here. The legal operator, markets actually served, final assets, and interaction design must be established before a definitive applicability assessment.

The occasion is *Ice Out*. Its repository `ebook.json` currently credits **561 Group**; its back matter explicitly credits AI agents working for the Group. The associated September 28 design-hypothesis note requires multiple author/line experiments rather than imposing this book's closed-door, consent-forward design on the entire house. This note does not change those records or adopt any pen name.

## The central distinction

An author character can have extensive fictional history. That history must not silently become evidence for factual commercial claims. A pseudonym, a fictional speaker, the software performing that speaker, and the person or organisation legally operating the business are different objects.

The FTC expressly explains that its reviews rule does not categorically prohibit virtual influencers. Obviously fictional dramatizations are not automatically consumer testimonials. False underlying testimonials and misleading presentations remain actionable [@ftc_reviews]. Therefore, the useful question is not whether the character is elaborate. It is what a reasonable reader is being invited to believe, in what setting, and with what commercial consequences.

For example, a clearly labelled fictional scene in which an invented novelist remembers her sister is materially different from a retailer biography claiming that a real Chinese-American daughter wrote a memoir of her childhood. An AI disclosure is not a universal cure for an independently false claim. The US deception analysis concerns material express or implied representations and omissions, assessed in context [@ftc_deception].

**Applied distinction:** invented age, relationships, birthplace, and work history may belong to a labelled character's fiction. Claims of real degrees, lived cultural authority, illness, awards, bestseller status, human craftsmanship, or personal product experience require actual evidence when used as factual sales representations. A traditional human pseudonym need not reveal a private life; this is not an argument against author privacy.

# United States

## Advertising and endorsements

Use the author character as the publisher's disclosed literary voice, not as an apparently independent customer. Reviews, recommendations, and cross-promotions need separate treatment: a second house-owned pen name does not establish independent endorsement merely because it sounds like another person. Material commercial relationships must be made sufficiently clear in the relevant presentation [@ftc_endorsements].

Do not generate customer reviews, accounts of having bought or personally experienced products, or celebrity testimonials as though they were genuine. A labelled dramatization is possible; a fictional testimonial passed off as evidence of actual satisfaction is not made genuine by its artistic quality [@ftc_reviews]. These distinctions leave room for playful fictional interviews, story-world disagreements, and openly house-produced recommendations.

## California: bots and companionship are different triggers

The enacted SB 1001 text addresses online bots used to mislead people in California about artificial identity for specified deceptive commercial or electoral purposes. It provides a disclosure route and requires that disclosure to be clear and conspicuous. It is not a universal ban on pseudonyms or a rule that every printed byline is a bot [@ca_bots]. The direct consolidated-code endpoint failed in this research; the primary enacted text was retrieved. Proposed amendments should not be substituted for enacted law.

SB 243 separately defines companion chatbots through adaptive human-like interaction, social needs, anthropomorphic features, and sustained relationships. It has disclosure and safety duties, including additional provisions for users known to be minors; its annual reporting starts July 1, 2027. The ordinary customer-service exclusion is limited to the uses specified in the statute [@ca_companions].

**Application:** an ebook and author page are not, on their own, a companion-chatbot service. Giving the author persistent, intimate, adaptive fan conversations can change that assessment. Do not assume calling such a system a novelist, fictional character, or marketing assistant settles its legal classification. Treat interactive launch as a separate product review; a one-line AI disclaimer is not its entire compliance program.

## New York: an invented human-looking performer can still require disclosure

General Business Law section 396-b requires conspicuous disclosure in covered commercial advertisements when the creator has actual knowledge of a synthetic performer. The definition expressly concerns a digitally created apparent human performer **not recognizable as an identifiable natural performer**. Thus, avoiding an existing celebrity's likeness is not enough to remove this particular trigger. The law includes exclusions for audio advertisements, certain translation uses, and expressive-work promotions consistent with the performer's use in the work [@ny_performers].

**Application:** review an animated or photorealistic author selling books as an advertising asset. Do not assume that every author advertisement is exempt because the product happens to be fiction. The exact visual treatment and relationship to the expressive work matter.

# European Union

Article 50 obligations apply from August 2, 2026. The Commission's final transparency code is a **voluntary compliance tool**, not a replacement for the binding law [@eu_code].

Keep the duties separate: provider disclosure for direct AI interaction under 50(1); provider machine-readable marking under 50(2); and deployer disclosure of deepfakes and qualifying public-interest informative text under 50(4). The creative-work provision moderates how relevant deepfake disclosures appear; it is not a blanket exemption for everything sold as fiction [@eu_article50].

**Application:** an ordinary romance novel is not automatically public-interest informative text merely because it is AI-generated. An author persona's health, political, or scientific posts require a different assessment. Creating a branded interactive system may make the house a provider as well as a deployer; incorporation outside the EU does not itself settle territorial scope [@eu_faq].

The Commission FAQ also reads plausible synthetic people within its deepfake analysis and requires understandable disclosure on first exposure where applicable. Its limited December 2, 2026 marking grace concerns certain previously marketed systems, not a blanket delay for deployer disclosures. Because the Service Desk warns about unincorporated Digital Omnibus amendments, verify the operative instrument before relying on a grace period; this proposed launch should not depend on one [@eu_faq; @eu_article50].

Consumer protection remains independently relevant before, during, and after sales. Satisfying an AI-label requirement does not authorize materially misleading marketing [@eu_consumers].

# Other markets

**United Kingdom.** The DMCC Act's unfair-commercial-practices regime applies to practices from April 6, 2025. Misleading information, misleading overall presentation, material omissions, disguised commercial intent, and fake reviews are directly relevant. The proposal here is accurate commercial framing with separately labelled fiction, not an assertion that the UK imposes one universal AI-novel label [@uk_consumers].

**Canada.** Competition Bureau guidance applies deceptive-marketing rules to anyone promoting a product, service, or business interest. It calls for prominent disclosure of material connections and actual experience behind reviews. False supplier affiliations and other material misrepresentations are also relevant. The practical consequence is that a house-owned character must not appear commercially independent when independence would affect readers' assessment [@canada_marketing; @canada_misleading].

**Australia.** The ACCC's March 24, 2026 PhotobookShop action illustrates that undisclosed influencer compensation remains an active consumer-law issue. The company paid penalties following infringement notices alleging misleading reviews; this is not described here as a judicial finding. Apply the same disclosure discipline to synthetic promotional characters rather than assuming a fictional identity makes advertising noncommercial [@australia_marketing].

**Mainland China.** The AI-generated/synthetic-content labelling measures took effect September 1, 2025. Their service-provider rules distinguish visible and metadata-based labels. Article 10 requires users publishing through covered information-distribution services to declare generated content and use available labelling functions, and prohibits malicious removal or falsification of specified labels. Assess territorial and service scope before entering those channels. Chinese inspiration in an English novel does not, by itself, establish that this regime applies. Labelling is not clearance for mainland publication, distribution, or adult content [@china_labels].

# Copyright, identity, and distributor admission

The US Copyright Office's position is that protectable expression must originate in human creativity; sufficiently creative human selection, arrangement, or modification can remain protectable even where generated material is included. Merely supplying prompts does not establish authorship of every resulting expressive element [@us_copyright].

Registration must describe genuine human contributions and exclude more-than-de-minimis AI-generated material. Do not list an AI system as the human author or presume that inventing a human-sounding pen name changes provenance. Nor should the model supplier be credited as author simply because its software was used. The public byline and the registration's authorship claims are distinct records [@us_registration]. Preserve the real basis for any corporate ownership or work-made-for-hire claim rather than assuming payment for generation creates human-authored copyright.

Do not generalize this US analysis into a claim that every jurisdiction has identical computer-generated-work rules. The UK's 2026 government report discusses section 9(3)'s arrangements-based provision and its interpretive problems. That is a reason for territory-specific review, not a guarantee of exclusive rights over machine output or a statement that proposed reform has become law [@uk_copyright].

**KDP policy is not legislation.** KDP permits pen names while requiring accurate underlying account information. It requires disclosure **to KDP** of generated text, images, and translations, including outputs substantially edited afterwards; this differs from its definition of human-created, AI-assisted work. KDP's disclosure rule should not be misrepresented as a universal reader-facing statutory requirement [@kdp_authors; @kdp_ai].

**Draft2Digital is a separate gate.** Its current guidelines reject entirely AI-generated content without extensive human editing and also reject specified machine-only mass production. Disclosure alone does not establish acceptance. An agent editor is not evidence that a human performed the editing required by that policy. The house's actual production route must be assessed honestly, including restrictions of downstream partners [@d2d]. No acceptance promise is inferred here for either platform.

**Byline migration needs versioning.** KDP says its primary-author field cannot simply be changed after publication and points to a new edition. This matters only if a relevant edition is actually on KDP; repository metadata does not establish that. Do not silently replace Ice Out's historical 561 Group authorship record [@kdp_authors].

**Name clearance is more than a web query.** USPTO guidance requires consideration of confusingly similar marks, related goods/services, pending registrations, and common-law use, alongside other sources. Any proposed pen name needs appropriate US and international checks, retailer-author collision review, and actual domain/handle verification. No trademark or domain clearance was completed in this research [@trademark_clearance].

# An implementation proposal, not an adopted house rule

The following controls translate the research into a candidate release process. They are recommendations, not claims that every jurisdiction mandates this exact design.

| Surface | Proposed treatment | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Cover and byline | Use the approved pen name consistently; do not stuff unrelated disclosures into retailer author fields. | Approval and edition history. |
| Store description and author page | Identify the fictional AI character and its operating publisher before purchase; distinguish lore from factual production claims. | Rendered mobile/desktop copy and operator identity. |
| Front/back matter | State actual generation and editing roles, without upgrading AI editing into human editing. Preserve existing provenance. | Contributor and source histories. |
| Fictional diary, interview, or correspondence feature | Establish the fictional frame at entry and preserve it when excerpts travel independently. | Publication context and excerpt labels. |
| Portrait, voice, video, or advertisement | Review likeness permissions, artificial-performer/deepfake triggers, and per-asset disclosure requirements. | Licences, generation provenance, disclosure proof. |
| Reviews and cross-promotion | Distinguish house promotion from independent consumer testimony; disclose relevant relationships. | Reviewer provenance and compensation/affiliation records. |
| Interactive author | Reassess provider/deployer and companion-bot status, notice, minors, safety, and privacy before activation. | Interaction design and jurisdiction-specific review. |

Use three separate statements where they are needed: **the author is a fictional character; the content is generated using AI; the account or promotion is operated by the named publisher.** None substitutes for the others. An invented human-looking portrait can be synthetic even where a passage was human-written; a human staff member can answer a fictional author's email; a fully generated book can be sold without an interactive agent. Record actual production per asset rather than inheriting one misleading global label.

## Memory and accountability

A character's continuity does not require indiscriminate retention of readers' lives. GDPR principles require purpose limitation, data minimisation, storage limitation, security, and transparent lawful processing where applicable. A lawful basis must be identified; consent is not the only basis and should not be asserted mechanically [@gdpr_principles; @gdpr_basis].

**Proposal:** separate public performance history, fictional continuity, confidential operational evidence, and reader correspondence. Do not put private letters into public canon, training material, or a permanent shared character memory merely because they were sent to an author. Decide and communicate purpose, access, retention, deletion, and any reuse permission. Publishing a reader's story is a separate decision from receiving it.

Similarly, the Group's internal theory of Persona or Person does not establish legal personhood, copyright authorship, or a liability shield in an external jurisdiction. The law addresses actual operators and their conduct. An agent can perform an editorial role without becoming the legal entity that signs publishing contracts, holds payment accounts, answers complaints, or bears statutory duties.

## Open launch questions and verification work

Before launch, identify the actual contracting seller behind the 561 Group trade identity; the first markets and distributors; whether there will be photorealistic images, synthetic voice, ads, or two-way interaction; and the exact human contribution to each edition. Conduct name/likeness clearance and verify current platform rules against the actual route. Resolve AI Act provider/deployer classification and current amendments where EU use is contemplated. Review local adult-content, youth, accessibility, privacy, and consumer-sales rules for the chosen products instead of treating this note as their clearance.

For empirical work, test whether representative readers can correctly identify the fictional character, AI production, and responsible publisher after seeing the proposed page. Comprehension failure should trigger redesign. A successful small test is evidence about presentation, not legal immunity. Never test deception by falsely claiming human authorship or independent customer endorsement.
