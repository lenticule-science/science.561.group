# Minnesota law for assigning property contracts

The 561 Group's property-resale business puts land under a purchase agreement
that names the buyer "or assigns", then assigns that agreement to a builder or
investor for a fee (see [contract assignment](../../../../../../../../../economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/contract-assignment.md)
and the business's own section,
`public-economic-services-section/services/duluth-infill-land-services-section`).
This note records the law that shapes how that is done in Minnesota, where the
business starts, and in Wisconsin, across the bridge from Duluth. Sections read
directly from the Minnesota Revisor of Statutes on 2026-09-26 are marked
**primary**; the rest is search synthesis and is marked so.

## 1. Who is a broker (Minn. Stat. 82.55, subd. 19) — primary

A [real estate broker](../../../../../../glossary/terms/real-estate-broker.md) is
anyone who, **for another** and for a fee or the expectation of one, lists,
sells, buys, or "offers or attempts to negotiate a sale, option, exchange,
purchase or rental of an interest or estate in real estate", or advertises that
they do (clause a).

Two consequences follow for the business.

- **The caller works in the deal, not for another.** A caller paid a
  commission to negotiate someone else's purchase is acting "for another". The
  caller's joint-venture agreement makes each deal the caller originates a joint
  venture in which the caller is a principal, and the caller qualifies owners
  and sets offer calls while the Group makes and signs every offer.
- **The Group sells its own contract right.** Assigning a purchase agreement is
  selling the Group's own [equitable interest](../../../../../../glossary/terms/equitable-interest.md),
  not negotiating a sale for the landowner. The Group therefore markets the
  contract (its right to buy parcel X at price Y) and not the land, which it
  does not own.

Clause (g) is the one that sets the business's scale:

> "engages wholly or in part in the business of selling real estate to the
> extent that a pattern of real estate sales is established, whether or not
> the real estate is owned by the person. A person shall be presumed to be
> engaged in the business of selling real estate if the person engages as
> principal in five or more transactions during any 12-month period, unless
> the person is represented by a licensed real estate broker or salesperson."

The business plan's second-quarter pace (two to four assignments a month) is
24 to 48 transactions a year, well past five. There are two ways to run at
that pace, and either one is enough:

1. **Be represented.** Each transaction past the fourth in a 12-month window
   has a licensed Minnesota broker or salesperson representing the Group. A
   flat-fee broker who represents the Group on its purchases and assignments,
   and who can also put contracts in front of the broker's own buyer network,
   is a working arrangement brokers already offer to investors.
2. **Hold the license.** Someone in the Group holds a Minnesota salesperson
   license under a broker. The license takes 90 hours of pre-licensing
   education (Courses I, II and III, 30 hours each; Course I before the exam),
   a PSI exam of 120 questions, and roughly $600 to $900 in course, exam and
   license fees (search synthesis, 2026 guides; Minnesota Department of
   Commerce, Education for Licensees).

The Group's agency rule `land-deals-past-four-a-year-are-represented`
(section `lib/agency/rules/`) states the first route as a check on every deal
recorded as contracted.

## 2. Who is exempt (Minn. Stat. 82.56) — primary

The exemptions include licensed attorneys (a), court-appointed fiduciaries such
as personal representatives and trustees acting under court order (b), and:

> (i) any person who acquires real estate for the purpose of engaging in and
> does engage in, or who is engaged in the business of constructing
> residential, commercial or industrial buildings for the purpose of resale if
> no more than 25 such transactions occur in any 12-month period and the
> person complies with section 82.75

Builders, the business's buyers, are exempt up to 25 transactions a year. The
Group, which does not build, is not. Clause (b) matters on the seller side: a
personal representative selling an estate's lot signs under the court's
authority, and the purchase agreement's seller block and the deed type
(personal representative's deed) follow from it.

## 3. Assignment itself — search synthesis

No Minnesota statute prohibits or specially regulates assigning a real estate
purchase agreement, and no 2025 or 2026 Minnesota law changed that (search
synthesis: Waypoint Law, "Assignment Clauses in MN Purchase Agreements";
realestateskills.com, updated 2026). Assignability comes from the contract: a
purchase agreement that says the buyer "may assign this agreement without
Seller's consent" is assignable, and one that is silent leaves room for
dispute. The Group's template says it expressly and also tells the seller the
buyer may assign for a fee.

## 4. Wisconsin: 2023 Wisconsin Act 208 — search synthesis

Superior sits across the bridge from Duluth in Douglas County, Wisconsin.
Wisconsin's Act 208 (SB 870 / AB 918, signed 2024-03-22) defines a
[real property wholesaler](../../../../../../glossary/terms/real-property-wholesaler.md)
as a person who enters a purchase agreement as buyer intending to assign the
buyer's rights to a third party for consideration, and requires the wholesaler
to give the seller written notice of that status no later than signing, and the
assignee the same notice. A seller who did not get the notice may rescind
before closing and keep any deposit; an assignee may rescind and recover option
fees. Wisconsin's broker definition separately presumes a "pattern of sales"
at five or more sales in one year or ten in five years (WRA Legal Hotline,
June 2024; Wis. Stat. 452; search synthesis). Working Superior means one more
page in the purchase agreement packet: the Act 208 notice, signed with it.

## 5. Calling owners — search synthesis, with the Minnesota statute cited

- **Federal (TCPA).** Several federal district courts have held that calls
  asking whether an owner will sell, and making an offer to buy, are not
  "telephone solicitations" or telemarketing, because the TCPA's definitions
  cover encouraging the called party to buy, rent or invest, not offers to buy
  from them; courts are not unanimous, and a call that gathers owners'
  information to sell to brokers is treated as marketing (Roth Jackson,
  2025-06; search synthesis). A caller who dials by hand, says who they are,
  makes an offer to buy, and honors "don't call me" works on the solid side of
  that line.
- **Minnesota (Minn. Stat. 325E.27).** An automatic dialing-announcing device
  (a machine that dials and plays a recorded message) may not be used unless
  the subscriber consented or a live operator comes on first, and not before
  9 a.m. or after 9 p.m. Live calls by a person are outside that section.

## 6. Tax-forfeited land after *Tyler v. Hennepin* — primary

In *Tyler v. Hennepin County*, 598 U.S. 631 (2023), the Supreme Court held that
a county keeping the surplus from selling a tax-forfeited property takes the
owner's property without compensation. Minnesota rewrote chapter 282 in 2024
(H.F. 4822, effective 2024-07-01; Minnesota Department of Revenue analysis,
2024-04-29). As codified in Minn. Stat. 282.005:

- **Repurchase (subd. 3).** Before the county's sale, an interested party (an
  owner, lienholder or other party with an interest) may repurchase the
  property by paying all delinquent taxes, assessments, penalties, interest
  and costs.
- **Auction (subd. 4).** The county auditor must sell every forfeited property
  at public auction, online auctions included, within six months of the
  forfeiture certificate or of the occupant vacating, whichever is later, with
  at least 30 days' published notice. The minimum bid is the tax debt.
- **Surplus claims (subd. 6, 7, 9).** When the auction brings more than the
  minimum bid, the auditor must mail notice of the
  [surplus proceeds](../../../../../../glossary/terms/surplus-proceeds.md) and a
  claim form to interested parties within 60 days (certified mail, then a
  second notice between 90 and 120 days), and must publish on the county's
  website a list of sales with surplus whose claim periods are open.
  Interested parties have six months from the first mailing to claim; after
  that the surplus returns to the county's forfeited tax sale fund.
- **Minerals (subd. 8).** Mineral interests pass to the state for $50 on
  forfeiture, with a separate claim if their value exceeds the minimum bid.

Three things follow for the business.

1. **What a caller says about forfeiture changes.** An owner whose lot
   forfeits is no longer simply wiped out: the county auctions the lot and the
   owner may claim what it brings above the tax debt, months later, by filing a
   form in time. The accurate offer is speed, certainty and a price the owner
   agrees to now, against a public auction's price and a claims process later.
2. **Every forfeited parcel is now auction supply.** Counties must auction
   them within six months, online and with published notice, so each county's
   auction list is a second supply line of lots to match to buy boxes.
3. **Unclaimed surplus is a claims-recovery market.** The counties publish
   which sales have open surplus claims. Former owners, and above all the heirs
   of owners who died, often do not know a surplus is waiting; finding them is
   the heir search business's work (`heir-search-services-section`). Chapter 282
   sets no fee cap for someone who helps a claimant file; the claim is the
   claimant's own and the county pays the claimant.

## Open

- Whether an interested party's subdivision 3 repurchase right passes with a
  deed or assignment of the owner's interest after forfeiture, which would let
  a buyer of that interest repurchase at the tax debt, is not settled by the
  statute's text and needs a Minnesota real estate attorney's reading.
- Wisconsin Act 208's exact notice wording is to be read from the enrolled act
  before the first Superior contract.
