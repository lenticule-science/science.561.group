# Minnesota law for forming and running an LLC

This note records the law for forming a Minnesota limited liability company
(LLC) and running it with several members, read 2026-09-26. Items read from
a statute, regulation or agency page are marked **primary**; the rest is
synthesis from formation services and law-firm summaries and is marked so.
Fees and forms change; each dated item is a thing to re-read at filing time.
The Group's own plan built on this is the 561-group note on forming the
Group's first company.

## 1. What an LLC is under Minnesota law

Minnesota's LLC Act is chapter 322C, the Revised Uniform Limited Liability
Company Act as Minnesota adopted it. A
[limited liability company](../../../../../../glossary/terms/limited-liability-company.md) is a legal person separate from its
members: it holds property, signs contracts, sues and is sued in its own
name, and its members and managers are not personally liable for its debts
"solely by reason of" being members or managers (Minn. Stat. 322C.0304)
**(primary: [322C.0304](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0304))**.
Courts can still reach members personally in the ordinary veil-piercing
cases (mixing personal and company money, an undercapitalized shell used to
defraud), which is why the company keeps its own bank account and books.

## 2. Filing the articles of organization

- **Where.** The Secretary of State's business filing portal,
  [mblsportal.sos.mn.gov](https://mblsportal.sos.mn.gov/), or on paper by
  mail. The same portal's free business search checks whether a name is
  taken.
- **Fee.** $135 by statute (322C.0201 subd. 4); the Secretary of State's fee
  schedule lists $155 online and $135 by mail **(primary:
  [322C.0201](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0201);
  [fee schedule](https://www.sos.mn.gov/business-liens/business-filing-and-certification-fee-schedule/))**.
  Online filings are reported processed within days (search synthesis).
- **What the articles must say** (322C.0201 subd. 2) **(primary)**:
  1. the company's name, which must contain "limited liability company",
     "LLC" or "L.L.C.", must not contain "corporation" or "incorporated", and
     must be distinguishable from names already on file (322C.0108,
     **primary: [322C.0108](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0108)**);
  2. the street address of the initial [registered office](../../../../../../glossary/terms/registered-office.md)
     and, if it has one, the name of its agent for service of process;
  3. the name and street address of each organizer.

  Anything else in the articles is optional (subd. 3).
- **Organizers.** "One or more persons may act as organizers" (322C.0201
  subd. 1), and a person is any individual or entity (322C.0102). Nothing in
  chapter 322C requires an organizer, member or manager to be a citizen or a
  resident, or to have a Social Security number **(primary:
  [322C.0102](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0102))**. An
  organizer's role ends with the filing; organizing does not make anyone a
  member.
- **Registered office.** Every LLC has a registered office; a registered
  agent is optional (322C.0113) **(primary:
  [322C.0113](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0113))**. The
  office must be an actual office location, which may also carry a mailing
  address or post office box, but may not be solely a post office box
  (Minn. Stat. 5.36) **(primary: [5.36](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/5.36))**.
  The statute does not exclude a residence where the business is actually
  run. The address is public in the Secretary of State's records.

## 3. Members, managers and who decides

- **Who becomes a member.** At formation, the members are whoever the
  founding persons agree will be members; later, a new member needs the
  consent of all members. A person may become a member without contributing
  anything (322C.0401) **(primary:
  [322C.0401](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0401))**.
- **Member-managed or manager-managed.** An LLC is member-managed unless its
  operating agreement expressly says it is "manager-managed" (or
  "board-managed"). In a member-managed LLC every member has equal rights in
  management, ordinary matters are decided by a majority of members, and
  anything outside the ordinary course needs all of them. In a
  manager-managed LLC the managers decide the company's ordinary matters,
  by majority among them, but the members' unanimous consent is still
  needed to sell substantially everything, merge or convert, or amend the
  operating agreement (322C.0407) **(primary:
  [322C.0407](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0407))**. A
  manager need not be a member.
- **Duties.** Whoever manages owes the company loyalty (no self-dealing, no
  competing, account for company property), care (good faith, reasonable
  judgment) and good faith and fair dealing (322C.0409) **(primary:
  [322C.0409](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0409))**.

## 4. The operating agreement

An [operating agreement](../../../../../../glossary/terms/operating-agreement.md) is the members'
contract about the company. Minnesota does not require one, and where it is
silent chapter 322C's defaults govern (322C.0110) **(primary:
[322C.0110](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0110))**. The
defaults matter because they are rarely what a working group intends:

- Distributions before dissolution are made **in equal shares among
  members**, and no member has a right to one until the company decides to
  make it (322C.0404) **(primary:
  [322C.0404](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0404))**. Equal
  shares ignore who contributed money or who does the work.
- Management is member-managed with equal votes (322C.0407).

So an agreement is how a group sets unequal shares, names managers, says
what the managers may do alone, and says what happens when someone leaves.
It cannot waive the duties of loyalty, care and good faith beyond what the
Act allows, or a member's right to sue (322C.0110 subd. 3).

## 5. Many businesses under one company

- **No series LLCs.** Chapter 322C has no provision for series or protected
  series (search of the chapter, 2026-09-26); Minnesota did not adopt them.
- **One LLC with assumed names.** One company can run many businesses, each
  under its own name, and all share one liability pool: a claim against one
  line can reach the assets of all.
- **Separate or subsidiary LLCs.** Each separate LLC ($135–155 to form, $0 a
  year to renew) walls its assets off from the others'. A holding LLC can own
  the others.

## 6. Assumed names

A business that operates under any name other than its legal name files a
[certificate of assumed name](../../../../../../glossary/terms/assumed-name.md) with the Secretary of State
before using it and publishes it in a qualified legal newspaper of the
county of its principal place of business for two consecutive issues (Minn.
Stat. 333.01) **(primary: [333.01](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/333.01))**.
The filing costs $30 by mail or $50 online, plus the newspaper's charge;
publication is reported due within 60 days, and the newspaper returns an
affidavit of publication to keep (search synthesis;
[Secretary of State, naming your business](https://www.sos.mn.gov/business-liens/start-a-business/naming-your-business/)).
The legal newspapers are listed by the Secretary of State
([legal newspaper list](https://www.sos.mn.gov/legal-newspaper)).

## 7. Keeping the company alive

A Minnesota LLC files an annual renewal with the Secretary of State by
December 31 each year, at no charge; one that does not is administratively
terminated on January 1 and can retroactively reinstate by filing a renewal
and paying a fee (322C.0705, 322C.0706) **(primary:
[322C.0705](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0705);
[322C.0706](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/322C.0706))**. The
statute sets the reinstatement fee at $25; the Secretary of State's fee page
read on 2026-09-26 showed a higher figure, so the renewal date is the thing
to keep.

## 8. The EIN and its responsible party

- An employer identification number (EIN) is the company's federal tax
  number, needed for its tax returns and to open a bank account. It is free
  from the IRS.
- The IRS's online application works only when the
  [responsible party](../../../../../../glossary/terms/responsible-party.md) has a Social Security number
  or an individual taxpayer identification number (ITIN), and allows one EIN
  per responsible party per day **(primary:
  [IRS, get an EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number),
  updated 2026-08-19)**. Otherwise the company files Form SS-4 by fax or mail
  ([SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/iss4.pdf)).
- The responsible party is the individual who ultimately owns or controls
  the entity, or exercises ultimate effective control over it and its funds,
  and the SS-4 instructions state that a nominee is never the responsible
  party (search synthesis of the SS-4 instructions). A manager who in fact
  holds authority over the company and its accounts is a responsible party;
  a person named only to fill the box is not.
- An individual who needs a US tax number and is not eligible for a Social
  Security number applies for an ITIN on Form W-7
  ([W-7 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7)). ITIN holders
  can be members and receive the partnership's K-1.

## 9. How a multi-member LLC is taxed

A domestic LLC with two or more members is taxed as a partnership unless it
elects otherwise: it files Form 1065 and gives each member a Schedule K-1,
and each member pays income tax (and self-employment tax, for members
working in the business) on their allocated share of profit whether or not
it was paid out to them **(primary:
[IRS, LLC filing as a corporation or partnership](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/llc-filing-as-a-corporation-or-partnership))**.
Because members owe tax on allocated profit, operating agreements commonly
provide tax distributions. An S corporation election is unavailable while
any member is a nonresident alien (26 U.S.C. 1361(b)(1)(C)). In Minnesota
the partnership files Form M3 by March 15; the minimum fee applies to
partnerships only once Minnesota property, payroll and sales reach
$1,280,000 **(primary:
[Minnesota minimum fee](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/minimum-fee),
2026 brackets)**.

## 10. Beneficial ownership reporting has ended for US companies

FinCEN's final rule of 2026-08-11, effective 2026-08-14, permanently exempts
every entity created in the United States, and its beneficial owners, from
Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership reporting **(primary:
[Treasury press release](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0603);
[FinCEN BOI](https://www.fincen.gov/boi);
[Federal Register 2026-16576](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision))**.
A new Minnesota LLC files nothing with FinCEN.

## 11. What a bank must identify

A bank opening an account for a company must identify and verify each
individual who owns 25% or more of it and one individual with significant
responsibility to control or manage it (31 CFR 1010.230), the company's
[beneficial owners](../../../../../../../../../sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/beneficial-ownership.md) **(primary:
[31 CFR 1010.230](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-B/section-1010.230))**.
For each it takes a name, date of birth, address and an identification
number under the customer identification rule: for a US person a taxpayer
identification number, for a non-US person one or more of a taxpayer
identification number, a passport number and country, an alien
identification card number, or the number and country of another
government-issued photo document evidencing nationality or residence; and it
verifies identity with an unexpired government-issued photo ID such as a
driver's license or passport **(primary:
[31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1020/subpart-B/section-1020.220))**.
Banks may ask for more by their own policy. Stating ownership falsely to a
bank is a federal crime (18 U.S.C. 1014, 1344), so the bank's ownership
form lists the company's real 25% owners. Online banks state differing
policies for owners without a Social Security number: Mercury and Bluevine
state they accept non-US owners with a passport (search synthesis;
[Mercury eligibility](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770467511060-Eligibility);
[Bluevine](https://www.bluevine.com/blog/introducing-bluevine-for-international-business-owners)).

### Without a photo ID

The rule lets a bank verify identity by documents, by non-documentary methods
(checking the information given against a consumer reporting agency, public
database or references from other financial institutions), or both, and
requires it to say what it does when a person cannot present an unexpired
government-issued photo ID (31 CFR 1020.220(a)(2)(ii)) **(primary:
[31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1020/subpart-B/section-1020.220))**.
A person who already holds an account at the institution, which the
institution reasonably believes it has identified, is not a new "customer"
for the identification program (31 CFR 1020.100(b)(2)(iii)). A company's
account is a new customer, but an existing account holder who owns and runs
it is someone the institution has already identified. Whether a given
institution opens the company's account on that basis is its own policy.
Fidelity's business brokerage account (the Fidelity Account for Businesses)
is opened on a paper application, mailed or taken to a branch, with the
filed articles, and existing customers are processed faster
([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/open-account/fidelity-account-for-businesses);
[application](https://www.fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/new-fidelity-account-business-application.pdf)).

### Getting a Minnesota ID

Minnesota issues a standard driver's license or ID card (not REAL ID) without
proof of immigration status since 2023, on **two primary documents, or one
primary and one secondary** **(primary:
[Driver's License for All document list, 2025-02-21](https://assets.dps.mn.gov/files/dvs/dvs-standard-required-docs.pdf))**:

- **Primary** (name and date of birth): an unexpired passport from any
  country; an unexpired consular ID with photo; a certified birth record from
  any jurisdiction (a state birth certificate, a foreign birth certificate, a
  consular report of birth abroad); a certified adoption certificate; an
  unexpired tribal identification card with security features; USCIS
  documents (naturalization or citizenship certificate, permanent resident
  card, employment authorization, American Indian card I-872 and others); a
  US Defense Department ID; a Canadian naturalization certificate.
- **Secondary**: a Social Security card; an IRS document showing an ITIN; an
  expired government photo ID (five years or less); a certified school
  transcript with full name and date of birth; a certified marriage
  certificate or court order; a Minnesota property tax statement or a deed or
  mortgage for the applicant's residence; a health benefits or assistance
  program card; a Minnesota vehicle title; a Selective Service card; and
  others on the list.

So a Social Security card and a certified birth certificate, ordered from the
vital records office of the state or country of birth, are enough. A
standard ID serves for banking; REAL ID matters only for federal purposes such
as boarding domestic flights.

## 12. After formation: Minnesota taxes and local licenses

- **Sales tax.** Minnesota taxes specified digital products, including
  e-books, digital audio and video, and prewritten software however
  delivered; custom software and hosted software services are not taxed
  **(primary: [Minnesota digital products](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/digital-products))**.
  A company selling e-books to Minnesota buyers registers for a Minnesota tax
  ID with the Department of Revenue.
- **Withholding** registration comes with employees.
- **Duluth** licenses particular activities (food carts, massage,
  gambling, cannabis retail and others), not businesses generally
  ([City of Duluth business licensing](https://duluthmn.gov/city-clerk/licenses-permits/business-licensing/)).

## 13. An office outside Minnesota

The registered office must be in Minnesota (5.36); the principal executive
office may be anywhere (322C.0102, subd. 21). A company whose office is in
another state registers there too; the Superior case is worked in
[a Minnesota LLC with an office in Wisconsin](2026-09-26-note-on-a-minnesota-llc-with-an-office-in-wisconsin.md).
