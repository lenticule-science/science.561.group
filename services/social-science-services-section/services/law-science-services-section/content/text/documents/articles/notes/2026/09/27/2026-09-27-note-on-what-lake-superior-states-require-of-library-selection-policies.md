# What Lake Superior states require of library selection policies

The policy reading (Binding Generations' `policy-assessment.mjs`, and the
Group's planned per-call service) checks a library's written policy against
what the law asks of it. Today the checker hard-codes Minnesota. This note
records each state's requirements so they can be admitted as records the
reader applies, one set per state and kind of library. Everything below was
read from the primary text on 2026-09-27 unless marked otherwise.

## Minnesota

Minn. Stat. 134.51 (2024) covers public and school libraries. 124D.991 adds
requirements for school libraries. The checker already reads these nine
requirements:

| Requirement | Law | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Procedures for selecting materials | 134.51, subd. 5(a) | public and school |
| Procedures for challenges and reconsideration | 134.51, subd. 5(a) | public and school |
| Who administers selection and reconsideration (licensed library media specialist, MLS holder, or a trained professional) | 134.51, subd. 5(c) | public and school |
| A report of each completed challenge to the commissioner of education | 134.51, subd. 5(d) | public and school |
| The policy does not limit parents' and adult students' review rights | 134.51, subd. 5(b); 120B.20 | public and school |
| Materials are not removed for their viewpoint alone | 134.51, subd. 1 | public and school |
| An intellectual (and academic) freedom statement | 124D.991 | school |
| Deselection (weeding) procedures | 124D.991 | school |

## Wisconsin

Wisconsin places the requirement on **school boards**, through the
pupil-nondiscrimination rules:

- **Wis. Admin. Code PI 9.03(1)(e).** Each board's nondiscrimination policies
  include "an instructional and library media materials selection policy
  consistent with s. 121.02 (1) (h), Stats., and s. PI 8.01 (2) (h)."
- **PI 8.01(2)(h)** (the standard under s. 121.02(1)(h)) requires:
  1. a written, long-range plan for library services development;
  2. a licensed library media person who directs and coordinates the
     district's library media program;
  3. library facilities in each school, and for every pupil "a current,
     balanced collection of books, basic reference materials, texts,
     periodicals, and digital materials which depicts in an accurate and
     unbiased way the cultural diversity and pluralistic nature of American
     society";
  4. library services performed by, or under the direction of, licensed
     library media personnel in grades K–6, and by licensed personnel in
     grades 7–12.
- **PI 9.04.** The board designates an employee to receive discrimination
  complaints. It has a complaint procedure: written acknowledgement within 45
  days and a determination within 90, unless both sides agree to extend. It
  tells the complainant of the right to appeal a negative determination to the
  state superintendent. A challenge to library materials that alleges
  discrimination against pupils falls under this procedure.
- **Board authority.** The board is responsible for district rules (s.
  120.12(1)) and for adopting textbooks (s. 118.03).
- **2025 AB 5 did not become law.** It would have required boards to let
  district residents inspect textbooks, curricula and instructional materials
  within 14 days. It passed both houses, was vetoed on 2025-10-31, and the
  Assembly failed to override on 2026-05-13. A similar bill was vetoed in the
  previous session.

A Wisconsin school policy reading therefore checks for: a selection policy;
criteria reflecting a current, balanced and culturally diverse collection;
the licensed library media person who directs the program; the long-range
plan; and a complaint route with the 45- and 90-day timelines and the appeal
to the state superintendent.

**Not found:** a Wisconsin statute requiring a *public* library board to adopt
a selection or reconsideration policy. Public library policy is the board's
own under ch. 43, and s. 43.30 governs the confidentiality of library
records (not re-read today). A public library reading in Wisconsin checks
best practice, not law, and must say so.

## Michigan

This session's searches found no Michigan statute or rule requiring a school
or public library to adopt a selection or reconsideration policy. The Library
of Michigan's guidance on book challenges (michigan.gov) refused automated
retrieval. The Library Privacy Act (1982 PA 455, MCL 397.601 et seq.)
governs the confidentiality of library records. Its citation comes from
general knowledge and was not re-read today. Until a Michigan requirement is
found in primary text, a Michigan reading checks best practice only and says
so.

## What the policy reading does with this

- One requirement set per jurisdiction and library kind: `MN/public`,
  `MN/school`, `WI/school`, with `WI/public` and `MI/*` marked best practice.
- Each requirement carries its citation, what it asks in plain words, and
  whether it is law or practice. Each result says found (with the passage)
  or not found, and "not found" remains a reason to ask, not a verdict.
- The requirement records belong with the law science section. The reader
  (Binding Generations' checker today, the Group's service next) applies
  whichever set a request names.
