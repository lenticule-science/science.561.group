Surplus proceeds are what a public sale of [tax-forfeited land](./tax-forfeited-land.md) brings above the minimum bid, which in Minnesota is the parcel's delinquent taxes, special assessments, penalties, interest and costs (Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 2).
Within 60 days of the sale the county auditor must mail notice and a claim form to every interested party (by certified mail, then a second notice between 90 and 120 days), mail the property's occupants unless it is vacant land, and post a list of sales with open claim periods on the county website.
An interested party has six months from the first mailing to claim; a single undisputed claim is paid after the period closes, multiple claims are divided by interest, and disputes go to district court.
Unclaimed surplus returns to the county's forfeited tax sale fund (subd. 9).

Surplus proceeds are a claims-recovery market in the same family as [unclaimed property](./unclaimed-property.md): money owed to a person who may not know it exists, most often the heirs of an owner who died, which a finder can locate and help claim.
Chapter 282 sets no fee cap for that help as of 2026-09-26.
