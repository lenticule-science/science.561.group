A partition action is a court proceeding in which a co-owner of jointly held property asks a court to divide the property among the co-owners or, when physical division is impractical, to order it sold and the proceeds divided according to each co-owner's fractional share.
Any co-owner of property held as tenants in common can generally petition for partition, which means a single fractional-interest holder — including one holding only a small share — can force the process even over the objection of co-owners holding the great majority of the interest.

Partition actions are the specific legal mechanism through which [heirs' property](./heirs-property.md) has historically been vulnerable to involuntary loss: an outside buyer acquires a single heir's fractional interest, often for a small sum from an heir who may not fully understand its value or may live far from the land, and then petitions for partition by sale, forcing a court-ordered auction of the whole parcel — at which the outside buyer is frequently the only informed, capitalized bidder, able to acquire the full property well below its value to the family that had held it for generations (search synthesis; see the [source record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-27-mission-aligned-service-markets/service-market-survey.md)).

The **Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act** (UPHPA), adopted by roughly twenty U.S. states as of this research pass, reforms this process specifically for heirs' property: it requires courts to obtain an independent appraisal before ordering a sale, gives co-tenant heirs a right of first refusal to buy out the petitioning interest before any outside sale, and generally prefers an open-market sale over a forced auction when a sale is ordered at all (search synthesis).
Every UPHPA-state partition action involving heirs' property requires, as a practical matter, a court-ready [chain of title](./chain-of-title.md) and a complete heir tree establishing exactly who the co-owners are and what fractional share each holds — the reconstructed evidentiary product this section's [note on mission-aligned service markets](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-mission-aligned-service-markets-in-land-tenure-and-probate-records.md) identifies as a real, ongoing source of demand for title-clearing work, whether the reconstruction is used to bring a partition action, to defend against one, or to exercise a UPHPA right of first refusal before a sale is ordered.
