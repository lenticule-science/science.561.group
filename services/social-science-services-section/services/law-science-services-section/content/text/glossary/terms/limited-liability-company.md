A limited liability company (LLC) is a legal person formed by filing articles of organization with a state, separate from the people who own it (its members) and the people who run it (its managers, who may be members or not). It holds property, signs contracts, and sues and is sued in its own name. Its members and managers are not personally liable for its debts merely by being members or managers (in Minnesota, Minn. Stat. 322C.0304); a court can still reach them where the company was a sham or its money was mixed with theirs.

What makes the form flexible is that most of its rules are the members' to write. The state's LLC act supplies defaults (in Minnesota: member management with equal votes, and distributions in equal shares) that apply wherever the members' [operating agreement](./operating-agreement.md) is silent. Federal tax law treats a one-member LLC as disregarded (its owner reports its income) and a multi-member LLC as a partnership, unless it elects to be taxed as a corporation.

In Minnesota an LLC is formed under chapter 322C for $135–155, renews every year by December 31 at no charge, and needs no member or manager to be a citizen or resident. See [the 2026-09-26 note on Minnesota law for forming and running an LLC](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-minnesota-law-for-forming-and-running-an-llc.md).

The concept's boundaries: an LLC is not a corporation, whose owners hold shares and elect a board; not a partnership, whose general partners are personally liable; and not a sole proprietorship, which is its owner.
