The National Do Not Call Registry is run by the US Federal Trade Commission.
People register phone numbers for free. Telemarketers must download the
registry, scrub their calling lists against it at least every 31 days, and stop
calling a number within 31 days of its registration.

## Who pays

The callers pay, not the people listed. For fiscal year 2027 the FTC charges
$85 per area code, with the first five area codes free and a ceiling of
$23,425 a year for the whole country. Exempt callers, such as some charities
and political campaigns, get access free.

## Scale and enforcement

The registry held a record 258 million numbers in 2026. In that year the FTC
brought 173 cases against 570 companies and 449 individuals and collected about
$400 million. Fines can reach $50,120 per call.

## Why it matters here

It shows that a free-to-register, paid-to-query opt-out list can fund itself
and reach national scale, but only because law makes checking it mandatory.
Email has no such list; see
[National Do Not Email Registry](national-do-not-email-registry.md).
