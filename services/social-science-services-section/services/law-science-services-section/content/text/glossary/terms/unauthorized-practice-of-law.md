Unauthorized practice of law (UPL) is engaging in an act reserved to licensed attorneys — preparing legal documents, giving legal advice, representing another person's legal interests — for a fee, without holding a law license.
General UPL doctrine, per bar-association guidance surveyed, treats the collection of a fee as the factor weighing most heavily toward a UPL finding: "if you open and operate a business to assist others in the completion of legal forms and filing for a fee, unless you are a licensed attorney, you are engaged in the unauthorized practice of law" (search synthesis, aggregated across Illinois State Bar Association ethics guidance and general UPL explainers; see the [2026-08-28 claims recovery mechanism record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-claims-recovery-mechanism/claims-recovery-survey.md)).
Two real precedents illustrate enforcement without resolving where the line falls for claims recovery specifically: **LegalZoom** was sued in a certified class action in New Jersey over its online legal-document-preparation service, and **DoNotPay**, marketed as "the world's first robot lawyer" while being, in the plaintiff's own words, "not actually a robot, a lawyer, nor a law firm," was sued in California state court on the same theory.

Across the four claims-recovery channels surveyed, UPL and its sister occupational-licensing regimes disqualify unevenly rather than uniformly.
No source found treats the core act of locating and filing an [unclaimed-property](./unclaimed-property.md) claim as UPL itself; the barrier there, where one exists, is a specific occupational license (Texas requires a private-investigator-adjacent Investigations Company License), not UPL doctrine.
Filing a class-action claim form **on one's own behalf** is never UPL — asserting one's own rights is unrestricted — but filing **for others, for a fee** is real UPL-adjacent territory; a cluster of consumer apps (Payout, ClaimHood, Settlemate) appear, by inference from who has and hasn't faced UPL litigation rather than from a stated rule, to survive by staying ministerial (matching a claimant's records against a settlement's own already-adjudicated eligibility criteria) rather than exercising legal judgment about a contested claim — a working hypothesis, flagged **[UNVERIFIED]** as an articulated legal test.
Medical bill auditing is functionally unregulated as a profession (no license found required), but negotiating directly with an insurer over a denial risks crossing into the licensed **public-adjuster** regime — [subrogation](./subrogation.md)'s consumer-facing edge (deductible recovery) carries the same risk more directly, since public adjusting requires "you can't negotiate a claim with the insurance company on behalf of the owner without a license," a regime distinct from UPL proper but functioning the same way: a fee-for-representation activity gated to a licensed class of practitioners.
