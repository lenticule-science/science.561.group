Foreign registration (also called foreign qualification, or a certificate of authority) is a company's registration to transact business in a state other than the one where it was formed. To that second state the company is "foreign." Registering names a registered agent and office in that state, pays a fee, and brings that state's annual report; a company that transacts business without registering owes the back fees with penalties and, in most states, cannot sue in that state's courts until it registers. Its members' limited liability is not lost.

Each state lists activities that are not transacting business, such as litigation, holding bank accounts, internal meetings, isolated transactions and interstate commerce (in Wisconsin, Wis. Stat. 183.0905; in Minnesota, Minn. Stat. 322C.0803). A rented, staffed office is not on either list. A Minnesota LLC with its office in Superior therefore registers in Wisconsin (Form 521, $100, then a $65 annual report), and a Wisconsin LLC doing land business in Minnesota registers in Minnesota ($205, free renewal).
