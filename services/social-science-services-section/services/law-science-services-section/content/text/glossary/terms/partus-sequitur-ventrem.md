# partus sequitur ventrem

A historical rule assigning a child's status of bond or free according to the mother's legal condition. In colonial Virginia the 1662 Act XII stated the rule in the specific context of children fathered by Englishmen upon enslaved Black women. The law made maternal legal condition decisive for the child's bondage, without giving the mother control of the child's political or property status.

## Scope and counterexamples

The Latin shorthand describes a legal mechanism, not inherited genetic character or a modern universal rule. Consult the precise jurisdiction and date; maternal ancestry can also support freedom litigation, as documented in Virginia cases involving illegally enslaved Indigenous mothers. Do not assume consensual conception or a purpose-built breeding facility.
