An FDA US agent is the person or firm, physically located in the United States, that a foreign establishment must designate when it registers with the Food and Drug Administration. Foreign food facilities designate one under 21 CFR 1.227; foreign medical device establishments under 21 CFR 807.40; foreign drug establishments under 21 CFR 207.69. The agent must have a US street address (not a post office box) and be available by phone during US business hours. The FDA sends it communications about the establishment: registration problems, import holds, inspection scheduling and emergencies; for device establishments the FDA treats information given to the agent as given to the establishment.

A US agent is not the [importer of record](../../../../../economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/importer-of-record.md), takes no title to goods, and needs no license. The FDA charges nothing for the designation; private agents charge from about $250 a year to $1,500 for staffed firms, by 2026 search synthesis. Device establishments renew registration every year between October 1 and December 31, and food facilities every even-numbered year in the same window.

Device registrations, with each establishment's named US agent (name, business, phone, email, address), are public in openFDA's device registration and listing data: 42,240 records for establishments in mainland China as of 2026-09-14 ([openFDA](https://open.fda.gov/apis/device/registrationlisting/)). That makes the device agent market legible: who the agents are, how many clients each holds, and which establishments are due to renew. See [the 2026-09-26 law note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-us-law-for-doing-business-with-and-for-chinese-firms.md), §4.
