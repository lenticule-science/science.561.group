A reverter is the grantor's right, written into a deed or sale agreement, to have title come back if the grantee fails a condition.
Free-lot programs secure the lot this way: Duluth's Rebuild Duluth agreements required construction to start within a year of closing and finish with a certificate of occupancy by a stated date, and "if Buyer fails to meet either ... deadline ... DEDA may cancel the sale and title to the Property shall revert to DEDA."
Small-town programs use the same device, or a deposit refunded at completion (Tyler, Minnesota holds $5,000; Elwood, Nebraska $500), or both.

Minnesota's own conveyances of forfeited land for an authorized public use are conditional deeds that revert "by operation of law" when the use fails (Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 1c).
