A library materials policy is the written procedure by which a library's governing body selects materials, receives challenges to them, and reconsiders them. Libraries have long kept one as part of a [collection policy](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/library-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/collection-policy.md); since 2024 Minnesota requires one.

Under Minn. Stat. 134.51, subd. 5, the governing body of every Minnesota public library, school district or charter school library, and public college library must adopt a policy for selection, challenges, and reconsideration that respects parents' rights under 120B.20, names who administers it (a licensed library media specialist, a holder of a master's degree in library science, or a professional librarian or person trained in collection management), and reports each completed challenge to the commissioner of education. The policy may not remove material for its viewpoint alone. School libraries' plans must also cover deselection and carry an intellectual and academic freedom statement (124D.991). See [the 2026-09-27 note on Minnesota law for library materials policies](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/27/2026-09-27-note-on-minnesota-law-for-library-materials-policies.md).
