Minnesota's alien farm law, Minn. Stat. 500.221 (1977), provides that no natural person may acquire an interest in agricultural land in Minnesota unless a US citizen or a permanent resident alien, and that no corporation, partnership, trust or other entity may do so unless at least 80% of its stock or beneficial interest is held by citizens or permanent resident aliens ([Revisor](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/500.221)). Exemptions cover land acquired by inheritance or in collecting a debt (to be sold within three years), land for mining, timber and forestry, research, pipelines and utilities, and some processing uses. The Minnesota Department of Agriculture administers it ([MDA](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/minnesotas-alien-farm-law)).

The statute names no country. As of 2026-09-26 no Minnesota law singling out China or other "foreign adversaries" for land ownership had been found, while Iowa, North Dakota and South Dakota have such laws and Wisconsin bills would bar foreign-adversary ownership of any real property ([Committee of 100](https://www.committee100.org/our-work/federal-and-state-bills-prohibiting-property-ownership-by-foreign-individuals-and-entities/)).

For the Group's land contracts business, a buyer's nationality matters only for agricultural land: a Chinese buyer can take assignment of an urban lot, a dwelling or a commercial site in Minnesota, but not of farmland.
