Earnest money is the deposit a buyer makes when a real estate purchase agreement is signed, held by a neutral closing agent (in Minnesota usually a title company) and credited to the price at closing.
The agreement says when it is returned (a failed contingency, a buyer's termination inside a due-diligence window, unmarketable title) and when the seller keeps it (buyer default; in Minnesota a seller's remedy of keeping earnest money is tied to cancellation under Minn. Stat. 559.21).

In a business that assigns purchase agreements, earnest money is the only capital at risk before closing: a few hundred dollars on a vacant lot rather than the price of the land.
It is also what makes the agreement serious to the seller, and why a deal recorded as contracted names both the closing agent holding the deposit and its amount.
