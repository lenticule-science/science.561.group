A real estate broker, under Minn. Stat. 82.55, subd. 19, is any person who, for another and for a commission, fee or other consideration, lists, sells, exchanges, buys, rents or manages real estate, or offers or attempts to negotiate a sale, option, exchange, purchase or rental of an interest or estate in real estate, or advertises that they do.
Acting in Minnesota as a broker requires a license from the Department of Commerce; a salesperson is licensed to act on a broker's behalf.

The definition has a second, quantitative branch (clause g): a person is presumed to be in the business of selling real estate, owned or not, after engaging as principal in five or more transactions in any twelve-month period, unless a licensed broker or salesperson represents them.
That presumption is what sets how a business that assigns purchase agreements grows past its first few deals: either a licensee represents it on each transaction or someone in it holds the license.
Minn. Stat. 82.56 exempts, among others, attorneys, court-appointed fiduciaries, and builders who buy land to build and resell, up to 25 transactions a year.
