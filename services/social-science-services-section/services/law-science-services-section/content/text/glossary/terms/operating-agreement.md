An operating agreement is the members' contract about their limited liability company: who manages it and what they may decide alone, what each member's share of profits and losses is, when money is paid out, how a new member is admitted and how a leaving member is bought out, and how disputes are settled. It is not filed with the state and is usually not public.

Its force comes from the LLC act's structure: the act's rules are defaults that govern only where the agreement is silent. In Minnesota the defaults are member management with equal votes and distributions in equal shares among members (Minn. Stat. 322C.0404, 322C.0407), so a group whose members contribute differently, or where one person leads the work, needs an agreement to say so. An agreement cannot waive what the act makes mandatory, such as the core duties of loyalty, care and good faith, or a member's right to sue (322C.0110).

A multi-member LLC's agreement also sets how taxable profit is allocated among members, and commonly provides tax distributions, because each member owes tax on their allocated share whether or not it was paid out. See [the 2026-09-26 note on Minnesota law for forming and running an LLC](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-minnesota-law-for-forming-and-running-an-llc.md), §4.
