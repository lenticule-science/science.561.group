A terms sale is a sale of [tax-forfeited land](./tax-forfeited-land.md) in which the county takes part of the price at purchase and the rest in installments.
Under Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 4, at least ten percent is paid at purchase and the balance in no more than ten equal annual installments, or on the county board's schedule of up to twelve payments a year, over no more than ten years, with interest at the rate of section 279.03.
St. Louis County's terms in 2026 are ten percent or $500, whichever is greater, up to ten years, and seven percent; its settlement sales are cash only.

The buyer holds a certificate of sale, and the state conveys title only when the price is paid.
Standing timber may not be cut until its appraised value has been paid.
Missing an installment, the interest, or the current year's property taxes is a default on which the county board may cancel the certificate and resell the land (subd. 5).
A terms sale is the county's own seller financing, and it lets a buyer with a few hundred dollars take possession of forest land.
