Intestate succession is the body of default inheritance law that determines who inherits a deceased person's property when that person dies without a valid will.
Every U.S. state maintains its own intestacy statute, a fixed schedule that distributes an estate among a legally defined set of surviving relatives — typically a spouse and children first, then more distant relatives if none survive — with no room for the decedent's actual wishes, because none were formally recorded.

The distribution mechanism is the specific fact that produces [heirs' property](./heirs-property.md): where a will names one executor and can direct real property to a single heir, intestate succession instead distributes real property to every qualifying descendant simultaneously, as undivided tenants in common, with each descendant's fractional share fixed by the statute's formula rather than by any deed naming them individually.
No single step in this process requires anyone to record a new deed reflecting the new ownership — the property simply becomes co-owned by operation of law, often without any of the new co-owners realizing it or taking any action, and the county land record continues to show the original decedent as the owner of record.

Because the process repeats at each subsequent co-owner's death, intestate succession is recursive: if a second-generation co-owner also dies without a will, that co-owner's own fractional share is further subdivided among their own heirs, and the parcel's true ownership structure grows more complex and more undocumented with each generation.
This compounding is the mechanism behind [fractionated heirship](./fractionated-heirship.md), and it is why reconstructing a usable [chain of title](./chain-of-title.md) for heirs' property requires tracing every death in the ownership line, not just the first one — the [source record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-27-mission-aligned-service-markets/service-market-survey.md) behind this section's mission-aligned-markets note describes this multi-generation reconstruction as the specific work an heir-search vendor is paid to perform.

Intestate succession is a general feature of every U.S. jurisdiction's property law, not a phenomenon unique to any one community; its disproportionate documented impact on Black-owned Southern land reflects, per the [source record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-27-mission-aligned-service-markets/service-market-survey.md), historically unequal access to estate-planning legal services rather than any difference in the underlying statute (search synthesis).
