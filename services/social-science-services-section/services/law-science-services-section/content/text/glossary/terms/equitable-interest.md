An equitable interest in real estate is the buyer's right, arising from a signed and enforceable purchase agreement, to acquire the property on the agreement's terms: to have the seller convey title at closing for the agreed price.
The seller keeps legal title until closing, but the buyer's right is itself property: it can be enforced (by specific performance, where the agreement allows) and, when the agreement permits, assigned.

Assigning a purchase agreement transfers that equitable interest, not the land.
This is why a business that assigns contracts for a fee markets its contract (the right to buy a named parcel at a named price) and not the land it does not own, and why it is selling its own interest rather than negotiating the landowner's sale.
