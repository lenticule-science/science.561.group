The consolidated screening list is a single list, published free by the International Trade Administration, of every party on which the US Departments of the Treasury, Commerce and State impose export, import, financial or dealing restrictions. It merges Treasury's Specially Designated Nationals list and its sectoral, non-SDN and CAPTA lists; Commerce's Entity List, Military End User List, Unverified List and Denied Persons List; and State's debarred and nonproliferation lists. Each entry carries its names and aliases, addresses, identifying numbers, the program it is listed under and the source list ([data.trade.gov consolidated.json](https://data.trade.gov/downloadable_consolidated_screening_list/v1/consolidated.json), read 2026-09-26, about 34 MB).

Screening a counterparty means checking its name, its aliases and its owners against the list before an engagement and before significant payments. A match on a Treasury SDN entry means US persons may not deal with that party at all, with strict civil liability; a match on the Entity List restricts what may be exported to it; other lists carry other restrictions, so a hit is read by the list it comes from, not treated as one flag. A near match (a shared common name, a different city) is resolved by the identifying details. Commerce's affiliates rule, suspended until 2026-11-09, extends Entity List restrictions to firms 50% or more owned by listed parties, which is why owners are screened too.

Screening is what a US business does for itself; the payment rails it uses screen as well, and a bank's own caution can cut off a customer never named on any list ([secondary sanctions](./secondary-sanctions.md)). See [the 2026-09-26 law note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-us-law-for-doing-business-with-and-for-chinese-firms.md), §5.
