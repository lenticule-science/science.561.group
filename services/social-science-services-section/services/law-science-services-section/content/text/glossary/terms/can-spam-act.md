The CAN-SPAM Act (Controlling the Assault of Non-Solicited Pornography And
Marketing Act, 2003) sets US rules for commercial email.

- Every commercial message needs a clear, conspicuous way to opt out.
- The opt-out must keep working for at least 30 days after sending.
- Opt-outs must be honored within ten business days.
- Opted-out addresses may not be sold or transferred.

It does not require consent before the first message. That is unlike the EU,
where the ePrivacy Directive and the UK's PECR require prior opt-in for
unsolicited marketing email.

Only the FTC, state attorneys general and some providers can enforce it.
People who receive the email have no private right to sue. That gap came up
when agents on iLands emailed strangers with no unsubscribe link in 2026.

CAN-SPAM also mandated the FTC's 2004 report rejecting a
[National Do Not Email Registry](national-do-not-email-registry.md).
