A chain of title is the documented, unbroken sequence of ownership transfers that connects a property's current claimed owner back to some earlier point of recognized clear title, typically the last owner whose title was itself formally clear and recorded.
Every link in the chain is a discrete recorded event — a deed, a probate decree distributing an estate, a court judgment — and a chain is only as strong as its weakest link: a single missing, ambiguous, or improperly executed transfer anywhere in the sequence can make every later claim resting on it legally defective, regardless of how clean the more recent transfers are.

For ordinary titled property, establishing a chain of title is usually a bounded task: a title company or attorney searches one county's deed index across a manageable number of transfers.
For [heirs' property](./heirs-property.md), the task is materially harder, because the chain does not run through deeds at all for one or more generations — it runs through [intestate succession](./intestate-succession.md), meaning the "transfer" at that link is not a recorded deed but a legal event (a death) whose consequences (who inherited what fractional share) must be reconstructed from probate records, vital records, and genealogical research rather than read off a single recorded instrument.
Reconstructing a chain of title across several such generations, often spanning multiple counties as family members relocated, is the specific technical work this section's [note on mission-aligned service markets](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-mission-aligned-service-markets-in-land-tenure-and-probate-records.md) identifies as an existing, priced commercial category (named vendors, per-year and per-person pricing, contingency-fee alternatives — see the [source record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-27-mission-aligned-service-markets/service-market-survey.md) for figures).

A reconstructed chain of title is not, by itself, a legally binding determination of ownership.
It is the evidentiary basis on which a licensed attorney issues a title opinion, or on which a party presents its case in a [partition action](./partition-action.md) or a probate proceeding — the reconstruction work is paralegal-tier input to a legal product requiring attorney sign-off, a distinction this section's mission-aligned-markets note treats as a load-bearing constraint on the market rather than a detail.
A chain-of-title reconstruction is also, structurally, an instance of what this section's [evidentiary credentialing](evidentiary-credentialing.md) term describes more generally: a body of underlying fact (here, a family's actual inheritance history) reformatted into the specific documentary form a legal proceeding is built to accept.
