The Foreign Agents Registration Act (22 U.S.C. 611 et seq., 1938) requires a person who acts in the United States as an agent of a foreign principal in political activities, as a public-relations counsel, publicity agent or political consultant, in collecting or disbursing things of value, or in representing the principal before US officials, to register with the Justice Department and disclose the relationship, its terms and the materials it distributes. A foreign principal includes a foreign government or political party, a person outside the US, and a company organized under foreign law or with its principal place of business abroad.

Its commercial exemption, 22 U.S.C. 613(d), exempts private and nonpolitical activities in furtherance of a foreign principal's bona fide trade or commerce, and other activities not serving predominantly a foreign interest ([22 U.S.C. 613](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A22+section%3A613+edition%3Aprelim%29)). Selling a foreign company's products, representing it to customers, holding a regulatory agent role for it, staffing its trade-show booth or running its online store is commercial and exempt. Lobbying US officials, or shaping US public opinion on policy, for it is not. The Justice Department proposed in 2025 to narrow the second limb of the exemption; no final rule had been found as of 2026-09-26.

A US representative of a Chinese company keeps its engagement letters specific about the commercial scope of the work, since that scope is what the exemption turns on. See [the 2026-09-26 law note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-us-law-for-doing-business-with-and-for-chinese-firms.md), §6.
