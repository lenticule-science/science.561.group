Contingency recovery is a fee structure, not a legal category on its own, in which a service that locates, documents, or asserts a claim on someone's behalf is paid only out of what it actually recovers — typically a percentage of the recovered amount — and receives nothing if the claim fails or is never filed.
The structure recurs across every channel surveyed in the [2026-08-28 claims recovery mechanism record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-claims-recovery-mechanism/claims-recovery-survey.md): state [unclaimed property](./unclaimed-property.md) finders charge a statutorily capped [finder's fee](./finders-fee.md) (commonly 10-15% of recovered value); medical bill auditors reported contingency rates clustering at 10-25% of documented savings (search synthesis; below the 20-35% range the record's own research brief anticipated, for reasons not resolved in that pass); and B2B insurance [subrogation](./subrogation.md) recovery firms and accounts-payable recovery-audit firms (PRGX being the clearest example found) both operate on contingency, tied to the percentage of funds actually recovered, with no fee owed on an unsuccessful attempt.

The structure's appeal to a capital-constrained, reputation-sensitive operator is direct: no fee is owed if the attempt fails, so a failed claims-recovery attempt costs the claimant nothing and stakes no reputation on the operator's side either, which is precisely the property this class of mechanism was surveyed for.
Contingency recovery is not, by itself, a legal safe harbor — the fee percentage is independently regulated in the unclaimed-property channel (capped by statute), and the act of recovering the claim can independently trigger [unauthorized-practice-of-law](./unauthorized-practice-of-law.md) or occupational-licensing questions regardless of how the fee is structured, as the public-adjuster licensing regime governing consumer-facing insurance-claim negotiation illustrates.

## Related terms

- [Unclaimed property](./unclaimed-property.md) — the channel with the most heavily regulated, statutorily capped contingency fee
- [Finder's fee](./finders-fee.md) — the specific, capped contingency percentage in the unclaimed-property channel
- [Subrogation](./subrogation.md) — the channel where contingency recovery is practiced almost exclusively at enterprise B2B scale
