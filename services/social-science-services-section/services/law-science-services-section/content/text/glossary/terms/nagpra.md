NAGPRA — the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act — is the 1990 U.S. federal law requiring museums and federal agencies that receive federal funding to inventory Native American human remains and cultural items in their holdings, determine those items' cultural affiliation, and repatriate them to lineal descendants, culturally affiliated tribes, or Native Hawaiian organizations.
A revised implementing rule took effect in **January 2024**, and it set a hard compliance deadline of **2029-01-10** for museums and agencies to complete tribal consultation and file updated inventories; as of 2025 only **48%** of reported remains had been returned, leaving the deadline set against a base of roughly half the covered work still undone (search synthesis; see the [source record](../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-27-mission-aligned-service-markets/service-market-survey.md)).

The 2024 rule's most consequential change is evidentiary: it closed the previously available "culturally unidentifiable" category, which institutions had used to withhold items by asserting that no documentary record could establish a cultural affiliation, and it deliberately **privileges tribal oral history and tribal determination over documentary evidence** in establishing affiliation going forward (search synthesis).
This is a direct rebalancing of evidentiary weight away from the documentary chain-of-custody method that this section's other new terms — [chain of title](./chain-of-title.md), [intestate succession](./intestate-succession.md) — treat as the default reconstruction method for a related but distinct land-tenure problem.

Because of that rebalancing, a research capability skilled at documentary cross-referencing is not automatically mission-aligned in the NAGPRA context the way it is for [heirs' property](./heirs-property.md) title-clearing: the same skill can be hired either to help build the case for a repatriation, or to contest a tribe's claimed affiliation on documentary grounds — to argue, in effect, for withholding rather than returning an item.
Mission alignment in NAGPRA-adjacent work therefore depends entirely on who the buyer is, not on the nature of the research task itself, which is the reason this section's [note on mission-aligned service markets](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-mission-aligned-service-markets-in-land-tenure-and-probate-records.md) treats NAGPRA cultural-affiliation research as a narrow, buyer-gated niche — tribes, tribal consortia, and pro-repatriation firms specifically — rather than a business line to pursue against the larger, better-funded population of museums and universities racing the 2029 deadline.
