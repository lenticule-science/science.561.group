Computer game science is the systematic study of computer games and computational game systems: what they are formally, how their shared state reaches agreement, what happens at their boundaries with ordinary life, how their populations and institutions behave over time, and how they are observed and steered by the people who operate them.

This section studies the field through a portfolio of six standing research programs — Persistent World Foundations, Boundary and Membrane, Web Temporality, Settlement Substrate, Ludocratic Institutions, and Obsmetrology of Play — organized around one founding concern: online web games and persistent worlds, the games whose settling never reaches a fixed point and whose arenas outlive any player's session. The portfolio shares its formal apparatus with the obsmetrological section (arenas as polarized causal event structures; settlement as a monotone closure on a knowledge order) and its play-theoretic vocabulary with the philosophy section's ludics holdings (Huizinga, Caillois, Suits, Sutton-Smith, Geertz, and the persistent-world extensions of 2026-08-22).
