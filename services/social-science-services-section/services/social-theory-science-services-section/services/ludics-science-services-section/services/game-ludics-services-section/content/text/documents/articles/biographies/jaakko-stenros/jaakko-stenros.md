title: "Jaakko Stenros"
description: "The game scholar whose systematic reconciliation split the magic circle into a psychological bubble, a negotiated social contract, and a persisting arena."
date-created: 2026-08-22
---

Jaakko Stenros holds a PhD and is Senior University Lecturer in Game Studies at Tampere University, Finland, working within the Centre of Excellence in Game Culture Studies and its Game Research Lab. His research spans norm-defying play, game rules, folk competitions, queer play, role-playing games, pervasive games, and game jams; he has co-created several larps and curated exhibitions at the Finnish Museum of Games. He is co-author, with Markus Montola, of *Nordic Larp* (2010). In 2022 the Higher Education Video Game Alliance named him a HEVGA Fellow, and in 2025 DiGRA recognized him as a DiGRA Distinguished Scholar.

His load-bearing text for this portfolio is "In Defence of a Magic Circle: The Social, Mental and Cultural Boundaries of Play" (*ToDiGRA* 1(2), 2014, read in full), the most comprehensive literature review of the magic-circle debate among the section's sources, tracing the concept from Huizinga through Salen and Zimmerman and cataloguing the major critiques — Taylor, Copier, Malaby, Consalvo, Lammes, Juul, Pargman and Jakobsson, Liebe and Calleja. Stenros's contribution is to separate three things earlier writers had conflated. First, the psychological bubble: Apter's "protective frame," a personal and phenomenological sense of safety in a playful mindset, present even in solitary play — "In the play-state you experience a protective frame which stands between you and the 'real' world and its problems, creating an enchanted zone in which, in the end, you are confident that no harm can come." Second, the magic circle of play proper: "the social contract that is created through implicit or explicit social negotiation and metacommunication in the act of playing... [which] can become societal as other social frameworks (law, economics) can recognize it." Third, the arena: the culturally recognized spatial and temporal site — a tennis court, an empty chessboard — that persists as residue of play even when unused. The load-bearing move for a portfolio built around persistent worlds is his use of Goffman's interaction membrane: "the border around play is permeable; the wider world passes through more than just application of the transformation rules... it is also possible for the external elements to endanger the transformation rules and thus the encounter itself. It is possible for play to be collapsed by external events." The border, on this account, is not a valve that is either open or shut but a membrane that transforms what crosses it, and that can fail entirely under sufficient pressure.

Stenros's position in the field's boundary debate is that of its synthesizer: where Juul defends the circle by reframing it and Consalvo and Malaby attack it, Stenros's triple decomposition is the version the section treats as the working answer to what survives the critique. Two of the section's six programs hold him. Boundary and Membrane holds "In Defence of a Magic Circle" as its central reconciling text, and its own working answer to what the magic circle decomposes into is explicitly built on Stenros's triple. Obsmetrology of Play holds him a second time through "Deterding, Stenros and Montola's three-count demolition" of the dark-patterns literature, read as part of that program's account of the ethics of steering measured players — a paper on which Stenros is a listed co-author distinct from his solo magic-circle work.
