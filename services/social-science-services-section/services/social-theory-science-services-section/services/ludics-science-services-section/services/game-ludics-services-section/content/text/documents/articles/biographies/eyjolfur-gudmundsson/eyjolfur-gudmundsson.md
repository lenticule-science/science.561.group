Eyjólfur "Eyjo" Guðmundsson is an Icelandic economist and academic administrator. He earned a BSc in economics from the University of Iceland (1992) and a PhD in Environmental and Natural Resource Economics from the University of Rhode Island (2002), then held teaching and dean posts at the University of Akureyri before serving as its rector from 2014 to 2024. In 2007, CCP Games hired him as EVE Online's in-world economist — reported to be the first such hire by any game developer — a role he held until leaving for the Akureyri rectorship in 2014.

CCP's own announcement framed the hire in explicitly economic-infrastructure terms: CEO Hilmar Pétursson stated, "Just as entrepreneurs and executives rely on real-world economic indicators, EVE Online players need timely information and analysis of the in-game economy," and Guðmundsson's own framing of the job was, "By ensuring everyone has access to the same data, we will enhance the player experience and facilitate economic stability in EVE." He headed an eleven-person unit split across in-game economics, subscriber trends, liaison with EVE's player-elected Council of Stellar Management, and internal-affairs monitoring of CCP's own staff, publishing a Quarterly Economic Newsletter that at points ran to fifty pages and covered roughly 300,000 players. In a Ten Ton Hammer interview he described concrete instances of the observation apparatus steering design: he recommended removing NPC-seeded goods to push the economy toward being more player-driven, on the general principle that "anything that makes EVE more player driven is something that I really like"; he framed the PLEX time-for-currency system as embodying his preferred approach of facilitating exchange "without government-like banking oversight"; and the same monitoring apparatus that tracked prices was used for anomaly detection on the game's own infrastructure, discovering that a mere 2% of players — bots — were generating 30% of server CPU load. CCP later throttled the newsletter's publication cadence in favor of monthly price-index devblogs (2011, per secondary trade coverage), a reduction in observation frequency the section treats as itself a data point rather than incidental.

Guðmundsson's position in the field is unusual: he is not primarily a scholar publishing into an academic debate but a practitioner who institutionalized, as an actual standing job description, what the rest of the observe-and-steer literature describes as an emergent practice. His EVE tenure is the cleanest documented case of an operator formalizing continuous instrumented observation of a game economy rather than treating economic management as an ad hoc patch response. One program of the section holds him: Obsmetrology of Play, where the EVE economist apparatus — reporting cadence, the CSM liaison function, and the doubled use of economic telemetry for both market analysis and bot detection — is treated as the field's clearest real-world literalization of continuous operator observation as standing institution rather than metaphor.
