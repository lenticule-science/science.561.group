A shared daily instance is a design in which the game's content for a given day is one single instance, identical for every player rather than generated privately or at random per session. The clearest documented case in this section's sources, and the source of the label as it is used in the Web Temporality program charter, is Wordle. Josh Wardle, its creator, states the design's purpose directly in a TechCrunch interview: "It's something about the fact that it's one puzzle, and everybody is solving it" — and he is explicit that the effect depends on the sharedness, not merely on the daily cadence: had players received endless different words, in his account, the game's shared-culture effect would not have emerged.

Wardle frames the shared daily instance as deliberately opposed to the engagement mechanics documented elsewhere in this section's sources under idle game and appointment mechanic. Where those designs use scheduled return to extract attention or spend — Ian Bogost's Cow Clicker being this glossary's clearest instance — Wardle describes rejecting that entire playbook on principle: "I kind of deliberately did what you're not meant to do if growth is your goal," refusing push notifications ("Why don't you just forget about Wordle for a little bit?") and monetization alike ("I like giving it away for free — part of the point is it's free"). His stated design ethic generalizes the point: "I think people have an appetite for things that transparently don't want anything from you." The shared daily instance and the appointment mechanic thus present, in this section's sources, the same surface structure — a single scheduled daily return — put to opposite design purposes: bounded scarcity and synchronized community on one side, open-ended extraction on the other.

Kingdom of Loathing's daily-turn allowance, documented under action point, is a useful contrast for sharpening what "shared" adds here: its forty-turns-per-day cap is also daily and also banked against a ceiling, but it is a private allowance, not a single instance every player faces together — each player's turns are their own, while Wordle's word is the same word for everyone on a given day. The section's own reading, offered as a proposed framing rather than a claim in the sources themselves, describes the shared daily instance as a kind of circle drawn in time: one arena-instance synchronizing millions of otherwise disjoint, asynchronous sessions, which is what makes its results — the now-familiar grid of green, yellow, and gray squares — socially exchangeable in a way a private daily allowance's results are not.
