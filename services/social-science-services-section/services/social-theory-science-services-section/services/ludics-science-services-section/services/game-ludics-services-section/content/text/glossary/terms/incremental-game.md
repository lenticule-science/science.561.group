"Incremental game" is a term this entry must flag as carried at second hand relative to the section's sources: nowhere in the material gathered for the web-temporality research program is it given a definition of its own, distinct from idle game. The research angle that grounds this glossary names its subject matter as "PBBGs, idle/incremental games, asynchronous social-game mechanics, and daily-puzzle temporality," and the program charter Web Temporality lists "idle/incremental games and their design discourse" as a single object of study — in both places the word appears yoked to "idle," never defined against it. No source read for this section states what, if anything, distinguishes an incremental game from an idle game as a matter of genre boundary.

What the sources do supply is the concrete design lineage that the term is evidently meant to pick out: a line of games built around a visibly escalating counter that the player increases, directly or through generators, by repeated clicking. Sonia Fizek's genre history in "Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games" traces this line from Progress Quest (2002), through Cow Clicker (2010) and Cookie Clicker (2013), to Dreeps (2016). Cookie Clicker's creator, Julien "Orteil" Thiennot, describes the mechanic's appeal in a Vice retrospective in terms that read as a definition of the incremental loop even though he does not use the word: "It wasn't meant to be fun! We just tapped into the core psychological appeal" of visible, accumulating progress — a description of the generator-and-counter structure common to the games the sources place under this label.

Because the term is not independently theorized in the material available, this entry treats "incremental game" as naming the mechanical surface — the counter, the generator, the click — of games that the sources otherwise analyze under the heading of idle game, and refers the reader there, and to zero-player game and ambience act, for the theoretical apparatus (interpassivity, delegated play) that the sources do develop for this family of designs. Whether a principled line separates "incremental" from "idle" — for instance, on the axis of how much of the game's progress requires the player's clicks versus proceeds by itself while they are away — is an open question the section's sources do not settle, and this entry does not resolve it either.
