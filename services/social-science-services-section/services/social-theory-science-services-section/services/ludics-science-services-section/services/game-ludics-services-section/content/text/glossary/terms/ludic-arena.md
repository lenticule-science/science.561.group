The ludic arena is this glossary's name for the third of three boundaries that Jaakko Stenros distinguishes under the single umbrella term "magic circle" — Stenros himself calls it simply the arena, and this entry uses the fuller phrase only to keep it distinct from other uses of "arena" elsewhere in this section's vocabulary. Unlike the protective frame, which is personal and requires no other participant, and the magic circle proper, which is a social contract actively negotiated between participants, the ludic arena is a site: a culturally recognized spatial or temporal structure for play that persists as residue even when nobody is currently playing there. Stenros's own examples are a tennis court and an "empty" chessboard — both remain legible as structures for play in the intervals between games, requiring only activation by use to become the site of play again, and requiring no one to hold a playful mindset in order to exist as that kind of site in the first place.

This third boundary is the one most directly implicated by persistence. A tennis court between matches is a minor case of an arena outliving a session; a running MMORPG server is a far more consequential one, since — as the persistent-world literature converging around Richard Bartle, Mark Bell, and Raph Koster documents independently of Stenros — its systems, economy, and population continue to exist and develop whether or not any particular player is present. Celia Pearce's ethnography of the Uru diaspora supplies the clearest test of what the ludic arena's persistence actually consists in: when *Uru: Ages Beyond Myst*'s servers closed in 2004, the arena itself was deleted outright, yet the community she followed, "The Gathering," carried its shared identity intact across four subsequent worlds over eighteen months. That case shows the ludic arena and the community that plays within it can come apart entirely — the site can end while the play, relocated, does not — which is a sharper decoupling of arena from play than Stenros's own examples, drawn from games that simply go unused between sessions rather than being destroyed, anticipate.

Stenros treats the ludic arena as requiring "activation by use," a phrase that also connects this concept to the magic circle proper: an arena is where a magic circle, once negotiated, is enacted, but the arena's persistence is independent of any particular negotiation taking place within it. This glossary reads that relationship, following Stenros's own structure, as arena supplying the standing site and the magic circle proper supplying the recurring, renegotiable social contract that activates it.
