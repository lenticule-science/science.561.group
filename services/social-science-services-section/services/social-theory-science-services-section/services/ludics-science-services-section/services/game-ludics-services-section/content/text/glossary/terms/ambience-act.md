An ambience act, in Sonia Fizek's use of the term in "Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games," names "the moments when the digital game plays itself while waiting for the player to return and continue where they left off." Fizek draws the term from Alexander Galloway's Gaming: Essays on Algorithmic Culture (2006), a source this entry did not obtain and reads only through Fizek's citation of it, so Galloway's own fuller argument about ambience in games is carried here at second hand and should be treated with that caveat.

The term's analytic work in Fizek's essay is to draw a line inside the broader category of "the game does something while nobody is touching it." She distinguishes ambience acts that are merely incidental — the idling animations of characters in GTA III or Final Fantasy X when the player walks away and leaves the controller untouched, which "have no effect" once play resumes — from the ambience acts that constitute idle games proper, where, in her words, "the game plays itself and the changes in the gameworld are permanent and significant, whether the human player participates in their execution or not." The distinction is not about whether the screen shows motion during the player's absence but about whether that motion writes lasting state: an idling sprite is cosmetic, while a resource total that keeps climbing, a queue that keeps clearing, or a character that keeps leveling is not.

This is the concept that supplies idle game with its formal criterion for genre membership, and it is the mechanism Fizek's theory of interpassivity claims the player derives pleasure from delegating: the ambience act is what interpassivity is interpassive about. It also names, in existing game-studies vocabulary, close to what the section's own obsmetrological apparatus — as read by the web-temporality research program, and offered here only as a proposed reading, not an established identification — would describe as continued settlement of an arena's event structure on its own clock, absent any move from the player occupying the Player role. The zero-player game is the limit at which the ambience act is the entirety of the game's activity, with no non-ambient act ever occurring at all.
