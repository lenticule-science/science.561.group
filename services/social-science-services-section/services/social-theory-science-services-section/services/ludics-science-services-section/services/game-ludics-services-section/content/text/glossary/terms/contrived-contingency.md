Contrived contingency is Thomas Malaby's term for the manufactured, structured uncertainty that a game generates, and his 2007 paper "Beyond Play" builds an entire redefinition of "game" around it: "a game is a semibounded and socially legitimate domain of contrived contingency that generates interpretable outcomes." The definition is deliberately built to avoid the vocabulary of play altogether, since Malaby's larger argument, treated in this glossary under ludic exceptionalism, is that play's inherited features — separable, safe, pleasurable — are not intrinsic to games but historically specific cultural accomplishments. Contingency, in this reframing, is not a byproduct of play that a game happens to produce; it is what a game is organized to produce, and "contrived" marks that the uncertainty is engineered rather than found.

Malaby identifies four distinct sources from which a game can draw its contrived contingency, and treats a game as a domain that calibrates all four rather than eliminating any of them. Stochastic contingency is pure chance — dice, network lag, weather — what Malaby, following MacIntyre, calls "pure contingency." Social contingency arises from not knowing another participant's intentions or resources, the game-theoretic uncertainty central to chess or poker. Performative contingency is execution risk: the possibility that an attempted action simply succeeds or fails in the doing. Semiotic contingency is instability in what an outcome means once it has occurred, which Malaby illustrates from his own ethnographic experience as a foreigner winning at backgammon in Greece, where the win's significance shifted depending on who was interpreting it. His ethnographic anchor for all four is Cretan dice gambling, or *zaria*, which he describes as "an existential engagement with the game" bearing no resemblance to leisure or fun — the stakes there are inheritance, status, and standing with the state, not entertainment.

Malaby's own footnote extends the stakes of the concept directly into this glossary's persistent-world material, flagging virtual worlds as the site where treating contrived contingency as merely "a place where nothing happens" does the most political damage, since it forecloses ethical scrutiny of what a world's design actually does to the people playing in it — a claim this glossary reads as directly continuous with the breach cases treated under boundary-work and interaction-membrane.
