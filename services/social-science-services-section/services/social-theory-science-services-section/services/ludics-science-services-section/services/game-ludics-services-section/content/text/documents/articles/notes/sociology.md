Ludics is the sociological study of play. The subdomain commits to *play* as a real and analytically tractable phenomenon — not as the absence of seriousness, not as a contrast class to work, but as a positive form of human activity with its own structure, history, and political stakes. The classical sources are Johan Huizinga's *Homo Ludens* (1938), Roger Caillois's *Les jeux et les hommes* (1958), and the long line of subsequent work in anthropology, sociology, philosophy, and game studies that engaged the two.

The subdomain's terms name the analytical vocabulary. Caillois's four-fold typology — *agon* (competition), *alea* (chance), *mimicry* (simulation, role), *ilinx* (vertigo, the dissolution of stable perception) — is one organizing scheme; the *paidia*–*ludus* axis (free play versus rule-bound play) is the other. Huizinga's *magic circle* names the bounded space within which play takes place and the question of what holds the boundary; the long-running debate over whether the magic circle is a useful concept or a misleading one is part of the substance the subdomain carries. *Homo ludens*, *deep play* (Geertz), and *flow* (Csíkszentmihályi) are further canonical concepts the texts engage.

The subdomain's texts develop the substantive analysis at length: [*what-is-ludics*](./what-is-ludics.md) is the front-door essay; [*ludics-history*](./ludics-history.md) and [*ludics-genealogy*](./ludics-genealogy.md) lay out the historical and conceptual lineage; [*ludic-analysis*](./ludic-analysis.md) is the methodological statement; [*ludic-structures*](./ludic-structures.md) develops the structural analysis; [*play-and-culture*](./play-and-culture.md) and [*the-magic-circle-and-its-critics*](./the-magic-circle-and-its-critics.md) engage the canonical debates; [*theories-of-play*](./theories-of-play.md) is the survey.

The open work centers on the [Girard–Caillois substrate-correspondence question](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/notes/questions-on-girard-caillois-substrate-correspondence.md), held by the parent ludics-science section, about how Caillois's typology relates to Girard's proof-theoretic ludics.
