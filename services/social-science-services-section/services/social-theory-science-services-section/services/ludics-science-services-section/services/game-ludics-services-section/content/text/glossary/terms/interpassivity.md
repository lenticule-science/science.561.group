Interpassivity is a term originating in 1990s art theory, developed by Robert Pfaller and Slavoj Žižek against the more familiar idea of interactivity, and imported into the study of idle games by Sonia Fizek in "Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games" (2018). Pfaller's definition, as Fizek quotes it, is precise: "Interpassivity is delegated 'passivity' — in the sense of delegated pleasure, or delegated consumption. Interpassive people are those who want to delegate their pleasures or their consumption. Interpassive media are all the agents — machines, people, animals, etc. — to whom interpassive people can delegate their pleasures." Neither Pfaller's nor Žižek's original texts were obtained directly for this section; both are carried here as Fizek quotes them, and should be read with that second-hand caveat attached.

The canonical examples Fizek reports from this literature are meant to make the concept concrete before it is applied to games: the video cassette recorder that "passively enjoys for me" by recording a film the owner never watches (Žižek's phrase); canned laughter on a television soundtrack, which laughs so the viewer does not have to; the Tibetan prayer wheel, which prays continuously so its owner need not; and the photocopier that "reads" a scholarly text so the scholar is spared the reading. In each case an object or a mechanism performs an act of consumption or devotion that a person is nominally the subject of, while that person's own relation to the act becomes external, delegated, and in a strict sense passive even though something is undeniably happening.

Fizek's application to idle games treats the game's own self-play — its ambience acts, discussed elsewhere in this glossary — as exactly this kind of interpassive medium: the player delegates the doing of play to the clicker algorithm, and the algorithm's continued operation stands in for the player's own enjoyment while they are elsewhere. Her own formulation states the mechanism directly: "In idle games, the player assumes a role of a playing subject, but to a large degree escapes the pressure of the actual play, giving up their agency and delegating it to the clicker algorithms. The game still progresses and its progress relies on the human player's decision to externalise the act of play onto a technical device." She sets this against Björk and Juul's stricter agency criterion for what counts as playing at all, discussed under zero-player game, since interpassivity is precisely the condition under which that criterion becomes doubtful without becoming false: the player still, in some residual sense, plays, but has arranged for the game to do the playing. The section's web-temporality program treats the automatic-play literature on MMORPG bots — where a comparable delegation happens through mechanical automation of a persistent world's skilled labor rather than through an idle game's own designed self-play — as an open, unformalized cousin of the same phenomenon, a proposed connection rather than an established one.
