Sonia Fizek is a media and game studies scholar. She holds a PhD in media and game studies from Bangor University in the UK and an MA in English literature and linguistics from the University of Lodz, Poland, completed in 2007 with a thesis on cyberfiction and digital technology's effect on literature. She has taught at Bangor University, Abertay University, Leuphana University Lüneburg, Goethe University Frankfurt, Design Akademie Berlin, and the University of Lodz, and since 2020 has held a professorship in Media and Game Studies, with a specialization in player research, at the Cologne Game Lab, TH Köln, where she also serves on the institute's executive board. She is the author of Playing at a Distance (MIT Press, 2022), which examines automation, AI, and posthuman forms of play.

Fizek's load-bearing work for this portfolio is "Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games," a draft article for the Digital Games Research Association's journal read in full. Its governing move is importing Robert Pfaller and Slavoj Žižek's concept of interpassivity — the inverse of interactivity — into idle games: "Interpassivity is delegated 'passivity' — in the sense of delegated pleasure, or delegated consumption. Interpassive people are those who want to delegate their pleasures or their consumption. Interpassive media are all the agents — machines, people, animals, etc. — to whom interpassive people can delegate their pleasures" (quoting Pfaller). She defines idle games formally through Alexander Galloway's concept of "ambience acts" — "the moments when the digital game plays itself while waiting for the player to return and continue where they left off" — and distinguishes true idle games, where "the game plays itself and the changes in the gameworld are permanent and significant, whether the human player participates in their execution or not," from games with merely incidental idle animations, whose changes "have no effect" once play resumes. She traces the genre's lineage from Progress Quest (2002) — "an automated game, which cannot be affected by the player's actions at all, except for 'rolling' the character at the initial stages" — through Cow Clicker (2010) and Cookie Clicker (2013) to Dreeps (2016) and Rouguathia (2017), the latter linked back to Conway's Game of Life as the ur-example of a "zero-player game," and she cites Björk and Juul's criterion that "unless players have some agency to affect the outcome of a game and can intentionally exercise it, they are not really … playing a game" as precisely the boundary idle games test. Her own conclusion is the passage the section treats as most load-bearing: "In idle games, the player assumes a role of a playing subject, but to a large degree escapes the pressure of the actual play, giving up their agency and delegating it to the clicker algorithms. The game still progresses and its progress relies on the human player's decision to externalise the act of play onto a technical device." She is also independently quoted, in the 2019 CHI PLAY interview study of idle-game designers, defining idle games as those "in which humans are spectators and that playing is mostly an activity of game itself," the pleasure derived from "outsourcing gameplay onto the game."

Fizek's theory is the anchor of the Web Temporality program, which draws its central formal vocabulary — Galloway's "ambience acts," the interpassivity frame itself — directly from her article and lists it as the single richest theoretical source in its sweep. The program reads her ambience-acts language as naming, in existing game-studies vocabulary, roughly what the section's own apparatus would describe as continued settlement on an arena's own event structure absent any move from the player — a proposed correspondence the program marks explicitly as its own reading rather than a claim Fizek makes herself. It also treats her genre lineage, ending in Conway's Game of Life as a zero-player limit case, as posing the program's open formal question of what distinguishes a player's strategy from a player's absence when the executing agent is a delegated automaton.
