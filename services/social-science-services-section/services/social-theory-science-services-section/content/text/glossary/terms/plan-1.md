# Plan

The mathematical realization of a [plan](../../documents/articles/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/terms/plan.md): a closure-rule-driven coordination of multiple [task cell systems](task-cell-system.md) under a dependency partial order. A plan is a quadruple

$$P \;=\; (T_P,\; \preceq_P,\; M_P,\; \mathcal{S}_P)$$

where:

- $T_P = \{T_1, \ldots, T_n\}$ — a *finite* set of task cell systems comprising the plan. The work vocabulary's first plan-property (finite commitment) is this finiteness.
- $\preceq_P$ — a partial order on $T_P$, the *dependency order*: $T_i \preceq_P T_j$ iff $T_j$'s closure-rule subsystem references $T_i$'s goal cells in its antecedents. Per the [task term](../../documents/articles/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/terms/plan.md)'s third property (goal orientation) and the dependency-DAG requirement, the partial order is acyclic.
- $M_P \subseteq \Phi_W$ — the *milestone cells*: the cells whose joint settlement constitutes plan-level completion. Often $M_P = \bigcup_i G_{T_i}$ (all subtasks' goal cells), but a plan may also have its own plan-level goals (e.g., $\langle P, \texttt{plan-completed-by}, A\rangle$).
- $\mathcal{S}_P \subseteq \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{op}}$ — the plan's coordination rules. $\mathcal{S}_P$ is the union of the per-task subsystems $\bigcup_i \mathcal{S}_{T_i}$, the dependency-ordering rules, and the milestone-aggregation rules.

## The dependency-ordering rules

For each pair $T_i \prec_P T_j$, the rule

$$\bigwedge_{\phi \in G_{T_i}} v(\phi) = v^*_{T_i}(\phi) \;\Longrightarrow\; \texttt{antecedents of } \mathcal{S}_{T_j} \texttt{ are now admissible}$$

ensures that $T_j$'s closure rules fire only after $T_i$ has settled. This is encoded in the substrate by an explicit gate cell $\langle T_j, \texttt{depends-on-completed}, T_i\rangle$ that must be at $\mathbf{t}$ for $T_j$'s rules to admit antecedents — by [settling-monotonicity](../../documents/articles/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-monotonicity.md), once the gate fires, $T_j$ is permanently unblocked.

## Defeasible stability (Bratman 1987)

The work vocabulary's fourth plan-property — defeasibility — is the property that a plan can be *abandoned* or *revised* but not silently dropped. As substrate operations:

- **Plan abandonment** is an increment $\langle P, \texttt{abandoned-by}, A\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ that propagates to abandonment of all tasks in $T_P$ that have not yet settled.
- **Plan revision** is the addition or removal of a task in $T_P$: an increment that either adds $\langle P, \texttt{contains}, T_{n+1}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ for a new task or $\langle P, \texttt{contains}, T_i\rangle = \mathbf{f}$ for a removal. Revision is a substrate operation that produces witnesses; silent revision is forbidden (a revision-without-witness leaves the substrate in an inconsistent state).

The "reconsideration requires reasons" principle is encoded by the closure rule that any revision-witness must cite a *justification cell* $\langle \texttt{revision-witness}, \texttt{justified-by}, J\rangle = \mathbf{t}$.

## Normative layer: duty cells across the plan

Per [normative-operational-layering](../concepts/normative-operational-layering.md), each task dispatch within the plan generates a duty cell on the assigned agent (O→N rule). The plan carries a *normative profile* as well as an operational one: the set of duty cells generated by its dispatches. This matters at two points:

**During the plan's potential phase.** The duty cells on each agent $A_i$ who has been dispatched a task $T_i \in T_P$ constitute $A_i$'s obligation to complete $T_i$. If $T_i$ is suspended, the duty is not discharged but is also not violated (the suspension-epoch cell gates the violation-detection rule). If $T_i$ is cancelled, a duty-release rule fires: $\langle R^{\mathrm{duty}}_i, \texttt{released-by-cancellation}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, which counts as duty discharge for that task.

**At plan completion.** By the [plan-completion-as-meet](../../documents/articles/notes/undated/plan-completion-as-meet.md) normative-residue corollary, operational plan completion does not automatically discharge all duties. The plan's *normative residue* at completion is the set of duty cells that remain settled at $\mathbf{t}$ without a corresponding duty-discharge record. A plan's normative audit at completion checks this residue set; empty residue means the plan is normatively clean.

The plan's $\mathcal{S}_P$ may include an optional *normative-cleanup milestone*: a cell $\langle P, \texttt{normatively-clean-at}, \tau^*\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ that the plan-completion condition requires alongside the operational milestone cells. When this cell is in $M_P$, operational and normative completeness are *jointly required* for plan completion — the plan is complete only when all duties are discharged. When it is not in $M_P$, normative residue is tracked but not blocking.

## Plan hierarchy (goal → project → plan → task)

A *project* is a plan-of-plans: a plan $P_{\mathrm{project}}$ whose tasks $T_i$ are themselves plans rather than atomic task cell systems. The dependency order, milestone cells, and coordination rules generalize. The hierarchy continues upward: *campaign* contains projects; *goal* is the top-level milestone-set.

## Plan vs. recurrent practice

A plan and a [recurrent practice](../../../../../sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/recurrent-practice.md) are distinct: a plan has a single completion (its milestones jointly settle once); a recurrent practice has periodic firing without single completion. A plan with periodic re-execution (e.g., "run this plan every quarter") is a recurrent practice whose per-cycle content is the plan's execution.

## Regime

By the [regime-of-task theorem](../../../../../sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/content/glossary/concepts/regime-of-task.md) applied to the plan-level closure-rule subsystem $\mathcal{S}_P$: a plan is in the strict regime if all $\mathcal{S}_{T_i}$ are strict and the dependency-ordering and milestone-aggregation rules are finitary. A plan that includes inquiry-typed tasks (whose subtask-set is itself dynamic) can lift to higher regimes.

## CALM and coordination-free active-frontier queries

The gate cells that control task activation — $\langle T_j, \texttt{depends-on-completed}, T_i\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ — are $\leq_k$-monotone once settled. By the [predication-settling CALM corollary](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/formalization/infomaterial/lib/infomaterial/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/references/settling-calm-corollary.md):

- Confirming "is $T_i$'s dependency gate open?" is coordination-free: any replica can locally verify that $T_i$ is unblocked.
- Confirming "is $T_i$ still blocked?" requires coordination — it requires establishing the absence of a gate-settlement increment.

The plan's *potential-frontier* query — "which tasks are currently available for dispatch?" — is a $\leq_k$-monotone positive query: once a task enters the frontier (all its predecessor-gate cells settled at $\mathbf{t}$), it remains there until completed. Frontier membership is coordination-free to confirm. Tasks *exiting* the frontier (completion or abandonment) are anti-monotone and require coordination to confirm.

For distributed work systems: each worker node can independently read the gate cells and confirm which tasks are available for dispatch. The global plan's frontier is computed locally without central coordination. This is the CALM-stable foundation for parallel task dispatch in distributed operations.

## Temporal 2-category of plan execution

In the [temporal 2-category](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/temporal-2-category/temporal-2-category.md) $\mathbf{T}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}, \lambda)$, each task completion event is a 1-cell mapping the stage just before completion to the stage at which the milestone settles:

$$\Sigma_{T_i} : (\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}) \to (\tau_{T_i^*}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}})$$

where $\tau_{T_i^*}$ is the [completion-as-quiescence](../../../../../sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/content/glossary/concepts/completion-as-quiescence.md) stage for $T_i$. The plan's execution is the composite of these 1-cells, ordered by $\preceq_P$: completing a task is a 1-cell; the plan execution is the composite 1-cell from the plan's initialization stage to the final milestone's quiescence stage.

For parallel tasks in the plan (tasks with no order relation between them): their 1-cells are *parallel branches* in $\mathbf{T}$ — the temporal 2-category's pseudo-pullback of two 1-cells from a common 0-cell. The [temporal Beck-Chevalley condition](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/temporal-2-category/references/temporal-beck-chevalley.md) holds for these parallel branches iff the parallel tasks' settling fibers commute — meaning the order in which parallel tasks complete does not affect the plan's final milestone settling. This is the temporal-2-category formalization of "parallel tasks are independent": BC holds iff the tasks genuinely don't interfere.

When BC fails for a nominally-parallel task pair: the two tasks' settling fibers don't commute, meaning one task's completion changes the settling context for the other. This is the substrate-algebraic content of "hidden dependency": a dependency that the plan's explicit $\preceq_P$ did not record but that the substrate detects via failed temporal BC.

## Position in the dispatchable graph

The [dispatch morphism](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/social-sciences/working/morphisms/dispatch.md) operates on individual tasks. A plan's dispatch is the dispatch of its currently-active tasks — those whose dependencies have settled but whose goals have not. The plan's *potential-frontier* at stage $\tau$ is the set $\{T_i \in T_P : \text{dependencies settled, goal not yet settled}\}$. Dispatch consumes from this frontier.
