# Is liberal subjectivity a malfunction of the stability reward?

The source question is not merely whether liberal subjectivity can be described using information-theoretic language. The source question is whether liberal subjectivity is a specific malfunction of the information-theoretic stability reward: a system that receives reward for remaining coherent with itself while ceasing to adapt to the world it claims to know.

## The question

If the stability reward of an adapting system is a reward of coupling between that system and a reference system, is liberal subjectivity what happens when the reference system is replaced by the subject's own prior belief-state?

Equivalently: is liberal subjectivity a degenerate self-coupled optimization loop that mistakes low self-divergence for successful adaptation?

The source triage material describes liberal subjectivity as a feedback architecture whose reward function privileges internal consistency over adaptive correspondence. The predicated-sociology version should ask whether that diagnosis can be stated as a property of a social predication settling: a subject-position whose closure rules reward coherence inside the subject's belief fiber while leaving divergence from the wider social and material environment unpenalized.

## Predication restatement

In predicated sociology, a social subject is not a private mental substance. It is a stabilized configuration in a social settling: a bundle of predicates, values, roles, affects, permissions, prohibitions, and recognitions that closure rules keep reproducible.

On that substrate, the liberal subjectivity question becomes:

1. What is the entity set for the liberal subject?
2. What predicates make the subject recognizable as liberal?
3. What closure rules reward internal coherence among those predicates?
4. What external predicates should act as the reference system?
5. What happens when those external predicates no longer constrain the subject's settling?

A healthy stability reward should bind the subject's local settling to a reference outside the subject. A malfunctioning reward would bind the subject's next state mainly to its own previous state. The result would be epistemic overclosure: the subject remains stable by suppressing information that would force costly resettling.

## Answer-enabling questions

What counts as the reference system for a social subject? Candidate answers include the material environment, the dominated other, the social field, the institution, the colony, the market, the polity, or a structured bundle of these. The answer matters because a reward function is only meaningful relative to what it rewards coupling with.

What distinguishes stabilization from overclosure? A subject must have some stability to persist at all. The question is not whether stability is bad. The question is when closure rules stabilize the subject by preventing relevant contradiction from entering the settling.

Can ontological drift be measured predicatively? The source material names drift as the condition in which the subject's internal divergence remains low while divergence from the environment increases. In predication terms, this would require a way to compare a local subject-settling with the wider social/environmental settling it claims to track.

Does liberal subjectivity specifically produce this malfunction, or is it one instance of a broader class of subject-formation pathologies? If it is broader, liberal subjectivity may be the historically specific case where self-coherence, property, rights-bearing individuality, and procedural legitimacy install the pathological reference system.

## What would resolution require

A resolution would require a formal example of a social subject-position whose closure rules stabilize self-coherence while allowing divergence from an external reference to increase. The example should specify the relevant predication cells, the subject-forming closure rules, the external reference predicates, and the diagnostic by which divergence is observed.

A stronger resolution would prove a general condition: if a subject-position's closure operator rewards local self-coherence while failing to penalize loss of mutual information with its reference environment, then the settling enters pathological overclosure.

A sociological resolution would then show that liberal subjectivity satisfies those conditions, rather than merely resembling them.

## What this question should not collapse into

This question should not be reduced to "can liberal subjectivity be modeled as overclosure?" That is an answer-enabling technical question. The real question remains sharper: is liberal subjectivity a malfunction of the stability reward itself?

Nor should the question be answered by saying that all subjectivity is constructed. Predicated sociology already assumes subject-formation. The problem here is whether a particular subject-form is a pathological controller: stable because it is miscoupled.
