# Symbolic violence is the resolution of $\top$ toward $\mathbf{t}$ by authority suppression

The [symbolic violence morphism](../morphisms/symbolic-violence.md) is precisely characterized as the closure operation that resolves $\top$ states in capital and positional predicate cells toward $\mathbf{t}$ by suppressing the contradicting evidence — where the suppression is performed by an entity with settled classification authority rather than by adjudication of the competing evidence.

This theorem establishes that symbolic violence is a *structurally necessary* feature of any social settling in which:
1. Capital and positional cells enter $\top$ states (as they inevitably do in contested social formations), and
2. The social settling's repair system does not adjudicate evidence (examine both witnesses and determine which is valid) but instead resolves by authority (settles toward the position supported by the entity with classification authority in the relevant field).

## Statement

Let $\phi = \langle e, p_{\mathrm{cap}}, c\rangle$ be a capital or positional cell that has entered $\top$ — contradicted by witnesses $w^+$ (positive: $e$ holds capital/position) and $w^-$ (negative: $e$ does not). Let $I \in E_{\mathrm{soc}}$ be an entity with $v(\langle I, \texttt{has-classification-authority-in}, \mathrm{field}\rangle) = \mathbf{t}$.

**Theorem.** If the social settling's repair rule for $\top$ states is:

$$v(\phi) = \top \;\wedge\; v(\langle I, \texttt{has-classification-authority-in}, \mathrm{field}\rangle) = \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; v(\phi) \;\text{resolves to}\; v(\langle I, \texttt{classifies}, \phi, I\text{'s-verdict}\rangle)$$

— i.e., the resolution is determined by the classifying institution's verdict rather than by adjudication of $w^+$ and $w^-$ — then:

(a) The repair is *systematically biased* toward the dominant agent's witnesses when $I$ is positionally dominant (when $I$'s positional cells are settled at $\mathbf{t}$ on dominance predicates in the field).

(b) The suppressed witness $w^-$ (which produced the $\top$ state) is not refuted — it remains in the witnessing record — but is operationally inactive: no further closure rules in $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{soc}}$ fire from $w^-$ once $\phi$ has been re-settled to $\mathbf{t}$ by the institutional verdict.

(c) Dominated agents with [habitus-closure](../objects/habitus-closure.md) rules that recognize $I$'s classification authority fire the repair rule themselves, without requiring any act by $I$: they participate in the suppression of their own contradicting witnesses.

## Sketch

**(a) Systematic bias.** The repair rule fires $I$'s verdict on the contested cell $\phi$. When $I$ is positionally dominant in the field — when $I$'s field-authority cell is at $\mathbf{t}$ — $I$'s verdict favors $I$'s class position. By the standard sociological analysis of classification authority (Bourdieu: the power to name is the power to consecrate — to make legitimate what one names), $I$'s verdict on $\phi$ reflects $I$'s structural interest in maintaining the arrangement that $\phi$'s $\top$ state threatened. Hence the verdict resolves $\phi$ to $\mathbf{t}$ (the dominant arrangement is re-legitimate) rather than $\mathbf{f}$ (the arrangement is overturned). $\square$ (a).

**(b) Operational suppression without refutation.** Once $\phi$ is re-settled to $\mathbf{t}$ by $I$'s verdict, the social settling's closure rules treat $\phi = \mathbf{t}$ as the operative antecedent for all downstream rules. The negative witness $w^-$ remains in $\mathrm{Hist}_e$ (the evidential history) — it is not erased — but the closure rules do not fire from $w^-$ once the settling has moved on. The contradicting evidence is operationally inert: not refuted, but functionally absent. This is the substrate-algebraic meaning of *misrecognition*: the contradicting evidence was there; it is not acknowledged. $\square$ (b).

**(c) Self-participation of the dominated.** An agent $e'$ with habitus-closure $\mathcal{H}_{e'}$ that includes the rule `recognize $I$'s classification authority` fires the repair rule themselves:
$$v(\phi) = \top \;\wedge\; v(\langle e', \texttt{recognizes-authority-of}, I\rangle) = \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{$e'$ fires the repair rule toward $I$'s verdict}$$

If $e'$ is dominated by the same arrangement that $\phi$'s $\top$ state threatened, then $e'$'s own habitus-encoded recognition of $I$'s authority causes $e'$ to participate in resolving the $\top$ state against $e'$'s own interest. This is the substrate-algebraic content of the violence of symbolic violence: no external force is required. The dominated carry the repair rules inside their own closure structure. $\square$ (c).

## Corollary: symbolic violence is invisible at the level of individual closure-rule firing

Each individual closure-rule firing in the symbolic violence morphism appears locally legitimate: $I$ is recognized as the classification authority, $I$'s verdict is applied, the $\top$ state is resolved. No rule fires that says "suppress the dominated agent's evidence." The suppression emerges from the *structure* of the repair mechanism (resolve by authority, not by adjudication), not from any individual rule's explicit content.

This invisibility is the substrate-algebraic reason symbolic violence is difficult to contest: there is no single rule to point to and refuse. Contesting symbolic violence requires contesting the meta-rule that defines how $\top$ states are repaired — contesting the repair mechanism itself, not any particular application of it. This is a high-$D$ contestation (the repair-mechanism cells are antecedents for many downstream rules), and by [contestation-as-contradiction](contestation-as-contradiction.md), high-$D$ contestations require the highest sustained $\top$-introduction rate relative to repair capacity to succeed.
