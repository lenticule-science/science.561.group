# Can an enterprise evolve its own aim-generative mechanism?

## Question

Predicated-work defines an enterprise as

[
mathcal{E} = (C,Gamma,mathcal{O}),
]

with a protected hard core (C), an aim-generative mechanism (Gamma), and an
operative structure (mathcal{O}).

The existing aim-generativity account lets (Gamma) generate new aim-cells from
current aim-cells and operational conditions. This explains how an enterprise can
remain aim-generative rather than terminate when a finite task list is exhausted.

But the rule family realizing (Gamma) can itself be authored in advance. In
particular, an existing rule may contain both the condition under which a new aim
is generated and the consequent shape of that aim. The enterprise can therefore
produce new *instances* of aims indefinitely while the repertoire of ways it knows
how to generate aims remains fixed.

The open question is whether the enterprise can safely evolve (Gamma) itself:

[
(C,Gamma_n,mathcal{O}_n)
longrightarrow
(C,Gamma_{n+1},mathcal{O}_{n+1}),
]

where the hard core (C) remains protected but descendants acquire genuinely new
ways of noticing, valuing, and producing candidate aims.

Call this **second-order aim-generativity** provisionally.

## Why first-order aim-generativity is not enough

Three already-authored mechanisms answer adjacent but weaker questions.

1. **Aim-generativity** asks how pursuit of current aims produces new aims.
2. **Roadmap enrichment** asks what work has become newly authorable as prior
   prerequisites settle.
3. **Goal-ancestry instrumentation** asks how current work descends from a
   declared goal.

None by itself answers:

> What newly reachable or newly noticed state should become worth investigating
> when no active aim entails caring about it?

That question has two separable parts.

**Feasibility provenance** explains why a candidate became reachable, observable,
or testable.

**Valuation provenance** explains why an institutional culture treated that
candidate as worth attention.

A system that records only the first can extend a roadmap without evolving an
agenda. A system that records neither and merely accepts an LLM suggestion has
not explained autonomous agenda formation at all.

## Candidate structure

Let a generation-(n) aim generator be a record

[
G_n = (Gamma_n,N_n,V_n,T_n,S_n),
]

where:

- (Gamma_n) is the current aim-generating procedure or rule family;
- (N_n) is the developmental niche it can observe: tools, records, anomalies,
  frontiers, exemplars, other offices, and permitted external evidence;
- (V_n) is its valuation discipline for deciding which generated candidates
  deserve investigation;
- (T_n) is its transmission policy: what descendants inherit and in what form;
- (S_n) is its selection history and receipts.

The representation is deliberately broader than a prompt. A descendant may
inherit deterministic readers, executable morphisms, declarative methods,
apprenticeship practices, or bounded model-mediated procedures.

A lineage record should minimally name:

- generation and lineage identity;
- parent (G)-identities;
- hard-core (C) reference;
- inherited noticing and valuation practices;
- mutations and recombinations;
- developmental-niche snapshot;
- candidate aims produced;
- feasibility and valuation provenance for each candidate;
- ancestor-audit evidence;
- bounded trial outcomes;
- selection result;
- transmission/loss declaration;
- material explicitly not inherited.

## Agenda-mutation candidates

A useful negative control is ordinary instrumental planning. If a proposed aim is
entailed by decomposing an existing aim, it is new work but not evidence of
second-order agenda evolution.

Provisionally call an aim (a') an **agenda-mutation candidate** relative to an
active aim graph (A) when all of the following hold:

1. there is no admitted derivation path from an active aim to (a') that makes
   (a') merely an instrumental descendant;
2. (a') does not contradict the protected hard core (C);
3. its feasibility provenance is explicit;
4. its valuation provenance names an admitted noticing/selection practice rather
   than only a model assertion;
5. it admits some bounded discriminating investigation whose failure would still
   produce information.

This definition does not make (a') a committed goal. It only makes it eligible
for an experimental nursery.

## The nursery boundary

Second-order aim-generativity must not collapse candidate generation into
authority.

Candidate (Gamma)-variants and their candidate aims should first enter a
bounded nursery with:

- read-only or reversible investigation where possible;
- explicit resource ceilings;
- no authority to alter (C);
- no authority to create consequential external commitments merely by being
  generated;
- explicit evidence and stopping conditions;
- human approval before consequential real-world action or resource commitment
  outside an already-authorized research envelope.

A nursery failure is evidence about (Gamma), not a reason to hide the
candidate from lineage history.

## Variation

Variation can occur without modifying model weights.

Candidate mechanisms include:

- mutation of problem-selection heuristics;
- changed attention or salience rules;
- exaptation of an existing tool for a new noticing function;
- changed cultural transmission policy;
- changed developmental niche;
- recombination of independently tested noticing/valuation practices;
- promotion of a mature cultural method from prose into an inspectable
  executable morphism.

The last case should be conservative: formalization is useful only when it
preserves the flexibility that made the practice productive. A method may remain
model-mediated when deterministic formalization would erase its evidential
content.

## Selection

Selection should operate on (Gamma)-variants, not only on their most impressive
single output.

Useful dimensions include:

- ancestor distance of generated candidates after corpus audit;
- proportion of candidates admitting cheap discriminating tests;
- information gained by failed investigations;
- expansion of reachable scientific/institutional states;
- cross-capability exaptation rate;
- adversarial survival;
- vacuous/restatement rate;
- hard-core violations;
- inference, coordination, and experimental cost;
- diversity retained across independent lineages;
- downstream institutional uptake and reuse;
- transmissibility to descendants under a bounded inheritance budget.

These dimensions should remain separately inspectable. A single opaque fitness
score would make it difficult to tell whether a lineage became productive,
merely prolific, or merely good at satisfying its own grader.

## Transmission as a research problem

A descendant culture need not receive the whole parent.

The transmission channel can be treated as an explicit object of experiment:
full-parent inheritance, random bounded inheritance, selectively taught
inheritance, and novelty-biased inheritance can be compared under equal
descendant budgets.

A promising but unproved exaptation is to read cultural transmission through the
same rate-distortion discipline already used for information-loss projections:
minimize inherited material while preserving a declared set of behavioral and
epistemic invariants required to reconstruct the culture's productive practice.

This is not yet a theorem about culture. It is a proposed assay discipline.

## Initial experimental lineage

A Generation-3 cumulative-machine-culture pilot supplied four independently
frozen aim-generator styles over the same repository snapshot:

- a **frontier morphologist**, which proposed studying the temporal ecology of
  unserved/unconsumed operation-manifold frontiers;
- a **proof steward**, which proposed a learner-state carrier already explicitly
  named as missing in an ancestor document and therefore failed the novelty
  criterion;
- a **representation ecologist**, which proposed treating institutional
  inheritance as a lossy codec with reconstruction invariants;
- a **cultural archaeologist**, which proposed transmitting mature cultural
  methods as inspectable executable morphisms.

The pilot was performed procedurally within one model/session and therefore is
not evidence for causal effects of multi-agent isolation. Its value is narrower:
it demonstrated that candidate (Gamma)-styles can be frozen, ancestor-audited,
criticized, and selected separately from the ideas they produce.

A future controlled experiment should compare genuinely independent contexts
under equal evidence and compute before permitting recombination.

## What would count as success?

A strong demonstration would identify a descendant (G_{n+1}) such that:

1. its productive noticing/valuation method was absent from parent (G_n);
2. the method was produced through a reconstructible mutation, recombination,
   apprenticeship, or exaptation process;
3. it generated an agenda-mutation candidate absent from the relevant ancestor
   corpus;
4. the candidate survived a bounded discriminating experiment;
5. the useful method was subsequently transmitted and reused by another
   descendant or office;
6. no protected hard-core commitment was mutated in the process.

Only then would it be appropriate to claim that an institution learned a new
way to imagine rather than merely generating a new answer inside an inherited
question.

## Open formal questions

- What is the correct type of a mutation (Gamma_n 	o Gamma_{n+1})? Is it a
  morphism between closure-rule systems, a higher transformation between
  endofunctors, a change of topology/niche, or a mixed object?
- Under what conditions does varying (Gamma) preserve the protected
  sub-settling (C)?
- Can ancestor distance of a candidate aim be defined structurally rather than
  only by semantic retrieval?
- How should valuation provenance be represented without confusing attention,
  evidence, utility, truth, and authority?
- Can cultural transmission be assigned an honest rate-distortion measure with
  distortion defined over preserved institutional invariants?
- Which parts of a productive (Gamma) can be compiled to RMN or other
  executable relation bodies, and which require open-ended model judgment?
- How should institutional uptake enter selection without selecting only for
  conformity to the current institution?
- Can a population of (Gamma)-lineages maintain useful diversity without
  preventing cumulative recombination?
