# Work visibility, perceived usefulness, and institutional decision deferral

## A construct-discrimination and causal-identification framework

**Lenticule Science — Social Theory Science Services Section. Working research article, October 12, 2026.**

## Abstract

The terms *meaningless labor*, *invisible work*, *bullshit jobs*, *make-work*, and *bureaucratic delay* often enter the same critique of institutions. They do not identify the same phenomenon. Work may be invisible to formal accounting while essential to service provision; a worker may perceive a socially useful job as meaningless because of managerial alienation; a formally necessary process may fail to advance its stated outcome; and organizations may maintain activities whose reporting value exceeds their contribution to decisions. This working article distinguishes four constructs—unrecognized necessary work, perceived work uselessness, counterfactually low-contribution tasks, and institutional decision deferral—and specifies observable conditions under which they can be tested without moralizing the worker or presuming managerial deceit. We synthesize existing work-sociology surveys within Lenticule Science with Star and Strauss on visibility, Soffia, Wood and Burchell's empirical challenge to Graeber's bullshit-jobs theory, Cohen–March–Olsen's organizational ambiguity, Meyer and Rowan's decoupling, Brunsson's inconsistent organizational talk and action, and Power on audit. We define decision-centered process traces, present a conservative mathematical counterfactual model, and propose a stepped-wedge operational study measuring decision closure, source-quality improvement, hidden coordination cost and distributional consequences. **No original dataset was collected and no statistical result or causal effect is claimed.** The contribution is a set of disjoint *questions*, not a claim that all institutional complexity is deliberate obfuscation.

**Keywords:** sociology of work; invisible labor; alienation; organizational legitimacy; administrative discretion; ambiguity; decision delay; research operations; causal inference; institutional decoupling.

## 1. Problem: a shared title can obscure different scientific objects

Consider four observations.

1. A ward nurse spends time aligning medication changes, shift notes and family communications. The hospital reports only procedures performed. The nurse's coordination time is insufficiently credited. Removing that coordination may degrade clinical outcomes, even though administrative measures did not count it.
2. A worker states that their job has little value to society. That experience is evidence about the worker's appraisal, not by itself evidence that eliminating their work would leave all beneficiaries unharmed.
3. An institution produces a compliance report that creates a legitimate audit trail but does not alter the policy decision it was supposedly intended to guide. The report might still be necessary for other purposes, such as accountability, fairness or litigation.
4. A decision remains open while its sponsor repeatedly commissions reviews of the same issue without resolving disputed criteria or changing available evidence. The reviewing work may conserve legitimacy, displace accountability, reduce uncertainty or merely reveal a genuinely complex problem. Which explanation is true requires examination.

Calling all four "meaningless labor" silently moves from a claim about *measurement and recognition*, to a claim about *felt purpose*, to a counterfactual claim about *outcome contribution*, and finally to a claim about *causal organizational strategy*. Those moves are not licensed by the title alone.

This article treats *Meaningless Labor and Confusion as Crisis Deferral* as the **name of a research problem family**. An older authored prose text of similar title survives outside Lenticule Science and remains a different artifact. This research does not claim that the original prose is a peer-reviewed study, does not rewrite it into scientific diction, and does not assign it a newsletter publication state.

- [Immaterial Labor and Post-Operaismo: A Survey](../../05/03/2026-05-03-immaterial-labor-and-post-operaismo-survey.md), on affective/communicative work and value capture.
- [Activity Theory and CHAT: A Survey](../../05/03/2026-05-03-activity-theory-survey.md), on mediated action, contradictions and expansive learning.
- [Classical Organization Theory: A Survey](../../05/03/2026-05-03-classical-organization-theory-survey.md), on planning, coordination, bounded rationality and formal structures.

We cross-check the central *external* sources against their journal, publisher or university records. This is a **targeted critical narrative synthesis** selected to discriminate constructs that are commonly conflated. It is **not** a systematic review: no full database strategy, preregistered inclusion criteria, citation network census or source-quality score has been executed. Consequently the list cannot be used to estimate field consensus or empirical prevalence.

The sources answer different questions. Star and Strauss (1999) study the shifting visibility of work under selected indicators; Soffia, Wood and Burchell (2022) use representative European worker survey data to test five of Graeber's propositions; Cohen, March and Olsen (1972) model choice with problematic preferences, uncertain technology and fluid participation; Meyer and Rowan (1977) examine ceremonial formal structures and decoupling; Brunsson (1989) explains how organizational talk, decisions and action can diverge; Power (1997) studies the spread and limitations of audit practices. Treating their theories as a single "proof" would erase both unit of analysis and evidence class.

## 3. Literature: important empirical and theoretical limits

### 3.1. Work visibility is relational, not evidence of uselessness

Star and Strauss, *Layers of Silence, Arenas of Voice: The Ecology of Visible and Invisible Work* (*CSCW*, 1999), explicitly deny that a task is intrinsically visible or invisible. Visibility depends on indicators and whose perspective is being measured: finished objects, bodily exertion, record fields, or a change in a state of affairs. A hospital coordination shift might be invisible in an output dashboard but legible to workers who depend on it. Reclassifying invisible work as "unnecessary" would commit a category error.

The articulation-work tradition (Strauss 1985, 1988; Schmidt and Bannon 1992) adds that negotiation, repair and coordination are first-order labor needed to keep collective trajectories aligned. Feminist studies of care, domestic work and reproductive labor similarly demonstrate how work can sustain visible production while remaining unpriced or excluded from employment measures. The correct empirical question is **whose indicators omitted which dependency**, not how many workers can be deleted from an org chart.

### 3.2. Perceived uselessness is not a stable industry-wide fact

Graeber (*Bullshit Jobs*, 2018) advanced a provocative account of people undertaking paid work that they regard as pointless. Soffia, Wood and Burchell, *Alienation Is Not 'Bullshit': An Empirical Critique of Graeber's Theory of BS Jobs* (*Work, Employment and Society*, 2022; first published online in 2021), tested five related predictions with representative European data. Their abstract reports that self-reported useless work was **low and declining**, contrary to Graeber's proposed prevalence trajectory, while perceptions of uselessness were strongly associated with poor wellbeing and with factors consistent with labor alienation or work relations.

This evidence does not prove that unnecessary tasks are nonexistent or that every worker's assessment is mistaken. It does show why a research program cannot simply treat Graeber's prevalence claim as established fact. Surveyed meaning, measured organizational contribution and normative social value require separate variables. An occupational label cannot stand in for a causal audit of work.

### 3.3. Ambiguity need not be deliberately manufactured

Cohen, March and Olsen's *A Garbage Can Model of Organizational Choice* (1972) models "organized anarchies" in which preferences are problematic, technologies unclear and participants fluctuate. Problems, solutions, participants and choice occasions can couple contingently, yielding uneven decisions without any actor choosing to "manufacture confusion." Complexity, distributed knowledge and changing participation can generate precisely the pattern a critic might interpret as delay tactics.

For the present research, their model supplies an essential **rival explanation**. A file of repeated meetings with no final decision is not yet evidence of strategic deferral. The analyst must ask whether decision rules were knowable, who held information, whether the same question recurred, whether legitimate uncertainty was reduced, and when a veto or legal obligation prevented closure.

### 3.4. The official diagram can differ from working practice

Meyer and Rowan (1977) argue that rationalized institutional structures may provide external legitimacy and survival independent of technical production, with formal structures decoupled from actual activities. The work of producing visible procedural conformity may be valuable to an organization's relation with a regulator or funder even where its immediate technical effect is small.

Brunsson's *The Organization of Hypocrisy* (1989) differentiates organizational talk, decisions and actions, and shows how apparent inconsistency can be functional in conflicting institutional environments. Neither theory proves every audit or policy process fraudulent: the existence of multiple audiences and conflicting legitimate constraints can generate divergence even among sincere actors.

Power's *The Audit Society* (1997) similarly analyzes how accountability demands expand what must be recorded and checked, while emphasizing the limitations and possible dysfunction of audit. An audit can expose real harm; it can also consume staff effort or privilege easily inspected indicators. These possibilities require comparative measurement.

### 3.5. Production and control are not coextensive

Labor-process theorists distinguish substantive work from organizational control of work. Platform-work researchers distinguish who performs service, who allocates tasks, who records that service, and who appropriates the informational benefits. The resulting framework does not presume that market-facing profit, worker satisfaction, socially necessary care and bureaucratic compliance all share one scalar measure of value. They often conflict. A measurement program must name the value-bearing constituencies and the legal/ethical constraints **before** labeling tasks unnecessary.

## 4. Four distinct constructs

Let \(i\) index an organizational task, \(u\) an affected constituency and \(t\) the relevant observation period. For each task collect: authorization \(A_i\), stated purpose \(P_i\), actual activity \(W_i\), available evidence \(E_i\), affected constituencies \(U_i\), and proposed outcome set \(Y_i\). These are **measurement coordinates**, not a novel universal data type or implementation schema; the operating provider should use its existing typed resources.

**C1 — unrecognized necessary work.** The task has a plausible causal contribution to at least one legitimate outcome, but selected management indicators fail to represent part of its time, skill, or costs. Test using dependency traces, staff-reported coordination, and changes after controlled process alterations. It is logically possible for C1 and C3 to coexist; the categories are analytical dimensions, not a four-way partition of all workers.

**C2 — perceived low usefulness.** A worker, beneficiary or evaluator reports that the task lacks purpose or value. Record who is evaluating, with what scale and knowledge, at which time and relative to which counterfactual. Interobserver disagreement is substantive data. C2 is a reported perception and may coexist with substantial measurable contributions to care or safety.

**C3 — low measured marginal contribution.** For a predeclared constituency and outcome set, the removal or reduction of one task under a defensible intervention has no clinically/operationally meaningful deterioration within the defined time horizon, after considering substitute effort, tail risks and displaced labor. A null difference in one output indicator **does not** establish zero social value. Adequate power and bounds on harm are prerequisites to any strong claim of dispensability.

**C4 — decision deferral through work.** A pending consequential choice is accompanied by a sequence of review or process tasks that consume resources while leaving relevant uncertainty, authority or the decision criterion materially unchanged. This is a process-level **pattern**. Calling it "strategic deferral" additionally requires evidence that relevant actors had feasible options to reduce ambiguity/act, and that their choices predictably preserved non-resolution. Without that evidence, report "decision delay" or "unresolved with repeated process activity," not presumed deception.

C4 must not be equated with C3: an extra safety study may be necessary even when a decision is delayed. Nor does C2 imply C3: people can be alienated by socially necessary jobs. C1 may actually prevent C4 by providing uncatalogued coordination that makes a difficult decision possible.

## 5. The central causal problem

For a given decision \(d\), let \(T_d\) be elapsed time from a **declared, eligible** decision opportunity to a terminal recorded judgment (accepted, rejected, withdrawn, or superseded) under a documented protocol. Let \(Z_d\) represent unresolved ambiguity at intake, \(X_d\) exogenous complexity, \(R_d\) available staffing and institutional discretion, \(K_d\) legitimate evidence needs, and \(M_d\) process/reporting activity before closure.

Naively observing \(M_d\) rising with \(T_d\) cannot identify "confusion manufactures work" or "work defers crisis." Hard cases have longer delays *and* more reviews; evidence gathering may be legitimately required; unobserved priorities influence both. Further, "closure" is not necessarily beneficial: an unlawful, unsafe or coercive decision can be closed quickly.

A conceptually meaningful question is:

\[
\Delta(\tau)
 = \Pr(T_d\le\tau\mid \operatorname{do}(C_d=1))
 - \Pr(T_d\le\tau\mid \operatorname{do}(C_d=0)),
\]

where \(C_d\) is a **bounded clarity intervention** such as predeclaring the responsible decision-maker, the acceptance threshold, the required source evidence, legitimate vetoes, and the procedure for handling a contradiction. This equation defines a target estimand; it is **not** an estimated quantity. It must be stratified by decision class and constrained so that quicker decisions obtained by ignoring safety or legal requirements count as *harm*, not success.

For an empirical answer, both intervention arms must track follow-on rework, reversal, distribution of risk, stakeholder challenges and outcomes beyond the initial timestamp. A local increase in timely decisions at the expense of invisible labor or serious downstream harm is not evidence of net institutional improvement.

### 5.1. Process-event trace and evidential gates

Represent the history of a decision using already available organizational event and evidence resources. Minimum observable events include *issue raised*, *authority identified*, *criteria published*, *research commissioned*, *source received*, *claim admitted or rejected*, *decision made*, *decision communicated*, *action carried out*, *challenge*, and *revision or rollback*.

For each research/review task attached to an open decision, ask whether it:
1. introduced genuinely new source evidence;
2. changed the value of a declared decision criterion;
3. resolved or clarified which actor held decision rights;
4. discovered a new applicable obligation or risk;
5. consumed labor without any of the preceding—but perhaps contributed to another outcome.

A repeated review failing tests 1–4 is not automatically pointless. It may create a legitimate public account or procedural due process. The record must preserve test 5's possible alternate outcomes rather than assume fraud from an empty criterion-change field.

### 5.2. Deferral's possible institutional beneficiaries

The stronger "crisis deferral" mechanism predicts that prolonging ambiguity can protect particular agents or institutional positions from imminent choices, sanctions, funding shifts or resource reallocations. To test that, specify the affected actors **before looking for villains**: sponsor, workers, clients, affected communities, oversight, legal counsel and funders. Record any distributive effect, not simply "the institution benefited." It is possible that deferral protects vulnerable persons from a harmful rushed decision; any model unable to register that is politically and scientifically malformed.

An observational correlation between oversight requests and delay can reflect reverse causality, missingness or crisis severity rather than intentional strategy. The classification "strategic" needs evidence of knowledge, feasible alternatives, incentives and actual choices—for example, internal decision records showing a reason to avoid resolution combined with an identifiable change in rules after relevant evidence was already available. Treat such evidence as sensitive and subject to authorized access and ethical protection.

## 6. Empirical strategy: test the distinction, do not assume the result

### 6.1. Sampling and case construction

A pilot should choose an organization willing to retain lawful administrative process records, perhaps an internal non-sensitive service with no adverse decisions affecting third parties. Specify an observation window and a cohort of **eligible decisions**, not a sample of the most spectacular delayed cases. A useful cohort could comprise onboarding approvals, service quality remediations, non-sensitive procurement reviews, or internal method amendments.

Eligibility must be established before outcome; sampling only delayed items induces selection bias. Record priority, estimated legal risk, staff capacity, task class, known adverse dependencies, decision-maker and dates. A new topic reopened after a justified change of circumstances should not be coded as failure simply because its title resembles an old ticket.

### 6.2. Intervention

Use a stepped-wedge or parallel random allocation of an **in-process clarity service**. The intervention should (a) identify who can decide; (b) make acceptance and rejection criteria legible; (c) distinguish evidence needs from editorial or political preferences; (d) state the difference between *unknown*, *disputed*, *unsupported* and *out of scope*; and (e) present the available lawful options including deferral with a date-specific reason.

The control arm uses existing lawful practice. Never deprive a control group of safety, legal review or baseline worker rights. Include contamination and learning effects in the analysis; do not claim randomization is possible in every real organizational context.

### 6.3. Outcomes and analysis

Predeclare a primary endpoint such as **time to valid decision with no unresolved mandatory checks**, plus secondary measures: time to execution and independently received outcome; proportion of reopenings/reversals; total hours of visible and hidden coordination; worker-perceived usefulness (separately elicited); source adequacy; administrative burden; beneficiary effects; procedural fairness; externalities.

Fit a predeclared event-time or competing-risk model respecting right-censoring and decision states. Report survival curves, effect sizes, intervals and subgroup estimates, not only a favorable pooled p-value. With cluster or stepped-wedge allocation, adjust for site/time and within-cluster dependence. Treat changes in definitions of "valid" as protocol amendments, not retrospective tuning.

A particularly revealing negative case would be a site where publishing clear criteria **increases** justified duration because hidden legal or historical obligations become visible. That result could count as a genuine improvement in knowledge and legitimacy even if the quick-decision metric worsens. Another falsifier for deliberate make-work is evidence that the majority of repeated studies closed genuine unknowns or prevented serious harm.

### 6.4. Distributional and rights analysis

Record whose labor changes under the intervention, including unpaid care and undocumented coordination; whether marginalized workers lose recognition when "wasted work" is cut; whose testimony is discounted; whether decision rights move without recourse; and whether any public accountability obligations are newly bypassed. Estimate net cost including shifted burden. Institutional "efficiency" must not be defined as the reduction of work that is simply pushed onto households, recipients, contractors or minority communities.

## 7. A deliberately small formal model

The paper's claim about deferral can be pressure-tested with a transparent toy model, **not** with elaborate mathematical vocabulary that outruns measurement.

Suppose an unresolved decision has a constant independent per-period probability \(q\) of valid resolution, where \(0<q<1\). Suppose additional review activity consumes a fraction \(p\) of the relevant decision capacity and—under **a strong explicit assumption**—reduces that probability to \(q(1-p)\), with \(0\le p<1\). Then the model gives

\[
\Pr(T>t\mid p) = (1-q(1-p))^t,
\qquad
\mathrm{E}[T\mid p] = \frac{1}{q(1-p)}.
\]

For fixed \(q>0\), increasing \(p\) increases the expected time to valid closure. This algebra is elementary. It **does not prove** that real review work has no informational benefit, that its capacity cost is proportional to elapsed time, that period outcomes are independent, or that any individual intends delay.

If review activity also improves the quality or completeness of evidence, it can **increase** the probability of a valid decision through a new variable \(g(p)\). An alternative hazard \(q(1-p)+g(p)\) may rise or fall, subject to remaining in \((0,1]\). Without measuring both capacity displacement and informational contribution, the predicted direction of delay is **indeterminate**. The toy model is thus valuable mainly as a constraint on rhetoric: "more meetings → delay" is not a universal scientific law.

Another caution is that the observed sample can experience changes in queue arrival rates and personnel. Even a correct per-decision hazard model would not by itself identify organization-wide backlog dynamics. Queueing assumptions, stable incoming demand and nonstationary capacity must be separately tested.

## 8. Negative cases and counterpressure against the hypothesis

A fair science program must actively seek instances in which familiar complaints about "pointless work" are wrong:

- A meeting resolves an undocumented safety obligation; progress may be invisible in a dashboard until an accident is averted.
- An Indigenous community declines a rapidly imposed decision because its legal/protocol authority was not respected; delays are not "bureaucratic confusion" to be optimized away.
- A worker calls work pointless despite their role's substantial beneficiary contribution, because domination or disrespect estranges them from its meaning.
- An institution's ostensibly ceremonial documentation creates the evidential basis for later legal accountability.
- A project generates many reports because underlying measurements are inconsistent, not because someone gains from non-resolution.
- A technology makes a task disappear locally by transferring the same care or corrective work onto users, families or contractors.

Only a model able to identify such examples as real alternatives can responsibly adjudicate strategic deferral.

## 9. Scientific conclusions and unresolved obligations

The literature warrants **strong conceptual separation** of unrecognized useful work, reported uselessness, counterfactual low-contribution tasks, and delay through process activity. The Soffia–Wood–Burchell empirical critique directly rejects the easy assumption that self-described useless jobs are a growing, ubiquitous occupational class. Star–Strauss shows why visual absence from metrics is not absence of work. Garbage-can organization theory and institutional decoupling explain why ambiguous choices and ceremonial procedures can arise **without** the deliberate strategy postulated by a political critique.

The specific hypothesis that **institutions sometimes prolong unresolved crises by commissioning process work that neither resolves evidence deficits nor adjusts valid decision criteria** is coherent and falsifiable under the stated conditions. It remains **unverified** in this research. No organization, manager or public agency is accused of intentional obstruction by this article; no causal coefficient or incidence estimate has been calculated.

The next scientific step is not to "publish the essay" or "make the paper more emotional." It is to commission an actual source-audited cohort and competing explanations study from a reusable research-operations provider, then have the proper Lenticule science domain critically review the result. Scientific reception may support, narrow, or reject the hypothesis without determining what the author writes in any private letter.

**Scientific status:** substantive theoretical and methodological working paper, no original data, no formal theorem about actual institutions, no external peer review or publication. An authored personal essay, if any, remains a separate identity with its own version, publisher and voice.
