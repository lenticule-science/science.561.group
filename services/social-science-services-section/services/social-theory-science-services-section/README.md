The Social Theory Science Services Section maintains social-theoretical
knowledge and practices. Its Ludics Science Services Section studies ludics
and its game-specific applications.

Its material is organized by artifact role under `content/`; specialized
subdomains continue under `services/`.

## Current research problems (not newsletter assignments)

The section keeps **research questions** separate from submitted papers, working
articles, and authored essays. A poetic title may be useful for finding a
problem, but it is not its definition, an exclusive manuscript identity, or a
publication-state flag.

### Anticipatory completions and the authorization of successors

**Research line:** How do ranking, interface friction, institutional workflow
and historically specific authority relations affect which suggested successor
state becomes actionable? The recovered [*Gray Completion* working research
article](content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/07/10/2026-07-10-the-gray-completion-working-research-article.md)
is a **July 2026 working contribution** to this larger problem, not the entire
research program. It is not accepted/published science.

- **Candidate mechanism:** an AI suggestion can alter the distribution of
  accepted articulations without issuing an order; a later institution can
  convert the accepted wording into an operational decision.
- **Empirical discrimination:** compare suggestions shown, accepted, edited
  and refused across different interfaces, user tasks, language communities,
  and institutional consequences; distinguish low-friction uptake from
  command/coercion and from genuine voluntary improvement.
- **Literature and source boundaries:** the Indian/U.S. autocomplete case in
  the working article is **not** evidence about Indigenous populations or a
  measured colonial impact. Ground claims about Deloria, political temporality,
  law and settlement in their own disciplinary and community authorities.
- **Possible falsifiers/limits:** evidence that differences vanish when
  interface costs are equalized; cases where users reject model defaults or
  change their meaning; model suggestions that have no downstream institutional
  uptake. Do not infer governance merely from next-token prediction.
- **Scientific outputs owed:** source audit and literature adjudication;
  specified experimental/comparative study design; results separating model,
  interface, user and organizational effects; reviewed interpretation, not
  necessarily another article with this poetic title.

### Temporality, situated obligation and institutional jurisdiction

The phrase *What Does It Mean to Believe in Time?* also names a question about
how time, place, evidence and administrative recognition acquire political
authority. It must **not** be treated as identical to emsenn's unpublished
release-ready personal letter of that name. Historical treaty cases, particular
Indigenous arguments and contemporary administration require their own source
criticism. The first deliverable is a scoped and disciplined literature and
source map; no universal claim about Indigenous time follows merely from
Deloria's time/place distinction. Historical source work should be served by
the existing History and Archaeology Science Section where appropriate.

### Meaningless labor, invisible work, and confusion as crisis deferral
**Current scientific working article:** [Work visibility, perceived usefulness, and institutional decision deferral](content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/10/12/2026-10-12-work-visibility-and-institutional-decision-deferral.md). This **28 KB independent paper** distinguishes unrecognized necessary work, perceived uselessness, counterfactually low-contribution tasks, and decision delay, and proposes competing hypotheses and a controlled study; its causal claims are **not yet established by original data**. The thematic title remains larger than this single paper.


This is a **family of researchable propositions**, not a Substack essay
assignment. Reuse existing scientific surveys already resident here:

- [Articulation Work and Invisible Labor](content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/05/03/2026-05-03-articulation-work-and-invisible-labor-survey.md)
- [Labor Process Theory](content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/05/03/2026-05-03-labor-process-theory-survey.md)
- [Platform and Algorithmic Work](content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/05/03/2026-05-03-platform-and-algorithmic-work-survey.md)

Separate at least three potential mechanisms before claiming results:
(1) work that is invisible to accounting but necessary for coordination or
care; (2) work that is socially unnecessary relative to a specified purpose;
and (3) work manufactured or prolonged when uncertainty or institutional
contradictions are not resolved. The categories may overlap, but the evidence
for one cannot establish another. Compare institutions and counterexamples,
define whose criteria determine “necessary,” and ask who gains authority or
relief from the delayed resolution.

### Provider boundary

For any of these lines, **Coesite Research Operations** supplies reusable
search, evidence custody, experimental execution and verification services.
The scientific field decides methods' disciplinary adequacy and substantive
results. **emsenn/substack** separately holds the author's own manuscripts and
release decisions. Neither provider gets to label a research topic
“published,” transform an author's essay into a paper, or change an empirical
claim to suit a platform's writing style.
