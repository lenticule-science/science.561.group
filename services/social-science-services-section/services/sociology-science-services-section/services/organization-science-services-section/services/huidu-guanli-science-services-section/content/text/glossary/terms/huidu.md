Huidu (灰度), "grey scale" or "grey-scale determination," names the discipline this library is built around: between any two poles an organization could resolve a question to — open or closed, centralized or devolved, control or freedom, aggression or restraint — there is a grey band, and leadership's real work is choosing and holding a specific point in that band rather than snapping to either extreme. Ren Zhengfei's own formulation states the claim directly: between black and white there is a grey area, and the leader's competence is measured by their skill in grasping the correct shade of that grey — a matter of degree (度, dù), not a binary choice.

Huidu is not a general attitude of moderation applied once and left alone. It is a per-dimension, continuously revisited calibration. An organization faces many independent axes at once — how much authority a unit holds versus how much the center retains, how aggressively the sales force is pushed versus how much it is protected from burnout, how openly information circulates versus how tightly it is compartmented — and each axis can sit at a different point in its own grey band, correctly, at the same time. Setting one axis toward an extreme is not automatically wrong; the discipline is that the setting is chosen deliberately, revisited as conditions change, and never treated as a fixed rule that has stopped being a judgment.

The mechanism this requires is a leader (or leadership body) willing to hold a contradiction open rather than discharge it. A black-or-white resolution is attractive because it is legible and defensible — a rule, once fixed, requires no further judgment. But Huawei's own theory holds that the questions huidu governs are exactly the ones that do not have a stable correct answer independent of context, so fixing them prematurely trades a real, continuously-tuned fit for a false, static certainty. This is the same failure mode fa (法) exists to prevent at the level of codified rule — fa's whole force comes from being fixed and public — which is precisely why huidu does not apply to fa itself: an organization does not run its written law at some grey setting between enforced and unenforced. Huidu governs the questions fa has deliberately not answered, not fa's own bindingness.

Huidu also has real boundaries. It is not indecision or a leader's unwillingness to commit — a grey-band judgment, once made, is a specific, actionable determination, held until conditions warrant revisiting it, not an open question left permanently unresolved. It is not moral relativism or an excuse to relax a standard whose whole value is being non-negotiable: kexin (可信), trustworthiness, is explicitly not a huidu question in Huawei's own account — security, resilience, and the other kexin attributes are treated as architecture decided once and not re-litigated case by case, because trustworthiness "cannot be added late," and a property that must be either present or absent is not one a leader is supposed to be tuning along a grey band. Nor is huidu the same discipline as wu wei (無為): wu wei is the choice not to intervene once an institution can carry the work on its own, whereas huidu is the ongoing exercise of judgment about where, exactly, an institution's settings should sit — the two combine (an organization mature enough for wu wei still needs its huidu settings tuned; huidu badly practiced is one reason an organization is not yet ready for wu wei), but they answer different questions. And huidu is not shi (勢) or shu (術): shi is the advantage a configuration confers regardless of who occupies it, and shu is a specific technique for accomplishing an end, while huidu is the higher-order judgment about which configuration or technique is even called for on a given axis at a given time.

The concept is, candidly, the one this library took longest to define, because it names the register the other instruments are exercised in rather than an instrument itself — daoshi (mentorship), neibu jiangshi (internal teaching), lunzhi dongshichang (rotating chairmanship), and jingshen lingxiu (the spiritual leader's rare, high-authority interventions) are all huidu judgments made concrete: each is itself a specific, deliberately chosen point on some grey band (how much a newcomer is guided versus left to find their own way; how much authority rotates versus concentrates; how often the founder speaks versus stays silent) rather than an extreme. Lang xing wenhua (狼性文化), wolf culture, is a case where the library itself records a contested reading rather than forcing one — and that refusal to flatten a genuinely unsettled term to a single verdict is itself an instance of huidu in the library's own practice, not just its subject.

Kernel and hull: in this corpus's own governing pairing, huidu-guanli is the doctrine of a node's kernel — the internal register in which a unit judges its own settings — while rendanheyi is the doctrine of its hull, the register in which units meet each other and their users. Huidu is the specific judgment exercised inside that kernel: the grey-band calibration a unit's own leadership holds before anything about the unit is expressed outward in rendanheyi terms.
