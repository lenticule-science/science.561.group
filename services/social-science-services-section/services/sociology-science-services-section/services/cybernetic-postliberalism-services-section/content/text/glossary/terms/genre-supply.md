Genre supply is the rate at which near-fitted, unburned genre-frames become available to a genre-consuming population, measured against the rate at which the contradiction those frames format arrives in that population's sensory field as a refutation. A genre, in **Lauren Berlant**'s sense ([berlant2011](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/berlant2011.md)), is a set of expectations about how a situation unfolds and what to feel at each stage. A frame is *near-fitted* when it already formats that harm for that audience, and *unburned* when no dated verdict has yet refuted it for that harm in that audience's memory. The core claim is that [californication](./californication.md) holds only where genre supply keeps pace with contradiction, and fails where contradiction outpaces available genre. The denominator is the harm's refutation rate, not its raw repeat rate: a harm that recurs without falsifying a promise (disinvestment, fire season) draws nothing from the stock, however often it recurs or is witnessed.

The strongest objection is that the numerator is unbounded. Platform media format any event within hours, and generative systems make narrativization free. If frames are produced faster than any harm can recur, supply never falls behind, the mechanism cannot fail, and californication returns to unfalsifiability by the opposite route from the one the failure conditions were written to close.

The answer is that production is not supply. A produced frame enters supply only when it reaches the genre-consuming population at coverage exceeding the harm's and carries credibility not already spent on that harm. Credibility-coverage is built slowly, by cultural work outside the platform cycle (multiculturalism took a quarter century from the artistic critique to its 1992 deployment, per [boltanskichiapello2005](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/boltanskichiapello2005.md)), and burned in one afternoon. Abundant production therefore lowers supply rather than raising it: each frame offered against a refuting harm is consumed on the harm's next return, so a population offered ten frames per refutation burns its inventory ten times faster than one offered one. Genre supply is a stock variable; production abundance is its drawdown rate.

Three consequences follow. First, for any contradiction with a nonzero refutation rate, frames-per-contradiction should rise across the platform era while holding-time-per-frame falls. Second, frames reached after repeated refutation should be visibly recycled, never new. Third, the term is wrong if a contradiction with a high refutation rate is held, for longer than its own repeat interval, by a frame with no prior inventory: a generative-era novel framing of homelessness that lasts two election cycles without coercive escalation would refute it.
