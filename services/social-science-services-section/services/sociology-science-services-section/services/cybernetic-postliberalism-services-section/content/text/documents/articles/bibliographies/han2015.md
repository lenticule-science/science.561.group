## Why this is central

*The Burnout Society* is the note's entire occasion: "In 2015, the South Korean-born German philosopher Byung-Chul Han published *The Burnout Society*, describing a subject who compulsively self-optimizes, burns out, and keeps going. This paper traces that subject's institutional genealogy..." Han supplies both the diagnostic vocabulary the note argues has an eight-centuries-prior ecclesial origin (achievement-subject, positive compulsion, transparency society, auto-exploitation) and two direct quotations the note leans on: "exploits itself until it burns out," and "succumbs to the destructive compulsion to outdo itself over and over."

## What it does not settle

Han's book is framed, on its own terms, as diagnosing a genuinely contemporary condition distinct from Foucault's disciplinary society. The note's central thesis — that this is not a novel condition but the terminal state of a specific eight-century-old ecclesial mechanism (the note's own frontmatter names this contrast directly: `contrasts-with: han-as-diagnosing-novel-condition`) — argues against Han's own framing rather than extending a claim Han's book makes. *The Burnout Society* does not discuss Lateran IV, Luther, Calvin, or monastic acedia at all; the entire theological genealogy connecting them to Han is the note's construction, using Han only as the diagnostic endpoint.
