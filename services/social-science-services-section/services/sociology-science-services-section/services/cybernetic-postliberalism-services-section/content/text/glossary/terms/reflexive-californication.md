Reflexive californication is emsenn's term for the case in which analyzing [californication](./californication.md) performs the operation it describes. Producing theory, vocabulary, and bibliography about structural collapse converts collapse into a project the analyst can manage, by the same displacement into a manageable register that californication runs on the governed subject. A glossary term is such a register. A corpus that answers each contradiction by minting one is doing to itself what the wellness industry does to its customers.

The distinguishing criterion is not what the analysis exports. The overview's test — does the work transferably reduce others' interpretive cost? — is satisfied by every successful genre, since genre in [Berlant's](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/berlant2011.md) sense is amortized interpretation. The criterion the note argues for is instead what the practice can *lose*. Analysis is californicated when its glossary is monotone: terms are added, bounded, and refined but never deleted because a dated prediction failed, so the analyst is never on record standing with a contradiction and no frame for it. Recovery faster than refutation is inventory management; recovery slower than refutation is loss.

The strongest objection is that deletion is itself a genre. Retraction has feeling rules in [Hochschild's](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/hochschild1983.md) sense — the humility display, the dated "I was wrong" — and a corpus can satisfy the note's 2027 condition by retiring one peripheral term while its load-bearing vocabulary stands untouched. Sacrificing a minor term is a frame already in stock, pulled forward: multiculturalism in 1992, at the scale of one glossary.

The answer is that the objection specifies which deletions count, not that none do. A deletion is loss only if the term was load-bearing, measured by inbound references from other entries, and those references are left dangling rather than rewritten to a successor. Rewriting is inventory rotation at the file level: the slot never empties. The criterion therefore sharpens to *slot vacancy* — the interval between a term's deletion and the appearance of any entry its former referrers link in its place.

The falsifiable form: a corpus is under reflexive californication if and only if its mean slot-vacancy time is zero. The sibling note's test needs one added clause — the deleted term must have carried at least three inbound links, and at least one must remain broken across a dated revision — or the deletion certifies compliance, and the corpus, this entry included, remains a genre inventory.
