## Why this is central

Caillois's paidia-ludus spectrum supplies the note's contrast between the institutional day job (rule-bound, monitored, "the ultimate ludus") and the bootstrapper's ad hoc AI project (unstructured, improvisational, "radical paidia"). The spectrum gives the note a vocabulary for the two projects' different relations to structure and risk that does not depend on judging either as more or less legitimate on its face.

## What it does not settle

Caillois's taxonomy of play (paidia and ludus, and the four categories of agon, alea, mimicry, and ilinx) is a sociology and phenomenology of games and play; it is not a theory of institutional power, state legitimacy, or colonialism, and Caillois does not connect paidia-ludus to a "state's monopoly on legitimate risk" — that synthesis with immunopolitics is the note's own construction. Caillois also treats paidia and ludus as poles of a continuum along which any activity can slide in either direction, not a binary; the note's flat labels ("the ultimate ludus," "radical paidia") compress a spectrum the source insists is dynamic.
