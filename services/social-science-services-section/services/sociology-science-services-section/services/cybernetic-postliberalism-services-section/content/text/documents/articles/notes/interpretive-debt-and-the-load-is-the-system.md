The [overview](./overview.md) admits that "the load is the system" entails a sink it has not named. [Recursive governance](../../../glossary/terms/recursive-governance.md) consumes sense-making faster than it regenerates; the system persists; so the deficit lands somewhere. The corpus's own [Bataille](../bibliographies/bataille1949.md) substitution, made in [Governing by confusion](./governing-by-confusion.md), picks an answer: "what must be burned now is sense-making itself." Burning is dissipation. Taken at its word, the framework predicts a visible failure curve, a population going interpretively dark on a schedule. That curve has not appeared. This note argues that Bataille is a theory of transfer, and that [californication](../../../glossary/terms/californication.md) is the transfer institution the framework already had.

## Bataille designates a bearer

Bataille's expenditures are never losses into nothing. Potlatch destroys the giver's wealth and *obligates* the recipient; the burn is what creates the debt. Sacrifice discharges the community's excess through one body designated to bear it. War spends surplus onto an enemy population; the Marshall Plan exports it as a gift that binds. In every case the expenditure is non-recuperable *to the party that spent it, in the currency spent*, and borne in full by a party the institution names. The general economy is a theory of which institution designates the bearer. Catastrophic dissipation, in Bataille, is what happens when no institution does: the excess is spent anyway, without form, as ruin. Dissipation is the failure mode of transfer, not its alternative. The question is therefore not whether interpretive load is lost but which institution names who carries it.

## What interpretation becomes when it cannot continue

Active interpretation is generative and priced per event: each refuting disruption demands a new frame. Affective management is not generative; it applies an inventoried frame (pivot, healing, resilience) at a standing cost per subject per period. [Interpretive saturation](../../../glossary/terms/interpretive-saturation.md) is the moment a subject's rate of frame-generation falls below the system's rate of frame-refutation. At that moment the load does not vanish. It changes form: from a demand that the subject *make sense* to a demand that the subject *maintain a mood about* sense already made by someone else. This is what californication's existing definition ("structural contradiction as affectively manageable content") describes but does not locate. It is not a process running beside recursive governance; it is the stage that receives governance's output once the interpreter is spent.

Three things make the affective register a superior sink rather than merely a second one. First, it runs on stock. The corpus already argues that californication's recovery is a supply problem drawing on inventoried genres; those genres *are* accumulated interpretive labor. The passive stage spends what the active stage banked, so it can absorb load without generating sense, and its failure condition is stock depletion. Second, its exhaustion does not read as failure. An exhausted interpreter produces no interpretation, and silence registers as loss of signal. An exhausted affect-manager produces flat affect, numbness, depression, and each is itself an affect: trackable, therapizable, monetizable, still signal. The affective sink has no saturation point legible as saturation. Third, because it is a standing rather than per-event cost, it is portable: payable by the care labor that repairs depleted interpreters ([Hochschild](../bibliographies/hochschild1983.md)), by populations who never had narrative access and receive only the mood-format ([Povinelli](../bibliographies/povinelli2011.md)), by the future self carrying chronic depletion, and by automation that now generates the frames so that the human's remaining role is to feel about a machine's summary. Each is a designated bearer; californication does the designating.

## The strongest objection

The dissipation reading has a serious defender. [Han](../bibliographies/han2015.md)'s achievement subject exploits itself to burnout, and the residue is depression, not transfer: the capacity is gone and nobody picks it up. Worse, "transfer" reintroduces the conservation law Bataille's theory was written to deny. Glorious expenditure is by definition non-recuperable; if the burn is passed on and absorbed elsewhere it is restricted-economy bookkeeping wearing Bataille's name. And once "the future" and "the environment" are admitted as bearers, every dissipation can be redescribed as a transfer, and the thesis says nothing.

## Answer

On Bataille: non-recuperability concerns the spender's ledger and says nothing about whether the burn lands. In potlatch the gift is lost to the giver *and* becomes the receiver's obligation; the two coincide in every case Bataille examines. What he denies is reinvestment, not bearing. The transfer thesis is Bataillean at exactly the point the objection says it is not.

On Han and falsifiability: the difference between transfer and dissipation is observable in signal. Dissipation means the load stops producing system-readable output; the subject goes silent and the silence is a gap. Transfer means the load keeps producing output at a different site or in a different register. Han's depressed subject, under californication, is not silent: the depression is diagnosed, tracked, content-ified, sold back as wellness ([Illouz](../bibliographies/illouz2007.md)). That is not a counterexample; it is transfer's characteristic output, depletion formatted as manageable content. Dissipation would look like *unformatted* depletion, depletion the system cannot read.

## Prediction

If this is right: as per-capita generation of novel interpretation about governance declines, the volume of affective-maintenance signal (therapeutic, wellness, mood-tracking, automated-summary consumption) should rise correspondingly, and the maintenance burden should land disproportionately on care laborers, later cohorts, and non-interpreting populations rather than on the original interpreters. If instead interpretive collapse is followed by silence, with no compensating rise elsewhere and no displaced care load, then the sink is dissipation, californication is merely parallel, and the failure curve is coming.
