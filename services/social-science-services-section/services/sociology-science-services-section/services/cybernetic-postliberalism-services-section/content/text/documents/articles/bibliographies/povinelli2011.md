## Why this is central

Povinelli is quoted twice by name in [A timeline of californication](../notes/history-of-californication.md) — once for the claim that "late liberalism governs not by producing life, but by maintaining the appearance of its ongoingness," once for governance's capacity to absorb rather than eliminate alternate worlds by folding them into regulatory imaginaries — yet until this entry the corpus's most-cited theorist had no bibliography carrier of her own. *Economies of Abandonment* is the book where that framing of late liberalism is developed at length: liberal governance, on Povinelli's account, increasingly manages populations and alternative social projects not by actively destroying them but by letting them endure — withholding recognition, deferring resolution, converting potential ruptures into "quasi-events" that never rise to the level of history. That is the mechanism californication names for a specifically Californian, cybernetic register: contradiction is not resolved, it is kept ongoing and made to feel survivable. [Governing by Confusion](../notes/governing-by-confusion.md) draws on the same book's vocabulary of endurance and the quasi-event directly, applying it to the 2025 tariff cycle.

## What it does not settle

Povinelli's ethnographic ground in this book is late-liberal Australia — Indigenous land-recognition politics in the Northern Territory — read alongside U.S. queer and AIDS-era activism; it is not a history of California, missions, Hollywood, or Silicon Valley, and it says nothing about cybernetics, feedback loops, or AI. The corpus's genealogy of californication and its account of a "feedback-loop subject" are this theory's own extension of her framework into a domain and a mechanism (cybernetic self-regulation) that Povinelli's book does not address. I am also not certain "governance of the otherwise" is a single quotable line from this specific book rather than a through-line she develops across her wider corpus (this book together with *Geontologies*, 2016); the corpus's own phrasing should be read as a compressed paraphrase of that broader argument, not a page-cited quotation.
