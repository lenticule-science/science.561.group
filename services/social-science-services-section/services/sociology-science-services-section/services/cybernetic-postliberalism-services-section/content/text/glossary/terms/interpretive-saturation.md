Interpretive saturation names the point at which a population's capacity to interpret is consumed faster than it regenerates. A cybernetic system requires continuous interpretive throughput — explanation, narrativization, sense-making — to convert disruption into legible signal and close its feedback loops. That throughput is produced by a finite population of interpreters (analysts, journalists, platform users, publics) working at a roughly fixed human rate, while the volume of events requiring interpretation is generated endogenously, by the same acceleration the system exists to manage. Saturation is not a temporary glut of information that a bigger newsroom or a faster feed could clear; it is the structural condition in which demand for meaning permanently outruns the supply, because the system's own operation is what manufactures the demand.

## Bataille's general economy, reframed

The concept reframes Georges Bataille's general economy in [_The Accursed Share_](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/bataille1949.md) (Les Éditions de Minuit, 1949; English translation Zone Books, 1988). Bataille argues that a system does not primarily face a problem of scarcity but of surplus: energy accumulates beyond what subsistence and growth require, and that excess must be expended somehow — lavishly, uselessly, through potlatch, sacrifice, festival, or war — because there is nowhere else for it to go. Cybernetic postliberalism keeps Bataille's structure (a system organized around compulsory, non-recuperable expenditure) while inverting its direction. Where Bataille's accursed share is an excess that must be burned off before it destabilizes the system, the cybernetic system's accursed share is a shortfall manufactured as excess: it generates volatility faster than its interpreters can absorb it, so what gets burned is not surplus energy but the population's already-insufficient capacity to make sense. The [note on governing by confusion](../../documents/articles/notes/governing-by-confusion.md) performs this substitution directly, arguing that under cybernetic capitalism "explanatory strain replaces the sacrificial economy" and "what must be burned now is sense-making itself." Bataille's own cases — Aztec sacrifice, cathedral-building, the Marshall Plan — belong to a general theory of political economy, not a theory of information; the substitution is cybernetic postliberalism's synthesis, not a claim Bataille makes himself.

## Why the system needs the shortfall

Saturation is not a malfunction the system would prefer to correct; it is productive. [Genre calibration](./genre-calibration.md) requires every disruption to resolve into a satisfying narrative, and no achievable supply of interpreters can keep pace with an endogenously accelerating supply of disruptions — so the shortfall itself becomes the resource. Burnout functions as proof-of-work; explanatory density functions as a signal of trustworthiness independent of its correspondence to fact; and the [neurotic platformal intellectual](./neurotic-platformal-intellectual.md) is drafted, willingly, into producing that density under conditions [industrial intellectualism](./industrial-intellectualism.md) already formats as genre-compliant labor. The system does not need its subjects to understand what has happened. It needs them still trying.
