## Why this is central

The note states: "Martin Luther, in *A Treatise on Christian Liberty* (1520), declared all honest work a sacred calling — Berufung. The cobbler's bench became as sacred as the altar... It also sacralized productivity: if work is a calling from God, failing to work hard is failing God." This supplies the "sacralize productive labor" row of the note's Measurement table (§5) — the note's second load-bearing genealogical link, after Lateran IV confession.

## What it does not settle

The note separately discusses Luther's 95 Theses (1517) attacking the indulgence system (§1.3), a second Luther text with no citekey or `references:` entry of its own in the note; readers should not conflate the two Luther citations under this one carrier. The 1520 Treatise's own argument concerns Christian freedom from the law through faith and the priesthood of all believers; its consequence that "failing to work hard is failing God" is the note's own extrapolation from Luther's dignification of ordinary labor, not a sentence the Treatise states.
