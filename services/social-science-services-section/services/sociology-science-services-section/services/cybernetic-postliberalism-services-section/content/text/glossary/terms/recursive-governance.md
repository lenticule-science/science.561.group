Recursive governance is emsenn's term, developed across "Governing by confusion" (2025-04-11) and "Citing for containment" (2025-04-12), for a mode of governance that operates not through top-down directives but through distributed feedback containment. Rather than issuing commands, the system detects deviation and corrects through feedback loops operating on different timescales: microseconds for financial markets, days for procurement and logistics, weeks for policy adjustment. Interpretation and narrative close the loop by converting volatility into coherent content that participants can absorb.

The key insight emsenn advances is that interpretive strain is not a failure mode of this governance but its mechanism. Confusion, information overload, and the felt pressure to make sense of contradictory signals are not byproducts — they are how recursive governance operates. Curiosity and sensemaking become system resources: the more people work to understand what is happening, the more they participate in the feedback loops that stabilize the system.

emsenn draws on [Stafford Beer](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/stafford-beer.md) and the [viable system model](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/cybernetics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/viable-system-model.md) to describe the nested regulatory structure, but argues that contemporary recursive governance exceeds [Beer](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/stafford-beer.md)'s framework because it has no explicit designer and no [requisite variety](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/cybernetics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/requisite-variety.md) constraint — it absorbs excess variety through narrative rather than filtering it. [Lauren Berlant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/lauren-berlant.md) and her concept of [crisis ordinariness](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/political-philosophy-services-section/services/liberalism-services-section/services/late-liberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/crisis-ordinariness.md) inform emsenn's account of how recursive governance normalizes its own operation: each crisis feels singular while functioning as a routine correction. [Wendy Chun](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/wendy-chun.md) contributes the concept of synchronization — the way networked systems produce temporal alignment among distributed actors without explicit coordination.

emsenn summarizes: "the load is the system." The interpretive burden people carry under recursive governance is not a symptom of mismanagement but the governing mechanism itself.
