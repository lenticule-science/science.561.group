## Why this is central

describing-californication.md's babble list names the mechanism without developing it: "repressive desublimation: pleasure becomes governance: amplifying desiring by producing it into commodity." That phrase is Marcuse's own coinage; the chapter of *One-Dimensional Man* titled "The Conquest of the Unhappy Consciousness: Repressive Desublimation" argues that advanced industrial society no longer suppresses instinctual gratification but administers it — sexuality and pleasure are loosed from taboo and delivered as commodity and entertainment, and this loosening does not weaken social control, it completes it, because satisfied desire stops generating the surplus of unsatisfied longing that once fed art, critique, and the "Great Refusal." This is the uncited theoretical ground under the corpus's babble, and it is the direct source for a new term the corpus develops, repressive desublimation, and for a note connecting that mechanism to zen fascism's "eroticization of detachment."

## What it does not settle

Marcuse is diagnosing the mid-century American welfare-warfare state — advertising, mass media, and consumer affluence circa 1964 — and he says nothing about platforms, algorithmic feeds, wellness apps, or the spiritualized idiom of calm that zen fascism names; extending "repressive desublimation" to that later, more specifically affective register is the corpus's own move, not a claim the book makes. The book's own resolution, the "Great Refusal" it closes on, is asserted as a possibility rather than demonstrated, and Marcuse gives comparatively little account of how refusal could arise from within a one-dimensional society he otherwise describes as nearly total — a gap the corpus inherits along with the diagnosis, and does not close by naming it.
