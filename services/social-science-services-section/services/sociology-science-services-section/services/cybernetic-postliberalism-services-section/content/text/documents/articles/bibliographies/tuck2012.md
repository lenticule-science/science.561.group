## Why this is central

Tuck and Yang supply "settler moves to innocence" — the taxonomy of strategies (claimed ancestry, metaphorical decolonial language, guilt-as-self-work, equating all oppressions with land theft, fantasies of inclusion) by which settlers relieve themselves of complicity without returning land or dismantling settler-colonial structure. The note borrows this diagnostic to explain why projecting purity onto an endangered language substitutes for the harder, material work of preserving it.

## What it does not settle

Tuck and Yang's essay explicitly refuses the metaphorical extension of "decolonization" to other social-justice projects — its title is a warning against exactly the move of borrowing decolonial vocabulary for problems that are not about land return. This note's own use of "settler move to innocence" for an intra-community dynamic about AI use, rather than for settler subjects relieving themselves of complicity in land theft, risks the metaphorical flattening the essay is written against; the source does not license this extension, and the tension is not resolved here.
