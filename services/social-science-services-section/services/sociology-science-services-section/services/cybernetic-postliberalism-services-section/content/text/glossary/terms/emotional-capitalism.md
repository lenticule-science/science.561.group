Emotional capitalism names a two-way fusion of market rationality and therapeutic discourse. Economic institutions come to speak the language of feeling — the "emotionally intelligent" manager, the corporate wellness program, the self-actualizing employee whose engagement is measured and coached. Simultaneously, intimate and emotional life comes to speak the language of the market — the calculating search for a partner, the self audited as a portfolio of "emotional capital" to be developed, tested, and traded for return. The fusion is not decorative. It restructures what emotion is *for*: feeling stops being a private interior that capitalism merely regulates at its edges and becomes the medium of value itself — evaluated, benchmarked, optimized, exchanged.

This is a deliberate extension, not a repetition, of **Arlie Russell Hochschild**'s earlier account. In *The Managed Heart* (1983), Hochschild's [feeling rules](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/feeling-rules.md) describe the social prescriptions that tell a worker which affect a role requires — the flight attendant's manufactured warmth, the service worker's suppressed irritation — and the emotional labor of producing that affect on demand. In Hochschild's frame, emotion is *managed*: a resource disciplined at the boundary of the wage relation, but still analytically separable from it, still something the worker has that the job extracts. **Eva Illouz**, in [*Cold Intimacies: The Making of Emotional Capitalism*](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/illouz2007.md) (2007, Polity), reframes the relation as constitutive rather than incidental. Emotion is not merely disciplined at the edge of the market; the market's own reproduction — and the reproduction of intimate life — runs on it. The manager is trained to be "emotionally competent" because competence has become a technical, gradable skill; the single person comparison-shops on a dating platform because courtship itself has been rationalized into a market; the self, on both sides of this exchange, is held as capital to be managed for a return. Feeling rules tell you what to feel; emotional capitalism explains why feeling is worth managing at all — it is what is being valorized.

This is why emotional capitalism supplies the political economy missing from [californication](./californication.md)'s mechanism of affective normalization: normalization is not incidental smoothing of feeling to make crisis bearable, it is extraction of a resource whose accumulation the normalization enables. And it is why the promise of getting one's emotional capital "right" — professionally, romantically, therapeutically — behaves as [cruel optimism](../../../../../critical-theory-science-services-section/services/affect-theory-services-section/services/berlantian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/cruel-optimism.md): pursuing the correctly managed feeling reproduces the very fusion of market and affect that forecloses the flourishing it promises. Capitalism does not merely use emotion. Under emotional capitalism, emotion is what it accumulates.
