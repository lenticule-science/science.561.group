The corpus's own genealogies have stated, three separate times and without a source, that "1945–1965" is when a Palo Alto military-academic-industrial complex produces "the feedback loop as governance." [Palo Alto Complex](../../../glossary/terms/palo-alto-complex.md) now names the mechanism that claim gestures toward. What it has not yet argued — and what this note argues — is a more specific claim: that inside this same window, at this same site, two models the corpus's later chapters treat as separate lineages meeting only at Esalen in 1963 were in fact forged as one apparatus, by the same institutions and often the same funding line, before either model had the name it now carries.

## Two models, one funding stream

Call the first model feedback-loop-as-governance. Its source is Cold War cybernetics: Norbert Wiener's claim, in [*The Human Use of Human Beings*](../bibliographies/wiener1950.md) (1950), that governance is nothing but a system continuously measuring its own deviation from a set point and correcting, with no controller who commands the whole. [Turner2006](../bibliographies/turner2006.md) traces this vocabulary's institutional home directly to the ARPA-funded research culture at the Stanford Research Institute — Douglas Engelbart's Augmentation Research Center among its programs — a defense-contracting apparatus fused onto the university by engineering dean Frederick Terman.

Call the second model the therapeutic-self: the self as an object continuously screened, tested, and optimized against a normative curve. [Harris2023](../bibliographies/harris2023.md) locates this model's institutional root a generation earlier and considerably uglier than Terman's contracts. Leland Stanford founded his university on his own horse-breeding ranch, where his method for optimizing an animal's speed through selective breeding was known, in his own words, as "the Palo Alto System" — a phrase and a logic the university's first president, David Starr Jordan, extended from horses to people as a campaigning advocate of eugenics.

These are not two histories that later intersect at a conference or a commune. They run through one set of buildings, on one funding stream. The behaviorist personnel science that Terman's engineering school absorbed into its wartime and postwar contracts — screening, testing, sorting people for military and industrial roles — is Jordan's breeding logic wearing a lab coat. The cybernetics that same contracting apparatus funded at SRI is Wiener's feedback loop wearing a systems-theory vocabulary. **Palo Alto did not import two techniques that happened to share a zip code; it ran one deviation-from-norm logic through two registers at once, under one roof.**

## Selection and correction, on one clock

Both registers share the exact structure the corpus's own [Viable-System Subject](../../../glossary/terms/viable-system-subject.md) already names in Stafford Beer's terms: a regulator holding a state by measuring deviation and correcting against it. What the Palo Alto fusion adds is that this structure runs at two temporalities inside the same institution. Selection is discrete and prior — you are screened once, admitted or rejected, bred or culled. Regulation, in [Beer1972](../bibliographies/beer1972.md)'s Viable System Model, is continuous and ongoing — you are corrected again and again against a setpoint, your requisite variety tested each cycle rather than once. 1945–1965 is the window in which the site that had always run the first began running the second under the same contracts, the same laboratories, frequently the same staff moving between behaviorist testing programs and systems-theory seminars. **The feedback-loop-as-governance model and the therapeutic-self model are not two lineages that meet at Esalen — they are one apparatus's two temporal faces, fused at Palo Alto before either reaches the coast.**

## What Esalen launders

The structural contradiction this fusion carries forward is exact and unresolved by design. Selection logic says some inputs are constitutionally unfit no matter how they are corrected afterward — Jordan's eugenics offers no path back for the culled. Feedback logic promises the opposite: that any system, given enough requisite variety, holds its own viability through continuous correction alone. Palo Alto's apparatus needed both claims running simultaneously — screen the unfit out, then promise the retained infinite correctability — and never had to resolve the tension, because a defense contract requires a working system, not a consistent philosophy. When Esalen strips the military-eugenic scaffolding in 1963 and hands the apparatus a therapeutic vocabulary, and when *californication* later routes it through affect and genre, what gets aestheticized is not a new insight into the self but an old selection-and-correction machine, relicensed. [Repressive Desublimation](../../../glossary/terms/repressive-desublimation.md) names the laundering mechanism with precision: the anxiety of having once been sortable, of remaining correctable, is not suppressed under the therapeutic genre — it is licensed back as growth, as self-work, as the pleasure of watching one's own feedback loop close.
