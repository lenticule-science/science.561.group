## Why this is central

Jameson appears by name in describing-californication.md's babbles list, dropped alongside **Herbert Marcuse**, **Guy Debord**, and others as a source the note means to draw on but never develops. This book is the missing development: Jameson's argument that postmodern culture is not merely produced *under* late capitalism but is structurally isomorphic *with* it — pastiche replacing parody, "waning of affect," a "great schizophrenic break" with historicity, depthlessness — supplies the periodizing mechanism the corpus needs but leaves implicit. Following Ernest Mandel's three-stage schema of capitalism, Jameson explains why cultural and intellectual production would take on a specifically industrial, market-circulated, genre-compliant form *now*, in late/multinational capitalism, rather than treating that compliance as culture's timeless condition. That is the load industrial-intellectualism.md is quietly carrying without citing it.

## What it does not settle

*Postmodernism* diagnoses culture broadly — architecture, film, video, "theory" itself as a genre — under late capitalism generally; the book precedes platformization and algorithmic curation entirely, so it offers no account of the citation-as-signal mechanics or feeling-rules enforcement that industrial-intellectualism.md builds out for social media and academic production specifically. Jameson's own proposed response to depthlessness, "cognitive mapping," is a pedagogical-aesthetic project aimed at restoring collective political orientation; it is not the same claim as emsenn's diagnosis that analysis is produced to satisfy a genre's affective demand rather than to correspond to outcomes, and the corpus's extension into that specific mechanism is its own synthesis, not one Jameson makes.
