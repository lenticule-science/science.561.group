## Why this is central

Esposito's immunization paradigm — a community protects life by incorporating a controlled, regulated dose of what threatens it — supplies the note's explanation for why an unsanctioned, un-"dosed" intervention like a bootstrapped AI translation project reads to the institutional worker as contagion rather than as a benign or welcome innovation: it arrives outside any institutionally administered dose.

## What it does not settle

Esposito's book is a general political-philosophical account of immunity as the logic of rights, sovereignty, borders, and community; it does not address language preservation, artificial intelligence, or intra-community peer policing specifically — the note's "immunopolitics" application is its own synthesis, built on Esposito's vocabulary rather than a case Esposito treats. It is also worth distinguishing what the note describes from what Esposito calls auto-immunity: the note describes the ordinary, still-functioning operation of the immunization paradigm (treating the unsanctioned as pathogen), not the pathological, self-destroying intensification Esposito reserves the term auto-immunity for; nothing here should be read as a claim that settler immunopolitics has become auto-immune in Esposito's stricter sense.
