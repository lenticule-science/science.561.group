## Why this is central

The note states: "Michel Foucault, in *The History of Sexuality, Volume 1* (1976), traces a direct line from [mandatory confession] to the psychiatrist's couch, the HR performance review, and the social media post. Each requires the subject to articulate their interior state to an authority that processes the disclosure. The confessional produces knowledge about the subject and exercises power over them through the same act." This supplies the note's first genealogical row (mandatory confession → platform self-disclosure → Han's "transparency society").

## What it does not settle

Foucault's book is a genealogy of the discourse of sexuality and confession as a technology of power/knowledge across the modern West broadly; the confessional is one instance within a much wider argument about biopower, and the book does not discuss Han, social media platforms, or HR performance reviews — all published decades after Foucault's 1984 death. Extending Foucault's confessional analysis to platform self-disclosure and Han's transparency society, as the note's §1.1 does, is the note's own extension of Foucault's argument, not a claim the source makes.
