## Why this is central

Mike Davis's *City of Quartz* (1990) is the founding social history of Los Angeles as a manufactured landscape, and it supplies the political economy this corpus's own genealogy has so far only gestured at. Its account of the Chandler family's real-estate boosterism, the studio system's manufacture of a "sunshine" mythology, and the postwar buildout of a militarized "Fortress L.A." of private security and policing gives material grounding to entries in *A Timeline of Californication* that treat Hollywood, suburbia, and the 1992 LA Uprising as discrete cultural beats without saying what built or defended them. Davis's organizing move — reading Los Angeles through the dialectic of official sunshine against a shadow history of dispossession and repression, often shorthanded as "sunshine and noir" — is arguably the single most direct real-world precedent for what this corpus calls californication: a mechanism that produces a place-image which absorbs and re-narrates the structural violence subtending it. Named repeatedly in the corpus's own theorist lists for exactly this reason, the book has until now had no entry of its own here.

## What it does not settle

Davis writes urban political economy and history, not affect theory or cybernetics, so *City of Quartz* never frames its subject in terms of feedback, regulation, or the savior-slave subject the corpus's californication term develops; the bridge from his institutional analysis of developers, police, and studios to a theory of the californicated self is work this corpus has to do, not something the book already does. The book is also fixed at its 1990 publication: it precedes the 1992 Uprising it is often read as having anticipated, and it has nothing to say about the platform era, the wellness industry, or Silicon Valley, all of which the corpus's genealogy treats as later phases of the same process Davis describes only up to the Reagan-era city.
