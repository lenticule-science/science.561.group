## Why this is central

Peters supplies the technical distinction the note's central move depends on: the difference between a time average (what happens to one trajectory repeated over time) and an ensemble average (what happens on average across many parallel trajectories at one moment). Without this distinction, "the game is ergodic" and "cultural extinction is an absorbing barrier" are metaphors; with it, they name a specific, well-defined mismatch between two ways of averaging a process, and the note's claim that Kelly-style caution is a categorical error when applied to language death rests on exactly this mismatch.

## What it does not settle

Peters's ergodicity-economics program is contested among economists: critics argue that expected-utility theory, correctly specified with a concave (e.g. logarithmic) utility function, already captures the risk aversion Peters attributes to non-ergodicity, making the "ergodicity" reframing a restatement rather than a new result. This note does not adjudicate that debate; it borrows Peters's vocabulary (time average, ensemble average, absorbing barrier) without resolving whether the ergodicity framing is the uniquely correct account of why ruin-avoidance matters. Peters's paper is also about individual wealth dynamics under repeated gambles or investment, not about intergenerational cultural or linguistic survival — extending it to a language's speaker population is this note's own move, not a claim in the source.
