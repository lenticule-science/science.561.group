## Why this is central

James R. Beniger's *The Control Revolution* argues that the "information society" is not a late-twentieth-century novelty but the culmination of a much older "crisis of control": nineteenth-century industrial systems (railroads, factories, wholesale distribution) began moving material and people faster than existing bureaucratic and communication technologies could track, schedule, or account for, and the resulting innovations — the telegraph, standardized time, statistical bureaus, scientific management, and eventually computing — were built specifically to close that gap between throughput and control. This is the historical link telemetricism.md's own "Lineage of Modulation" is missing: that lineage credits Wiener and Beer for showing systems "regulate through feedback, not control," but names no one who traces how feedback-based regulation actually migrated out of engineering theory and into the bureaucratic, logistical, and statistical infrastructure that now carries it. Beniger supplies exactly that: a continuous institutional history running from the 1840s railroad dispatcher's control problem through to the mid-century cybernetic theory Wiener and Beer formalized, showing that "interpretation becomes infrastructure" (telemetricism's phrase) describes a process Beniger dates to more than a century before any platform existed.

## What it does not settle

Beniger writes as a sociologist of bureaucratic and technological rationalization, not as a critic of it: his control revolution is functionalist, describing how societies solved a coordination problem, and the book predates the commercial internet, so it stops well short of the platform-era, networked systems telemetricism.md addresses. He also has no equivalent to the corpus's diagnostic claim that contemporary governance has "no explicit designer" — his control systems, however large, are still built and staffed by identifiable administrators, engineers, and statisticians pursuing a legible goal, not the "post-goal," designer-less recursion the corpus locates in telemetricism.
