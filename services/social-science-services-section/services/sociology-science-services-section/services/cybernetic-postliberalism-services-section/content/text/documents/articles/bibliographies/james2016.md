## Why this is central

James's captive maternal — the caretaker whose stabilizing labor is captured to reproduce the very racial-carceral order that consumes it — supplies the structural template for the note's own coinage, "the captive moral economy": the marginalized worker whose moral-policing labor (attacking the bootstrapper, defending institutional norms) stabilizes an order that continues to endanger them and their community.

## What it does not settle

James develops the captive maternal specifically in relation to racial-carceral capitalism and the gendered, often Black women's, caretaking labor that stabilizes it, tracing stages from caretaker through protester, movement maker, to maroon. The note's "captive moral economy" is explicitly this note's own extension of the concept to a different domain — settler-facing moral policing around AI and language preservation — and James's essay neither names nor argues for that extension; the note states the extension plainly rather than presenting it as James's own term, and that distinction should not be allowed to blur on a later reading.
