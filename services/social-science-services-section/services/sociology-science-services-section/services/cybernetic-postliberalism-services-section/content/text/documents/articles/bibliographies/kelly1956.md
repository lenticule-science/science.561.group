## Why this is central

Kelly's paper is the mathematical origin of the position-sizing rule this note calls "the settler's risk calculus": maximizing expected logarithmic wealth under repeated favorable bets. It supplies the note's governing image — a wager sized to survive forever while compounding — that the note maps onto the institutional day job as a "safe wager" against personal ruin. Kelly derives the criterion from a gambler with private side information about a noisy communication channel, reframing Shannon's channel-capacity theorem as an optimal betting problem; the growth-optimal fraction the note leans on ({f* = (bp - q) / b}) traces back to this derivation.

## What it does not settle

Kelly's paper says nothing about non-ergodic social processes, cultural extinction, or absorbing barriers outside of ordinary gambling ruin — it is a paper in information theory, addressed to a communication-engineering audience, not a social theory. The extension this note makes — treating language death as an absorbing-barrier process to which Kelly-style sizing is a categorical mismatch — is [Peters's](./peters2019.md) ergodicity-economics framing applied by this note, not a claim Kelly himself makes or anticipates. Kelly's model also assumes the bettor already knows the true win probability; it has nothing to say about estimation error, which real applications (including the institutional-risk analogy this note draws) must add on top.
