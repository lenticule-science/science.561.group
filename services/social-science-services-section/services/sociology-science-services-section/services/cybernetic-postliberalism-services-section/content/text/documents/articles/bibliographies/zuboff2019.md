## Why this is central

Zuboff's book names and diagnoses the economic logic that the corpus otherwise treats as self-evident: a "surveillance capitalism" in which human experience is unilaterally claimed as free raw material, translated into behavioral data, and fed forward into "prediction products" sold in "behavioral futures markets." Her account of "instrumentarian power" — power that shapes conduct not by command or ideology but by rendering the subject continuously observable and nudgeable toward "guaranteed outcomes" — is the obvious, currently uncited grounding for what [californication.md](../../../../glossary/terms/californication.md) calls "platformal process," the mechanism it currently attributes only to Wendy Chun's account of programmability. Zuboff supplies the economic motive Chun's media-theoretic account leaves implicit: platforms select for platform-compatible affect because that affect is the extracted resource, and behavioral surplus is what the whole apparatus is built to harvest.

Zuboff is already named, unlinked, among the theorists listed behind californication's formation in the corpus's own notes (68 hits across the wider repo), which makes this entry's absence from the bibliography section a gap the corpus had already flagged rather than a new addition. Filing her here gives the "platformal process" bullet, and any future note on surveillance and intimacy, an actual citation to stand on instead of an implied one.

## What it does not settle

Zuboff's frame is fundamentally economic and institutional: she indicts specific firms (principally Google and Facebook) and a specific market logic, and her proposed remedies run through law, regulation, and competition policy. She does not develop an account of the subject's own affective self-management — the californication thesis's claim that structural contradiction gets metabolized as personally manageable mood is not her argument, and her "instrumentarian power" describes behavior modification at the population level more than the lived, narrativized experience of the californicated subject. Critics (not engaged in this entry) have also argued her periodization overstates surveillance capitalism's novelty against longer histories of corporate data extraction; this entry takes no position on that debate.
