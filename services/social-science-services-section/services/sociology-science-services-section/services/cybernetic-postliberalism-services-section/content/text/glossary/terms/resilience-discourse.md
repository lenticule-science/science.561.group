Resilience discourse is a mechanism, not a mood: it takes a claim that would register, in a structural idiom, as evidence of system failure — wages that do not cover costs, institutions that do not hold, infrastructure that does not survive contact with crisis — and reroutes it into a diagnostic of the individual nervous system. Collapse is real, but coping with it is rewritten as trainable. Vagal tone, breathwork, sleep hygiene, cognitive reframing, "hardiness," and "grit" become the vocabulary in which the failure to recover from shock is legible — and that failure is coded as personal maladaptation, never as evidence that the shock was structural. This is a genre operation before it is an ideology: a switch in which register of explanation is permitted to count.

The switch is measurable because it is first governmental. **Nikolas Rose**'s [*Governing the Soul: The Shaping of the Private Self*](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/rose1999.md) (Free Association Books, 1999) traces how psy-expertise — psychology, therapy, personnel management — installs the interior self as an object each person is obligated to know, monitor, and improve; resilience discourse is a late, instrumented extension of that same apparatus, now delivered through self-report inventories, wearable biometrics, and workplace "resilience trainings" that make adaptive capacity auditable at the level of the soul. **Eva Illouz**'s [*Cold Intimacies: The Making of Emotional Capitalism*](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/illouz2007.md) (Polity, 2007) supplies the other half of the mechanism: her coined term "emotional capitalism" — the fusion of economic rationality with therapeutic idiom — explains why resilience becomes a *competency*, listed on a résumé, trained like a portfolio asset, offered as an investment yielding productivity and promotion. Resilience discourse is emotional capitalism's most fully monetized affect: hardiness with a return on investment.

The corpus has already dated this discourse's saturation without naming it: [A timeline of californication](../../documents/articles/notes/history-of-californication.md) records 2020's "Pelotonized resilience," 2021's polyvagal-discourse uptake, and 2022–2024's "decline of DEI + rise of 'resilience' in institutions" as a single sequence — institutions abandoning structural remediation for affective retraining at scale. Resilience discourse names that sequence's mechanism: [californication](./californication.md)'s genre-calibration performed specifically on the register of institutional repair.
