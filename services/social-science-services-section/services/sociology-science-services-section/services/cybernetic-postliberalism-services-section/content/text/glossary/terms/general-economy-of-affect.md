A restricted economy of affect is a closed ledger: an individual manages felt intensity against an expected return, discharging exactly as much as therapy, self-care, or a night of doomscrolling can absorb, on the assumption that if the books are kept — mood tracked, triggers named, coping applied — the account will balance. A general economy of affect makes no such assumption. It names the scale at which affective surplus is produced, tallied, and burned off not by any single subject's bookkeeping but by the system whose own operation manufactures more feeling than any finite population of feelers could ever recoup. Where the restricted economy asks how a person processes what they feel, the general economy asks what happens to the remainder that no amount of individual processing was ever going to clear.

The distinction is Georges Bataille's, extended. In [_The Accursed Share_](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/bataille1949.md) (Les Éditions de Minuit, 1949; English translation Zone Books, 1988), Bataille argues that classical political economy mistakes its object: a system's primary problem is not scarcity but surplus. Energy accumulates past what subsistence and growth require, and that excess must be spent somehow — lavishly, uselessly, "unproductively" — because there is nowhere else for it to go. Potlatch, sacrifice, festival, war: these are not failures of economic rationality but the general economy discharging what the restricted economy of household and firm was never built to hold. Bataille's own cases belong to a general theory of political economy across historical civilizations, not to a theory of affect or information; the move from energetic surplus to affective surplus is cybernetic postliberalism's synthesis, not a claim Bataille makes himself.

Applied to affect, the general economy names what no amount of individual [structural contradiction](./structural-contradiction.md)-management ever touches: a cybernetic system's own operation continuously manufactures more feeling — rage, dread, grief, jouissance — than its population can discharge at a human rate, and [genre calibration](./genre-calibration.md) only decides which discharges count as resolved, not how the underlying volume shrinks. Interpretation is one channel this surplus runs through, not the only one; the general economy of affect is the whole accursed share, of which interpretive load is a single, named tributary.

A subject can keep an immaculate restricted economy of feeling — therapized, self-cared, genre-compliant — while the general economy's accursed share of affect keeps growing regardless, because the ledger that was never going to balance was never theirs to balance.
