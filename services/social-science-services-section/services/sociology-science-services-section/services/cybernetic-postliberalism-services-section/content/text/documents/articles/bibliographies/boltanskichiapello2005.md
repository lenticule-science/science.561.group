## Why this is central

Boltanski and Chiapello are named in describing-californication.md's babbles list under "incorporation/valorization cycle: absorbs the other as economic productivity," never developed there. This book is the direct source: their argument that capitalism survives each crisis of legitimacy by absorbing the critique leveled against it, converting the critic's own vocabulary into capital's next justifying "spirit." Their central case is exactly this mechanism at the scale the corpus needs — the post-1968 "third spirit" (network- and project-based "connexionist" capitalism) formed by absorbing the counterculture's *artistic critique* (its demand for authenticity, autonomy, and creative self-realization against bureaucratic conformity) into management's own language of flexibility, networks, and passionate work, while leaving the *social critique* (inequality, exploitation, security) largely unanswered. That distinction and that absorption sequence is the "incorporation/valorization cycle" the babble names but never sources: the other's objection becomes, once metabolized, a new form of the same system's productivity.

## What it does not settle

Boltanski and Chiapello are analyzing French management and business literature from roughly the 1960s through the 1990s; they say nothing about platformized, telemetric, or algorithmically-mediated production, so the third spirit's absorption mechanism has no built-in account of what changes when the absorbing apparatus is a recommendation system rather than a management text or a corporate seminar. Nor do they claim the incorporation cycle is total or inescapable — their own framework leaves open, and their later work revisits, whether a critique could be formulated that capitalism's apparatus of justification cannot metabolize, a question the corpus's babble list assumes an answer to rather than argues for.
