## Why this is central

The note credits Aquinas with systematizing acedia (ST II-II, Q.35: "sadness regarding the spiritual good, a sin against charity, whose daughters include both torpor and restless busyness") and with the theology of merit (ST I-II, Q.114: *meritum de condigno* and *meritum de congruo*). Both are load-bearing to the note's central genealogical claim — that Han's burnout and "positive compulsion" toward self-optimization have a precise theological ancestor the Church diagnosed and analyzed eight centuries earlier.

## What it does not settle

The Summa's treatment of acedia and merit is scholastic moral theology addressed to a monastic and clerical readership operating inside the Church's own apparatus of confession and penance; it draws no line itself to Weber's capitalism, Foucault's confessional apparatus, or Han's burnout society — those genealogical links are the note's own construction, not claims Aquinas makes or anticipates. The note cites the Summa only by its Question references (ST I-II, Q.114; ST II-II, Q.35) rather than a specific edition or translator, consistent with the "various editions" publication field the note's own references map already carries.
