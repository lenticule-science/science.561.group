## Why this is central

Barbrook and Cameron name and diagnose a "bizarre hybrid" faith they call the Californian Ideology: New Left communalism and New Right laissez-faire economics fused by a technologically-determinist optimism that digital networks and cybernetic feedback would dissolve the state and produce a self-organizing, egalitarian order without anyone having to build one — hippie and yuppie converging on the same conviction that computers plus markets equal freedom. This essay is the actual belief-system babbles in this section invoke by name (repeatedly, and without a bibliography entry until now) whenever they gesture at Silicon Valley's self-image. It matters to this corpus specifically because [californication](../../../glossary/terms/californication.md) — emsenn's term for the mechanism by which liberal governance absorbs structural contradiction affectively — needs a named historical object to metabolize, and the Californian Ideology is one of the actual, dated, arguable belief-systems it feeds on and reproduces as regional common sense.

The exact original venue is worth flagging plainly rather than pinned down further than the record supports: the essay circulated online (via mailing lists and, per most accounts, Mute magazine) from 1995 before its formal print appearance in *Science as Culture* in 1996, and this entry does not claim certainty about which counts as first publication.

## What it does not settle

Barbrook and Cameron have no theory of affect, genre, or a feedback-loop subject as psychic structure; their target is an ideology's arguments and internal contradictions — its libertarian anti-statism sits uneasily with its actual dependence on state-funded infrastructure (DARPA, the university system) — not a mechanism by which crisis gets metabolized into personal narrative. They also do not use the word "californication," and their polemic is dated to Wired-era techno-utopianism circa the mid-1990s, not a claim about the longer genealogy (missions, Gold Rush, Hollywood, Palo Alto) this corpus traces under that term.

This distinction is the point of this entry: the Californian Ideology is a describable, historically specific set of claims — a thing people asserted and could be wrong about — while californication is emsenn's name for a transhistorical governance process. Collapsing the two into synonyms would be an error; the Californian Ideology is one input californication absorbs and metabolizes, not another name for the absorbing itself.
