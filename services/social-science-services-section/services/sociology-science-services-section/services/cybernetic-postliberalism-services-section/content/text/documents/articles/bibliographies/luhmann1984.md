## Why this is central

*Social Systems* makes the claim [telemetricism.md](../../../glossary/terms/telemetricism.md) needs but does not cite: society is composed of autopoietic systems — law, the economy, science, and so on — each producing its own elements (communications, decisions) through its own internal code, and each therefore "operationally closed": nothing crosses into a system as information unless that system's own code can process it as such. This is a stronger claim than the cybernetic feedback model Wiener and Beer supply elsewhere in the same lineage of modulation: feedback presupposes a signal the regulator and the regulated both register, where Luhmann's closure denies that shared registration is even available across differently-coded systems. Telemetricism.md's line crediting Luhmann with "systems respond only to internally legible information" is exactly this operational-closure thesis, redirected from societal function systems onto telemetric infrastructure — it is what explains why an unmodeled agent is excluded from a participation loop outright rather than merely regulated badly.

## What it does not settle

*Social Systems* is a general theory of societal function systems, built from Luhmann's own adaptation of Maturana and Varela's biological concept of autopoiesis combined with a communication-based reworking of Talcott Parsons's structural functionalism; it says nothing about digital platforms, algorithmic feedback, or telemetry, so telemetricism.md's extension of "internally legible information" to recommendation engines, pricing models, and moderation tooling is the note's own application, not a case this book analyzes. It was originally published in German (Suhrkamp, 1984); beyond the fact of the 1995 Stanford translation given above, this entry does not vouch for further edition or translator detail. The book also operates at the scale of society-wide function systems, not individual platforms or algorithms, so the note's shift to a single infrastructure's feedback loop is a scale-shift Luhmann's own framework does not license by itself.

## Bridges

- [The Human Use of Human Beings (wiener1950)](./wiener1950.md) — the feedback-regulation model Luhmann's operational closure sharpens: Wiener's controller and controlled still share a registered signal, where Luhmann's closed systems do not.
- [Brain of the Firm (beer1972)](./beer1972.md) — Beer's viable system model still assumes a metasystem can filter environmental variety by structure; Luhmann's closure is the reason an un-coded agent cannot enter that filter as variety at all.
- [telemetricism](../../../glossary/terms/telemetricism.md) — the term this entry directly grounds, at the line crediting Luhmann for "systems respond only to internally legible information."
