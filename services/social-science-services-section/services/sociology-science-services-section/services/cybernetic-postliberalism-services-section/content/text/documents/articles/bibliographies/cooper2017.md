## Why this is central

Cooper's book is the direct academic precedent for the move the corpus's own name performs and has not, until now, cited: it argues at length that Friedman-style market economics and the "family values" social conservatism of the Christian Right were never opposing tendencies temporarily allied by electoral convenience, but a single formation, co-designed from the 1970s on to answer the same problem — the welfare-rights, feminist, and gay-liberation movements' claims that the state, not the family, should bear the cost of care and risk. Where overview.md glosses "postliberal" as inherited from Deneen's critique of liberalism and "cybernetic" as the framework's own systems-theoretic contribution, treating the two as a combination emsenn is first to fuse, Cooper's subtitle alone — "Between Neoliberalism and the New Social Conservatism" — already names that combination as one historical apparatus, worked out in welfare law, family law, and household-debt policy well before "postliberal" became a self-description of the intellectual right. Citing her here grounds, for the first time in the corpus, the claim implicit in the framework's own name: that its economic and social-conservative components are not two ideologies in temporary coalition but one apparatus that reprivatizes risk onto the family unit and calls the result virtue.

## What it does not settle

Cooper's book is a legal and economic history — of welfare reform, family law, human-capital theory, and household debt in the United States from the 1970s through the 2008 crash — not a theory of cybernetic feedback, platforms, or affect, so it supplies the historical fusion this corpus's name presupposes without supplying the corpus's own mechanism (californication, telemetricism, recursive governance) for how that fusion is lived and processed day to day. It is also a US-focused account centered specifically on welfare and family law, and does not itself establish that the same neoliberal/social-conservative fusion holds in the platform-governance or international contexts the wider corpus also analyzes; extending it that far is this corpus's claim to make, not one the book makes on its own.

## Bridges

- [Undoing the Demos (brown2015)](./brown2015.md) — Brown supplies the *homo oeconomicus* account of neoliberal subject-formatting; Cooper supplies the historical claim that this same formatting was co-produced with, not opposed by, the social-conservative moralization of the family.
- [Why Liberalism Failed (deneen2018)](./deneen2018.md) — Deneen's postliberal case for family and tradition against liberal individualism is, on Cooper's account, downstream of the very alliance she historicizes; Cooper reads as manufactured formation what Deneen reads as recovered virtue.
- [Responsibilization](../../../glossary/terms/responsibilization.md) — Cooper's central mechanism, the reprivatization of welfare risk onto the family unit, is a named historical instance of what this term describes at the level of the individual subject.
