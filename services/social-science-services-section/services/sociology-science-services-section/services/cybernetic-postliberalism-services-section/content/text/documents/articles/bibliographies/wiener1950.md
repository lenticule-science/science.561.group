## Why this is central

This is the founding text of the framework's cybernetic-governance level: the claim that "the system regulates itself through distributed feedback loops" is Wiener's before it is emsenn's. Wiener's popularization of cybernetics — coined from the earlier, more technical *Cybernetics* (1948) — argues that governance, communication, and control are the same phenomenon: a system holds a state by continuously measuring its own deviation from a set point and correcting, with no single controller who commands the whole. His central image, the feedback loop of measurement and correction (he draws it from the thermostat and the servomechanism), is the mechanism [cybernetic-governance.md](../notes/cybernetic-governance.md) names when it describes governance as "distributed feedback loops" rather than a "sovereign who decides" and "subjects who comply." Wiener also argued, against the optimism of his moment, that this same self-regulating capacity could be turned toward social control and dehumanization if left unexamined — a warning that anticipates the corpus's own wariness toward feedback-based governance as something to diagnose, not merely admire.

## What it does not settle

Wiener is describing communication engineering and organismic self-regulation in the early Cold War, decades before networked, platform-mediated publics existed; he has no account of how interpretive labor itself — commentary, punditry, sensemaking — becomes the substance of the feedback loop, which is the specific move [recursive-governance.md](../../../glossary/terms/recursive-governance.md) makes. He also writes as a mathematician-engineer optimistic that clearer feedback and better information could make society more humane, not as a critic of who benefits when the loop's interpretive burden falls on the public. The book does not anticipate governance that has "no explicit designer," a condition the corpus treats as decisive for how recursive governance differs from Wiener's own model of a legible, engineerable system.

## Bridges

- [Cybernetic Governance: The Load Is the System](../notes/cybernetic-governance.md) — the note that names Wiener directly and derives "distributed feedback loops" from his account of self-regulation.
- [Recursive governance](../../../glossary/terms/recursive-governance.md) — the term that extends Wiener's feedback loop past his own framework, arguing contemporary governance has no designer and absorbs variety through narrative rather than filtering it.
