Surveillance intimacy names the mechanism by which data extraction is engineered to be felt, and presented, as care. It is not surveillance that happens to an unwilling subject; it is surveillance the subject wants, because the interface through which every [telemetric signal](./telemetric-signal.md) they emit — scroll, pause, like, location ping, a draft typed and then deleted — is registered and returned to them as evidence of being known. The platform does not conceal its extraction; it stages the extraction as the relationship. Being tracked closely enough, continuously enough, is what produces the feeling of being remembered, understood, seen. Extraction and intimacy stop being distinguishable because both are architected to deliver the same affect: the small relief of mattering to something that is paying attention. This is what separates surveillance intimacy from ordinary surveillance — the latter is tolerated or evaded; this is solicited.

The mechanism has two moving parts, and each has its own theorist. Shoshana Zuboff (*The Age of Surveillance Capitalism*, 2019, PublicAffairs) supplies the extraction half: platforms unilaterally claim human experience as free raw material, translate it into behavioral surplus, and feed it forward into prediction products sold in behavioral futures markets, exercising what she calls instrumentarian power — governing conduct not by command but by rendering the subject continuously observable and nudgeable. Zuboff explains why the watching happens. She does not, on her own, explain why the watched lean into the sensors. That half is supplied by José van Dijck (*The Culture of Connectivity*, 2013, Oxford University Press), who distinguishes connectedness — the felt, valorized social experience users believe they are having — from connectivity, the coded, monetizable infrastructure that produces it. Van Dijck shows platforms engineering connectedness as an experience while metabolizing it as connectivity data: the sense of being close to someone, or of a feed that knows you, is manufactured by the same architecture that harvests the closeness as signal.

Surveillance intimacy is the point where Zuboff's extraction and van Dijck's engineered connectedness collapse into one loop, and it is the affective completion of what californication's own [platformal process](./californication.md) names structurally: platforms selecting for platform-compatible behavior. Here the selection pressure is recognition. [Socially ontogenic media](./socially-ontogenic-media.md) supplies the survivable posture — disclose, and you will be seen — that makes constant legibility feel like belonging rather than exposure. The [structural contradiction](./structural-contradiction.md) between care and extraction is not resolved; it is metabolized affectively, so that being cared for and being extracted from arrive in the same gesture, at the same moment, through the same feed.

Where ordinary surveillance produces a subject who hides, surveillance intimacy produces a subject who performs.
