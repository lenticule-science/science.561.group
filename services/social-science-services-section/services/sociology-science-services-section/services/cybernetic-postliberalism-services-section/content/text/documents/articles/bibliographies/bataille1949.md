## Why this is central

Bataille's general economy argues that a system does not primarily face a problem of scarcity but of surplus: energy accumulates beyond what subsistence and growth require, and that excess must be expended somehow — lavishly, uselessly, "unproductively" (through potlatch, sacrifice, festival, war, or luxury) rather than reinvested — because there is nowhere else for it to go. The note "governing-by-confusion" already invokes this directly, arguing that under cybernetic capitalism "explanatory strain replaces the sacrificial economy" and "what must be burned now is sense-making itself." Bataille's book is the source of that move and grounds the topic overview's own claim that "the accursed share is not surplus energy but surplus sense-making" — the corpus keeps Bataille's structure (a system organized around compulsory, non-recuperable expenditure) while substituting interpretive labor for Bataille's own candidates (ritual, war, spectacle) as the thing that must be burned.

## What it does not settle

Bataille's own examples of accursed-share expenditure are drawn from Aztec sacrifice, medieval cathedral-building, the Marshall Plan, and Soviet industrialization — a general theory of political economy across historical civilizations, not a theory of information, attention, or interpretation. He gives no account of "sense-making" as a substance that can be produced, hoarded, or burned, and nothing in the text anticipates a cybernetic system that manufactures its own surplus explanatory demand as a mode of governance; that substitution of interpretive load for Bataille's energetic surplus is the corpus's synthesis, not a claim Bataille makes or a case he analyzes. Readers should also note this is a translated title cited by its well-known English rendering; the exact original 1949 Éditions de Minuit printing history and pagination are not verified here.
