## Why this is central

Santos supplies "epistemicide" — the destruction of a knowledge system together with the people who carry it, as one event rather than two — which the note needs to name the refusal to use available tools for language preservation as itself a form of violence, rather than a neutral or cautious inaction. Santos's broader call for an "ecology of knowledges" also underwrites the note's claim that treating Western-style purity norms as the only legitimate preservation method is itself a colonizing epistemic stance.

## What it does not settle

Santos's program argues for pluralism among knowledge systems and against any single epistemology sitting in judgment over the rest; it does not itself decide whether using a commercial, scraped-data LLM to preserve a language is an act against epistemicide or a further instance of it. A commercial model trained on unconsented extraction from marginalized language data could equally be read, on Santos's own terms, as an epistemically violent appropriation — the note's claim that refusal is "itself an act of epistemicide" states one side of a tension Santos's framework does not resolve on its own.
