Cybernetic postliberalism is emsenn's original theoretical framework, developed through the letters-to-the-web and the research program that houses this library. It analyzes how late-liberal governance operates through cybernetic feedback loops rather than through ideological persuasion or direct coercion — how the system maintains itself by processing disruption as ordinary input rather than as crisis.

The framework's key concepts include industrial intellectualism (the conversion of thought into narrative products whose truth-value is determined by genre satisfaction rather than material correspondence), fascist grammar (the rhetorical structure that produces inevitability through temporal claims about destiny and sacrifice), and interpretive saturation (the condition in which the capacity to interpret is consumed faster than it can regenerate, making the accursed share not surplus energy but surplus sense-making).

Cybernetic postliberalism extends critical theory by taking seriously the cybernetic character of contemporary governance — the feedback loops, the self-correcting mechanisms, the processing of disruption as signal rather than as threat. It draws on Foucault's governmentality, Povinelli's late liberalism, Berlant's crisis ordinariness, and Bataille's general economy, but synthesizes them through the specific claim that the governing mechanism is cybernetic: the system does not repress dissent, it processes it.

## Boundary conditions

Everything this corpus has so far used as evidence is Californian: settler-frontier mythology, Hollywood's image-production infrastructure, Silicon Valley's capital concentration, and the Palo Alto fusion of cybernetics with the counterculture ([Turner 2006](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/turner2006.md); [Barbrook 1996](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/barbrook1996.md)). Of these four, three are local material and one is a load-bearing component of the mechanism. Frontier mythology supplies the specific content of the destiny-claim in fascist grammar, but every nationalism has a destiny-claim; the frontier is a variable, not the function. Hollywood and Silicon Valley supply the apparatus and the capital that make narrative production the dominant intellectual industry, but industrial intellectualism requires only *some* apparatus whose output is judged by genre satisfaction rather than correspondence, and a party press, a state broadcaster, or a short-video platform will do. Cybernetics itself is not Californian either: [Beer 1972](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/beer1972.md) built Cybersyn for Allende, the Soviet Union had OGAS, and China had Qian Xuesen's engineering cybernetics before Stewart Brand had a catalog. What is genuinely Californian, and not décor, is the fusion Turner documents: it manufactured the affective alibi under which being processed feels like participating, and that alibi is what lets the loop run without dissenters experiencing themselves as inputs. So the portable mechanism is affective governance that metabolizes structural contradiction under legitimacy exhaustion — the system substitutes processing for persuasion once persuasion is spent — and it has three conditions: a legitimacy claim that is exhausted while its institutions persist; an interpretive apparatus whose capacity, rather than the state's concession budget, is the scarce resource dissent consumes; and a locally produced alibi that codes processing as participation. California is where the third condition was met in its euphoric form, and the corpus has been describing that form as if it were the mechanism.

The strongest objection has two horns. First: the portable core is not new. Absorption of opposition is [Gramsci 1971](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/gramsci1971.md)'s trasformismo, neutralization-by-permission is [Marcuse 1964](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/marcuse1964.md)'s repressive tolerance, and self-referential processing of environment as information is [Luhmann 1984](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/luhmann1984.md)'s autopoiesis; strip the Californian material and the framework is a regional ethnography in general clothes. Second: China already processes dissent as signal — the petition system, the responsiveness apparatus, social credit — with no liberalism to be post to, none of the four Californian features, and its own cybernetics lineage. Either the mechanism applies there, in which case "postliberalism" is a misnomer and legitimacy exhaustion is not a condition, or it does not, and the framework is Californian all the way down. The answer to the first horn is that the mechanism is distinguishable from each predecessor by what dissent costs the system. In trasformismo absorption is paid for: the opposition is granted something, and the concession budget is the binding constraint. In repressive tolerance the outcome still depends on the content of what is permitted. Autopoiesis has no exhaustion condition at all, because a system that never grounded itself in legitimacy cannot lose it. Cybernetic postliberalism names the case where dissent costs the system nothing distributively and instead feeds it, because the good it consumes is interpretive capacity, and where this substitution occurs *because* a prior legitimacy claim is spent; that is why the accursed share ([Bataille 1949](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/bataille1949.md)) reappears here as surplus sense-making rather than surplus goods. The diagnostic separating the framework from its predecessors is therefore empirical: does dissent extract concessions or consume interpretation? The answer to the second horn is that China is at present a non-instance, not a counter-case. Its responsiveness apparatus processes grievance as signal *and pays for it* — local complaints receive material redress precisely so that they do not aggregate — and its legitimacy claim is performance, not consent, and has not been exhausted. The framework therefore predicts that the mechanism appears in China when performance legitimacy fails while the party's institutions persist, with different local material: short-video platforms in place of Hollywood, national-rejuvenation temporality in place of the frontier, the engineering-cybernetics tradition in place of the counterculture fusion. In that case "postliberalism" becomes a type-specimen name, as Fordism names a regime and not a factory, and the misnomer is the price of the generality.

The evidence that the mechanism is already portable exists and the corpus has not used it. [Fisher 2009](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/fisher2009.md) describes, in a Britain with none of the four Californian features, the pre-absorption of alternatives and the consumption of critique as content; [Han 2015](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/han2015.md), writing from Berlin out of Korea, arrives at seductive rather than repressive power with no Californian material at all. What varies across these cases is the affective register — Barbrook's euphoria, Fisher's depression, Han's exhaustion — and register is exactly what local material should be expected to account for, and nothing more. The position taken here is that the mechanism is portable, the register is local, and the framework's definition should drop California from the mechanism and retain it only as the type-specimen. This is falsifiable in two directions. If a case is found with all four Californian features in which dissent still extracts distributive concessions, the framework has misidentified a hegemonic mechanism as a cybernetic one. If interpretive saturation is found only where the Palo Alto fusion is present, the alibi is not one local realization of a general condition but the mechanism itself, and the framework is Californian after all. Absent either finding, the framework holds as a general theory whose first and best-documented instance happened to be built on the Pacific coast.
