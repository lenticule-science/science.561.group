## Why this is central

The note states directly: "Calvin's *Institutes of the Christian Religion* (1559) added the mechanism that made this compulsive. Calvin's predestination doctrine holds that God has already chosen who is saved and who is damned. The individual cannot change this and cannot know the verdict... The Calvinist works compulsively because stopping is indistinguishable from damnation." This supplies the "produce election anxiety" step of the note's Measurement table (§5), the mechanism the note needs between Luther's sacralized labor and Weber's capitalist work ethic.

## What it does not settle

The *Institutes* went through several editions between 1536 and the definitive 1559 Latin edition the note cites, without the note naming a particular English translation — consistent with its own "various editions" field. Calvin's text argues the theological doctrine of predestination itself; the note's (and, following it, Weber's) reading of that doctrine as producing psychological "election anxiety" channeled into compulsive productivity is an interpretive claim about the doctrine's effects, not a thesis the *Institutes* states in those terms.
