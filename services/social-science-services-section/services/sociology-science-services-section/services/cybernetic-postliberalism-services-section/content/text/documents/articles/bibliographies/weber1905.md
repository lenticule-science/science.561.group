## Why this is central

The note states: "Max Weber's *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism* (1905) traces this mechanism into modern capitalism: Calvinist election-anxiety, channeled through compulsive productive labor, produces the work ethic that drives accumulation. Han's achievement-subject is the endpoint." Weber supplies the note's explicit bridge from Calvin's theological doctrine to secular capitalist compulsion; the note situates Weber as the pivot from ecclesial to secular in its own chronology ("The Church had 800 years of clinical experience with this condition before Weber traced its secular descendant into capitalism").

## What it does not settle

Weber's original German essay appeared in two parts, in 1904 and 1905, in the *Archiv für Sozialwissenschaft und Sozialpolitik*; the commonly cited English edition (translated by Talcott Parsons, reprinted by various publishers including Routledge) postdates the original by decades. The note's single year (1905) and publisher (Routledge) name a specific modern edition rather than the original serial publication — a simplification already present in the note's own `references:` map that this carrier does not resolve. Weber's own thesis concerns an elective affinity between Protestant asceticism and the spirit of capitalism generally; it does not itself mention monastic acedia, Aquinas, or Han — the note's placement of Weber as the midpoint between Calvin and Han is the note's own construction.
