Geontologies, as theorized by [Elizabeth Povinelli](../../../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/elizabeth-povinelli.md) in *Geontologies: A Requiem to Late Liberalism* (2016), names the governance of the distinction between Life and Nonlife — the [ontological](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/science/theories/information/terms/ontology.md) division that undergirds late liberal power. Where [biopolitics](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/abolition-services-section/services/state-repression-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/biopolitics.md) governs through the management of living populations, geontopower governs through the maintenance of the boundary between what is alive and what is inert, what counts as a living being with interests and what counts as mere matter available for use.

[Povinelli](../../../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/elizabeth-povinelli.md) argues that the Life/Nonlife distinction is the foundational [ontological](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/science/theories/information/terms/ontology.md) commitment of [late liberalism](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/povinellian/terms/late-liberalism.md) — more fundamental than the divisions between human and animal, civilized and savage, or normal and pathological that structure earlier forms of governance. Late liberalism governs by sorting everything into Life (which can suffer, which has interests, which demands ethical consideration) and Nonlife (which cannot suffer, which has no interests, which is available for extraction). The classification of a river, a forest, or a geological formation as Nonlife — as resource rather than relation — is a geontological act that enables [extractivism](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/science/theories/ecology/disciplines/climate/terms/extractivism.md).

This framework connects directly to Indigenous ontologies that do not draw the Life/Nonlife distinction where Western [metaphysics](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/philosophy/theories/western/theories/metaphysics/terms/metaphysics.md) draws it. When [Vine Deloria Jr.](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/vine-deloria-jr.md) insists on place-based knowledge, or when Indigenous legal traditions recognize the personhood of rivers and mountains, they challenge not merely Western [property](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/anarchism/terms/property.md) law but the geontological framework that makes [property](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/anarchism/terms/property.md) law possible. The geontological critique reveals that [relational ontology](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/philosophy/theories/western/theories/metaphysics/theories/ontology/terms/relational-ontology.md) — the position that relations are ontologically prior to the entities they relate — is not merely a philosophical alternative but a political challenge to the form of power that governs through the Life/Nonlife distinction.

Geontologies names the governance of the distinction between Life and Nonlife — the [ontological](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/science/theories/information/terms/ontology.md) division that structures late liberal power. [Elizabeth Povinelli](../../../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/elizabeth-povinelli.md) develops the concept in *Geontologies: A Requiem to Late Liberalism* (2016) to identify what she argues is the more fundamental operation beneath [biopolitics](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/biopolitics/terms/biopolitics.md): not the management of living populations but the prior determination of what counts as alive at all.

The concept works through three figures — the Desert, the Animist, and the Virus — each of which represents a threat to the Life/Nonlife boundary that late liberalism must manage. The Desert is the figure of Nonlife threatening to overwhelm Life (ecological collapse, resource exhaustion). The Animist is the figure that refuses the distinction altogether — that insists rocks, rivers, and ancestors are alive and agentive. The Virus is the figure that crosses the boundary, neither fully alive nor fully inert, disrupting the classification from within.

These are not merely philosophical categories. They name the concrete mechanisms through which [settler-colonial](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/2026-06-17-emsemioverse-terms-index.md) governance classifies land, water, and geological formations as inert matter available for extraction. When Indigenous ontologies recognize the personhood of a river or the agency of a landscape, they do not merely disagree with Western [metaphysics](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/philosophy/theories/western/theories/metaphysics/terms/metaphysics.md) — they challenge the geontological infrastructure that makes [property](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/anarchism/terms/property.md) law, [extractivism](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/science/theories/ecology/disciplines/climate/terms/extractivism.md), and resource governance possible.

Geontologies thus extends [Foucault](../../../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/michel-foucault.md)'s analysis of [biopower](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/foucauldian/terms/biopower.md) by showing that the governance of Life presupposes a prior governance of the Life/Nonlife distinction itself. Biopolitics asks: how are living populations managed? Geontologies asks: how is the category of "living" produced and maintained in the first place?
