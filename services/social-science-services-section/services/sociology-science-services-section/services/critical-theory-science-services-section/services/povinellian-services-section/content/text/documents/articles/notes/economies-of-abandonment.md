Audience: readers who have completed the [geontologies lesson](./geontologies.md).

Learning goal: describe how late liberal governance operates through abandonment and how endurance functions as a political practice.

[Povinelli](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/elizabeth-povinelli.md) argues that [late liberalism](../../../glossary/terms/late-liberalism.md) governs not primarily through direct oppression but through withdrawal. [Economies of abandonment](../../../glossary/terms/economies-of-abandonment.md) names the process by which the state assesses populations and places, classifies them as viable or unviable, and withdraws resources from those it deems unviable — while maintaining the procedural appearance of care.

Abandonment is self-reinforcing: the community deprived of infrastructure appears to lack the capacity for self-governance; the population denied resources appears to have failed to thrive. The withdrawal produces the conditions that retroactively justify the withdrawal.

[Endurance](../../../glossary/terms/endurance.md) is the political practice that responds to abandonment — not heroic resistance but the ongoing work of persisting in places and practices that have been declared obsolete. Endurance does not promise transformation. It names the political significance of refusing to disappear on the schedule that governance has set.
