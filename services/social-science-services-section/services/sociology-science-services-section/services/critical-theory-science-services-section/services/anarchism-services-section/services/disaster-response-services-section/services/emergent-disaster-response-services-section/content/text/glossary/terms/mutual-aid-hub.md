A mutual aid hub is a decentralized coordination site where volunteers,
supplies, information, and care practices are gathered, matched, and
redistributed [@landau2022; @ambinder2013].

Within emergent disaster response, hubs matter because they turn
convergence into usable collective capacity. They can function at once
as intake points, kitchens, sorting sites, canvassing bases, dispatch
centers, and places where local assessments are turned into action
[@watters2014; @landau2022].

A hub is not defined only by storage or administration. It is also a
relational form. It concentrates trust, attention, and coordination
without requiring a fully centralized command structure.
