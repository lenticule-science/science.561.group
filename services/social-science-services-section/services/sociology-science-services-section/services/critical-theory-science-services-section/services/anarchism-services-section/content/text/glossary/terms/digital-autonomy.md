Digital autonomy is the extension of the autonomist tradition's commitment to self-organized, non-hierarchical struggle into the domain of computer networks: building and governing communication infrastructure according to the same principles of self-management and refusal of external control that autonomia applied to factory and territorial organizing.

Digital autonomy is distinguished from a general commitment to open-source or decentralized technology by its specific lineage: Wright's history of Italian autonomist Marxism traces the operaista and autonomia tradition's central concept of class composition — the specific, situated form a struggle takes given its material and organizational conditions — from the factory councils of the 1950s and 1960s through the wider social and territorial autonomia of the 1970s (Wright 2002); this tradition fed directly into the Italian hacktivist networks of the late 1980s and 1990s, including the European Counter Network and its Isole nella Rete server, that in turn produced Autistici/Inventati in 2001, a lineage the collective's own retrospective documents as continuous political practice rather than a change of terrain from territorial to digital struggle (Autistici/Inventati 2012).

The consequence of this lineage is that digital autonomy names a claim about governance and composition, not about which specific technologies are used: a digital-autonomy project can in principle use mainstream or purist tooling, so long as its governance remains self-managed and its composition remains answerable to the movement it serves rather than to a state or commercial funder — the same governance criterion that distinguishes a tech collective from a commercial platform.
