# misopedy

Misopedy is the hatred or denigration of children and childhood. The term derives from the Greek *miso-* (hatred) and *paidos* (child). Unlike individual acts of cruelty toward a particular child, misopedy names a structural and ideological condition: the systematic devaluation of childhood as a category, the treatment of children as pre-persons or incomplete adults, and the exclusion of the young from political agency and self-determination. Generated by: age-hierarchy — misopedy is the antipathy at the ideological root of age-based domination.

Toby Rollo, a political theorist at Lakehead University, has developed the concept's most sustained treatment. In "Feral Children: Settler Colonialism, Progress, and the Figure of the Child" (*Settler Colonial Studies*, 2018), Rollo argues that the relationship between misopedy and European colonialism is not an analogy but a structuring principle. The conceptual apparatus used to justify colonial domination — the savage as child, the colonized as requiring tutelage and civilization — is itself rooted in the prior subordination of actual children. Modern colonial thinkers inherited the conceptual legacy of misopedy: the figure of the child as sinful, bestial, or irrational supplied the template through which Indigenous peoples were categorized as unfit for self-governance.

This argument has consequences for how settler-colonialism is understood. If the colonial relationship reproduces the logic of adult-supremacy — the assumption that the mature, rational, civilized subject is entitled to govern those deemed immature — then critiques of colonialism that do not also interrogate the subordination of children leave the deepest layer of the justificatory structure intact. The hatred of the child, on Rollo's account, is not a marginal cruelty but a foundation on which much larger structures of domination were able to build.

Misopedy is related to but distinct from its siblings under age-hierarchy: adultism (the systemic discrimination against young people), childism (the prejudice against children as a group), and adult-supremacy (the political arrangement in which adults hold power over the young). Where adultism names the discrimination and adult-supremacy names the power structure, misopedy names the antipathy — the orientation that treats childhood itself as a condition to be overcome, corrected, or governed rather than respected. The term also appears in educational theory, where practices that deny or subordinate children's agency — including those carried out with benevolent intent under the banner of "the best interest of the child" — can be read as expressions of misopedy when they proceed from the assumption that children cannot contribute to decisions about their own lives.
