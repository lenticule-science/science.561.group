Domination is the structural condition in which one party's capacity for self-determination is systematically constrained by another's exercise of power. It is not a single act of force but a sustained arrangement — a relationship maintained through [hierarchy](./hierarchy.md), [authority](./authority.md), [coercion](./coercion.md), dependence, or the organization of material and social life so that refusal is punished and compliance is compelled. [Anarchism](../index.md) defines itself as opposition to domination in all its forms.

The breadth of this definition is the point. Liberalism opposes specific forms of domination — tyranny, censorship, certain kinds of discrimination — while accepting others as legitimate or necessary: the employer's authority over the worker, the state's monopoly on violence, the property owner's right to exclude. [Marxism](../../marxism/index.md) centers the domination of capital over [labor](./labor.md) but accepts [the state](./the-state.md) as a transitional instrument. Anarchism refuses these exceptions. The claim is that domination is the problem — not this or that particular kind, but the structural relationship itself, wherever it appears.

This produces a distinctive analytical habit: anarchism reads across domains. The domination of [the state](./the-state.md) over its subjects, the employer over the worker, the colonizer over the colonized, the man over the woman, the adult over the child, the human over the nonhuman — these are not separate problems requiring separate theories but instances of the same structural relationship. [Green anarchism](./green-anarchism.md) holds that ecological destruction and social domination share a root. [Youth liberation](../schools/youth-liberation/index.md) holds that age-based hierarchy is continuous with other forms. [Total liberation](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/total-liberation.md) names the convergence: the liberation of any one group is incomplete without the liberation of all, because domination is a structure that connects them.

Domination is not the same as power. People exercise power in every relationship — influence, persuasion, coordination, care. Domination is the subset of power that operates through fixed [hierarchy](./hierarchy.md): where positions are arranged vertically, where the relationship is not mutual, and where the subordinated party cannot exit or refuse without consequences imposed by the dominant party. This is why [voluntary association](./voluntary-association.md) is the foundational anarchist principle: a relationship you can leave without punishment is a relationship that must sustain itself through mutual benefit rather than through domination.
