The centros de apoyo mutuo that emerged in Puerto Rico after Hurricanes
Irma and Maria are one of the clearest examples of emergent disaster
response becoming territorial, autonomous, and explicitly political
[@camcoconspirators2019; @cambuilding2020]. Faced with state collapse,
FEMA abuse, and colonial abandonment, people organized self-managed
spaces to meet urgent needs while building durable community power.

## Self-managed spaces

Mutual Aid Disaster Relief's CAM materials describe these centers as
self-managed spaces created around the island after Maria
[@camcoconspirators2019]. This matters because the centers were not only
distribution points. They were institutional forms through which
communities could organize themselves rather than wait to be governed.

## Aid linked to justice and political discussion

The `Building Power in Paradise` account makes this current especially
clear. CAMBU in Bucarabones is described not just as a relief site but
as a project rooted in social, economic, cultural, and environmental
justice [@cambuilding2020]. The centers hosted community kitchens,
classes, workshops, cultural activities, and communal space while also
serving immediate survival needs.

## Seizing and defending space

`This is Caguas` emphasizes another distinguishing feature: some of
these centers involved reclaiming and defending space itself
[@caguas2017]. In Caguas, a building was seized and defended by the
community, then turned into a site for water infrastructure, mutual aid,
and collective use. This adds a community-defense dimension to emergent
disaster response that goes beyond service provision alone.

## Infrastructure for future storms

The `Solar Power to the People` report shows that CAM-related efforts
also built energy infrastructure meant to strengthen future resilience
[@solarpower2018]. Solar micro-grids, water systems, and shared
facilities linked present disaster response to longer-term autonomous
capacity. This makes the CAM current especially important for thinking
about disaster response as institution building.

## Significance

The centros de apoyo mutuo matter because they make visible a distinct
political current within emergent disaster response. Disaster relief is
organized here as self-management, territorial autonomy, and community
infrastructure, not just as volunteer service or temporary solidarity.
