Paranoia as affect is Sedgwick's name for a hermeneutic stance, not merely a clinical disposition: an anticipatory, self-confirming affective posture toward a text, a system, or a social situation that organizes inquiry around exposing a hidden threat or hidden violence before it can happen, treating surprise itself as the one risk that must be foreclosed at any cost (Sedgwick 2003).

Sedgwick distinguishes paranoid reading from reparative reading: paranoid reading is "strong theory," faster than the phenomena it accounts for and confirmed by any outcome including its own negation, while reparative reading is "weak theory," localized, non-totalizing, and oriented toward assembling resources for survival and pleasure rather than toward exposure (Sedgwick 2003); the two are not opposed methods a reader must choose once and for all but stances that can interdigitate within the same practice.

The consequence for a security-conscious community is that a security culture practiced as paranoid affect — treating every gap in disclosure as itself evidence of threat, and every ordinary uncertainty as intolerable — reproduces the totalizing, self-confirming structure Sedgwick describes, whereas a security culture calibrated to a stated threat model can hold genuine uncertainty without converting it into free-floating suspicion of the community's own members.
