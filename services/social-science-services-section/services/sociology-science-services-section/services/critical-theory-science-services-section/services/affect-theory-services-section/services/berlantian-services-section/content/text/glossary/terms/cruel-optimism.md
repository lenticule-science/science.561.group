Cruel optimism is a concept developed by [Lauren Berlant](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/lauren-berlant.md) to name the condition of being attached to something — a fantasy, a relationship, a political project, an institution — that actively impedes the flourishing it promises. The optimism is cruel not because the subject is foolish but because the attachment itself is the obstacle. The good life, the stable career, the inclusive institution, the reformed state: these are objects of desire whose pursuit sustains the conditions that prevent their realization.

[Berlant](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/lauren-berlant.md) argues that cruel optimism is not a personal pathology but a structural condition of life under late [capitalism](./capitalism.md) and [late liberalism](../../../../../../../povinellian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/late-liberalism.md). The fantasy of upward mobility sustains a [labor](../../../../../../../anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md) market that destroys the conditions for it. The fantasy of inclusion sustains institutions whose function is exclusion. The fantasy of reform sustains a political order that absorbs every reform into its own reproduction.

The concept operates in the space between affect and structure. [Berlant](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/lauren-berlant.md) shows that people do not maintain cruel attachments because they are deceived but because the attachment provides a sense of continuity — a way of inhabiting the present that feels like participation in a future, even when that future is structurally foreclosed. This is what makes the optimism cruel: it is not false hope but real attachment to conditions that are organized against the thing hoped for.

In emsenn's letters-to-the-web, cruel optimism names the condition of settler-facing politics: the attachment to recognition, reform, and institutional inclusion as paths to [decolonization](../../../../../../../povinellian-services-section/services/settler-colonialism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/decolonization.md) — when these paths are themselves mechanisms of [settler colonialism](./settler-colonialism.md)'s reproduction. It also describes the condition of the organic intellectual whose attachment to pedagogical [legibility](../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/abolition-services-section/services/state-repression-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/legibility.md) sustains the very formatting that neutralizes insurgent thought.
