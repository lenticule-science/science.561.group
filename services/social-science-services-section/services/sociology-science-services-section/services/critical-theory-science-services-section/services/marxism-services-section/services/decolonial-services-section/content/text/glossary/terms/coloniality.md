Coloniality is a decolonial term for the way colonial domination persists even when formal colonial rule ends. Where [colonialism](/sociology/terms/colonialism.md) names direct conquest and administration, coloniality names the ongoing organization of power through racial hierarchy, extraction, legal regimes, and epistemic authority.

In a Marxist register, coloniality matters because it marks limits of accounts that treat dispossession as merely a prehistory of capitalism. It insists that the colonial order is reproduced in the present: in the governance of land, in who is made available for exploitation, and in which forms of knowledge count as "universal."

In settler contexts, coloniality often appears through [settler colonialism](/sociology/terms/settler-colonialism.md): a structure oriented toward the elimination of Indigenous political presence and the permanent occupation of land. This is why decolonial critiques treat [decolonization](../../../../../../../povinellian-services-section/services/settler-colonialism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/decolonization.md) as irreducible to reform or redistribution.
