Alienation describes the estrangement that arises when people lose connection to their [labor](../../../../../../../content/glossary/terms/labor.md), its products, their own capacities, and each other. [Karl Marx](../../../../../../../../../references/people/karl-marx.md) developed the concept in his *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844*, identifying four dimensions: alienation from the product of [labor](../../../../../../../content/glossary/terms/labor.md) (which belongs to the employer), from the activity of laboring (which is directed by another), from species-being (the human capacity for free creative activity), and from other people (who become competitors or instruments).

Under [wage labor](./wage-labor.md), workers do not decide what to make, how to make it, or what happens to what they make. The product confronts its maker as something alien — owned by someone else, sold for someone else's profit. The [labor](../../../../../../../content/glossary/terms/labor.md) process itself becomes a site of unfreedom rather than expression. [Marx](../../../../../../../../../references/people/karl-marx.md) held that this was not a psychological complaint but a structural feature of capitalist production.

The concept has limits. It centers [wage labor](./wage-labor.md) as the primary form of exploitation, which obscures other forms of dispossession — enslavement, land theft, unwaged reproductive [labor](../../../../../../../content/glossary/terms/labor.md) — that do not fit the wage relation. [Silvia Federici](../../../../../../../../../references/people/silvia-federici.md) and theorists of [social reproduction](./social-reproduction.md) have argued that alienation theory, taken alone, cannot account for the gendered and racialized [labor](../../../../../../../content/glossary/terms/labor.md) that sustains the wage system from outside it.

From the perspective of [relational ontology](../../../../../../philosophy/theories/western/theories/metaphysics/theories/ontology/terms/relational-ontology.md), alienation names a real severing of constitutive relations — between maker and made, between person and community, between [labor](../../../../../../../content/glossary/terms/labor.md) and purpose — imposed by a specific historical arrangement of production.
