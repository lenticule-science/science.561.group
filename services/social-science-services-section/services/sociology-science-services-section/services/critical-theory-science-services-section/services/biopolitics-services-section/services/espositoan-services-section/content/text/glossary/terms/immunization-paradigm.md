The immunization paradigm is Roberto Esposito's name for the logic by which a community protects life by incorporating a controlled dose of what threatens it. Vaccination is the paradigm case: a weakened form of the pathogen is introduced into the body to produce the resistance that protects it. Esposito, in *Immunitas* (2002), argues that modern political institutions — rights, property, sovereignty, the nation-state, quarantine, border control, deterrence — work by the same logic: the community immunizes itself by internalizing a regulated measure of the danger it fears, rather than by excluding that danger entirely.

The consequence Esposito draws is that immunization, pushed far enough, becomes auto-immune: the protective mechanism that requires the presence of the threat within the body it protects can grow so powerful that it destroys the very thing it was meant to protect, so that protection negates the life it protects. This is the pathological limit named [auto-immunity](./auto-immunity.md) — a sibling concept, not identical to the immunization paradigm itself: the immunization paradigm names the ordinary, functioning logic of protection-through-controlled-exposure, and auto-immunity names what happens when that logic intensifies past the point where it still protects.

The immunization paradigm is also distinguished from [immunitas](../../../../../../../../../../../../../natural-science-services-section/services/biological-science-services-section/services/medical-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/immunitas.md) as such: immunitas names the state of exemption from communal obligation that immunization produces for the protected individual, while the immunization paradigm names the operating logic — incorporate a controlled dose of the threat — by which that exemption is produced and maintained.
