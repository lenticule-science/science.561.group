Geontologies, as theorized by [Elizabeth Povinelli](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/elizabeth-povinelli.md) in *Geontologies: A Requiem to Late Liberalism* (2016), names the governance of the distinction between Life and Nonlife — the [ontological](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/metaphysics-services-section/services/ontology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ontology.md) division that undergirds late liberal power. Where [biopolitics](./biopolitics.md) governs through the management of living populations, geontopower governs through the maintenance of the boundary between what is alive and what is inert, what counts as a living being with interests and what counts as mere matter available for use.

[Povinelli](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/elizabeth-povinelli.md) argues that the Life/Nonlife distinction is the foundational [ontological](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/metaphysics-services-section/services/ontology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ontology.md) commitment of [late liberalism](../../../../../povinellian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/late-liberalism.md) — more fundamental than the divisions between human and animal, civilized and savage, or normal and pathological that structure earlier forms of governance. Late liberalism governs by sorting everything into Life (which can suffer, which has interests, which demands ethical consideration) and Nonlife (which cannot suffer, which has no interests, which is available for extraction). The classification of a river, a forest, or a geological formation as Nonlife — as resource rather than relation — is a geontological act that enables [extractivism](../../../../../../../../../biological-science-services-section/services/ecology-services-section/services/climate-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/extractivism.md).

This framework connects directly to Indigenous ontologies that do not draw the Life/Nonlife distinction where Western [metaphysics](./metaphysics.md) draws it. When [Vine Deloria Jr.](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/vine-deloria-jr.md) insists on place-based knowledge, or when Indigenous legal traditions recognize the personhood of rivers and mountains, they challenge not merely Western [property](../../sociology/terms/property.md) law but the geontological framework that makes [property](../../sociology/terms/property.md) law possible. The geontological critique reveals that [relational ontology](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/metaphysics-services-section/services/ontology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/relational-ontology.md) — the position that relations are ontologically prior to the entities they relate — is not merely a philosophical alternative but a political challenge to the form of power that governs through the Life/Nonlife distinction.
