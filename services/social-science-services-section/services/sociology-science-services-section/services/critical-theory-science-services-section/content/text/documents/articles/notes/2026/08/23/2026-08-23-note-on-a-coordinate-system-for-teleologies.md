## 0. Abstract and plan

Disputes among ideologies are ordinarily treated as disputes over positions — policies, values, visions.
This note argues they are better treated as differences of *address* in a shared coordinate space, and it constructs the space.

The construction is not a list but a **generating principle**:
every axis of the space is one question — *how settled is it? can it be settled? should it be?* — asked of a different object.
Ask it of the world-process and the axis is **stationarity**; of position, **ergodicity**; of truth, **determinacy**; of change, **reversibility**; of conflict, **resolvability**; of value, **value-locus**; of the trajectory's form, **temporal shape**; of time, **tense of the good**; of agency, **efficacy**; of the ought, **warrant** — with **legibility** as a candidate eleventh.
An actor's teleology — call it the actor's **bearing** — is then specified by *two* vectors over the axes:
**settings** (what the actor believes is the case on each axis) and **weightings** (how much each axis matters to the actor, and which pole is prized).
The two-vector structure is load-bearing: phenomena that no single-vector account can express — Gramsci's "pessimism of the intellect, optimism of the will"; the conservative and the revolutionary sharing a setting while weighting it oppositely — are its direct evidence.

## 1. The seed axes, and their documented ideological character

The construction begins from two concepts usually filed under statistics, whose ideological load is a matter of record.

**Stationarity** — the assumption that a process's generating rules are time-invariant, so that past statistics bind the future — is the silent foundation of actuarial reasoning, infrastructure design, and institutional planning; and its collapse is now official in at least one field:
the landmark declaration in water management — "Stationarity is dead" (Milly et al., *Science*, 2008) — announced that anthropogenic climate change had invalidated the assumption under which a century of hydrological engineering was designed.
The announcement is an epistemic event of general significance:
a civilization's operating assumption about the *settledness of the process itself* failed, publicly, in a domain with mortal stakes —
and the divergent responses to that failure (denial, doom, adaptation, transformation) are visibly not statistical positions but *teleological* ones.
Knight's old distinction between risk and uncertainty (1921), and Keynes's insistence on irreducible uncertainty, mark the same axis in economics:
whether the future is a lottery with knowable odds or something the past does not price.

**Ergodicity** — the assumption that time-averages equal ensemble-averages, so that one actor's trajectory through time samples the same distribution as the population at an instant — carries even more direct political freight.
Peters ("The ergodicity problem in economics," *Nature Physics*, 2019) showed that standard expected-utility reasoning silently assumes ergodicity, and that for multiplicative dynamics (wealth, ruin) the assumption is simply false: the individual's time-average diverges from the ensemble-average, and an entire moral vocabulary — "on average, everyone gains" — is built on the conflation.
North (2005) had already drawn the institutional consequence: "we live in a non-ergodic world," in which historical paths do not resample and institutional prescriptions do not transplant.
And standpoint epistemology (Hartsock; Harding) is, in this vocabulary, a *non-ergodicity claim about knowledge*:
social positions are not interchangeable sampling stations; some things are visible only from particular trajectories.
Meritocratic ideology, conversely, is applied ergodicity: the ensemble's success statistics offered to each individual as their own prospective distribution.

Two lessons generalize from the seeds.
First: assumptions of this type are *settings* — beliefs about how settled some structural feature of the world is — and they are ideologically load-bearing precisely while presenting as technical.
Second: they are truth-apt and domain-relative — stationarity really is dead in hydrology and really does hold for planetary orbits — so the space under construction is not a relativism; some settings are simply wrong for given domains, and part of politics is the fight over which domain we are in.

## 2. The generating principle

The seeds share a form: each is the question of settledness asked of one object (the process; the position).
The principle, stated once:

**For each structural object O of an actor's world-model, the question "how settled is O — how settled can it become, and how settled should it be?" generates an axis; the space of teleologies is the product of these axes.**

The principle does three jobs a mere list cannot.
It *derives* the axes rather than collecting them, so the inventory is principled and its completeness is discussable (any object admitting degrees of settledness spawns a candidate axis).
It explains why the axes feel akin despite ranging over metaphysics, epistemology, and politics — they are one question refracted.
And it builds in, from the start, the distinction §5 develops: "how settled is it" (a setting) and "how settled should it be" (a weighting) are different questions about the same axis, and an actor answers both.

## 3. The axes

**Axis 1 — Stationarity** *(the process)*. §1. Poles: the rules hold ↔ the rules are changing.
The weighting side has its own classic literature: Oakeshott's conservatism ("to prefer the familiar to the unknown, the tried to the untried") is not primarily a stationarity *belief* but a stationarity *preference* — a weighting on the axis, articulable independently of the setting.

**Axis 2 — Ergodicity** *(position)*. §1. Poles: trajectories interchangeable ↔ paths irreducibly distinct.
Path-dependence economics (David; Arthur) and ruin-centered ethics occupy the low pole with formal apparatus; the "view from nowhere" and universalist proceduralism occupy the high pole, usually without noticing they have taken a position.

**Axis 3 — Determinacy** *(truth)*. Is truth already settled — is the open genuinely open?
The axis is ancient and was formative for logic itself: Aristotle's sea-battle argument (*De Interpretatione* 9) over future contingents is the founding dispute, and Łukasiewicz's three-valued logic (1920) was invented explicitly to give the open future a truth-value — many-valued logic originating as a *metaphysical position on this axis*.
Poles: all is settled somewhere (fatalisms, Platonisms, completed-science imaginaries) ↔ the undetermined is real (fallibilism, process thought, open-future views).
Structured intermediate positions are common and important: classical theism holds high determinacy at the divine register with low determinacy at the creaturely one — a two-level setting that one-dimensional readings misclassify.

**Axis 4 — Reversibility** *(change)*. Can settlements be reopened?
Arendt (*The Human Condition*, 1958) made the axis anthropological: action's two predicaments are irreversibility and unpredictability, and the human faculties answering them are *forgiving* and *promising* — a direct pairing of remedies to axes 4 and 1 respectively.
Jonas (*The Imperative of Responsibility*, 1979) built an entire ethics for the low pole: technological civilization's novel capacity for irreversible harm demands a precautionary teleology.
Poles: the undoable world (experimentalism, restorative justice, markets-as-self-correction) ↔ the world of no return (tragedy, precaution, conservation).

**Axis 5 — Resolvability** *(conflict)*. Can all contradictions be discharged in principle?
The high pole is consensus liberalism and dialectical synthesis — Habermas's discourse ethics presumes conflicts are in principle adjudicable under ideal speech.
The low pole holds Berlin's value pluralism (genuinely incommensurable goods; tragic choice as permanent) and Mouffe's agonism, whose critique of deliberative democracy is precisely that the political is constitutively conflictual — that a residue of contestation is not a failure of procedure but the human condition.
The axis also carries constitutional consequences: traditions that *institutionalize* undischargeable difference (licensed contraries, permanent oppositions, protected dissent offices) are low-resolvability positions with engineering attached.

**Axis 6 — Value-locus** *(worth)*. Does value live in settled stock or in circulation?
Poles: the holding (property, accumulation, attained states) ↔ the flowing (gift, process, relation).
The gift literature (Mauss; Hyde) documents whole economies on the flow pole; enclosure history and the commons literature (Ostrom) record the political violence of forcing one pole's regime onto the other's goods; and much economic ideology is a proxy war on this axis conducted in the vocabulary of others.

**Axis 7 — Temporal shape** *(the trajectory's form)*. What shape does the good history have?
Four principal settings:
**point-terminal** — history ends in a settled state; Löwith (*Meaning in History*, 1949) showed modern progress-narratives to be secularized eschatology, the Kingdom re-dated, and the terminus is always rest;
**return-cyclical** — Eliade's archaic ontology of eternal return; golden-age restorationism;
**shapeless line** — sequence without form or completion: Benjamin's "homogeneous, empty time" as the target of his theses, and the burnout temporality diagnosed by Han as dyschronia — notably *not* the nihilist's shape but the exhausted late-modern default;
**helical** — directional recurrence, return-with-difference: the under-theorized fourth setting, gestured at from Vico's *corsi e ricorsi* to spiral readings of dialectic, in which the cycle accumulates without terminating.
The shapes are teleology's deepest grammar: most other axes inflect *within* a shape.

**Axis 8 — Tense of the good** *(time)*. When does the good obtain — past (restoration), deferred future (the promissory), or present?
Distinct from shape: a helix can be lived present-tense (each turn is the point) or deferred (the spiral as ladder).
Benjamin's *Jetztzeit* — now-time, the messianic present that blasts open the continuum — is the great theoretical statement of present-tense militancy against both the deferralists and the restorationists.

**Axis 9 — Efficacy** *(agency)*. Can actors at my scale move settlement, and which actors?
The structure–agency literature is this axis's academic residence; its political settings run from fatalism through heroic individualism (the founder, the great man — against which Tolstoy's second epilogue remains the classic broadside) to collective and distributed conceptions.
Efficacy crosses the other axes freely and generates the recognizably distinct militancies: high-efficacy with point-terminal shape yields vanguardism; high-efficacy with present tense yields direct action; low-efficacy with high determinacy yields quietism.

**Axis 10 — Warrant** *(the ought)*. What grounds normativity?
Three stable settings recur across every register of the tradition:
**given** (divine command, natural law, transcendent value);
**made** (constructivism, existentialist self-legislation, will);
**standing** (relational: obligation grounded in actually existing relations — the kinship-centered accounts of the Indigenous philosophical literature (Deloria; Wilson), and the care-ethics tradition's relational obligation).
The trichotomy's recurrence across theology, metaethics, and political theory marks it as an axis rather than a local dispute.

**Candidate Axis 11 — Legibility** *(knowledge's capture)*. How much of what is settled can be captured in representation?
Distinct from determinacy: the mystic combines high determinacy with low legibility; high-modernist planning combines high settings on both, which is Scott's indictment (*Seeing Like a State*, 1998) — and Hayek's knowledge-problem argument (1945) occupies the low-legibility pole from the market right, demonstrating that the axis crosses the conventional spectrum.

## 4. The bearing, defined, with exemplars

**Definition.**
An actor's **bearing** is the pair (S, W):
**S**, the settings vector — the actor's belief, per axis, about how the world is;
**W**, the weightings vector — per axis, how much it matters to the actor and which pole is valued.
Ideologies, in this vocabulary, are *crowded neighborhoods*: historically stabilized regions of the space with names, canons, and recruitment; an individual actor's bearing may sit inside one, between several, or at an address no tradition occupies.

Exemplar mappings (compressed; settings unless noted):

| Bearing | 1 Stat. | 2 Ergod. | 3 Determ. | 5 Resolv. | 6 Value | 7 Shape | 8 Tense | 9 Efficacy | 10 Warrant |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Promissory revolutionary socialism | low (rupture) | high (class position transcended at the end) | high (science of history) | high (synthesis) | stock-at-the-end | point-terminal | deferred | collective | made |
| Liberal proceduralism | high | high | moderate | high (adjudication) | stock | shapeless-ascending | mildly deferred | individual-institutional | made |
| Burkean conservatism | high (valued, W) | low-ish | moderate | low | stock-inherited | slow helix read as continuity | past-leaning | low individual | given |
| Anarcho-nihilism (the *Desert* current) | low | low | low | low | flow | no terminus; present recurrence | present | local-distributed | made |
| Achievement-society subject (Han's diagnosis) | high (market as nature) | high (anyone can) | high | high | stock-as-self | shapeless line | perpetually deferred-by-a-step | hyper-individual | made-by-self |
| Relational-Indigenous bearing (as per the published literature) | low (adaptation expected) | low (place and path matter) | low locally | low (held contraries honored) | flow | helical | present | distributed | standing |

The table is illustrative, not census-grade; each row is defensible from the named traditions' canonical texts and each is falsifiable against them — which is the point: the mapping is *checkable*, position by position, in a way that spectrum-talk never is.
Two observations the table makes visible:
the last row is a fully coherent address that the standard ideology-names do not cover — traditions occupy it, but the Euro-American label-set has no word for it, which is a fact about the label-set;
and Han's diagnosed subject differs from liberal proceduralism *only* in intensification and in shape — the same neighborhood, lived at burnout tempo — which matches his account of continuity between liberalism and its neoliberal radicalization.

## 5. Settings and weightings: why two vectors

The framework's most consequential structural choice is the (S, W) pair, and the evidence for it is that famous political phenomena are *divergences between the vectors*:

- **Gramsci's motto** — pessimism of the intellect, optimism of the will — is a declared S/W split on the efficacy-adjacent axes: a low-hope setting deliberately paired with a high-commitment weighting. The motto is quotable precisely because single-vector frameworks make it sound paradoxical; here it is simply an address.
- **The conservative and the revolutionary** can share the setting "the rules are changing" (low stationarity) while weighting it oppositely — dread against opportunity. Their fight is real but it is a *weighting* fight; mistaking it for a settings fight produces the familiar mutual accusations of delusion.
- **The doomer and the prepper** share settings on nearly everything and differ almost purely in efficacy-weighting — which is why argument between them about facts changes nothing.
- **Conversion** — religious, political, theoretical — decomposes into two distinct event-types with different phenomenologies: *setting-updates* (evidence-driven, often sudden — the stationarity collapse of a Milly-moment) and *weighting-shifts* (value-driven, often slow, biographical). Histories of conversion that conflate them misdescribe both.
- **Coalitions** sort by the pair-structure: durable coalitions agree on the *weightings that matter* even across divergent settings (they can act together while believing differently); slogan-coalitions share vocabulary across incompatible bearings and shatter on first contact with a decision that forces the hidden axes into the open.

The pair-structure also states precisely what propaganda and ideology-maintenance *do*:
propaganda attacks settings (what is the case); moralization attacks weightings (what matters);
and the most durable capture — Han's achievement-subject again, or administered hope — installs a *weighting* while presenting as a description, which is why it survives factual refutation.

## 6. Ancestors, and what the framework adds

Three research programs are genuine predecessors, and the framework should be positioned against them honestly.

**Grid-group cultural theory** (Douglas; Douglas and Wildavsky, *Risk and Culture*, 1982) is the closest ancestor:
two dimensions (grid: prescription; group: incorporation) generating four cosmologies — hierarchist, egalitarian, individualist, fatalist — with the explicit thesis that risk-perception follows cosmology.
Its virtues (a generating structure; cosmologies as addresses) are the ones aimed at here; its limits are dimensional: two social-organization axes cannot express the process-beliefs (stationarity, reversibility, shape) that §1's cases show doing the ideological work.
The present framework is, in one description, grid-group generalized: more axes, derived from a principle, with world-model settings included.

**Moral foundations theory** (Haidt and colleagues) profiles actors on weightings over moral values (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty).
It is a *W-only* framework: it has no settings vector, and therefore cannot express the phenomena of §5 — it can say the conservative weights sanctity, but not that the doomer and the prepper differ only in efficacy, nor what a Milly-moment does to a bearing.
The present framework contains an MFT-like structure as the W-projection of a richer space.

**Ideal-point estimation** (Poole and Rosenthal's NOMINATE lineage) recovers low-dimensional ideology from behavior (votes), typically finding one or two dimensions.
Its lesson is sobering and must be taken: *expressed* political conflict compresses far below the space's conceptual dimensionality.
The reconciliation is that institutional channels (parties, binary votes) are compression codecs — the full bearing-space collapses onto the axes the institution forces into conflict — and the framework predicts where the compression breaks: novel shocks (a stationarity collapse; a forced efficacy question) decompress the space, and realignments are decompression events.
Behavioral low-dimensionality is thus a fact about institutions, not about teleologies.

What the framework adds over all three:
a **generating principle** (none of the ancestors derives its dimensions);
the **two-vector structure** (none distinguishes settings from weightings);
and the **dimensionality to express non-Western bearings as coherent addresses** rather than as deviations from a Euro-American default — the relational warrant, the helical shape, and constitutive non-ergodicity of place are first-class coordinates here and are expressible in none of the ancestors.

## 7. The framework's own coordinates

A coordinate system for teleologies is itself written from a bearing, and the honest move is to state it.
This framework presumes: low-to-moderate determinacy (multiple bearings are treated as live, not as errors awaiting correction); non-ergodicity of position (it expects standpoints to differ irreducibly — that is why it wants coordinates rather than a ranking); low resolvability weighted positively (it institutionalizes disagreement as geometry rather than seeking a winner); a helical shape-preference in its very method (return to old disputes with accumulated structure); and a made-or-standing warrant (it treats the axis-set as constructed and revisable, answerable to the traditions it maps).
It is, in short, a pluralist instrument and says so — with the one non-negotiable realist commitment already stated in §1: settings are truth-apt and domain-relative, so the pluralism is about *bearings*, not about facts.
Whether an instrument declaring its own address undermines its neutrality is answered by the framework itself: there is no address-free position from which to map addresses; the choice is between declared and undeclared coordinates, and the instrument's contribution is precisely to make declaration cheap.

## 8. Objections and replies

**O1. The axis inventory is arbitrary — why these ten and not thirty?**
*Reply.* The generating principle constrains candidates (an axis must be the settledness-question asked of a structural object), the inventory is explicitly open at the margin (legibility is flagged as a candidate), and the reduction question is *empirical*: coded corpora can reveal whether the conceptual axes collapse statistically (§9.2). Arbitrariness would be a list; this is a derivation plus a measurement program.

**O2. This is relativism with coordinates.**
*Reply.* §1 and §7 both refuse it: settings are truth-apt and domain-relative — stationarity is genuinely dead in hydrology, ergodicity genuinely fails for wealth dynamics — so bearings can be *wrong on the facts* axis by axis and domain by domain. What the framework refuses is only the shortcut of declaring one full bearing correct a priori; correctness is adjudicated per-setting, per-domain, which is more discipline than the shortcut ever imposed.

**O3. The axes are not independent — real ideologies cluster, so the space is mostly empty.**
*Reply.* Statistical correlation among axes is expected and is exactly what "ideologies are crowded neighborhoods" means; conceptual independence is what the framework claims, and it is demonstrated by the split cases (Gramsci; the two-level determinacy of classical theism; Hayek and Scott sharing a legibility setting across opposed politics). The empty regions are findings, not failures: unoccupied coherent addresses are where new formations appear, and the framework is the first instrument that can *say where they are*.

**O4. Deriving axes from 'settledness' smuggles in one tradition's master-concept; the framework universalizes a local obsession.**
*Reply.* The concept earns its generative role on the evidence assembled: the two seed axes — discovered independently by hydrologists, economists, and social theorists, with no shared framework — are both settledness-questions, which is the note's motivating datum, not its assumption. And the test of a master-concept is expressive adequacy: §6's closing claim is that this one expresses bearings (relational warrant, helical shape) that the available alternatives cannot. A better generator is welcome; none of the ancestors offers one at all.

**O5. Bearings are post-hoc redescription — the framework predicts nothing.**
*Reply.* §5 and §6 contain its predictions: coalition durability tracks weighting-agreement rather than slogan-agreement; conversion decomposes into setting-updates and weighting-shifts with distinct phenomenology and distinct triggers; institutional realignment follows decompression shocks on previously suppressed axes; and profile-coding of texts should recover bearing-distances that predict alliance and schism better than left–right placement. Each is testable and none is guaranteed.

## 9. Research program

1. **The coding instrument.** Operationalize bearing-profiling from text: for each axis, a codebook of setting-markers and weighting-markers; validate inter-coder reliability; then scale with computational assistance. Political speeches, movement documents, and theological texts as first corpora.
2. **The factor question.** Do the ten conceptual axes reduce empirically? Run the reduction on coded corpora; compare the recovered dimensionality against NOMINATE-style behavioral spaces to measure institutional compression directly.
3. **Coalition dynamics.** Test §5's durability hypothesis on documented coalition histories: code member-organizations' bearings from their founding documents; predict schism-points from weighting-divergence.
4. **Axis shocks.** Study forced setting-updates historically — the stationarity collapse in climate-exposed professions is the natural experiment (Milly's field before and after 2008) — measuring how weightings respond when a setting breaks: inertia, conversion, or reaction.
5. **The unoccupied-address survey.** Systematically enumerate coherent bearings without named traditions; examine whether emergent formations (post-2008 movements; climate-adaptation politics; the post-liberal reactions) are converging on any of them.
6. **The compression codecs.** Treat institutions (party systems, electoral rules, platforms) as dimensional-compression schemes on the bearing-space; characterize which axes each institution forces into expression and which it suppresses — a comparative-politics program with a precise vocabulary.
