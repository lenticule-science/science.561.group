## 0. Abstract and plan

The freedom-family of concepts — freedom, liberty, liberation, license, sovereignty — is notoriously entangled, and the entanglement is usually treated as a matter for conceptual analysis.
This note proposes instead a *geometry*, and derives it from an unexpected quarter: the ethology of play.
The best-documented fact about mammalian play-fighting is that it is genuine contest conducted *at the edge of known rules*, under voluntary restraint, with built-in reversibility — and that it occurs only under conditions of sufficient safety.
Generalizing:
**freedom is edge-play on holding** — the voluntary, reversible excursion toward the unheld, grown out of security rather than opposed to it —
and its dark twin, abandonment, is *the same position under the opposite modality*: unholding imposed rather than chosen, irreversible rather than reversible.
Once the modality axis (chosen/imposed; reversible/irreversible) is crossed with the holding axis (held/unheld), the freedom-family sorts into distinct vectors with distinct logics and distinct pathologies, and several classical puzzles — why liberation does not yield freedom, why liberty and freedom diverge, why "freedom" can name both emancipation and exposure — resolve as differences of vector rather than disputes about one essence.

§1 assembles the play evidence; §2 states the generalization and its developmental confirmation; §3 distinguishes the vectors; §4 derives the pathologies; §5 states the manufacture argument — that the capacity for freedom is an attention-economic product, and provision its literal manufacture; §6 answers objections; §7 gives directions.

## 1. The play datum

### 1.1 Play-fighting is fighting at the edge of rules

The scientific study of play converged on a definition and a set of findings that political theory has never absorbed.
Burghardt's five criteria (2005) define play as behavior that is
(1) not fully functional in its immediate context,
(2) spontaneous, voluntary, and rewarding,
(3) structurally modified relative to the serious form — exaggerated, incomplete, reordered,
(4) repeated but not stereotyped, and
(5) initiated only in a **"relaxed field"** — under adequate health, safety, and freedom from acute stress.
The fifth criterion is the one this note builds on, and it is robust: stressed, threatened, or deprived animals do not play; play is the first behavior to vanish under adversity and among the first to return with security.

Play-*fighting* — rough-and-tumble, the most widespread form of social play in mammals — exhibits the additional structure the analysis needs (Pellis and Pellis, *The Playful Brain*, 2009; Panksepp's play research):
it is real contest, run with genuine competitive moves,
but conducted under **self-handicapping** (the stronger partner voluntarily does not win outright; adult animals playing with juveniles restrain force),
**role-reversal** (advantage is periodically surrendered to keep the game alive),
and continuous **signaling** that maintains the frame (Bateson 1955: the play-signal marks the nip as denoting the bite without carrying its consequence).
And the function ascribed to it by the leading account is exactly edge-work:
Spinka, Newberry, and Bekoff (2001) argue that play is **"training for the unexpected"** — the deliberate, self-imposed production of loss-of-control (being thrown, tumbled, destabilized) *within recoverable bounds*, building the capacity to regain control after real destabilization.

Assembled: play-fighting is **contest at the edge of the rules, entered voluntarily, kept reversible by restraint and signal, and possible only from safety.**
The players seek the edge — the near-loss, the vertigo Caillois catalogued as *ilinx* — and the seeking is not despite the safety but *funded by it*.

### 1.2 The human developmental confirmation

Winnicott's "The Capacity to be Alone" (1958) supplies the human-scale version of the relaxed-field criterion, in a formulation whose paradox is the finding:
the capacity to be alone *develops in the presence of the mother* —
the child playing absorbed, alone-in-company, in the field of a reliable holding that demands nothing —
and only the child who has had this experience becomes an adult capable of chosen solitude.
Chosen unholding is a *capacity*, and the capacity is built inside holding.
The pair this yields — solitude versus loneliness, famously distinguished by Tillich ("language has created the word 'loneliness' to express the pain of being alone, and the word 'solitude' to express the glory of being alone") — is the first instance of the note's general schema:
the same position (unaccompanied) under opposite modalities (chosen and furnished with return; imposed and without).

## 2. The generalization: freedom as edge-play on holding

### 2.1 The schema

Let *holding* stand for the general condition of being secured — attended to, protected, given standing — and *unholding* for its absence.
Cross this with **modality**: whether the position is chosen or imposed, and whether it is reversible (a return path exists) or irreversible.
The schema generates the family:

| | chosen, reversible | imposed |
|---|---|---|
| **unheld** | **freedom** (the excursion), solitude, adventure, renunciation, the wander | **abandonment**, exile, neglect, exposure |
| **held** | belonging, sanctuary, membership | captivity, surveillance, smothering, the gilded cage |

The table's central claim:
**freedom and abandonment are not opposites on the holding axis — they are the same region of it, distinguished entirely by modality.**
What the free person and the abandoned person share is the unheld position;
what divides them is everything — consent, and the return path.
And the table's second, less familiar diagonal deserves equal weight:
holding itself has an imposed form.
Surveillance is imposed attention; the total institution is imposed care; paternalism is holding without consent —
and the intuition that these violate freedom, though they secure their objects, is the schema working correctly:
they occupy the held row under the wrong modality.

### 2.2 The edge, and why it is sought

The play evidence explains what a purely negative conception of freedom cannot: why free beings *seek* the edge of the unheld rather than resting in maximal security.
Play's function is training-for-the-unexpected; the organism deliberately samples loss-of-control within recoverable bounds because the capacity to recover is built nowhere else.
Freedom's excursions have the same structure and plausibly the same function:
the wander, the risk, the venture, the leaving-home are voluntary samplings of unholding that build the capacities (self-possession, judgment, recovery) which no held position can teach —
which is why traditions as different as the walkabout, the wanderjahr, the pilgrimage, and the vision quest institutionalize a *bounded excursion into the unheld* as the making of an adult.
Freedom, on this account, is not a state but a **practiced motion**: out toward the edge, and back.
The return is not freedom's failure but its completing half — exactly as in play, where the game that cannot end has become something else.

## 3. The vectors distinguished

The schema sorts the freedom-family into vectors with different directions and different logics.

**Freedom — the motion vector.**
The capacity for chosen, reversible excursion.
Arendt's conception is its deepest philosophical statement: freedom as *natality*, the capacity to begin — to introduce into the world what was not implied by its prior state — exercised in action among others; not a possession but a performance.
Graeber and Wengrow (*The Dawn of Everything*, 2021) give its anthropological cash-out as three primordial freedoms whose presence they trace across the ethnographic and archaeological record:
the freedom to *move* (to leave, knowing one will be received elsewhere), the freedom to *disobey*, and the freedom to *reshape* social arrangements.
All three are motion-freedoms — and the first is explicitly an excursion-with-return-path structure: what made leaving free, on their account, was precisely the standing expectation of hospitality elsewhere; the return path was socially manufactured.

**Liberty — the standing vector.**
Where freedom is motion, liberty is *secured position*.
The republican tradition recovered by Pettit and Skinner defines liberty as **non-domination**: not the absence of interference (Berlin's negative liberty) but the absence of *arbitrary power over one* — the condition of not living at another's mercy, even a kind master's.
Stated in the schema's terms: liberty is holding that does not depend on anyone's ongoing goodwill —
standing that persists whether or not the powerful are watching or feeling generous.
This is why liberty is institutional in its essence (rights, law, citizenship) where freedom is practical in its essence (capacities, motions, beginnings);
why liberty can be conferred but freedom only exercised;
and why each is hollow without the other — the excursion needs a secured base to leave from and return to; the secured base without excursion is just a well-appointed cell (§4).

**Liberation — the repair vector.**
Arendt's distinction (*On Revolution*, 1963) is decisive and routinely ignored:
liberation — the removal of imposed conditions, the throwing-off of the yoke — is the *precondition* of freedom and not the thing itself;
revolutions that stop at liberation deliver the unheld position without the practiced motion, and their history is the record of what that yields.
In the schema: liberation moves occupants out of the imposed cells (captivity; abandonment) — an indispensable vector that terminates at the *starting line* of the other two.

**License — the frame-edge permission.**
The bounded grant to play at edges otherwise forbidden — the fool's license, the carnival, diplomatic immunity, the sandbox.
License is freedom's institutional simulation: excursion permitted within a frame someone else holds, revocable at the frame-holder's pleasure.
Its value is real (frames are where edge-capacities are trained — §1) and its limit is definitional: licensed play is play inside another's sovereignty, which is why license's decay-form, licentiousness, is edge-seeking that has forgotten the frame is borrowed.

**Sovereignty — the frame-authorship vector.**
The capacity to *set* the bounds within which play, license, liberty, and excursion occur.
Patterson's tripartite analysis (*Freedom*, 1991) — personal freedom, sovereignal freedom, civic freedom — documents its historical entanglement with the others and its danger:
sovereignal freedom is freedom-as-power-over, the master's freedom, and the tradition's persistent temptation is to mistake it for the whole family.
The schema places it precisely: sovereignty is meta to the table — authorship of the frames that determine, for others, which cells are open.
Distributed, it is self-determination; concentrated, it converts everyone else's freedom into license.

## 4. The pathologies, derived

Each vector has a characteristic corruption, and the schema derives them systematically.

**Freedom without holding: exposure sold as liberty.**
By §1's relaxed-field criterion and §2.2's logic, the excursion is only free when a base and return path exist.
Remove them and the identical position — unheld, mobile, "flexible" — is abandonment wearing freedom's vocabulary.
This is the modern labor-market's signature move, and the critical literature has named it repeatedly:
the gig economy's freedom-talk describes imposed unholding (no standing, no return path) in the excursion's language;
the "free agent" without non-domination is Pettit's dominated subject with better branding.
The diagnostic is always modality: who chose, and can they come back?

**Holding without freedom: the well-kept cell.**
Security that forbids excursion — the total institution, the smothering family, the paternal state, the surveilled workplace — occupies the held row under the imposed modality.
Its inhabitants are safe the way exhibits are safe.
Berlin's fear about positive liberty (that "making men free" licenses coercion in freedom's name) is, in the schema, the fear of exactly this cell: holding imposed for the held one's own alleged good, with the excursion criminalized as ingratitude or pathology.

**Liberation mistaken for the destination.**
The removal of imposed conditions without the construction of standing (liberty) or the practice of excursion (freedom) — the revolution that ends at the fallen wall.
Arendt's analysis predicts its sequel: the unheld, unpracticed population re-seeks holding on any terms, and the frame-authors who offer it inherit the revolution.

**Edge-play without return: self-abandonment.**
The excursion that abandons its own return path — recklessness, burn-it-down exit, the flight that is really a fall — is freedom's motion under abandonment's modality, self-imposed.
Play theory marks the boundary precisely: when the game can no longer end, it has stopped being play.

**Sovereignty hoarded: the conversion of freedom to license.**
Where frame-authorship concentrates, every other party's freedom becomes revocable permission — the master's household, the platform's terms of service.
Patterson's history is the long documentation of this conversion and its intimacy with the freedom-ideal itself.

## 5. The manufacture argument

The schema's pieces assemble into a single argument with policy-grade consequences, and it deserves statement as a chain.

**P1.** The capacity for freedom — edge-play, excursion, venture — is exercised only from the relaxed field (§1.1) and developed only inside reliable holding (§1.2).
**P2.** The relaxed field is an *attention state*: freedom from acute threat is, concretely, freedom from compulsory threat-simulation — an unburdened bandwidth budget, in the precise sense the scarcity research measures.
**P3.** Therefore the capacity for freedom is an **attention-economic product**: it exists where the survival tax leaves surplus, and cannot exist where it does not.
**C1.** A regime that governs through precarity and abandonment does not merely inflict suffering *alongside* freedom; it eradicates its population's capacity for freedom as a matter of budget arithmetic — the bandwidth tax of survival leaves nothing to fund the field.
Its cruelest refinement is then to name the resulting immobility a character defect.
**C2.** Conversely, provision and procedural certainty — income floors, enforceable notice, reliable rules, anything that converts unverifiable threat into checkable procedure — are not comfort spending.
They are the **literal manufacture of freedom's preconditions**: inputs to liberty, not alternatives traded against it.

Three strengthenings:

- **The argument supplies the mechanism republican liberty lacked.**
  Pettit's non-domination holds that living at another's arbitrary mercy unfree­s even without interference; the manufacture argument says why, arithmetically:
  arbitrary power *compels threat-simulation whether or not it strikes* — the kind master taxes the bandwidth as surely as the cruel one, because unpredictability, not blows, is what the mind must continuously model.
  Non-interference is thus insufficient for freedom by accounting, not merely by intuition, and the Berlin-framed tradeoff of liberty against security is incoherent below the threshold: beneath it, security *is* the liberty input.
- **The empirical footing already exists.**
  The same farmers score markedly higher on cognitive measures post-harvest than pre-harvest (the bandwidth-tax paradigm); unconditional cash-transfer trials find increased venture, investment, and risk-taking rather than the predicted idleness (Haushofer & Shapiro 2016; the Banerjee et al. 2017 review finding no work-reduction).
  The direction of these results is exactly C2's prediction, against the "dependency breeds passivity" doctrine — which the schema diagnoses as a category error: passivity is the *flinch posture of the taxed*, not the temperament of the provided-for.
- **The manufacture curve is nonlinear.**
  Near subsistence the tax consumes everything, so small provisions produce discontinuous freedom-gains; the curve is steepest exactly where such spending is conventionally judged least productive.

One caveat completes rather than weakens the argument:
**freed bandwidth is itself a harvestable resource.**
The commercial attention economy (Wu, *The Attention Merchants*, 2016) stands ready to re-expropriate whatever provision liberates — converting surplus into engagement rather than excursion.
The manufacture of freedom therefore has two components, not one:
*provision* (freeing the budget) and *protection* (defending the freed budget from capture).
A society that supplies floors while auctioning its citizens' attention has built the field and sold the sky above it.

## 6. Objections and replies

**O1. The schema reduces a normative family to a descriptive grid; freedom is a value, not a coordinate.**
*Reply.* The grid is where the values live, not their replacement: the note's normative claims (return paths matter; modality distinguishes freedom from abandonment; liberty is non-domination) are inherited from the traditions cited, and the grid's contribution is to stop them colliding — to show that emancipation-freedom, excursion-freedom, and standing-liberty are different goods with different conditions, so that arguments trading on their conflation (exposure sold as freedom; captivity sold as care) can be caught in the act.

**O2. The play analogy is romantic: human freedom concerns power and property, not rough-and-tumble.**
*Reply.* The analogy imports exactly two findings, both load-bearing and neither romantic: that edge-capacities are built only through recoverable excursions (training-for-the-unexpected), and that such excursions occur only from safety (the relaxed field). Both have direct human developmental confirmation (Winnicott) and direct political corollaries (§4's first two pathologies). Power and property enter as the machinery of holding and frame-authorship — the analogy's terms, not its casualties.

**O3. Non-domination already covers most of this; the schema adds vocabulary, not content.**
*Reply.* Non-domination is one vector — secured standing — and its tradition says little about the excursion (why free people court edges), nothing about the relaxed-field dependency (why capacity for freedom is built inside holding), and treats the imposed-holding cell only as domination, missing its benevolent variants. The grid situates Pettit's insight rather than repeating it.

**O4. "Chosen versus imposed" is too clean: choices are structured, consent is manufactured, and the gig worker "chooses" flexibility.**
*Reply.* Granted, and the schema sharpens rather than suffers from it: modality is exactly where ideology operates, so the analysis directs scrutiny to the *production of the modality* — the manufacturing of ostensible choice over actually imposed positions is the definitive freedom-washing move, and §4's diagnostic (who chose; can they return) is a two-question audit designed for it. A grid that locates the lie is not undone by the lie's existence.

**O5. The manufacture argument reduces freedom to welfare economics — a spending line cannot be a liberty.**
*Reply.* The argument concerns preconditions, not the exercise: the excursion remains a practiced motion no administration can perform on anyone's behalf, and §4's second pathology already condemns holding imposed "for their own good." What the argument denies is only the coherence of demanding the motion from the bandwidth-taxed — and Berlin's legitimate fear targeted the state *prescribing* the excursion; C2 prescribes nothing but the field.

**O6. Indigenous and communal conceptions of freedom-as-responsibility fit poorly in an individualist excursion schema.**
*Reply.* They fit in its foundations rather than its cells: the relaxed field and the return path are *communally produced* (Graeber and Wengrow's freedom-to-move rests on standing hospitality; Winnicott's capacity rests on reliable presence), so the schema's own logic entails that freedom is a social product before it is an individual motion — a conclusion the communal traditions state directly and the individualist ones perpetually rediscover.

## 7. Directions

1. **The return-path audit.** Operationalize §4's two-question diagnostic (who chose the position; does a return path exist, and who maintains it) as an instrument for evaluating freedom-claims in labor, migration, and platform-governance debates.
2. **The excursion institutions.** A comparative study of bounded-excursion rites (wanderjahr, walkabout, pilgrimage, sabbatical) as freedom-pedagogy: what frame, titration, and return machinery they share, and what their decline removes from societies that dropped them.
3. **The relaxed-field dependency, tested politically.** The schema predicts that security interventions (housing-first, unconditional income) should *increase* measured excursion behaviors (mobility, venture, dissent) — against the standard fear that security breeds passivity. The prediction is discriminating and partially testable on existing program data.
4. **The imposed-holding cell.** A taxonomy of benevolent captivities (care institutions, protective surveillance, paternal platforms) using the modality diagnostic, aimed at the design question: what converts imposed holding into chosen holding — consent machinery, exit rights, or frame co-authorship?
5. **Formal treatment.** The schema's structure (a holding dimension crossed by modality, with freedom as bounded reversible excursion) is exact enough to formalize; a companion treatment in the settlement-mathematics register is queued separately.
