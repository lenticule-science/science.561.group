Procedural certainty names the condition of facing clear, enforceable, and predictable rules governing one's status, property, employment, or standing, as opposed to arbitrary, unpredictable, or contestable authority over the same domains. Examples include a secure land title as against informal occupancy contestable at will; a stable legal immigration status as against renewable, revocable, or uncertain status; and a published, enforceable work schedule as against one subject to short-notice, on-call, or "clopening" changes. The term is used in the estate's attention-economic notes to name the surviving half of an argument that originally leaned on the bandwidth-tax mechanism: that removing status unpredictability returns a resource to the person subject to it, independent of any change in their income.

Unlike the bandwidth-tax mechanism itself, procedural certainty's benefits are supported by direct, replicated evidence, though the evidence runs through stress, mental health, and time allocation rather than through measured cognitive capacity — no study has run a fluid-intelligence battery before and after a tenure or status change. Three independent literatures converge on the pattern. Erica Field's study of Peru's urban land-titling program (*Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 2007) found that granting formal title to squatter households raised total household labor hours by about 17%, cut in-home work by 47%, and reduced child labor by 28% — consistent with removing the need to physically guard untitled property. Hainmueller, Lawrence, Martén and colleagues (*Science*, 2017) used the natural experiment of DACA eligibility to show that mothers' protected status significantly reduced their US-citizen children's diagnosed anxiety and adjustment disorders — a demonstration that status security, not income, drove the effect. And Kristen Harknett and Daniel Schneider's Shift Project (*American Sociological Review*, 2019), studying roughly 60,000 hourly service-sector workers, found that unpredictable scheduling was associated with worse sleep, more psychological distress, and lower happiness, and that schedule predictability was a stronger predictor of worker wellbeing than wage level itself.

Procedural certainty should be treated as an independent good in the estate's framework, supported at replicated, if modest, effect sizes across labor supply, mental health, and subjective wellbeing outcomes — not as an application of the bandwidth-tax concept, whose cognitive-restoration mechanism remains empirically unconfirmed. Arguments that provision and procedural certainty jointly "manufacture freedom's preconditions" (as in the freedom note's manufacture argument) can cite this term's supporting literature directly, without needing the retired IQ-scale framing to carry the weight.
