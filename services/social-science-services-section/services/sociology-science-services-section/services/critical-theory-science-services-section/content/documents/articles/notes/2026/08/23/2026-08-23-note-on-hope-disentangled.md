# Hope Disentangled

## 0. Abstract and plan

Two bodies of thought appear to collide head-on.
One treats hope as a virtue — indeed as *the* virtue of finite existence: Aquinas's theological virtue with its twin opposed vices, Pieper's wayfarer condition, Bloch's ontology of the not-yet, Lear's radical hope under cultural devastation.
The other says, in so many words, *fuck hope*: the anarcho-nihilist current (the anonymous *Desert*; Serafinski's *Blessed Is the Flame*), with philosophical artillery from Nietzsche and Camus, treats hope as a chain — the great deferral mechanism, the pacifier, the last and worst evil in Pandora's jar.

This note argues the collision is mostly nominal and partly real, and does the work of separating the two.
The word "hope" carries at least six distinct referents (§2).
The nihilist refusal, read in its own texts (§1), targets exactly three of them — and the virtue-tradition, read strictly, condemns the same three.
What nihilist practice *affirms* — action without guarantee, joy in revolt, meaning decoupled from outcome — is the virtue-tradition's remainder under other names (§3), a convergence already staged and partially resolved in queer theory's futurity debate (§4) and deliberately sidestepped, for good historical reasons, by the Indigenous vocabulary of survivance and desire (§5).
One genuine fork survives the disentangling — the question of the forward lean's *warrant* — and it admits three resolutions, not two (§6).
A vocabulary policy follows (§7); objections (§8); directions (§9).

## 1. The refusal, in its own texts

The contemporary anarcho-nihilist statement is *Desert* (anonymous, 2011), and its argument is more precise than its reputation.
Its target is the movement-orthodoxy that global salvation — the revolution, or the managed avoidance of climate catastrophe — is coming, must be believed in, and organizes all present action.
*Desert*'s claim: this belief is false, and — the point usually missed — **its abandonment is liberating**:
released from the obligation to save everything, one is finally free to act *here*, defend *this* place, live anarchy now rather than administer its arrival.
Crucially, the text counsels letting go of *hope and fear together* — borrowing explicitly from the Buddhist teaching in which the two are one attachment to outcome seen from its bright and dark sides.
The renunciation is a pair-renunciation, not a despair.

Serafinski's *Blessed Is the Flame* (2016) supplies the limit case:
armed resistance in the Nazi concentration camps, undertaken by people who *knew* they would not win.
The book's argument is that their action was not irrational residue but the purest form of revolt — resistance whose meaning was entirely internal to the act, requiring no future payout —
and it generalizes this into an "anarcho-nihilist" ethic: the value of revolt does not run through its probability of success.

The philosophical artillery behind both:
Nietzsche's Pandora reading (*Human, All Too Human* §71) — hope as "the worst of evils, for it prolongs the torments of man"; and his distinction between **passive nihilism** (the collapse into nothing-matters — despair by another name) and **active nihilism** (the destruction of dead values as the precondition of living ones), with the anarcho-nihilists explicitly claiming the active side.
Camus (*The Myth of Sisyphus*, 1942) — hope as the characteristic *evasion* of the absurd, the leap that refuses to live within what is actually given; the absurd hero acts, and even is happy, without it.
And the older political critique: hope administered from above as pacification — the opiate function, the "wait and it will be given" that keeps the aggrieved seated.

## 2. The referents disentangled

Six things travel under the one word, and they have different structures, different failure conditions, and different politics.

**(A) Promissory hope — the deferral.**
Investment of present meaning in a future arrival: the Revolution, the Kingdom, the payout that justifies the suffering.
Its structure is a *dated check*; its characteristic failure is Berlant's cruel optimism — the attachment whose object obstructs the flourishing it promises, held in place precisely by its promissory form.

**(B) Optimism — the forecast.**
The belief that things will improve; hope as prediction.
Its failure conditions are epistemic: under genuine non-stationarity there is no stable distribution over futures to have expectations about, and its certainty-form is what the scholastic analysis isolates as **presumption** — the future treated as settled possession.

**(C) Administered hope — the pacifier.**
Hope produced and distributed by power to manage the hopeless: the campaign promise, the deferred improvement, the heaven of the overworked.
Structurally it is attention-capture by fantasy — occupation of the political imagination with arrival-simulations — and it is the referent of the opiate critique.

**(D) The held-open — the discipline.**
The refusal of premature settlement in *either* direction:
neither despair (the open treated as lost) nor presumption (the open treated as won), but the trained inhabitation of the undetermined — Aquinas's virtue read structurally, Pieper's *status viatoris*.
Note that (D) condemns (B) as strictly as it condemns despair: the virtue-tradition's hope is *not* optimism, and the conflation of the two is the single largest source of the apparent collision.

**(E) Radical hope — the thin commitment.**
Lear's limit-form: commitment to a goodness that transcends one's current ability to conceive it, held under framework collapse, expressed as readiness to learn what the unimaginable future requires.
Not a forecast; not an object; a lean.

**(F) Present-tense continuance — desire, joy, survivance.**
The register that is not futural at all:
Bloch's hunger read as present drive; the anarchist affirmation of joy-in-revolt now (Bergman and Montgomery's *Joyful Militancy*, 2017, with its Spinozan definition — joy as increase of capacity — set against "rigid radicalism");
Vizenor's *survivance* — active presence, the stories continuing;
Tuck's desire-centered framework (2009) — replacing both damage-narratives and rescue-narratives with desire: complex, present, not deferred.
(F) does the motivational work usually credited to hope while never issuing a check against the future.

## 3. The reconciliation

Map the refusal onto the taxonomy and the collision largely dissolves.

**What "fuck hope" kills is (A), (B), and (C)** — and the virtue-tradition, read strictly, kills them too:
(A) is cruel optimism, the perpetual non-converting attachment; the scholastic analysis of *presumption* condemns (B)'s certainty-form as a vice against hope itself; and (C) is hope's captured, weaponized form, which no account of hope-as-virtue defends.
The nihilist texts are best read as a *purification* performed from outside the tradition: burning the three corrupt referents with which the word had become saturated in political speech.

**What nihilist practice keeps is (D) and (F).**
The camp resisters of *Blessed Is the Flame* are (D) enacted at the limit — refusing the despair-verdict without purchasing any forecast — plus (F): the act's meaning internal to the act.
*Desert*'s freed defender-of-this-place is (F)'s present-tense care plus (D)'s double refusal.
And the pair-renunciation — hope *and* fear together — is the analysis's sharpest confirmation:
what is dropped is the *valence pair*, the two leanings over outcome, which are one attachment;
what remains is engaged action from present commitment.
This is equanimity-with-stakes — the contemplative traditions' outcome-detachment fused with full engagement — arrived at through the black flag rather than the monastery,
and its internal quality-control is Nietzsche's own distinction: active nihilism keeps acting and creating; passive nihilism, the collapse into nothing-matters, is simply despair — referent (D)'s first vice — wearing the same clothes.
The tradition's honest members police exactly this line.

**And the speech-act itself deserves its own entry:**
"fuck hope," uttered by someone who then keeps fighting, is not a report of despair but a *performed conversion* — the public burning of an imposed deferral to reclaim the present.
It is refusal executed within commitment: structurally kin to lament (grief and protest that stay in relation), which is why the people who say it keep showing up, and the people who actually give up rarely bother saying anything.

## 4. The staged precedent: the futurity debate

Queer theory ran this entire collision a decade earlier, with the referents similarly tangled.
Edelman's *No Future* (2004) refuses futurity wholesale: the Child and the future it warrants are the regime's alibi, and queerness should occupy the position of the death drive rather than petition for a place in tomorrow.
Muñoz's *Cruising Utopia* (2009) answers with hope as horizon: "queerness is not yet here" — a critical, Blochian not-yet glimpsed in performance and ephemera.
Sorted by taxonomy, the fight thins:
Edelman's target is (A) in its ideological form — *reproductive futurism*, the compulsory promissory register — and his refusal is a (D)-style anti-collapse plus a polemical style;
Muñoz's hope is explicitly not a forecast (not (B)) and not a promise (not (A)) but horizon and lean — (E) with (F)'s evidentiary practice.
Genuine disagreement remains between them (about negativity, about whether any futural lean is recuperable), but it is a §6-type disagreement about the lean's warrant and cost — not a disagreement in which one side is "for hope" and the other "against" it.

## 5. Why the Indigenous register sidesteps the word

Survivance's avoidance of hope-vocabulary is deliberate, and its reasons are historical before they are philosophical.
The promissory register was, for Native nations, the *treaty* register — futures formally promised and systematically defaulted; hope-talk arrives pre-poisoned by the archive of broken checks.
And the futural register generally was the colonizer's genre about Native people — the vanishing-narrative, the future in which the Indian is absent, or the salvage-narrative's future in which he is redeemed by others.
Vizenor's coinage therefore refuses the tense itself:
survivance is **active presence** — continuance, the stories going on, "renunciation of dominance, tragedy, and victimry" — a present-progressive virtue that never issues or accepts checks against the future.
Tuck's desire-framework performs the same displacement methodologically: against damage-centered research (which fixes communities in despair's genre) *and* against rescue-framing (promissory hope's genre), desire names what is wanted and lived *now*, in full complexity.
The upshot for the reconciliation:
the Indigenous register does the virtue's work — sustains action, refuses both collapses — in a grammar that never exposes it to (A)-(C)'s corruptions.
It is not a rejection of what (D)-(F) name; it is their most weathered implementation.

## 6. The fork that survives: the warrant of the lean

One genuine disagreement remains after the disentangling, and it should be stated rather than harmonized.
Referents (D)–(F) all involve a *forward lean* — continued action toward good under no guarantee.
The question is what warrants the lean, and three answers structure the field:

**Transcendent warrant.**
Aquinas's hope leans on divine assistance; even Lear's radical hope retains a thin metaphysical commitment — *that there is a goodness* exceeding current concepts.
The lean is trust, and something is trusted.

**Self-warrant.**
The hard nihilist answer: no goodness is guaranteed beyond what we make; the lean is will, desire, jouissance — self-generated, requiring nothing beyond the living being's own affirmation.
Its strength is austerity; its standing difficulty is the one Nietzsche named and the tradition's history keeps confirming: self-generated affirmation is exhausting, and its failure mode is the slide from active into passive nihilism when the will tires.

**Relational warrant.**
The third answer, most explicitly developed in the published Indigenous philosophical literature (Deloria on the moral universe of relations and obligations; Wilson on relational accountability; the kinship-centered accounts of grounded normativity):
the lean needs neither a forecast nor a transcendent guarantor because **the relations already exist**.
One acts for one's relatives — human and other-than-human — and the warrant is the present, actual, undestroyed web of kinship, which no outcome can retroactively void.
This dissolves the exhaustion problem of self-warrant (the ground is not conjured by will) without the metaphysical bill of transcendence (nothing beyond the actual relations is invoked),
and it is fully compatible with the nihilist purification: it issues no checks, defers nothing, promises nothing — it simply locates the lean's source in what is already the case.
The structure of this trichotomy — gift from beyond, achievement of the will, or standing ground — will be recognized by readers of the old grace disputes; the positions recur because the question recurs.

The fork is real: transcendent, self-willed, and relational warrants are different metaphysics with different failure modes and different practices.
But it is a fork *within* the shared refusal of (A)–(C), among parties who all keep acting — a family argument among the unpacified.

## 7. A vocabulary policy

The disentangling yields a usable rule.
**In theoretical registers**, "hope" is retainable and worth defending, *strictly defined* as (D)/(E): the literatures (scholastic, Blochian, Lear's) are anchored to the word, and the definitions guard it.
**In political registers**, the word is damaged goods — saturated by (A)–(C), pre-poisoned in colonial contexts (§5), and every use invites the corruptions back in.
There, the work is better carried by the undamaged vocabulary: *survivance, desire, joy, continuance* — words that do (D)/(F)'s work with no promissory surface.
And "fuck hope" remains sayable in both registers, read correctly:
it is the demolition charge for (A)–(C), and what it kills, the virtue-tradition also wants dead.
The only usage genuinely worth contesting is the slide it can license — the purification tipping into passive nihilism — and the tradition's own active/passive distinction is the sufficient police.

## 8. Objections and replies

**O1. This assimilates the nihilists into a tradition they explicitly reject; the reconciliation is a capture.**
*Reply.* The test is their own texts' affirmations, cited in §1: present action, joy in revolt, resistance whose meaning is internal to the act. The note claims these affirmations are (D)/(F) under other names — a structural identification, checkable against the texts — while preserving, in §6, the metaphysical disagreement (self-warrant versus the alternatives) as genuine. Identification of common practice plus honest location of residual difference is analysis, not annexation.

**O2. The virtue-hope always smuggles theology; a secular reader should side with the refusal wholesale.**
*Reply.* Bloch's hope is militantly atheist; Lear's commitment is metaphysically thin by design; and the relational warrant (§6) invokes nothing beyond actually existing kinship. The theological version is one member of the family, not its hidden essence — and the secular reader who rejects (D) wholesale must then explain the camp resisters without it, which *Blessed Is the Flame* itself cannot do except by re-inventing (D) under the name of nihilism.

**O3. Six referents is taxonomy inflation; ordinary usage blends them because they blend in life.**
*Reply.* They blend in *feeling*; they separate in failure. (A) fails by obstruction, (B) by falsification, (C) by capture, (D) by collapse into its two vices, (E) by exhaustion of the thin commitment, (F) by burnout of unreplenished desire. Different failure conditions are the operational test of real distinctness, and the political cost of blending them — pacification defended as virtue, or virtue burned as pacification — is the note's entire occasion.

**O4. The pair-renunciation borrow (hope-and-fear) misreads the Buddhist teaching for anarchist convenience.**
*Reply.* The teaching that hope and fear are one attachment is standard in the sources the milieu drew on, and *Desert*'s use is explicit borrowing, cited as such. Whether the full soteriological context survives the transplant is a fair question for Buddhist studies; the structural point the note takes — that the renunciation is of the valence-pair, not of engagement — is exactly what the transplanted teaching says in both settings.

**O5. §5 romanticizes survivance as an implementation of a European virtue-schema.**
*Reply.* The direction of fit is stated the other way: survivance is the *most weathered* implementation — older in practice, cleaner in grammar — and the schema is the newcomer that (D)-(F) reconstructs. Where the mapping distorts, the published Indigenous literature has priority over the schema, and §6 places the relational warrant as a distinct position precisely so it is not absorbed into either European alternative.

## 9. Directions

1. **The genre test.** Code movement discourse for referent — promissory (A), forecast (B), held-open (D), desire/continuance (F) — and test the demobilization hypothesis: that (A)-heavy framings predict burnout and post-defeat collapse, while (F)-heavy framings predict persistence. The camp-resistance and long-defense literatures offer historical cases; contemporary movements offer prospective ones.
2. **The treaty-register study.** A focused intellectual history of hope-vocabulary in colonial promissory instruments and its afterlife in Indigenous political speech — the philological ground of §5's claim.
3. **The warrant ethnography.** §6 predicts different exhaustion profiles: self-warranted militancy should show the active-to-passive slide under attrition where relationally warranted action persists. Comparative movement studies could operationalize this.
4. **The renunciation practices.** A comparative study of hope-and-fear pair-renunciation across its settings — the Buddhist sources, the contemplative outcome-detachments, and the anarcho-nihilist borrowing — on the question of what sustains engagement after the valences are dropped.
5. **The lexicon audit.** Develop §7 into an editing instrument: for a given political text, identify which referent each hope-occurrence carries, and whether undamaged vocabulary would carry it better.
