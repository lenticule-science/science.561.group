# Kyriarchial Work

A theory of [work](../../../../../_index.md) analyzed through the lens of [kyriarchism](../../../../../../../../critical-theory/theories/queer/theories/kyriarchism): work as organized within interlocking systems of domination (patriarchy, white supremacy, capitalism, heteronormativity, ableism) rather than as neutral activity. Tasks, labor relations, workplace hierarchies, employment contracts, workplace cultures — the work vocabulary's terms — are exhibited as specific structures produced and reproduced through kyriarchal dynamics.

## What this theory commits to

Kyriarchial work analyzes how work is structured, valued, and experienced within intersecting systems of power:

1. **Work is never neutral** — all work occurs within and reproduces kyriarchal relations of power
2. **Valorization is intersectional** — what counts as "valuable work" or "skilled labor" varies depending on where workers sit in intersecting hierarchies of gender, race, class, sexuality, ability
3. **Exploitation is systemic** — workplace exploitation flows from the same interlocking systems that produce domination in other spheres of life
4. **Resistance is situated** — strategies for transforming work emerge from specific positionalities within the kyriarchy
5. **Transformation requires dismantling** — liberating work requires dismantling the interlocking systems themselves, not just reforming workplace practices

## Key Concepts

- **Interlocking exploitation**: How different forms of workplace exploitation (wage theft, harassment, occupational segregation) reinforce each other across axes of domination
- **Positional labor value**: How the social value assigned to identical labor tasks varies based on worker identity within kyriarchal hierarchies
- **Institutional isomorphism**: How workplace structures mirror and reproduce broader societal power distributions
- **Intersectional precarity**: How vulnerability to job loss, wage instability, and unsafe conditions compounds across multiple marginalized identities
- **Solidarity fractures**: How kyriarchal divisions undermine collective worker power and how solidarity can be rebuilt across differences

## Relationship to Other Work Theories

- **Distinct from predicated-work**: While predicated-work analyzes work through formal predication mathematics, kyriarchial-work analyzes work through power relations
- **Complementary to predicated-work**: A predicated-kyriarchial-work synthesis would apply predication mathematics to kyriarchial work structures
- **Informs critical work studies**: Builds on feminist labor theory, critical race theory approaches to work, queer theory of labor, and disability studies of work
