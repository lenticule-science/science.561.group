## The problem

The temporality-coverage-enrichment plan created bridging terms (duration, horizon, lifecycle, recurrence, urgency) that connect philosophical time to operational time. But those terms reference concepts that don't yet exist in the library:

- `duration` references effort (no term), interval (no term)
- `lifecycle` references phase (no term), cycle (no term)
- `recurrence` references cycle (no term)
- `urgency` references priority (no term)

Meanwhile, the system aspires to ERP/ICS capabilities but lacks the practical vocabulary of organizational time management: how organizations assign people to time periods, ensure continuous coverage, manage availability, and coordinate calendars. These are the concepts that every scheduling system, incident command system, and resource planner depends on.

## Two layers

**Layer 1: Foundations** — concepts the temporal terms already depend on
- `interval` — bounded stretch of time between two points (what schedule allocates, what shifts fill)
- `phase` — distinct stage within a lifecycle (what lifecycle is made of)
- `cycle` — one complete iteration of a recurrence (what cadence counts)
- `effort` — active work time within a duration (what capacity measures)
- `priority` — importance ranking that dispatch uses (what urgency converts to action)

**Layer 2: Organizational temporality** — ERP/ICS building blocks
- `shift` — bounded work period with duty assignment (the 8-hour shift, the day shift)
- `watch` — shift within continuous coverage (naval/military: the system is never unstaffed)
- `rotation` — cycling through role-holders or duty assignments
- `roster` — assignment of personnel to time slots (who works when)
- `calendar` — shared temporal coordinate system (the grid that scheduling operates on)
- `time-block` — reserved interval for specific work (Newport's deep work blocks)
- `availability` — when an agent can work (the constraint roster respects)
- `coverage` — ensuring all required intervals are staffed (the invariant shifts maintain)

## All tasks are parallel

No dependencies between terms — each can be written independently. The foundation terms ground the existing temporal vocabulary; the organizational terms ground ERP/ICS capabilities.
