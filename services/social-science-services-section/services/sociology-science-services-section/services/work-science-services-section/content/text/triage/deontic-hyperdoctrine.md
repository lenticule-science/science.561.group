# Is there a deontic hyperdoctrine over the predication fibration?

A bridge question imported from [the math program's corresponding open question](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/questions/deontic-hyperdoctrine-for-governance.md). That question identifies the gap; this question holds the PredGov-side work.

## The gap

The math program's [modal-necessity-operator](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/modal-necessity-operator.md) $\Box_R$ and [modal-possibility-operator](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/modal-possibility-operator.md) $\Diamond_R$ act on world-indexed valuations via the [bilattice accessibility relation](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/bilattice-accessibility-relation.md) $R$. These give *alethic* modalities — what *is* necessarily or possibly the case.

PredGov's normative apparatus requires *deontic* modalities — what *ought* to be done, what is permitted, what is obligatory, what is forbidden. The [jural-relation](objects/jural-relation.md) object uses duty/privilege/power/immunity directly, and [deontic-modality](concepts/deontic-modality.md) names the semantic category. But the formal substrate — a deontic hyperdoctrine over the predication fibration — has not been constructed.

## The Hohfeldian map to deontic modalities

The four Hohfeldian first-quartet positions correspond to deontic modalities on the [world-set](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/world-set.md):

- **Duty** ↔ obligation $O(\text{action})$: the agent must perform the action in all permitted worlds.
- **Privilege** ↔ permission $P(\text{action})$: the agent may perform the action; there is no duty to refrain.
- **Power** ↔ deontic possibility of state-change: $P(\text{outcome})$ via the agent's exercise — performing the action can alter the normative state of affairs.
- **Immunity** ↔ deontic impossibility of another's state-change: $\neg P(\text{outcome-by-other})$ — no other agent's action can alter this jural position.

The second quartet (liability, no-right, disability, subjection) are the correlatives and opposites. By [correlative-pair-as-modal-dual](theorems/correlative-pair-as-modal-dual.md), the eightfold is the orbit of a single bilattice modal generator under the Klein-four × $\mathbb{Z}/2$ action. This is the right formal statement for the alethic case; the deontic case requires a separate *deontic accessibility relation*.

## Sub-questions

### (1) Deontic accessibility structure

Standard deontic logic (Kanger, Hilpinen, von Wright) uses an "ideal worlds" relation $R^\mathrm{d}$ distinct from the alethic accessibility relation: $w' \in R^\mathrm{d}(w)$ iff $w'$ is a deontically ideal continuation of $w$ — a world in which all obligations are fulfilled. Can the predication hyperdoctrine accommodate a deontic accessibility relation without leaving the bilattice setting?

The bilattice structure already carries truth- and knowledge-order separately. The deontic dimension needs a third order — the normative order, distinguishing "what is" from "what ought to be." One candidate: the bilattice with a designated "ideal" suborder where obligation cells carry $\mathbf{t}$ and no duty-violating cells carry $\mathbf{t}$ simultaneously. Whether this is a new structure or a restriction on the existing bilattice is the first structural question.

### (2) Does the deontic extension preserve closure-2-monad lax-idempotence?

Governance updates (charter amendments, installation of new officers, delegation) change the normative state — they alter which closure rules fire and who has standing to invoke them. If the deontic accessibility relation $R^\mathrm{d}$ changes when authority is modified, does [closure-2-monad lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md) still hold?

This is the PredGov stability question: does the structural guarantee that "applying the closure operator twice is the same as applying it once" survive governance updates? The [charter-amendment-as-visible-automorphism](theorems/charter-amendment-as-visible-automorphism.md) theorem gives one answer for the rule-structure level; the deontic question asks whether the modal-level stability transfers.

### (3) Formal completeness of the Hohfeldian eightfold as a deontic structure

Hohfeld's analysis is widely used in legal theory as an exhaustive analysis of first-order legal relations. Whether the eightfold is *formally complete* as a deontic structure on the predication hyperdoctrine — whether every deontic condition expressible in the hyperdoctrine is expressible in Hohfeldian terms — is open. Completeness would mean: the eightfold is the deontic hyperdoctrine's canonical generating set, the orbit of a single generator as [correlative-pair-as-modal-dual](theorems/correlative-pair-as-modal-dual.md) establishes for the bilattice modal case.

### (4) Cross-substrate deontic coherence

When two federated substrates (two polities, two governance jurisdictions) have different governance structures, how does the deontic accessibility relation merge at the boundary? [federation-cohomology-as-mayer-vietoris](theorems/federation-cohomology-as-mayer-vietoris.md) handles the rule-structure merging; the deontic analog would handle the normative-modal merging. Does the existing federation machinery extend, or does cross-substrate deontic coherence require new structure?

## Why this question is PredGov's foundational gap

The existing PredGov apparatus — jural relations, offices, delegation, normative systems, charter — is built and formally correct at the rule-structure and object level. But the modal-logical foundation that would ground "ought" semantics in the predication-hyperdoctrine framework is absent. Without it, PredGov's deontic commitments are asserted as closure-rule patterns without a semantics that grounds why those patterns have normative force.

The deontic hyperdoctrine would give PredGov what the math program's alethic modal apparatus gives PredCat's spatiotemporal reasoning: a principled semantic foundation, not just operational patterns.

## Relation to the trust-boundary question

The math program's [trust-boundary-modal-logic question](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/questions/trust-boundary-modal-logic.md) asks for a modal characterization of epistemic trust (what a substrate *believes* about content from another substrate). The deontic question is the normative complement: what a substrate *ought to do* vs. what it *knows* about. Both extend the alethic modal apparatus, and a unified treatment would handle governance (deontic) and trust (epistemic) under a shared modal framework.
