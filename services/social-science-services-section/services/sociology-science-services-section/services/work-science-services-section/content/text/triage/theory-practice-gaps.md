# Theory-Practice Gaps Analysis

## Identified Gaps Between Predication-Categories Theory and Current Governance/Offices Structure

Based on analysis of the theoretical overview and current governance structures, here are three specific gaps:

### Gap 1: Missing Closure-Regime Awareness in Office Design

**Theory**: The predication-categories framework identifies four distinct closure regimes (strict, Scott-continuous, transfinite-bounded, aperiodic) that determine how settlements converge and what cohomological invariants are preserved.

**Current Practice**: Governance offices in `work/governance/offices/` appear to be designed without explicit consideration of which closure regime they operate in or what cohomological properties they should preserve.

**Evidence**: Office records (like `steward.md`, `executive-officer.md`) focus on powers, duties, and skills but don't reference closure regimes or cohomological invariants that should govern their operation.

**Recommended Action**: Analyze each office's function to determine its appropriate closure regime and document required cohomological properties to preserve.

### Gap 2: Incomplete Four-Position Substrate Entity Classification in Governance Records

**Theory**: The four-position classification (settled/partnered, settled/unpartnered, unsettled/partnered, unsettled/unpartnered) provides a structural framework for understanding entity states in the predication settling.

**Current Practice**: Governance records use ad-hoc status fields or lifecycle predicates that don't align with the formal four-position classification derived from fiber analysis.

**Evidence**: While some records mention lifecycle predicates, they don't systematically use the settled/unsettled and partnered/unpartnered axes that derive from closure rule satisfaction and partner cell presence.

**Recommended Action**: Map existing governance entity classifications to the four-position framework and update records to use this standardized classification.

### Gap 3: Missing Federated Cohomology Awareness in Inter-Office Relations

**Theory**: The federation-cohomology-as-mayer-vietoris theorem shows that federated structures produce cohomological phenomena that exist only at the federal level - not reducible to constituent signatures.

**Current Practice**: Governance offices appear to be designed as independent entities without explicit consideration of how their interactions produce emergent cohomological properties at the polity level.

**Evidence**: Office records focus on individual scope and duties but don't analyze how combinations of offices create polity-level invariants that would be lost if offices operated in isolation.

**Recommended Action**: Analyze pairs/groups of offices to identify what cohomological invariants emerge only from their interaction and document these as federation-level properties requiring preservation.
