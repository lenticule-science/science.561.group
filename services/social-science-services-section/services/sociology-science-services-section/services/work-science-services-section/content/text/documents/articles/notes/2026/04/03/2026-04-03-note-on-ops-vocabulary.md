This plan cannot be fully specified until the research plan completes, because the research will surface terms we don't know we need.

## Known gaps (pre-research)

Based on the inventory done 2026-04-03, these concepts are likely needed. The research may confirm, revise, or expand this list.

### Planning stage
- schedule, milestone, deadline, estimate, capacity, priority, horizon, backlog, commitment

### Dispatch stage
- assignment, queue, routing, scope

### Execution stage
- execution state (the state machine), artifact, dependency satisfaction, interruption, handoff, work in progress

### Tracking stage
- status (as a defined concept), progress, metric, velocity, bottleneck, audit trail, rollup

### Review stage
- review, retrospective, approval, cadence, variance, lessons learned

### Feedback stage
- adjustment, course correction, experiment, regression, settlement (as a work concept, not just math)

### Cross-cutting
- event, record, cycle, flow

### Work types
- The four existing types may need revision or expansion based on the work-types research.

## Process

For each term:
1. Check what the research surveys say about the concept
2. Search the library for existing related content
3. Write the term following writing-terms conventions — secondary intension, grounded in at least one real disciplinary source, distinguishable from adjacent concepts
4. Connect to the existing work vocabulary via frontmatter relations
5. Note where the term becomes an operational type (used in frontmatter fields, file types, validation)

This is derivation-type work: we follow the writing-terms rules, but meaning emerges from the research.

## Task files

Task files will be created after the research plan produces its survey texts. Each survey will identify which terms are needed and what sources ground them. Creating task files now would be premature — we'd be defining terms before understanding them.
