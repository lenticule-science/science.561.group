## Annotation

Schmidt and Bannon's *articulation work* — the meshing of interdependent activities so they cohere into a workable arrangement — is the substantive content that predicated-work's closure-rule subsystem encodes. Their distinction between the *primary work* (the substantive task) and the *articulation work* (coordination overhead) is what the predicate-typed structure of $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}$ makes substrate-visible: action–agent and action–goal predicates carry primary work; action–action predicates (`decomposes-into`, `depends-on`) and Hohfeldian-position predicates carry articulation work.

The relevance to [closure-driven-coordination](../../../glossary/concepts/closure-driven-coordination.md): articulation work is the operational reality CSCW identified, and predicated-work formalizes it as the closure-rule subsystem rather than as separate "coordination machinery." The substrate is the articulation; there is no second-order layer.
