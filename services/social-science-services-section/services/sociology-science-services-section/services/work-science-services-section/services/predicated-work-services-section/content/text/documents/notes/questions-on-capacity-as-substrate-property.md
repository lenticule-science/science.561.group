# How is capacity expressed as a substrate-algebraic property?

The work vocabulary's [dispatch](../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/terms/dispatch.md) names three checks: capability, authority, capacity. The [dispatch morphism](../morphisms/dispatch.md) handles capability via residuation on bilattice-valued fibers and authority via the existence of a power [jural-relation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/jural-relation.md). Capacity — "the agent has bandwidth; current open assignments are below the agent's declared capacity" — is a *cardinality* condition: count the agent's open assignment cells, compare to a threshold.

Bilattice algebra does not natively encode cardinalities. A predication fiber's value at a cell is in $\mathbf{2}^\bowtie$; counting cells across an entity-row requires aggregation across the row, which is not a per-cell operation. Whether capacity is expressible as a closure rule (and which closure-rule-language fragment is needed for the cardinality bound), or whether capacity must live outside $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{op}}$ as substrate-resident scheduling logic, is open.

Until resolved, the dispatch morphism's "capacity check" is a substrate-side annotation rather than an operation in the substrate algebra.
