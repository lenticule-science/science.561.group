# Contract

The mathematical realization of a [contract](../../../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/theories/governance/terms/contract.md): a bilateral normative binding between two parties, generating a Hohfeldian web of duties and claims that persist independently of performance. Per the library's five-tuple definition, $\mathcal{C} = (P_1, P_2, \Phi_{12}, \Phi_{21}, V)$.

## Data

A contract $\mathcal{C}$ is a substrate entity carrying:

- $\langle \mathcal{C}, \texttt{first-party}, P_1\rangle = \mathbf{t}$
- $\langle \mathcal{C}, \texttt{second-party}, P_2\rangle = \mathbf{t}$
- $\langle \mathcal{C}, \texttt{P1-promise-set}, \Phi_{12}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ — references the cell-pattern $\Phi_{12}$ comprising the acts $P_1$ commits to perform for $P_2$
- $\langle \mathcal{C}, \texttt{P2-promise-set}, \Phi_{21}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ — symmetric for $P_2$
- $\langle \mathcal{C}, \texttt{formation-conditions}, V\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ — the validity predicate (offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality, or civil-law equivalents)

## Realization as paired jural relations

A contract instantiates *four* [jural relations](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/jural-relation.md) — two duties paired with their correlative claims, by [correlate-of](./correlate-of.md):

1. $R^{D}_{1}$ with `kind: duty`, `active-holder: P_1`, `passive-party: P_2`, `scope-action: Φ_{12}`. ($P_1$ owes $P_2$.)
2. $R^{C}_{1}$ with `kind: claim`, `active-holder: P_2`, `passive-party: P_1`, `scope-action: Φ_{12}`. ($P_2$ has a claim against $P_1$ for $\Phi_{12}$.) Generated by correlate-of from $R^D_1$.
3. $R^{D}_{2}$ with `kind: duty`, `active-holder: P_2`, `passive-party: P_1`, `scope-action: Φ_{21}`. ($P_2$ owes $P_1$.)
4. $R^{C}_{2}$ with `kind: claim`, `active-holder: P_1`, `passive-party: P_2`, `scope-action: Φ_{21}`. ($P_1$ has a claim against $P_2$ for $\Phi_{21}$.) Generated by correlate-of from $R^D_2$.

The four-relation pattern is what makes a contract *bilateral*. A unilateral obligation has only $\{R^D_1, R^C_1\}$; a contract has both pairs. The substrate-algebraic check: a candidate contract entity has all four jural-relation cells settled at $\mathbf{t}$.

## Five invariants as substrate properties

Per the library's five invariants, the substrate-algebraic conditions:

**1. Bilaterality.** All four jural-relation cells exist and are at $\mathbf{t}$. As substrate-condition: a closure rule that admits a contract increment checks for the presence of all four relations. A contract-attempt with only two relations (one direction) produces a *promise-or-gift* witness, not a contract.

**2. Consideration.** $\Phi_{12} \neq \emptyset$ and $\Phi_{21} \neq \emptyset$. As substrate condition: closure rule checks both promise-set cells reference non-empty cell-patterns. Past consideration is rejected: $\Phi_{21}$'s cells must be at stages $\geq$ the contract's formation stage.

**3. Normative persistence after formation.** Once formed, the contract persists by [settling-monotonicity](../../../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-monotonicity.md) until a discharge witness arrives. The cells of all four jural relations stay at $\mathbf{t}$ regardless of performance state. Performance settles the *promise-cells* ($\Phi_{12}$, $\Phi_{21}$); breach settles different cells; the underlying contractual-relation cells are independent.

**4. Discharge / breach / frustration / release.** Four ways the contract can retire:

- *Discharge* (full performance): both $\Phi_{12}$ and $\Phi_{21}$ are settled at $\mathbf{t}$ at completion stages. A closure rule fires retiring the four jural relations.
- *Breach*: one party fails to perform. A `breach-by-P_i-of-Φ_{ij}` cell settles to $\mathbf{t}$. The other party gains additional jural relations (claim for damages, possibly power to terminate).
- *Frustration*: external conditions (per $V$) become impossible. A `frustration-of-C` cell settles; closure rule retires the contract without breach.
- *Mutual release*: bilateral [novation](./novation.md) extinguishing both pairs of jural relations with three-party (here two-party) consent.

**5. Authority for formation.** Both parties must hold the [jural-relation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/jural-relation.md) of `kind: power` over their respective promise-set actions. Capacity (the meta-power to enter contracts) is a precondition; the contract's $V$ check verifies both parties' capacity.

## Contract vs. delegation

Per the library's enterprise-vs-contract distinction, both involve commitments but with structurally different cell-patterns:

| | Delegation | Contract |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | One-way (principal → beneficiary) | Bilateral |
| Authority | Transferred (with retained accountability) | Created (mutual) |
| Jural relations | 1 power + 1 liability | 2 duties + 2 claims (4 total) |
| Termination | Revocation by principal | Discharge / breach / frustration / release |

[Delegation](./delegation.md) creates a derived power; contract creates a mutual-binding. They are not reducible to each other. A delegation paired with a delegate's promise to perform creates a contract on top of a delegation; the two structures coexist.

## Contract formation as second-quartet exercise

Per the [hohfeldian-position](../../../../../../governance-services-section/services/hohfeldian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hohfeldian-position.md) text, contract formation is a second-quartet phenomenon. The substrate-algebraic content:

The four contractual jural relations — once formed by the offer-acceptance closure rule — are $\leq_k$-monotone at $\mathbf{t}$.
By the [predication-settling CALM corollary](../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/formalization/infomaterial/lib/infomaterial/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/references/settling-calm-corollary.md):

- Confirming "is contract $\mathcal{C}$ formed?" is coordination-free: any replica with local snapshots of the four jural-relation cells can confirm formation.
- Confirming "*breach* has occurred" is also partially coordination-free: the positive breach-witness cell $\langle \texttt{breach-by-P_i-of-Φ_{ij}}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ is $\leq_k$-monotone — once a breach is witnessed and settled, confirming that it occurred is coordination-free.
- Confirming "*no* breach has occurred" requires coordination: it is the absence of a breach-witness cell, which is anti-monotone.

For distributed commercial substrates (distributed trade platforms, multi-party smart-contract systems): confirming that a contract is in force and that no breach has been witnessed is the combination of two coordination-free positive queries.
Disputing breach — "did the party *not* breach?" — requires coordination.

The [truth-knowledge long exact sequence](../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/references/truth-knowledge-cohomology-long-exact-sequence.md) applied to the contract's jural-relation sheaf $\mathcal{F}_\mathcal{C}$:

**Clean contract (no breach):** $\mathcal{F}^{(\top)}_\mathcal{C} = 0$.
The four jural relations are consistently settled; the LES collapses to $H^n_t \cong H^n_k$.
No cohomological obstruction.

**Contested breach** (one party asserts breach, the other asserts performance): The breach-witness cell and performance-witness cell are both asserted — $\langle \texttt{breach-by-P_1}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ and $\langle \texttt{performed-by-P_1}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ — driving the relevant cell toward $\top$.
The inconsistency sheaf $\mathcal{F}^{(\top)}_\mathcal{C}$ is non-zero; the connecting morphism $\partial: H^0(\mathcal{F}^{(\top)}) \to H^1_t$ is non-zero.
The $H^1_t$ class is the substrate-algebraic signature of *unresolved contractual dispute*: a degree-1 cohomological obstruction encoding the contradiction between breach-assertion and performance-assertion.

Resolution (adjudication by an institution, or mutual agreement): the dispute is settled in one direction; the breach cell or the performance cell is definitively established; $\mathcal{F}^{(\top)}_\mathcal{C}$ returns to zero; the LES collapses.
The substrate-algebraic content of "the dispute has been resolved" is: $\mathcal{F}^{(\top)}_\mathcal{C} = 0$ after adjudication, $H^1_t$-obstruction class becomes trivial.

1. *Offer* — $P_1$ exercises a power to create a power-and-liability in $P_2$. Substrate increment: cell `offer-from-P_1-to-P_2-on-Φ` at $\mathbf{t}$, plus a new jural-relation $R^P_{\mathrm{accept}}$ with `kind: power`, `active-holder: P_2`, `scope-action: accept-or-reject-the-offer`.
2. *Acceptance* — $P_2$ exercises the just-created power. Substrate increment: cell `acceptance-by-P_2-of-offer` at $\mathbf{t}$. A closure rule fires: if both offer and acceptance cells are at $\mathbf{t}$ and $V$ holds, instantiate the four contractual jural relations.
3. *Formation* — the four jural relations settle at $\mathbf{t}$ jointly. The contract is now formed and operative.

Formation is *not* the same as performance: the relations exist (cells settled) regardless of whether either party has yet performed. The substrate-algebraic distinction between *being bound* and *having performed* is exactly the bilattice value at the duty cell ($\mathbf{t}$, contract-bound) vs. at the promise cell ($\bot$, not yet performed; $\mathbf{t}$, performed; $\mathbf{f}$, breached).

## Contract within an institution

Most contracts are formed within an [institution](./institution.md) — a normative system whose rules define what counts as a valid contract, what consideration counts, what discharge means, etc. The contract's $V$ is *institution-relative*: the same offer-acceptance pattern that forms a contract under common-law fails to form one under civil-law if `cause` (rather than `consideration`) is absent. As substrate-condition: $V$ is a closure-rule subsystem of the relevant institution's $\mathcal{S}_I$, not a stand-alone validity check.

This is the substrate-algebraic content of "contracts are creatures of legal systems": the binding force comes from the institution; the contract is one form the institution's rules generate.
