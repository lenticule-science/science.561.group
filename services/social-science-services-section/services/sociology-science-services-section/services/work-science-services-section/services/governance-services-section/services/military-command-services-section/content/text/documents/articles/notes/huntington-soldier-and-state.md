Samuel Huntington's *The Soldier and the State* is a foundational work on the military profession and civil-military relations in the United States. For this school it is primarily useful as an institutional lens: it helps explain why service branches behave as enduring organizations with distinctive cultures and interests, and how professional identity becomes a constraint on reform.

The book's arguments are contested, but its framing of professionalism and institutional autonomy remains influential.
