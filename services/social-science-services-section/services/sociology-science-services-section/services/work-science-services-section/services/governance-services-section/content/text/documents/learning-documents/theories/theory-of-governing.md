# Theory of Governing

Governing is the fourth moment of existence: the open field of work brought under a settled, bounded order. Its face is the individual, the bounded self to whom deeds are imputed. The settled order and the settled self are two faces of one fixed point, which is why denying agency is one of the operations of power.

This theory states what a governance act is, how governance structure is generated from three primitive judgments, and how the Group's rules and requirements are derived rather than written by hand.

## The Governance Thesis

A process π is a **governance act** if and only if it has all three of:

1. **Attribution.** π is done *by* a collective C, not merely by a member who happens to belong to C.
2. **Binding.** π produces or changes a normative constraint on at least one member of C.
3. **Becoming.** π changes C's state.

No one condition picks out governance. A market exchange has becoming and perhaps binding but no attribution. A preamble has attribution but neither binds nor changes state. The thesis is the theory's analog of the Church–Turing thesis.

## The primitive calculus

Six sorts: collectives, members, acts, norms, constraints, states. Three judgments:

- attribution  a :: C — act a is attributed to collective C;
- binding  n · m ▷ k — norm n binds member m to constraint k;
- becoming  s ⇝_a s′ — act a takes state s to s′.

Gov is the syntactic category of this calculus (Lambek). Its structure is exactly what the calculus forces.

## Laws

- **G1. Standard modalities are derived.** Obligation Oφ := ∃n. ∀m. n · m ▷ require(φ); permission and prohibition follow. Agentive STIT is attribution to a singleton plus becoming. Coalition ability and counts-as are definable the same way. None is imported as a separate logic.
- **G2. Operativity is FOUR-valued.** Each judgment settles independently in FOUR: attribution may be contested, binding claimed but unenforced, becoming partial. The operativity of an act is the knowledge meet attribution ⊗k binding ⊗k becoming.
- **G3. Stratification.** At level n+1 the states are the level-n apparatus. Hart's primary rules are level 0, rules of change level 1, rules of recognition and adjudication level 2; Ostrom's operational, collective-choice and constitutional levels are the truncation at three. Gov is the colimit of the tower. A constituent act founding a collective is an ordinary act one level up. A self-amending act lives one level above the apparatus it changes, so no paradox arises.
- **G4. Office is not incumbent.** An office is a bearer-independent position with a deontic profile mapping each work it governs to exactly one of duty, liberty, deontic power or deontic immunity. Incumbency is the double settlement of recognition (the honor axis, backward) and induction (the munus axis, forward): vacant is (⊥, ⊥), dignitas is (t, t), ⊤ in either is a repair condition. The office's profile passes whole to the next holder.
- **G5. Accountability is total and non-transferable.** Accountability is a triple (bearer, scope, A) with A mapping every outcome in scope to the bearer. Delegation distributes authority and never lifts accountability.
- **G6. Delegation is residuation.** A delegated grant lands at a ⇒ g, where a is the principal's authority over the parent scope. It is derivative (no one grants what they do not hold), bounded (granting the whole scope is abdication, not delegation), and revocable (revocation drives the grant cell to ⊤ and then to f).
- **G7. Constitution is counts-as.** "X counts as Y in C": a new level of description on the same entity, not a new entity and not a replacement.
- **G8. Governance is executable semantics.** A governance act changes the admissible transition space A(E) of an enterprise. A governance claim that changes nothing about what is lawful, obligatory, forbidden or reviewable is not yet operative.

## Directives, policies, rules

The Leader's ontology (2026-09-11): "rules are derived from policies which are in turn derived from directives. I issue directives: (subject, predicate, object) triplets where every S & O is some resource in our system and P is some Action an Agent can make, if capable. Policies are the directives active over a resource, and Rules are those policies which apply under the preconditions of a program."

In the theory's terms:

- A **directive** is a predication cell (S, A, O) whose predicate A is an action an agent can perform. Uttering it is a governance act, attributed to the collective at the issuer's level.
- A **policy** is the projection of the directive set onto one resource: the directives whose subject or object is that resource. Subject-projection and object-projection are two of predication's four generators.
- A **rule** is the specialization of a policy to a program's precondition. Specialization is the third generator. A program's precondition is a position in an arena, so a rule is a constraint on the moves legal at that position.

Rules are therefore generated from directives by predication's own generators. A list of hand-written rule files is not a rule system: nothing generated it, nothing derives from it, and no collective is attributed for it.

## What a requirement is

Required, derived from G1, G2, G8 and the Group's one work procedure (require → specify → design → fabricate):

A **requirement** is an obligation Oφ whose content φ is an acceptance condition on the plays of an enterprise's arena, issued by the party whose need it is.

- Its **attribution** is to the owning enterprise: the section or micro-enterprise whose arena it constrains.
- Its **binding** falls on that enterprise's offices through its policies and rules.
- Its **becoming** is the change in the enterprise's admissible transitions.
- Its **content** is about the world, not the machine. It names an outcome and its judge, never a builder, and it carries checks that can fail.
- It is **met** when the cell (requirement, met-by, play) settles to t: a WIT by the promiser that delivered, and an ACK by the asker, a different party, through an assay the promiser does not control.

A requirement filed in a global folder has no attributed collective. Its attribution settles to ⊥, or to ⊤ when several sections claim it, so by G2 its operativity is never t. That is the precise sense in which "misfiled handwritten requirements" fail: they are inoperative by construction. A properly derived local requirement relates to Arena and Strategy because its content is a condition on the plays of the owning enterprise's arena and its satisfaction is decided by the strategies played there.

## Rendanheyi, formally

- **Capability cell.** A pair (active site, binding sites): the internal execution that realizes a cell's one capability, opaque to every other cell; and the schemas, contracts, duty claims and interfaces through which others may act on it. An office's profile is binding site; an incumbent's conduct is active site.
- **R1. Admissibility (zero distance).** A governance action is admissible if and only if it acts on binding sites and never writes an active site. This is líng jùlí, the requirement that nothing stand between the person doing work and the user whose value that work creates, and it excludes middle management, internal proxies and aggregate measures. An internal specification or requirements document standing in for the user is an internal proxy.
- **R2. Governability.** A coordination graph is governable only if its algebraic connectivity outruns its cells' drift: λ₂(L_G) ≳ max δ_c. Below that, governance corrects a configuration the cell has already left. The three futures are rising incoherence, a new organism boundary, or interventionism, which is large-enterprise disease.
- **R3. Error catastrophe.** Correction at fidelity f every τ has a threshold. Above it, no local diligence keeps the organism coherent; only reducing drift or raising per-pass fidelity does.
- **R4. Genets and borders.** Cells under one correction process transfer value at cost. Crossing a genet boundary requires priced exchange (Coase's boundary of the firm; mycorrhizal markets reach the same partition).
- **R5. The micro-enterprise is a program.** E = (regime, local law, state, append-only witnessed history, resources, offices, customer relations, border, refusal and continuation conditions). The executor is not the enterprise; the office is not the enterprise; a swarm is not the enterprise. Sovereignty is a property of the program's continuation space: being reachable is not being writable.
- **R6. The customer relation is the axis.** customer ↔ enterprise-program, not manager → worker → customer. A xiaowei holds the three rights (decision, personnel, distribution) in full and a contract to a user; a unit that draws a budget and answers to a manager is a department, whatever it is called.
- **R7. Union without interior.** A Union-wide object is legitimate only if it can be reconstructed and challenged from constituent evidence without write authority over that evidence. Harmony is not consistency: compose where composition is warranted, preserve witnessed differences where it is not, and continue without a universal interior.

## Consequences for the Group's systems

Required:

1. **Rules are generated.** The rule system is derived from attributed directives by projection and specialization, and every rule names the arena positions it constrains. The existing hand-written records under `content/requirements`, `data/requirements` and `lib/agency/rules` are not authority.
2. **Requirements live with their owners.** Each requirement is attributed to the enterprise whose arena it constrains, carries falsifiable checks, and is met by WIT plus ACK.
3. **No allocation across enterprises.** Governance acts on binding sites. Nothing outside a micro-enterprise writes its work, its staffing or its model choice.
4. **Offices, incumbency, delegation and accountability are cell systems** whose states are read off settled cells, never stored.
5. **Governability is monitored.** The correction cadence and the coordination graph's connectivity are measured, because below threshold no amount of local care keeps the Group coherent.
