The duty of plan stewardship is the obligation on the owner of a [plan](../../../terms/plan.md) to ensure it converges toward its [goal](../../../../../../philosophy/terms/quality.md). The steward does not necessarily DO the work — the steward ensures the work gets done, by maintaining the plan's task list, tracking dependencies, surfacing blockers, and reporting progress.

## The five-tuple

| Component | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Bearer** | The office designated as the plan's owner |
| **Position** | The owning office |
| **Normative system** | The governance framework governing the plan |
| **Required act** | Maintain tasks, track dependencies, surface blockers, converge toward definition-of-done |
| **Correlative party** | The containing [project](../../../terms/project.md) and the [goal](../../../../../../philosophy/terms/quality.md) it serves |

## Distinction from the duty of execution

The [duty of execution](./duty-of-execution.md) applies to individual tasks. Plan stewardship applies to the plan as a whole. A plan steward may [delegate](../../../terms/delegation.md) every task to other offices and execute none personally — the duty is to ensure convergence, not to perform work. This is the distinction between operational accountability (did the plan succeed?) and task accountability (did this specific task get done?).

## Discharge

[Discharged](./discharge.md) when the plan's definition-of-done is met, or the plan is explicitly abandoned with recorded reasons. A plan that lingers with no events is evidence of non-discharge — the steward is not maintaining it.
