## Scope

The governance domain (30 terms, 10 Hohfeldian sub-terms, 6 jurisdiction sub-terms, 1 survey text) draws heavily on two traditions:

1. **Hohfeldian legal theory** — appears in 6+ term files; provides the relational structure (duty-claim, power-liability, privilege-no-claim, immunity-disability)
2. **Category-theoretic formalization** — the nuclear/bilattice framework appears in nearly every governance term as a "nuclear reading" section

These are valuable but they dominate. The research analysis found these traditions **absent**:

| Missing tradition | What it would add |
|---|---|
| Cybernetics / systems theory (Wiener, Ashby, Beer) | Self-regulation, feedback, adaptive governance |
| Indigenous governance beyond Haudenosaunee | Consensus, kinship-based accountability, non-state governance |
| Institutional logics (Thornton, Ocasio, Lounsbury) | How multiple normative systems coexist and conflict |
| Black radical tradition (Robinson, Gilmore) | Governance as inherited control/freedom, abolition as governance theory |
| Feminist organizational theory (Acker, Calás & Smircich) | How governance encodes power asymmetrically, invisible labor |
| Actor-Network Theory (Latour, Callon) | Non-human agents in governance, material agency |
| Pragmatism (Dewey, contemporary) | Experimental governance, deliberative democracy |

## Approach

Phase 1 (parallel surveys): Each survey task reads external sources and produces a survey text in the governance domain. These are inquiry work — the researcher reads, decides what's valuable, writes it as library content.

Phase 2 (enrichment): With broader traditions available, revisit existing governance terms. Not rewriting — adding "Competing accounts" or "Other traditions" sections that reference the new survey texts. This prevents the existing Hohfeldian/categorical work from being the only lens.

## What this is NOT

This is not about replacing or diminishing the Hohfeldian framework. Hohfeld's relational structure is genuinely useful. The problem is monoculture: when every governance concept is framed through Hohfeld + category theory, we lose the ability to see what those frameworks miss.
