Data collected January 2022 from 52,370 trips by 1,088 drivers across six ride-hailing and delivery platforms in five metro areas (Boston, Chicago, Los Angeles, San Francisco Bay, Seattle); published via National Equity Atlas.

## Why this is central

This is the most methodologically direct post-Proposition-22 measurement of net driver pay: rideshare passenger drivers earned a median $7.12 per hour net of expenses and before tips, against California's contemporary $16 minimum wage, and delivery workers averaged $5.93 per hour net of expenses and before tips. Because the data postdates Proposition 22's 2020 passage and its 2021 effective date, it measures the earnings guarantee's real-world floor rather than the ballot measure's promised one, which is exactly the gap the [note on American gig work as a workforce and a cost structure](../notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-american-gig-work-as-a-workforce-and-a-cost-structure.md), section 2, cites it for.

## What it does not settle

The study's five metro areas are not exclusively California, so its California-specific Proposition 22 framing applies most directly to the Los Angeles and San Francisco Bay Area trip data within the sample rather than to all 52,370 trips; this note did not obtain the California-only subsample's figures separately (the fetch of the National Equity Atlas page itself returned an HTTP 403, so this carrier relies on the study's widely corroborated headline figures rather than a directly re-read primary table).
