Multi-apping is running two or more gig-platform apps at once, or switching between them through a shift, so that whichever app offers the best-paying task at a given moment is the one the worker accepts. A rideshare driver running Uber and Lyft simultaneously, or a delivery worker running DoorDash and Instacart, is multi-apping when they compare offers across apps rather than committing to one platform's queue.

Multi-apping is distinguished from simply holding accounts on several platforms and working for each on separate days: the defining feature is comparing live offers across platforms in the same window of time, which is exactly the pattern that gave rise to worker-built comparison tools such as Driver's Seat Cooperative, whose app synced with permission across multiple gig apps to show drivers combined earnings and trends no single platform's own dashboard reported ([The Rockefeller Foundation 2023](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-american-gig-work-as-a-workforce-and-a-cost-structure.md)).

The consequence of multi-apping for measurement is that no single platform's earnings statement, mileage log, or hours count can state a worker's true [net hourly rate](./net-hourly-rate.md): a multi-apping worker's real vehicle costs, [deadhead miles](./deadhead-miles.md), and hours worked are the sum across every app running that day, and a computation that reads only one platform's statement will systematically overstate the worker's net pay by omitting the unpaid time and mileage spent between apps.
