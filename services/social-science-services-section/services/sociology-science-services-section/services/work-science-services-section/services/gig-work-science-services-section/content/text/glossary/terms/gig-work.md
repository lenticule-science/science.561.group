Gig work is paid work obtained task-by-task or trip-by-trip, with no ongoing employment relationship and no guaranteed hours. The Federal Reserve's Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking uses a broad definition covering reselling goods, renting property, and short-term tasks such as driving or delivery; under that definition 20 percent of U.S. adults did some gig activity in the month before the 2024 survey wave, and 31 percent of those who did said they would have trouble making ends meet without it ([Federal Reserve Board 2025](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-american-gig-work-as-a-workforce-and-a-cost-structure.md)). The Bureau of Labor Statistics measures a narrower, main-job version through its Contingent Worker Supplement: 7.4 percent of total U.S. employment (11.9 million workers) held independent-contractor status as their sole or main job in July 2023, a category that includes gig work but is not limited to it — construction and real estate contracting are larger shares of that population than platform work ([BLS 2024](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-american-gig-work-as-a-workforce-and-a-cost-structure.md)).

Gig work is distinguished from ordinary employment by the absence of the employer relationship: there is no scheduled shift, no guarantee of continuing work, and — in nearly every current U.S. arrangement — no employer withholding income tax or paying the employer's half of payroll tax, because the worker is classified as an [independent contractor](./independent-contractor.md) rather than an employee. It is distinguished from [platform work](./platform-work.md), its narrower and more specific form, by not requiring an app or a company's software to assign, price, and dispatch the task; a lawn-mowing job arranged by a paper flyer is gig work but not platform work.

The consequence for a gig worker is structural rather than incidental: because no employer withholds tax, sets aside payroll-tax contributions, or guarantees a minimum wage, the entire burden of computing take-home pay net of vehicle costs, platform fees, and self-employment tax — the [net hourly rate](./net-hourly-rate.md) — and of setting aside quarterly estimated tax falls on the worker, with no institutional backstop unless the worker or a third party builds one.
