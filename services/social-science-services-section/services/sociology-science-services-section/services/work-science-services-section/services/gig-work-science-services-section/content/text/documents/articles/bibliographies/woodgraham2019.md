*Work, Employment and Society* 33(1), 2019, pp. 56-75.

## Why this is central

This paper generalizes Rosenblat and Stark's single-platform finding into a cross-platform claim: gig workers experience real schedule autonomy and low pay, social isolation, and algorithmic control at the same time. Autonomy and algorithmic control are not opposite ends of one scale on these platforms; they co-occur, which is why a worker can accurately report feeling both free and controlled without contradiction. This is the reading the [note on American gig work as a workforce and a cost structure](../notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-american-gig-work-as-a-workforce-and-a-cost-structure.md), section 7, uses to connect the algorithmic-management literature to the critical-theory note's freedom/imposed-unholding distinction.

## What it does not settle

The paper's fieldwork is international (drawing substantially on remote digital labor platforms and Southeast Asian and Sub-Saharan African gig workers in the companion 2019 *Sociology* paper by the same authors), so its generalization to the specifically American, physical-labor gig work (rideshare, delivery) this note covers is an extension by the note, not a claim the paper itself makes about the U.S. market.
