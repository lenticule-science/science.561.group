An independent contractor is a worker classified, under federal and state legal tests, as running their own business rather than as an employee of the company that pays them, which removes that company's obligations for minimum wage, overtime, payroll-tax withholding, and most benefits. The Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 7.4 percent of total U.S. employment, 11.9 million workers, as independent contractors on their sole or main job in July 2023, up from 6.9 percent in May 2017, spanning industries from construction and real estate to app-based transportation and delivery ([U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics 2024](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-american-gig-work-as-a-workforce-and-a-cost-structure.md)).

Independent-contractor status is distinguished from employee status by the legal test applied, not by the actual character of the work performed: a driver who is told what to wear, how to interact with customers, and is deactivated for a low rating can still be classified an independent contractor if the applicable test (common-law control, the ABC test, or a statutory carve-out) is satisfied on paper. California's Proposition 22 (2020) created exactly such a carve-out for app-based transportation and delivery drivers, exempting them from the state's ABC test in exchange for a narrower earnings guarantee and benefits scheme of the ballot measure's own design; a coalition of drivers and unions challenged the carve-out as unconstitutional, and the California Supreme Court unanimously upheld it in Castellanos v. State of California, decided 2024-07-25 ([Supreme Court of California 2024](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/05/2026-09-05-note-on-american-gig-work-as-a-workforce-and-a-cost-structure.md)).

The consequence of independent-contractor classification for [gig work](./gig-work.md) is that every obligation an employer would otherwise carry — withholding income tax, paying the employer half of payroll tax, guaranteeing a minimum wage, providing unemployment insurance — instead falls to the worker or goes unmet, which is why [self-employment tax](./self-employment-tax.md), quarterly estimated payments, and the [standard mileage rate](./standard-mileage-rate.md) deduction exist as the worker's own substitute machinery for what a payroll department would otherwise handle.
