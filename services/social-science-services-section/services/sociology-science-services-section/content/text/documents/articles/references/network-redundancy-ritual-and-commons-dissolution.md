# Network Redundancy, Ritual, and Commons Dissolution

Companion bibliography to "Cycles, Ceremony, and the Empirics of Held Structure" (2026-08-26). Entries are grouped by the section of that note they support. Where a finding has a known solidity problem — a retraction, an experimenter-blind confound, an ecological-design limitation, an unresolved dispute — it is flagged explicitly in the entry rather than left implicit.

## Network science and redundancy

**Centola, D., & Macy, M. (2007). Complex Contagions and the Weakness of Long Ties.** *American Journal of Sociology*, 113(3), 702-734. Establishes that behaviors requiring social reinforcement — costly cooperation, contested norm adoption — spread only across "wide bridges," i.e. multiple, redundant, overlapping ties, not single long-range ties. Simple contagion (information) spreads on trees; complex contagion (trust-dependent coordination) needs cycles. Foundational and widely cited; solid.

**Centola, D. (2018). *How Behavior Spreads: The Science of Complex Contagions*.** Princeton University Press. Book-length elaboration and empirical extension (including Centola's own networked lab experiments) of the 2007 result across health behaviors, political mobilization, and innovation adoption. Solid; the standard reference for the complex-contagion mechanism.

**Krackhardt, D. (1999). The Ties that Torture: Simmelian Tie Analysis in Organizations.** *Research in the Sociology of Organizations*, 16, 183-210. Formalizes Georg Simmel's observation that triadic closure changes the character of ties (see the glossary entry). Establishes Simmelian ties as a distinct, measurable network object with independent effects on organizational behavior beyond dyadic tie strength. Solid within organizational-network research; the underlying theoretical claim (Simmel 1908) is over a century old and durable.

**2024 multiplex-network studies of Honduran village networks.** Find that cohesion outcomes track multiplex/redundant tie overlap more closely than raw tie count. Supports the redundancy-as-asset reading; a single study population, so generalization should be treated as suggestive rather than settled.

**2026 network-resilience special issue.** Names "diversified and redundant ties" explicitly as load-bearing infrastructure for network resilience. Recent synthesis-level claim; solid as a statement of where the field's consensus currently sits, not yet a single decisive empirical test.

**Battiston, F., et al. (2020). Networks beyond pairwise interactions: structure and dynamics.** *Physics Reports*, 874, 1-92. **(2021)** The physics of higher-order networks. *Nature Physics*, 17, 1093-1098. **(2025)** synthesis in *Nature Reviews Physics*. Establishes simplicial complexes and hypergraphs as the correct mathematical objects for group (not just pairwise) interaction, with higher Betti numbers as the natural generalization of cycle rank. Foundational, well-cited physics literature; solid as mathematics. Its application to kinship/gift-exchange/commons data specifically remains an open niche, not yet attempted (see the audited note's §1).

**Sizemore, A. E., Giusti, C., & Bassett, D. S. (2018). Classification of weighted networks through mesoscale homological features.** and related work in *Network Neuroscience*. Treats persistent one-dimensional cycles (nonzero Betti-1 classes) in human brain connectome data as functional "cavities" — structure that does real work precisely because it is not resolved into a tree. Solid within computational neuroscience; the social-network analogy is the audited note's extrapolation, not a claim made in the original papers.

**Iacopini, I., et al. (2019). Simplicial models of social contagion.** *Nature Communications*, 10, 2485. Extends complex-contagion dynamics onto simplicial (group-interaction) structures rather than pairwise graphs. Closest existing formal apparatus to what the audited theory needs; solid as published, but not built with the cycle-equals-ceremony reading in mind.

**2024 *PNAS* paper on higher-order homophily.** Extends homophily (like-attracts-like) findings to group-level (simplicial) interactions rather than dyads. Recent, solid within its own claims; adjacent rather than directly confirmatory of the audited theory.

**2025 arXiv preprint proposing "thick"/"cohesive" Betti numbers as resilience metrics; 2026 *Scientific Reports* paper linking persistent cycles to network resilience.** Both recent and specifically relevant to treating cycle rank as a resilience measure; UNTESTED against real kinship/community/commons data as of this audit — exactly the empty niche the audited note flags.

## Ritual, synchrony, and collective effervescence

**Durkheim, É. (1912). *Les formes élémentaires de la vie religieuse*.** Originates the concept of collective effervescence: the heightened emotional state produced by ritual co-presence, theorized as the generative mechanism of social solidarity and the sacred. Foundational to the entire tradition audited here; a theoretical origin point, not itself an empirical test by modern standards.

**Bouchat, P., Pizarro, J. J., & Páez, D. (2024). meta-analysis, *Group Processes & Intergroup Relations*.** 13 studies. Finds robust associations between perceived emotional synchrony and self-transcendent emotion/wellbeing, a weaker but present prosociality association. **CONTESTED for magnitude** — see the Atwood/*Open Mind* entry below, which shows much of the underlying synchrony-prosociality literature is confounded.

**Rennung, M., & Göritz, A. S. (2016). Prosocial consequences of interpersonal synchrony.** *Zeitschrift für Psychologie*, 224(3), 168-189. Meta-analysis, 60 studies, medium synchrony-prosociality effect size. Same caveat applies: the underlying primary literature has since been shown to be substantially confounded by non-blind experimenters.

**Atwood, S., et al.; and the 2023 *Open Mind* exchange on synchrony and expectancy.** Shows that most synchrony-prosociality experiments were conducted with experimenters aware of condition assignment, and that effects shrink substantially — in some cases to null — once expectancy effects are controlled. **This is a load-bearing caveat**: it does not eliminate the synchrony-bonding mechanism (the naltrexone studies below are a cleaner, non-behavioral test that survives this critique) but it means published effect sizes for synchrony-driven prosociality should be treated as probable overestimates.

**Whitehouse, H. (multiple works). Modes of religiosity; identity fusion typology.** Establishes a durable descriptive typology distinguishing high-frequency/low-arousal ("doctrinal") from low-frequency/high-arousal ("imagistic") ritual modes, and links high-arousal ritual to identity fusion (see the glossary entry). Solid as typology and widely used; the typology itself is not disputed even where its scale-up application (next entry) failed.

**Whitehouse, H., et al. (2019). Complex societies precede moralizing gods throughout world history.** *Nature*, 568, 226-229. Used the Seshat databank to argue against the "Big Gods" causal ordering (moralizing gods causing, rather than following, social complexity). **RETRACTED.** See the next entry — this is the single most important negative result in the bibliography and should never be cited without noting the retraction.

**Beheim, B., et al. (2021). Treatment of missing data determined conclusions regarding moralizing gods.** *Nature*, 595, E29-E34. Reanalysis showing the 2019 Seshat finding reversed once missing data were handled correctly, prompting the retraction above. Solid, and decisive: the civilizational-scale ritual/religiosity-cooperation claim currently has no standing large-scale confirmatory result.

**Norenzayan, A., & Henrich, J. (multiple works). "Big Gods" priming literature.** Meta-analytically robust at the level of individual lab experiments (approximately 26 experiments show priming effects of supernatural-monitoring cues on prosocial behavior). Solid at lab scale; **not shown** to aggregate into society-level cooperation gains — the lab-to-society bridge remains an open gap, sharpened by the Seshat retraction above.

**Tarr, B., Launay, J., & Dunbar, R. I. M. (multiple double-blind naltrexone studies).** Show that opioid-receptor blockade (naltrexone) measurably reduces ritual-synchrony-induced social bonding, corroborated by pain-threshold field studies. **The strongest causal evidence in the entire bibliography** for a specific mechanism (the endorphin system) underlying ritual bonding; solid, because it is pharmacological and double-blind rather than behavioral self-report. Supports small-group bonding function specifically; does not address large-scale economic or civilizational claims.

**Hayden, B. (2014). *The Power of Feasts: From Prehistory to the Present*.** Cambridge University Press. Documents feasting as literal debt-cycling, alliance-formation, and redistribution infrastructure across archaeological and ethnographic cases. **The strongest limb of the P3 evidence base** — feasting-as-infrastructure is well-documented historically and ethnographically, not merely theorized.

**Dietler, M. (2001). Theorizing the Feast: Rituals of Consumption, Commensal Politics, and Power in African Contexts.** In Dietler & Hayden (eds.), *Feasts: Archaeological and Ethnographic Perspectives on Food, Politics, and Power*. Smithsonian Institution Press. Companion theoretical framework to Hayden's empirical case; solid within archaeology/anthropology of feasting.

## Enclosure, allotment, and commons dissolution

**Allen, R. C. (multiple works). English enclosure raised rents without raising productivity.** Long-standing position in the economic-history debate; **CONTESTED**, see Clark and Heldring/Robinson/Vollmer below.

**Clark, G. (multiple works). English enclosure produced modest productivity gains.** The competing long-standing position; **CONTESTED**, unresolved as a pure Allen-vs-Clark dispute, though see the next entry for the most recent causal evidence.

**Heldring, L., Robinson, J. A., & Vollmer, S. (NBER Working Paper w29772, updated 2024). The Long-Run Impact of Agricultural Enclosure.** Most recent causal-identification attempt: finds English Parliamentary enclosure raised agricultural yields approximately 45% and spurred innovation. **This directly challenges P2's aggregate-value form** — it does not support an "enclosure destroys aggregate value" reading for England specifically. Recent and methodologically the strongest of the three enclosure entries, though a single research team's result, not yet independently replicated.

**Dippel, C., Frye, D., & Leonard, B. (2025). The impact of US assimilation and allotment policy on American Indian mortality.** *PNAS*, 122. Culminating paper of a multi-year research program: finds Dawes Act allotment raised young American Indian mortality by roughly one-third (an estimated ~20% decline in life expectancy at birth), often on objectively better land than what was previously held; Native landholdings fell from 138 million to 52 million acres by 1934. **Close to a clean confirmation of circulation-destruction-beyond-asset-accounting** — the strongest single piece of evidence for P2 in its distributional/circulation-specific form, precisely because the human cost is directly measured rather than inferred from aggregate output.

**De Moor, T., et al. (2020). Ostrom's promise... assessing commons persistence.** *PLOS ONE*, 15. Survey of 400+ Dutch commons over roughly a millennium, finding "mass extinction" of commons peaking around 1850. Descriptive base-rate evidence for long-run commons attrition; solid as description, not itself a causal test of what destroys them.

## Diversity of holding arrangements and resilience

**Ostrom, E., & Folke, C. (foundational social-ecological resilience literature). "Response diversity" as a resilience mechanism.** Establishes that heterogeneous institutional/functional arrangements across a system provide shock-buffering that uniform arrangements cannot, because different arrangements fail under different stresses. Foundational and well-established within social-ecological systems research; solid.

**Chandler, M. J., & Lalonde, C. (1998). Cultural continuity as a hedge against suicide in Canada's First Nations.** *Transcultural Psychiatry*, 35(2), 191-219. Finds community-level cultural-continuity markers (self-government, active land claims, control over ceremonial and health services) predict dramatically lower youth suicide rates across roughly 200 British Columbia bands; data replicated across the 1993-2000 period. **Caveat, load-bearing:** the design is ecological (band-level aggregates, not individual-level data), small-n relative to the number of markers examined, and correlational — causal direction and confounding with the differential severity of colonial violence experienced by different bands are both unresolved. The association has replicated, which is more than most findings in this bibliography can claim, but it has not been isolated causally.

**Burack, J. A., Bombay, A., & Kirmayer, L. J. (eds., 2024). Special issue reaffirming Chandler & Lalonde's influence.** *Transcultural Psychiatry*. Confirms the finding's continued standing in the field and its adoption into British Columbia public-health surveillance practice; does not resolve the ecological-design caveat above.

## Social capital and austerity natural experiments

**Putnam, R. D. (2020). *Bowling Alone: The Collapse and Revival of American Community*, 20th anniversary edition.** Simon & Schuster. Reaffirms the original associational-decline thesis with two additional decades of data. Newer composite social-capital measures find a non-smooth (lag-then-collapse-consistent) rather than steadily declining trajectory. **Caveat:** critics fault the original and updated analyses for underweighting racial, ethnic, and gendered variation in associational life, which matters for generalizing any derived measure (such as ceremony fraction) across communities unevenly represented in the underlying data.

**UNISON (2026). UK public library staffing and closures report.** Documents 47% library staffing cuts and 276+ net library closures in England, 2010-2024. Solid as a documented austerity natural experiment; the causal link from closures to specific trust/cooperation outcomes is not established within this report itself.

**Institute for Fiscal Studies (2024-25). Sure Start program analyses.** Finds the program generated causally identified gains (GCSE improvement, an estimated £2.4bn long-run benefit) during its operation. The subsequent 73% funding cut and 1,340+ centre closures coincide with worse child-development and obesity outcomes in longitudinal ecological studies. **Caveat:** the closure-effect identification is markedly weaker than the original build-effect identification — closures were not randomly assigned, and post-closure outcome data are ecological rather than experimental.

## Data infrastructure for future tests

**National Congregations Study (NORC/ARDA, 1998-2019).** Approximately 5,300 congregations surveyed across multiple waves; proposed here as a source for building co-attendance hypergraph nerves to compute Betti-1 as a network-validation check on the ceremony-fraction measure. Well-established, high-quality survey infrastructure; has not previously been used for this specific topological purpose.

***Nature* (2026). "Punctuated Decline of Human Cooperation."** Cooperative-lending panel data: 47,931 payments across 7,108 borrowers. Proposed as a second candidate dataset for the same Betti-1 validation exercise. Recent; solid as a data source, untested for this specific application.
