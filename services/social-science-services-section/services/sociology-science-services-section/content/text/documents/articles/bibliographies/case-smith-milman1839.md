# Superior Court of Delaware; opinion by Chief Justice John M. Clayton (1839) — Smith v. Milman

**Canonical citation:** 2 Del. 497, decided July 5, 1839.
**Original or catalogued text:** https://openjurist.org/2/del/497/smith-v-milman-3381841
**Reading grade (2026-10-17):** Public-domain complete judicial opinion read in OpenJurist transcription sourced from CourtListener; original historical reporter not image-collated.

## Case facts / exact analytical contribution

The freedom petitioner Stephen was born to Hessy, an enslaved woman whose lifetime tenancy was divided among heirs by Rachel Marvel's 1791 will. The tribunal used Delaware's life-tenant rule, expressly rejecting use of partus sequitur ventrem to decide which enslaver owned the child; it held Stephen not owned by Nancy Smith and ordered his freedom under Ann Smith's deed of manumission. Ownership and inherited slave/free status are separate legal questions, and this case shows that legal contests could secure freedom.

**Source locator:** 2 Del. 497–499, paragraphs 2–6; reasoning at pp.498–499 and concluding decree of freedom.

## Evidentiary limits and counter-reading

Court's alternative analyses reflect conflicting accounts of Stephen's birth order; do not resolve it by guessing. The decision documents a freedom petition and its outcome, not general benevolence of courts or an overall liberation rate.
