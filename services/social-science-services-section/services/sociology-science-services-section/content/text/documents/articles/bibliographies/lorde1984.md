# Lorde 1984 — *Sister Outsider*

Audre Lorde. *Sister Outsider: Essays and Speeches*. Trumansburg, NY: Crossing Press, 1984. ISBN 978-0-89594-141-6.

## Status

Read locally; foundational reference for the [black-feminism](../../../triage/theories/critical-theory/theories/feminism/theories/black-feminism/_index.md) subdomain. Many of the essays were first delivered as talks between 1976 and 1983 at women's-studies conferences, lesbian-feminist gatherings, and university lectures, and were collected here.

## Annotation

The collection contains the essays through which Lorde's theoretical contribution circulates: "Poetry Is Not a Luxury," "Uses of the Erotic: The Erotic as Power," "The Master's Tools Will Never Dismantle the Master's House," "Age, Race, Class, and Sex: Women Redefining Difference," "The Uses of Anger: Women Responding to Racism," "The Transformation of Silence into Language and Action," "An Open Letter to Mary Daly," and "Eye to Eye: Black Women, Hatred, and Anger."

What makes the volume foundational is the combination. Each essay does specific analytical work — on the political function of the erotic, on the inadequacy of inherited theoretical categories, on anger as analytical content rather than disruption, on silence as the condition of survival within structures of domination. Together they constitute a sustained argument that mainstream U.S. feminist theory of the 1970s was structurally incapable of describing the conditions of Black, lesbian, and other women whom the white-middle-class-heterosexual paradigm subject did not include — and that the analytical resources for an adequate theory had to come from those conditions themselves.

The essay "The Master's Tools Will Never Dismantle the Master's House" is one of the most cited texts in late-twentieth-century feminist theory and one of the most flattened. Lorde's full point requires the second half: difference is not a deficit to be transcended but the analytical resource from which adequate theory has to be built. The essay was delivered at NYU's Second Sex Conference in September 1979 and is in part a direct response to that conference's marginalization of Black, lesbian, and Third World women's contributions.

"Uses of the Erotic" is the foundational text for feminist analysis of affect and labor. Lorde distinguishes the erotic (the deep capacity for joy, satisfaction, and fully felt living) from the pornographic (its commodified perversion) and argues that capitalist work-discipline systematically suppresses the erotic because contact with it would make the diminished forms of life capitalist society offers unbearable. The argument is upstream of much of what becomes affect theory in the late 1990s and 2000s.

"The Uses of Anger" is the direct intervention against the white-feminist managerial response to Black women's anger as disruptive of movement coalition. Lorde reframes the anger as analytical content — "an information and an energy" — that the movement cannot do without.

The "Open Letter to Mary Daly" (1979) is the documentary record of the kind of intra-feminist confrontation the volume's other essays generalize.
