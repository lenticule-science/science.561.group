# Idol culture and the economics of WeiqIdol

**Question.** What does empirical and critical scholarship on Japanese oshi-katsu and Korean platform fandom actually support for a repeat-play, profitable Go entertainment world?

This is the sociology evidence owner, not a replacement for the [existing original WeiqIdol vision](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/board-games-services-section/services/weiqi-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026-08-29-note-on-weiqidol-product-vision.md). A commercial product hypothesis is not an observed player result or a formal theorem.

## Evidence register — primary surfaces, precise limits

- **EC-01. Oshi-katsu and attachment:** Yizhen Zhou and Hideaki Kawabata (2026), *Human Arenas* 9(3), pp. 2533–2553, DOI [10.1007/s42087-025-00546-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42087-025-00546-2), [Keio University research record](https://keio.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/how-attachment-relationships-are-established-in-oshi-katsu-psycho/). Oshi-katsu means intentionally supporting a favored idol or figure. Authors propose a symbolic attachment framework; it is not a causal test of an AI-game subscription.
- **EC-02. Platform fandom:** Grant Bollmer and Bethany Tillerson (2025), *Social Media + Society* 11(2), DOI [10.1177/20563051251326689](https://doi.org/10.1177/20563051251326689), [publisher article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/20563051251326689). Weverse/Weverse Shop bind audience community and commerce while concentrating control over fan activity. Case analysis; not proof that centralized enclosure is best for WeiqIdol.
- **EC-03. Relational labor:** Sanghwa Lee and Robert Prey (2025), *Popular Music and Society* 48(4), pp. 416–436, DOI [10.1080/03007766.2025.2492505](https://doi.org/10.1080/03007766.2025.2492505), [University of Groningen](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/the-labor-process-of-relational-labor-the-case-of-the-k-pop-fan-p/). Bubble sells mediated relationships that require economically governed artist work; a scripted message must not be represented as human intimacy.
- **EC-04. Platform observation:** [Weverse Magazine, 2025 Weverse Fandom Trends](https://magazine.weverse.io/article/view/1725?lang=e), published 2026-02-06, reports analysis of **30 million users across two years**. That is a provider's selected user base, not 30 million paying customers or the addressable market for a Go game.

**Coverage:** selected first-party scholarship and a producer report. No original-language Japanese fan ethnography, Chinese celebrity economy, Korean grassroots club dataset, representative player cohort or customer purchasing test was completed. Europe PMC/ClinicalTrials.gov alone cannot adjudicate a cultural studies question.

## Analytical distinctions

An **oshi** (favored figure), **idol** (public performing role), **performer** (responsible operating actor), **character** (authored fiction), **fan** (voluntary supporter), **sect member** (authorized affiliation), **follower** (communication subscription), and **customer** (party to an exchange) are different relations. A Weiqi capture establishes none of them automatically. A **match** is an engine-accepted legal history; a **performance** is a staged appearance; an **episode** is an edited media artifact; an **event ticket** is a purchased right; an **earning** is a funded, provider-settled liability. Projected spectacle may cite game results but cannot author them.

These are provisional terms to reconcile with the existing Science/Obsmetrologia actor, work, message, obligation and ledger owners, **not** independently proven functors or a competing game ontology.

## Mechanisms to test, not imported facts about our audience

**SC-01 — Voluntary favorites and named career history.** EC-01 studies the practice of actively favoring a figure. Our hypothesis is that players will return to *the same specific rival* because the rival remembers real accepted matches, not because we attach a character portrait to anonymous bots. The fan chooses their oshi; no telemetry may silently enroll them. Compare seven- and thirty-day rematches against skill-matched anonymous opponents, and ask whether players can independently identify two rivals' previously observed strategies. An AI rival must really lose, change policy when authorized, and have a replayable history; random dialogue is not character continuity.

**SC-02 — A fandom is a producer–audience–community relation.** EC-02 supports joint community, media and commerce as a real platform arrangement, but also observes corporate domestication of fans. A WeiqIdol sect's proposed value is one group with actual membership, matches, events, fan activity, optional purchases and exits; it should use existing ActivityPub++/Semioverse actor and message identities rather than an isolated in-game friends ledger. Falsifier: a second client or actor cannot recover an invitation, accept a match, view its authoritative result and take a new action.

**SC-03 — Performance and access are saleable work.** EC-03 shows that mediated access generates relational labor obligations. We hypothesize a named AI rival can create entertainment through actual appearances, live matches, voice, learned quirks and occasional customized dialogue, while a human performer/creator may supply writing, illustration, music, live presentation or coaching. Distinguish operator identity from fictional character and disclose AI generation. Test actual delivered performances and producer costs. Reject pretending a prerecorded friendly message is reciprocal personal intimacy or replacing contracted human labor with a fictitious “AI performer royalty”.

**SC-04 — Productized releases instead of hollow point economies.** EC-02/04 show entertainment platforms with varied content and commerce, **not** willingness to buy WeiqIdol items. Candidate goods: self-contained rival campaign chapters, season passes with defined appearances, spectator events, licensed commentary/replay packs, original media, teacher-approved lessons and club/institution licenses. Seller, rights, exact price, buyer receipt, fulfilled right, refund and net contribution must all resolve under existing Commerce/Finance providers. Falsifier: content stays inaccessible after a finalized payment, or revenue cannot be reconciled against inference and creator cost.

**SC-05 — Agency and participation are not spending.** We must not confuse fan contribution with buying a digital item, winning a contest or acquiring a legal entitlement. Fans may organize a live match, submit a tactics annotation, choose a public performance topic or bring a new community member; each has a different authorization and output. Test voluntary fan participation, repeat involvement, moderator/producer labor and real creator payment where contractually promised. A supporter spending zero can still be a fan. A user spending money does not become a sect member unless they separately choose the affiliation.

**SC-06 — Gameplay is the evidence, not its decorative substrate.** Weiqi differs from conventional fictional-idol content because Go moves have independently replayable legality, opponent resistance and outcomes. A believable career event requires an accepted game history and its responsible actor; a scene, character dialog or recap cannot change the winner. The extant upstream Weiqi engine already provides replay, tactical event observations and SGF. Test two named agents against humans and each other, preserving adjudication, signatures/attribution, observed weakness and a full rematch; refuse a fake “professional” rank, invented tournament or LLM-authored victory.

**SC-07 — Fans can create durable assets under actual rights.** A source-backed move highlight can support fan commentary, commissioned instruction, short original dramas and tournament coverage without multiplying Go histories. Contracts determine reuse, creator credit, royalties, paid goods and revocation. Test whether a correctly licensed move-derived media item is published, independently viewed, purchased where offered and legitimately paid to its producer; not whether the editor rendered a picture.

**SC-08 — Small funded earnings are a distinct economic contract.** Player game tickets, fictional energy, fan support, earned reward eligibility and BTC Lightning sats are separate units. Existing Energy Bank and group financial software carry metrology/ledger; the ZBD adapter is currently not proof of payout. Require a sponsor reserve, eligibility from replayed source game or approved creator work, idempotent obligation and provider-finalized transfer to a real beneficiary. Paid output requires third-party confirmation and treasury reconciliation. Never convert spend into simulated satoshis or describe mock balances as wages.

**SC-09 — Cultures are not interchangeable.** Japanese oshi-katsu (EC-01), Korean platform fandom (EC-02/03), Chinese wuxia jianghu narrative, documented Chinese/Japanese/Korean Go traditions and Lakota Keya each belong to distinct contexts. Fictional player sects are invented IP; historical teaching lineages require real sources and consent. Research original-language community practices, translation, appropriate public portrayal and licensing before ascribing a Go technique to any historical school or national style. Do not infer participant identity, ethnicity or devotion from tactical telemetry.

**SC-10 — Competing on a market is an empirical comparison.** BadukPop's official [product site](https://badukpop.com/) and app-market representations demonstrate that large puzzle catalogs, named AI strength and online rank exist. Our offline Weiqi app's 56 problems, legal rules, five bot tiers and one-move visitors are real capabilities but not evidence of competitive entertainment parity. Compare observed first-session satisfaction, legal match depth, return behavior and willingness-to-pay on contemporaneous opponent products. Screenshots, smoke-test passes and speculative “world class” rankings cannot receive market fitness.

## Required reception assays

The counterfactual for the first coherent WeiqIdol season is **the same underlying legal Go game played with anonymous opponents and without persistent performer history**. Recruit new consenting users into matched first sessions; record cohort composition, device and previous Go skill. Pre-register the interval and sample logic before interpreting results. Measure time-to-first-move, completed legal contests, independent identification of a recurring rival after seven days, voluntary rematches, actual accepted visitor-to-replayed-mission conversion, public/spectator-to-match conversion, completed paid goods, creator payouts, disputes and total contribution margin. Include session lengths and player feedback but never substitute them for delivered value.

Define net contribution per cohort as actual merchant-finalized receipts less reversals/refunds, payment fees, allocated licensed content/artist payments, inference/compute/network and verified reward liabilities. Distinguish a creator's earned contractual share from a player's incentive and from fictional tickets. A gross transaction count cannot qualify profit.

## Receiver-bound scientific requirements

- **WI-SCI-01:** No tactic-derived performance may assert a social relation without explicit voluntary authorization and an addressable actor record. Independent receiver: player across sessions; negative example: replaying somebody's capture enrolls them in a sect.
- **WI-SCI-02:** Every narrated win, lesson or contest shall carry one source-versioned Game/Cartridge address and the actual opponent's reply or resignation. Independent receiver: game replay and spectator; negative example: scene contradicts rules.
- **WI-SCI-03:** Every idol encounter that offers a game shall be actionable as legal Go with real counterplay and a durable further encounter. Independent receiver: second device and opponent; negative example: clicking just increments a fan counter.
- **WI-SCI-04:** A support, purchase, membership, creator contract and earning shall have separate consent, identity, right and settlement, even when attached to the same performer. Independent receiver: claimant, merchant and beneficiary; negative example: a local “earned” balance credited without a funded provider.
- **WI-SCI-05:** For each proposed sellable idol product, Causeway shall observe real customer willingness to pay, fulfillment, resource costs and net contribution rather than treating supply of design documents as fulfillment. Independent receiver: buyer, producer and finance office.
- **WI-SCI-06:** Cultural/history claims presented as such shall cite attributed original-language/translated sources and usage rights, separate from explicitly invented sect lore. Independent receiver: knowledgeable reader/teacher, not the game's own classifier.

**Next empirical unknown:** whether fandom mechanics add durable demand among *Go players and Go-curious players* relative to high-quality anonymous Go and puzzle competition, without consuming more performer/inference/marketing cost than the extra collected revenue. Current scholarship cannot answer this for the 561 Group; a genuine game and receiving cohort must.
