# Clymer, Jeffory A. (2012) — Blood, Truth, and Consequences: Partus Sequitur Ventrem and the Problem of Legal Title

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199897704.003.0002
**Publication:** Family Money: Property, Race, and Literature in the Nineteenth Century, ch. 2, pp. 43–70
**Review level (2026-10-17):** Publisher chapter abstract inspected.

## Annotation

Studies contradictions among maternal enslaved/free status, notions of ancestry and racial identity, and property title. White paternal ancestry need not yield a socially white child or recognized heir where maternal legal status determines bondage. Do not collapse legal classification into biological ancestry.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Why this source is essential:** Maternal status and legal title.

**Recoverable evidence and locators:** This Oxford chapter examines the problem of legal title when kinship, descent, maternal bondage and racial appearance do not coincide. White male ancestry does not confer freedom under a statute assigning slavery through the mother; descendants of women legally free could contest illegal bondage. This bridges the 1662 primary law with nineteenth-century legal/property contradictions.

**Inferential boundary / competing evidence:** The available chapter abstract does not establish a prevalence of sexual coercion or a national ancestry distribution. Any quotation of a particular case must be checked in the chapter or original court reports.
