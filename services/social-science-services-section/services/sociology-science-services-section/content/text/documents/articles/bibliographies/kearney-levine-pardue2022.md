# Melissa S. Kearney; Phillip B. Levine; Luke Pardue (2022) — The Puzzle of Falling US Birth Rates since the Great Recession

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.36.1.151
**Published in:** Journal of Economic Perspectives 36(1):151–176.
**Reading grade (2026-10-17):** Original American Economic Association article abstract and published results summary read; full tables and public replication materials have not been rerun

## Exact contribution to the commissioned thesis

The decline in U.S. birthrates 2007–2020 occurred across several groups including college-educated white women. Authors attribute part of the early fall to the Great Recession, but cannot identify another period-specific economic, policy or social variable explaining much of the continued decline; they hypothesize cohort-level changes in aspirations and parenting norms. This is powerful counterevidence to a single facile material-depletion or eugenics explanation of all white fertility decline.

## Alternative explanations and evidence limits

The nonidentification result does not prove economics has no role, nor that personal priorities are exogenous to class/policy. It requires reading the full empirical specification and trying the replication package before drawing stronger causal conclusions. It does not identify genetic white decline or arbitrary purity selection.
