# Wolfe, Patrick (2006) — Settler colonialism and the elimination of the native

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/14623520601056240
**Publication:** Journal of Genocide Research 8(4): 387–409
**Review level (2026-10-17):** Publisher article abstract, citation and conceptual framing inspected.

## Annotation

Distinguishes the structural settler-colonial tendency to eliminate Indigenous territorial/political relations from genocide as a distinct event or outcome. Necessary to avoid treating settler expansion, captive labor increase and Indigenous kinship continuity as interchangeable population series.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Research role:** Elimination and the structural reproduction of colonial sovereignty.

**Evidence available at its source:** Wolfe's well-known account treats settler colonialism as an ongoing structural logic, not one episode of arrival. Dispossession and elimination of Indigenous territorial relations organize the colonizer's attempt to permanently take place on the land. Black hereditary slavery and Indigenous territorial elimination can be interlinked without operating by the same incentive.

**Interpretive/measurement limits:** Do not collapse 'elimination' into the statement that Indigenous populations actually vanished; they persist and exercise sovereignty. Also do not imply Wolfe proves white reproduction depends on slave breeding or international migration in a fixed fraction.
