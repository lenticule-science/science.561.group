# Michael P. Olson (2025) — “Restoration” and Representation: Legislative Consequences of Black Disfranchisement in the American South, 1879–1916

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12868
**Source class:** peer-reviewed political science empirical study.
**Read boundary (2026-10-17):** Original publisher abstract, documented 19,400+ legislative roll calls, DOI and published data declaration inspected; regressions not independently replicated.

## Source-located evidence

Abstract and publisher verification dataset https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/5OUHOW.

## What the source supports

Disfranchisement of Black residents changed actual legislative representation and policy, providing a later institutional counterpart to the early constitutional power derived from counting legally captive people. It is a better evidentiary bridge than inferring political control from population share alone.

## What it does not establish

Studies state legislative voting, not the precise additional congressional seats due to slavery in 1790–1860. It cannot be used to equate the post-Reconstruction system with pre-Civil War apportionment mechanisms.
