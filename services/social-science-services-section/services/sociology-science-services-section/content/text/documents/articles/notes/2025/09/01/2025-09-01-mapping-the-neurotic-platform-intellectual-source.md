# Rough Formation

I'm [imagining](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/imagining.md) some [idea](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/idea.md) that in rough formation involves these several [claims](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/services/essay-writing-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/claim.md):

- During times of crisis, some people begin to explore ideas outside of what they know
- People who perform as public experts in one topic, i.e. feminist or water cycle theorists, tent to meet the conditions that describe that "some people"
- These public experts are economically incentivized to perform
  - There are contradicting incentives:
    - They must maintain their public image as experts
    - They must produce novel content
  - This leads to them educating people on ideas they've only just learned, from a position of expertise
    - this misleads readers as to the position of the author relative to these new ideas:
      - that is, they may assume the author to be an expert on what they're talking about, because that is the assumption so far

Once I've developed these ideas, I'd like to explore how experts and the public can navigate this contradiction.

And, I'd like to recognize that I am exactly this sort of public expert, though **what** exactly I am an expert of is some debate.

Subjection may be described as the process of systems inducing, cohering, and stabilizing an individual as a subject, while subjectivzation may be described as the process of how individuals cohere and stabilize themselves as subjects of those systems.

I've heard of the [back stage](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/back-stage.md) and [front stage](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/front-stage.md) [bifurcation](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/bifurcated-classification.md) that's in [Erving Goffman](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/erving-goffman.md)'s [dramaturgical theory](../../../../../../glossary/terms/dramaturgical-theory.md) before; I feel like a lot of people have a relatively intuitive understanding of that concept, though the theory definitely adds texture to the its shape.

Basically, there is a *front stage*, where performing occurs, and a *back stage*, where performance is planned and structured.

Some influencers complicate this split: their front stage is where they are performing a… scripted? performance for their [audience](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/influencer-audience.md), and their *back stage*, where influencers are performing personal life for their audience.

I'm trying to point to things like a beauty brand representative uploading a video where they're crying about their girlfriend in messy room, or the less caustic, homesteader who normally talks about cultivating tomatoes shows themselves walking their dog on the beach.

Both types of video from each person is a performance, but of a very different sort: one performs the front stage, the other the back. The influencer isn't aiming for [intimacy](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/intimacy.md), but an [illusion of intimacy](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/illusion-of-intimacy.md). I don't think I've said it yet, but the term [parasocial](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/parasocial.md) needs to get a mention.

I also see here a sort of cascading confession, because social media influencers are not just [broadcasting](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/broadcasting.md) content: their audience is encouraged to engage… that is, confess, themselves. So the influencer and [influencer audience member](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/influencer-audience-member.md) are both incited to confess to each other, by the social media system.

What this means for the pentinent/confessor relations is worth examining, especially from a [Catholic](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/catholic.md) perspective, given the origins of [confessing](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/confessing.md) (as I'm thinking about it) in the [Church](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/catholic-church.md).
