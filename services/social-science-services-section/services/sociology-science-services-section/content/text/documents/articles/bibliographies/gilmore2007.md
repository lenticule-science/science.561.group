# Gilmore 2007 — *Golden Gulag*

Ruth Wilson Gilmore. *Golden Gulag: Prisons, Surplus, Crisis, and Opposition in Globalizing California*. Berkeley: University of California Press, 2007. ISBN 978-0-520-24201-2.

## Status

Read locally; foundational reference for the structural-geographic analysis of mass incarceration that anchors the [abolition](../../../triage/theories/critical-theory/theories/abolition) subdomain.

## Annotation

Gilmore's central thesis: the explosion of California's prison population from the late 1970s onward is not explained by crime rates or by the actions of any single political agent. It is the spatial and political fix for a four-fold surplus crisis: surplus labor (deindustrialization left workers without work), surplus land (rural counties lost their economic base), surplus state capacity (post-Keynesian state needed new functions), surplus capital (capital needed new investment outlets). The prison absorbed all four. Mass incarceration is what California's racial capitalism did with the populations, places, and resources its restructuring made redundant.

The book combines historical-geographic analysis (sustained engagement with Gramsci, Marx, Hall, and the geographic-Marxist tradition through Harvey and Lefebvre) with empirical work (the location decisions of new prisons, the prison-construction boom of the 1980s and 1990s, the political economy of the rural-county prison) and with the political work of "Mothers ROC" (Mothers Reclaiming Our Children), the organizing tradition Gilmore writes from inside.

Gilmore's later definition of *racism* — "the state-sanctioned and/or extralegal production and exploitation of group-differentiated vulnerability to premature death" — is the synthesis the book argues toward and one of the most cited contemporary formulations.
