# Davis 2003 — *Are Prisons Obsolete?*

Angela Y. Davis. *Are Prisons Obsolete?* New York: Seven Stories Press, 2003. ISBN 978-1-58322-581-3.

## Status

Read locally; foundational reference for the [abolition](../../../triage/theories/critical-theory/theories/abolition) subdomain and a primary text for the contemporary prison-abolition movement.

## Annotation

Short, accessible, and structurally argumentative. Davis makes the case that the prison system as it exists in the contemporary United States cannot be reformed into justice and must be abolished — and argues the case by reconstructing the historical conditions under which the prison became taken-for-granted. The book is in this sense methodological as well as political: showing that the prison is a historical formation is part of arguing that other arrangements are possible.

The argument moves through the prison's continuities with slavery and Jim Crow (the Thirteenth Amendment's exception clause; convict leasing; chain gangs); the racial composition of contemporary incarceration as not accidental but structural; the gendered regime inside women's prisons; the prison-industrial complex (corporate beneficiaries of mass incarceration); and the affirmative content of abolition — not absence (no prisons) but presence (the social infrastructure that makes caging unnecessary: housing, healthcare, education, restorative practice).

The book reads as a primer because it was written as one — the audience is the broader public, not the academy — and this is part of its value. The deeper structural account is in Davis's *Abolition Democracy* (2005) and in Ruth Wilson Gilmore's *Golden Gulag* ([Gilmore 2007](gilmore2007.md)), both of which the abolition subdomain also draws on.
