# Cybernetic postliberalism as predicated sociology

[Cybernetic postliberalism](../../../cybernetic-postliberalism/) is the most developed sociological theory in this library. It analyzes how late-liberal governance reproduces itself through cybernetic feedback between affect, platforms, economy, and the body — not through ideology or coercion but through the *formatting* of subjects. Predicated sociology does not supersede this theory; it gives it substrate-algebraic form.

The translation is not mechanical. Cybernetic postliberalism is written as critical sociology — analytical, case-driven, historically grounded. Predicated sociology gives it a predication settling, a predicate category, closure rules, and a regime classification. The gain is structural precision about *how* the mechanisms work and *what rate of contestation* would be required to interrupt them. The loss, if any, is rhetorical force — the formalism is rigorous but cold. The two registers are complementary.

## The central mechanism: californication as strict-regime closure

Cybernetic postliberalism names its central mechanism *californication*: the process by which structural contradictions in liberalism are formatted as personally manageable affect. The person who meditates through a housing crisis, journals through a pandemic, microdoses through ecological collapse — this person is not failing to see the structural condition; they are formatting it, as the system requires, into personal self-regulation.

Predicated sociology formalizes this as the [californication morphism](../morphisms/californication.md): a family of strict-regime closure rules that fire on structural-crisis cells and produce affective-predicate cells. The [theorem](../theorems/affect-formatting-is-closure.md) that californication operates in the strict regime while collective-action rules operate in the Scott-continuous or greater regime gives the cybernetic postliberal insight a structural foundation: affect formatting *always wins the race* against structural analysis because its antecedent chains are shorter. The social system does not need to suppress alternatives; it needs only to have installed rules that fire faster.

## The late-liberal social base

The [late-liberal social base](../objects/late-liberal-social-base.md) is the specific social-base instantiation of cybernetic postliberalism's social formation. Its predicate category $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{ll}}$ adds to the generic social base the platform predicates (is-platformed-by, has-engagement-metric-of, is-genre-sorted-as), the affect-governance predicates (routes-crisis-through-personal-affect, inhabits-subject-position-of-savior/slave), and the fascist-grammar predicates (the six-step rhetorical sequence as predicate cells that discourse events can fire).

The settling bound $\omega_1$ — the aperiodic regime — is the substrate-algebraic statement of cybernetic postliberalism's claim about *operational closure*: the late-liberal social formation has no countably finite resolution. This is not a value judgment but a structural description. The system's governance mechanisms (californication circuit, coherent confusion loop, zen fascism feeling rule) are limit-aggregating processes that collectively produce a social settling with no countable closure ordinal.

## Coherent confusion as an aperiodic self-sustaining loop

The [coherent confusion](../../../cybernetic-postliberalism/terms/coherent-confusion.md) mechanism — governance through interpretive load — is, in predicated sociology's terms, a self-sustaining closure rule:

$$v(\langle e, \texttt{produces-interpretation-of}, \mathrm{event}\rangle) = \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; v(\langle \mathrm{event}, \texttt{remains-interpretively-open}\rangle) \geq_k \mathbf{t}$$

Every interpretation event fires a rule that keeps the event open to further interpretation. This is not a Scott-continuous process (it does not converge at $\omega$); it is aperiodic: each interpretation generates further interpretive demand without bound. The subject is kept *in motion* — always interpreting, always settling some cell — but the event never closes. The interpretive labor is the governance mechanism: subjects engaged in sense-making are subjects engaged in sustaining the system, not contesting it.

The coherent confusion loop is the substrate-algebraic reason why the cybernetic postliberal subject is *functional confusion*: not lost or paralyzed, but productively confused — generating content, analysis, commentary, takes, threads — all of which adds to the interpretive load without resolving any structural cell. The $H^0$ (quiescence) diagnostic fails here: there is always something settling; the issue is that what settles is not structural cells but interpretive-process cells.

## Zen fascism as a feeling-rule closure

The [zen fascism](../../../cybernetic-postliberalism/terms/zen-fascism.md) mechanism — the delegitimation of public distress and the eroticization of non-reactivity — is realized as the feeling-rule closure in $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{ll}}$:

$$v(\langle e, \texttt{publicly-expresses-distress-at}, c\rangle) = \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; v(\langle e, \texttt{authorized-speaker-in}, \mathrm{field}\rangle) \leq_k \mathbf{f}$$

The rule fires on any public distress expression and reduces the distressed subject's authorized-speaker cell. This is not a symbolic violence operation (it does not require an institution with classification authority); it fires from any witnessing event of public distress. The feeling rule is embedded in the collective closure structure — it is diffuse, not centralized.

The strategic implication: the contested cell is not `is-distressed` (which is not directly delegitimated) but `publicly-expresses-distress` in conjunction with `authorized-speaker`. The contestation increment that disrupts zen fascism must target the `authorized-speaker` predicate — introducing $\top$ states by producing authorized expressions of distress that the collective closure cannot suppress without contradiction. This is a high-$D$ contestation, per [contestation-as-contradiction](../theorems/contestation-as-contradiction.md).

## The savior-slave subject as a joint settling outcome

The [savior-slave subject](../../../cybernetic-postliberalism/terms/savior-slave-subject.md) — simultaneously responsible for managing global crises (savior) and structurally incapable of transforming their conditions (slave) — is realized as the joint settlement of:

- $v(\langle e, \texttt{feels-responsible-for-crisis}, c\rangle) = \mathbf{t}$ (savior pole)
- $v(\langle e, \texttt{cannot-transform-structural-conditions-of}, c\rangle) = \mathbf{t}$ (slave pole)

These cells are not contradictory in the bilattice sense — they are settled on different predicates, both at $\mathbf{t}$. The contradiction that cybernetic postliberalism identifies (being responsible for what you cannot change) is a *truth-order contradiction at the level of the person's situation* — both cells are positively evidenced and together form a logically contradictory situation — not a bilattice $\top$ state within either cell. The bilattice $\top$ would arise on a single cell that has both positive and negative witnesses; the savior-slave situation has two different cells, each individually settled, that together form a contradiction at a higher level of analysis.

This distinction is important: the savior-slave formation is stable in the substrate precisely because no single cell is in a $\top$ state. There is no repair rule that fires, because nothing is contradicted within the substrate's resolution logic. The contradicted situation is only visible at the level of the person's self-understanding — which is why it requires critical theory (not just quiescence detection) to diagnose. This is the substrate-algebraic reason why cybernetic postliberalism's analysis is not redundant: it performs the $H^1$-level diagnostic that the settling's quiescence machinery cannot.

## What predicated sociology adds to cybernetic postliberalism

The formal treatment adds three things:

**Rate analysis.** The [theorem](../theorems/affect-formatting-is-closure.md) that californication is strict-regime and collective-action is Scott-continuous-or-greater gives a structural account of why the mechanism dominates: it is not stronger, it is faster. Any account of transformation must be an account of reducing the closure-ordinal gap.

**Regime classification of contested formations.** The [contestation theorem](../theorems/contestation-as-contradiction.md) gives a structural account of the conditions under which contestation can succeed. The late-liberal social base is aperiodic: transformation is possible in principle but requires sustained $\top$-introduction rates exceeding repair capacity. The $H^1$-class of the social settling is the correct diagnostic for progress in this regime.

**Dependency structure.** The high-$D$ / low-$D$ distinction identifies which structural cells are load-bearing. Contesting low-$D$ cells (symbolic positions, individual representation) produces visible contestation that is rapidly absorbed. Contesting high-$D$ cells (the classification-authority mechanism, the collective-infrastructure predicates, the antecedent chains of the californication rules) requires more, but produces propagating contradictions that can reach transformation thresholds.

Cybernetic postliberalism's case studies — the tariff cycle, Stop Cop City, the ecclesial recurrence — are, in predicated sociology's terms, analyses of specific high-$D$ and low-$D$ contestation sequences. The formal substrate does not replace the case analysis; it gives it a structural vocabulary for characterizing what kind of contestation is occurring and why some contestations succeed while others are absorbed.
