# Aisha Djeliid (2024) — “The master wished to reproduce”: slavery, forced intimacy, and enslavers’ interference in sexual relationships in the antebellum South, 1808–1861

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/14664658.2024.2317499
**Source class:** peer-reviewed article.
**Read boundary (2026-10-17):** Publisher-hosted original article first-page abstract and bibliographic details; full article not independently close-read in this round.

## Source-located evidence

Abstract, pp. 21–43; start with discussion of ‘stock men’, the ages of girls and women, forced pairing, and the author’s treatment of agency.

## What the source supports

Directly addresses a central element that prior conversation repeatedly sidelined: enslavers sometimes coerced particular people into reproducing, sometimes designated enslaved men for reproductive exploitation, and treated descendants as available labor/property. The abstract explicitly distinguishes a spectrum of coercion and uses cases rather than a universal count.

## What it does not establish

The abstract alone cannot quantify prevalence, prove purpose-built ‘breeder plantations’ were ordinary, determine the proportion of births under coerced pairing, or establish consent. The article is evidence against denying documented intentional forced reproduction, not grounds for flattening every enslaved birth into it.
