# Graeber & Wengrow 2021 — The Dawn of Everything

**Graeber, David, and David Wengrow. (2021). *The Dawn of Everything: A New History of Humanity*. New York: Farrar, Straus and Giroux.**

## Annotation

A historical-anthropological re-reading of the deep human past, arguing that the standard story — bands → tribes → chiefdoms → states, with agriculture as the developmental hinge — is empirically wrong. The archaeological and ethnohistorical record shows that human polities have been wildly diverse, that large-scale social organization has been achieved without states many times over, and that peoples have historically *moved between* polity-forms — seasonally, generationally, deliberately — rather than progressing along a fixed ladder.

A second major thread is the *Indigenous critique* of European political thought: the book argues that key Enlightenment-era European debates about freedom, equality, and political organization were shaped by Indigenous voices, particularly the Wendat philosopher-statesman Kandiaronk, whose critique of European hierarchical society circulated in eighteenth-century France in the form of dialogues that profoundly influenced Rousseau, Diderot, and the broader Enlightenment political imagination.

## For the politics domain

The book is the most prominent contemporary statement of the empirical refutation of unilinear political evolution — backing up Clastres' ([clastres1974](../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/reference-articles/bibliographies/clastres1974.md)) earlier theoretical case with a broader archaeological and ethnohistorical record. For [polity-form](../../../triage/teaching/explanations/polity-form.md), the implication is that the genus is genuinely diverse and switchable; for the lakotaean-politics subtheory, the implication is that the Lakota polity is not on a developmental path toward state-formation but is its own polity-form whose principles are stable across deep time.

The Indigenous-critique thread also matters: the European political theories the politics domain inherits (Weber, Rousseau, etc.) are not a neutral starting point. They are themselves partly shaped by Indigenous political thought that European authors absorbed without attribution. Re-grounding Indigenous polities as political-theoretic agents — not merely as objects of European political theory — is part of the work of this domain.

## Caveats

The book is broad and synthetic; specialists in particular regions have raised disputes about specific archaeological claims. The structural argument — diverse polity-forms, no developmental ladder, Indigenous political-theoretic agency — is more robust than any individual case.
