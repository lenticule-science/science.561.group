# J. David Hacker; Michael R. Haines; Matthew Jaremski (2021) — Early Fertility Decline in the United States: Tests of Alternative Hypotheses using New IPUMS Complete-Count Census Microdata and Enhanced County-Level Data

**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9409409/
**Source class:** scholarly demographic and econometric study.
**Read boundary (2026-10-17):** Published bibliographic/abstract materials consulted; full research article available, regression tables not completely audited.

## Source-located evidence

Abstract, 1830–1880 currently married white-couple models; county, household and linked-couple designs.

## What the source supports

Direct empirical research that the client’s original fertility premise requires. Uses new complete-count censuses 1830–1880 and linked family observations to test multiple explanations of early white fertility decline; separation of regional land, household labor and nuptiality mechanisms makes a generic immigration-only explanation inadequate.

## What it does not establish

A regression association between slaveholdings and marital fertility cannot simply be interpreted as aggregate racial self-destruction; historical white population reproduction must be separated from household net fertility and contingent survival. Re-read exact tables and model before quoting coefficients.

## Receiving and reuse

Paper I and Paper II contrasting empirical designs; scientific question is whether decline was caused by purity policy, labor/property arrangements, or ordinary demographic transitions.
