# After Haudenosaunee Grand Council consensus, who has implementation authority?

The Haudenosaunee Grand Council reaches decisions through consensus (unanimous agreement among the nations). After consensus is achieved on a decision, the predicated-governance theory needs to know: which role — clan mothers, chiefs, the council as a collective body, a designated office, or some other structure — holds the standing to *implement* that decision? Is implementation authority held by the same body that deliberated, or is there a distinct implementation role separate from deliberative authority?

## Why this matters for the substrate

The [consensus-settlement-as-authority-gate-update](../../../../../../services/work-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/consensus-settlement-as-authority-gate-update.md) theorem shows that consensus-closure settlement does not automatically confer implementation authority — a separate charter rule must fire to update the authority-gate for whoever acts on the decision. The theorem holds generally; but the specific charter rule depends on how the Haudenosaunee tradition assigns implementation standing.

## Specific sub-questions

1. Does the Grand Council's consensus on a decision simultaneously authorize all chiefs to act, or does it authorize a specific office or sub-body?

2. What is the role of clan mothers in the implementation phase, compared to their role in the deliberative phase? The consensus-closure object notes that clan mothers can remove chiefs who fail to uphold responsibilities — but does this extend to directing or authorizing implementation of specific decisions?

3. Are there categories of decisions where implementation authority is distributed (each nation implements within its territory) versus centralized (a designated body implements on behalf of all)?

4. Is the concept of a distinct deliberative/implementation split recognized in Haudenosaunee governance, or is the council's decision inherently also an authorization to act?

## Notes for the answering researcher

This question is raised to inform substrate formalization, not to adjudicate Haudenosaunee governance from the outside. The goal is to translate the tradition's own account of post-consensus authority into the substrate's authority-gate framework accurately. Sources should prioritize Haudenosaunee scholars and nation-authorized descriptions.
