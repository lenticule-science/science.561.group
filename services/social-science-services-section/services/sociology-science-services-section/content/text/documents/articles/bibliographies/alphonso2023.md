# Alphonso, Gwendoline (2023) — Expropriating Reproduction, Generating Property—Enslaved Childbearing, Property Law & Racial Capitalism in the American South

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.33774/apsa-2023-2nnzm
**Publication:** APSA Preprints, version 1 (September 2023)
**Review level (2026-10-17):** Original preprint publisher record and abstract inspected; not peer-reviewed at posting.

## Annotation

Analyzes antebellum law treating enslaved Black women's future increase as property distinct from the value of the women themselves. Supplies a mechanism for the capitalization of unborn reproductive futures. A legal system of hereditary expropriation must not be falsely collapsed into an assertion that all pregnancies occurred under specialized breeding-farm management.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Why this source is essential:** Prospective issue in property jurisprudence.

**Recoverable evidence and locators:** The 2023 APSA preprint argues antebellum courts increasingly treated enslaved women's **future increase** as a valuable claim differentiated from the woman's already owned labor. That is a direct instance of property claims aimed at unborn potential descendants, which can reproduce slaveholder wealth without reproducing the captive woman's juridical autonomy.

**Inferential boundary / competing evidence:** It is a preprint (not automatically peer-reviewed). The legal cases and their exact holdings, dissents and jurisdictions have not yet been independently verified in this register. Ownership of *possible issue* does not demonstrate the deliberate coercion of all conceptions.

The commissioning papers cite the [23-claim source crosswalk](bibliography-and-evidence-crosswalk-2026-10-17.md); this audit records a source's contribution without upgrading a limited read to full-text verification.

## Full-preprint and independently read court opinions (17 October 2026)

The original **41-page APSA preprint**, https://preprints.apsanet.org/engage/api-gateway/apsa/assets/orp/resource/item/64f27d5579853bbd78e65a03/original/expropriating-reproduction-generating-property-enslaved-childbearing-property-law-racial-capitalism-in-the-american-south.pdf , has now been read beyond the abstract. On printed pp. 31–37 it discusses future issue as a property interest, life-tenancy allocation and material costs of a woman's pregnancy and childcare. Alphonso reports **212 NexisUNI keyword-hit cases** concerning prospective issue and nearly **80% dated 1830–1860**: these are the author's unreplicated search-frame findings, **not** a denominator of all slave-law cases nor a forced-pregnancy prevalence estimate.

Independent original opinion readings now support bounded propositions: [Covington v McEntire (1842)](case-covington-mcentire1842.md), [Nelson v Nelson (1849)](case-nelson-nelson1849.md), [Smith v Milman (1839)](case-smith-milman1839.md), and [Johnson's Administrator v Johnson's Heirs (1848)](case-johnson-johnson1848.md). [Banks v Marksberry (1823)](case-banks-marksberry1823.md) remains original-unretrieved, reconstructed only from secondary discussion and later case citation. *Smith* yields **a decree freeing petitioner Stephen**, demonstrating why condition and ownership cannot be conflated. *Johnson* grants mother Lytha freedom while her seven previously born children remain enslaved.
