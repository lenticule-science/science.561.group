# Jennifer L. Morgan (2021) — Reckoning with Slavery: Gender, Kinship, and Capitalism in the Early Black Atlantic

**Publication:** Duke University Press. 
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1215/9781478021452.
**Read status (2026-10-17):** Original scholarly monograph, publisher record and accessible descriptions reviewed; not all chapters available in full to this inquiry.

## Contribution to the commissioned papers

Reproductive labor, legal kinship, gender and racialized property are constitutive in the early Atlantic system, rather than enslaved birth being a mere natural-stock change. Its methodological attention to the politics of numerical reduction, including the chapter ‘Refusing Demography’, provides a critical counterposition to any argument that population percentages represent captive women's full lives.

## Provenance and interpretive limit

Before hypothetical submission, conduct close reading of the full book, especially its argument about method and numbers; do not claim it establishes that every enslaved pregnancy was engineered.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Why this source is essential:** Reproduction as social and analytical violence.

**Recoverable evidence and locators:** Morgan's historical account places gender, kinship and maternal reproductive work at the creation of New World racial capitalism, rather than treating children and women as an abstract flow of labor units. The argument also calls for care about the violence and erasures performed by the slave archive's numerical categories. The commissioned study's 17% calculation must be read in tension with, not as a replacement for, that analysis.

**Inferential boundary / competing evidence:** Publisher materials and linked descriptions do not equal full independent close reading of the monograph. Before publication quote/check the specific passages on demography, family structures, kin and finance, retaining enslaved people's agency beyond ownership records.
