# Supreme Court of North Carolina; opinion by Gaston J. (1842) — Covington v. McEntire

**Canonical citation:** 37 N.C. 316 (2 Ired. Eq. 316), December 1842.
**Original or catalogued text:** https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59145de2add7b049341ffa35
**Reading grade (2026-10-17):** Full public-domain judicial opinion read in court-report transcription; original printed reporter page image not compared.

## Case facts / exact analytical contribution

William McEntire's 1832 will granted the enslaved woman Binah to his widow for life or widowhood, then to Rachel Covington. At the widow's death in 1842, FOUR of Binah's children born during the life tenancy were living. Executor Prior McEntire held them, arguing they fell in the residuary estate. The court held the children followed the remainder interest belonging to Rachel; explicit different drafting for another woman, Nan, did not override the presumption. The record also names one child, Catharine, proposed in a rejected distribution bargain involving $55.

**Source locator:** Opinion 37 N.C. 316–321, esp. factual statement at 317–318 and Gaston's analysis at 318–320.

## Evidentiary limits and counter-reading

Four living children = reported surviving persons in this particular lawsuit, not all of Binah's lifetime births or coerced conceptions. Court describes title contest between enslavers, not testimony from Binah, and does not establish property-market appraisal of her fertility.
