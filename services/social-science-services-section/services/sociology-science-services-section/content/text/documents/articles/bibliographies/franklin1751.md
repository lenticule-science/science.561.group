# Benjamin Franklin (1751) — Observations Concerning the Increase of Mankind, Peopling of Countries, etc.

**Source:** https://cooperative-individualism.org/franklin-benjamin_observations-concerning-the-increase-of-mankind-1751.htm
**Publication:** Original 1751 pamphlet
**Read status:** Full historical English transcription examined, especially §§1–3, 23–24

## Annotation

Distinguishes cities unable to supply inhabitants by natural generation from settler environments; advocates importing English settlers and increasing a narrowly defined white population while excluding multiple European nationalities from that category.

## Limits

These are historically situated racial classifications, not genetic realities; city natural increase does not describe all settlers.

Used in the [white-increase research note](../notes/2026/10/17/2026-10-17-white-increase-and-reproductive-selection-contradiction.md).

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Research role:** Political arithmetic and selective population target.

**Evidence available at its source:** Sections 1–3 of the 1751 original essay describe settlement, resources, household formation and the possibility of cities losing residents by mortality relative to births while being replenished externally. Sections 23–24 articulate a wish to increase a narrowly described preferred white population while disfavoring particular European, African and Indigenous populations. This is primary contemporary rhetoric about **which increase** mattered to an early colonial author.

**Interpretive/measurement limits:** Do not represent Franklin's words about particular towns as statistical proof that U.S. settlers at large required immigration. His racial description is a historically contested political ideology, not a biological population definition. His wish to expand a selected group does not make later absolute demographic disappearance a demonstrated consequence.

Evidence can be followed through the [23-claim crosswalk](bibliography-and-evidence-crosswalk-2026-10-17.md); the annotation is neither an assertion that every page of the source was read nor an independently replicated dataset.
