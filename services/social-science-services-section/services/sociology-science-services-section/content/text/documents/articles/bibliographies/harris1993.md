# Cheryl I. Harris (1993) — Whiteness as Property

**Source:** https://harvardlawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/1993/06/1707-1791_Online.pdf
**Source class:** peer-reviewed legal scholarship.
**Read boundary (2026-10-17):** Publisher-hosted original 85-page PDF; examined abstract and table of contents, not a line-by-line reading of all pages.

## Source-located evidence

pp. 1715–1744 (emergence from enslavement and Indigenous dispossession; traditional property functions and status property); pp. 1757 onward (persistence).

## What the source supports

A foundational direct prior formulation: whiteness itself can function as legally recognized property/status with exclusion, reputation and expectation benefits, emerging from Black enslavement and Native land seizure. This anchors the distinction between reproduction of persons and reproduction of a structure distributing property/status.

## What it does not establish

This is not an econometric estimate of fertility, a claim that all people labeled white receive identical benefits, or a prediction that whites cease to exist. Manuscript must cite Harris rather than presenting whiteness-as-property as a new 561 discovery.

## Receiving and reuse

Paper I theoretical framework; explicitly compare status-preserving exceptions in Virginia to functions of whiteness as property.
