# Supreme Court of North Carolina; opinion by Ruffin C.J. (1849) — Nelson v. Nelson

**Canonical citation:** 41 N.C. 409 (6 Ired. Eq. 409), December term 1849.
**Original or catalogued text:** https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914cf83add7b04934822097
**Reading grade (2026-10-17):** Full nineteenth-century judicial opinion read in court-report transcription; underlying 1843 will incorporated in opinion, not independent probate manuscript.

## Case facts / exact analytical contribution

James Nelson's September 1843 will gave the enslaved woman Leah and her child Anderson to Elizabeth, while a separate clause contemplated dividing future 'increase' among three daughters. Nelson died January 1844. Leah had THREE more children after his death; the court assigned these children exclusively to Elizabeth. The opinion explicitly mentions $329.75 in costs of maintaining Leah and family and discusses the labor and material expense of pregnancy and confinement against the profits of ownership, not as a consensual parental arrangement.

**Source locator:** 41 N.C. 409–418, especially 410–412 (wills and family), 416 (cost $329.75), 417–418 (issue and interpretive result).

## Evidentiary limits and counter-reading

The $329.75 is litigation-era maintenance expense for Leah and family, NOT a quantified pregnancy expense alone, slave sale proceeds, national market price, or estimated value of a child's life. Three post-testament children are litigation-reported, not an assessed annual fertility rate.
