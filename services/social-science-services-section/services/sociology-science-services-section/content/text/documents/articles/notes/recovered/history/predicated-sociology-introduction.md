# Introduction: Why predicated sociology

Sociology has always had a formalization problem. The discipline's best concepts — habitus, field, capital, governmentality, subject, structure — name real phenomena with real causal force. But they resist formal treatment because they are historically contingent, contested in meaning, and multiply determined. Every attempt to formalize them has either lost the sociological content (reducing structure to a network graph, habitus to a preference function) or remained in the register of metaphor (treating structure as if it were a mechanism without specifying the mechanism).

Predicated sociology takes a different approach. It does not impose a formalism on sociology from outside. It observes that sociological knowledge is already, at its core, knowledge about *predication relations*: who occupies what position, what capital types an entity holds, what norms govern what agents, what structural conditions produce what effects. These are bilattice-valued questions — each cell in the social fabric may be settled (positively or negatively evidenced), unsettled (unknown), or contested (contradicted by competing witnesses). The mathematics of predication categories is not foreign to sociology; it is the explicit articulation of what sociology already does implicitly.

## What the social base provides

The [social base](../objects/social-base.md) gives sociology a substrate: a predicate category $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{soc}}$ over a social entity set $E_{\mathrm{soc}}$, with evidence accumulating via a family of valuations indexed by an ordinal. The predicate category is sociologically chosen, not mathematically dictated: which predicates matter, which entities are relevant, which closure rules govern the settling — all of these are substantive sociological decisions. The mathematics tells us what follows from those choices. The sociology makes the choices.

This division of labor is the theory's central methodological commitment. Predicated sociology is not a derivation of sociology from mathematics; it is a substrate for doing sociology with greater structural precision. The predication settling formalizes the evidential dynamics that sociologists already reason about: how social facts accumulate, how positions stabilize, how crises destabilize, how structural arrangements reproduce themselves.

## What settling dynamics add

The four-regime classification — strict, Scott-continuous, transfinite-bounded, aperiodic — is perhaps the theory's most consequential formal addition to sociological thinking. It gives a structural account of *the speed of social reproduction and social transformation*.

In the strict regime (finite closure ordinal), social arrangements stabilize quickly. Individual social encounters, institutional classification events, and positional assignments settle in finite steps. This is the regime of what Goffman called the *interaction order*: the fine-grained, rapid, structurally determined micro-level encounters that reproduce social position without conscious deliberation.

In the Scott-continuous regime (closure ordinal $\omega$), social arrangements stabilize only in the limit of a potentially infinite evidential accumulation. Institutional careers, class trajectories, and field positions settle at $\omega$: they require a lifetime of witnessing events and never achieve a final, irrevocable settlement at any finite stage. This is the regime of Bourdieu's field analysis: the position is never fully determined by any finite slice of one's history.

In the aperiodic regime (closure ordinal $\geq \omega_1$), social arrangements do not stabilize at any countable stage. This is the regime of social formations whose contradictions are genuinely not resolvable within the existing structural arrangement — where the reproduction mechanism itself generates conditions that outrun any finite repair. The [late-liberal social base](../objects/late-liberal-social-base.md) lives here.

## The theory's three pillars

**Bourdieu's field theory**, formalized. Fields, positions, capital, habitus, symbolic violence — these are the inherited vocabulary, given substrate-algebraic shape. The [capital fiber bundle](../objects/capital-fiber-bundle.md) realizes capital as bilattice-valued predication fibers. The [habitus closure](../objects/habitus-closure.md) realizes habitus as acquired closure rules. The [symbolic violence](../morphisms/symbolic-violence.md) morphism realizes misrecognition as the authority-biased resolution of $\top$ states.

**Foucault's subject formation**, formalized. Subjectification, interpellation, the confessional incitement to speak oneself into subjection — these are realized as the settling of subject-predicate cells via the [interpellation](../morphisms/interpellation.md) morphism and its iterated platform variant.

**Cybernetic postliberalism**, formalized and integrated. The mechanisms of the late-liberal social formation — californication, affect governance, coherent confusion, zen fascism — are realized as specific closure rules in the [late-liberal social base](../objects/late-liberal-social-base.md). Cybernetic postliberalism is not a separate theory imported into predicated sociology; it is predicated sociology applied to the historically specific social formation of late liberalism. See [cybernetic-postliberalism-as-predicated-sociology](cybernetic-postliberalism-as-predicated-sociology.md) for the full treatment.

## What the theory does not do

Predicated sociology does not resolve the classical agency/structure debate by decree. It provides a substrate in which the debate can be made precise: structural determination is the strict-regime character of action-cell settling given positional antecedents; agency is the introduction of contestation increments that move settled cells toward $\top$. Whether any given social arrangement is more determined or more agentive is an empirical question about the regime of the relevant social settling — not a philosophical commitment.

Predicated sociology does not prescribe politics. The theory's diagnostic tools — regime classification, $H^1$-class analysis of structural transformation, rate comparison between californication and collective action — are analytical. They identify what is structurally possible, what rates of contestation would be required for transformation, which structural cells are high-$D$ targets for contestation. Acting on those diagnoses is not the theory's work; it is the work of the people and movements the theory analyses.
