# Patricia Ferguson Watkinson — George Major Cook (1860–1930)

**Scholarly biography:** https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cook-george-major-1860-1930/ . **Underlying print:** Watkinson, “Cook, George Major,” in *Dictionary of Virginia Biography*, volume 3, pp. 422–423 (Library of Virginia, 2006). **Reading level:** Full publisher biography and surrounding chronological record inspected, but original February 1930 hearing minutes and Cook's original letters not read.

## Contribution: a named Indigenous political actor

George Major Cook, also known as **Wahunsacook or Wahansunacoke**, served as elected **Pamunkey chief from 1902 until his death in 1930**. He opposed Virginia's effort to erase the distinct legal and political identity of Indigenous nations via coercive racial classification. The biography specifically records Cook's **February 1, 1930 legislative committee objection**: an exception restricted to people domiciled on two state reservations would fail to recognize Pamunkey and Mattaponi living elsewhere. It quotes his account of land, forests, fishing grounds and even the tribal *name* being taken by settlers. That is a political assertion of continuing Indigenous existence and belonging, not a request for a preferred racial blood quantum.

The biography reports Cook's earlier July 1925 public letter and opposition to Plecker's registration policies, as well as his intervention in a 1917 contest over reservation taxation and military drafting. These events locate Cook's political identity **outside** the racial-integrity code itself. The State's recognition or refusal did not manufacture Pamunkey citizenship or sovereignty.

## Evidence grade and alternative reading

Cook's quotation is reproduced by a credible historical biography, not independently checked against original February 1930 committee transcript. A journal manuscript should source the original hearing and contemporary Pamunkey political record. This secondary source does not justify claiming that all Indigenous people sought one racial classification: different communities and political positions must be recovered on their own terms.
