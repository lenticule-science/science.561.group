# Karin Wulf (2023) — Making Partus: Law, Power, and Heritable Slavery in 18th-Century British America

**Canonical citation:** Age of Revolutions, May 1, 2023.
**Original or catalogued text:** https://ageofrevolutions.com/2023/05/01/making-partus-law-power-and-heritable-slavery-in-18th-century-british-america/
**Reading grade (2026-10-17):** Full peer-authored historical interpretive article read online including endnotes; not original 17th-/18th-century court records.

## Case facts / exact analytical contribution

Corrects a common legal overgeneralization: maternal heritability was statute in Virginia 1662, New York 1706, South Carolina 1740, Georgia 1754 and differently specified in Maryland, but NOT enacted uniformly in every British American colony. Slaveholders and courts could assert custom and property claims well beyond explicit statute. The essay describes Virginia's 1849 clarification of freedom for children of a woman manumitted by will from the testator's death.

**Source locator:** Main essay paragraphs beginning 'But an important feature', 'In 1849', 'Slaveowners behaved', endnotes 3–10.

## Evidentiary limits and counter-reading

Modern historical synthesis, not a codebook of every colony's law. Read the quoted original statutes, freedom suit outcomes and 1849 Virginia statute before treating jurisdiction list as exhaustive. Strong negative control for uniform `partus` claims.
