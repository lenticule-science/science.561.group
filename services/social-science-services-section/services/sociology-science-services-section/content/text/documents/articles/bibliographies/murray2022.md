# Murray, Melissa (2022) — Abortion, Sterilization, and the Universe of Reproductive Rights

**Source:** https://scholarship.law.wm.edu/wmlr/vol63/iss5/4/
**Publication:** William & Mary Law Review 63(5): 1599 ff.
**Review level (2026-10-17):** University-hosted journal abstract and metadata inspected.

## Annotation

Treats state-supported coercive sterilization as an important technology of eugenic reproductive control, correcting attempts to equate it with voluntary contraception or abortion. Gives a legal test for whether a selective regime actually deprived people of reproductive agency.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Research role:** Sterilization as a distinct mode of reproductive compulsion.

**Evidence available at its source:** Murray's constitutional/legal-history work shows why forced sterilization cannot be assimilated to an autonomous right to prevent pregnancy. Eugenic governance frequently targeted those deprived of reproductive choice in the name of allegedly improving population quality. This is specifically relevant to defining institutional reproductive expendability.

**Interpretive/measurement limits:** The paper is not a retrospective national birth register. Historic eugenic rhetoric does not prove a general net decline among all white-classified people; a causal study needs the treated population, age-specific exposure and comparison.

Evidence can be followed through the [23-claim crosswalk](bibliography-and-evidence-crosswalk-2026-10-17.md); the annotation is neither an assertion that every page of the source was read nor an independently replicated dataset.
