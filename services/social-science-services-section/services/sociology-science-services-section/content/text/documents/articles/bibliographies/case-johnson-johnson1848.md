# Court of Appeals of Kentucky; opinion by Judge Simpson (1848) — Johnson's Administrator v. Johnson's Heirs

**Canonical citation:** 47 Ky. 470 (8 B. Mon. 470), decided June 15, 1848.
**Original or catalogued text:** https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/johnsons-admr-v-johnsons-heirs-kyctapp-1848

## Case facts / exact analytical contribution

Job Johnson granted his wife Margaret a life interest in the enslaved woman Lytha, with Lytha's contingent emancipation after Margaret's death (or on reaching 21 if widow died sooner). Lytha had SEVEN children before the widow's death. Court held Lytha became free upon the death of Margaret and attainment of majority; the children were born while she was enslaved and were themselves enslaved. The court ruled the children belonged to Margaret's estate rather than to Job Johnson's heirs or Lytha herself, because no remainder was retained. It distinguished deferred freedom from emancipation already made effective but enjoyment delayed.

**Source locator:** 47 Ky. 470–474, especially 470–471 (seven children/status), 471–473 (life versus remainder), 473–474 (decision reversing lower court).

## Evidentiary limits and counter-reading

The reported seven children cannot be treated as directed pregnancies or planned birth output. The court does NOT declare the seven free even though Lytha attained freedom. This is a separate jurisdictional ownership rule, not identical to North Carolina.
