# Wanamaker, Marianne H. (2014) — Fertility and the Price of Children: Evidence from Slavery and Slave Emancipation

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022050714000850
**Publication:** Journal of Economic History 74(4): 1045–1071
**Review level (2026-10-17):** Publisher article abstract and metadata inspected.

## Annotation

Models slave labor and white slaveholders' own children as potentially substitutable household productive inputs, examining slaveowner fertility between 1850 and 1870. Reported results concern responses to relative child prices; they do not demonstrate a universal decline in white population.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Why this source is essential:** Slaveholder household fertility substitution.

**Recoverable evidence and locators:** Cambridge's 2014 original abstract specifies panel data from slaveowning households 1850–1870, a household-production model incorporating **both the owner's children and enslaved labor**, and a negative correlation between the implied price of own children and fertility. This is the directly relevant microeconomic substitute mechanism: slaveholdings alter the marginal productivity of a household's descendants.

**Inferential boundary / competing evidence:** The paper reports an econometric relationship, not that enslaved babies mechanically replaced particular white babies, and not that white settlers collectively became unable to reproduce. A causal claim must inspect the full identification strategy, regression coefficients and emancipation shock, rather than simply repeating the abstract.
