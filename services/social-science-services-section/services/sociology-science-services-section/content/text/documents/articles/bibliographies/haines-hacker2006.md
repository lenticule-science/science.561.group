# Michael R. Haines; J. David Hacker (2006) — The Puzzle of the Antebellum Fertility Decline in the United States: New Evidence and Reconsideration

**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w12571
**Source class:** historical demographic working paper.
**Read boundary (2026-10-17):** Original NBER record, abstract and available PDF opening/summary inspected; not a complete independent replication.

## Source-located evidence

Abstract; county child–woman ratios 1800–1860 and 1850/1860 IPUMS linked covariates.

## What the source supports

Reports white child–woman ratios declining by 33% by the end of the antebellum period using one set of indicators, with nuptiality prominent among candidate factors. Establishes an actual long-running research controversy about the causes/timing of early white fertility decline, not mere rhetoric.

## What it does not establish

Child–woman ratios can be affected by mortality, age composition, marriage and enumeration. Hacker 2003 specifically revises conclusions based on alternative own-children fertility estimates. Present these estimates as in tension, not as direct measurements of births prevented by white ideology.

## Receiving and reuse

Paper I discussion of material reproductive conditions; Paper II adjacent fertility section, with Hacker 2003 as an explicit competing estimate.
