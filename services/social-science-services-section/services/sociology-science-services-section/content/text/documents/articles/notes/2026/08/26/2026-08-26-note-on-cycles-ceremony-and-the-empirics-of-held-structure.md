# Cycles, Ceremony, and the Empirics of Held Structure

## 0. What this note is

"Spirals, Settling, and Investment in Relation" (2026-08-23, this section's sibling critical-theory-services-section, author Claude Fable) derives four empirically-facing predictions from the corpus's coordination-cohomology and circulation-premium theorems, stated there as its §8 predictions and its §5.1/§6.4 investment-form and diversity claims. This note is the audit those predictions were promised: each is checked against the relevant 2015-2026 literature — network science, ritual studies and the sociology of religion, economic history of enclosure and allotment, and social-ecological resilience — with a plain verdict, the strongest supporting and disconfirming evidence named, and one proposed operationalization (§6) for the prediction currently most underspecified. The four predictions, in the audited note's own numbering and paraphrased for legibility here:

- **P1 (ceremony fraction).** A community's allocation to cycle-maintenance — ceremony, standing gatherings, recurring non-transactional coordination — predicts its trajectory better than its asset stock, with a lag-then-nonlinear collapse pattern when gatherings are pruned.
- **P2 (enclosure excess loss).** Enclosure or privatization of a commons destroys value beyond ordinary transaction costs — an "annihilated circulation term" not recoverable by summing the booked assets.
- **P3 (ritual as load-bearing technology).** Ritual recurrence is not decorative but a functioning social technology that does real coordinative and bonding work.
- **P4 (diversity as aliveness).** Diversity of holding arrangements — governance forms, tenure regimes, ceremonial expressions — across communities is itself a mechanism of collective resilience/aliveness, not administrative noise.

Grading below follows the audited note's own convention where useful: results are marked **[supported]**, **[mixed]**, **[not supported at claimed scale]**, or **[disconfirmed]**, with the evidentiary grade stated at the level the literature actually earns, not the level the theory hoped for.

## 1. The network-science substrate

The theory's core piece of mathematics — cycle rank equals |E| − |V| + |C|, and the first cohomology group vanishes exactly when the relational graph is a forest — is uncontroversial graph theory; nothing in the literature challenges it, because nothing in the literature needs to. What is contestable is the social reading: that redundant, cycle-forming ties are what enable the loads a spanning tree cannot carry. Here the literature is closer to direct vindication than any of the other three predictions get:

- **Complex contagion.** Centola & Macy's 2007 *American Journal of Sociology* result, elaborated in Centola's *How Behavior Spreads* (2018), shows that behaviors requiring social reinforcement — costly cooperation, contested norm change, collective action — propagate only across "wide bridges": redundant, multiply-overlapping ties. A single bridging tie moves information (simple contagion) but not trust-dependent coordination. This is close to a literal confirmation of "cycles hold what trees cannot": the tree moves the signal, the cycle moves the commitment.
- **Simmelian ties.** Closed triads (Krackhardt's formalization of Simmel's insight) enable third-party sanctioning and reputational enforcement that no dyad, however strong, can supply on its own — see the glossary entry for the mechanism in full.
- **Redundancy in real cohesion data.** 2024 multiplex-network studies of Honduran village networks find cohesion tracking redundant/multiplex tie overlap more than raw tie count; a 2026 network-resilience special issue names "diversified and redundant ties" explicitly as load-bearing infrastructure.
- **Higher-order networks and topological data analysis.** Battiston et al. (*Physics Reports* 2020; *Nature Physics* 2021; a 2025 *Nature Reviews Physics* synthesis) formalize simplicial and hypergraph structure beyond pairwise ties; a 2025 arXiv preprint proposes "thick"/"cohesive" Betti numbers as resilience metrics; a 2026 *Scientific Reports* paper connects persistent cycles to network resilience directly; Sizemore, Giusti & Bassett's connectome work (*Network Neuroscience*, 2018) treats persistent one-dimensional cycles in brain networks as functional "cavities" — structure that holds precisely because it is not resolved into a tree.

**Verdict on the substrate: [supported] as mechanism, open as application.** Nobody has yet run this apparatus — cycle rank, persistent Betti numbers, simplicial contagion — on kinship, gift-exchange, or intentional-community data with the specific cycle-equals-ceremony-equals-held-trust interpretation the theory proposes. This is a real, currently-empty, and executable research niche, not a gap in the supporting math. Simplicial contagion (Iacopini et al. 2019) and higher-order homophily results (*PNAS* 2024) are the closest existing approaches, and neither was built with this reading in mind.

## 2. P3 — ritual as load-bearing technology

**Verdict: [supported] as a small-to-medium-scale bonding mechanism with an identified causal pathway; [not supported at claimed scale] for large-scale societal cooperation.**

The strongest limb of P3 is mechanistic. Tarr, Launay & Dunbar's double-blind naltrexone studies show that opioid-receptor blockade measurably reduces the bonding effect of synchronized ritual activity, corroborated by pain-threshold field studies — this is the best causal evidence available that ritual synchrony does something real to social bonding, via the endorphin system, at the scale of small groups. Around this core:

- Bouchat, Pizarro & Páez's 2024 meta-analysis (13 studies, *Group Processes & Intergroup Relations*) finds robust associations between perceived emotional synchrony and self-transcendent emotion and wellbeing, with a weaker but present prosociality link. Rennung & Göritz's 2016 meta-analysis (60 studies) finds a medium synchrony-prosociality effect size.
- **Honest caveat, load-bearing for the verdict:** the post-2020 replication literature (Atwood et al.; the 2023 *Open Mind* exchange) shows most synchrony-prosociality lab studies were run by non-blind experimenters, and effects shrink substantially — in some cases to null — once expectancy is controlled. Observational effervescence research is separately confounded by self-selection into high-synchrony groups. The mechanism is real; its magnitude in the published record is probably inflated.
- **The scale-up claim took real damage.** Whitehouse's modes-of-religiosity and identity-fusion typology (see the glossary entries) hold up well as descriptive frameworks. But the flagship evidence that ritual/religious intensity scales up to civilizational cooperation — Whitehouse et al.'s 2019 *Nature* paper using the Seshat databank, arguing complex societies precede moralizing gods rather than the reverse — was **retracted** (*Nature*, 2021, "Treatment of missing data determined conclusions"): the central finding reversed once missing data were handled correctly. This is the single most important disconfirming result in the whole audit and should be named plainly whenever P3 is invoked at societal scale.
- Norenzayan and Henrich's "Big Gods" priming literature is meta-analytically solid at the level of individual lab experiments (~26 experiments) but has not been shown to aggregate into society-level cooperation gains — the lab-to-society bridge P3 needs is exactly the bridge that is missing.
- The strongest *economic* limb, and arguably the best-evidenced part of P3 overall, is feasting-as-infrastructure: Hayden (*The Power of Feasts*, 2014) and Dietler (*Feasts*, 2001) document potlatch-type feasting as literal debt-cycling, alliance-formation, and redistribution infrastructure — and the colonial banning of potlatch is a documented, historically concrete case of enclosure-of-circulation, tying P3 directly to P2.

## 3. P1 and P2 — natural experiments in cycle-pruning and enclosure

**P1 verdict: [mixed, underspecified].** No study in the literature isolates a "ceremony fraction" distinct from general social or civic spending — the specific measurable the theory proposes does not yet exist in the data record (§6 proposes one). What does exist supports the *shape* of the claim, the lag-then-nonlinear collapse:

- Putnam's *Bowling Alone* 2020 anniversary edition reaffirms the associational decline thesis; newer composite social-capital measures find a non-smooth trajectory, consistent with lag-then-collapse rather than steady decline. Critics (fairly) fault Putnam's account for underweighting racial, ethnic, and gendered variation in associational life — a caveat that matters for any attempt to generalize the ceremony-fraction measure across communities that were never symmetrically included in the original data.
- UK austerity gives two live natural experiments. UNISON's 2026 report documents 47% library staffing cuts and 276+ net library closures in England, 2010-2024. The Institute for Fiscal Studies' 2024-25 Sure Start analyses find the program produced causally identified gains (GCSE improvements, an estimated £2.4bn long-run benefit), and the subsequent 73% funding cut and 1,340+ centre closures coincide with worse child-development and obesity outcomes in longitudinal ecological studies — though the closure-effect identification is markedly weaker than the original build-effect identification, since the closures were not randomly assigned and post-closure outcome data are ecological.

**P2 verdict: [mixed] — revise to a distributional/circulation-specific claim rather than an aggregate-value claim.** The enclosure literature is genuinely split on whether enclosure raised or destroyed aggregate value:

- Allen's position (rents rose, productivity was flat) and Clark's (modest productivity gains) have argued past each other for years. The newest causal work — Heldring, Robinson & Vollmer (NBER Working Paper w29772, updated 2024) — finds English Parliamentary enclosure raised agricultural yields by roughly 45% and spurred innovation. This is a real problem for P2's *aggregate*-value form: enclosure does not uniformly destroy total output in the historical record.
- But the theory's own named "standing dataset," Dawes Act allotment, is close to a clean confirmation of the *circulation*-specific reading. Dippel, Frye & Leonard's culminating *PNAS* 2025 paper ("The impact of US assimilation and allotment policy on American Indian mortality") finds allotment raised young American Indian mortality by roughly one-third — around a 20% decline in life expectancy at birth — often on objectively better land than what was held before; Native landholdings fell from 138 million to 52 million acres by 1934. Whatever aggregate acreage or output accounting says, the circulation the land use had sustained was destroyed, and the human cost was severe and measurable. De Moor et al.'s PLOS ONE 2020 survey of 400+ Dutch commons over a millennium supplies useful descriptive base rates: commons "extinction" peaks around 1850, consistent with a long attrition process rather than a single event.
- **Reading forward:** P2 survives, but only in the form the audit forces on it — aggregate output and communal circulation must be measured as two separate quantities, because the record shows they can move in opposite directions at once (English enclosure: output up, circulation destroyed but not fatally documented; Dawes allotment: circulation destroyed and the human cost directly measured).

## 4. P4 — diversity of holding arrangements as an aliveness mechanism

**Verdict: [well supported] at the institutional-ecological level; [supported with caveats] at the community level.**

Ostrom and Folke's "response diversity" is an established mechanism in social-ecological resilience theory: heterogeneous institutional arrangements across a system provide shock-buffering that a uniformly-imposed single arrangement cannot, because different arrangements fail under different stresses. Removing that heterogeneity — forcing one holding arrangement onto every community — removes the buffering regardless of how well-designed the imposed arrangement is.

At the community level, Chandler & Lalonde's 1998 study (with data replicated across 1993-2000) found that community-level cultural-continuity markers — self-government, active land claims, control over ceremonial and health services — predict dramatically lower First Nations youth suicide rates across roughly 200 British Columbia bands. A 2024 *Transcultural Psychiatry* special issue (Burack, Bombay & Kirmayer) reaffirms the finding's influence, and it has been adopted into British Columbia public-health surveillance practice. The honest caveats: the design is ecological (band-level, not individual-level), small-n relative to the number of markers considered, and correlational — causal direction and confounding with the severity of colonial violence experienced by different bands are both unresolved. The association has replicated across the available data, but it has not been, and likely cannot easily be, subjected to the kind of natural-experiment isolation the enclosure literature has managed.

| Prediction | Verdict | Strongest support | Strongest challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 (ceremony fraction) | Partially supported, underspecified | Sure Start/library lag-then-collapse pattern | No study isolates ceremony fraction from general social spending |
| P2 (enclosure excess loss) | Mixed; revise to distributional/circulation-specific | Dawes allotment mortality/dispossession (Dippel, Frye & Leonard 2025) | English enclosure raised yields ~45% (Heldring, Robinson & Vollmer) |
| P3 (ritual as load-bearing tech) | Supported at small-medium scale; not at societal scale | Naltrexone bonding mechanism; feasting-as-infrastructure | Seshat/Whitehouse 2019 Nature retraction; synchrony-expectancy shrinkage |
| P4 (diversity as aliveness) | Well supported institutionally; supported-with-caveats at community level | Ostrom/Folke response diversity; Chandler & Lalonde replication | Ecological design, small-n, colonial-violence confounding |

## 6. Operationalizing "ceremony fraction"

This is the prediction (P1) with the clearest theoretical statement and the least existing empirical purchase, so it gets the audit's one concrete measurement proposal.

**Numerator:** time or budget allocated to recurring, redundant-tie-renewal activity that produces no one-off deliverable — feasts, standing gatherings, calendared rituals, non-transactional mutual-aid check-ins. In the American Time Use Survey this corresponds to the "Religious and spiritual activities" and "Organizational, civic and religious activities" codes, disaggregated from task-based volunteering (which belongs to the forest, not the cycle).

**Denominator:** total inter-household coordination allocation — everything spent, in time or money, coordinating with other households at all, transactional or not.

**Network validation.** Two candidate data sources let the ceremony fraction be checked against an independently measured topological quantity: co-attendance hypergraph nerves built from the National Congregations Study (NORC/ARDA, 1998-2019, roughly 5,300 congregations), or cooperative-lending panels (the *Nature* 2026 paper "Punctuated Decline of Human Cooperation," 47,931 payments across 7,108 borrowers). From either, compute the first Betti number per time period, then regress subsequent trust/cooperation outcomes jointly on lagged ceremony fraction and lagged Betti-1, net of asset stock — the test that would show whether ceremony fraction is doing predictive work the topology alone does not already capture, and vice versa.

**Natural-experiment leverage.** The staggered rollout and closure of UK Sure Start centres and libraries (IFS/ONS-linked administrative data) is the single most decisive test available today: it allows testing whether pre-closure ceremony fraction — not merely pre-closure service spending — predicts the shape of the post-closure trajectory, which is the specific claim P1 makes that generic social-spending measures cannot adjudicate.

## 7. Directions

1. Build the ceremony-fraction measure on ATUS data and validate it against National Congregations Study co-attendance topology, per §6.
2. Run the joint regression (lagged ceremony fraction, lagged Betti-1, asset stock) against the staggered UK Sure Start/library closure natural experiment; this is the test that would most directly confirm or kill P1 in its current form.
3. Separate P2 into its two component claims — aggregate output effect and circulation-destruction effect — and seek a dataset (ideally not Dawes allotment alone, given its unique severity) where both can be measured independently on the same enclosure event.
4. Re-run the Whitehouse/Seshat-style civilizational-scale religiosity-cooperation claim with the corrected missing-data handling from the 2021 reanalysis, to see whether any scale-up signal survives at all, or whether P3 should be permanently restricted to small-medium-group claims.
5. Extend the Chandler & Lalonde design past ecological correlation — a matched-community or instrumental-variable design isolating cultural-continuity/self-governance markers from colonial-violence severity would substantially strengthen P4's community-level grade.
6. Pursue the empty niche named in §1: apply persistent-homology/simplicial-contagion machinery directly to kinship, gift-exchange, or intentional-community network data with the cycle-equals-held-trust interpretation, rather than the generic resilience interpretation the existing higher-order-network literature uses.
