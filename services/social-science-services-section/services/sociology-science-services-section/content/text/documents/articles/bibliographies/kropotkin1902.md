# Kropotkin 1902 — *Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution*

Peter Kropotkin. *Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution*. London: William Heinemann, 1902.

## Status

Read locally (public-domain, widely available editions); foundational reference for the [anarchism](../../../triage/theories/critical-theory/theories/anarchism) subdomain and for the contemporary mutual-aid disaster-response work in [`anarchism/theories/disaster-response/`](../../../triage/theories/critical-theory/theories/anarchism/theories/disaster-response).

## Annotation

Kropotkin's argument against the social-Darwinist reading of evolution as competition only. Drawing on his own zoological observations in Siberia and on a wide review of biology, anthropology, and history, Kropotkin argues that *mutual aid* — cooperation, mutual support, the practical solidarity that allows organisms (and societies) to survive — is as fundamental to evolutionary success as competition. Species that cooperate within themselves outcompete species that do not.

The biological argument is the foundation; the political argument is the load. If mutual aid is constitutive of human social life across history (as Kropotkin documents through chapters on tribal societies, medieval cities, peasant villages, guilds, friendly societies, trade unions, and modern voluntary association), then the modern state's claim to be the unique source of social order is empirically false. The state is recent, partial, and has actively suppressed the mutual-aid associations that organized social life before it. Anarchism is not utopian projection but recovery.

The book remains in print and in active use. Contemporary mutual-aid disaster response (in Puerto Rico after María, in the Pacific Northwest after wildfire, across the COVID-19 response, in the work of mutual-aid disaster-relief networks) explicitly draws on Kropotkin as method — not just as theoretical reference.
