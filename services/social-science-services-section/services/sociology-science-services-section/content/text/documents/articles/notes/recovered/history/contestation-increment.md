# Contestation increment

The morphism $\delta_{\mathrm{con}}$ that introduces contradicting witnesses into structural, positional, or affective predicate cells in the social settling — the substrate-algebraic realization of social contestation: the act of disputing established social arrangements, challenging classification systems, or refusing subject-positions.

Contestation is not merely disagreement in the truth order ($\mathbf{f}$ versus $\mathbf{t}$). It is the act of producing a witness that contradicts a settled cell — moving a positional or structural cell from $\mathbf{t}$ (settled, stable) toward $\top$ (contradicted, unstable). The $\top$ state is the substrate representation of *open struggle*: a social fact that is now contested rather than settled.

## Definition

Let $\phi = \langle s, p, o\rangle$ be a cell that has settled at $\mathbf{t}$ in the social settling. A contestation increment at $\phi$ is an event $\delta_{\mathrm{con}}(\phi)$ that asserts a negative witness $w^-$ for $\phi$:

$$\delta_{\mathrm{con}}(\phi) : \quad v(\phi) \;\mapsto\; \top$$

The cell moves from $\mathbf{t}$ to $\top$ (by settling-monotonicity, it cannot move backward to $\bot$; it can only gain knowledge — and the negative witness adds knowledge, producing contradiction). The settling now faces a repair situation: the social system must respond to the $\top$ state by resolving it.

## Resolution pathways

Once $\delta_{\mathrm{con}}$ has moved a cell to $\top$, the social settling's closure rules face three structural response patterns:

**Absorption (symbolic violence resolution).** The social system fires the symbolic violence morphism, suppressing the negative witness and settling the cell back toward $\mathbf{t}$. This is the dominant pathway in high-structural-determination regimes. The contestation is absorbed without changing the social arrangement.

**Exclusion.** The social system responds by moving the contesting agent's positional cells toward $\mathbf{f}$ — excluding them from the field rather than addressing the contested cell. The $\top$ state on the original cell may resolve toward $\mathbf{t}$ by default (once the contesting witness-producer is excluded) or may persist if other agents sustain the contradicting witnesses. This is the repressive response.

**Transformation.** The contradicting witnesses accumulate faster than the social system's repair rules can fire. Additional agents add further negative witnesses. The cell remains at $\top$ long enough for other cells that depend on the contested cell (via the social settling's closure rules) to inherit $\top$ states — the contradiction propagates through the dependency structure of the social settling, destabilizing a broader region of the social base. If the propagation reaches enough structural cells, the social settling enters a transformation regime: the closure rules themselves are modified as part of conflict resolution, and the new settled state of the contested cell is $\mathbf{f}$ — the social arrangement is genuinely altered.

## The regime of contestation

By the [four-regime dichotomy](../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/four-regime-dichotomy.md), the regime of contestation determines whether transformation is possible:

- **Strict-regime social settlings:** Repair rules fire faster than contestation accumulates. Every $\top$ is resolved to $\mathbf{t}$ (absorption) or $\mathbf{f}$ (exclusion) within finite steps. Structural transformation requires external perturbation that the regime cannot absorb — a change in the entity set or the closure-rule system itself.
- **Scott-continuous-regime settlings:** Repair rules are finitely-antecedented but may take $\omega$ steps to fire. Sustained contestation over long periods can accumulate sufficient contradicting witnesses that the repair backlog grows without bound. Structural transformation is possible if contestation is sustained long enough.
- **Aperiodic-regime settlings:** The social settling has no countable closure ordinal. Neither contestation nor repair can fully determine the outcome at any finite stage. Both are ongoing: this is the regime of social formations that are simultaneously contested and reproduced without final resolution — the regime appropriate to the [late-liberal social base](../objects/late-liberal-social-base.md).

## Contestation and the $H^1$-class

In the aperiodic regime, the progress of contestation is not measurable by quiescence (no $\top$ states remain) — transformation in the aperiodic regime leaves $\top$ states as traces. The relevant diagnostic is movement in the social settling's $H^1$-class (per the [cohomological](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/cohomological-equivalence.md) classification of the settling): genuine structural transformation moves the $H^1$-class of the social settling's predicate structure, not merely individual cell values. This is the substrate-algebraic content of the sociological distinction between reform (moving individual cells) and structural transformation (changing the predicate structure that determines what cells can have what values).
