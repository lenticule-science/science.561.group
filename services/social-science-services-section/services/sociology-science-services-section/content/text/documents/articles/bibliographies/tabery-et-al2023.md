## Quantitative or causal contribution

Table1 reconstructs 830 sterilization cases in Utah (1925–1974), known non-Hispanic white665, other recorded identities15, race missing150. Minimum counted white share 80.12% of cases; 97.79% of cases with race known, not missing-at-random. Authors note 1961 substitution of an 'unfit to parent' justification for earlier hereditarian sterilization rationale. The modification is evidence of adaptive institutional authority, not a historical proof of white-population decline.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Research role:** Reconstructed Utah cohort with missing categories.

**Evidence available at its source:** The original 2023 *Lancet Regional Health – Americas* article reconstructs 830 archival cases under Utah's sterilization program from 1925 to 1974, including 665 known non-Hispanic-white cases and 150 cases with unobserved usable racial coding. The latter is 18.07% missing; conditioning only on known race would misrepresent overall case composition. The article discusses the 1961 shift toward a broad 'unfit to parent' justification.

**Interpretive/measurement limits:** Do not turn missing categories into a guessed racial assignment or treat all archival cases as a complete census of sterilizations. The documented rationale change demonstrates adaptive institutional rule, not automatically an endless intensification of hereditary purity; the authors also describe vulnerable victims' actual experiences.
