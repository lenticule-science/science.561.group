# When is overclosure a subject-formation pathology?

This question grounds [is-liberal-subjectivity-a-malfunction-of-stability-reward](is-liberal-subjectivity-a-malfunction-of-stability-reward.md). It does not replace that question. It asks for the substrate condition that would make the larger claim answerable.

## The question

In a predicated social settling, when do closure rules for subject-formation stabilize a social subject by suppressing contradiction rather than resolving it?

A subject must be stable enough to remain recognizable. Stability is not itself pathological. The pathology begins when a subject-position remains coherent by preventing relevant external predicates from changing the local settling. In that case the subject's internal coherence is not evidence of successful adaptation. It is evidence that the subject's closure rules have insulated the subject from the reference system it claims to track.

## Predication restatement

Let a social subject-position be represented as a local settling over predicates for role, entitlement, affect, legitimacy, threat, responsibility, harm, agency, property, and recognition. Subject-formation is the process by which closure rules make that local configuration durable.

Overclosure occurs when those rules continue to fire in a way that restores the subject-position after contradiction, but the restoration does not incorporate the contradictory information into a richer settling. It absorbs, displaces, negates, or privatizes the contradiction so that the subject remains the same kind of subject.

The pathology is therefore not closure. It is closure against the wrong boundary.

## Candidate formal condition

A subject-position is pathologically overclosed when all three conditions hold:

1. The local subject-settling remains internally coherent across updates.
2. The wider social or material reference settling diverges from the subject-settling across those same updates.
3. The closure rules of the subject-position reduce local contradiction without increasing coupling to the reference settling.

This condition would distinguish ordinary stabilization from pathological self-stabilization. Ordinary stabilization reduces contradiction while preserving or improving reference-coupling. Pathological overclosure reduces contradiction by weakening the subject's sensitivity to the reference system.

## Answer-enabling subquestions

What are the relevant contradiction predicates? They may include predicates for harm, dependency, domination, extraction, refusal, unpayable debt, colonial relation, racialization, gendered labor, disability, ecological limit, or infrastructural dependence.

What are the repair operations? Candidate operations include privatization, affect-formatting, genring, moral displacement, symbolic violence, liberal ontologizing, procedural deferral, and conversion of structural contradiction into personal feeling.

What is the reference system? A subject-position might be referenced to the material environment, the social field, the dominated other, institutional records, kinship relations, collective memory, or the subject's own prior state. The pathology depends on this choice.

How is divergence detected? The question requires a diagnostic for divergence between local subject-settling and reference-settling. Without such a diagnostic, overclosure remains a metaphor for stubbornness rather than a predicated-sociology object.

## What would resolution require

A resolution would require a finite example with explicit social predicates and closure rules. The example should show a subject-position encountering contradiction, applying repair operations, and returning to coherence while losing coupling to a reference system.

A stronger resolution would identify a theorem-shaped condition: under specified closure rules, self-coherence increases while reference-coupling decreases. That condition would ground the claim that a subject-form is malfunctioning, rather than merely stable or ideologically committed.

## Relation to the larger question

If liberal subjectivity satisfies this overclosure condition, then the larger question becomes tractable: liberal subjectivity can be studied as a specific historical subject-form whose stability reward has become self-referential.

If liberal subjectivity does not satisfy this condition, then the source question may still be politically or phenomenologically useful, but it is not yet grounded as a predicated-sociology claim.
