# Solinger, Rickie (2017) — Politics of Reproductive Rights in 20th-Century America

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199329175.013.430
**Publication:** Oxford Research Encyclopedia of American History
**Review level (2026-10-17):** Publisher research-encyclopedia abstract inspected.

## Annotation

Documents the stratification of reproductive legitimacy by race, gender and class, from promotion of births among favored white women to coercive restrictions on others. A comparative case must distinguish coercive sterilization of poor whites from the different forms of violence inflicted on Black, Indigenous and immigrant communities.

## Commission-specific source audit (2026-10-17)

**Research role:** Whose reproductive rights were stratified.

**Evidence available at its source:** The Oxford historical overview positions contraception, sterilization, abortion and parenting within different state treatment of groups defined by race and class. Positive policies for supposedly suitable reproducers and coercive policies for people designated unfit could coexist, making aggregate fertility an inadequate account of whose autonomy was sacrificed.

**Interpretive/measurement limits:** A broad historical synthesis is not an original causal estimate of a particular law's effect. Reproductive autonomy and population quantity are different outcomes; pregnancies prevented without consent cannot be ethically represented solely as lost future workers.
