# Abstract

Thesis. Platform governance, dual‑audience incentives, and stage collapse jointly create a structural double bind that rationally produces a confident, high‑cadence, low‑hedging style during topic shifts—even among conscientious experts.

We ground this claim in critical theory (confession/governmentality, interpellation, dramaturgy, field/affect), map constructs to variables, prove flip conditions (generation vs. resistance), and offer detection measurements and practical levers.

# 0. Thesis (Centered)

- Claim. Under high interpellation (i) and pastoral pressure (p), with stage collapse (σ) and field conversion (φ) elevating style over substance, and strong affective regimes (A_reg) penalizing expressed uncertainty, local ascent on visibility/uptake favors confidence (k↑), cadence (c↑), novelty (n↑), and suppressed hedging (h↓), even at low competence (s).
- Mechanism. A_perc(s,k; σ,φ) shifts authority from substance to style as σ,φ rise; β(i), γ(p) steepen novelty/cadence gains; λ(A_reg) increases the cost of hedging; recognizable authenticity (R_auth) rewards confident affect. Together they tilt ∇U toward performed authority.
- Consequence. A “neurotic” style (Loveday; Lehto) emerges as structural adaptation, not moral failing: confident tone, steady output, stylized vulnerability, late backfill.
- Contributions. Synthesis (theory→variables), flip conditions (regimes), compact demonstrations, platform heuristics, and measurable proxies (uncertainty index, authority markers, cadence, novelty, support lag).

# 1. Introduction

The contradiction targeted here is structural, not moral. A person who has earned recognition as an expert in domain A is thrust—by events, by audience demand, by algorithms—into domain B. The platform will reward a confident, portable style more reliably than it rewards careful uncertainty. Meanwhile, the same subject is learning B in real time. Without a practice for managing that tension, a reliable drift occurs: the performance of authority colonizes the space of inquiry, and unripe ideas are received as settled claims.

Stakes. Epistemic calibration (what counts as settled), public learning (what audiences take as knowledge), and labor conditions for experts under crisis (what practices are viable without penalty).

Vignette. A water‑systems researcher with a strong public audience is asked to interpret urban public health after a climate disaster. Notifications hail: “You’ve been tagged in 23 posts,” “Trending: heat and mortality.” Followers want steady guidance; partners expect professionalism. Draft reading notes (“not results”) are reframed as threads; a “behind‑the‑scenes” video becomes a reel. The confident tone that worked in water carries over; cadence rises; explicit limits shrink. Three weeks later, sources and derivations appear—but the confident take is already in circulation.

We ask: What are the conditions under which this drift happens? How can we explain them clearly and give readers and writers concrete ways to recognize and reduce harm?

# 2. Background and Related Work

We build on established lines in critical theory: confession/governmentality (foucault-1976-history-of-sexuality), interpellation (althusser-1971-isa), dramaturgy (goffman-1959-presentation-of-self), field/habitus (bourdieu-1996-on-television), affective labor (hochschild-1983-managed-heart; wetherell-2012-affect-and-emotion), authenticity as ideology (hund-2023-influencer-industry; boltanski-chiapello-2005-new-spirit), communicative capitalism/spectacle (dean-2010-blog-theory; debord-1967-society-of-spectacle), and recent articulations of “neurotic” styles under governance (loveday-2018-neurotic-academic; lehto-2020-neurotic-influencer). We integrate these in §3 and map each construct to model parameters there.

# 3. Critical Theory Foundations (Explanatory)

## 3.1 Foucault: Confession, Governmentality, Dispositif
Modern power operates by inciting discourse; truth is produced through compelled disclosure rather than prohibition. The platform is a confessional dispositif: interfaces, prompts, and feeds channel self‑narration—progress updates, mea culpas, learning diaries—into a discipline of visibility. “Learning in public” binds the author to repetition and regularity; the self is both the subject and the object of governance (foucault-1976-history-of-sexuality).

Consequences: (i) disclosure is normative (silence reads as deviance), (ii) truth‑effects attach to the person (not the method), (iii) the calendar (cadence) becomes a moral index.

Mechanism. Incitement to speak now increases pastoral pressure p and steepens γ(p) in C(c; p). Normative disclosure lowers tolerance for pauses, quietly penalizing low cadence and boosting the payoffs of regular output.

Observable effects. Regular “updates” are valorized; omission triggers inquiries; apologies for delay appear as ritual confession.

Variable mapping. p↑ ⇒ γ(p)↑; norms about “always on” make c a moral signal, not just a schedule.

## 3.2 Althusser: Interpellation and Ideology
Hailing constitutes subjects: “Hey, you—expert—speak now.” Notifications, trending prompts, reply demands are everyday ISAs. The platform’s hail is double: it names you as already competent while also enjoining you to prove it again. The subject thus strives to mirror an image of expertise before competence is rebuilt in a new domain (althusser-1971-isa). The imaginary unity (“the expert you”) precedes material acquisition; performance fills the gap.

Mechanism. The hail drives interpellation intensity i, steepening β(i) in N(n; i). Reactive novelty n pays more, pushing authors to address adjacent topics rapidly.

Vignette. “You’ve been tagged in 47 posts on heat and mortality.” The tag confers an imaginary mandate—expertise is presumed—so a thread appears tonight, before the literature review does.

Variable mapping. i↑ ⇒ β(i)↑; novelty becomes a rational first move.

## 3.3 Goffman: Dramaturgy and Stage Collapse
Front stage and back stage are ordinarily separated by norms and space. Platforms commodify the back stage: drafts, routines, and personal life become content. The cues that say “this is rehearsal” are stripped or stylized (jump cuts, captions), so the audience reads preparation as performance. Provisional, situated talk thereby acquires the legibility of finished claims (goffman-1959-presentation-of-self).

Mechanism. Stage collapse σ blends rehearsal into performance, shifting perceived authority A_perc toward k (displayed confidence) and away from s (substance). The same sentence in a “behind‑the‑scenes” clip is received as a claim.

Observable effects. “Notes to self” go viral as “authoritative takes”; “draft” markers are reframed as a vibe, not a status.

Variable mapping. σ↑ ⇒ ∂A_perc/∂k↑ and ∂A_perc/∂s↓.

## 3.4 Bourdieu: Field, Habitus, Capital, Illusio
Creators enter a field where symbolic capital is counted by metrics. Habitus (dispositions) adjusts to what circulates: you learn to “feel” that persistent output and assertive tone are the right moves because they convert to visibility (bourdieu-1996-on-television). Illusio—investment in the game—locks in: if you step off cadence, you exit the field of perception. Hysteresis follows topic shifts: old expert habitus migrates into new domains faster than competence, producing misaligned but recognizably “expert” performances.

Mechanism. Field conversion φ scales how efficiently style converts to capital. Hysteresis means the habit of confident presentation migrates immediately; competence s lags. The field rewards the recognizable style regardless of topical depth.

Observable effects. Early cross‑domain posts keep the same “voice” and structure; audiences respond as if authority had transferred intact.

Variable mapping. φ↑ amplifies the returns to k; hysteresis widens the window where k outruns s.

## 3.5 Affective Labor: Feeling Rules and Routines
“Authenticity” is organized affect. Feeling rules prescribe sincerity, vulnerability, resolve; affective practices routinize their display (hochschild-1983-managed-heart; wetherell-2012-affect-and-emotion). Anxiety is not an accidental byproduct but a resource: the “neurotic update” foregrounds struggle and perseverance, delivering intimacy and cadence simultaneously.

Mechanism. Affective regime strength A_reg increases penalties for visible uncertainty via λ(A_reg); recognizability of authenticity T_auth raises ρ·R_auth for specific affect patterns (confident resolve + curated vulnerability).

Vignette. “Hard week, but we push through. Thread below.” The pairing of confession with resolve checks both boxes: intimacy and leadership.

Variable mapping. A_reg↑ ⇒ λ↑; salient T_auth ⇒ ∂R_auth/∂k>0 and possibly ∂R_auth/∂h<0.

## 3.6 Authenticity as Ideology of Work
Hund shows authenticity as a historical, industrial construct; Boltanski and Chiapello show how calls for autonomy and self‑expression are absorbed into the projective city of capitalism (hund-2023-influencer-industry; boltanski-chiapello-2005-new-spirit). The upshot: “be real” becomes a specifiable production target with readable surface markers—hence optimizable.

Mechanism. Once “authenticity” is encoded as markers (tone, staging, disclosure frequency), it becomes a target function R_auth that content can optimize against. Surface alignment can outpace epistemic maturity.

Observable effects. “Authenticity checklists” circulate in creator guides; brand briefs specify “relatable voice,” “confident leadership,” “vulnerable moment.”

Variable mapping. Salient T_auth ⇒ higher ρ, stronger ∂R_auth/∂k relative to ∂R_auth/∂h.

## 3.7 Communicative Capitalism and Spectacle
In communicative capitalism, circulation substitutes for transformation; posting is participation (dean-2010-blog-theory). The spectacle elevates appearance over process (debord-1967-society-of-spectacle). Confident coherence travels; situated uncertainty does not. This biases selection toward portable authority styles.

Mechanism. Visibility is the currency; posts that compress a stance into a sharable unit dominate. The return to careful qualification is discounted; ∂U/∂h tilts negative absent strong counter‑norms.

Observable effects. Quote‑cards of bold claims outperform “here’s my reading list.”

Variable mapping. Increases the effective weight on k and decreases the marginal value of h.

## 3.8 Corporate Personhood and Real Abstraction
When persona is the unit of accumulation, life is processed into outputs on a schedule. The branded self is kept coherent across contexts; continuity of output outcompetes episodic accuracy. Real abstraction (the accounting cycle) disciplines the concrete (the inquiry day) into repeatable commodities.

Mechanism. The calendar becomes infrastructural. Missing cadence c implies lost capital; the cost of pausing to learn (raise s) is experienced as revenue loss.

Observable effects. Content calendars, “editorial months,” and “pipeline health” dashboards govern personal pacing.

Variable mapping. Raises effective γ(p) through organizational overlays; keeps c high even when s would benefit from a pause.

## 3.10 Theory→Model Synthesis: Constructs, Variables, Functions

Below, each theoretical construct is paired with a variable or function that will be used in the formal section. The point is not to mathematize the world, but to make our claims checkable and to expose the hinges where different conditions flip outcomes.

- Interpellation intensity (i): strength/frequency of hails to speak now (trends, notifications). Drives reactive novelty.
- Pastoral/disciplinary pressure (p): norm to disclose progress regularly. Raises cadence as a moralized practice.
- Stage collapse (σ ∈ [0,1]): how much back stage is read as front stage. σ=0 keeps rehearsal distinct; σ=1 collapses it entirely.
- Field conversion (φ > 0): how strongly style converts to symbolic capital relative to substance in a given field segment.
- Affective regime strength (A_reg ≥ 0): prescriptiveness of feeling rules; higher values penalize hedging and valorize confident resolve.
- Authenticity target (T_auth): recognizable markers of “being real”; stronger targets make surface markers easier to optimize.
- Competence (s ≥ 0): topic knowledge/skill actually possessed.
- Confidence/display style (k ∈ [0,1]): asserted certainty/authoritative tone.
- Hedging/uncertainty markers (h ∈ [0,1]): explicit limits, caveats, “open question” signaling.
- Novelty (n ≥ 0): newness to channel or reactivity to events.
- Cadence (c ≥ 0): publication rate/regularity.

Functions (named to carry theory):
- Perceived authority A_perc(s,k; σ, φ) = φ·[(1−σ)·s + σ·k]. Stage collapse σ tilts perception from substance to style; φ scales how perception translates to capital.
- Novelty reward N(n; i) = 1 − e^{−κ_n(i)·n}. Interpellation i steepens κ_n.
- Cadence reward C(c; p) = 1 − e^{−κ_c(p)·c}. Pastoral pressure p steepens κ_c.
- Authenticity recognizability R_auth(k,h; T_auth) ↑ with confidence and certain affect cues; may fall if hedging h is too high for the target style.
- Uncertainty penalty λ(A_reg) = λ_0 + λ_1·A_reg. Stronger affect regimes penalize expressed uncertainty more.
- Hedging production H(h | s,k) with ∂H/∂s < 0 (more competence reduces true uncertainty) and ∂H/∂k < 0 (performed authority suppresses expressed uncertainty).

Reading guide. When we later write U, the weights on novelty and cadence (β, γ) will depend on i and p; the penalty on uncertainty (λ) will depend on A_reg; and authority will be A_perc(s,k; σ, φ). This lets us ask: Which social change (weaker stage collapse? rewarding careful talk?) actually flips the local optimum away from performed authority?

## 3.11 Parameter Regimes: Generation vs. Resistance

Generation (neurotic style likely):
- Stage collapse high (σ≳0.7): rehearsal reads as performance ⇒ A_perc dominated by k.
- Field conversion high (φ large): style converts efficiently to symbolic capital.
- Interpellation/pastoral strong (i, p high): β(i), γ(p) steep; novelty and cadence pay quickly.
- Affective regime strong (A_reg high): λ(A_reg) large; visible hedging costly.
- Authenticity target surface‑readable (T_auth salient): ρ·R_auth rewards recognizable confidence/vulnerability mix.

Resistance (neurotic style suppressed):
- Stage collapse low (σ≲0.3): authority tied to s; rehearsal clearly marked.
- Hedging rewarded (∂R_auth/∂h>0 via community norms): readers value scope limits and method notes.
- Uncertainty penalty weak (λ small): explicit caveats do not reduce uptake.
- Slower cadence norms (p low): γ(p) shallow; publishing less often is acceptable.
- High substance weight (αφ(1−σ) large): competence visibly moves outcomes.

Practical levers: community labeling (explicit “notes/not results”), editorial policies that require scope/limits, ranking models that up‑weight references/derivations, and UX that separates drafts from results (σ↓), all move the system into the resistance regime.

## 3.12 Two Lemmas (Flip Conditions)

Lemma 1 (Authority‑Drift). Suppose σ≥σ*, φ≥φ*, and A_reg≥A* with σ*,φ*,A* such that:

1) α·φ·σ + ρ·(∂R_auth/∂k) − λ(A_reg)·(∂H/∂k) > 0,
2) λ(A_reg)·(∂H/∂h) − ρ·(∂R_auth/∂h) > 0,
3) αφ(1−σ) ≪ 1 (substance marginal).

Then for small steps of ascent on U, k increases and h decreases locally, even when s is low. If β(i),γ(p) are also large, ascent favors higher n and c. Proof sketch: (1) gives ∂U/∂k>0, (2) gives ∂U/∂h<0, and (3) makes ∂U/∂s small so learning s does not dominate the local gradient; thus ascent follows confidence/cadence/novelty.

Lemma 2 (Resistance Regime). Suppose σ≤σ°, λ(A_reg)≤λ°, and the community rewards carefulness so that ∂R_auth/∂h≥κ>0, while αφ(1−σ) is large. If further

1) α·φ·(1−σ) − ρ·|∂R_auth/∂k| ≥ δ>0,
2) ρ·(∂R_auth/∂h) − λ(A_reg)·(∂H/∂h) ≥ δ>0,

then ascent on U locally favors investing in learning (via s) and expressing appropriate uncertainty (h↑), while confidence does not dominate. Proof sketch: (1) raises the return to s relative to k under low σ; (2) flips the sign on h; together they move the system toward “expert naïveté.”

These are sufficient, not necessary, conditions. They identify actionable hinges: reduce σ (mark drafts/results), reduce λ (normalize hedging), and increase αφ(1−σ) (make substance legible and rewarded).

 

# 4. Definitions and Necessary Conditions

Definition (Neurotic Platform Intellectual). A platformed public expert performing authority under novelty pressure while actively learning a domain, produced by confessional incentives and affective labor norms.

Necessary conditions (diagnostic):
- Prior recognition as expert (role persistence across topics).
- Dual‑audience constraint (followers seek intimacy/authenticity; brands seek professionalism/engagement).
- Confessional cadence (regularized disclosure of process/persona).
- Novelty demand outpacing learning rate for the new domain.

Border cases and exclusions:
- Laboratory notebooks with explicit “not results” labels and constrained audiences do not satisfy the dual‑audience constraint.
- Domain transfer with close adjacency (A→A′) may not trigger the contradiction if epistemic debt is openly budgeted and repaid before prescriptives.

Terminology (usage of “neurotic”). We use “neurotic” in the sociological/organizational literature sense to denote an anxiety‑structured, cadence‑driven performance style that emerges under governance and affective labor regimes (loveday-2018-neurotic-academic; lehto-2020-neurotic-influencer). The term is descriptive of a style and its conditions of production; it is not a clinical label.

# 5. Mechanism: A Minimal Model

Let s(t) be competence, n(t) novelty, c cadence; k is displayed confidence and h hedging. Interpellation i, pastoral pressure p, stage collapse σ, field conversion φ, affective regime A_reg, and authenticity target T_auth parameterize the social setting.

Performance utility (reduced form):

U = α·A_perc(s,k; σ, φ)
  + β(i)·N(n; i)
  + γ(p)·C(c; p)
  + ρ·R_auth(k,h; T_auth)
  − λ(A_reg)·H(h | s,k).

Interpretation. Interpellation/pastoral increase the pull of novelty/cadence; stage collapse and field conversion tilt authority toward style; affective regimes raise penalties for uncertainty; authenticity targets add separate rewards for recognizable affect. If these levers are “high,” following ∇U steers toward k↑, c↑, n↑, h↓ even when s is low.

Dynamics (stylized):
- Topic shift raises n, c; dual‑audience pressure raises λ (low tolerance for hedging) and γ (high cadence). Absent explicit practice, updates to style drift toward higher A and lower E, stabilizing a confident persona independent of s.
- Repetition locks the style (fixed point). Anxiety becomes renewable fuel; cadence substitutes for calibration.

Prediction: In longitudinal traces, an increase in novelty and cadence coupled with decreasing uncertainty markers predicts a rise in confident statements before citations/replications appear—a measurable lead/lag pattern.

## 5.1 Variables and Functions (Educational Notes)

See §3.10 for construct→variable mapping. We recall only the functions:

- A_perc(s,k; σ, φ) = φ·[(1−σ)·s + σ·k] (perceived authority as mix of substance/style).
- N(n; i) = 1−e^{−κ_n(i)·n}; C(c; p) = 1−e^{−κ_c(p)·c} (novelty/cadence rewards, steeper with i,p).
- R_auth(k,h; T_auth): recognizability of the authenticity target; often ↑ with k and specific affect, may ↓ with high h.
- H(h | s,k): expressed uncertainty; ↓ with higher s and higher k.

Educational point: the model is not claiming posts literally compute U; it formalizes pressures so you can reason about trade‑offs and sensitivities.

## 5.2 Partial Derivatives: What Moves Utility

For U defined in §5 (with A_perc, N, C, R_auth, H):

- ∂U/∂s = α·∂A_perc/∂s = α·φ·(1−σ). If σ≈1 (stage collapse), ∂U/∂s is tiny: learning s barely moves U.
- ∂U/∂k = α·φ·σ + ρ·∂R_auth/∂k − λ·∂H/∂k (>0 if confidence both looks authoritative and hides uncertainty).
- ∂U/∂n = β(i)·N'(n; i) > 0, larger when i is high (strong interpellation).
- ∂U/∂c = γ(p)·C'(c; p) > 0, larger when p is high (strong pastoral pressure).
- ∂U/∂h = ρ·∂R_auth/∂h − λ(A_reg)·∂H/∂h (often negative if authenticity target penalizes visible hedging and regimes are strong).

Implication: when β,γ,λ are large relative to α·A'(s), gradient‑following behavior (conscious or not) pushes toward higher n and c and lower expressed uncertainty—i.e., portable confidence.

## 5.3 Dynamics and Stability (Fixed Points)

Suppose a simple update rule for style parameters θ (e.g., confidence, hedging rate) follows θ_{t+1} = θ_t + η·∇_θ U(θ_t), with step size η>0. A fixed point θ* satisfies ∇_θ U(θ*)≈0. If platform feedback keeps β,γ,λ high and A'(s) low at current s, then θ* tends toward styles that maximize A via stylistic proxies, maximize N and C, and minimize expressed uncertainty.

Educational test: a fixed point here means “if you keep doing what currently gets rewarded, your style stops changing.” Stability requires the Hessian of −U to be positive definite near θ* (small perturbations decay). Intuitively: once cadence + confidence + low‑hedging are rewarded, small increases in uncertainty reduce U, so the system falls back to the confident style.

## 5.4 Worked Numeric Example

Let A(s)=s, N(n)=1−e^{−n}, C(c)=1−e^{−c}, E(u)=u. Pick α=0.5, β=1.2, γ=1.0, λ=0.8.

Case 1 (low competence, high novelty push): s=0.3, n=1.0, c=1.0, uncertainty=0.2.
- U ≈ 0.5·0.3 + 1.2·(1−e^{−1}) + 1.0·(1−e^{−1}) − 0.8·0.2
- U ≈ 0.15 + 1.2·0.632 + 0.632 − 0.16 ≈ 1.375

If uncertainty rises to 0.5 (more hedging), ΔU ≈ −0.8·(0.5−0.2)=−0.24. A small increase in hedging costs more than the small gain achievable by a modest s increase (say Δs=+0.1 → ΔU≈α·Δs=+0.05). So the gradient favors lowering uncertainty rather than improving s in the short run.

Case 2 (raising cadence): c from 1.0 to 2.0 → ΔU ≈ γ·(C(2)−C(1)) ≈ 1.0·(0.865−0.632)=+0.233. Again, cadence wins in the short term.

Takeaway: with these parameters, short‑run gradients point away from learning and toward confidence and cadence unless α or A'(s) increase (e.g., audience that rewards uncertainty‑aware expertise).

## 5.5 Observable Proxies (How To Measure)

- s proxy: fraction of references to field standards (textbooks, surveys), presence of derivations or worked examples.
- n proxy: novelty score via topic shift or event‑reaction tagging.
- c proxy: inter‑arrival times of posts.
- uncertainty proxy: linguistic hedging markers, presence of explicit scope/limits, “next steps” sections.

Educational note: you can chart these over time. A telltale pattern is novelty and cadence rising while uncertainty markers fall, followed later by a backfill of citations. Measuring the lag between confident claims and support gives a simple red‑flag metric.

## 5.6 Demonstration 1: Two‑Audience Principal–Agent (Why confidence wins)

Setup. The author chooses two continuous controls each cycle: learning investment l∈[0,1] and displayed confidence k∈[0,1]. Competence s updates slowly by learning; content cadence c is exogenous or partially chosen.

- Competence update: s_{t+1} = s_t + η·l − μ·c with η≪1 (slow learning) and μ≥0 (output crowds out deep study).
- Follower utility (intimacy/"authenticity"): F = a_f·(ω_k·k + ω_c·C(c) − ω_u·uncertainty(l,k,s)).
- Brand utility (professionalism/consistency): B = a_b·(θ_k·k + θ_c·C(c)).
- Author cost of learning: cost(l) = (ψ/2)·l^2.

Author objective each cycle (reduced form):

U(l,k | s,c) = α·A(s) + β·k + γ·C(c) − λ·uncertainty(l,k,s) − (ψ/2)·l^2.

Minimal uncertainty surrogate: uncertainty = u_0 + u_l·l − u_k·k − u_s·s (more learning lowers true uncertainty but can increase expressed hedging; more confidence lowers expressed uncertainty; higher s lowers both true and expressed uncertainty).

First‑order conditions (hold s,c fixed):

∂U/∂l = −λ·u_l − ψ·l  ⇒ l* = −(λ·u_l)/ψ.

∂U/∂k = β + λ·u_k  ⇒ k* increases with β and with λ·u_k.

Interpretation. When the penalty for expressed uncertainty λ is large, and when learning tends to raise expressed uncertainty (u_l>0), the optimal l* is pushed down (bounded below by 0). Meanwhile, if confidence directly improves payoffs (β>0) and reduces expressed uncertainty (u_k>0), the optimal k* is pushed up. With slow competence η≪1, improving s pays off too slowly to counteract the short‑run gains of higher k and lower l: exactly the neurotic drift.

Parameter takeaway: Raising ψ (cost of learning) or λ (penalty for uncertainty) depresses l*. Raising β (confidence reward) or u_k (how much confidence hides uncertainty) raises k*.

## 5.7 Demonstration 2: Selection Dynamics (Why the style spreads)

Two strategies in the wild:
- S_A (Expert‑Naïveté): lower confidence (k_A), higher learning (l_A), more explicit uncertainty.
- S_B (Performed Authority): higher confidence (k_B>k_A), lower learning (l_B<l_A), less expressed uncertainty.

Expected payoffs (followers + brands), with weights a_f,a_b and audience parameters r (reward for confidence), q (reward for explicit uncertainty/learning), and v (cadence reward):

π_A = a_f·(q·l_A − r·k_A) + a_b·(−r·k_A + v·c)

π_B = a_f·(r·k_B − q·l_B) + a_b·(r·k_B + v·c)

If r≫q (confidence rewarded more than explicit uncertainty/learning), then π_B − π_A ≈ r·(k_B−k_A) − q·(l_B−l_A) > 0; S_B outcompetes S_A. Under replicator dynamics 

ẋ_B = x_B·(π_B − ȳπ)

the population share x_B of S_B grows until fixation. This matches observation: performed authority becomes the dominant visible style even if individual authors value accuracy, because the environment selects for it.

Numerical example. Let a_f=a_b=0.5, r=1.0, q=0.2, v·c=0.3, (k_B,k_A)=(0.8,0.4), (l_B,l_A)=(0.2,0.6).

π_B ≈ 0.5·(1.0·0.8 − 0.2·0.2) + 0.5·(1.0·0.8 + 0.3) = 0.5·(0.76) + 0.5·(1.1) = 0.38 + 0.55 = 0.93

π_A ≈ 0.5·(0.2·0.6 − 1.0·0.4) + 0.5·(−1.0·0.4 + 0.3) = 0.5·(−0.28) + 0.5·(−0.1) = −0.14 − 0.05 = −0.19

π_B ≫ π_A, so S_B spreads rapidly.

Cycle t=0 → choose (l,k) by FOCs: l*≈max(0,−λ·u_l/ψ)=0 (learning suppressed); k* large (β+λ·u_k>0). Post goes out confident; U high.

Cycle t=1–2: repeated confident outputs (k high), l stays near 0; s drifts or rises very slowly; early claims accumulate without support; audience learns to expect confident takes.

Cycle t=3+: backfill begins (some l>0), but now the local optimum for U is entrenched: lowering k or raising hedging lowers U immediately; the author is “locked in” unless the environment changes (audience norms, platform weights, or explicit self‑imposed constraints).

Conclusion: The combination of (i) two audiences, (ii) cadence rewards, (iii) penalties for expressed uncertainty, and (iv) slow competence growth produces a short‑run optimum of high confidence, low learning. Selection then propagates that style.

# 6. What to Look For: Patterns and Signals

You can spot the contradiction without special tools:
- Persona persistence across topics: the same expert tone moves into new areas quickly.
- High cadence + topic drift: many posts close together while the subject matter changes.
- Authority without support: early confident claims come with few citations or reading notes; support appears much later (if at all).
- Confessional coupling: “behind‑the‑scenes” or personal posts appear around big claims, sustaining output.

Field checklist (quick scan):
- Time between posts ≤ a few days for a stretch? Yes/No
- Topic breadth expands in the same window? Yes/No
- Early claims lack references/links/derivations? Yes/No
- Later posts add those supports (lag)? Yes/No

## 6.1 Platform Regimes (Parameter Heuristics)

- Short‑video feeds (TikTok/Shorts/Reels): σ high (stage collapse via format), i and p high (trend/react cadence), A_reg high (strong style cues), φ high. Drift likely unless counter‑norms are explicit.
- Microblogs (X/Twitter): σ medium‑high (threads compress rehearsal), i high, p medium‑high, A_reg medium‑high. Drift likely during crises; can be resisted with strong scope labels and link‑dense notes.
- Longform video (YouTube essays): σ medium, i medium, p medium; φ medium. Mixed; resistance possible with source packs and chaptered derivations.
- Blogs/Newsletters: σ lower (drafts vs. results legible), i/p lower, ∂R_auth/∂h can be positive in some communities. Resistance easier.
- Preprint/Open review: σ low, λ low, ∂R_auth/∂h>0; αφ(1−σ) high via method visibility. Drift suppressed; “expert naïveté” rewarded.

## 6.2 Typology of Publication Ecologies (Quadrants)

Axis 1: Cadence pressure (low ↔ high). Axis 2: Stage collapse (low ↔ high). Illustrative types:

- Quiet Lab (low cadence, low collapse). Drafts, notebooks, preprints. Authority tied to s; slow drift; hedging rewarded.
- Show‑Your‑Work (high cadence, low collapse). Frequent updates, clear “not results” labels, link‑dense notes. Portable authority limited by visible rehearsal.
- Slow Canonical (low cadence, high collapse). Polished outputs read as finished; drift limited by infrequency but rehearsal cues still weak.
- Hot Authority (high cadence, high collapse). Short‑form feeds, live reacts. Strong drift: k↑, c↑, n↑, h↓; backfill comes late or not at all.

Tip. Moving left or down the axes (lower cadence, lower collapse) generally shifts a field toward resistance—provided community norms also reward hedging and method visibility.

# 7. Practices for Stabilization (Design Patterns)

Pattern: Expert Naïveté
- Always state scope: “Exploration; not results.” Add uncertainty markers at claim‑level (e.g., modal verbs, confidence intervals where applicable).
- Budget uncertainty: define an “uncertainty budget” per post; if exceeded, defer conclusions or split into notes.
- Provenance discipline: include `depends_on` and `cites_internal` early; if none exist, state the absence and the plan to backfill.

Pattern: Topic Drift Quarantine
- Separate “learning logs” from “results.” Results require either replication, external critique, or internally linked derivations.
- Enforce a cooling‑off window before prescriptives (no calls‑to‑action until ℓ citations/replications are present).

Pattern: Co‑Reading Circuits
- Establish peer circuits (co‑readers) and reflect them as virtual cells/edges; only promote claims that survive a round‑trip through the circuit.

Pattern: Claim Cardinality Policy
- For each document, limit the number of novel prescriptive claims; favor one claim‑per‑artifact to improve traceability and backfill.

# Appendix A: Measurement Recipes (Proxies and Thresholds)

- Uncertainty Index (UIdx):
  - Hedging lexicon rate (per 1k words) + presence of scope labels (binary bonus) + “Limitations/Next steps” section (binary bonus).
  - Thresholds: UIdx_low < 2; UIdx_med 2–5; UIdx_high > 5.

- Authority Markers (AMark):
  - Modality/stance (imperatives, categorical statements), title assertiveness, absence of question marks.
  - Score normalized 0–1 by counts per length.

- Cadence (Cad):
  - Median inter‑arrival time over sliding window; flag high cadence if median ≤ 3 days over ≥ 5 posts.

- Novelty (Nov):
  - Topic shift via embedding or directory change; reactive tag if within 48h of trend/event.

- Support Lag (ℓ):
  - Days between first confident claim and first cites_internal/derivation; flag if ℓ > 14 days in high‑impact posts.

Composite red‑flag:
- (Cad high) AND (Nov high) AND (AMark high) AND (UIdx low) with ℓ>14 → likely authority–learning contradiction window.

# Appendix B: Glossary (Plain‑Language)

- Confession (platform sense): regular public self‑report (thoughts, feelings, learning) made normative by interface and audience.
- Front stage vs. back stage: polished performance vs. preparation; platforms blur them.
- Authenticity (as used here): an industrialized style that “feels real,” with recognizable surface markers.
- Parasocial relation: one‑sided feeling of intimacy with a creator; boosts persuasion of confident styles.
- Dual‑audience: followers reward intimacy; brands/partners reward professionalism; together push toward steady, confident output.
- Cadence: how often/regularly someone publishes; can substitute for depth if unexamined.

# 10. Quick Guides

Author’s checklist (before posting):
- Am I stating scope (exploration vs. result)?
- Do I include at least scaffolding references or reading notes?
- Is my cadence pushing me to sound more certain than I am?
- Did I mark what I’ll check next and when I’ll revisit?

Reader’s checklist (while evaluating):
- Does the tone match the evidence provided?
- Is support present now or promised later (and does it show up)?
- Is the author switching topics quickly while keeping the same authoritative stance?
- Are personal disclosures appearing to maintain cadence around big claims?

# 11. Ethics and Position

We name a structural style produced by contradictory imperatives; we do not pathologize individuals. “Neurotic” is a term of art in this literature (Loveday; Lehto). Writers and creators navigate governance, audiences, and labor markets that constrain viable choices. Our aim is to surface levers (σ, λ, β, γ, ρ, αφ(1−σ)) that communities and platforms can act on, and to offer practices that let conscientious experts preserve inquiry without disappearing from view. We recognize different risks and resources across identities and sectors; universal prescriptions are inappropriate—hence our focus on regimes and hinges instead of one‑size‑fits‑all rules.

# 12. Discussion and Limits

Confounders: pedagogical threads that look confident but are scoped to foundational exposition; communities that norm hedging and slow cadence; domains where rapid sense‑making is necessary (EM, safety). The account predicts weaker drift where explicit uncertainty is rewarded or where audience weight a_f ≫ a_b.

Falsifiability: if longitudinal traces show equal or higher uncertainty and support density during topic expansion while engagement remains stable, the drift is not structural but contingent; our mechanism assumptions need revision.

# 13. Conclusion

The neurotic platform intellectual is best treated as an emergent style stabilized by incentives, not a moral failing. Making the dynamics legible—and adopting simple habits like scope labels, uncertainty budgets, co‑reading, and cooling‑off windows—helps preserve inquiry while meeting the realities of public communication.
