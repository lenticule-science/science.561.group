# J. David Hacker (2003) — Rethinking the ‘Early’ Decline of Marital Fertility in the United States

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1353/dem.2003.0035
**Source class:** peer-reviewed demographic study.
**Read boundary (2026-10-17):** PubMed publisher-indexed abstract consulted; not full-text examined.

## Source-located evidence

Abstract: new mortality estimates, IPUMS own-children white fertility; distinction between overall and marital fertility.

## What the source supports

Reports that revised estimates place the secular overall U.S. fertility decline at approximately 1840 rather than the turn of the nineteenth century and marital fertility decline later, after the Civil War. A major contradictory interpretation when compared with earlier declines in census child–woman ratios.

## What it does not establish

The result does not negate evidence of changing nuptiality, fertility or local conditions; it demonstrates strong estimator sensitivity. Do not describe the 33% child–woman decline as a proven continuous fall in births per married white woman from 1800.

## Receiving and reuse

Paper I/Paper II methodological conflict register; prompts investigation of denominators and proxies before drawing claims about settler demographic replacement.
