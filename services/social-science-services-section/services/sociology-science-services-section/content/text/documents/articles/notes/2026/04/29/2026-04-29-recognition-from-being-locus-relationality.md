**Recognition** in the [relationality](../../../../../../_index.md) framework is the act by which a system registers an act as fitting a category. Where [witnessing](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/witnessing.md) is the evidence-side act — recording that a claim has been affirmed or denied — recognition is the form-side act: registering that the claim has been recognized *as the kind of claim it is*.

The two are distinct and both required for full settlement. A witnessing without recognition is uncategorized evidence: data that has been recorded but not classified into the system's normative or institutional vocabulary. A recognition without witnessing is the type-claim without the token-evidence: the system has acknowledged that this *kind* of claim is in scope, but has not yet recorded any evidence about whether *this particular* claim is true or false.

## Recognition as form-settlement

Where witnessing settles the evidence-direction of a claim (toward affirmation or denial), recognition settles the form: which predicate this claim is an instance of, which normative system it falls under, which scope it operates within. A claim that has been recognized has had its category determined; it now sits as an instance of a known kind, with all the structural commitments that membership in that kind implies.

Recognition is necessary because the framework's predicates and norms are not pre-given as a flat vocabulary. They are categories the system has built up through its own activity, and any new claim has to be placed within those categories before its evidence can be accumulated. The act of placing — of recognizing the claim as falling under one of the system's predicates — is what recognition is.

## Recognition and authority

In institutional contexts, recognition is closely tied to authority. A normative system's authoritative recognition is what makes a claim count as having a certain status: a court's recognition of evidence as admissible, a community's recognition of an act as authoritative, a discipline's recognition of a finding as a contribution. Recognition by an authoritative source is structurally distinct from recognition by an arbitrary observer; the framework's [normative system](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/normative-system.md) terms develop this institutional dimension in detail.

But recognition is not exclusively institutional. The framework treats any act of category-registration as recognition, whether performed by an authority, a community, an inquirer, or a sensor. What unifies the cases is the structural function: the act registers a claim as falling under a category, making it a candidate for further settlement.

## Distinct from acknowledgment

Recognition in this framework should not be conflated with acknowledgment in the everyday sense. Acknowledging something is, ordinarily, asserting one's awareness of it. Recognition in the framework's sense is more specific: it is the structural act of registering the claim as an instance of a known kind. Two parties may both acknowledge a claim without either recognizing it in the framework's sense; recognition requires the system's category vocabulary to be applied, not just an assertion of awareness.

This distinguishes the framework's *recognition* from the recognition-as-acknowledgment that figures in some social-theoretic literature (Hegel, Honneth, Taylor). Those traditions analyze recognition as inter-subjective acknowledgment — one subject affirming another. The framework's recognition is a structural act on the side of category-registration; it can coincide with inter-subjective acknowledgment but does not reduce to it.

## Recognition and settlement

A claim's [settlement](../../../../../../triage/settlement.md) position — its joint coordinate on the truth and knowledge axes — is built up through both witnessing and recognition. Recognition contributes the form: that the claim is the kind of claim that admits this kind of evidence, this kind of denial, this kind of further inquiry. Witnessing contributes the substance: that the evidence has been recorded, in this direction, at this amount.

A claim's full settlement requires both: the form must be recognized for the evidence to count, and the evidence must be witnessed for the recognized form to receive a determinate truth-axis position. The framework's mathematical layer encodes recognition as one of the two predicates whose joint settlement constitutes determination — the *recognition predicate*, alongside the *execution* (or *performance*) predicate that witnessing settles.
