# Thomas Jefferson; recipient John Wayles Eppes (1820) — Letter to John Wayles Eppes, June 30, 1820

**Canonical citation:** Thomas Jefferson Papers; Founders Online Jefferson/98-01-02-1352; Virginia Humanities annotated transcription.
**Original or catalogued text:** https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/letter-from-thomas-jefferson-to-john-wayles-eppes-june-30-1820/
**Reading grade (2026-10-17):** Full original letter transcription read through Virginia Humanities; short text compared with Monticello's archival quote `tjrs.monticello.org/letter/380`; handwritten manuscript not independently inspected.

## Case facts / exact analytical contribution

In a discussion of debt, intra-family estate transfers and selecting TWENTY enslaved people of varied ages/sexes instead of ten men, Jefferson describes women bearing future children as capital accumulation. It is direct primary evidence that an elite enslaver weighed potential captive births more highly than men's present labor product, integrated into land/property succession and valuation decisions.

**Source locator:** Letter paragraph on Bedford people, after creditor/debt discussion and before Bedford land paragraph; original dated June 30, 1820.

## Evidentiary limits and counter-reading

This is Jefferson's stated valuation argument; no named pregnancy, measured pregnancy rate, independent market price, or proof of implemented transfer follows from the statement. Some secondary transcriptions inaccurately date it August; use original June 30 date.
