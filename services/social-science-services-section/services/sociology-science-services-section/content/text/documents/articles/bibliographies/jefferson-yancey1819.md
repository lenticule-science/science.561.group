# Thomas Jefferson; recipient Joel Yancey (1819) — Letter to Joel Yancey, January 17, 1819

**Canonical citation:** Jefferson Papers: Retirement Series vol.13 p.582; Founders Online identifier Jefferson/03-13-02-0522.
**Original or catalogued text:** https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/03-13-02-0522
**Reading grade (2026-10-17):** Original documentary transcription inspected through Lehigh University original-letters collection and Monticello historical interpretation; Founders Online host inaccessible on this retrieval, manuscript image not compared.

## Case facts / exact analytical contribution

Jefferson's instruction to his overseer discusses five recent infant deaths across four years, worries enslaved mothers were not permitted time for infant care, and explicitly treats the successful raising of children as economically more valuable than the mothers' contemporary field labor. The source documents plantation MANAGEMENT PRIORITY and surveillance of child mortality, not a named forced conception, nor verified fulfillment of his instructions.

**Source locator:** Jefferson to Yancey 1819; Lehigh University History on Trial `Jefferson on Race & Slavery`, item 63; Monticello `The Business of Slavery at Monticello`, footnote 10.

## Evidentiary limits and counter-reading

Jefferson's causal attribution for infant deaths is a self-interested enslaver's conjecture, not an independent medical finding. The five deaths are his reported number, not a population rate. Do not conflate protection of slaveholder capital with care or freedom for enslaved mothers.
