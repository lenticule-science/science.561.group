# Social settling

The [predication settling](../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) over the [social base](social-base.md) $B_{\mathrm{soc}}$: the family of valuations $(v_\tau^{\mathrm{soc}})_{\tau < \lambda}$ indexed by an ordinal $\lambda$, where each $v_\tau^{\mathrm{soc}}$ assigns bilattice values to the $(s, p, o)$-cells of the social base, and the family is $\leq_k$-monotone (knowledge only grows) under the closure rules $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{soc}}$.

$$\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{soc}} \;=\; (B_{\mathrm{soc}},\; \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{soc}},\; v_0^{\mathrm{soc}})$$

where $v_0^{\mathrm{soc}}$ is the initial valuation (all cells at $\bot$ at the beginning of observation) and $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{soc}}$ is the social closure-rule system encoding the structural regularities, institutional constraints, and affective-governance mechanisms of the social formation under study.

## The closure-rule system $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{soc}}$

$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{soc}}$ is partitioned by the layer structure of the social base's predicate category:

**Structural reproduction rules.** Rules that fire when a social institution or structural arrangement has settled positively, producing further structural predicate cells that sustain it. These are the substrate encoding of Bourdieu's *reproduction*: established fields reproduce their rules of play because the closure rules associated with established field positions generate actions that sustain those rules.

**Positional constraint rules.** Rules that derive positional predicate cells from capital-predicate cells and field-membership predicates. An agent with high cultural capital in the academic field fires rules that settle their `licensed-to-speak-in` predicates at $\mathbf{t}$ and their `excluded-from` predicates at $\mathbf{f}$ — within that field.

**Affective formatting rules.** Rules in the P→A and S→A cross-layer direction (from the social-base partition): structural and positional predicates fire affective predicate cells. These rules encode affect governance: the social system's installation of feeling rules as closure rules.

**Interpellative rules.** Rules that fire on institutional-recognition events (an institution hailing a subject) and settle subject-position predicate cells. The [interpellation morphism](../morphisms/interpellation.md) is the single-event version; its action in the settling is the closure rule that fires from the `recognized-as-subject` witnessing event to the full battery of subject-position predicate cells.

**Contestation repair rules.** Rules that fire when $\top$ states arise in structural or positional predicate cells — the social system's attempt to resolve contradictions by re-settling them toward $\mathbf{t}$ (absorbing contestation) or $\mathbf{f}$ (excluding the contesting agent). The speed and success of these repair rules determines whether contestation succeeds in transforming the social settling.

## The social settling as historical trajectory

The social settling $(v_\tau^{\mathrm{soc}})_{\tau < \lambda}$ is a historical trajectory: each ordinal stage $\tau$ corresponds to a historical moment (a witnessing event, an institutional act, a social encounter, a media event) that introduces new cells or modifies existing ones under the closure rules. The trajectory as a whole is the social history of the formation under study.

This historicity is not a defect of the model but its substance: predicated sociology does not model social structure as a static object but as a dynamic settling that accumulates evidence, reaches stable periods (structural equilibria), and undergoes perturbations (crises, movements, institutional ruptures) that introduce $\top$ states into previously settled cells.

## Relation to other settlings

The social settling is superordinate to the [operations settling](../work/theories/predicated-work/objects/operations-settling.md) and the governance settling of [predicated-governance](../work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/): work and governance happen *within* a social settling, drawing on positional and structural predicates that the social settling has already stabilized. The predicate categories are related by inclusion: $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}} \subset \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{soc}}$ (the operational predicates are a subdomain of the social predicates); the operational settling is a restriction of the social settling to the operational predicate sub-category.

The social settling is subordiate to what might be called a *cosmological settling* (the full predication settling over all of reality at all scales) — but predicated sociology does not require that scale. The social base is the relevant restriction: social entities, social predicates, social evidence.
