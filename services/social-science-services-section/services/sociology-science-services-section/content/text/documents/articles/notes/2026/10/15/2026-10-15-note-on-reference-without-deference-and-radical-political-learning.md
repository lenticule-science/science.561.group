# Reference Without Deference

## Radical Political Learning, Anarchist Praxis, and the Nihilist–Insurrectionary Horizon

**Lenticule Science · Sociology Science Services Section · Working paper 3.0 · 15 October 2026**

This paper examines a proposed pattern of political learning, not a hierarchy of political worth. It combines a conceptual argument with a bounded analysis of published documents. The question originated with emsenn; research assistance, interpretation, and drafting used ChatGPT. Human authorship approval and independent scholarly review remain pending.

The [documentary evidence register](../../../../../../../../data/records/radical-political-learning-evidence-2026-10-15.json) contains fourteen records from ten receiving documents. E01–E10 preserve the preceding edition's findings; E11–E14 add directly inspected cross-setting evidence. Several records share a document or institutional setting. These counts describe the register, not a population of political actors.

## Abstract

Do political actors look toward anarchists for theory and praxis, while anarchists look toward nihilist and insurrectionary interlocutors? This paper separates the reach of political references from directional source selection, conceptual learning, practical adoption, and deference. A critical integrative review is combined with fourteen source-located records from ten purposively selected receiving documents. The record includes anarchist-oriented synthesis, reported conceptual change, argumentative reuse, explicit non-reading, anarchist references in democratic-socialist publications, and organizational borrowing attributed to Holacracy by Extinction Rebellion UK. These observations establish particular forms of reception, not their population prevalence or behavioral effects. We propose a problem-relative learning mechanism: a source may become useful by questioning a commitment that restricts a recipient's response to an impasse. However, borrowing can also reinforce a retained strategy without revising its premises. The mechanism must therefore compete with selective incorporation, access, expertise, affiliation, and source reputation. Reference-group theory and learning scholarship limit any claim that borrowing perspectives or revising evaluative criteria is itself novel. A measurement analysis shows how unequal documentation can generate apparent directional asymmetry without unequal learning rates. A redesigned prospective experiment compares a relevant constraint challenge with an equally novel within-constraint alternative, while separating argument content from source descriptions. The contribution is a more discriminating account of political reference: selective, sometimes mediated, potentially directional, but neither necessarily transitive nor equivalent to ideological succession.

## 1. The question concerns a contribution, not just a citation

A political setting may depend on ideas or practices developed elsewhere without sharing their sources' identities. A recipient may value an argument while rejecting its conclusion, use a procedure without knowing its history, or consult someone without giving that person authority. The originating conjecture draws attention to a possible asymmetry in these relations: widespread reference toward anarchists, with further reference from anarchists toward nihilist and insurrectionary positions.

The question should not be weakened into the observation that people sometimes read one another. Its stronger possibility is a disproportionate contribution to a shared political repertoire. Nor should it be inflated into a rigid ladder that every actor must climb. A spectrum can describe a local tendency while allowing overlap, reverse relations, several dimensions, and indirect transmission.

We distinguish five claims. **P1, reach:** a specified proportion of recipients consult anarchist sources or use elements with a supported anarchist transmission history. **P2, directional selection:** reference disproportionately favors sources that question additional commitments retained by the recipient. **P3, recursive reference:** comparable relations connect anarchist recipients with nihilist or insurrectionary interlocutors. **P4, consequential uptake:** encounters change descriptions, criteria, or practices. **P5, ordering:** a common spectrum usefully explains these relations. A scalar, transitive ladder is only the strongest version of P5.

These claims need different evidence. Finding a public attribution establishes neither direct reading nor changed understanding. Finding a changed understanding establishes neither changed practice nor the superiority of the new understanding. Finding several positive cases cannot establish predominance without an appropriate denominator.

The present contribution is twofold. First, it documents specific receiving relationships, including some beyond the explicitly anarchist publishing settings emphasized in version 2. Second, it states a mechanism and a measurement design that could distinguish problem-relative learning from other reasons for borrowing. The new documents strengthen the existence of cross-setting reference while also showing why not every such reference should count as a revision of retained commitments.

## 2. Concepts and prior theory

### 2.1 Reference, reception, learning, and authority

**Reference** denotes an observable or reported engagement with a source concerning a particular element. **Theoretical consultation** concerns explanation, diagnosis, justification, or evaluation. **Practical consultation** concerns how to perform or organize an activity. These domains can overlap. A procedure may embody a theory of authority, and reading together may itself be a political practice.

**Textual uptake** occurs when a receiving document uses or transforms an identifiable element. **Reported learning** is a source's account of changed understanding. **Enacted adoption** requires evidence of changed practice. **Formal adoption** concerns the admission of a rule or design; it must be distinguished from a document reporting that admission and from subsequent compliance. A posted constitution is directly observable as a document, even when its adoption procedure and lived application have not been observed.

**Deference** concerns accepting a judgment on the basis of attributed authority. It is not equivalent to considering a source's reasons. Neither deference nor its absence is inferred merely from a citation. Trusting relevant expertise can be reasonable; the analytic question is what judgment is delegated, under what conditions, and whether it remains contestable. The title names a possible relation, not a virtue automatically possessed by anarchist settings.

Reference can also stabilize an existing position. A recipient may find a better explanation for something it already does, recruit an authoritative name to defend it, or combine a new method with unchanged goals. These possibilities are not failed observations. They are competing outcomes. A study concerned with learning must distinguish novel conceptual revision from justification, reinforcement, display, and practical incorporation.

### 2.2 Stance is not an outcome variable for learning

Agreement and disagreement describe a recipient's stance toward a source. They do not reveal whether anything was learned. A hostile argument can expose a previously unnoticed assumption; an approving citation can add nothing to understanding. Conversely, disagreement can be wholly repetitive. Changed descriptions, demonstrable argumentative use, or an attributed report of revision are needed to establish more than exposure or engagement.

Composite documents create a further attribution problem. A reply may quote an opponent at length. Those words remain the opponent's unless the reply adopts them. Treating all statements on a page as the named author's position can reverse the relationship under study. The codebook therefore separates source voice, mode, stance, evidence basis, outcome, and authority.

The distinction applies to organizations too. An article hosted by an organization is not automatically its adopted policy. An organizational page may state a program without demonstrating every activity it describes. An umbrella movement's document may bind only specified constituent units. Institutional scope is part of the observation, not an optional qualification added after an influence claim.

### 2.3 Reference groups and learning theory already occupy part of this territory

Shibutani's reference-group formulation treats a group's perspective as a frame through which an actor understands the world and relates shared perspectives to communication channels [S32, abstract]. This is a close antecedent to the idea that what travels can be a perspective rather than a tactic. It prevents the present paper from claiming that distinction as a discovery.

Argyris's work on double-loop learning is another necessary comparator. The inspected 2002 article abstract describes case-based diagnosis and the testing of learning and change [S33]. The full article was not available for continuous inspection in this round, so no detailed reconstruction of its theory or empirical findings is claimed. Revising standards of judgment is not, by itself, this paper's novelty claim. A complete comparison with that literature remains a requirement for independent scholarly assessment.

Prefiguration research also already addresses experimentation, norms, consolidation, and circulation [S26, S27]. The narrower proposal here concerns the relation between a recipient's specific impasse, a retained commitment, its choice of interlocutor, and the kind of uptake that follows. It earns explanatory value only if those relations add something beyond established accounts of perspectives, learning, diffusion, and brokerage.

### 2.4 Political labels do not supply an ordering by themselves

The inspected anarchist, nihilist, and insurrectionary texts have overlapping audiences and self-positionings [S05–S07, S21–S24]. They are not three exclusive membership bins. An actor may hold several identifications, reject a label used by a critic, or change position over time. Public self-description, publisher framing, and analyst classification must remain separate fields.

Nihilism is not treated as a synonym for apathy, despair, every critique of progress, or every refusal of a constructive program. Insurrectionary anarchism is not reduced to a lack of organization. Bonanno's collection itself concerns organizational questions, and its introduction records a translation difficulty around the term informal [S06]. A label cannot replace edition-sensitive interpretation.

Likewise, three propositions about futurity are distinct: victory is not guaranteed; victory is impossible; and an action need not derive all its value from eventual victory. None logically entails the others. The debate examined below cannot be described accurately by assigning hope to one camp and rejection of guarantees to another.

An **accommodation** is defined here as a specified arrangement, dependency, or commitment that a recipient treats as a constraint. Candidate dimensions include institutional authorization, markets, delegation, permanence, and the role of a projected future. This is not a validated scale. What one actor calls compromise, another may understand as responsibility or infrastructure. Greater refusal is not a measure of truth, emancipatory value, or ethical worth.

## 3. Method and source custody

### 3.1 A bounded documentary analysis

This is a purposive critical integrative review with documentary analysis, not a systematic review, bibliometric census, representative survey, or causal study. The current register contains fourteen observations from ten receiving documents. It combines the preceding round's ten records with four newly inspected documents selected to address the outward-transmission gap and a contrasting source pathway.

Selection partly followed references and deliberately sought discriminating examples. It is therefore unsuitable for estimating source prominence. Several records concern the same writer, book, institution, or circulation pathway. Two XR documents do not become independent confirmation simply because they have different URLs. The DSA publication and chapter page share a broader institutional setting, although no direct transmission between those two pages has been established.

The primary documentary observation is what a text does: attributes an argument, reports a learning episode, makes a recommendation, denies reading, or states an institutional arrangement. Such an observation can be real even when the text's account of earlier events remains uncorroborated. We do not promote a publication into proof of its historical narrative or of later behavior.

### 3.2 Access levels and dates

Appendix A records recoverable third-round queries and direct retrievals. Appendix B distinguishes the earlier source-access record from newly inspected sources. Primary HTML was used for the four new receiving documents. For several scholarly publications, the accessible evidence consisted of publisher-indexed abstracts or substantial indexed passages rather than uninterrupted full-text access. Those restrictions limit the claims made from them.

Original publication, online-first publication, republication, archive upload, access, and reported event dates are different quantities. An undated webpage remains undated even when it displays a current copyright year. A publication's platform migration date does not replace its original journal year. No inferred publication date is used to establish temporal precedence.

No complete export of all search results and exclusions was retained, and no reproducible denominator of eligible publications was assembled. The search log is not a PRISMA screening flow. Search ended after the identified revision questions were addressed, not after a demonstrated saturation test.

### 3.3 Existing knowledge and revision boundaries

The owning Sociology corpus contains an [anarcho-nihilism note](../../../undated/anarcho-nihilism.md), inspected previously at blob `13826cb10bd4de1e8d663fbe171943a47f80e9fe`, and an [anarchist-grammar note](../../04/27/2026-04-27-anarchist-grammar.md), at `3671c345501a22ffb0c4dcf3d60025f9d809ba28`. They are local theoretical inputs, not independent population observations. This paper neither overwrites their distinct arguments nor uses similarity to them as evidence of influence.

Version 1.0's claimed execution log was not reconstructable from the retained evidence used in the second round. Version 2.0 superseded that log rather than certifying it retrospectively. The current edition preserves that correction. Its newly reported inspections are confined to this third round; inherited access claims are explicitly labeled as such. A later citation to an earlier analysis cannot multiply the number of independent sources supporting it.

Sociology remains the owner because the central question concerns learning relations, collective practice, and transmission. Political theory, intellectual history, organizational learning, and Indigenous scholarship provide constraints and comparisons. The paper creates no new institutional authority, operational instruction, or executable implementation.

## 4. What the literature supports, and where it resists the conjecture

### 4.1 A situated affirmative case

Graeber argues for anarchism's importance in global-justice organizing, particularly its organizational practice [S01]. Gordon's 2007 article offers a close scholarly antecedent: anarchism is presented as a major reference point in the radical networks he studies, with contemporary forms developing through convergences among movements [S21]. These arguments make the conjecture substantive, not merely an association among words.

Their scope matters. Interpretation of particular networks is not a prevalence estimate for all politics. Moreover, Gordon locates open-endedness and present-focused experimentation within anarchism itself. Those features cannot be reserved exclusively for nihilist interlocutors as though anarchists necessarily encounter them only at a subsequent stage.

### 4.2 Prefiguration, diffusion, and plural genealogy

Maeckelbergh treats prefiguration as potentially strategic rather than the opposite of strategy [S26]. Yates separates processes including experimentation, circulation, formation of conduct, consolidation, and diffusion [S27]. Gordon's later work distinguishes ethical relations between means and ends from temporal promises about the future [S04]. Juris's author overview supplies a related account of networked activist organization [S03]. These are antecedents to the present question, not phenomena renamed as new discoveries.

Polletta's historical analysis connects participatory organization with Black organizing, Quaker influence, and labor-educational traditions, while examining changes in the social meanings attached to organizational forms [S02]. Simpson's Nishnaabeg account emphasizes embodied, land-related learning and consensual engagement; her discussion also complicates an exclusive intellectual genealogy [S09, pp. 15–16]. The latter is not merely another ideological position to insert into a European-derived ranking.

These works require a distinction between resemblance and transmission. A practice can have several antecedents or be independently developed. A later carrier can make it available without originating it. Anarchist influence can be important without implying ownership of every cooperative practice. Equally, the existence of other traditions does not establish the absence of anarchist transmission.

McAdam and Rucht, Meyer and Whittier, and Zhou and Yang provide established accounts of diffusion, spillover, and brokerage [S10, S11, S20]. The present paper must specify what its problem-relative mechanism adds. Network position alone does not establish what was borrowed; Zhou and Yang explicitly identify a lack of tweet-content analysis as a limitation of their study [S20].

### 4.3 Recent research makes institutional distance a contested variable

Hill and Egefur's 2026 study of two Swedish social centers describes anarchist and punk commitments alongside contracts, associations, and relations with municipal authorities. Their multi-source historical analysis examines different responses to pressures on autonomy [S34]. This is reported scholarship, not an independent reanalysis of their archives here.

Pagirenaite's 2026 article argues, through the Scottish social-enterprise case, for strategic combinations of co-production, institutionalization, and withdrawal. It explicitly disputes state-avoidant accounts of prefiguration [S35]. Its historical interpretation and normative strategic argument should be distinguished rather than treated together as a general efficacy result.

The implication for this manuscript is analytic: institutional contact, dependence, ideological commitment, and capacity for action must be measured separately. More contact with an institution need not mean less opposition to its governing arrangements; less contact need not mean greater practical autonomy. Neither cited study establishes a universal advantage for institutional cooperation. Both make it harder to justify a source ranking based simply on institutional distance.

### 4.4 Radical-flank effects are not evidence of learning

Simpson, Willer, and Feinberg examine effects on support for moderate factions, and Dasch and colleagues report differentiated support effects across their studies [S13, S15]. The correction to the former supplies an omitted informed-consent statement, not replacement numerical findings [S14]. These studies concern responses to factions; they do not demonstrate that one faction learned another's theory or practice.

An actor can benefit from contrast while adopting nothing. It can also borrow something useful while losing public support. Media visibility, policy concessions, repression, resources, and learning therefore require separate dependent variables. A result about one pathway cannot settle another simply because both are described as influence.

## 6. New cross-setting findings

### 6.1 An anarchist reference can accompany a retained electoral strategy

In a DSA publication, Parker uses Kropotkin in arguing for mutual aid alongside continued electoral work [S28; E11]. This is a concrete receiving argument, not an inference from resemblance. It also draws on other political sources. The observed result is selective textual incorporation into the author's proposal, not a national DSA decision or a demonstrated change in practice.

A Quad Cities DSA program page explicitly invokes Kropotkin as an anarchist when presenting mutual-aid work and plans [S29; E12]. Its attributed quotation was not collated against Kropotkin's original text. The page establishes a public attribution in an organizational setting; it does not establish the source of each practice, the chronology of learning, or completed program outcomes.

These are positive observations for outward reference, but not proof that either recipient has no anarchist commitments. The defensible boundary is between identified publication settings and uses, not between imagined ideologically pure populations.

The cases also discipline the mechanism. A recipient can use an anarchist source without abandoning a retained electoral commitment. That does not demonstrate that the reference changed the commitment, and it does not refute a directional tendency elsewhere. It shows why attributed borrowing and premise revision need separate codes. Selective incorporation can reinforce a strategy as well as transform it.

### 6.2 A different immediate source for decentralized organization

XR UK's explanation of its Self-Organising System credits Holacracy as an organizational source and places the initial decision in early 2019 [S30; E13]. Its posted constitution also names Holacracy, reports a revised adoption on 8 June 2026, and specifies the units to which its rules apply [S31, A3, A6; E14]. The two pages share an institutional source and are not independent corroboration of lived practice.

This provides a contrasting immediate pathway. It is not proof that Holacracy lacks earlier influences, that XR is uniformly anarchist, or that a measured ideological gradient has reversed. It rules out a simpler inference: an analyst cannot attribute every decentralized organizational feature to anarchist transmission merely because the feature appears compatible with anarchism. A declared alternative source deserves investigation on its own terms.

The constitution also gives us a different evidential object from a reading recommendation. A specified governance document exists. Its reported admission and its implementation are further claims. The paper can recognize that additional documentary substance without pretending to have observed meetings, appointments, participation, or outcomes.

### 6.3 What changes, and what remains untested

The enlarged register supports **documented cross-setting reference and differentiated uptake**. It now contains public program attribution and a posted institutional instrument alongside political essays. It still does not estimate how common these patterns are, whether anarchists contribute more than alternative sources, or whether more refusal predicts source selection.

The four added records were chosen because they illuminate specific questions. Their distribution cannot be interpreted as a ratio of forward to reverse influence. A directional tendency can coexist with other pathways; the appropriate test is a comparison within a defined opportunity set, not the collection of a few favorable or unfavorable examples.

Most importantly, no new record independently establishes that a source-induced change improved reasoning or practice. The existence of a receiving document is a completed documentary observation. The strength of the proposed learning mechanism remains an empirical question.

## 7. A discriminating mechanism rather than a universal ladder

### 7.1 Learning across retained commitments

The proposed **problem-relative radical-reference mechanism** has four parts: a recipient encounters an impasse; a relevant commitment is treated as fixed; a source puts that commitment into question; and the recipient revises its understanding or feasible response. The adjective radical describes the relation to that particular commitment, not an overall score assigned to a person.

Each part must be evidenced separately. A source's invitation supplies no recipient outcome. A recipient's later endorsement supplies no prior impasse. A retrospective account can speak to several parts, but chronology and competing explanations remain to be examined. Coding a source as more radical because it was useful would make the explanation circular.

The distinctive prediction is an interaction: a contribution that questions the particular commitment involved in the impasse should matter more than an equally accessible and equally novel contribution that does not address that constraint. Mere novelty, ideological affinity, and a prestigious label must be credible comparators, not straw alternatives.

### 7.2 Beliefs, possibilities, and criteria are different revision targets

For analytic clarity, represent a recipient's problem description by B, its considered feasible actions by F, and its evaluative ordering by V. These are proposed descriptive carriers, not measured mental states or a complete psychology. A received argument can alter one without altering the others.

A factual correction can change B. A newly recognized organizational possibility can change F. A new way of judging an existing practice can change V. A recipient can also use an argument to defend an unchanged configuration. No change is presumed merely because a source is named.

This distinction sharpens the nihilist question. The potentially important transfer may concern why an activity matters under uncertainty, rather than a new technique. Yet a change in V is not automatically better learning: it can expose neglected value, rationalize a failure, or do both. Evidence of changed criteria must be separated from the normative assessment of those criteria and from outcomes for affected people.

The analysis does not treat every constraint as disposable. Some candidate constraints are revisable conventions; others preserve consent, accessibility, or other obligations. Expanding a considered choice set can introduce inadmissible or worse options. Neither logical flexibility nor greater refusal guarantees improvement.

### 7.3 Three pathways that should not be collapsed

**Premise revision** occurs when an encounter contributes to changing a previously retained assumption or evaluative rule. This is the proposed mechanism's principal outcome.

**Selective incorporation** occurs when an element is used within a substantially retained strategy. It may be useful and consequential without meeting the premise-revision definition. The new DSA receiving argument makes this alternative concrete.

**Mediated transmission** occurs when an element reaches a recipient through another carrier, perhaps with altered attribution or justification. A recipient need not know its earlier source. Establishing this pathway requires evidence about the particular element at successive links; broad thematic similarity is insufficient.

These pathways can combine over time. But allowing combinations is not permission to label every observed outcome as confirmation. A study testing premise revision must report selective incorporation without revision as a different outcome. A study testing mediated lineage must report unresolved ancestry rather than assign it to a favored source.

### 7.4 Why a source can matter without becoming an authority

A source may concentrate an argument, demonstrate a possibility, preserve an otherwise inaccessible text, or make an existing problem intelligible. None requires exclusive origination. This leaves room for substantial anarchist contributions without implying that other traditions lack the relevant capacities.

A source can also become an informal arbiter. Freeman's critique of unacknowledged structures makes that possibility relevant even in explicitly anti-authoritarian settings [S08]. Reference centrality, control over access, and the power to settle a dispute are different relations. The current documentary records do not establish their distribution. Accordingly, the register leaves authority unknown rather than interpreting every disagreement as proof of independence.

## 8. Relational specification and measurement implications

### 8.1 Units, elements, and relation fields

Let I be receiving units, J source units, P identifiable arguments or practices, and T observation intervals. A unit can be an author-document, a collective-period, or a consented participant; the level must be declared. Write R(i,j,p,t) for a reference episode. The arrow i → j means that recipient i looks to source j. A source-to-recipient dissemination diagram must explicitly reverse that convention.

Each episode has separate fields for reference domain, stance, evidence basis, observed outcome, authority, source locator, date bounds, and verification status. A theoretical and practical domain can coexist. Critical stance can coexist with uptake. A posted document and its report of adoption can coexist without establishing behavior.

For continuity, the earlier T, Q, A, N, D, K, and H labels can identify theoretical reference, practical reference, adoption, normative orientation, deference, critical stance, and non-learning exposure. They are not mutually exclusive categories of one variable. The companion register now implements this separation with controlled fields instead of leaving its proposed distinctions only in prose.

A missing outcome is unestablished, not proven absent. A null institutional cluster means that the register has not assigned one; it does not certify independent institutions. Shared documents, quotation chains, and institutional authorship must remain visible when counting observations.

### 8.2 Mention counts and graph paths underdetermine the target relation

Two corpora can have identical counts of source names while one recommends an argument and the other rejects it. A third can mention a book while explicitly denying reading it. Therefore name counts do not uniquely determine direct reading, learning, or deference. They can measure a defined visibility outcome or locate candidate passages; promoting them into a different relation requires further evidence.

Likewise, R(i,j,p,t) and R(j,k,q,t') do not entail direct engagement of i with k. When p and q differ, they establish no element-level lineage. Even when a temporally valid path for the same element is documented, i can receive an adapted version without endorsing or knowing k. Reachability and ideological assent are different properties.

These are elementary measurement arguments, not new empirical discoveries or machine-checked theorems. E07 supplies a concrete coding discriminator, not a general performance estimate for a retrieval system.

### 8.3 A spectrum is a model to compare

Let x_i(t) describe selected commitments. Sources can be incomparable componentwise: one rejects state authorization while retaining durable organization, and another has the opposite pattern. A scalar score requires a projection or weighting. That choice must not be smuggled in through ideological labels.

A useful local spectrum remains possible. Compare it with a multidimensional model using the same population, outcomes, and observation opportunities. More dimensions improve neither truth nor prediction by definition. A richer model must justify its complexity on held-out observations or another appropriate criterion.

### 8.4 Incomplete observation yields bounds

Suppose a completely enumerated roster contains N units. Correctly classified positives number k, correctly classified negatives n, and unresolved units u, with N = k + n + u. Under error-free classification of the specified relation and interval, the prevalence pi satisfies:

**k/N ≤ pi ≤ (k + u)/N.**

The number of unresolved positives can be any integer from 0 to u; adding k and dividing by N gives the bounds. These are identification bounds, not confidence intervals. An incomplete roster, classification error, or a change in the target relation violates the premises.

The current purposive register supplies no such roster. Its fourteen records cannot be inserted into this formula as though they represented fourteen sampled people. The relevant denominator belongs to a future population study, not to a collection assembled for interpretive contrast.

### 8.5 Unequal visibility can manufacture an apparent gradient

Even a well-defined denominator is not sufficient when evidence is recorded differently across settings. Consider two comparable strata g. Let p_g be the true probability of a specified learning event. Let q_g be the probability that the event is recorded and correctly detected, conditional on occurring. Assume no false positives and consistent units and intervals. The probability of an observed positive is then:

**m_g = p_g q_g.**

For positive denominators:

**m_1/m_0 = (p_1/p_0)(q_1/q_0).**

As a deliberately synthetic illustration, set both true probabilities to 0.25, but detection probabilities to 0.80 and 0.20. Observed probabilities are 0.20 and 0.05: a fourfold apparent difference with no true difference in learning. These numbers are constructed to check the inference; they are not estimates from the documentary register.

Allowing false positives f_g gives m_g = q_g p_g + f_g(1 − p_g), which adds another unidentified component unless appropriate information is available. The point is not that one tradition necessarily documents more than another. It is that an observed excess of textual references cannot identify an excess of learning without assumptions about observation.

This matters particularly for theory–praxis comparisons. Published argument leaves a different record from tacit instruction or an unrecorded organizational adjustment. Apparent differences between reference domains can arise from evidence availability rather than different learning processes. A future study must measure coverage and source opportunity, triangulate genres where ethically justified, and conduct sensitivity analysis instead of declaring missing practical records to be negative outcomes.

### 8.6 Estimands and a candidate descriptive model

Three estimands answer different questions: the share of eligible recipients consulting a tradition; the share of consultation allocated to it; and the share of adopted elements with a supported direct or mediated lineage. Multiple-source episodes require a declared counting convention. Fractional credit is not a measured causal contribution merely because its fractions sum to one.

For an adequately observed binary outcome Y, a candidate descriptive model is:

**logit Pr(Y(i,j,p,t) = 1 | X) = alpha + a_i + b_j + h_p + tau_t + f(d_ijm) + gamma Z_ijpt.**

Here d_ijm is an independently coded, recipient-relative difference on a named commitment m. Z contains justified pre-encounter information about opportunity, language, familiarity, expertise, relevance, and affiliation. Recipient, source, element, and time terms account for heterogeneity, but do not automatically resolve dependence among records. No parameters are fitted here.

The choice set must include plausible alternatives available before the encounter, not just sources later cited. Accessibility measured after an encounter cannot automatically serve as a pre-encounter control. Theoretical engagement, criterion revision, formal adoption, and implemented practice require separate outcomes and observation models.

Shalizi and Thomas show why selection and influence can remain confounded in observational networks [S12]. More covariates do not, by declaration, create causal identification. The proposed analysis begins with description and prediction; stronger causal claims require a separately justified design.

## 9. Competing explanations and falsifiable hypotheses

The main alternatives are not peripheral objections. They determine what evidence could favor the proposed mechanism.

**Prior agreement or strategic reinforcement:** the recipient already held the relevant view and uses the source to defend it. A later citation is then evidence of use, not of the origin of the view. Earlier recipient records are particularly valuable.

**Expertise, accessibility, and brokerage:** a source is chosen because it answers the task well or is available through trusted channels. A putative refusal effect must add explanatory value beyond these features.

**Source reputation and identification:** readers react to a label rather than to the distinctive work of an argument. A label can also convey perceived competence or trustworthiness, so an experimental label effect cannot uniquely identify prestige or identity signaling.

**Shared antecedents and independent invention:** similarity results from an earlier common source, a shared problem, or separate development. Temporal precedence and resemblance alone do not establish borrowing.

**Retrospective reconstruction and differential recording:** a narrated encounter compresses gradual change, or one kind of learning leaves more accessible documents. These alternatives concern observation and memory, not necessarily dishonesty.

**External contrast:** a source changes third-party support without transmitting an argument to the recipient. That is a different pathway, as the radical-flank comparison illustrates.

The prospective hypotheses retain their identities while sharpening their tests:

**H1 — Problem-specific source selection.** Among credible pre-encounter alternatives, a source's challenge to the relevant retained commitment predicts engagement beyond novelty, access, expertise, and affiliation. An equally novel and relevant within-framework alternative is the comparator. A null incremental relationship or an explanation fully supplied by the alternatives challenges H1.

**H2 — Distinct outcomes.** Retained-commitment differences relate differently to engagement, conceptual or evaluative revision, and practice. Equivalent patterns under comparable observation challenge the proposed differentiation. A public text corpus alone cannot establish a difference from unobserved behavior.

**H3 — Recursive reference.** Specified anarchist recipients use nihilist or insurrectionary contributions in their revisions. The documentary record supports particular instances, not predominance. A prevalence claim requires alternative sources, overlapping categories, non-recipients, and a defined denominator.

**H4 — Selective transformation.** Cross-setting uptake more often preserves some elements while modifying others than entails adoption of an entire program. To test this, define which elements constitute the program and observe both preservation and alteration. A generic favorable citation cannot by itself confirm selective transformation.

**H5 — Task-dependent source choice.** Choices vary between diagnosis, evaluation, and sustained provision, with the task defined before observing the choice. Stable choices across genuinely contrasting, comparably observed tasks weaken this account. Post hoc relabeling of tasks is not an admissible rescue.

**H6 — Added explanatory or predictive value.** A problem-relative, stance-separated model improves on a scalar spectrum and on access/affiliation models after accounting for complexity and observation. Failure to improve is a negative result. More elaborate vocabulary is not evidence of superiority.

The wording nearly everyone remains a separate population target. Its threshold, relation, interval, and eligible population must be specified before a confirmatory test. The manuscript neither assigns an arbitrary threshold to the originating intuition nor claims that a few counter-pathways disprove a possible overall tendency.

## 10. A prospective design that can distinguish these accounts

### 10.1 Documentary sampling and historical inference

Define recipient rosters by activities and institutional settings rather than known citations to anarchists. Candidate domains include labor organizing, neighborhood provision, environmental campaigns, participatory governance, and political publishing. Include recipients with no apparent target references, explicit rejections, and sources from other traditions. Do not begin with a nihilist reading list and then estimate nihilist influence among anarchists generally.

Use author-documents for textual claims and collective-periods for organizational claims. Link them only where evidence supports the connection. Translations and republications are versions until a distinct receiving intervention is demonstrated. Several passages from one essay are dependent observations, not new actors.

For candidate learning episodes, seek earlier and later recipient material, evidence of encounter, a particular element, and a documented alteration or rejection. A detailed historical argument can assess these links without pretending to be a randomized study. It must still distinguish source testimony from corroboration and examine plausible competing explanations.

Indigenous settings require appropriate governance relationships before a new empirical study includes them. Published Indigenous scholarship can constrain categories without authorizing a contemporary dataset about communities or treating them as convenient comparison cases.

### 10.2 A codebook that distinguishes what is observed

Every record needs an identifier, unit, interval or date bounds, recipient document, source or unresolved source category, element, locator, domain, stance, evidence basis, outcome, authority status, alternatives, and release status. Source dates and access modes remain separate. The current register fills only what the inspected publications warrant.

A normalized outcome can identify textual use, reported conceptual revision, reported practice, a posted governance artifact, or reported formal adoption. It must not silently convert one into another. The register leaves behavioral verification unestablished for every current record and authority unknown where the evidence does not decide it.

Train coders on exploratory examples and assess an independently coded subset before adjudication. Retain disagreements and sensitivity to classifications. A second model pass is not an independent human coder. A confidence score is not a probability of historical truth unless calibrated for that task.

E07 should be included as a known-answer extraction check. Additional checks should distinguish embedded quotation, an attributed but unauthenticated quotation, an author proposal from organizational policy, and a constitution's scoped rule from universal movement practice. They assess specific errors; they do not certify an extractor or the historical account as a whole.

### 10.3 Preventing selective observation and leakage

Report which records were accessible, which were unavailable, and which were not collected for ethical reasons. Compare comparable genres and intervals before interpreting between-group differences. Where coverage differs, use sensitivity analyses such as the observation model in Section 8.5 rather than assuming perfect detection.

For prediction, hold out entire recipients, documents, source families, or periods according to the target use. Splitting quotations from the same text across training and test sets can inflate apparent generalization. Dependence from shared sources and institutional authorship requires treatment beyond independent-record standard errors.

Confirmatory definitions, exclusions, primary comparisons, and analysis choices should be registered after pilot design work but before inspection of confirmatory outcomes. Sample size must follow a design-specific precision or power analysis. No adequate sample size, completed preregistration, or participant study is claimed here.

### 10.4 An experiment on relevant challenges, not a reward for novelty

The previous proposal contrasted a constraint-questioning argument with an argument working inside the constraint. Without stronger matching, that contrast could simply reward a more novel or informative stimulus. The revised design uses expressly fictional organizational scenarios and a factorial comparison.

Let **A** indicate an argument that challenges a specified revisable constraint rather than an equally novel within-constraint alternative. Let **M** indicate whether that constraint is genuinely implicated in the scenario's impasse rather than incidental to it. Randomize both factors across matched scenarios. Counterbalance content so that one political or organizational prescription is not always the designated correct answer.

Consent, accessibility, and other protected requirements remain fixed. The challenged constraint can be an administrative convention, not an invitation to discard protections. Length, readability, information quality, plausibility, and novelty need pilot assessment. A matching claim must be supported by the pilot rather than inferred from the researcher's preference.

A primary outcome Y could be blinded assessment of the participant's reasoned problem formulation and proposed response. Its rubric should reward evidence, feasibility, recognition of tradeoffs, and preservation of protected requirements. It must allow justified non-revision to score well. Otherwise the outcome would define successful learning as compliance with the experimental argument.

The planned contrast is an assignment interaction:

**tau = [E(Y | A=1, M=1) − E(Y | A=0, M=1)] − [E(Y | A=1, M=0) − E(Y | A=0, M=0)].**

Under the randomized design and its specified assumptions, this is an intention-to-treat interaction for the fictional task. It asks whether the challenge has an additional effect when it addresses the relevant impasse. It is not an estimate of historical anarchist influence or a unique test of political radicality. A task-relevance explanation may still account for an observed interaction; field evidence is needed to connect it to the proposed political pattern.

An optional, separately randomized fictional source description can test source-label effects. Such effects may involve perceived expertise, trust, familiarity, or identification as well as prestige. The earlier edition's inference from a label effect specifically to prestige or identity signaling was too strong. Credibility ratings can be exploratory process measures, but should not be conditioned on as though they were pre-treatment covariates when estimating the assignment effect.

This design tests responses to assigned arguments. It does not directly test whom people would choose to consult. H1's source-selection component therefore requires a separate choice task or suitably observed field opportunities. All scenarios and source descriptions must be disclosed as fictional; no real author receives a fabricated statement. Ethics review, piloting, power analysis, registration, recruitment, and execution remain prospective.

### 10.5 Stopping and negative findings

Report whether collection ends because of access, language, resources, or an explicitly evaluated stopping rule. Do not rename a practical stopping point theoretical saturation. Preserve non-adoption, reinforcement without revision, failed implementation, alternative lineage, and an observation procedure that cannot distinguish the proposed mechanisms.

A useful result may be that the public record cannot establish prevalence or historical sequence. That result should change the design rather than authorize inference of invisible borrowing. No human-participant contact, private conversation collection, purchase, or behavioral intervention has been executed for this manuscript.

## 11. Ethics and scholarly qualification

### 11.1 Published relations are not permission to map private people

The register concerns published arguments and institutional documents. It does not resolve pseudonyms, infer private affiliations, record personal contacts, or map operational dependencies. Even public data can become more exposing through aggregation. A future release must consider that change in consequence.

AoIR's guidance addresses privacy expectations, the research lifecycle, and risks to participants and researchers [S19, pp. 7, 10–13]. CARE emphasizes collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics in Indigenous data governance [S18]. These references inform requirements; citation is neither consent nor evidence that governance has been implemented.

Critical examination of a political text is not endorsement of its prescriptions. The present paper does not validate contentious historical analogies merely by documenting their reuse, assign later political identities to historical victims, or justify violence against civilians. The sources' human subjects remain distinct from the positions of later interpreters.

### 11.2 Field requirements and limits of this edition

Intellectual history requires sensitivity to edition, context, authorship, and the function of an argument. Movement sociology requires mechanisms, comparison, and bounded populations. Network analysis requires correct units and relation definitions. Qualitative inquiry requires inspectable interpretation and a defensible treatment of disagreement. A conceptual paper can contribute without a population estimate, but must not claim the estimate it lacks.

JARS-Qual provides relevant reporting guidance; PRISMA-S concerns the reporting of systematic-review searches [S16, S17]. This purposive review does not claim systematic-review compliance or certification as completed primary fieldwork. Its mathematical implications are elementary arguments under stated premises, not independent empirical validation or checked Lean proofs.

This edition supplies an enlarged documentary register, contemporary scholarly comparisons, prior-theory constraints, normalized evidence fields, an observation-bias counterexample, and more discriminating prospective tests. It does not supply a measured political gradient, a causal estimate, independent coding, verified practice effects, ethics approval, or peer review. A source commit establishes availability of the manuscript, not scholarly acceptance or downstream operational delivery.

### 11.3 Independent review questions

An independent reviewer should be able to challenge the attribution of voices and sources, the separation of borrowing from changed understanding, the validity of the proposed commitment comparison, and the incremental contribution beyond reference-group, organizational-learning, diffusion, and prefiguration scholarship. The new cases should be evaluated for what they actually establish, not praised merely for increasing the record count.

The original question favors a directional interpretation, and the search followed it. Deliberate counterexamples reduce specific mistakes but do not make the analyst independent or the corpus representative. A full comparison with learning theory and a broader, multilingual source base remain substantive research needs. They are not reasons to promote the present bounded result into a finished empirical law.

## 12. Discussion and conclusion

The third round strengthens the outward-reference component of the question while narrowing what can count as learning. The documentary record now includes anarchist references used in democratic-socialist arguments and program presentation, alongside the earlier anarchist–nihilist and anarchist–insurrectionary engagements. It also contains a declared organizational source pathway that cannot be assigned to anarchism merely from resemblance.

The resulting picture is not a choice between a universal radical ladder and no meaningful asymmetry. Selective borrowing can be widespread, mediated, and directionally uneven while recipients retain important commitments. The same source can provoke revision in one encounter and help stabilize an existing strategy in another. The existence of both possibilities is exactly why the empirical relation must be typed and located.

The proposed mechanism remains specific: sources may become useful by questioning a constraint implicated in a recipient's impasse. It does not own the broader insight that perspectives or evaluative criteria can be learned. Its additional value depends on predicting source choice or outcomes beyond novelty, access, expertise, affiliation, and reputation. The revised experiment and observation model make that burden clearer rather than making the claim harder to falsify.

The strongest supported answer is therefore: **particular cross-setting uses of anarchist sources and particular anarchist engagements with nihilist and insurrectionary arguments are documented; some include reported conceptual change, others show selective incorporation or institutional self-description. Their comparative prevalence, direction, and behavioral consequences remain unmeasured.**

The next decisive advance would not be a larger collection of favorable mentions. It would be a well-bounded comparison showing when a source changed a recipient's problem, criteria, or practice, when it merely reinforced them, and whether alternative sources and unequal documentation explain the pattern better. Reference without deference names that researchable relation, not a completed ideological succession.
