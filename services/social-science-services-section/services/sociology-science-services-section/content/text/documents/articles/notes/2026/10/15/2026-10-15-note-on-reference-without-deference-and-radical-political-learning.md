# Reference Without Deference

## Radical Political Learning, Anarchist Praxis, and the Nihilist–Insurrectionary Horizon

**Lenticule Science research working paper · Version 1.0 · 15 October 2026**

**Document status.** This is a completed conceptual working-paper manuscript, not a report of a completed empirical study. It develops an argument, audits selected supporting and contrary evidence, and specifies how to test its uncertain claims. It has not received independent peer review. The question was supplied by emsenn; research assistance and drafting used ChatGPT. No human author is represented as having approved the manuscript for journal submission. No new interviews, surveys, private communications, or participant-level network data were collected.

## Abstract

Political actors may consult traditions that reject compromises they themselves retain. This paper examines the proposition that political learning tends toward anarchists, while anarchists in turn look toward nihilist and insurrectionary positions. We distinguish a universal population claim from a conditional mechanism of learning across differences in institutional accommodation. A critical integrative review places anarchist participant accounts beside historical research on participatory democracy, movement-diffusion scholarship, primary anarchist disagreements, Indigenous theorization, and experiments on radical-flank effects. The selected evidence supports the existence of anarchist influence and explicit invitations to anarchists from nihilist perspectives, but does not establish their prevalence, a universal genealogy, or a hierarchy culminating in nihilism. We propose a conditional radical-reference gradient: within sufficiently intelligible political relationships, actors may seek critique from sources that reject additional settlements, while selecting practical arrangements through different tests of feasibility, accountability, and care. Reference, adoption, endorsement, and authority are therefore separate relations. The paper formalizes their distinction, supplies countermodels to transitive interpretations of political influence, and develops falsifiable hypotheses about differentiated learning, brokerage, attribution, and reversal. A prospective protocol specifies sampling, source provenance, multilingual interpretation, non-adoption, causal limitations, and protection against surveillance-like data products. The contribution is a researchable alternative to both a universal radical ladder and an account that dismisses cross-boundary learning altogether: reference without deference in a plural, directed, and historically situated learning network.

## 1. The question: who supplies the next question?

A political organization can judge a proposal too disruptive to adopt while finding it indispensable to think with. Another can adopt a useful practice while declining its source's political identity. A third can read an opponent carefully without regarding that opponent as an authority. These are logically different relationships. A theory of political learning must preserve their differences before counting them.

The motivating conjecture is that there is a spectrum of looking to others for theory and praxis: many actors look toward anarchists, and anarchists themselves toward nihilists and insurrectionaries. Its strongest interpretation is not merely that radical texts circulate. It is that political actors repeatedly obtain conceptual or practical resources from people who decline an accommodation that the learners still accept. What appears locally as the edge of reasonable politics could therefore be an interface with another field of experimentation and criticism, rather than the end of political thought.

This is worth investigating without making the proposed ordering true by definition. Calling any decentralized practice anarchist, any severe critique nihilist, and any confrontation insurrectionary would produce the desired pattern through classification. Conversely, requiring every borrowing actor to identify with the source would erase the very cross-boundary learning under examination. The problem calls for relational evidence, not an expanded vocabulary of ideological ownership.

We separate five propositions. **P1, prevalence:** most political actors consult anarchists. **P2, asymmetry:** consultation disproportionately runs toward actors perceived as rejecting more institutional accommodation. **P3, recursive extension:** anarchists disproportionately consult nihilist or insurrectionary sources. **P4, adoption:** these consultations change practices. **P5, ordering:** the relations form a broadly transitive, one-dimensional spectrum. These propositions neither imply one another nor have the same evidential requirements. A documented invitation to anarchists bears on an available intellectual relationship; it does not estimate the fraction of anarchists who accept that invitation.

The paper's position is consequently asymmetric. It treats the universal formulations of P1 and P3 as unestablished; regards the scalar interpretation P5 as a restrictive model requiring unusually strong evidence; and develops conditional versions of P2 and P4 as the productive research target. The aim is not to replace an interesting conjecture with caution alone, but to identify the mechanism that could make a limited version true, the situations that could reverse it, and the observations that would discriminate between explanations.

## 2. Method, scope, and evidential discipline

### 2.1 Review design

This is a purposive critical integrative review, not a systematic review, bibliometric census, qualitative meta-analysis, or meta-analysis of effect sizes. Searches used a general web-search interface and direct visits to publisher, university, author, and archival sources on 15 October 2026, America/Chicago. Appendix A records the principal executed queries and selection decisions. No Scopus, Web of Science, PsycINFO, or complete library-database search was performed. No screening-flow counts are reported because a reproducible export of all returned and discarded records was not maintained.

Selection pursued conceptual contrast rather than frequency. Sources were sought for anarchist influence, anarchist engagement with nihilism, disagreements over insurrectionary organization, independent participatory traditions, movement diffusion, and the distinction between learning and radical-flank effects. The review therefore deliberately includes texts that complicate the starting conjecture. It does not infer that the selected works are representative of their traditions. Historical political texts are treated as primary evidence of articulated positions; research articles as evidence of their reported analyses; and author or publisher abstracts only as evidence for what those accessible summaries state.

The source-access register in Appendix B distinguishes full HTML texts, publisher excerpts, abstracts, author overviews, and announcements. Availability of a full article is not equivalent to independent replication of its results. An archive's upload date is not the original publication date. An author's account of influence is evidence to investigate, not an independently verified census of influence.

### 2.2 What counts as a finding here

The present findings concern the defensibility of an argument and the content of inspected sources. They include the existence of a nihilist address to anarchists, explicit disagreement within anarchist discourse, and incompatible outcomes that a single word such as influence would conceal. They do not include measured learning rates, estimates of anarchist centrality, or evidence that political radicality improves truth, effectiveness, or ethical standing.

The proposed model, formal examples, hypotheses, coding rules, and sampling design are contributions of this manuscript. They are not reported as propositions established by the cited authors. Citations accompany the narrower claims that motivate them. This matters particularly where a reasonable interpretation of a text becomes a conjecture about many people: the inferential transition must remain visible.

## 3. Reconstructing the terms without pre-building a ladder

### 3.1 Reference is not one relation

For this study, **theoretical reference** means treating an identifiable source as useful for describing a problem, criticizing an arrangement, or developing an explanation. **Practical reference** means consulting a source about how to perform or organize an activity. **Adoption** requires evidence that a recipient subsequently used or adapted a specified element. **Normative orientation** means treating a source as an example of what should be valued or refused. **Deference** means accepting a source's judgment because of its attributed authority, rather than merely considering its reasons. These are proposed operational definitions, not claims that ordinary political language has fixed boundaries.

A source can occupy several roles. A collective might learn a facilitation practice from an anarchist group, reject that group's economic theory, and retain an independently developed account of land, kinship, or public obligation. Coding such a case as either conversion or no influence would lose the mechanism. A record should identify the particular element, the mode of use, and the evidence for the relation.

**Critical engagement** is a further category: an actor addresses a source in order to dispute it. Hostile monitoring, policing, journalistic attention, algorithmic exposure, and reputational comparison must not be silently added to affirmative learning. They may become relevant explanatory variables, but they are not affirmative reference edges by default. The claim that nearly everyone looks to anarchists becomes trivial and misleading if watching, fearing, condemning, and learning all count identically.

### 3.2 Anarchism, nihilism, and insurrection are not successive membership bins

The primary texts selected here do not license three mutually exclusive populations. Bonanno explicitly situates his insurrectional project within anarchist argument [S06]. The Workers Solidarity Movement text also discusses insurrection from within anarchism while criticizing an insurrectionalist ideological orientation [S07]. These are intersecting and disputing positions, not evidence that one leaves anarchism by becoming interested in insurrection.

Aragorn!'s pamphlet is particularly relevant because it explicitly addresses anarchists and proposes a nihilist contribution [S05]. The evidence is an offer of intellectual resources, not a measurement of its reception. Within this paper, its challenge to inherited assurances is the relevant usage. We do not make existential pessimism, philosophical denial of objective value, Russian historical nihilism, political refusal, and everyday apathy interchangeable. Nor do we diagnose an author's psychological state from a political label.

A defensible dataset would retain an actor's own public identification, the date, the textual usage, and a separate research classification. An individual or collective may hold several identifications or reject the label a commentator assigns. When the relation is inside an overlapping intellectual field, treating it as movement between three exclusive ideological camps can manufacture a developmental sequence that the evidence never contained.

### 3.3 A local coordinate, not an absolute measure of radicality

The proposed explanatory variable is **perceived refusal of a specified accommodation**, not an analyst's universal radicality score. Relevant accommodations might concern reliance on state authorization, market dependence, delegated decision-making, permanence of organization, or confidence in a guaranteed political future. These are candidate dimensions for interpretation and measurement, not a validated scale.

A source could reject state mediation while favoring durable federation. Another could favor temporary association while relying on an extensive commercial infrastructure. A third could reject guaranteed historical progress while maintaining demanding ethical responsibilities. No scalar ranking follows from those descriptions. A locally defined comparison may be useful, but it must name the issue, the observer, and the period. Stronger refusal does not mean greater truth, courage, authenticity, or moral worth. In particular, violence is not a proxy for any of those qualities.

## 4. What the selected evidence actually supports

### 4.1 A situated case for anarchist influence

Graeber's account identifies anarchism as an important source of the global-justice movement's creative energy and connects that claim to organizational practice rather than universal acceptance of an ideological label [S01]. It is valuable as a situated participant argument. Its scope is the movement under discussion, not all contemporary politics. Juris's author overview of his ethnographic research similarly foregrounds transnational activist networking and the relationships between communication and organization [S03]. Neither accessible source provides a denominator for humanity, the political public, or all social movements.

Our inference is narrower: the conjecture has a recognizable domain in which to begin, but its population claim must be separately established. A study that begins with global-justice networks and concludes that all political fields depend on anarchists has changed its target population without evidence. The relevant next question is which relations and artifacts carried identifiable elements, not how forcefully an observer describes the movement's general spirit.

### 4.2 Participatory democracy has plural genealogies

Polletta's historical analysis traces participatory practices through SNCC, Black freedom organizing, Quaker influence, and the radical labor-educational commitments associated with Ella Baker and Myles Horton [S02]. It also analyzes changes in the social associations attached to organizational forms. This is a substantive challenge to treating every horizontal practice as evidence of anarchist ancestry. Leanne Betasamosake Simpson's account of Nishnaabeg land-based pedagogy explicitly locates political learning in land, language, stories, and Indigenous relationships [S09]. It must not be translated into a European ideological identity simply because an outside reader perceives affinities.

The methodological consequence is decisive: resemblance is an invitation to investigate a relation, not proof of descent. Anarchist influence can be real without being the exclusive origin of every practice anarchists value. A theory that makes independent political traditions invisible in order to establish anarchist centrality has undermined its own evidence.

### 4.3 The nihilist address and its uncertain reception

Aragorn!'s stated intention to expose anarchists to nihilist contributions documents precisely the kind of intellectual address implicated in the question [S05]. It makes the anarchist-to-nihilist relation a concrete research object rather than a speculative association invented for this paper. However, intended readership, distribution, attentive reading, endorsement, and changed practice remain different stages. A text can address a field and be ignored, resisted, selectively adopted, or reinterpreted by it.

Our proposed interpretation is that such work can function as a test of an anarchist argument's remaining guarantees: what still warrants action when a promised future or a total theoretical assurance is withheld? This is a hypothesis about one possible use of the text. It is not a report that all nihilists ask the same question, nor that anarchists generally regard nihilists as their intellectual superiors.

### 4.4 Insurrectionary theory contains an organizational dispute

Bonanno's text argues over forms of self-organization rather than presenting insurrection simply as the absence of organization [S06]. The WSM article distinguishes involvement in insurrections from agreement with an insurrectionalist ideology and criticizes aspects of the latter [S07]. Their juxtaposition establishes disagreement over political organization and strategy. It cannot establish which orientation is most common or most effective.

For the proposed model, this disagreement is useful evidence against coding every citation as admiration. A critic who reads an insurrectionary text closely may be refining an opposing account of organization. The same document can therefore establish attention while defeating the inference of deference. An adequate analysis needs the argumentative function of a reference, not just its existence.

### 4.5 Constructive accountability is not necessarily a retreat from critique

Freeman's argument about structurelessness identifies the possibility of informal power becoming difficult to hold accountable when formal responsibility is denied [S08]. Her own online version also records a publication and revision history that warns against treating all reprints as identical evidence. Gordon distinguishes ethical means–ends relations from a temporal framing in which a future is imagined to authorize the present [S04]. These are different interventions, but both make a simple opposition between uncompromising critique and practical organization inadequate.

The interpretation advanced here is that an actor can learn in more than one direction without contradiction. Refusal may supply a diagnostic challenge; an organizational practice may supply a way to answer it. The actor need not regard one source as the final authority over both activities. That possibility is the basis of reference without deference, not an empirical generalization established by these two texts alone.

### 4.6 Diffusion is already a relational research problem

McAdam and Rucht's model distinguishes relational identification from nonrelational channels of transmission [S10]. Meyer and Whittier examine movement-to-movement influence through organizational and social connections rather than treating movements as sealed units [S11]. Zhou and Yang's research further identifies brokerage that becomes difficult to see when movements are studied separately [S20]. Together these works establish that movement diffusion and brokerage are existing research problems; this paper does not claim to discover political borrowing.

The proposed extension is narrower: disaggregate what learners seek across a locally perceived difference in accommodation, and test whether theoretical consultation and practical adoption have different distributions. Anarchism and its nihilist or insurrectionary interlocutors provide a difficult substantive setting in which this distinction may matter. Whether the resulting model adds explanatory value beyond diffusion, affiliation, familiarity, and brokerage is an empirical question.

### 4.7 Radical-flank benefits do not demonstrate learning

Simpson, Willer, and Feinberg report experiments in which a radical faction can increase support for a moderate faction [S13]. The publisher's 2024 correction adds the omitted informed-consent statement; it is not presented as a revision of the numerical findings [S14]. Dasch and colleagues report differentiated results across two experiments: increased support for moderates in one and decreased support for radicals in the other, with prior sympathy relevant in the second [S15].

These studies concern observers' responses to factions. They do not establish that moderates learned ideas or practices from radicals. A moderate organization could receive increased external support while refusing every proposal from its radical counterpart. Benefiting from a contrast is therefore not a reference relation. Conversely, learning can occur without an observable benefit in public support. Conflating the two would allow evidence for one causal pathway to stand in for another.

## 5. The conditional radical-reference gradient

### 5.1 Core mechanism

We propose the following conditional mechanism. An actor confronting a problem may find its usual repertoire constrained by arrangements it is committed to retaining. A source that refuses one of those arrangements can make an alternative diagnosis or practice visible. If the source remains intelligible and relevant to the learner, that difference may increase its value as a reference. The learner can then take up a question, explanation, or selected practice without adopting the source's complete program.

This is a **constraint-distance hypothesis**. The distance is between specified commitments, not between superior and inferior people. Its effect depends on at least four proposed conditions: the learner has an unresolved problem; the source offers something not already available in the learner's immediate repertoire; enough shared language or experience makes the contribution interpretable; and engagement is possible at an acceptable cost. Absence of any condition can prevent the proposed gradient.

An anarchist reference may, in this model, expose an unnecessary dependence on institutional permission. A nihilist reference may expose reliance on a guaranteed account of historical progress. An insurrectionary reference may challenge the postponement of action until a prescribed organizational threshold. These are possible questions that such encounters could produce, not a claim that any label uniquely owns the question or provides a correct answer. The references become empirically relevant only where documents or consented accounts show that an actor actually used them.

### 5.2 Why theory and praxis could diverge

Consultation and implementation confront different selection problems. Considering an argument does not require reorganizing a care arrangement, changing a collective's decision rules, or accepting a new distribution of risk. Adopting a practice may do all three. Consequently, theoretical attention could travel farther across a perceived accommodation difference than practical uptake. This is a hypothesis, not an excuse to infer action from rhetoric.

The reverse is also possible. A practice can be adopted because it works in a local setting even when the recipient rejects its source's explanation. Similarly, a text can be admired but prove unusable. Theory and praxis should therefore be measured separately rather than placed on one continuum of sincerity. A disagreement between their measured patterns is potentially informative, not automatically hypocrisy or failed political development.

The practical selection problem also includes responsibilities to people who bear implementation costs. It would be analytically defective to measure the learner's sense of radical consistency while ignoring whether a proposed change makes participation harder for caregivers, newcomers, disabled participants, or people with insecure livelihoods. These are proposed outcome dimensions for future research, not demographic facts inferred about any cited group.

### 5.3 Reference without deference

A source can be valued for keeping a question open rather than for supplying a final answer. On this account, actors may consult critics whose refusal they respect while reserving the authority to judge each argument. Reference is then a relation of learning, whereas deference concerns the allocation of judgment. Neither logically entails the other.

This distinction addresses a possible paradox in the starting conjecture: how can anti-authoritarian actors look toward others without reproducing an authority hierarchy? The answer is not that they necessarily succeed in avoiding such a hierarchy. It is that consultation alone does not establish one. A separate inquiry must examine whether judgments become insulated from criticism, whether reputation substitutes for reasons, and whether practical decisions can be challenged.

An intellectual field could therefore contain high reference centrality and low decision authority at the same node. Another could contain an informal gatekeeper whose influence exceeds its explicit citations. Both configurations are possible under the model. Their empirical identification requires different observations.

### 5.4 When the direction reverses

The proposed gradient should weaken or reverse when additional refusal makes a source irrelevant to the learner's problem, when the learner needs expertise available elsewhere, when an experiment exposes practical failure, or when previously retained arrangements are judged essential to an actor's obligations. More distant sources may also become harder to understand, less accessible, or less credible.

The model consequently predicts no inevitable endpoint. Actors could learn outward in diagnosis and inward in implementation; learn across rather than along the selected dimension; or revise the dimension itself. A nihilist-identified collective could seek technical or organizational knowledge from people it does not regard as philosophically radical. This is a model-implied possibility, not an observation asserted about a real collective.

A useful political-learning theory must permit such cases without treating them as secretly part of the same progression. Otherwise the theory becomes immune to correction: every borrowing counts as forward motion, and every counterexample is relabeled after the fact.

## 6. A minimal formal model and three logical results

### 6.1 Objects and edge types

Let V_t be a set of identifiable public collectives or consented research participants during interval t. Membership labels are attributes with provenance, not mutually exclusive node types. Let P be a set of identifiable practices, claims, or arguments. Let L contain the following relation types:

- T: theoretical consultation;
- Q: practical consultation;
- A: adoption or adaptation of a specified element;
- N: normative orientation;
- D: deference;
- K: critical engagement;
- H: hostile monitoring or non-learning attention.

Define R^l_ij(p,t) as a coded relation in which recipient i engages source j concerning element p in mode l. Throughout this manuscript, the arrow i → j means that i looks to j. A material transmission diagram could instead point from source to recipient; the direction convention must not be silently reversed when presenting results.

An adoption record uses the same recipient-first indices, R^A_ij, but requires evidence of use, not merely consultation. Each record includes an observation state: present, explicitly absent within a defined observation procedure, unresolved, or unobserved. Absence of a citation does not justify coding absence of learning.

Let x_j(t) be a vector of source commitments. Let d_ijm(t) represent i's perceived difference from j on accommodation dimension m, measured independently of the reference outcome. This is not an objective scalar radicality ranking. A study may analyze a predeclared local projection of x, but should retain the underlying dimensions and report disagreements about ordering.

### 6.2 Result 1: attention counts cannot identify affirmative reference

**Proposition.** A dataset containing only counts of references to source names cannot, without additional semantic evidence or assumptions, identify the amount of affirmative consultation or deference.

**Proof by countermodel.** Consider two corpora with the same ten references from i to j. In the first, all ten references recommend j's arguments. In the second, all ten reject them. The name-count data are identical, but the affirmative-reference relations differ. The same construction separates consultation from deference: reasons can be considered in both corpora while attributed authority is accepted in only one. Therefore the aggregated counts do not uniquely determine the target relation. ∎

This is a logical limitation of the measurement proposal, not a new estimate about political discourse. Adding sentiment alone may still be insufficient: respectful disagreement and hostile appropriation can be more complicated than positive or negative vocabulary. The relation must be coded in argumentative context.

### 6.3 Result 2: a reference path does not establish transitive endorsement

**Proposition.** The existence of R^T_ij and R^T_jk does not entail R^T_ik, R^N_ik, or R^A_ik.

**Proof by construction.** Let i consult j about a meeting procedure. Let j consult k about the justification of historical hope. Let i have no contact with k and no use of that argument. The first two consultation edges exist, while the three proposed endpoint relations need not. Even where the same element travels through both links, i can adopt a modified element without knowing or endorsing its earlier source. ∎

A network path can establish reachability under a specified relation. It does not by itself establish direct learning, ideological ancestry, assent, or an endpoint's authority. The proposed sequence from broad politics through anarchism to nihilism must therefore be tested at its individual links and at the level of identifiable elements.

### 6.4 Result 3: plural commitments need not admit a unique ladder

Suppose two sources are compared on rejection of state authorization and rejection of durable organization. In a hypothetical coding, one rejects the first while retaining the second; the other does the reverse. Neither dominates the other componentwise. A scalar order can still be imposed by weighting dimensions, but the order then depends on those weights. Without an independently justified weighting, the ordering is not a finding supplied by the descriptions themselves.

This elementary observation explains why the present model begins with a vector and local comparisons. It does not prove that no field ever has a dominant ideological axis. A scalar model could outperform a multidimensional alternative in a specified population. That result would require evidence rather than being assumed by the language of the research question.

### 6.5 Candidate statistical formulation, not an estimated result

For a binary, adequately observed consultation outcome, one possible model family is:

logit Pr(R^l_ij = 1 | X) = alpha_l + a_i,l + b_j,l + f_l(d_ijm) + gamma_l Z_ij.

Here a and b represent recipient and source heterogeneity; f_l is a predeclared or transparently exploratory function of the local accommodation difference; and Z contains measured opportunity for contact, shared affiliations, language, task relevance, and other justified covariates. These terms are a proposed analysis specification, not fitted parameters. Network dependence and shared artifacts require treatment beyond independent-dyad standard errors.

The key comparative prediction is not simply a positive coefficient. It is a difference between layers: conditional theoretical consultation may increase over a region of positive accommodation distance while practical adoption does not increase equally. No particular linear, quadratic, or monotone function is asserted in advance as a law. Tests should compare the proposed gradient with models based on familiarity, access, task-specific expertise, and shared affiliation alone.

Crucially, including covariates does not magically identify a causal effect. Shalizi and Thomas show why homophily and influence are generically confounded in observational network settings [S12]. The present paper therefore proposes descriptive and predictive tests first. Causal language would require a separately justified design and assumptions.

## 7. Alternative explanations and the observations that matter

### 7.1 Shared antecedents and independent invention

Similar practices may arise from a common source, a shared practical problem, or separate invention. A borrowing claim needs a temporally possible pathway and element-level evidence. Finding an earlier anarchist text that resembles a later practice is insufficient when the later actors might never have encountered it. Finding no explicit credit is likewise insufficient to prove concealed borrowing.

The appropriate comparison includes non-anarchist sources available to the recipient, records of local experimentation, and the possibility that several traditions jointly shaped a practice. A multi-parent account should not be compressed into a single origin because one lineage is easier to narrate.

### 7.2 Selection rather than influence

Actors may choose to read sources because they already share the relevant commitments. A change in their network can reflect sorting rather than persuasion. Retrospective accounts may also compress a long process into a memorable encounter. Time ordering helps exclude impossible pathways but does not by itself remove common causes or selection.

Future process tracing should ask what changed, when it changed, what alternatives were considered, and what records predate the purported encounter. An account that survives those questions becomes more credible; it does not automatically become a population-level causal estimate.

### 7.3 Brokerage rather than ideological primacy

A source may be frequently consulted because it translates, republishes, teaches, or connects people, not because it invented the element being transmitted. Distinguish origination, custody, translation, demonstration, and adoption. An anarchist collective could be an important carrier of a practice without being its sole originator. The same distinction applies to nihilist publications, academic authors, and this paper itself.

A useful test would compare source centrality before and after accounting for accessibility and brokerage. The model gains explanatory value only if the proposed accommodation relation explains something that those alternatives do not already explain.

### 7.4 Strategic credit and archival visibility

Public attribution can be encouraged by affinity, discouraged by stigma, or altered during translation. Nevertheless, unattributed influence is not observable merely because a researcher expects it. A claim of credit loss needs positive evidence: for example, an attributable earlier version, a documented transmission, and a later version that retains a distinctive element while changing its acknowledgment.

Conversely, explicit citation can be ceremonial. A bibliography may advertise affiliation while operational records show no practical change. The protocol therefore separates attention, acknowledgment, uptake, and outcomes. It also retains inaccessible or lost records as missing evidence rather than converting them into zeros.

### 7.5 External effects rather than learning

A radical faction may alter how a third party treats a moderate faction, without transmitting any idea to that moderate faction. This is the competing pathway illustrated by the radical-flank literature discussed above. A study testing the present model needs recipient learning measures. Public support, media coverage, repression, fundraising, and policy concessions may be important outcomes, but cannot substitute for those measures.

## 8. Falsifiable hypotheses and adjudication rules

The following are prospective hypotheses, not findings of the review.

**H1: Conditional outward theoretical reference.** Within a predeclared political field and accommodation dimension, recipients with unresolved problems will show more affirmative theoretical consultation toward somewhat less accommodating sources than toward equally accessible sources offering redundant explanations. Evidence against H1 includes a null or opposite pattern after credible measurement of opportunity, relevance, and shared affiliation. A pattern confined to hostile attention does not support it.

**H2: Layer separation.** The relationship between accommodation difference and theoretical consultation will differ from its relationship with practical adoption. Equivalent patterns across adequately measured layers, or practical uptake consistently extending farther than theoretical consultation, would challenge the proposed formulation. An alternative layer pattern should be reported rather than redescribed as confirmation.

**H3: Conditional anarchist-to-nihilist or insurrectionary reference.** Some anarchist-identified recipients facing disputes about justification, mediation, or organizational delay will affirmatively use relevant nihilist or insurrectionary arguments. Establishing the existence of this pattern is easier than establishing predominance. A claim of predominance requires a denominator of eligible anarchist recipients, including those who do not read or who reject these sources.

**H4: Brokerage and transformation.** Cross-field uptake will more often involve identifiable translation or adaptation than unchanged adoption of an entire ideological package. This hypothesis fails where well-documented transfers are predominantly unmodified and accompanied by broad deference. It is not confirmed merely by the existence of brokers; their contribution to particular transfers must be shown.

**H5: Contextual reversal.** When practical obligations change, source preferences can reverse without corresponding ideological conversion. A prospective study could examine a transition from exploratory discussion to sustained collective provision. Consistent preference for the same sources across genuinely different tasks would weaken the proposed role of task-dependent selection.

**H6: Multidimensional improvement.** A model retaining distinct accommodation dimensions and relation layers will explain or predict held-out observations better than a single radicality ladder, after accounting for added complexity. Failure to improve out-of-sample performance is a real challenge. Conceptual richness alone is not empirical superiority.

**Universal-claim rule.** Terms such as everyone, most, and predominant must be translated into population, observation window, relation type, and threshold before confirmatory analysis. A threshold such as 90 percent would be an explicitly chosen convention for a future study, not a number supplied by the original question or by this review. No such prevalence estimate is presently available from this manuscript.

## 9. Prospective research protocol

### 9.1 Sampling without selecting the answer

The first empirical study should define a bounded population through activities and institutional settings rather than through preselected ideological labels. Candidate strata include environmental campaigns, labor organizing, neighborhood provision, participatory-governance initiatives, and explicit anarchist discussion or publishing settings. These are proposed study domains; no claim is made that the present review has sampled them representatively.

Within each chosen stratum, construct an eligible-collective roster using declared inclusion dates and criteria. Include non-adopters, organizations without apparent anarchist ties, and collectives whose records show rejection. Starting only with texts that cite anarchists would make P1 unanswerable. Starting only with nihilist reading groups would make P3 equally unanswerable.

Indigenous political settings require an appropriate governance relationship before inclusion in a new empirical dataset. They are not a convenient comparison stratum that can be added solely to improve representativeness. The published scholarship discussed here can inform the critique of categories without authorizing collection about contemporary communities.

### 9.2 Units, periods, and denominator

Use a collective-period as the main unit when studying organizational learning. Keep the source document, the borrowing element, and the alleged reference relation as separate linked records. An author's individual statement is not automatically a collective's position. Reprints of one text are not independent observations of endorsement. A change of organizational name should not create a new independent actor without a documented reason.

For prevalence, report the number of sufficiently observed eligible recipients and the number with evidence meeting the definition. Report incompletely observed recipients separately. For source share, account for the number and accessibility of candidate sources. A larger source category will tend to receive more aggregate references even without stronger per-source preference.

Two denominators answer different questions: the proportion of recipients who consult a tradition at least once, and the proportion of all observed consultation allocated to that tradition. A rare but decisive encounter can matter differently from frequent routine citation. Neither measure should silently replace the other.

### 9.3 Evidence acquisition

The initial corpus should consist of public, non-sensitive texts, published organizational histories, public manuals, and ethically reusable archival materials. Record each item's original date, edition, archive date, access date, language, authorship claim, and location. Where editions differ, retain the difference rather than choosing the version that best fits the conjecture.

A later interview or survey phase would require an ethics review appropriate to its setting, consent procedures, a withdrawal policy where feasible, and a clear explanation of data reuse. This paper does not authorize contacting people, scraping private groups, or linking pseudonyms to identities. It also does not treat public availability as sufficient justification for building a searchable relational map of contemporary activists.

### 9.4 Coding and adjudication

Appendix C specifies the minimum record. Train coders on an exploratory set that is excluded from the confirmatory evaluation where feasible. Use at least two independent coders for an assessed subset; record disagreements before adjudication. Assess agreement for the actual variable types, report uncertainty and prevalence effects, and do not present a single threshold as a universal certificate of validity.

Coders should distinguish what a source explicitly states from what they infer. An inferred borrowing claim needs an explanation and a stated confidence basis. Disagreements over ideological identity or the meaning of nihilism should remain available for sensitivity analysis. The object is not to make dissent disappear into one authoritative code.

A language model may assist retrieval or suggest candidate passages, but its classification is not independent human corroboration. Human review must be able to reject its framing. Machine-generated confidence values are not probabilities of historical truth unless separately calibrated for the task.

### 9.5 Analysis sequence

Begin with descriptive provenance: which relations can actually be demonstrated, and which remain unresolved? Next compare the distribution of edge types and source categories within each declared population. Then test the proposed layer differences and local gradients against simpler alternative models. Only afterward consider stronger causal claims where the design supports them.

Use held-out evaluation or another appropriate complexity control for predictive comparisons. Account for collective, source, artifact, and temporal dependencies rather than treating every citation as independent. Plan precision and sample size from a pilot and justified simulations or design calculations; this manuscript supplies no invented power calculation or guaranteed adequate sample size.

Preregister confirmatory hypotheses, exclusions, outcome definitions, and analysis choices after exploratory design work and before examining the confirmatory data. The protocol in this paper is not itself a preregistration. Any future departure should be documented with the reason and timing.

### 9.6 Negative cases and stopping rules

A fieldwork or archival phase should stop claiming theoretical saturation when access, language, or funding merely prevents further search. Report the actual stopping reason. Actively seek cases of non-reference, rejection, alternative genealogy, failed uptake, and reversal. These are necessary to estimate boundaries, not decorative objections added after a preferred narrative is complete.

A useful interim result could be that the broad claim is not measurable from the accessible public corpus. That would direct the next design decision. It would not justify silently treating missing records as confirmation of hidden influence or as proof of no influence.

## 10. Ethics, accountability, and field-facing requirements

### 10.1 Do not turn scholarship into a targeting directory

The proposed research concerns learning relationships, not identifying people for surveillance. Publication should avoid fine-grained contemporary maps that expose private affiliations, meeting locations, vulnerabilities, or operational dependencies. Preserve pseudonyms chosen by authors and do not resolve them to legal identities. Prefer aggregate reporting and historically appropriate evidence where individual exposure is unnecessary.

AoIR's announcement of its Internet Research Ethics 3.0 guidance emphasizes ethical attention across the research lifecycle [S19]. A future empirical phase must consult and apply the full guidance; the announcement alone is not a completed ethics assessment. The CARE principles place collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics at the center of Indigenous data governance [S18]. A citation to CARE does not substitute for the governance relationships its application would require.

### 10.2 Positionality and the originating conjecture

The question was deliberately framed around anarchist influence. That creates a risk of favorable source selection and interpretive overreach. This manuscript responds by distinguishing evidence types, including internal criticism and independent genealogies, and stating falsifiers. Those measures improve inspectability but do not make the analysis neutral or independently validated.

The research should remain open to criticism from the people whose traditions it describes, including criticism of its categories. No person or community is represented as endorsing the proposed model. The paper does not infer the originating questioner's political affiliation from the question.

### 10.3 Standards crosswalk

The relevant fields impose overlapping but nonidentical requirements. Intellectual history asks for dated, edition-sensitive interpretation; movement sociology asks for mechanisms, comparison, and population boundaries; political theory asks for defensible distinctions and non-circular argument; network research asks for valid edge definitions and dependence-aware inference; qualitative research asks for transparent interpretive procedures; and research ethics asks what collection and publication make possible for others.

Levitt and colleagues' reporting standards explicitly address methodological integrity across qualitative and mixed-method traditions [S16]. Rethlefsen and colleagues' PRISMA-S provides a framework for reporting literature searches in systematic reviews [S17]. This manuscript borrows the demand for explicit reporting without claiming to be a JARS-compliant primary qualitative study or a PRISMA-compliant systematic review.

| Requirement | Delivered in this manuscript | Not established by this manuscript |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit research problem | P1–P5 and a conditional alternative | Population prevalence |
| Primary-source accountability | Source IDs, locators, access limits, publication distinctions | Exhaustive historical coverage |
| Contrary evidence | Plural genealogies, internal disagreement, competing mechanisms | Representative balance of every tradition |
| Conceptual validity | Separate consultation, adoption, authority, and attention | Validated measurement scales |
| Mathematical clarity | Defined indices, edge types, countermodels, candidate model | Estimated parameters or novel empirical laws |
| Qualitative integrity | Prospective coding and disagreement procedure | Completed independent coding |
| Search transparency | Executed-query log and purposive selection disclosure | Reproducible comprehensive search export |
| Causal restraint | Confounding and endpoint distinctions | Causal identification |
| Ethical stewardship | Data-minimization and governance requirements | Ethics approval or community consent |
| Scholarly accountability | AI-assistance disclosure and review status | Human authorship approval, peer review, journal acceptance |

The ambition to improve on ordinary work should be judged against these concrete outputs, not a claim to have surpassed whole disciplines. The manuscript's strongest additional discipline is to make the unsupported universal claim, the plausible mechanism, and the prospective test separately inspectable. Whether that improves the literature remains a matter for independent criticism and empirical performance.

## 11. Limitations and the most valuable next observation

This review is English-dominant, internet-access dependent, and purposive. It gives substantial attention to accessible anarchist and related texts while lacking a representative account of other languages, informal learning, oral transmission, or poorly archived organizations. It cannot estimate the global or historical prevalence of the proposed gradient. Publisher abstracts and author overviews support narrower interpretations than independently examined full studies.

The term accommodation is itself contestable. What one actor describes as compromise another may regard as a binding responsibility, an enabling infrastructure, or an entirely different political problem. Local measurement must therefore preserve disagreement rather than encoding one faction's vocabulary as an objective developmental scale.

The proposed distinction between theory and praxis can also become artificial if imposed too rigidly. A meeting procedure may already embody an explanation of authority; reading may itself be a collective practice. The separation here concerns observable modes of engagement, not an ontological claim that thought and action occupy sealed worlds. Coding should allow one event to carry several relations.

The most valuable next observation is not another broadly favorable quotation. It is a well-documented recipient episode with a named problem, a dated encounter, a specific element taken up or rejected, a visible decision process, and a competing explanation that can be checked. Several such episodes, selected independently of outcome and including negative cases, would begin to test the mechanism. A large collection of mentions without those distinctions would not necessarily advance it.

## 12. Conclusion

The claim that virtually everyone looks toward anarchists and anarchists toward nihilists and insurrectionaries contains several distinct research questions. The reviewed material does not justify treating it as a universal law, a single genealogy, or an ordering of political worth. It does justify taking cross-boundary reference seriously and investigating particular anarchist–nihilist and anarchist–insurrectionary relationships without assuming agreement.

The proposed alternative is a conditional radical-reference gradient. Actors may seek questions and examples from sources that refuse an accommodation they retain, while practical uptake follows different demands of interpretation, accountability, feasibility, and care. Those relations can be selective, reciprocal, contested, and multidimensional. They need not culminate in a final position.

The central formulation is therefore: **political actors can learn from a refusal without delegating judgment to the people who articulate it.** Anarchist, nihilist, and insurrectionary texts may participate in that process, but their actual roles must be demonstrated rather than awarded by resemblance. Reference without deference is the proposition offered for criticism and testing—not a completed empirical verdict.
