# Reference Without Deference

## Radical Political Learning, Anarchist Praxis, and the Nihilist–Insurrectionary Horizon

**Lenticule Science · Sociology Science Services Section · Working paper 2.0 · 15 October 2026**

**Status and contribution.** This edition combines a conceptual argument with a completed, bounded analysis of published texts. It is not a completed population study. The question originated with emsenn; research assistance, interpretation, and drafting used ChatGPT. No human author or independent reviewer is represented as having approved the manuscript. The accompanying [documentary register](../../../../../../../../data/records/radical-political-learning-evidence-2026-10-15.json) contains the evidence records, including a negative control, rather than a proposed empty dataset.

## Abstract

Do political actors look toward anarchists for theory and praxis, while anarchists look toward nihilist and insurrectionary interlocutors? This paper treats the proposal as a question about directional learning, not necessarily a hierarchy of ideological development. A critical integrative review is combined with ten source-located records from six purposively selected receiving documents. The analysis identifies anarchist-oriented synthesis of nihilism, retrospective reports of changed understanding, attributed argumentative reuse, selective recommendation within disagreement, and an explicit declaration of non-reading. These observations establish particular textual relations and reported learning episodes, but not population prevalence or independently verified changes in practice. We propose problem-relative radical reference: an actor confronting an impasse may seek resources from sources willing to question a commitment that the actor still retains. Such encounters can transmit criteria for judging action, not only methods of action. Reference, stance, learning, adoption, and deference must consequently be measured separately. A revised relational model permits critical learning, overlapping affiliations, mediated transmission, and plural sources without assuming transitive endorsement. Elementary measurement results clarify non-identification from mention counts and bounds under incomplete observation. A prospective protocol distinguishes descriptive, predictive, and experimental questions, incorporates non-adopters, and makes source prestige compete with argument content as an explanation. The contribution is a more testable account of learning across differences in refusal, with concrete documentary support and explicit limits on causal and universal claims.

## 1. Recovering the question without weakening it or overstating it

The originating proposition describes a spectrum of looking to others for theory and praxis: many political actors look toward anarchists, while anarchists themselves look toward nihilists and insurrectionaries. A productive interpretation asks who supplies questions, practices, and evaluative standards that another political setting does not readily generate for itself. This can concern a disproportionate contribution to shared repertoires even when recipients neither identify with nor explicitly cite the sources.

A spectrum does not necessarily mean a ladder. The first edition placed too much argumentative weight on refuting a transitive, one-dimensional hierarchy, which the originating question did not require. This edition treats that hierarchy as one optional strong model. It preserves the more important possibilities: directional asymmetry, recurrent consultation across differences in accommodation, and indirect dependence on circulating practices.

Five claims should be distinguished. **P1, reach:** a specified proportion of political recipients consult anarchist sources. **P2, directional selection:** reference is disproportionately directed toward sources that question additional retained commitments. **P3, recursive reference:** analogous relations connect anarchist recipients with nihilist or insurrectionary interlocutors. **P4, consequential uptake:** encounters change descriptions, evaluative standards, or practices. **P5, ordering:** the relations admit a useful common spectrum. The strongest version of P5 makes that spectrum scalar and transitive, but neither P2 nor P3 requires it.

P1 itself has two forms. Direct consultation concerns an identifiable encounter. Mediated dependence concerns a practice or argument transmitted through other people, organizations, or artifacts. A recipient can depend on a transmitted element without knowing its earlier source. Conversely, recognizing a famous author need not involve using that author's work. A study of direct reading cannot settle the full infrastructural-dependence claim; a speculative genealogy cannot substitute for evidence of an actual pathway.

This paper finds concrete support for some instances of P3 and for textual or self-reported forms of P4. Existing scholarship supplies field-specific support for investigating P1. P2 and the comparative strength of P5 remain hypotheses. No result here estimates how frequently these relations occur among political actors generally.

## 2. Method and source custody

### 2.1 What was done

The second revision round used general web search, direct source retrieval, close inspection of relevant published passages, and comparison with the existing manuscript. Appendix A reports principal executed queries and retrieval limitations. Appendix B supplies bibliographic identities, access levels, and locators. The documentary register records ten observations from six receiving documents. Several observations occur in one text and are not independent cases.

Selection was purposive and partly followed references. It sought positive reception, disagreement, alternative intellectual lineages, and a discriminator between mention and use. It was not a systematic search of all anarchist publishing, a random sample, or a census of a defined political field. Search-result rankings, publication availability, and language all affected discovery. No reproducible denominator of all eligible publications or recipients was assembled. Counts of records therefore describe this analysis alone.

The documentary outcome is deliberately modest but real: the inspected text performs an attributed use, states an influence, or denies a particular reading. Whether an autobiographical account accurately reconstructs an earlier encounter is a further question. Whether it caused a later practice is another. Neither is settled by observing the publication.

## 3. Concepts: what can travel across a political difference?

### 3.1 Reference, learning, and adoption

**Reference** is an observable or reported engagement with an identifiable source concerning a particular question or element. **Theoretical consultation** concerns explanation, diagnosis, justification, or evaluation. **Practical consultation** concerns how to perform or organize an activity. **Textual uptake** occurs when a receiving argument actually uses or transforms an identifiable element. **Reported learning** is an attributed account of changed understanding. **Enacted adoption** requires evidence of changed practice, not merely a quotation or declared intention.

These definitions allow a text to matter without producing ideological conversion. A recipient might borrow a method, reject its justification, and draw its normative commitments from elsewhere. Another might borrow a criterion for judging success while retaining familiar methods. The latter is especially important to the present question: learning can alter what counts as a worthwhile activity even when the visible repertoire initially remains the same.

**Deference** concerns the allocation of judgment: accepting a conclusion because of a source's attributed authority. It is not identical to attending to that source's reasons. Reference without deference means that a recipient can use a contribution while retaining the ability to dispute, modify, or reject it. This is a possible relationship, not an assertion that all anti-authoritarian settings avoid informal authority.

### 3.2 Disagreement is not the opposite of learning

The first edition risked coding criticism outside the learning relation. That is too restrictive. A critique can supply a problem, reveal an assumption, or make a competing explanation more precise. Conversely, a favorable citation can be ornamental. **Stance toward a source and learning from it are separate variables.** A critical response counts as engagement; evidence of a changed argument or explicit reported revision is needed to claim more than engagement.

The revised scheme therefore separates mode, stance, evidence basis, outcome, and authority. A passage may be theoretical in mode, mixed in stance, demonstrably reuse an argument, and leave deference unknown. Hostile monitoring, algorithmic exposure, and mere name recognition remain separate from demonstrated learning. Negative sentiment alone proves neither learning nor its absence.

This separation also protects speakers within composite texts. A reply can quote an opponent at length. Words inside that quotation belong to the quoted voice unless the reply adopts them. Corpus analysis that assigns all words on a page to its named author can reverse the very disagreement it is supposed to measure.

### 3.3 Anarchism, nihilism, and insurrection overlap without becoming synonyms

The inspected texts place disputes about nihilism and insurrection within and across anarchist discourse [S05–S07, S21–S24]. They do not establish three successive membership bins. A research record must distinguish public self-positioning, publisher framing, and analyst classification. It must also permit multiple labels, refusals of labels, and changes over time.

Nihilism can name substantially different philosophical, historical, and political positions. This paper studies specified uses in contemporary political argument; it does not identify them with apathy, psychological despair, Russian historical nihilism, or every rejection of a positive program. Insurrectionary anarchism is not defined simply as an absence of organization. Bonanno's collection concerns forms of self-organization; its introduction also records a translation problem concerning the term informal [S06, Introduction]. That edition-sensitive point prevents labels from being mistaken for invariant organizational facts.

Three propositions about political futurity require separate codes: eventual victory is not guaranteed; eventual victory is impossible; and action need not derive its entire value from eventual victory. These propositions are not equivalent. A person can accept uncertainty while maintaining hope, or reject a guaranteed outcome while pursuing durable constructive work. The source analysis below makes this distinction necessary, not optional.

### 3.4 Accommodation is problem-relative

An **accommodation** here is an arrangement, dependency, or commitment that a recipient currently treats as a constraint on its political problem. Candidate dimensions include institutional authorization, market dependence, delegated decision-making, organizational permanence, and the role of a projected future in justifying present action. They do not constitute a validated scale.

What one participant calls compromise, another may call responsibility, infrastructure, or a condition of participation. Accordingly, a difference must specify the issue, observer, and period. No moral superiority is assigned to rejecting more constraints. Violence is not a measure of theoretical depth or emancipatory value. An additional refusal can be illuminating, irrelevant, mistaken, or harmful; the theory must allow all four possibilities.

## 4. What existing research already explains

### 4.1 Anarchism as a reference point, with an empirical boundary

Graeber's participant account argues for anarchism's importance in the global-justice movement, especially in political organization [S01]. Gordon makes an even closer antecedent to the originating conjecture: his 2007 article presents anarchism as a major point of reference in the radical movement networks he studies. It draws on participant research, while interpreting contemporary anarchism through convergences among several movements rather than a one-way export from a self-contained tradition [S21, Abstract and Introduction].

This is stronger support than the first edition's invitation-only treatment suggested. It is also a boundary: an interpretive claim grounded in particular networks is not an estimate for every political actor. Gordon's account further makes present-focused experimentation and open-endedness features of anarchism itself. They cannot be assigned exclusively to nihilist interlocutors as if anarchists acquire them only at a later intellectual stage.

### 4.2 Prefiguration already includes learning and strategy

Maeckelbergh explicitly argues that prefiguration can itself be strategic when movement goals are multiple and not predetermined [S26, Abstract]. Yates distinguishes experimentation, circulation of perspectives, formation of norms and conduct, material consolidation, and diffusion [S27, Introduction and Conclusion]. Gordon's later work distinguishes ethical means–ends relations from temporal promises that can be attached to them [S04, Abstract]. Juris supplies a related ethnographic orientation toward networked activist organization [S03, author overview].

The proposed theory therefore cannot claim novelty merely for saying that movements experiment, learn from practice, or connect means and ends. Its narrower question is whether a **recipient's unresolved problem and retained commitments help explain source selection and the kind of learning that follows**. That mechanism must improve on explanations based on access, affinity, expertise, prestige, and brokerage; a new name alone adds nothing.

### 4.3 Plural lineages are causal alternatives, not ceremonial acknowledgments

Polletta analyzes the changing social associations of participatory democracy, including SNCC and SDS, and the significance of Black organizing, Quaker influence, and labor-educational traditions [S02]. The analysis cautions against explaining organizational choices through either effectiveness or ideology alone. A practice's social meaning can affect who adopts it and whose contribution is recognized.

Simpson's account of Nishnaabeg learning is not merely another point on an anarchist spectrum. Her discussion of consensual engagement and embodied, land-related knowledge directly challenges a model confined to citations among human theorists. Her discussion of learning across intellectual traditions also refuses an exclusive genealogy [S09, pp. 15–16]. We use these arguments to limit the model, not to annex Nishnaabeg political life to an external ideology.

Thus, resemblance cannot establish ancestry. A practice may be independently developed, multiply inherited, or transformed through a carrier that did not originate it. Anarchist importance does not require exclusive ownership of cooperation, direct action, or participatory organization. Nor does refusing exclusive ownership establish that anarchist transmission is absent.

### 4.4 Diffusion and radical-flank effects answer different questions

McAdam and Rucht distinguish relational and nonrelational transmission; Meyer and Whittier examine movement spillover; Zhou and Yang study cross-movement brokerage [S10, S11, S20]. These are antecedents, not phenomena newly discovered here. Zhou and Yang's stated limitation that their network study did not analyze tweet content is particularly relevant: a structural connection need not reveal an argumentative stance [S20, Limitations].

Experiments on radical-flank effects concern changes in support for factions, not necessarily learning by one faction from another [S13, S15]. The correction to the Simpson, Willer, and Feinberg paper supplies an omitted informed-consent statement, not replacement effect estimates [S14]. A faction can benefit from comparison with another while adopting none of its ideas. Conversely, a recipient can learn without gaining public support. These outcomes cannot substitute for one another.

## 5. Documentary findings: reception, recombination, and a negative case

### 5.1 An anarchist author already doing the interpretive work

Aragorn!'s introduction does more than invite anarchists to consider nihilism: it explicitly locates the writer's own evaluation in an anarchist orientation. E01 therefore records an observable anarchist-oriented synthesis as well as an address to readers [S05, Introduction]. This corrects the first edition's narrower claim. It still does not tell us how many readers learned from the pamphlet.

### 5.2 A reported encounter with more than one source

Serafinski attributes the book's initial juxtaposition to reading Bæden alongside a Warsaw Ghetto memoir; a separate passage explicitly uses Aragorn!'s account [S22, Collision and Anarcho-nihilism; E02–E03]. These are positive evidence of a reported intellectual encounter and textual uptake, not merely thematic similarity. The episode is already plural-source. The memoir is not independently identified from that passage, and this analysis does not validate the book's historical analogies or classify historical victims through its later interpretation.

### 5.3 Changed understanding can coexist with continuing disagreement

Flower Bomb credits Zerzan, reports a change of understanding associated with nihilist individualist anarchists, and uses an attributed passage from Serafinski in a reply to Zerzan [S24; E04–E06]. These are distinct relations within one receiving document, not three independent people or replications. The account supports reported conceptual change and argumentative reuse; no subsequent practice is independently verified.

Zerzan, meanwhile, selectively recommends Stirner within a critical argument [S23; E08]. The paired texts make stance separation essential. They also make it incorrect to portray the dispute simply as guaranteed victory against refusal of guarantees: Zerzan explicitly denies a guarantee while defending possibility. Stirner is not automatically classified as a nihilist merely to make the example fit.

### 5.4 The negative control changes what a network would contain

The same reply explicitly says its author has not read Desert [S24; E07]. A page-level co-occurrence graph would connect the writer to that book, but a direct-reading graph must not assert that connection at the time of the statement. The writer may still have secondhand exposure through the quoted criticism. The correct result is therefore not no relation of any kind, but a typed distinction between mention, mediated exposure, and direct reading.

This is a useful completed discriminator. It shows how a superficially plausible method would manufacture evidence for the hypothesis. It does not require discovering a private identity or measuring an unobservable mental state.

### 5.5 Critical engagement and later recontextualization

The WSM article quotes and contests insurrectionalist arguments while granting some points; its notes identify Bonanno works including The Anarchist Tension and Armed Joy [S07; E09]. They must not be silently replaced with The Insurrectional Project, the different work independently inspected here. Critical engagement is established, but a resulting organizational change is not.

A contributor published by CrimethInc. in 2024 explicitly reuses Serafinski in another political interpretation [S25; E10]. That is evidence of reception beyond the original book, not proof of agreement by every contributor, audience uptake, or the truth of the analogy. The present analysis neither adopts the essay's wider historical claims nor endorses violence against civilians.

### 5.6 What these findings establish

The register contains ten observations from six receiving documents. It establishes that the proposed anarchist–nihilist relationship is not limited to invitations addressed to hypothetical readers. It includes authorial synthesis, reported learning, and actual argumentative use. It also contains mixed stance, multiple sources, and an explicit negative control.

None of the records independently establishes enactment of a specific practice. Selection followed references and deliberately sought discriminating examples, so the register cannot estimate a directional gradient or population share. The appropriate result is **documented existence and differentiated form**, not measured predominance. The counterexamples modify the measurement model; they do not erase the positive evidence.

## 6. A mechanism: learning across retained commitments

### 6.1 The problem-relative radical-reference hypothesis

A recipient may confront an impasse while continuing to treat some arrangement as non-negotiable. A source willing to question that arrangement can supply an alternative description, possibility, or evaluative criterion. When the contribution remains intelligible and relevant, the difference can make the source useful even without agreement on a complete political program.

This is the proposed mechanism, not a demonstrated population law. It has four distinguishable components: a problem the recipient cannot resolve through its usual repertoire; a retained commitment relevant to that problem; an encounter that puts that commitment into question; and an observable or reported revision. A text that calls for an encounter supplies only one component. A retrospective report can supply several, but still leaves competing explanations and chronology to be examined.

The empirical signature should be specific. After an encounter, the recipient should be able to name an assumption, alternative, or criterion that it previously excluded or could not formulate. A declaration that the source is more radical does not suffice. Neither does adopting a fashionable label. The mechanism concerns the work an argument performs on a particular problem.

### 6.2 What is borrowed may be a criterion, not a tactic

A political actor can change its assessment of an activity without learning a new way to perform it. For example, in an explicitly hypothetical case, a collective might shift from asking whether a care arrangement hastens an eventual transformation to asking also whether it makes present relationships less dominating. The practice could initially remain unchanged while its justification, selection, and vulnerability to abandonment change.

Such a revision can be studied without assuming the new criterion is better. It may disclose neglected value, rationalize failure, or do both. Evidence about reduced harm, participation, durability, or other claimed outcomes must be collected separately. A criterion's emotional attractiveness is not a scientific validation of its effects.

This makes the nihilist question analytically interesting. A challenge to the requirement of eventual success can affect how recipients evaluate present action. But uncertainty about eventual success, rejection of progress narratives, and the claim that consequences do not matter are distinct positions. This paper does not infer the third from either of the first two.

### 6.3 Sources need not be exclusive owners of the questions they raise

The mechanism does not require anarchists to own permission-independent action or nihilists to own criticism of guarantees. A source can become important by concentrating, articulating, demonstrating, or circulating a problem that others also recognize. The relevant difference is between what is available to a particular recipient at a particular time, not between cultures with and without the capacity to think.

A possible division of intellectual labor follows. Some settings may sustain challenges that another setting filters out; others may contribute the practical means to preserve a useful insight. Yet theory, maintenance, care, translation, and confrontation can all occur within the same collective. The paper does not assign inventive theory to radicals and practical competence to moderates. Such an assignment would reproduce the very evaluative hierarchy the study should investigate.

### 6.4 Refusal has no automatic positive sign

Relaxing a constraint can enlarge a search space, but a larger search space can contain worse answers. A retained constraint can protect consent, accessibility, or other valued relations. The proposition is therefore about source selection and revision, not a recommendation to discard constraints indiscriminately. Additional refusal can reduce credibility or relevance, and some problems are better answered by a source with more accommodation or entirely different expertise.

Possible reversals are part of the proposed boundary conditions. A recipient may seek a severe diagnosis and then learn implementation from a source that rejects that diagnosis. An actor may return to an earlier source after an unsuccessful experiment. These possibilities must not all be relabeled forward movement on an invisible ladder. A theory that counts every possible direction as confirmation has ceased to discriminate.

### 6.5 Reference without deference is an empirical question about authority too

The title names a possibility, not a compliment automatically bestowed on anarchist discourse. A highly cited source may have little decision authority. Another may become an informal arbiter despite few explicit references. Evidence of the latter would require observing how challenges are handled and whether attributed status substitutes for reasons.

Freeman's argument about informal structure is relevant to that separate inquiry [S08]. A practice of rejecting formal authority does not by itself settle who controls information, access, or judgment. Equally, disagreeing with a source does not establish that the source has ceased to matter. Reference, influence, and authority must be investigated through different evidence.

## 7. Relational specification and measurement results

### 7.1 Objects, roles, and direction

Let I be receiving units, J source units, P identifiable arguments or practices, and T observation intervals. A unit may be a public document, a collective-period, or a consented participant. These levels must not be collapsed: a statement in one contributor's essay is not automatically a collective position.

Write R(i,j,p,t) for a reference episode. The arrow i → j means recipient i looks to source j. A dissemination diagram may reverse the arrow to show source-to-recipient transfer, but must declare the change. Membership labels are time-indexed attributes with provenance, not exclusive node types.

An episode has separately coded fields:

- **Mode:** theoretical consultation, practical consultation, or both.
- **Stance:** affirmative, critical, mixed, or unresolved.
- **Evidence basis:** observed textual performance, retrospective report, contemporaneous record, or independently corroborated behavior.
- **Outcome:** exposure, engagement, textual use, reported conceptual revision, enacted adoption, or unknown.
- **Authority relation:** explicit deference, explicit reservation of judgment, or unknown.

These are not an obligatory staircase. A person can use a practice without reading its source. A report of learning and a verified textual quotation are different evidence kinds, not universally ordered quality scores. Multiple outcomes can occur together.

For continuity with version 1.0, T and Q denote theoretical and practical consultation; A denotes adoption; N denotes normative orientation; D denotes deference; K denotes critical stance; and H denotes non-learning exposure or monitoring. They are retained as labels for separate fields or derived views, not mutually exclusive values of one variable. A K-coded episode can also contain theoretical uptake. An H observation cannot be promoted to learning without additional evidence.

### 7.2 Direct reference and mediated lineage

Direct engagement should be distinguished from a transmission path j → k → i. To claim mediated uptake of element p, investigators need evidence about p at the relevant links, including transformations. A chain involving unrelated elements proves no lineage for either one. A matching practice with no known pathway remains an unresolved genealogy, not automatic borrowing.

Transformation is itself an outcome. A broker may remove a political label, change a justification, combine several elements, or preserve a method while rejecting a goal. Attribution loss requires positive transmission evidence; lack of credit alone cannot establish appropriation. Likewise, a broker's visibility does not establish origination. Origination, translation, custody, instruction, and adoption should have distinct provenance fields.

### 7.3 Measurement result 1: mention counts underdetermine learning

**Claim.** Counts of source-name references cannot identify affirmative consultation, learning, or deference without additional semantic evidence or assumptions.

**Argument.** Two texts can contain identical counts while one recommends a source and the other rejects it. A third can quote both positions without adopting either. The counted observations are the same while the target relations differ. Therefore the count-to-relation map is not one-to-one. E07 supplies a concrete direct-reading discriminator: a mention occurs alongside an explicit denial of reading.

The result does not say counts are useless. They can locate candidate passages or measure a defined kind of visibility. The invalid step is converting that visibility into a different relation without evidence. Sentiment alone also fails to resolve the problem because criticism and learning can coexist.

### 7.4 Measurement result 2: paths do not entail endorsement

**Claim.** R(i,j,p,t) and R(j,k,q,t') do not entail direct engagement of i with k, or i's endorsement of k.

**Argument.** Let i consult j about a meeting practice p, while j consults k about a criterion q for historical judgment. The two edges can exist while i has no relation to k. Even when p = q and a temporally valid transmission is established, i may use a modified element without knowing or endorsing its earlier source. Reachability is not direct consultation or deference.

This restricts an inference, not the original intuition. Mediated practical influence can exist without transitive endorsement. A responsible test should seek that influence rather than reject it because the recipients do not share identities.

### 7.5 Measurement result 3: commitments can be incomparable

Represent selected commitments by a vector x_i(t). A source rejecting state authorization while retaining durable organization and another taking the opposite positions need not dominate one another componentwise. A scalar score requires a weighting or projection. Its predictive usefulness is testable, but the weighting is not supplied by the labels themselves.

A useful local spectrum can still emerge. The study should compare it with multidimensional alternatives rather than prohibit it in advance. More dimensions do not guarantee a better model, and improved in-sample fit alone does not establish improvement.

### 7.6 Measurement result 4: missing observations imply bounds, not zeros

Suppose a completely enumerated target roster contains N units. Let k have correctly established positive reference, n have correctly established non-reference under the specified observation procedure, and u remain unresolved, with N = k + n + u. Assume the positive and negative classifications are error-free for the target relation and interval. Then the prevalence pi satisfies:

**k/N ≤ pi ≤ (k + u)/N.**

**Derivation.** The total number of positive units is k + x, where the unresolved positives x can lie anywhere from 0 to u. Division by N gives the bounds. These are identification bounds, not confidence intervals or estimates of this study's prevalence. Classification errors, an incomplete roster, or a different relation invalidate the stated assumptions and require a different analysis.

The documentary register does not meet these premises: its six receiving documents are a selected set, not a complete target roster. The formula therefore cannot convert its ten records into a population result. The practical lesson is to disclose unknowns and the denominator needed to resolve the broad claim.

### 7.7 Candidate estimands and models

Three future estimands should not be interchanged: the share of eligible recipients consulting a source tradition at least once; the share of observed consultation allocated to that tradition; and the share of adopted elements with a supported direct or mediated lineage. A rare, consequential encounter can affect these differently. Multi-source episodes also require declared counting rules; fractional attribution is a convention, not a discovered causal share.

For an adequately observed binary episode outcome Y, a candidate descriptive model is:

**logit Pr(Y(i,j,p,t) = 1 | X) = alpha + a_i + b_j + h_p + tau_t + f(d_ijm) + gamma Z_ijpt.**

Here d_ijm is a recipient-relative difference concerning accommodation m, coded independently of Y; Z contains justified measures of prior access, language, familiarity, relevance, and affiliation. Effects a, b, h, and tau represent recipient, source, element, and time heterogeneity. No parameters are fitted in this paper. A future analysis must account for shared documents, repeated recipients, and network dependence; independent-dyad errors are not adequate by default.

The comparison of interest is between theoretical engagement, criterion revision, and practical adoption, each observed on its own terms. Compare models with and without the accommodation term on held-out units or periods, using the same defined choice opportunities. Do not measure source accessibility only after an encounter and then treat it as a pre-encounter control.

Shalizi and Thomas show why observational similarity, selection, and influence can remain confounded [S12]. This candidate model is not a causal identification strategy. Neither directional coefficients nor additional covariates remove that problem by declaration.

## 8. Competing explanations

**Shared antecedents or independent invention.** Similar practices can arise without direct borrowing. Temporal priority and resemblance are not sufficient. The relevant evidence includes actual availability, encounter, distinctive elements, local experimentation, and alternative sources.

**Selection and retrospective reconstruction.** Recipients may choose sources because they already agree. Autobiographical accounts may compress a gradual change into one memorable encounter. Earlier records and alternative explanations are needed before attributing a change to the named source.

**Task-specific expertise or brokerage.** A source can be useful because it has practical knowledge, offers an accessible translation, or connects people. The radical-reference mechanism contributes only if it explains something beyond those features. A popular carrier is not necessarily an originator.

**Prestige and identity signaling.** A source label may attract reference independently of an argument's content. Conversely, stigma can discourage explicit acknowledgment while a practice circulates. Both are hypotheses requiring evidence, not permission to infer hidden borrowing whenever citations are absent.

**Archive and platform selection.** Text-producing and well-preserved settings are easier to observe. A public corpus can overrepresent theoretical debate relative to tacit practice, making an apparent theory–praxis difference a measurement artifact. Failed, deleted, oral, inaccessible, or withheld material must not silently become negative observations.

**External contrast rather than learning.** Third-party support, media coverage, repression, fundraising, or policy response may change without recipient learning. Those outcomes belong in a separate causal account, not as substitutes for the proposed dependent variables.

## 9. Hypotheses with ways to lose

**H1: Problem-specific source selection.** Among sources available before an encounter, a contribution that questions a recipient's relevant retained commitment predicts engagement beyond familiarity, access, expertise, and shared affiliation. A null incremental association or one explained entirely by those alternatives challenges H1. Merely labeling the source radical does not support it.

**H2: Distinct learning outcomes.** Retained-commitment differences relate differently to theoretical engagement, criterion revision, and enacted adoption. Adequately observed equivalent patterns challenge the proposed differentiation. A theory-only public archive cannot test this hypothesis by itself.

**H3: Recursive reference in specified anarchist settings.** Nihilist or insurrectionary arguments supply identifiable contributions to some anarchist recipients' revisions. The present documentary cases support existence in a narrow setting. Any stronger claim of predominance must compete against other sources and include non-recipients, rejections, and overlap in source categories.

**H4: Selective transformation.** Supported cross-field transfers more often preserve some elements while modifying others than entail wholesale program adoption. A corpus showing predominantly unchanged adoption with broad deference would challenge this proposal. Several references in one essay are not several independent transfers.

**H5: Task-dependent reversal.** Source choices change when a recipient moves between problem diagnosis, evaluation, and sustained provision. Stable choices across genuinely contrasting, comparably observed tasks weaken that explanation. Post hoc redefinition of the task is not an admissible rescue.

**H6: Added value of the relational model.** A stance-separated, problem-relative model improves held-out explanation or prediction over a single spectrum and over ordinary access/affinity models. Failure after complexity control is a negative result. A richer vocabulary is not evidence of superiority.

The universal wording of the originating question remains a separate empirical target. A future confirmatory study must specify population, relation, interval, and what numerical threshold it will regard as nearly everyone. No threshold or prevalence estimate is assigned by this paper.

## 10. A prospective study that can distinguish the mechanisms

### 10.1 Sampling and units

Construct a bounded recipient roster through activities and settings rather than known anarchist references. Candidate domains include labor organizing, neighborhood provision, environmental campaigns, participatory-governance projects, and political publishing. Include recipients without apparent anarchist ties, people or collectives that reject the cited arguments, and sources drawn from other traditions. A sample beginning with citations to nihilists cannot estimate nihilist influence among all anarchists.

Use a collective-period for organizational claims and an author-document for textual claims. Keep the levels linked but separate. A writer's retrospective account is not a whole organization's position. Reprints and translations remain versions of a source unless a receiving intervention is demonstrated. Do not identify distinct pseudonyms as one person without appropriate public evidence, or infer private identities at all.

Indigenous settings require an appropriate governance relationship before a new empirical study includes them. Published Indigenous scholarship can criticize the analytic categories without functioning as consent to build a contemporary community dataset.

### 10.2 Evidence acquisition and codebook

The next documentary phase should collect public, ethically reusable arguments, manuals, histories, and records that predate and follow candidate encounters. Record original publication, edition, translation, archive, and access dates separately. Preserve source names as published. Compare any claim of borrowing with evidence of the recipient's earlier practice and alternative sources.

A minimum episode record contains: stable record ID; unit and interval; recipient document; source or explicitly unresolved source category; particular element; mode; stance; evidence basis; observed outcome; authority relation; exact locator; date bounds; opportunity for encounter; alternative explanations; attribution status; independent codes and disagreements; and ethical release status. Unknown, unobserved, and explicitly negative are separate values. The companion register implements the subset warranted by the current published texts; it does not pretend to populate unavailable participant attributes.

Direct quotations need speaker attribution, including embedded quotations in replies. Distinguish a recipient quoting an intermediary from directly reading an earlier source. Where original editions were not collated, record that limit. The E07 non-reading declaration is a mandatory negative-control case for any later automated extractor.

### 10.3 Independent interpretation and falsification

Train coders on an exploratory set and assess an independently coded subset before adjudication. Report disagreements, variable-appropriate reliability information, and sensitivity to alternative classifications. A universal agreement threshold cannot guarantee historical validity. A model's second pass is not an independent human coder, and a machine confidence score is not a calibrated probability of historical truth.

The confirmatory phase should freeze hypotheses, definitions, exclusions, intended comparisons, and analysis choices before examining its outcomes. This paper is a prospective protocol, not a preregistration. Pilot cases used to develop the coding rules should not be silently reused as an untouched confirmation set.

For descriptive analyses, report denominators and incomplete observation. For prediction, hold out documents, recipients, or periods in a manner that prevents the same quoted passage from entering both training and test sets. Treat the many observations inside one document as dependent. Determine sample size through a design-specific precision or power analysis after a pilot; no adequate N has been calculated here.

### 10.4 A low-risk content-versus-prestige experiment

A complementary experiment could test the proposed mechanism using consented participants and expressly fictional organizational scenarios. It would not assign real political authors fabricated statements or seek to change participants' electoral preferences.

One randomized factor would compare an argument that questions a specified organizational constraint with a matched argument that works within it. A second would vary an explicitly fictional source description, such as an autonomous collective versus a formal advisory unit. Length, readability, evidence quality, and task relevance would require pilot matching. Participants would know the scenarios and source descriptions are invented.

Primary outcomes could be recognition of the previously fixed assumption, the quality of newly articulated alternatives under independently stated criteria, and willingness to revise a decision. Source approval, emotional response, agreement, and practical feasibility would be measured separately. Blind assessors should score argument quality; a preference for the less institutional label is not by itself learning.

An argument-content effect on justified revision would be consistent with the proposed mechanism. A source-label effect without improved reasoning would favor prestige or identity signaling. No effect would constrain the mechanism in the tested task. Neither result would establish the historical origins of real movements. Ethics review, sampling, piloting, power analysis, and preregistration remain prospective; this experiment has not been conducted.

### 10.5 Stopping, release, and negative findings

A search that ends because of access, language, time, or resources must report that reason, not claim theoretical saturation. Retain non-adoption, failed uptake, alternative genealogy, and task reversal. If public evidence cannot distinguish the mechanisms, that is a useful measurement result rather than a license to infer invisible influence.

A human-participant phase requires appropriate review, consent, data stewardship, and realistic withdrawal provisions. It is not authorized by the existence of this document. No request to contact authors, buy data, collect private conversations, or run behavioral interventions has been executed.

## 11. Ethics, methodological integrity, and review requirements

### 11.1 Do not make a surveillance product from a learning question

The present register links published arguments, not private individuals or operational groups. It omits private identities, contact details, locations, and descriptions of alleged illegal activity. A later release must consider whether even public records become more dangerous through aggregation. Preserve pseudonyms and contextual limits rather than treating discoverability as consent.

AoIR's guidelines address contextual expectations of privacy, the research lifecycle, and risks to both participants and researchers [S19, pp. 7, 10–13]. CARE places collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics at the center of Indigenous data governance [S18]. These are requirements for a future governed process, not certifications acquired by citing them.

The critical use of politically contentious texts is evidence of scholarly engagement, not endorsement of their prescriptions. Studying a text's reuse does not validate its historical analogies or justify violence against civilians. No source's human subjects or historical victims are assigned the politics of a later interpreter.

### 11.2 Relevant standards and what this edition actually delivers

Intellectual history requires attention to editions, contexts, and the function of an argument. Movement sociology requires bounded populations, alternative mechanisms, and negative cases. Network research requires correct unit and relation definitions. Qualitative reporting requires methodological integrity and inspectable interpretation. These obligations converge here on a simple principle: the conclusion must not outrun the observation.

JARS-Qual supplies reporting guidance across qualitative approaches, while PRISMA-S concerns transparent reporting of systematic-review searches [S16, S17]. The present purposive review does not claim compliance as a systematic review or a completed primary interview study. It adopts applicable transparency practices without borrowing an inappropriate certification label.

This edition delivers a revised conceptual argument, a filled source-located register, explicit contrary and negative evidence, edition/access distinctions, a measurement specification, and testable future comparisons. It does not deliver independent coding, a representative prevalence result, a causal estimate, an executed experiment, ethics approval, a formal proof checked in Lean, or independent peer review. The elementary mathematical arguments are given in prose within their stated assumptions; they are not empirical validation.

### 11.3 Author position and independent receiving

The starting question favors a particular directional interpretation, and the source search deliberately followed that possibility. This creates selection and confirmation risks. The negative control, competing lineages, and mixed-stance cases reduce specific errors but do not make the corpus representative or the analyst independent.

The next scholarly receiver should challenge at least four points: whether the documentary records accurately distinguish quoted voices; whether the mechanism adds to existing diffusion and prefiguration scholarship; whether accommodation can be measured without encoding one faction's self-description as the truth; and whether the proposed empirical design could contradict the theory. A source commit and a structural data check cannot satisfy that review.

## 12. Discussion and conclusion

The second round strengthens the existence claim while changing the explanatory picture. We now have more than texts addressing imagined readers: the inspected record includes explicit synthesis, attributed reuse, and reports of changed understanding. At the same time, the receivers do not fall neatly into successive ideological stations. Their relations include disagreement, several sources in one account, and mediated discussion without direct reading.

The strongest researchable interpretation is therefore **problem-relative reference across differences in retained commitments**. Sources may matter because they help a recipient formulate what its current arrangements make difficult to ask. What travels can be a practice, a diagnosis, a justification, or a criterion for judging action. Those transfers can occur without deference, without conversion, and without exclusive ownership of the underlying ideas.

This account remains exposed to failure. Access, affinity, prestige, and task-specific expertise may explain source selection better. Apparent theory–praxis differences may disappear under comparable observation. The selected textual encounters may be unrepresentative. A single local spectrum may sometimes work better than a multidimensional model. These outcomes would revise the proposal rather than being renamed its confirmation.

The answer to the originating question is consequently stronger and more precise than in version 1.0: **particular anarchist engagements with nihilist and insurrectionary arguments are documented, including reported learning and textual uptake; their prevalence, direction relative to alternatives, and practical effects remain to be measured.** The broader suggestion that anarchist contributions circulate far beyond explicit anarchist identity remains an important mediated-transmission question, not something a reading-list census can settle.

Reference without deference is neither a final ideological destination nor a guarantee of good judgment. It names a relation worth studying: learning from another's refusal while retaining responsibility for the answer.
