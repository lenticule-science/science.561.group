# reproductive expropriation

The seizure of rights, control, labor, children, kinship recognition or economic value associated with another person's reproductive activity or possible descendants. Under hereditary slavery, the captive child's status and transferability could constitute wealth or labor claims for someone other than the parent or child.

## Boundaries and admissible measurement

Distinguish (a) expropriation of a future child by status law, (b) coerced conception or pairing of named persons, and (c) a specifically organized breeding establishment. One is neither proof nor a complete measure of another. This term does not erase kinship and resistance among enslaved people.

## Observed case boundary (2026-10-17)

Sam and Louisa Everett's 1936 Federal Writers' Project retrospective interview ([archival annotation](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/everett-randolph1936.md)) attests to a specific coercively imposed sexual relationship on McClain's Virginia plantation and subsequent children. It demonstrates **reported directed pairing in one named account**; it neither measures the prevalence of such coercion among enslaved mothers nor proves every captive birth was deliberately orchestrated. Later mutual support and love are part of the record, but cannot retroactively make the initiating coercion consensual ([Reader 2023](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/reader2023.md), p. 484). The reported date **1936** conflicts with **1937** in [Djeliid's opening](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/djeliid2024.md), and all 1930s oral recollection is mediated by the interviewer/editor.
