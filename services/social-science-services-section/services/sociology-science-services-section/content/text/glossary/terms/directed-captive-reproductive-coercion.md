# directed captive reproductive coercion

A *specified action* by an enslaver or delegated controller compelling enslaved people to form, sustain or undertake a reproductive/sexual relationship, with the aim of producing descendants, or coercing reproductive circumstances without a person's free agreement. This is a historical practice, not the same thing as the legal transfer of a child's status or the statistical observation of enslaved births.

## Conditions of evidence

An actual case requires source-attributed testimony, documentation of specific conduct or an explicit contemporaneous instruction, with a stated date, place, speaker, record genre, and mediation. Distinguish an eyewitness's account from a recorder's summary; a direct command from a court's property rule; a birth after compulsion from a numerical count of all coerced births; and an allegation of a specific case from a population prevalence estimate.

## Source-located case and limits

A Federal Writers' Project 1936 interview with [Louisa and Sam Everett](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/everett-randolph1936.md) documents one reported forced relationship directed by enslaver Jim McClain. The source records later children and Louisa's subsequent affection, but that affection does not undo the initial coercion ([Reader 2023](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/reader2023.md), p. 484). [Djeliid (2024)](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/djeliid2024.md) investigates other examples and the coerced use of men as “stock men”; its abstract does not establish how frequent either practice was.
