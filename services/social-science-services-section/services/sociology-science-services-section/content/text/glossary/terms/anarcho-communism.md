Anarcho-communism is the current within [anarchism](../_index.md) that advocates collective ownership of productive resources and distribution based on need rather than on wages, markets, or contribution. It is [communism](../../marxism/terms/communism.md) without [the state](./the-state.md): the means of production — land, tools, factories, workshops — are held in common by the communities that use them, and goods are distributed according to need rather than according to how much someone can pay or how much they produced.

The tradition's central theorist is [Pyotr Kropotkin](../../../../../../../../references/people/pyotr-kropotkin.md), who argued in *The Conquest of Bread* (1892) and *[Mutual Aid](/general/references/mutual-aid-a-factor-of-evolution.md)* (1902) that communism — collective ownership and need-based distribution — is not a utopian dream but a practice already embedded in human social life. Communities share resources, neighbors help each other, workers cooperate informally — all of this is communism in practice, happening alongside and despite [capitalism](./capitalism.md). What anarcho-communism proposes is not the invention of new social relations but the removal of the [hierarchies](./hierarchy.md) that prevent existing cooperative tendencies from organizing all of social life.

Anarcho-communism differs from Marxist communism in how it proposes to get there. Marxism-Leninism holds that a workers' party must seize [state power](./the-state.md), establish a "dictatorship of the proletariat," and use the state to reorganize the economy before the state eventually "withers away." Anarcho-communists argue that the state never withers away — whoever controls it becomes the new ruling [class](./class.md). The transition to communism must be anarchist from the start: organized through [federation](./federation.md), [direct action](./direct-action.md), [mutual aid](./mutual-aid.md), and [self-organization](./self-organization.md), without passing through a state phase.

[Errico Malatesta](../../../../../../../../references/people/errico-malatesta.md) contributed the argument that anarcho-communism must be voluntary — communities that wish to organize on communist principles should do so, but forcing communism through [coercion](./coercion.md) contradicts the [freedom](./freedom.md) it claims to serve. The Spanish Revolution of 1936–1939 is the most extensive historical example: anarcho-communist collectives organized agriculture and industry across large areas of Spain, demonstrating that production and distribution could function without owners, managers, or state direction.
