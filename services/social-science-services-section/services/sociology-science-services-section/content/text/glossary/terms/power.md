Power is the capacity to affect the world and other people. Everyone exercises power: when you persuade someone, care for someone, build something, organize a group, teach a skill, or refuse a demand, you are exercising power. Power is not something [anarchism](../index.md) opposes. What anarchism opposes is a specific arrangement of power: [domination](./domination.md) — power fixed into [hierarchical](./hierarchy.md) positions that compel [obedience](./obedience.md).

The distinction is sometimes expressed as *power-over* versus *power-with*. Power-over is the capacity to command and be obeyed — the boss who can fire you, the police officer who can arrest you, the landlord who can evict you. It operates through [hierarchy](./hierarchy.md): the person with power-over occupies a structural position above the person they command. Power-with is the capacity to act together — the workers who organize a strike, the community that builds a mutual aid network, the friends who coordinate a project. It operates through [self-organization](../../../../services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/self-organization.md): no one is above or below; power is distributed among participants.

Anarchism's claim is not that power should not exist but that power-over should be abolished and replaced with power-with. This is not utopian: power-with is how most human cooperation already works. Friends, families, informal groups, communities, and whole societies have coordinated through shared decision-making, [mutual aid](../../../../services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/services/organization-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/mutual-aid.md), and [voluntary association](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/voluntary-association.md) without anyone holding [authority](./authority.md) over anyone else. Power-over is not the natural condition — it is a specific historical arrangement maintained by [coercion](../../../../services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/coercion.md), [property](../../../../services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/property.md), and the training of [obedience](./obedience.md).

The concentration of power-over is what defines the major institutions anarchism opposes. [The state](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/the-state.md) concentrates the power to make laws and enforce them through violence. [Capitalism](../../../../services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/capitalism.md) concentrates the power to control work and extract surplus through [property](../../../../services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/property.md) ownership. Patriarchy concentrates the power to define gender roles and punish deviation. Colonialism concentrates the power to dispossess peoples of their land, governance, and ways of life. Each is a system for converting distributed power-with into concentrated power-over — and anarchism opposes each for the same structural reason.
