Communism names three distinct things that are often conflated: a political goal (classless, stateless society with common ownership of the means of production), a body of theory (accounts of how such a society might be achieved), and a set of historical movements (parties, revolutions, states claiming the name). These three do not align neatly. The goal predates any particular theory; the movements have often contradicted both.

[Karl Marx](../../../../../../../../references/people/karl-marx.md) and Friedrich Engels formulated the most influential European communist theory, grounding it in [historical materialism](./historical-materialism.md) and the analysis of [class struggle](./class-struggle.md). In this framework, communism is the resolution of the contradiction between social production and private appropriation — the end point of a historical process driven by the development of productive forces. But Marxist communism is one lineage among several. Anarcho-communism, developed by [Pyotr Kropotkin](../../../../../../../../references/people/pyotr-kropotkin.md) and others within the [anarchist](../../anarchism/terms/anarchism.md) tradition, pursues the same goal through the immediate construction of non-hierarchical, federated communes rather than through seizure of [state power](../../anarchism/terms/the-state.md). [Antonio Gramsci](../../../../../../../../references/people/antonio-gramsci.md) redirected communist strategy toward cultural and intellectual work, recognizing that [hegemony](theories/gramscian/terms/hegemony.md) operates through consent, not just coercion.

The concept is contested in part because European communist theory treats [commons](theories/political-economy/terms/commons.md)-based social organization as a future to be achieved, when Indigenous societies across the world organized collective stewardship of land, resources, and knowledge long before European political philosophy began theorizing the problem. [Decolonial](../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/decolonization.md) critique observes that Marxist communism, despite its opposition to [capitalism](../../anarchism/terms/capitalism.md), often reproduces the [settler-colonial](../topics/settler-colonialism/index.md) assumption that land is a resource to be administered rather than a relation to be maintained. [Indigenous resurgence](../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/indigenous-resurgence.md) movements offer living alternatives to both [capitalism](../../anarchism/terms/capitalism.md) and European communist models — alternatives grounded in existing relational practices rather than in theoretical projections.
