Colonialism is the [domination](./domination.md) and dispossession of one people by another — the seizure of their land, the destruction of their governance systems, the extraction of their resources and labor, and the imposition of the colonizer's [authority](./authority.md) over every aspect of life. It is not an event that happened in the past. For Indigenous peoples living under settler states — the United States, Canada, Australia, Israel, and others — colonialism is an ongoing structure: the land is still taken, the governance systems are still suppressed, the resources are still extracted, and the colonizer's [state](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/the-state.md) still claims [authority](./authority.md) over Indigenous nations.

Settler colonialism, specifically, is the form of colonialism in which the colonizers come to stay. They do not just extract resources and leave — they replace the existing population on the land, building a new society on top of the one they are destroying. This means settler colonialism does not end when a colony gains "independence" — the settlers are still there, still occupying the land, still governing through the institutions they built. The United States is not a post-colonial society; it is an active colonial project.

[Anarchism](../_index.md) opposes colonialism as a form of [domination](./domination.md) — but the relationship goes deeper than that. Colonialism relied on [the state](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/the-state.md) (colonial governments, military force, legal systems designed to dispossess), [capitalism](./capitalism.md) (the extraction of wealth for metropolitan economies, the creation of dependent labor forces), and [property](./property.md) law (the conversion of collectively governed Indigenous lands into privately owned colonial parcels). It also imposed [hierarchies](./hierarchy.md) that did not previously exist in many of the societies it conquered: wage [labor](./labor.md), industrial time-discipline, patriarchal family structures, and the replacement of consensus governance with centralized [authority](./authority.md).

Many Indigenous governance systems — consensus decision-making, distributed authority, collective land stewardship, governance through kinship and seasonal cycles — embody what anarchist analysis describes as non-hierarchical organization. They did not derive these practices from European anarchism; they practiced them for millennia before Europeans arrived. This recognition is the basis of Indigenous anarchism: not the application of a European framework to Indigenous peoples, but the acknowledgment that Indigenous governance already operates on principles that European anarchism only theorized.

Colonialism also structured [class](./class.md) and labor relations globally. The Atlantic slave trade, plantation economies, colonial resource extraction, and the forced proletarianization of Indigenous peoples created the wealth that funded European industrialization. [Capitalism](./capitalism.md) did not develop separately from colonialism — it developed through it.

Colonialism is the practice of one people establishing political control over another people's territory, exploiting its resources and [labor](../../../../../../../terms/labor.md), and imposing its governance systems. When European powers seized territories across Africa, Asia, and the Americas, they didn't just take land — they restructured entire societies. They imposed legal systems, religious institutions, languages, [property](../../../../anarchism/terms/property.md) regimes, and racial hierarchies designed to make extraction efficient and resistance difficult. Colonialism is not simply foreign rule; it is the reorganization of a society for the benefit of the colonizer.

Colonialism takes different forms. Extractive colonialism treats the colonized territory as a source of raw materials and [labor](../../../../../../../terms/labor.md) — the Belgian Congo, British India, and the Spanish silver mines of Potosi are examples. The colonizing power governs through local intermediaries or direct administration, extracting wealth while investing as little as possible in the colonized population. [Settler colonialism](../topics/settler-colonialism/index.md) operates differently: it aims to replace the existing population with settlers, as in the United States, Canada, Australia, and Israel/Palestine. The goal isn't just extraction but permanent occupation — the colonizer comes to stay and requires the removal or elimination of the Indigenous population to secure territory. [imperialism](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/imperialism.md) is a broader category: the extension of a state's power over other territories, which may operate through colonialism but also through economic domination, military threats, and political influence without direct territorial control.

The effects of colonialism persist long after formal colonial rule ends. Borders drawn by European powers — often cutting through existing nations, [kinship](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/kinship.md) systems, and ecological regions — continue to shape political conflicts. Economic structures designed for extraction still channel wealth from formerly colonized regions to former colonial powers. Legal systems imposed during colonization remain the basis of law in many postcolonial states. [decolonization](./decolonization.md) names both the historical process of ending formal colonial rule and the ongoing project of dismantling colonial structures — economic, legal, cultural, epistemic — that continue to operate.

Colonialism is not an aberration or a phase that civilization has moved past. It is a constitutive feature of the modern world-system: the global division of [labor](../../../../../../../terms/labor.md), the distribution of wealth between nations, the racial hierarchies that structure life chances — all have their roots in colonial processes that began in the 15th Century and continue in transformed forms today.
