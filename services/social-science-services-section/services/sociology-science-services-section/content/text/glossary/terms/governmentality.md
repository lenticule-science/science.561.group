Governmentality is a concept developed by [Michel Foucault](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/michel-foucault.md) in his 1977–1978 lectures at the Collège de France (*Security, Territory, Population*) to name the mode of power that operates through the management of populations. Governmentality does not govern by prohibiting behavior but by inciting, shaping, and optimizing conduct through norms, statistics, institutional design, and the production of subjects who govern themselves.

The concept marks a shift in [Foucault](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/michel-foucault.md)'s analysis of power from sovereignty (the right to kill or let live) and discipline (the training of individual bodies through [surveillance](./surveillance.md) and normalization) to a third mode: the government of populations through the regulation of life processes — birth rates, health, productivity, circulation, and security. Governmentality is the art of governing that takes the population as its object, political economy as its knowledge, and security apparatuses as its instruments.

The critical move is that governmentality produces freedom as one of its techniques. The liberal subject is not free despite power but free through power: the subject's freedom to choose, to consume, to optimize is what makes them governable. [Foucault](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/michel-foucault.md) calls this the shift from prohibition to incitement — the system does not say "you must not" but "you must manage yourself."

In emsenn's letters-to-the-web, governmentality is the structural foundation of [californication](../schools/cybernetic-postliberalism/terms/californication.md): the process by which structural contradiction is reframed as personal crisis requiring self-management. The californicated subject does not experience governance as external constraint but as internal responsibility — wellness, productivity, ethical consumption, affective regulation. [Recursive governance](../../../../services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/recursive-governance.md) extends [Foucault](../../general/people/michel-foucault.md)'s insight into the [cybernetic](../../information/disciplines/cybernetics/index.md) domain: governance through distributed feedback loops in which the governed participate as self-correcting elements.

Governmentality is the rationality through which modern states govern populations — not primarily by commanding obedience but by shaping the conditions within which people conduct themselves. Foucault fused "governing" and "mentality" to capture a specific point: governance is not just what governments do but a way of thinking about how conduct should be conducted. The "conduct of conduct" — directing the ways people direct themselves — is the formula that defines governmental reason.

Foucault introduced the term in his 1977–78 Collège de France lectures (*Security, Territory, Population*). He traced its emergence to the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, when the problem of government separated from two older models. Sovereignty asked how a king could hold territory and demand obedience — the relationship between sovereign and subject, mediated by law. Pastoral care asked how a shepherd could guide each soul toward salvation — the relationship between pastor and flock, mediated by conscience. The new problem was different from both: how to manage a population — its health, its wealth, its productivity, its security — which required new forms of knowledge: political economy, statistics (*Statistik*, the "science of the state"), and demography. The population became an object with its own regularities, measurable and optimizable.

Governmentality extends biopower by specifying its institutional logic. Where biopower names the general fact that modern power takes biological life as its object, governmentality names the specific rationality through which that management is organized. Different governmentalities produce different subjects. A liberal governmentality governs through the market and the framework of rights — it produces the autonomous, self-interested subject who must be left free to pursue their own ends. A neoliberal governmentality governs through competition and entrepreneurial norms — it produces the self-investing, self-optimizing subject who treats their own life as human capital to be managed. A welfarist governmentality governs through social provision and risk management — it produces the citizen-client of the welfare state. Each rationality depends on specific power/knowledge formations that make its subjects visible, calculable, and administrable.

The 1978–79 lectures (*The Birth of Biopolitics*) extended the analysis to neoliberal governmentality specifically, examining how the Chicago School and German ordoliberalism reimagined the relationship between state and market — not as state withdrawal but as the active construction of market conditions through government intervention.
