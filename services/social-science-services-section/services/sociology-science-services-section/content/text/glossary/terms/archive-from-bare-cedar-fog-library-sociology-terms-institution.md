An institution is a stable, organized pattern of [practices](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/social-sciences/sociology/_archive/from-working-locus-norm/working/library/norm/role/institution/docs/dev/scraps/practice.md) that structures how people act in a domain. Hospitals, schools, courts, prisons, the military, churches, and markets are institutions. So are less visible things: marriage, medical diagnosis, academic peer review, the job interview.

What makes something an institution rather than just a habit or a preference is that it persists beyond the individuals who participate in it. A teacher retires; the school continues. Institutions carry rules, roles, procedures, and expectations that exist before any particular person fills them and continue after that person leaves.

Institutions don't just constrain people — they produce the categories through which people understand themselves and each other. A hospital doesn't just treat sick people; it produces the category of "patient," the role of "doctor," and the boundary between "healthy" and "ill." [Michel Foucault](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/social-sciences/sociology/_archive/from-working-locus-norm/working/library/norm/role/institution/docs/general/people/michel-foucault.md) analyzed institutions as the sites where [power/knowledge](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/social-sciences/sociology/_archive/from-working-locus-norm/working/library/norm/role/institution/docs/philosophy/disciplines/critical-theory/schools/foucault/terms/power-knowledge.md) operates — where knowledge about people is produced through the same practices that exercise power over them [@foucault1977]. The categories institutions use to describe their subjects ("delinquent," "mentally ill," "learning disabled") aren't descriptions of pre-existing types but products of the institution's own practices of observation, classification, and management. Institutions are [contingent](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/social-sciences/sociology/_archive/from-working-locus-norm/working/library/norm/role/institution/docs/philosophy/terms/contingency.md): each emerged from specific historical developments that a [genealogical](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/social-sciences/sociology/_archive/from-working-locus-norm/working/library/norm/role/institution/docs/philosophy/disciplines/critical-theory/schools/foucault/terms/genealogy.md) analysis can trace.
