# Reproductive purity ratchet

**Reproductive purity ratchet** names an *unassayed mechanism* in which failure to realize an imagined ideal white breeder leads institutions to tighten reproductive eligibility repeatedly, shrinking the eligible population even when its ideology claims to preserve that population.

Evidence that some white people were sterilized or excluded through eugenic criteria (Stubblefield 2007; Caron 2008) establishes an **internal exclusion mechanism** but does not establish self-reinforcing escalation, demographic collapse or deliberate pursuit of extinction.

Test by tracing policy changes, justifications, eligible groups, actual births and survival, migration and pro-natalist countereffects. If the criteria broaden, stop escalating, or do not reduce reproductive continuity, the ratchet hypothesis is weakened or falsified. ## Quantitative evidence and alternative process (2026-10-17)

The 2026 archival slice supports an **internal exclusion mechanism**, not proof of a ratchet: North Carolina's 1966 report documents **4,218 sterilizations of people it categorized white** out of 6,851 total (July 1929–June 1966). In two-year operations, the share recorded white declined from **81.79% in 1946–1948** to **34.83% in 1964–1966**, a **−46.96 percentage-point shift** (Brophy–Troutman 2016; late endpoint corroborated by NC's original report). Meanwhile the state white-category headcount grew **13.95% in 1950–1960** (Gibson and Jung, Census Table 48). These counts do not identify births prevented or prove that purity selection caused white demographic contraction.

Utah's 1961 modification of the legal rationale for sterilization despite discredited genetic justifications (Tabery et al. 2023) indicates **institutional selection adapting to preserve its authority**, which need not mean ever-narrower racial classification or more restrictive marriage rules. Empirically distinguish that *adaptive control* pathway from a true positive-feedback *purity* ratchet, and from the possibility that the regime incorporates previously excluded groups.
