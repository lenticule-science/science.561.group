Collective care is an internal group practice of supporting
participants' needs, communication, and well-being so that disaster
response can continue without consuming its own people
[@fernandesjesus2021; @madrjoin2022].

Within emergent disaster response, the term matters because grassroots
work can fail not only from outside pressure but from exhaustion,
isolation, and silent overload inside the group. Fernandes-Jesus and
coauthors identify a culture of care and support as one of the group
process strategies that helped mutual-aid groups sustain involvement
over time [@fernandesjesus2021].

Collective care includes regular check-ins, attention to participants'
needs, supportive norms, and spaces where strain can be made visible
before it hardens into burnout or withdrawal.
