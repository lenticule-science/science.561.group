Care work in infrastructure is the application of care ethics to sociotechnical systems: treating server administration, content moderation, backup verification, and user support as ethico-political labor of tending a community's shared means of communication, not as a neutral or purely technical function.

Care work in infrastructure is distinguished from care work in its general sense — childcare, eldercare, domestic labor — by its object: the cared-for relation is with a system and, through the system, with the community that depends on it, rather than directly with a person's body. Puig de la Bellacasa extends care ethics beyond the interpersonal and the biological specifically to make this kind of maintenance-as-care visible in more-than-human and sociotechnical worlds (Puig de la Bellacasa 2017), building on Tronto's account of care as an ongoing political process rather than a private virtue (Tronto 1993).

The consequence of naming this labor as care rather than as neutral technical upkeep is that its systematic under-support becomes visible as a structural and ethical failure of the collective, not merely a personnel gap — the same reframing that lets burnout among a tech collective's maintainers register as a collective governance problem rather than an individual failing.
