# institutional reproductive expendability

Observable institutional willingness to bear or impose predictable losses of a group's reproductive opportunities, family security or survival while maintaining some other institutional objective such as capital accumulation, racial status, national belonging or a purported ideal future lineage.

## Boundaries and admissible measurement

Not a literal preference of an anthropomorphized 'whiteness'. This construct requires an actual policy choice, available alternative, decision-maker and observed cost. Poverty and declining births by themselves do not establish a coordinated ideological preference; many harms have nonracial and competing causes.

## Contradictory material reproductive conditions

The original client's hypothesis includes attrition of *the conditions to produce and sustain actual families*, even where no eugenic classification formally prevents childbirth. [Fraser's (2016) original essay](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/fraser2016.md) presents accumulation's tendency to deplete indispensable care as a structural mechanism; [Cooper (2017)](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/cooper2017.md) supplies a distinct family-values/privatized responsibility mechanism. Neither source proves those processes were intended to produce a pure white reproductive type, nor that they uniquely affect people currently classified white. The [Kearney–Levine–Pardue (2022)](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/kearney-levine-pardue2022.md) study is a direct countercontrol: not all broad fertility decline has an identified policy or economic cause. The construct therefore requires a demonstrable *decision under alternatives* and causal harm, not simply a population's falling birth rate.
