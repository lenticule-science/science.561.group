# captive increase

An enslaver's or property's 'increase' may refer to children born to people held enslaved and, in some legal contexts, a distinct claim over the potential future descendants of an enslaved woman. It thus names the juridical transfer of reproductive possibility into the owner's anticipated capital.

## Boundaries and admissible measurement

Do not confuse growth of the enslaved population through natural increase, a legal title to future issue, a domestic sale of existing persons, and deliberately compelled reproduction. They require distinct sources and cannot be summed as one event count.

## Originally adjudicated variations in ownership of reproductive futures (2026-10-17)

Four judicial opinions demonstrate different legal uses of captive increase: [Covington (North Carolina 1842)](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/case-covington-mcentire1842.md) awards Binah's four then-living children with the remainderman's interest; [Nelson (NC 1849)](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/case-nelson-nelson1849.md) awards Leah's three post-testator children with the bequest of Leah and reports **$329.75 in whole-family maintenance**, not a cost per pregnancy; [Smith (Delaware 1839)](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/case-smith-milman1839.md) distinguishes child-status from a particular owner's title and orders Stephen free; [Johnson (Kentucky 1848)](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/case-johnson-johnson1848.md) frees Lytha after her widow-owner died but leaves seven earlier-born children enslaved. These are not a uniform national *partus* or property rule. Claims over **possible unborn issue** are a [separate legal category](future-issue-property-claim.md), especially as described by [Alphonso 2023](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/alphonso2023.md); the original 1823 Banks opinion behind a leading example remains unrecovered.
