Method concepts in sociology. Each note here describes a family of practice — what kind of evidence the method produces, what kind of claim the evidence supports, and what the method's characteristic limits are. Methods are not neutral instruments; they carry commitments about what counts as a social fact, where social structure is visible, and whose perspective is treated as evidence.

The discipline does not have a single method. The notes here describe seven families that recur across the schools and lineages in sociology. School-specific method commitments (e.g. participant-observation as developed inside the Chicago School, or comparative-historical analysis as developed by Skocpol and Tilly) live with the schools that specialized them; the notes here describe the family in general.
