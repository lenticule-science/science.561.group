# Social fact

*The role concept for things that exist only because a society does — collective representations, institutions, currencies, languages, legal categories, kinship systems — and that exert pressure on individuals from outside any one individual's making.*

Durkheim's *Rules of the Sociological Method* (1895) defined social facts as ways of acting, thinking, and feeling that are external to the individual and endowed with a coercive power by virtue of which they impose themselves on the individual. The definition does two things at once: it makes a metaphysical claim — there are facts whose mode of existence is irreducibly collective — and a methodological one — sociology should treat such facts as facts, not reduce them to psychology or biology.

The metaphysical claim has a long afterlife. Searle's *Construction of Social Reality* (1995) reconstructs it analytically: institutional facts (money, marriage, citizenship) exist because we collectively represent them as existing, and the collective representation is itself a fact about us. Money is real, but only because we treat it as real, and the "we" is irreducibly collective.

The methodological claim — explain social facts by social facts — is harder. Strict Durkheimianism rules out psychological reductions. Most contemporary sociology is more porous: the social-fact level is real and primary for some explanations, but not autonomous from the cognitive, the embodied, or the material.

For the Black-radical and decolonial traditions, social facts include racial categories. Race, in this analysis, is not a biological fact but a social fact in Durkheim's sense: it exists because society treats it as existing, and treating it as existing has produced devastating material effects across the modern world. Naming race as a social fact is not denying its reality; it is locating where its reality comes from.

The pairing with [institution](./institution.md): an institution is a stable cluster of social facts that hangs together as a recognizable form (the school, the prison, marriage). The pairing with [structure](./structure.md): structures are patterned arrangements of social facts.
