# Institution

*The role concept for stable, organized patterns of practice that structure how people act in a domain and persist beyond the individuals who participate in them. Hospitals, schools, courts, prisons, churches, markets, marriage, the job interview — all institutions, whether visibly such or not.*

What makes something an institution is persistence beyond participants and the carrying of rules, roles, procedures, and expectations that exist before any particular person fills them. A teacher retires; the school continues. The institution is what is left when the bodies move through.

Institutions do not just constrain people — they produce the categories through which people understand themselves and each other. A hospital does not just treat the sick; it produces "patient" as a [role](./role.md), "doctor" as a [role](./role.md), and the "healthy/ill" boundary as a classification. This is the move Foucault generalizes into the institution-as-site-of-power-knowledge: the categories institutions use to describe their subjects are products of the institution's own practices of observation, classification, and management, not descriptions of pre-existing types.

The schools' divergences. Durkheim treats institutions as crystallized [social facts](./social-fact.md) — collective representations that exert moral pressure on individuals. New-institutionalist sociology (Meyer, Rowan, DiMaggio, Powell) treats institutions as scripts that organizations adopt for legitimacy reasons, often decoupled from technical efficiency. Marxist accounts treat institutions as instruments and superstructure — the legal, educational, and ideological apparatuses that reproduce the relations of production. Foucauldian accounts treat institutions as the concrete sites where [power](./power.md)/knowledge operates. Black-radical analysis treats specific institutions — the prison, the police, the school, the hospital — as continuations of slavery and colonial governance under reformed legal forms.
