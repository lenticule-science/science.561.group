The concepts in this directory are sociology's recurring semantic anchors: action, structure, agency, institution, role, status, network, power, social-fact, habitus, field, capital. Each is a *role* — a place that different schools fill differently. Marxists, Weberians, structural-functionalists, Bourdieusians, network theorists, and the Black radical tradition all engage these concepts; their accounts diverge, and the divergence is itself one of the things sociology studies.

These notes are short. They state the concept, name the role it plays in sociological analysis, and gesture at how the major schools treat it differently. They are the discipline-wide layer; school-specific specializations live in the `terms/` directories of the relevant schools and lineages.

## Layout

- Foundational concepts: this directory's flat list.
- Method concepts: [`methodology/`](methodology/) — ethnography, comparative-historical analysis, network analysis, archival method, participant observation, case study, discourse analysis.

## Convention

Concept notes carry `type: concept` and a `class:` classifier. The body is short (≈10–30 lines). The first paragraph states the role in italics. Subsequent paragraphs describe how the role is realized differently across schools.
