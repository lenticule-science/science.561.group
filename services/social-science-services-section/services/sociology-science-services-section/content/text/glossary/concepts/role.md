# Role

*The role concept for the patterned bundle of expected behaviors, rights, and obligations attached to a position in a social structure — what someone in a given position is expected to do, by whom, and under what sanctions.*

A role is not the person who occupies it. The role of "teacher" exists before this teacher arrived and persists after she leaves; what changes when she retires is who is in the role, not what the role is. Roles are sociology's way of analyzing how individual conduct is patterned by social position rather than by personality.

Linton, then Parsons and Merton, did the foundational work on roles in mid-twentieth-century American sociology: roles cluster into role-sets, role-sets attach to [statuses](./status.md), and the system of statuses-and-roles is one of the things an [institution](./institution.md) is made of. Goffman complicated the picture with *role distance* (the actor signals that she is not fully identified with the role she is enacting) and with the dramaturgical analysis of role performance — roles are not just slots but are *played*, with all the contingencies of performance.

The Black-radical and feminist traditions have insisted that role analysis must reckon with whose roles are scripted as roles ("doctor," "judge") and whose are scripted as nature ("good mother," "angry Black woman"). The asymmetry is not incidental. Naming an arrangement as a *role* admits that the arrangement could be otherwise; treating it as nature forecloses the question.

Hohfeldian analysis of legal positions (see [hohfeldian/](theories/work/theories/governance/theories/hohfeldian/)) is one direction in which the role concept has been formalized: the bundle of claims, privileges, powers, and immunities that attach to a legal position is a precise, structured account of what a particular kind of role consists of.
