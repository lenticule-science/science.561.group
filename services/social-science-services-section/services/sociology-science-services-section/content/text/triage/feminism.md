Feminism is the tradition of thought and practice organized around the analysis of how gender structures social life — who has power, who does what work, whose experience counts as knowledge, whose body is governed by whom. The tradition is not a single position but a field of contestation: liberal feminism, socialist feminism, radical feminism, Black feminism, Indigenous feminism, transfeminism, and postcolonial feminism each offer different analyses of what patriarchy is and how to dismantle it.

The library's feminist content currently lives distributed across other domains: care ethics (in philosophy) draws on feminist ethics, queer theory draws on feminist analysis of gender, the Black radical tradition draws on Black feminist analysis of the intersection of race and gender, and autonomist Marxism draws on Federici's analysis of reproductive labor. Feminism as its own domain is currently sparse — the infrastructure exists for it to grow as the library develops.

What unites the tradition is the refusal to treat gender as natural. Whether the analysis focuses on labor (who does the unpaid work that sustains the economy), on violence (who is vulnerable to whose force), on knowledge (whose experience counts as evidence), or on representation (who speaks and who is spoken for), the feminist commitment is that these arrangements are produced by social structures and can be changed by social action.
