# Symbolic violence

The morphism $\mathrm{Sv}$ of the social settling that resolves $\top$ states in capital and positional predicate cells into $\mathbf{t}$ through the selective suppression of contradicting evidence — the substrate-algebraic realization of Bourdieu's *symbolic violence*: the naturalization of domination by making it appear as the legitimate order of things.

Symbolic violence does not operate by physical coercion. It operates by making the arbitrariness of social arrangements invisible — so that those who are dominated participate in their own domination because they have no available framework to recognize the arrangement as domination rather than merit, nature, or the right order of things. Predicated sociology treats this as a specific operation on $\top$ states: the social settling contains closure rules that, when a capital or positional cell enters $\top$ (when contradiction arises — when a dominated person and a dominant institution's evidence conflict), resolve the $\top$ not by adjudicating the contradiction but by suppressing the contradicting evidence.

## Definition

Let $\phi = \langle e, \texttt{holds-capital-type}, c\rangle$ be a capital cell that has entered the $\top$ state: both a positive witness $w^+$ (the dominated agent's claim to legitimate standing) and a negative witness $w^-$ (the dominant institution's denial of that claim) are present. The symbolic violence morphism is the closure rule:

$$v(\phi) = \top \;\wedge\; v(\langle I, \texttt{has-authority-to-classify}, \mathrm{field}\rangle) = \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; v(\phi) \;\text{resolves toward}\; \mathbf{f}$$

— the institution $I$ with recognized classification authority in the field resolves the contradiction by settling the cell against the dominated agent's claim. The resolution does not require the institution to examine the contradicting evidence; it requires only that $I$'s classification authority is settled.

The resolution toward $\mathbf{f}$ is symbolic violence because:

1. The dominated agent's witness $w^+$ is not refuted — it is suppressed. The cell moves from $\top$ (contradiction) to $\mathbf{f}$ (settled negation) without the positive evidence being addressed.
2. The resolution is *misrecognized*: the dominated agent, whose habitus-closure has been acquired through the same field that performs the resolution, does not experience the settlement as domination but as *natural*. Their own dispositional rules fire in agreement with the settlement.
3. The misrecognition is the violence: the dominated participate in the suppression of their own contradicting witnesses because their habitus-encoded rules treat the dominant institution's classification authority as legitimate.

## Symbolic capital and the amplification of $\mathrm{Sv}$

Symbolic violence is most effective when the dominated hold symbolic capital in the form of recognition of the dominant order's legitimacy. The [capital fiber bundle](../objects/capital-fiber-bundle.md)'s symbolic capital component records this: $v(\langle e, \texttt{holds-symbolic-capital}, \texttt{belief-in-dominant-order}\rangle) = \mathbf{t}$ means agent $e$ is a positive witness to the dominant order's legitimacy — including the order's classification authority. This positive symbolic-capital cell *amplifies* the symbolic violence morphism: an agent with high symbolic capital of this type fires the resolution-toward-$\mathbf{f}$ closure rule *themselves*, without requiring any institutional act. They are self-dominating.

This is the substrate-algebraic content of the paradox Bourdieu identifies: the dominated are the most effective agents of their own domination, because their habitus encodes the dominant order's rules as natural.

## Symbolic violence vs. physical coercion

Physical coercion is a different morphism — one that directly installs $\mathbf{f}$ or $\top$ states on action-predicate cells through force-witnessing events. Symbolic violence differs: it does not directly constrain action-predicate cells. It resolves the capital and positional cells that are the *antecedents* of action-predicate rules — by settling the capital and positional cells in ways that make the dominated agent's action rules fire *themselves* in the direction of their own constraint.

The practical difference is speed and self-sustainability. Physical coercion requires ongoing external application. Symbolic violence, once the habitus is formed, is self-sustaining: the dominated fire the resolution rules themselves, requiring no external force. This is why symbolic violence is the more efficient governance technology of mature social formations.

## Relation to californication

Symbolic violence and [californication](./californication.md) are complementary morphisms in the late-liberal social settling. Californication routes structural contradictions into affective predicate cells. Symbolic violence resolves positional and capital contradictions in favor of the dominant order. Together they constitute the two arms of late-liberal governance: the structural contradiction that isn't personally processed (californication) is resolved against the dominated (symbolic violence). The subject who both processes their structural condition as personal affect *and* participates in the dominant order's classification system is the fully formed late-liberal subject.
