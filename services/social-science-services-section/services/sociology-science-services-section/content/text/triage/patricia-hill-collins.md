Patricia Hill Collins (b. 1948) is the figure through whom the [black-feminism](../_index.md) subdomain holds its most fully worked-out sociological systematization. Distinguished University Professor of Sociology Emerita at the University of Maryland and the first Black woman to serve as president of the American Sociological Association (2009).

## What this subdomain takes from her

- **Black feminist thought as a critical social theory.** *Black Feminist Thought: Knowledge, Consciousness, and the Politics of Empowerment* (1990; expanded 2000; second-edition reprints continuing) is the systematic statement. The book argues that Black feminist thought is a distinctive and coherent body of social theory produced by Black women out of their particular position in the U.S. social structure — that the standpoint produces analytical resources that other standpoints do not, and that the body of work has to be read as theory, not only as testimony. The book is one of the founding documents of contemporary standpoint sociology.
- **The matrix of domination.** Collins's name for the structure the Combahee Collective called interlocking. Domination is organized through the intersection of multiple systems (race, class, gender, sexuality, nation, age) operating across multiple domains (the structural, the disciplinary, the hegemonic, the interpersonal). The framing refuses both the additive reading of oppressions (race + class + gender) and the reduction of the structure to any single axis. The framing is the proximate source for many of the contemporary intersectionality literatures and for the [`kyriarchism`](../../../../queer/theories/kyriarchism/_index.md) analysis. See the term [`matrix-of-domination`](../terms/matrix-of-domination.md).
- **The outsider-within standpoint.** From earlier essays and developed in *Black Feminist Thought*: Black women historically occupied positions inside white households (as domestic workers) and inside white institutions (as scholars, professionals) from which they could observe both the inside operations of those institutions and the structures of difference that made them outsiders within. The standpoint is epistemically productive: it produces the kind of double vision Du Bois named for Black Americans in general and that Collins extends to the gendered case. The framing is one of the founding texts of standpoint epistemology in U.S. sociology.
- **Intersectionality as critical social theory.** Collins's work since 2000 (with co-author Sirma Bilge in *Intersectionality* [2016] and in *Intersectionality as Critical Social Theory* [2019]) is the sustained argument that intersectionality is not a slogan, not only a method, and not only a description of identity, but a critical social theory in its own right with distinctive ontological, epistemological, and political commitments. The intervention is in part against the academic dilution of intersectionality into an additive demographic check, and in part for its further development as theory. See the term [`intersectionality`](../terms/intersectionality.md).
- **The four domains of power.** From *Black Feminist Thought* onward: the matrix of domination operates through a *structural* domain (the macro-level institutions: economy, polity, family, education), a *disciplinary* domain (bureaucratic surveillance and rule), a *hegemonic* domain (the cultural production that legitimates), and an *interpersonal* domain (the everyday encounters in which the structure is enacted). The four-domain frame is one of the analytical heuristics the subdomain uses for organizing its analyses.

## Where her work figures elsewhere

Collins is a load-bearing figure for the subdomain's analytical vocabulary; the matrix-of-domination framing and the four-domains heuristic are central to how the subdomain organizes its analyses. Her standpoint epistemology converges with [bell hooks](./bell-hooks.md)'s.
