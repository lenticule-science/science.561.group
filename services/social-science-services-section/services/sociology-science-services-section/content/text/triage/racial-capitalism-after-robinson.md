[Cedric Robinson](../figures/cedric-robinson.md)'s *Black Marxism* (1983) made an argument that did not, at the time, get traction in the disciplines its argument addressed. The book was reissued in 2000 with a new preface, again in 2020 in the wake of the Movement for Black Lives, and the [racial-capitalism](../terms/racial-capitalism.md) concept it named has since become one of the most circulated analytical terms in critical political economy, abolitionist organizing, and the sociological literature on race. This essay tracks the post-Robinson trajectory of the concept and notes where the subdomain takes a position on contested questions.

## The shape of Robinson's original claim

Three interlocking moves structure *Black Marxism*'s argument:

1. **Genealogical.** Capitalism did not emerge as a racially neutral economic system that later incorporated racial hierarchy. Intra-European racial logics — the racialization of the Irish, the Slavs, the Roma — predated colonial encounter; the Atlantic slave trade scaled a logic already present.
2. **Theoretical.** Race is therefore not superstructural in capitalism but structural. Marxism, when it treats race as ideology layered over a class base, misidentifies the system it is trying to describe.
3. **Historiographic.** A continuous tradition of Black thought — slave revolt, maroon societies, the Haitian Revolution, Du Bois, James, Wright, the twentieth-century liberation movements — has known this and theorized from it. The tradition precedes and exceeds Marxism.

The post-Robinson literature has extended each of these moves and contested them in distinct ways.

## Geographic extension: Gilmore

[Ruth Wilson Gilmore](../figures/ruth-wilson-gilmore.md)'s *Golden Gulag* (2007) and *Abolition Geography* (2022) are the most influential extension. Gilmore's contribution is to make racial capitalism geographically operational. Her definition of racism — the state-sanctioned production of group-differentiated vulnerability to premature death — is a definition of racial capitalism's *output*: the differential distribution of mortality across racialized geographies. The prison-as-spatial-fix argument shows how a particular crisis-management strategy (absorbing surpluses of land, labor, capital, and state capacity) takes racialized form. The extension is faithful to Robinson's frame and concretizes it in a way *Black Marxism* did not attempt.

## Conceptual extension: the Race & Capitalism Project

The 2010s produced a generation of scholarship under the racial-capitalism heading — Jodi Melamed's *Represent and Destroy* (2011) and her 2015 essay "Racial Capitalism" in *Critical Ethnic Studies*; Nikhil Pal Singh's "On Race, Violence, and So-Called Primitive Accumulation" (2016); Gargi Bhattacharyya's *Rethinking Racial Capitalism* (2018); Destin Jenkins and Justin Leroy's edited volume *Histories of Racial Capitalism* (2021). The literature shares a working definition: racial capitalism names the way capital accumulation operates through the production and management of group-differentiated value. The literature's productive disagreements are over scale (is racial capitalism a transhistorical structure or a periodizable formation?), geography (is it Atlantic, global, planetary?), and what counts as the analytic unit.

## Contestation: is "racial" doing analytical work?

Two lines of critique recur and deserve naming:

1. **The redundancy critique.** If all capitalism is racial, the modifier does no analytical work; we could just say capitalism. This critique misreads the claim. "Racial capitalism" is not the assertion that capitalism happens to be racist; it is the assertion that racialization is one of capital's accumulation strategies. Dropping the modifier loses the analytical move.
2. **The reification critique.** Naming "racial capitalism" risks treating racialization as a stable property of populations rather than as a process capital performs. The post-Robinson literature has largely heeded this critique by emphasizing racialization as an active production rather than race as a fixed attribute, and the subdomain follows that usage.

A third line — that the concept is too capacious to be operational — is more serious and is worked under the subdomain's [questions](questions/_index.md) heading.

## What the subdomain takes as the working frame

The subdomain treats racial capitalism as Robinson's argument plus Gilmore's geographic operationalization plus the post-2010 insistence on racialization-as-process. The working definition is: the political-economic structure in which capital accumulation operates through the production and management of group-differentiated vulnerability to premature death. The definition has the virtue of being analytically tight (one mechanism, one outcome) and of binding the political-economic and biopolitical registers in a single analytic.

What it does not yet do is generate predictive claims at the meso scale — claims about which forms racial-capitalist crisis will take in which conjunctures. That gap is the subdomain's principal substantive open question on this concept.
