Angela Y. Davis (b. 1944) is the figure through whom the [black-radical-tradition](../_index.md) holds together philosophical analysis, mass political organizing, and the abolitionist horizon. Trained in philosophy under Herbert Marcuse at Brandeis and in Frankfurt, she taught at UCLA, was prosecuted and acquitted in 1972 in a case that became an international cause, and helped found Critical Resistance with [Ruth Wilson Gilmore](./ruth-wilson-gilmore.md) and others in 1997. Distinguished Professor Emerita at UC Santa Cruz.

## What this subdomain takes from her

- **The prison-industrial complex.** Davis (with co-organizers in Critical Resistance) gave currency to the term as an analytical name for the integrated system of prisons, policing, surveillance, sentencing law, prison-construction industries, prison-labor extraction, and the political economy that produces prison expansion as a profitable response to social crises. The term refuses the framing of "the prison" as a neutral instrument of justice and reframes it as an industry with constituencies, beneficiaries, and growth dynamics.
- **Abolition democracy.** Reactivating Du Bois's term: abolition is not principally the destruction of carceral institutions but the construction of the social, economic, and political conditions that would make those institutions unnecessary — universal health care, education, housing, meaningful work, conflict-resolution practices that do not route through cages. Abolition is therefore a constructive political program, not a negative one.
- **Race, class, gender as a single analytical field.** *Women, Race & Class* (1981) is the foundational text for the argument that the analytical categories of race, class, and gender cannot be separated and then re-articulated; they are produced together and must be analyzed together. The book is one of the primary sources for what later became known as intersectional analysis, though Davis's framing emphasizes the political-economic constitution of the categories rather than their lived experience.
- **The continuity of slavery and the prison.** Davis reads the Thirteenth Amendment's exception clause ("except as a punishment for crime") as the legal architecture that allowed slavery to be reorganized through criminalization rather than abolished. The convict-lease system, chain gangs, the contemporary prison: each is a historical phase of a continuous structure. This reading is the proximate source for the subdomain's treatment of mass incarceration as racial capitalism's spatial fix.

## Key works

- *If They Come in the Morning: Voices of Resistance* (1971, ed.)
- *Women, Race & Class* (1981)
- *Are Prisons Obsolete?* (2003)
- *Abolition Democracy: Beyond Empire, Prisons, and Torture* (2005)
- *Freedom Is a Constant Struggle* (2016)
- *Abolition. Feminism. Now.* (with Gina Dent, Erica Meiners, Beth Richie, 2022)

## Where her work figures elsewhere

Davis is foundational for the [abolition](../../abolition/_index.md) sibling subdomain and for [Ruth Wilson Gilmore](./ruth-wilson-gilmore.md)'s carceral-geography work. Her insistence on the inseparability of race, class, and gender shapes the subdomain's reading of [Hortense Spillers](../../../../../../../../references/people/hortense-spillers.md) and [bell hooks](../../../../../../../../references/people/bell-hooks.md).
