Seven years ago, I wrote this essay to piece together the connective tissue between [[Facebook]], [[Russian finance|Russian financial networks]], and the [[Donald J. Trump Presidential Campaign, 2016]] campaign’s [[digital strategy]]. At the time, the conversation was focused on [[Cambridge Analytica]], [[disinformation]], and the fallout of the [[2016 United States Presidential Election]]. Since then, we’ve lived through another [[United States Presidential Election cycle]] shaped by [[platformized governance]], [[Elon Musk|a billionaire]] takeover of [[Twitter]], and a fresh wave of [[information warfare]] that makes [[2016]] look almost quaint by comparison.

Back then, much of the discourse treated Facebook’s role as a technological accident—an [[invention]] that got out of hand, leading well-meaning users to accidentally [[destabilize democracy]]. There was also plenty of [[speculation]] about foreign influence, but much of it remained just that: speculation. The real connective tissue, the documented financial relationships and strategic decisions that tied these players together, was available in public records. It wasn’t theoretical. It wasn’t hidden. It was just inconveniently clear.

It's worth keeping that in mind when sifting through contemporary analysis. What genuinely connects the dots, and what keeps you locked in an endless loop of speculation? What exposes mechanisms of power, and what merely feeds the spectacle?

As [[Guy Debord]] put it, “The spectacle is not a collection of images, but a social relation among people, mediated by images.” The issue was never just a platform or a particularly bad stretch of social media hygiene—it was how influence and power operated within that space, who benefited, and who knew exactly how to play the game.

This essay was my attempt to lay out the facts as they were: no grand theories, no convenient scapegoats, just the mechanics of influence, money, and control. If anything, it feels even more relevant now.
