Indigenous anarchism is the current within the broader anarchist tradition that refuses two equally damaging framings. The first is the framing of anarchism as a European political-philosophical inheritance (Proudhon, Bakunin, Kropotkin, Goldman, Malatesta) to be transmitted to or applied within Indigenous communities — a framing that reproduces the colonial pattern of European thought arriving with the answer. The second is the framing of Indigenous political traditions as *pre-*political nature whose value is exhausted by what they confirm about anarchism's a priori commitments — a framing that takes Indigenous communities as evidence for European theory's correctness rather than as polities with their own theoretical commitments and stakes.

What the current names is the encounter, on terms of mutual seriousness, between anarchism and Indigenous political traditions: the long-standing nation-to-nation diplomatic systems of the Haudenosaunee, the gift-economic and consensus traditions documented across many Indigenous polities, the practical refusal of state form that anthropologists from Pierre Clastres onward (and that David Graeber, in [Graeber 2004](../../../../../references/graeber2004.md), generalizes) have read in many of these traditions, and — centrally — the contemporary Indigenous land-defense movements whose practical political work is the live site at which the encounter takes place.

The subdomain commits to the project's stated charter. Indigenous Sovereignty is not, in this library, a topic that can be relegated to "settler-colonialism studies" while anarchism gets analyzed elsewhere; it is constitutive of what anarchism in this place and this moment can honestly mean. The relation to the [`settler-colonialism/`](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/) subdomain is structural: settler-colonial analysis describes the structure that Indigenous-anarchist practice refuses. Concretely, the [grounded-normativity](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/grounded-normativity.md), [indigenous-resurgence](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/indigenous-resurgence.md), [recognition-refusal](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/recognition-refusal.md), [indigenous-sovereignty](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/indigenous-sovereignty.md), and [land-back](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/land-back.md) terms in that subdomain are the analytical vocabulary this subdomain depends on; this subdomain's job is to think what the *anarchist* tradition has to do with the political life those terms name.

## Named figures

The substantive references the subdomain commits to. Each name below carries a person-level reference note in `library/references/people/`; the notes summarize core ideas, key works, and where each figure's work figures elsewhere in the library.

- **[Vine Deloria Jr.](../../../../../../../../../references/people/vine-deloria-jr.md)** (Standing Rock Sioux, 1933–2005) — *Custer Died for Your Sins* (1969), *God Is Red* (1973), *Behind the Trail of Broken Treaties* (1974). The foundational restatement, in modern argumentative form, of treaty-grounded sovereignty as the unbargained-for premise of any honest engagement; and the religion-of-place / religion-of-time distinction that the subdomain holds open against the assumption that political imagination must operate in the European time-register.
- **[Russell Means](../../../../../../../../../references/people/russell-means.md)** (Oglala Lakota, 1939–2012) — co-founder of the American Indian Movement; leader at Wounded Knee (1973). His 1980 essay *For America to Live, Europe Must Die* is the load-bearing reference for the subdomain's refusal of the "anarchism-as-export" framing: Marxism and the broader European revolutionary tradition share with capitalism a productivist, state-centric metaphysics, and Indigenous traditions are deformed by being absorbed into it.
- **[Glen Coulthard](../../../../../../../../../references/people/glen-coulthard.md)** (Yellowknives Dene) — *Red Skin, White Masks* (2014). The contemporary critique of the politics of recognition as a settler-colonial governance technology, paired with the constructive concept of [grounded-normativity](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/grounded-normativity.md) and the resurgence-over-recognition argument.
- **[Leanne Betasamosake Simpson](../../../../../../../../../references/people/leanne-simpson.md)** (Michi Saagiig Nishnaabeg) — *As We Have Always Done* (2017), *A Short History of the Blockade* (2021). [Indigenous-resurgence](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/indigenous-resurgence.md) as ongoing political-intellectual production rather than cultural preservation, Nishnaabeg internationalism, and the refusal of the settler-academic separation between scholarly argument and Indigenous narrative-ceremonial form.

The early-twentieth-century US lineage is anchored by **[Lucy Parsons](../../../../../../../../../references/people/lucy-parsons.md)** (c. 1851–1942, of Black, Mexican, and Indigenous descent) — the historical example of an organizer who explicitly took up anarchist language and organizational form while doing what would now be called intersectional analysis from lived experience rather than from European theory. The reading of Parsons in this subdomain is via the [indigenous-anarchism term file](terms/indigenous-anarchism.md): she belongs to the side of the relationship that *adopts* the label, against the side that rejects it; both positions are legitimate.

## Indigenous-feminism axis

Indigenous-feminist analysis is not a sub-topic of Indigenous-anarchist analysis; it is constitutive of it. Settler-colonial elimination operates centrally through gendered violence, and any account of Indigenous political life that does not begin from this fact is an account of a different subject.

- **[Audra Simpson](../../../../../../../../../references/people/audra-simpson.md)** (Kahnawà:ke Mohawk) — *Mohawk Interruptus* (2014); "The State Is a Man" (2016). [Nested sovereignty](../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/indigenous-sovereignty.md) as the analytical and political situation of Indigenous polities geographically encompassed by settler states; *ethnographic refusal* as a research methodology that takes the community's political authority seriously; and the gendered structure of settler elimination as constitutive (not adjacent) to settler-colonial analysis.
- **[Kim TallBear](../../../../../../../../../references/people/kim-tallbear.md)** (Sisseton-Wahpeton Oyate) — *Native American DNA* (2013); *Making Love and Relations Beyond Settler Sex and Family* (2018). The critique of settler-scientific indigeneity (genomics-as-identity displaces tribal citizenship), tribe-as-polity-not-population, and the Indigenous-feminist account of kinship as relational practice — including kinship with land, water, and other-than-human relatives — rather than biological pedigree or settler-legal household.

## Contemporary land-defense movements

The live sites at which the encounter between anarchism and Indigenous political tradition takes place — the ongoing political work that this subdomain takes as its primary referent rather than its illustrative material. The brief descriptions below are the subdomain's working notes; full reference notes for these movements are pending direct engagement and live as a registered follow-up.

- **Standing Rock / NoDAPL (2016–2017)** — the Standing Rock Sioux–led encampment opposing the Dakota Access Pipeline's crossing of the Missouri River at Lake Oahe, which became the largest convergence of Indigenous nations in modern US history. The encampment's organization (multiple camps, council protocol, prayer-led non-violent direct action) and its violent suppression by private security and state militarized police made the structure of US settler-state policing of Indigenous land defense visible to a wider public than any prior action.
- **Wet'suwet'en / Unist'ot'en (ongoing, escalating from 2009)** — the Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs' refusal of the Coastal GasLink pipeline through unceded Wet'suwet'en territory in what the Canadian state calls British Columbia. The Unist'ot'en healing camp, the *Yintah* and *Likhts'amisyu* checkpoints, and the repeated RCMP militarized raids since 2019 are the contemporary case in which hereditary Indigenous governance, the Canadian Indian Act band-council apparatus, and settler resource extraction are most directly in contradiction.
- **Mauna Kea / Kū Kiaʻi Mauna (2014–2019, ongoing)** — the Kanaka Maoli (Native Hawaiian) protection of Mauna a Wākea against the Thirty Meter Telescope, organized through *kapu aloha* — a discipline of non-violent ceremonial protection rooted in Hawaiian protocol and the recognition of Mauna Kea as sacred ancestor. The *kūpuna* line at the Mauna Kea Access Road in 2019 made the ceremonial-political form of Hawaiian land defense legible at international scale.
- **Kanehsatà:ke / "Oka Crisis" (1990)** — the Kanien'kehá:ka (Mohawk) defense of *the Pines* and the Kanehsatà:ke burial ground against a planned golf-course expansion, which produced a 78-day standoff with the Sûreté du Québec, the Royal Canadian Mounted Police, and the Canadian Armed Forces. Foundational reference for contemporary Indigenous land defense in what the Canadian state calls Quebec; the precedent that subsequent Mohawk and other Haudenosaunee actions, including the 2020 solidarity blockades during the Wet'suwet'en raids, position themselves with respect to.
- **Black Mesa / Big Mountain (Diné, ongoing since 1974)** — the Diné (Navajo) resistance to forced relocation under the 1974 Navajo-Hopi Land Settlement Act and the long Peabody Coal extraction on Black Mesa. The Black Mesa Indigenous Support tradition — the long settler-solidarity practice of materially supporting the elder resisters who refused relocation — is one of the practical sites at which what Indigenous-anarchist solidarity actually consists in has been worked out, not theorized.

## Adjacent collectives and traditions

- **Indigenous Action** — the Diné/Indigenous anarchist collective whose pamphlets — most notably *Accomplices Not Allies: Abolishing the Ally Industrial Complex* (2014) — are foundational references for what the encounter between settler-anarchist organizing and Indigenous-led struggle requires of settlers.
- **Native Youth Movement** — the late-1990s Coast Salish-rooted youth organization whose explicit rejection of NGO-mediated activism and insistence on warrior-society organizational form is one of the references the subdomain holds open as an alternative to the recognition-grant-funded settler-NGO apparatus.
