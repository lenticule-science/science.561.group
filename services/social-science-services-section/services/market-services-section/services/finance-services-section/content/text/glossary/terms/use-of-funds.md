A **use-of-funds** or **use-of-proceeds** statement explains how financing proceeds are expected or permitted to be allocated. It can identify amounts, percentages, entities, time periods, milestones, and categories such as equipment, inventory, hiring, [working capital](./working-capital.md), debt repayment, fees, or reserves.

Its force depends on context. In an internal plan it may be a revisable budget. In a loan, grant, restricted contribution, securities offering, or investor agreement it can be contractually or legally binding, with consent, reporting, segregation, or repayment consequences for deviations.

The statement should reconcile to the financing amount after issuance costs and to the cash forecast and [financial projections](./financial-projections.md). It should distinguish expenditure, capitalization, debt retirement, intercompany transfer, and retained liquidity because those uses affect the statements differently.

Changes should be authorized under the governing terms and recorded with the reason, approver, amount, and effect on milestones and liquidity. A variance is not automatically misconduct, but an unexplained mismatch is a control and disclosure problem.
