**Game-theoretic probability** formulates uncertain processes as sequential games rather than beginning with one fixed probability measure. A protocol typically specifies moves by a forecaster, an outcome-producing player, and a skeptic who chooses bets subject to a nonnegative-capital constraint.

Events can be assigned upper or lower game-theoretic probabilities through the initial capital needed for a strategy to superhedge their indicators. Limit-theorem-style results can be expressed constructively: if a stated regularity fails, an admissible betting strategy can make its capital grow greatly while remaining solvent under the protocol.

**Cournot's principle** supplies the interpretive bridge: an event that an admissible strategy could exploit to multiply capital enormously without bankruptcy is treated as implausible. Precise formulations vary, so the allowed strategies, collateral constraint, protocol, and meaning of “large” must be stated.

In finance, this language can test a market-efficiency or forecasting claim by making the exploiting strategy explicit. It does not itself prove that real markets are efficient or that a theoretical strategy survives transaction costs, price impact, leverage limits, and model mismatch.

**Defensive forecasting** uses related ideas to choose forecasts that prevent specified test strategies from systematically profiting from forecast errors. Its guarantees concern calibration or resistance to a class of tests, not investment profitability.
