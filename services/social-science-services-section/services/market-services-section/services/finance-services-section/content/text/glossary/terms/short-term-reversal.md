**Short-term reversal** is an empirical pattern in which assets with unusually high very recent returns subsequently underperform assets with unusually low recent returns over a stated short horizon.

Candidate mechanisms include temporary liquidity pressure, bid-ask bounce, inventory adjustment by intermediaries, delayed price discovery, and overreaction. These mechanisms have different implications: a return reversal caused by microstructure measurement is not automatically an executable profit opportunity.

A reversal claim must define the asset universe, ranking and holding periods, skip rules, weighting, benchmark, and treatment of spreads, market impact, shorting, borrow, and delistings. Gross effects concentrated in illiquid securities can disappear or reverse after executable prices and costs are used. Conditional screens selected after examining the data add further overfitting risk.

Short-term reversal and [momentum](./momentum.md) can coexist because their horizons, signals, and mechanisms differ. Neither label establishes which effect dominates for a particular asset or date.
