**Matched betting** combines a promotional wager with one or more offsetting wagers so that the net payoff varies less across the underlying event's outcomes. A common construction backs an outcome at a bookmaker and lays the same outcome at a betting exchange; where lay betting is unavailable, several bookmaker positions may sometimes approximate the hedge.

The relevant calculation includes the qualifying wager, promotional-credit terms, odds on every leg, exchange commission, stake-return rules, and the treatment of a free bet if it wins. Stakes are chosen to balance outcome payoffs or to optimize another stated objective. Because odds differ, the qualifying leg often has a known expected conversion cost that is accepted to unlock the promotion.

The construction is not riskless. Odds can move before all legs execute; a market can suspend; bets can be rejected, limited, voided, or settled under different rules; counterparties can fail; and an operator can make a stake or event-matching error. Funds may also be tied up across accounts. Legal availability, identity requirements, tax treatment, and promotion terms depend on jurisdiction and change over time.

Bookmakers may restrict promotions or wagering limits when activity is unprofitable to them. Using false identities or violating account terms is not part of the financial mechanism and can create fraud, forfeiture, and legal risk. The scalable unit is therefore the eligible offer under an authorized identity, not an unlimited repeatable edge.
