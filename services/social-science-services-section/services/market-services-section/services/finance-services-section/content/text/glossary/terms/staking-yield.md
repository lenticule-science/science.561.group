**Staking yield** is the token-denominated return associated with committing tokens to proof-of-stake validation, either by operating a validator or delegating to one. Rewards may come from new token issuance, transaction fees, or protocol-specific value flows.

The headline reward rate is nominal. An owner's economic return also depends on dilution from network issuance, validator commission, uptime, compounding, transaction costs, taxes, and the token's purchasing-power change. A staker can earn more token units while losing value in another unit of account. “Real yield” is meaningful only after naming the inflation measure and unit of account being used.

Risks and rules are protocol-specific. They can include slashing for attributable faults, missed rewards from downtime, unbonding delays, changing reward parameters, validator concentration, governance action, key compromise, custody failure, and smart-contract or liquid-staking-token risk. Delegators may share some validator penalties, but neither the conditions nor the magnitude are universal.

Liquid staking can provide a transferable claim while the underlying stake remains constrained. It replaces some direct illiquidity with redemption, depeg, contract, governance, and secondary-market risk. Comparing staking opportunities therefore requires a common return period, consistent dilution treatment, and explicit treatment of lockup and loss scenarios—not a comparison of headline percentages alone.
