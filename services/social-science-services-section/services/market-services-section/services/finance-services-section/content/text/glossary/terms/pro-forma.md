**Pro forma** financial information is prepared “as if” specified assumptions, transactions, structures, or adjustments applied. It can be forward-looking, but the term is not synonymous with forecast.

Examples include projected statements, historical statements adjusted as though an acquisition or financing had occurred earlier, and management presentations that exclude or reclassify selected items. These uses serve different decisions and may be governed by different accounting, securities, lending, or contractual requirements.

A pro forma presentation should state its purpose, reporting entity, period, assumptions, adjustments, and relationship to historical or standard-basis financial statements. Adjustments should be traceable and applied consistently across linked statements.

Because pro forma measures can omit unfavorable items or combine entities and periods hypothetically, they should not be labeled as actual results. Users need a reconciliation to the nearest standard or historical measure and enough detail to reverse or challenge each adjustment. Forward-looking pro forma statements are part of [financial projections](./financial-projections.md).
