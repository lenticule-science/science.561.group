Brokerage is [intermediation](./intermediation.md) with two further constraints: the broker is paid on [contingency](./contingency-fee.md) — nothing is owed unless a match actually completes — and the broker never takes ownership of the thing being matched, at any point in the transaction.
This second constraint is what separates a broker from a dealer or a merchant: a used-car dealer who buys cars and resells them is bearing inventory risk and earning a margin on ownership; a broker who connects a buyer directly to a seller and takes a cut of the resulting deal is bearing no inventory risk at all, and earning a fee for the *match*, not for anything owned in between.
The same distinction separates brokerage from franchising or licensing, where the intermediary supplies an ongoing asset (a brand, a system) in exchange for ongoing royalties — a broker's obligation and payment are both discharged at the moment of the match, not carried forward.

**Four documented forms**, surveyed together in [the 2026-08-28 record this term was founded from](../../../../../../../../../../data/records/2026-08-28-brokerage-mechanism/brokerage-survey.md) because they share this exact structure despite operating in unrelated industries:

- **Contingency recruiting**: a fee, typically 15-30% of first-year salary (search synthesis; see the record for the full range and role-level breakdown), paid by an employer only when a sourced candidate is hired — the recruiter never employs the candidate or the employer.
- **Real-estate wholesaling**: a fee captured via [contract assignment](./contract-assignment.md) — the wholesaler signs a purchase agreement as buyer of record, then assigns the contractual rights to an end buyer before closing, without ever taking title to the property.
- **Freight brokerage**: a fee for matching a shipper's load to a carrier's available capacity, without the broker owning any trucks or freight.
- **Insurance brokerage**: a fee or commission for matching a buyer to a carrier's policy, without the broker underwriting the risk itself (that risk sits with the carrier, the actual principal).

**What makes brokerage specifically attractive to an operator with search capacity and no capital**: because ownership never transfers through the broker, no purchase capital is required to originate a deal — the broker's only inputs are the labor of finding and verifying a match and, in most forms, some initial licensing or bonding cost.
This is also, symmetrically, why brokerage income is capped by the *size of the search problem being solved*: a broker earns nothing for effort that does not end in a completed match, so brokerage income is inherently lumpy and contingent rather than steady, and the fee only exists because the underlying [search-and-matching](./search-and-matching.md) friction is real — see that term for why a frictionless market leaves no room for a broker's fee at all.
Not every apparently brokerage-shaped activity clears a mission or a legal screen merely because the mechanism is capital-light: see [the note on brokerage as a market mechanism](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/28/2026-08-28-note-on-brokerage-as-a-market-mechanism.md) for the applied, form-by-form screening this term was founded to support, including the finding that real-estate wholesaling as typically practiced carries a documented pattern of harm to Black homeowners that the mechanism's capital-light structure does not excuse.
