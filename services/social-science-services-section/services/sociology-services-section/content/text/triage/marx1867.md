# Marx 1867 — *Capital: A Critique of Political Economy, Volume I*

Karl Marx. *Das Kapital: Kritik der politischen Ökonomie, Erster Band*. Hamburg: Verlag von Otto Meissner, 1867. Standard English: *Capital: A Critique of Political Economy, Volume I*, trans. Ben Fowkes (Penguin / New Left Review, 1976).

## Status

Read locally (Fowkes translation); the foundational reference for the [marxism](theories/critical-theory/theories/marxism/) subdomain and a primary source running through critical theory, the Black radical tradition (Robinson, Du Bois on slavery and capitalism), autonomist Marxism, and decolonial Marxism. Person-level note: [`library/references/people/karl-marx.md`](../../../../references/people/karl-marx.md). Marx's later volumes (II, 1885; III, 1894) were assembled by Engels from Marx's manuscripts; Volume I is the only volume Marx himself completed and authorized for publication.

## Annotation

The book that grounds the Marxist analysis of capitalism. Marx's central moves are well-known, here listed only as anchors for the references that follow.

The *commodity* as the cell-form of capitalist wealth and the analysis of its dual character as use-value and exchange-value (Part I). The *labor theory of value* — exchange-value reduces to socially-necessary abstract labor time. The *fetishism of commodities* — relations between people appear as relations between things. The *general formula for capital* M-C-M' (money becoming more money through circulation), and the question of where the surplus comes from. The answer: surplus value is extracted from labor power, the unique commodity whose use-value is the production of more value than its own reproduction requires (Part II–III). The structural distinction between *absolute* and *relative* surplus value, the analysis of cooperation, the division of labor, machinery, and the working day (Parts IV–V). The analysis of *primitive accumulation* — the violent historical process through which the conditions for capitalist relations were produced — including the chapter on "the genesis of the industrial capitalist" that names colonial conquest, the slave trade, and the enclosure of the commons as constitutive of capitalism, not external to it (Part VIII).

The reading the library's marxism subdomain gives priority to. The *Capital* of Volume I is not only an economic text; it is a critique of *political economy*, where the critique includes the categories of bourgeois economics themselves. Each of the categories Marx works through is presented as historically specific to capitalism, not as natural or universal. This methodological commitment is what later critical theory inherits as the analysis of categories-as-social-facts and what the Black radical tradition extends in arguing that capitalism's categories cannot be analyzed apart from race, slavery, and colonial extraction (Robinson's racial capitalism).

The Fowkes translation is the standard English version; the Penguin edition with the introduction by Ernest Mandel is the most widely-used scholarly edition.
