# Agency

*The role concept for the capacity of actors to act otherwise — to produce effects not fully determined by structural position, normative pressure, or causal antecedent. Agency is what is at stake whenever sociology asks whether the actor "could have done otherwise."*

Agency is not the same as [action](action.md). Action is what is done; agency is the capacity for that doing to be other than it is. The concept matters because every account of social life that takes structure seriously must answer: where is the room for change? An account in which actors are fully determined by structure cannot explain how structures change.

Schools differ on how much agency to credit. Strong-structuralist accounts (orthodox structural-functionalism, some structural-Marxism) credit very little: agency is the local enactment of structurally given roles. Voluntarist accounts (rational-choice theory, methodological individualism) credit a great deal: agents choose under constraint, but the choosing is theirs. Most contemporary accounts sit in between. Bourdieu's *regulated improvisation* — the [habitus](habitus.md) generates appropriate action without conscious calculation, but is not deterministic — is one influential formulation. Sewell's "agents are empowered to act with and against others by structures" treats agency as itself structurally distributed: not a uniform human capacity but unequally available depending on position.

Black-feminist and decolonial sociology have insisted that the agency question is not abstract: who is recognized as having agency, and whose acts are read as expressions of structure ("they could not have done otherwise, given who they are") is itself a political assignment. The denial of agency is one of the operations of [power](power.md).
