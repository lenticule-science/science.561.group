Revolution, in [anarchist](../_index.md) use, is the fundamental transformation of social relations — the dismantling of [hierarchy](./hierarchy.md), [authority](./authority.md), and [domination](./domination.md) and their replacement with [voluntary association](./voluntary-association.md), [mutual aid](./mutual-aid.md), and [self-organization](./self-organization.md). It is not the replacement of one set of rulers with another.

This distinguishes anarchist revolution from two other common meanings. In liberal use, "revolution" often means a dramatic political change — the American Revolution replaced British colonial government with an elected republic, but preserved [property](./property.md), [class](./class.md), slavery, and the state. In Marxist-Leninist use, revolution means the seizure of [state power](./the-state.md) by the working class (or a party claiming to represent it), which then uses the state to reorganize the economy. Anarchists argue that both of these are incomplete or self-defeating: the first changes the personnel but not the structure; the second changes the structure of the economy but preserves the structure of the state — and whoever controls the state becomes the new ruling [class](./class.md).

The anarchist argument is structural: [hierarchical](./hierarchy.md) means produce hierarchical outcomes. A revolution organized through a centralized party produces a centralized state. A revolution that seizes the existing machinery of government inherits that machinery's logic of command and [obedience](./obedience.md). This is why [prefigurative politics](./prefigurative-politics.md) is not merely a preference but a necessity: the only revolution that can produce non-hierarchical social relations is one that practices non-hierarchical social relations in the process of making them. The revolution is not an event that happens after which freedom begins — it is the practice of [freedom](./freedom.md) itself, happening now, in every act of [self-organization](./self-organization.md), [refusal](./refusal.md), [solidarity](./solidarity.md), and [direct action](./direct-action.md).

This does not mean anarchist revolution is gradualist or reformist. [Insurrectionary anarchism](./insurrectionary-anarchism.md) insists on the immediacy of revolutionary action — not waiting for conditions to be right, not building a party first, not petitioning for reforms. [The general strike](./the-general-strike.md), [direct action](./direct-action.md), and the construction of [dual power](./dual-power.md) — autonomous institutions that meet real needs outside the state and [capitalism](./capitalism.md) — are all revolutionary practices. What makes them revolutionary is not their scale but their logic: they operate outside the structures of [domination](./domination.md) rather than seeking to capture them.
