# Witness, acknowledgment, attention, and obligation under electoralism

**Research question.** Under what social and institutional conditions does a person who has witnessed an injustice acknowledge it through an electoral act, then experience or receive social permission to withdraw continuing attention while the relevant obligation remains unmet?

**Claim status.** This is an unreviewed, hypothesis-generating research note. The distinction among acts is conceptual. The mechanism of *electoral acknowledgment as perceived discharge* is a proposed, falsifiable explanation, **not** an established empirical effect of voting. The experiments below have not been run. This research does not advise a vote, a candidate, a party, or electoral abstention.

**Provenance and custody.** The initiating 2026-10-14 561 Group demand explicitly distinguishes witnessing, acknowledging, attending, and owing. This paper is owned by Lenticule Science, Political Science (OECD FORD 5.6), with social-theory, relationality, and attention-economics dependencies. It neither republishes nor modifies emsenn's separately authored essay [*Between Care and Control*](https://emsenn.substack.com/p/between-care-and-control) (2025-03-25), whose critique of electoralism and administrative theories of care is a motivating interpretive source, not evidence that the proposed causal mechanism occurs.

## 1. Distinct relations, not a ladder

The existing [relationality account of witnessing](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/witnessing.md) treats witnessing as an *evidence-side* act. The [relationality term Recognition](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/recognition.md) describes a different *form-side* act, classification under a system's categories, and expressly distinguishes it from everyday acknowledgment. Political-science inquiry must not collapse these into a single political-engagement measure.

**Witnessing (W).** An attributable source bears affirmative or negative evidence about a situated relation before a possible addressee: direct testimony, a contemporaneous record, or mediated witnessing of another's testimony. Witnessing is more than a raw stimulus hitting an eye. It does not certify the truth of everything attested and does not entail reception.

**Acknowledgment (K).** An addressee produces an attributable uptake of testimony or of the claim at issue. Uptake modes must be distinguished: receipt ("I heard you"), awareness ("I know about it"), acceptance ("I affirm this occurred"), and formal statement of concern. Acknowledgment is not necessarily agreement, and none of those stances guarantees continuing expenditure or redress. Acknowledging a specific speaker is also not identical to asserting a general policy preference.

**Recognition (R).** A system registers a claim as fitting a category of evidence, harm, entitlement, jurisdiction, or responsibility. It may classify a claim while rejecting its truth; it may affirm its truth while denying jurisdiction. Do not conflate this structural act with acknowledgment by ordinary speakers, nor with the colonial politics of recognition critiqued by Coulthard, Simpson, and others.

**Attention (A).** Actual allocation of scarce capacity across time: active inquiry, evidence review, monitoring, deliberation, organizing labor, material resources spent on understanding or responding. Attention has duration, opportunity cost, and direction. Not every unit of attention is effective, just, or reparative; obsession, propaganda, and performative debate also consume it. Stated interest and observed expenditure belong in different evidentiary classes.

**Attention's existing mathematical constraint.** Obsmetrologia's [`AttentionAllocation.lean`](https://github.com/lenticule-science/obsmetrologia/blob/main/Obsmetrologia/Cohomology/AttentionAllocation.lean) formalizes a bounded attention budget, reconciliation costs, captured benefits, and residual obstructions. Its proved arithmetic constraints make clear why attention cannot be conjured from an acknowledgment, and why spending scarce capacity on one issue can leave another unresolved. This is an *analogy and candidate interface* for political attention, not a proof of moral duty, causal efficacy, or any population-level electoral effect. Translating `Obstruction.cost` and `Obstruction.benefit` to measured social processes would require independent definitions and validation.

**Obligation (O).** A normative relation binding an actor or collective to some action, forbearance, or ongoing care under conditions that must be specified. In the 561 Group's [Emergent Obligation](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/emergent-obligation.md), obligations arise from situated relations, not from the prior completion of procedures. Distinguish their existence from an actor's knowledge, belief, acceptance, capacity to fulfill, or moral/legal liability. Witnessing may *reveal* such a relation; witnessing alone is not proof that the same obligation belongs to everyone who sees it.

The five predicates are analytically separable; the notation below is a conceptual reading aid, **not** a new canonical machine schema or a proven formal model:

- `W(source, relation, evidence, polarity, time)`
- `K(addressee, claim-or-testimony, uptake-mode, time)`
- `R(authority-or-system, claim, category, time)`
- `A(actor, concern, interval, resources, activity)`
- `O(owing-actor, beneficiary, required-conduct, grounds, conditions, time)`

No universal implication `W => K => A => fulfillment(O)` is licensed. A newspaper can publish a witnessed injury that officials ignore; officials can acknowledge it but devote no time to it; an agency can categorize it as a complaint but never allocate resources; sustained investigation can occur without acknowledging the injured person's account; a bystander may already owe assistance before any public testimony exists. Fulfilling one obligation does not mean all others vanish.

### Sources and normative limits

An account of obligation must say *to whom*, *for what conduct*, *on what ground*, *under which conditions*, and *with what capacity/authority*. The same event may impose different obligations on an institution responsible for causing it, an officeholder empowered to remedy it, a neighbor situated to help, and a person exposed only to news of it. Neither an injured person's testimony nor their suffering is a blank check to assign limitless work to them. Conflicts among obligations, risk, ability, non-transferability and consent are substantive—not excuses to infer duty from mere perception.

## 2. Electoralism as an institutional grammar, not as voting itself

For this inquiry, **electoralism** denotes the orientation that treats electoral procedures as the primary, sufficient, or uniquely legitimate medium for expressing and settling political demands. **Voting** is one action that can occur within, but does not necessarily instantiate, that orientation. A ballot can materially affect authority and policy, can coexist with other modes of political practice, and can initiate further organizing. The hypothesis concerns the *interpretation of completion*, not an assertion that ballots do nothing.

**Proposed electoral acknowledgment-discharge mechanism:**

1. An issue is witnessed and testimony circulates (W). Its credibility and distribution may be unequal.
2. People and institutions acknowledge the issue in language, public identity, campaign promises, or policy-platform categories (K and sometimes R).
3. Electoral participation is offered or interpreted as an authorized, socially legible response: one votes, endorses a representative, or supports a campaign. The action is real but generally addresses a bundle of issues and a representative-selection process, not a single fully specified obligation.
4. A person or audience treats participation as *perceived completion* ("I did my part"). The social setting supplies a public receipt of proper participation; attention can be reallocated without social sanction.
5. The affected relation and any continuing obligation do not necessarily change. Unless a specified obligation has been fulfilled, validly waived by its holder, or appropriately delegated with the required acceptance and residual accountability, *perceived discharge* and *normative discharge* diverge.

The critical seam is between steps 3 and 4: an institutional participation receipt becomes a **socially accepted closure claim** without an independent test of what was owed and what was done. Electoral administration counts valid votes and authorizes officeholders; it does not itself record whether a voter's distinct duty to a neighbor, a harmed community, or an institution's beneficiaries has been met.

This is a candidate **closure-by-representation** mechanism, not a claim that representatives cannot perform obligations. Accountable delegation may be an excellent way to meet a collective obligation. It requires attention to which office or actor accepted which task, with what authority, and how affected parties can verify performance. Even accepted delegation may retain residual duties of supervision or solidarity. A voter cannot unilaterally waive a beneficiary's claim merely by choosing a representative; the existing 561 Group [Hohfeldian Waiver](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/social-science-services-section/services/political-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/03/20/2026-03-20-hohfeldian-waiver-2026-03-20.md) distinguishes the right-holder's authorized waiver from an obligor's self-release.

### A non-entailment result

From `Voted(actor, election)`, we cannot logically derive `Accepted(delegate, obligation)`, `Satisfied(obligation)`, or `NoFurtherAttentionNeeded(actor, relation)`. The election event omits the obligation's identity, beneficiary, acceptance, performance conditions, and residual custody. This is a type/identification problem, not a theorem that voting is morally insufficient in all cases. A specified civic obligation *to vote* may indeed be satisfied by voting; many other obligations require different evidence.

## 3. Relation to existing literature

The proposed mechanism has **partial** scholarly analogues. Their populations, activities, and outcomes are different; **none should be represented as a direct experimental demonstration that voting absolves voters**.

- **Moral licensing and socially visible token support.** Kristofferson, White, and Peloza (2014), [doi:10.1086/674137](https://doi.org/10.1086/674137), find that the public/private visibility of initial token support matters for later prosocial behavior. Kim and colleagues (2024), [doi:10.1177/14614448231153971](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448231153971), experimentally examine clicktivism and donation. These are mechanisms and measurement examples involving token public support, **not ballots**.
- **Commitment, not just absolution.** Moussaoui and colleagues (2022), [doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2022.783995](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.783995), report that prosocial versus impression-management motivation moderates the later effect of small support actions; stronger subsequent engagement is possible. Robinson and colleagues (2026), [doi:10.1002/casp.70230](https://doi.org/10.1002/casp.70230), find settings where voluntary symbolic action increases identification and subsequent collective action. A theory that always predicts withdrawal would disregard these findings.
- **Expressive voting.** Studies of expressive voting investigate benefits from expressing identity or ideological affinity apart from instrumentally changing an election. The laboratory evidence in [doi:10.1016/j.ejpoleco.2016.12.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpoleco.2016.12.001) is mixed. Expressive benefits could enable the proposed completion interpretation but do not establish it.
- **Political responsibility beyond guilt.** Iris Marion Young's [social connection model](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195392388.003.0004) treats responsibility for structural injustice as forward-looking and concerned with collective transformation rather than assigning guilt. McKeown (2018), [Oxford repository](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A9f7541d5-7086-42dc-8b4c-a51e34163c19), and Gunnemyr (2020), [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/hypatia/article/why-the-social-connection-model-fails-participation-is-neither-necessary-nor-sufficient-for-political-responsibility/99265732F023B1E7BDD1493DE80C2FA8), qualify the grounds and scope of that model. These debates help distinguish an obligation's existence from its bearer and mode of fulfillment.
- **Voting versus other political practices.** Finkel's panel study of West German participation, [doi:10.2307/2131308](https://doi.org/10.2307/2131308), reports different effects on efficacy and regime support for voting, campaigning and protest, using 1970s data. A newer panel account of varied non-electoral trajectories, [doi:10.1057/s41269-023-00325-3](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41269-023-00325-3), cautions against assuming a single trajectory after electoral events. Mechanisms and populations must be qualified.
- **Public inquiry and continuing office.** Dewey's *The Public and Its Problems* (1927) and later [office-based readings](https://direct.mit.edu/ecps/article/7/1/75/126134/John-Dewey-on-public-office-and-representative) offer a conceptual comparison in which civic responsibility extends beyond choosing representatives into continuous inquiry and public oversight.

The 561 Group's existing [Proceduralism](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/proceduralism.md) supplies an internal comparison: procedural legitimacy and relational adequacy are different evaluative questions. Our [Recognition Refusal](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/recognition-refusal.md) further cautions that forced recognition within settler institutions cannot be assumed to be the sole legitimate path to action or obligation. That scholarship must not be collapsed into a claim that Indigenous communities have one uniform preferred relation to elections.

## 4. Competing hypotheses and falsification

**H1 — Electoral completion framing.** With otherwise comparable witnessing and acknowledgment, people experimentally exposed to a framing that depicts electoral participation as *having completed their response* will report less remaining felt responsibility and, when offered a real bounded chance, devote less subsequent attention than people exposed to a framing that explicitly preserves continuing responsibility. Prediction concerns the *completion frame*, not mere voting status.

**H2 — Electoral mobilization/commitment.** Electoral engagement can also raise identification, confidence, information-seeking, organized participation or attention. A positive effect, no effect, or different effect by setting is admissible. H2 directly competes with any universal substitution account.

**H3 — Accountable delegation.** Clear evidence of a delegate's accepted task, oversight rights, and independent performance readback may allow a legitimate redistribution of attention without false absolution. Here reduced personal effort is not necessarily negligence; it may accompany increased collective efficacy. Distinguish supervised delegation from unverified displacement.

**H4 — Constraint-driven withdrawal.** Attention may decrease because of lack of time, disability, socioeconomic constraint, threat, issue fatigue, access barriers or competing duties. Self-declared completion is not a sufficient explanation absent evidence and controls. Declining personal effort cannot simply be treated as moral failure.

**H0 — No electoral-completion effect** beyond these alternatives under the measured conditions.

**Potential falsifiers:** if manipulating completion framing produces no distinguishable belief or behavioral differences after preregistered controls; if participants who interpret voting as sufficient nonetheless maintain equal or greater issue-directed attention; if accountability arrangements explain the measured decline without perceived self-release; if the effect disappears under matched costs or credible alternative participation modes. Even a supported H1 would not establish a universal theory of electoralism.

## 5. Study design: distinguish the events in time

**Design A — nonpartisan preregistered vignette plus behavioral choice.** Use a fictional, non-electoralized community infrastructure problem with documented harm; give every consenting adult participant the same witness evidence, then independently vary (a) acknowledgment requirement (recorded private receipt vs public declaration), (b) civic-response framing (an election-related response described as complete vs one described as beginning an accountability process vs no election reference), and (c) explicitly receipted delegation/oversight. Randomize across conditions; do **not** ask anyone to choose or change a real vote. Outcomes: a multi-item measure of perceived remaining responsibility, recognition of distinct bearers and capacities, and a real optional time-allocation choice (reading an evidence update, filing an anonymous structured follow-up, or disengaging). Respect consent, privacy, and the option to do nothing.

The primary estimand is the difference in *post-acknowledgment issue-directed attention* attributable to the completion frame relative to an otherwise matched continuing-responsibility frame. Measure whether attention changes because participants believe an obligation was met; do not infer that mediation solely from a correlated survey item. Preregister the causal contrast and primary outcomes, identify manipulation-check failure, power/sample targets from pilot variance, exclusion criteria, and null/alternative analyses *before* collecting observations.

**Design B — longitudinal observational comparison.** Follow issue-specific attention across electoral and non-electoral intervals, where feasible and consented, separating people who only cast ballots, who combine ballots with organized activity, and who act without electoral participation. Record pre-existing attention, time budgets, issue salience, perceived efficacy, employment/care obligations, institutional responsiveness, and political opportunity. Treat selection and reverse causation as central; such associations alone do not identify a voting effect.

**Unit and event definitions.** Do not code news exposure as confirmed witnessing, a like or poll response as acknowledgment of a harmed person, words of concern as time expended, nor hours spent as verified benefit. Use separate witness/evidence, acknowledgment/stance, recognition/category, attention/cost, obligation/grounds, and outcome/performance records, each time-indexed with provenance. Define whose report and whose obligation are being measured. Report both positive and negative controls and null results.

**Ethical constraints.** The study is about a social mechanism, not a tool to increase or suppress anyone's turnout. No issue targeting tied to actual voter rolls, protected identities or imminent elections. Do not place the obligation to repair harm preferentially on the people already harmed; do not collect unnecessarily identifiable political beliefs. All participants can decline follow-up.

## 6. Interpretation and research receiving

The useful theoretical result would not be "voting is bad." It would be a conditional account of how a political institution converts a **witnessed relation** into an **acknowledged concern**, issues a **legible participatory receipt**, and sometimes allows a **perception of fulfilled obligation** without checking the **actual attention, acceptance and performance** the relation requires.

This account must always distinguish:

1. Evidence that harm or another relation was witnessed.
2. Evidence that it was heard, affirmed, denied or classified.
3. Evidence that someone actually devoted scarce attention to it.
4. The normative ground of a specified obligation and who bears it.
5. Evidence of fulfillment, valid waiver, accepted delegation and any continuing remainder.

A socially accepted performance of responsibility is not the same proposition as completed responsibility; conversely, continued personal labor is not the only lawful or effective discharge. Both errors must be testable rather than assumed away.

**Receiving criterion for this paper:** acceptance as a qualified working hypothesis after review of its definitional compatibility with relationality and emergent obligation, evidence citations, independent competing accounts, and an empirically executable study protocol. Its presence in the repository is neither social validation nor empirical confirmation. Later Engineering and Operations consumers should extend source-owned terms and event semantics, not invent a redundant social-science schema or certify causal impact from a content change.
