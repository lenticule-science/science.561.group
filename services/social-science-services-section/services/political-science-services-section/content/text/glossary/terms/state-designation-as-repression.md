State-designation-as-repression names the use of a state's formal designation power — terrorist-organization listing, proscription, or analogous administrative blacklisting — as an instrument to suppress or disable a political opponent, movement, or its support network, rather than, or in addition to, as a counter-terrorism measure targeting violent conduct; comparative research on proscription regimes documents that designation power is exercised "extremely widely — although inconsistently — across the globe" (Jarvis and Legrand 2018).

This differs from a designation's own stated legal predicate — a specific finding that the designated party committed or supports violent or terrorist acts — in that the political-science question is not whether a given designation is legally defensible under its own statute, but what function the designation performs against a broader landscape of movements, dissent, and government targets, a question comparative-proscription scholarship treats as answerable independent of, and sometimes in tension with, any single designation's formal legal justification, noting that "proscription may reflect political bias" (Jarvis and Legrand 2018).

Classifying a designation by function rather than solely by its statutory predicate lets comparable cases be grouped across otherwise very different legal regimes and targets — a decentralized political tendency designated as a domestic terrorist entity, and a communications-infrastructure provider designated as a global terrorist entity, are each classifiable by the comparative literature as targeting a political tendency's support network, independent of, and without this term resolving, either designation's own legal validity. See [a research note on the designation of a communications provider](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/09/08/2026-09-08-note-on-the-designation-of-a-communications-provider.md), which records both the 2025 US designation of Antifa and the 2026 designation of Autistici/Inventati as parallel instances in this literature, and records where first-party sources on each conflict or remain unconfirmed [@jarvislegrand2018; @whitehouse2025antifa].
