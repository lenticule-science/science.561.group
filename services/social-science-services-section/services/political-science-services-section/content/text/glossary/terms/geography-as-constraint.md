**Geography as constraint** names the thesis, popularized for a general readership by [Tim Marshall](../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/tim-marshall.md), that physical terrain sets durable limits on a state's available strategic options that persist across changes of government, ideology, or leadership. Recurring patterns in a state's foreign policy — Russia's historical anxiety about its flat, unbuffered western approach, or China's need to secure mountainous and desert land borders while its coast remains exposed — are read as consequences of terrain that any government controlling that territory would face, rather than as choices specific to a particular regime.

The thesis is a popularization of a longer tradition of geopolitical and geographic-determinist reasoning; geographers have criticized its application to specific contemporary questions as reductive, since institutions, technology, and political choice also shape what a state does within its terrain's constraints, not only the terrain itself. It shares its underlying assumption with [geopolitical forecasting](./geopolitical-forecasting.md) that durable structural factors, not leadership or ideology, are the primary determinant of state behavior.
