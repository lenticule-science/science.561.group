A **G-Zero world**, in [Ian Bremmer](../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/ian-bremmer.md)'s framework, is a global order in which no single nation, and no durable coalition of nations, has both the capacity and the political will to set and drive a global agenda. It is offered as the successor condition to earlier framings of international leadership — a unipolar American-led order, or a G7- or G2-style concert of powers — describing a state of affairs in which transnational problems (pandemics, climate change, financial instability, armed conflict) go chronically under-addressed not because no solution exists, but because no actor is willing to bear the cost of leading a coordinated response while others could free-ride on it.

The term names an absence — of will and capacity jointly, not of raw power alone — which distinguishes it from simple multipolarity, in which several powers might still coordinate through an accepted framework. Bremmer's G-Zero and [Ray Dalio](../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/ray-dalio.md)'s changing-world-order framework (see [long-term debt cycle](../../../../../economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/long-term-debt-cycle.md)) are read as complementary namings of the same period of multipolar disorder from political-science and economic-cycle vantage points respectively.
