# Ancestry tree

Tree of all backflow expansions of a recognition.

## Definition
- Nodes: recognitions reachable by applying backflow steps from a target.
- Edges: backflow transitions consistent with discipline/nucleus.

## Invariants
- Finiteness/branching as governed by admissible backflows.
- Collapse: branches may merge under nucleus equivalence.

## Proof
- Uses: backflow mechanics to expand ancestors.
- Shows that: ancestral provenance forms a tree-like carrier.

## Relation to this section's founding note

This structure is not about human ancestors — "ancestors" here names
prior recognitions reachable by backflow from a target recognition, in
the relationality corpus's own formalism, and "ancestry tree" names the
whole reachable set as a tree carrier. It was collected anyway because it
is the estate's one prior formal use of *ancestry* as a structural term,
and because the shape of the claim — that provenance naturally forms a
tree, with branches merging under equivalence — is a plausible adjacent
structure to test against the founding note's own claim that a settled
section's charges persist within the community sheaf's structure
(including its H¹).

Whether a settled human ancestor's provenance literally forms a tree of
this kind (as opposed to the richer sheaf/gluing structure the founding
note actually uses) is an open question this collected fragment does not
resolve — it is offered as a smaller, already-worked-out formalism worth
checking the founding note's claims against, not as a competing theory of
ancestry in this section's sense.
