Ilinx (from the Greek for "whirlpool") is Caillois's category of play
built on the deliberate pursuit of vertigo — a momentary destabilization
of stable perception achieved through spinning, falling, rapid
acceleration, or other disorienting physical sensation. Children
spinning until they collapse dizzy, swings, carousels, skiing, and
extreme rides are Caillois's paradigm cases: the pleasure is located in
the disruption of ordinary bodily orientation itself, sought for its
own sake rather than as a byproduct of some other activity.

Caillois considered ilinx important enough to name it, alongside
mimicry, in the original French subtitle of his study: *le masque et le
vertige*, "the mask and vertigo." He read the two as a closely related
pair — both offer an escape from ordinary, stable selfhood, mimicry by
fictional displacement of identity and ilinx by physiological
disruption of perception — set against agon and alea, which leave
ordinary selfhood and perception intact and instead stage a contest
over merit or fortune.

Ilinx sits furthest, of the four categories, from the calculated,
rule-governed pole of Caillois's paidia–ludus continuum: vertigo play
tends toward paidia (tumultuous, unstructured exuberance) rather than
ludus (disciplined, rule-bound structure), since the pursuit of
disorientation resists the kind of stable rule-following that
converts raw play into a formal game.
