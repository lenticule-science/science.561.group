Geography is the discipline that takes *places* as its primary subject: not what is generally true of all settlements or all watersheds, but what is true *of this place*. Its method is descriptive and empirical first — what is here, where is it, what flows through it — and analytical second, drawing on physical geography, human geography, and the regional tradition that holds them together.

Children of this theory are individual places (towns, cities, watersheds, regions) studied as such. Each child theory is a corpus of empirically-witnessed facts and open questions about its place, with sources cited; cross-place patterns belong in this `geography/` parent rather than in any individual place theory.
