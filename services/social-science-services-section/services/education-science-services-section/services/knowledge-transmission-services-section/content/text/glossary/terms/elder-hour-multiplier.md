The **elder-hour multiplier** is a design principle answering second-language acquisition's brutal arithmetic: acquisition requires far more interaction volume than scheduled classes can supply, and for a language like Lakota the pool of fluent first speakers is small, aging, and irreplaceable — speaker-hours are the scarcest resource in the entire design. The principle: agents must never be positioned as a replacement for speaker time. Their role is to multiply the value of each hour a fluent speaker gives.

The division of labor this implies:

- **Drill, recall, and structural checking are handled by agents and by the language system's own mechanical verification.** These are exactly the tasks that do not require a fluent human — repetition, spaced review, and confirming that a produced sentence is structurally well formed can all be automated.
- **Time with a real speaker is reserved for what only a speaker can give**: voice, prosody, story, and cultural protocol. These cannot be synthesized or automated without becoming a different, lesser thing — and the design that proposes this principle treats synthesizing a speaker's voice without their standing consent as categorically forbidden, not merely undesirable.
- **Every recorded speaker session is transcribed, aligned, and admitted into the corpus.** A single hour of a grandmother teaching one learner becomes, once transcribed and aligned, a resource available to every future learner — documentation as a direct byproduct of teaching, closing the loop with the [learning-documentation flywheel](../../../../../learning-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/learning-documentation-flywheel.md).

This is a formalization of a pattern already visible in [embodied transmission](./embodied-transmission.md) and [oral transmission](./oral-transmission.md) — that some knowledge cannot be transmitted except through sustained, direct relationship with a practitioner — combined with a specific claim about where automation belongs and does not belong in that relationship. It differs from ordinary [apprenticeship](./apprenticeship.md) mainly in adding a mechanical layer explicitly scoped to protect, rather than substitute for, the human relationship at the center.

The principle is a design hypothesis stated in a 2026-08-21 draft note, not yet built or tested against real tutoring sessions with fluent speakers. It should be read as a stated design commitment — what the system is intended to do with speaker time — rather than as an evidenced claim about how much value such multiplication actually produces.
