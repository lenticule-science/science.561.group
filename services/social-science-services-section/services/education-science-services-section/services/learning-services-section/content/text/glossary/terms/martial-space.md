**Martial space** is a design metaphor for the learner: not a store of facts to be filled, but a repertoire of composable moves the learner can make and understand, developed and tested the way a martial art develops and tests a practitioner's movement vocabulary.

The metaphor maps four familiar martial-arts structures onto four learning-design roles:

- **A form is a derivation practiced until its legs commute.** In the design dialogue's companion geometry (round two, "mastery is a commuting cone"), an item is mastered when its reading, listening, speaking, and kin/protocol legs all agree — production matches comprehension matches audition. A form-drill is repeated practice aimed at making those legs agree, the same way a martial form is repeated until its component movements flow into each other without conscious sequencing.
- **Sparring is conversation under pressure.** Free conversation, with a real interlocutor and real stakes, is where composed moves are tested against resistance rather than rehearsed in isolation — the analogue of live sparring against a form practiced solo.
- **Dojo grading is the house's own evidence-grading discipline.** A rank is not claimed on the practitioner's say-so; it is awarded against a checkable standard. Applied to language learning, this is the same discipline as [evidence-graded curriculum](../../../../../pedagogy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/evidence-graded-curriculum.md): a claimed skill level carries an explicit grade — built and checked, evidenced but unchecked, or merely scoped — rather than a flat "learned it" flag.

The metaphor is paired, in its source dialogue, with a second science aimed at the language itself rather than the learner (round three names this "the physics of the language"); martial space covers only the learner-facing science, named there "the kinematics of the learner." Both are stated as sharing one measurement loop: the learner's practice generates the witnessed observations that the language science works from, and the language science in turn sharpens the instrument used to measure learners.

As with the geometry it draws on, martial space is a design metaphor stated in draft notes, not a tested pedagogical framework. It should be read as organizing vocabulary for a proposed system, graded no higher than Research-grade until practice records exist to check the commuting-legs claim of mastery against.
