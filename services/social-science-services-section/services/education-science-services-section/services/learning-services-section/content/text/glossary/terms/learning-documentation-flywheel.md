The **learning-documentation flywheel** is a design pattern in which every tutoring interaction is, at the same time, a documentation event. When a learner and a more fluent relative or community member work through a lesson together, the resulting recording is simultaneously three things: the learner's lesson, a witnessed corpus entry carrying speaker, kin-position, dialect, register, and date, and a data point in the time-series of the living language. Learning generates documentation; documentation feeds learning — the two are not sequential stages but the same act observed from two directions.

The pattern departs deliberately from data flywheels built around engagement. A flywheel that grows engagement optimizes for time spent in an app; a learning-documentation flywheel optimizes for growth of the language itself, with engagement as a byproduct rather than the target.

Two design commitments give the pattern its shape:

- **The corpus is read as a sheaf over the speech community**, not a single authoritative table. Speakers and situations are treated as regions; agreement between overlapping regions is dialect coherence; disagreement is not noise to be resolved but exactly the interesting data. Variation is stored as signal, never as error.
- **Orthographies and dialect variants are charts on one manifold with explicit transition maps**, rather than rival claimants for a single standard. A system built this way can serve a dialect continuum (the proposing note's motivating case is the Lakota–Dakota continuum and its competing orthographies) without forcing a normative flattening that a single "correct" corpus entry would require.

This second commitment is the concrete alternative to the extractive pattern the design explicitly reacts against — outside parties asserting ownership over a community's own recorded speech and selling it back. In a learning-documentation flywheel, contribution provenance and consent status travel with every corpus entry as a structural requirement, not an added policy.

The pattern is a design hypothesis stated in a 2026-08-21 draft note, not yet implemented or practiced against real tutoring sessions. It self-grades Research-grade under the house evidence-grading discipline (see [evidence-graded curriculum](../../../../../pedagogy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/evidence-graded-curriculum.md)) until a working system produces practice records that can be checked.
