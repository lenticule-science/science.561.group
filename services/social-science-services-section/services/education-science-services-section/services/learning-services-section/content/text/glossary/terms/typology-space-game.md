The **typology-space game** is a design proposal for making an observed but informally-described skill of polyglots — that they learn the space of languages rather than languages one at a time — into an explicit, playable game board.

The board's coordinates are WALS-style typological features: word order, morphosyntactic alignment, evidentiality marking, tone, classifier systems, and the like. A learner's progress is shown as covered territory and unexplored territory across that space rather than as a per-language percentage. Because typological features recur across unrelated languages, competence gained on one feature transfers: a learner who has acquired SOV word order and person-marking morphology through Lakota should find those same features cheaper to acquire again in Quechua or Japanese; a learner who has acquired tone and classifier systems through Mandarin should find those features cheaper in the languages that share them. Each newly learned language is proposed to discount the cost of its typological neighbors, turning "absurdly polyglottal" from an unexplained personal talent into a completable atlas.

The proposal's underlying mathematics, developed alongside it, treats meanings and frames as a shared base space, individual languages as fibers over that base, and learning a new language as transport of already-held competence into a new fiber. Read this way, **obstruction-driven curriculum** falls out directly: whatever fails to transport cleanly from a learner's known languages into a target language — the features with no counterpart in anything the learner already knows, such as evidentiality for a monolingual English speaker approaching Quechua — is exactly the material that needs explicit teaching, rather than material a generic syllabus would have to guess at.

This proposal is a design hypothesis from a 2026-08-21 draft note. Its closest link to the working theory's falsifiable obligations is obligation 3 — that curvature-derived difficulty predicts observed L2 error distributions — since transport failure and typological curvature are proposed (in the same note's fuller geometric account) to be the same underlying quantity. Until that obligation runs against real learner error data, the typology-space game names a design target and a measurement plan, not a demonstrated learning aid.
