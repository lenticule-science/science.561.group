The [banking model](./banking-model.md) of education, as described by [Paulo Freire](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/paulo-freire.md) in *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* [@freire_PedagogyOppressed_1970], characterizes conventional teaching as a process of depositing information into passive recipients. The teacher possesses knowledge; the student receives it. The more faithfully the student stores and reproduces the deposit, the more successful the education.

[Freire](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/paulo-freire.md) argued that this model mirrors and reinforces structures of domination: it trains people to accept the world as it is presented to them rather than to question, investigate, or transform it. The banking model treats knowledge as a fixed quantity to be transmitted, not as something produced through inquiry, dialogue, and struggle.

[Freire](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/paulo-freire.md)'s alternative is [dialogic education](./dialogic-education.md), in which teacher and student investigate problems together.
