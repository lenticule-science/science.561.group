# Learn Decolonial Pedagogy

Decolonial pedagogy asks not just *what* is being taught but *whose* knowledge is being treated as legitimate, and how the structure of teaching reproduces or refuses the banking model — Paulo Freire's name for the practice in which a teacher deposits knowledge into passive students.

## When to use

You are evaluating an educational practice (a curriculum, a class, a library classification, a database schema, a training program) and want to see what it does at the level of who-knows and how-knowing-flows, not just at the level of content.

## How it works

### The banking model

In the banking model, knowledge is treated as a possession of the teacher to be deposited into the learner. The learner's role is to receive. Recognize it by: who speaks, who is silent, who is positioned as having the questions, who is positioned as having the answers, what counts as understanding (recall and reproduction).

### Problem-posing education

Freire's alternative: teaching as the joint posing of problems whose answers neither party fully holds in advance. The structure is dialogic; both teacher and learner are agents of inquiry. The shift is structural, not just stylistic — a "Socratic" lecture that already has the answers it's leading toward is still banking.

### Whose knowledge?

Every knowledge system centers some knowledge and excludes other knowledge. A curriculum, a library classification, a database schema, a vocabulary set — each makes a choice. The decolonial question is to make that choice visible and to ask whether the exclusions are honest.

### Decolonization is not a metaphor

Eve Tuck and K. Wayne Yang's corrective: "decolonization" is not a synonym for diversifying or critically examining. It names a specific demand — the repatriation of land and life to Indigenous people. Borrowing the word for general critique without engaging that demand is a settler move that the framework itself names.

## Prerequisites

None. This is a starting point.

## Verification

Pick an educational practice you've experienced. Describe it in banking-model terms: who holds the knowledge, how does it flow, what role does the learner play? Then describe what a problem-posing alternative would look like. Identify one epistemological assumption the practice makes — what kind of knowledge does it treat as legitimate, and what does it exclude?
