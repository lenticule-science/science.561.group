[Mutual aid pedagogy](./mutual-aid-pedagogy.md) is a form of learning organized through reciprocal support rather than hierarchical instruction. People teach what they know and learn what they don't, without fixed roles of teacher and student. The practice draws on [Pyotr Kropotkin](../../../../general/people/pyotr-kropotkin.md)'s account of mutual aid as a factor of evolution — the observation that cooperation, not competition, sustains communities and enables collective flourishing [@kropotkin_MutualAid_1902].

In mutual aid pedagogy, the distinction between teacher and learner dissolves or rotates. A study group where participants take turns presenting material, a skill-share where community members teach practical abilities, a reading circle where no one leads and everyone contributes — these are mutual aid pedagogical forms. The assumption is not that no one has expertise, but that expertise is distributed and that learning flows in multiple directions.

This approach appears across traditions: in [anarchist pedagogies](../schools/anarchist-pedagogies/index.md) as a political commitment to non-hierarchical education, in [Black radical pedagogies](../schools/black-radical-pedagogies/index.md) through study groups and [freedom schools](./freedom-school.md) organized by the community for the community, and in [Indigenous pedagogies](../schools/indigenous-pedagogies/index.md) through the relational transmission of knowledge across generations and between community members.

Contemporary practitioners include [Mariame Kaba](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/mariame-kaba.md), whose abolitionist study groups model mutual aid pedagogy in practice — participants learn together, share resources, and build collective capacity without a fixed authority [@kaba_WeDoThisTilWeFreeUs_2021].

## Related terms

- [dialogic education](./dialogic-education.md) — Freire's account of teacher and student as co-investigators, a related but more formalized concept
- [popular education](./popular-education.md) — community-based education that often uses mutual aid methods
- [prefigurative education](./prefigurative-education.md) — education that enacts the social relations it seeks to create
