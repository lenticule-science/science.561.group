# The evidence for family literacy programs

Binding Generations' workshops bring children and the adults in their lives
together to make books. This note sets out what can honestly be claimed for
that kind of program.

## Two meta-analyses, one lesson

- **Sénéchal and Young (2008)**, *Review of Educational Research* 78(4),
  880–907 ([doi:10.3102/0034654308320319](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654308320319)):
  16 intervention studies, 1,340 families, children from kindergarten to grade 3.
  Overall effect on reading, d = 0.65. The effect depended on what parents did:
  - parents **tutoring** specific literacy skills produced the largest effects;
  - parents **listening** to their children read produced moderate effects;
  - parents **reading to** their children, in the three studies of it, produced
    no significant gain in reading.
- **van Steensel, McElvany, Kurvers and Herppich (2011)**, *Review of
  Educational Research* 81(1), 69–96
  ([doi:10.3102/0034654310388819](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654310388819)):
  30 studies (1990–2010), 47 samples. Overall d = 0.18 (comprehension-related
  0.22, code-related 0.17), a small effect, and no moderator explained
  differences between programs. The authors suggest larger estimates elsewhere
  are driven by outlying studies and narrow outcome measures.

The two estimates, 0.65 and 0.18, differ because the studies included differ.
Neither refutes the other; both are kept. They agree on one thing a program
designer can use: effects follow **active adult–child literacy work**, not
exposure alone.

## The national evaluation that found nothing

The federal Even Start Family Literacy Program combined adult education,
parenting education and early childhood education. Its experimental national
evaluation (18 projects, 463 families, two years) found "no statistically
significant or educationally important impacts" on child literacy, parent
literacy, or parent–child interaction ([ERIC ED410037](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED410037)).
Analysts attributed the result to low participation intensity: families
attended far fewer hours than designed. The program was later defunded
([*Georgetown Journal on Poverty Law and Policy*](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/poverty-journal/wp-content/uploads/sites/25/2019/02/25-3-The-Even-Start-Literacy-Program.pdf)).
Dose matters. A single workshop is not a literacy intervention.

## Intergenerational and book-making programs

- **Intergenerational programs.** Programs pairing elders and children
  (reminiscence, storytelling, grandparent storytimes) report closer bonds, less
  age stereotyping and less loneliness among elders. The literature is mostly
  qualitative and small-sample, and rarely measures reading
  ([ILA Reporter on intergenerational library programming](https://www.ila.org/publications/ila-reporter/article/119/intergenerational-programming-in-libraries-building-bridges-between-age-groups-w)).
- **Indigenous programs.** Digital storytelling that pairs Elders with students
  is documented in the *Journal of Intergenerational Relationships* (2021).
- **Book-making.** Making books and zines with children appears in library
  practice write-ups (ALA's Programming Librarian; the ALSC Blog), not as a
  measured intervention. No effect size exists for it.

The non-profit National Center for Families Learning evaluates its own programs
with surveys and interviews and reports parent and child gains
([NCFL](https://familieslearning.org/about/research-evaluation/)). That is a
program self-evaluation, a different tier of evidence from the meta-analyses.

## What an honest workshop can claim

A family bookbinding workshop can honestly claim:

- that it puts adults and children in **active, shared literacy work**, making
  and writing a book together, which is the kind of activity the effective
  programs share;
- that it builds the **relationship and belonging** outcomes intergenerational
  programs report; and
- that it gives families a **made object** to read at home, which invites the
  repeated practice single sessions lack.

It cannot claim a measured reading gain. It should not cite 0.65 as its
effect. A series (four or more sessions, or a take-home kit with prompts
between sessions) is closer to the dose the evidence rewards than a single
event. Binding Generations' workshop plan is written to this note.
