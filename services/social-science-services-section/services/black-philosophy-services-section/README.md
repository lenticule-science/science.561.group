# Black Philosophy Services Section

The Black Philosophy Services Section maintains Black philosophical inquiry
as a general domain rather than as a synonym for critical theory, radicalism,
or abolition. It studies concepts and arguments developed from Black life and
intellectual traditions, including questions of personhood, labor, value,
freedom, knowledge, relation, ethics, aesthetics, religion, and political
order.

Its present specialized services are
`black-radical-tradition-services-section` and
`abolition-services-section`. Their placement records two developed parts of
the corpus. It does not claim that every Black philosophy is radical or
abolitionist, or that every abolitionist argument belongs exclusively to Black
philosophy.

The root section holds work that crosses or precedes those specializations. Its
current anchor inquiry reconstructs enslaved Black theories of labor. The
[field map](content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-the-field-architecture-of-black-philosophy.md)
organizes further development across metaphilosophy, being and personhood,
knowledge, ethics, aesthetics, religion, political life, gender and sexuality,
disability, ecology, and technology. These problem-spaces cross geographic and
intellectual traditions; they are not proposed as mutually exclusive schools.

New service sections should be opened when a problem-space has a source-bounded
topic definition, a research note or reference set, and enough distinct work to
require its own continuing home. Until then, root-level topics prevent an empty
directory hierarchy from standing in for scholarship.

Its material is organized by artifact role under `content/`; specialized
subdomains continue under `services/`.
