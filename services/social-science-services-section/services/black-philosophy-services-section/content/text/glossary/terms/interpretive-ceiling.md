# Interpretive Ceiling

An **interpretive ceiling** is the strongest conclusion a source warrants after its genre, mediation, production, alternatives, and [testimonial conditions](./testimonial-conditions.md) have been considered. The ceiling can differ for different questions asked of the same document.

A slaveholder's ledger may strongly support a claim about market classification while weakly supporting a claim about an enslaved person's self-understanding. A first-person narrative can strongly support an explicit moral judgment while requiring further evidence before that judgment is generalized to a population. A practice can establish what happened without settling why every participant acted.

The term is introduced by the [Black feminist labor note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-black-feminist-labor-reproduction-and-epistemic-authority.md) to make evidentiary restraint an explicit part of theory reconstruction.
