# Testimonial Conditions

**Testimonial conditions** are the relations that locate a statement as evidence: who spoke or wrote, the genre and intended audience, an editor or interviewer, the interval between event and recording, transcription and translation, preservation history, and the institutions that later authorized or marginalized the record.

The term does not imply that mediation cancels a speaker's agency or that an unmediated statement is possible. It requires the mediation to become part of the interpretation. An authored slave narrative prepared for abolitionist publication and a retrospective WPA interview can both sustain claims, but not as if they were the same kind of record.

The term is an analytic distinction introduced by the [Black feminist labor note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-black-feminist-labor-reproduction-and-epistemic-authority.md).
