Consistently for the past few years, I've structured my personal website as a [digital garden](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/digital-garden.md), influenced by [note-taking systems](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/note-taking-system.md) like [Zettelkasten](../../../../../../services/computer-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/zettelkasten.md). There are a lot of individual pages, and then pages that list other pages. For example, there's this babble, and [my blog](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/my-blog.md) (my babble-log), which lists all my public babbles.

I'm using [Emacs](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/emacs.md) as my [text editor](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/text-editor.md), using a custom configuration I've named [GnoponEmacs](../2025/08/08/2025-08-08-gnoponemacs.md) - a [neologism](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neologism.md) from gnosis ([Ancient Greek](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ancient-greek-language.md) for *to know*), ponos (*to toil*), and Emacs.

A big part of that is using [Org-mode](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/org-mode.md) and [Org-roam](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/org-roam.md), two powerful and related modules that extend Emacs toward creating complex documents and interlinking, respectively.

Org-mode is like Markdown, in that it's a way of adding formatting to [plain text](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/plain-text.md). But because it's built ontop of a specific piece of software, Emacs, it is able to do a lot more to let that markup be computable. Additionally, Org-mode has components for managing an agenda, templating the creation and modification of files, and notably, publishing files to other formats, including HTML.

Org-roam lets me build a database of a selection of Org-mode files, more readily working with some of that data. That's what lets me quickly find and insert links to other files in things like this babble, and build catalogs like my blog listing.

All this is a bit hard to explain, in this sort of babble, where I'm babbling with the intention that it be read by any random visitor to my website - who might be an expert Emacs user, but more likely hasn't ever used it, or probably even heard of it.

Before computers could do graphics, it was all text: You'd type commands, and the computer would type things back at you. (In the Beginning… was the Command Line, by [Neal Stephenson](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neal-stephenson.md) is a good (but out-of-date) essay about these [interfaces](../../../../../../services/computer-science-services-section/services/computer-systems-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/command-line-interface.md), early [graphical user interface](../../../../../../services/computer-science-services-section/services/computer-systems-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/graphical-user-interface.md), and the significantly different [user experience](../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/user-experience.md) of the two.)

Of course, as soon as they could, people started making games that worked that way. [Zork](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/role-playing-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/zork.md) is an early [text-interface adventure game](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/text-interface-adventure-game.md) that was many folks introduction to the concept. And, of course, as soon as [networked computing](../../../../../../services/computer-science-services-section/services/computer-systems-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/networked-computing.md) came around, people started making, and playing, [multi-user text-interface games](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/multi-user-text-interface-game.md). Coming from folk who already loved playing [tabletop war games](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/role-playing-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tabletop-war-game.md) and [tabletop role-playing games](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/role-playing-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tabletop-role-playing-game.md), the first [major genre](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/major-genre.md) of these games were called [multi-user dungeons](../../../../../../services/computer-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/multi-user-dungeon.md), or MUDs. (These days, that same acronym often gets expanded as [multi-user dimension](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/multi-user-dimension.md), as the genre developed significantly past its [role-playing game](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/role-playing-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/role-playing-game.md) roots.

One perspective on this from my anarchist lineage, which is that this project - my files, my website, and anything that might come from it, like essays, stories, or even MUDs, is an attempt to [subvert](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/subvert.md) what was [identified](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/identifying.md) in [A Thousand Plateaus](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/a-thousand-plateaus.md) as "[overcoding](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/overcoding.md)".

*Overcoding* is a [process](../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/process.md) by which systems [erase](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/erasing.md) or [invisibilize](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/invisibilizing.md) the [difference](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/difference.md) of [things](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/thing.md) that are [members](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/member.md) of that system. As a [concept](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/concept.md), its tied to [territorialization](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/territorialization.md) and [deterritorialization](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/deterritorialization.md), and how the [State](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/state.md) forms its power.

I've written in essays that I'm yet to import here that a lot of this overcoding, especially of social and embodied relations, is now done through distributed systems like social networking.

This website, these files, are a part of that, but are notably oriented toward developing my own individualized and holistic world-code, not participating directly-but-indiscretely in the development of the State's overcoding.

By selectively publishing parts of this, I'm able to perform a type of fugitive overcoding, where a typically normative practice is designed to hold space for what the normative overcoding would make illegible.

Looking at it from my sociologist lineage, I've [coined a term](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/coining-a-term.md) for this practice: [vernacular autoethnosis](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/vernacular-autoethnosis.md): the self-developed practice of studying and knowing the culture I make by being.

There's a lot of other ways to look at what I'm doing, and what it means from those different perspectives, but at this point it might be easiest to say:

I'm creating files not just for prose like babbles and essays, but to encode what I mean, functionally, with the words and phrases I use. Drawing on things like [semiotics](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/semiotic-theory.md), [actor-network theory](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/actor-network-theory.md), and [homotopy type theory](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/homotopy-type-theory.md), I'm hoping to establish a relational field, composed of nodes, each of which represents some form of relationality.

The philosophy that goes underneath the implementation is a bit complex to explain in this babble, but it results in the following:

- Every file is a node
  - Every node has a unique identifier
    - i.e. This babble has the ID `32d5e9f3-d254-4dd3-beed-18563f87d50c`
  - Every node has a form: a node that it is derived and differentiated from:
    - i.e. This babble has the form:
      - `babble`
    - (If a node doesn't have a form specified, it's assumed to just be a "node")

There's a set of derivations I've constructed that establish a core set of nodes and their forms, which are inherited by all nodes within the relational field. Notably, the inheritance between these forms is often postulated without proper derivation, or is even simply speculative.

These core forms are [act](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/tekodynamics-concepts-act-act.md), [condition](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/condition.md), and [structure](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/structure.md).

For example, the `babble` is a form of [text](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/text.md), which is a form of code, which is a structure that results from the act of encoding.

What differentiates a babble from another text is described in its file. (Or, will be; at present a lot of the nodes are empty placeholders, created to help me get a feel for the [naming of nodes](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2025/08/24/2025-08-24-knowledge-organization-guides-naming-a-node-naming-a-node.md).)

In the case of babble, there is a literal encoding of what a Babble is, as an [Org-roam capture template](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/org-roam-capture-template.md). This brings this babble that I'm doing back to [GnoponEmacs](../2025/08/08/2025-08-08-gnoponemacs.md), my Emacs configuration:

By writing Emacs-compatible code in my files, I can use Emacs as an interface for the relational field I'm mapping with my files. That's how I'm able to generate my blog: I use Emacs to generate a list of every node that is formed from a babble.
