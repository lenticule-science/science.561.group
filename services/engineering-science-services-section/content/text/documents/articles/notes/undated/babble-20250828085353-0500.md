This [morning](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morning.md), I'm [planning](../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/planning.md) on [adding](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/adding.md) [Org-cite](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/org-cite.md) to [GnoponEmacs](../2025/08/08/2025-08-08-gnoponemacs.md). This is part of the [2025 Relaunch of emsenn.net](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2025/08/28/2025-08-28-wsl-backup-dev-relationality-content-triage-per-enc-2025-relaunch-of-emsenn-net.md), where I'm using [my Omnibook](../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/biographies/emsenn/emsenns-omnibook.md) (my new laptop) to finally get back to [taking notes](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/taking-notes.md) and [writing](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/writing.md) the way I feel I should. (I'm still struggling to figure out how to term [my work](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2025/08/28/2025-08-28-my-work.md), beyond generally: I want some way to refer to this [digital gardening](../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/social-theory-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2025/08/28/2025-08-28-digital-gardening.md) that does something to hint at its orientation toward [formalism](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/formalism.md), [computability](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/computability.md), and [relational coherence](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/relational-coherence.md).)

(Why Org-cite and not [Org-ref](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/org-ref.md)? While newer, Org-cite is a native part of Org-mode, and aligns more closely with Org-mode conventions.)

The first step in adding Org-cite to GnoponEmacs is [teaching my self](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/self-teaching.md) about how [Org-mode](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/org-mode.md) handles [citations](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/citation.md). Looking at [the manual](https://orgmode.org/manual/Citation-handling.html), it looks like Org-cite builds on concepts from [LaTeX bibliographies](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/latex-bibliography.md), which I've used in the past, but not recently enough to remember much.

I have to make sure a file knows what [bibliographies](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/bibliography.md) to check, which can be done (like most [Org-mode configuration](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/org-mode-configuration.md)) through either local (`#+bibliography: some-refs.bib`{.verbatim}) or global variables (`org-cite-global-bibliography`{.verbatim}).

I keep the PDFs and such that I use as references in `./org/ref/`{.verbatim}; each file is named for its DOI or after its title, if there isn't a DOI. So, it makes sense (to me) to put a global bibliography file in that directory.

Checking, I apparently already have a line in GnoponEmacs that sets a global bibliography, just to the wrong path. But, that indicates that I was at least looking at using Org-cite at some point as recently as [2021](../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/2021ce.md), so maybe more will become familiar as I progress. (Seeing that is when Org-cite was released, so I guess I was hip, at the time.)

Reading through the rest of the relevant manual, I find no guide on how to actually format this `.bib` file. [Searching the Web](../../../../../../services/computer-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/searching-the-web.md) brought me to [this blog post](https://kristofferbalintona.me/posts/202206141852/) from [2022](../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/2022ce.md), which at a [skim](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/skim-reading.md) seems to be much more [educational](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/educational.md) than the manual itself, which seems to presuppose a lot of knowledge I just don't have.

Notably, early on into reading the post, I see it say that while users can write their own [BibTex bibliography](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/bibtex-bibliography.md) (that's what a .bib file is), "the most common way is exporting a [Zotero](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/zotero.md) library).

Zotero is one of those softwares I've heard of from exposure to [institutional academics](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/institutional-academics.md), but I'll be honest: I don't actually know what it is or what it does. I'm continuing to read through this Balintona blog post, but it sounds like I might want to look at Zotero for building a bibliography.

I'm glad to read the following quote, as it conveys I'm more experienced with some tools than the author, but equally unfamiliar with the core packages at hand:

> To those not familiar with another citation workflow (e.g. biblatex in LaTeX), it is quite daunting to leap into citations in Emacs. This was certainly the case for me: I leapt into org-mode citation integration with Zotero and LaTeX with no experience in org, Zotero, or LaTeX! Nonetheless, org-cite is quite simple once laid out.

Then, the author does me the great service of linking to the [post announcing Org-cite](https://blog.tecosaur.com/tmio/2021-07-31-citations.html), which does a lot to explain a lot!

The post explains that Org-cite is split into two parts: the part that interacts with citations, and the part that renders those citations. It goes into some detail about how this interacts with different citation processors: there's four things Org-cite uses them for:

- Inserting and editing citations (in Org-mode buffers, I assume? I think Zotero is what's for doing that to the bibliography itself.)
- Follow citations to their definitions (Open the PDF? Or… Org-roam file? I don't know.)
- Fontifying citations in the buffer (that is, I think, just making them render pretty)
- Exporting citations (making them show up in exported files)

The concept of "citation processors" has come up a few times this morning, and I've mostly skipped past, but here, I feel like I have enough context to start trying to understand it. As explained in the announcement post, there are four citation processors bundled in Org-mode:

`basic`  
no dependencies, can do all the citation stuff (insert, edit, follow, fontify, export)

`biblatex` and `natbib`  
exports, but only for LaTeX?

`csl`  
exports using Citation Style Language(?), to HTML, LaTeX, Org, and plain text. Depends on citeproc.el?

It sounds like, because I am planning to export things to [my website](../2025/07/19/2025-07-19-index.md), I will need to look at Citation Style Language, but for now, I'm trying to stay focused on initializing my bibliography file, so I can start with some hands-on learning.

Luckily the next part of the announcement post gives an example `orgcite.bib`:

``` bibtex
@article{OrgCitations,
         author={org, mode and Syntax, Citation and List, Mailing and Effort, Time},
         journal={Journal of Plain Text Formats},
         title={Elegant Citations with Org-Mode},
         year={2021},
         month={7},
         volume={42},
         number={1},
         pages={2-3}}  
```
