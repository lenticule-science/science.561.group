The ratio of wingspan to mean chord, governing the trade-off between aerodynamic efficiency and structural simplicity.

Aspect ratio (AR) is the ratio of a wing&rsquo;s span to its mean chord (average width), or equivalently, the square of the span divided by the wing planform area. A long, narrow wing (sailplane, AR 15–30) has high aspect ratio; a short, wide wing (delta fighter, AR 1.5–3) has low aspect ratio.

High aspect ratio reduces induced drag — the drag penalty caused by generating lift with a finite-span wing. This improves cruise efficiency and range. But high-aspect-ratio wings are structurally demanding: the long spar must resist large bending moments, requiring stronger (heavier, more expensive) materials and more precise manufacturing.

A delta wing with AR 2.0 has roughly four times the [induced drag](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/induced-drag.md) of a conventional wing with AR 8.0 at the same lift coefficient — but the delta&rsquo;s [spar](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/spar.md) weighs less, costs less, and can be manufactured by unskilled labor or [3D printing](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/additive-manufacturing-in-uav-airframes.md) with wide tolerances. The choice of aspect ratio is always a compromise between aerodynamic efficiency and structural/manufacturing cost — see [Wing Planform Selection for UAVs](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wing-planform-selection-for-uavs.md).
