---
title: "Rib"
type: "term"
date-created: 2026-03-06
---

A chordwise structural member that maintains the airfoil cross-section and transfers aerodynamic loads to the spar.

A rib is a structural member oriented along the [chord](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/chord.md) direction (perpendicular to the [spar](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/spar.md)). Ribs define and maintain the [airfoil](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/airfoil.md) cross-section, prevent the wing [skin](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/skin.md) from buckling, and transfer distributed aerodynamic pressure loads to the spar.

Rib spacing is a design trade-off: closer spacing provides better skin support (preventing buckling and maintaining airfoil accuracy) but adds weight and assembly time. Typical rib spacing ranges from 50–100 mm for small model-scale wings to 200–500 mm for tactical UAVs.

**Rib construction methods in UAVs:**

- **Laser-cut plywood or balsa** — light, cheap, precise. The traditional model aircraft method. A laser cutter produces a complete rib set from a 3 mm plywood sheet in minutes.

- **[3D-printed](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/additive-manufacturing-in-uav-airframes.md)** — can be printed integrally with the skin, eliminating assembly. Alternatively, printed as separate pieces that slide onto the spar.

- **CNC-cut foam** — ribs can be integral with a foam wing core, cut by hot-wire or CNC router.

- **Sheet aluminum** — stamped or CNC-routed. Used in larger tactical and MALE UAVs.

In fully [3D-printed wings](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/additive-manufacturing-in-uav-airframes.md), the distinction between rib and infill blurs: the internal lattice structure (gyroid, rectilinear, etc.) serves the rib&rsquo;s function of supporting the skin and transferring loads, without being a discrete chordwise member. This is one reason printed wings can use fewer or no separate ribs — the [infill](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/infill-density.md) does the work.
