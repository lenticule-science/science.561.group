The distance from a wing's leading edge to its trailing edge, the fundamental length dimension of an airfoil.

Chord is the straight-line distance from the leading edge to the trailing edge of an [airfoil](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/airfoil.md). On a rectangular wing, the chord is constant across the [span](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/span.md). On tapered or delta wings, the chord varies — longer at the root, shorter at the tip.

The **mean aerodynamic chord** (MAC) is the chord-weighted average across the span, used as the reference length for aerodynamic calculations (pitching moment, [Reynolds number](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reynolds-number.md), center of gravity position). For a rectangular wing, MAC equals the chord. For a tapered wing, MAC lies inboard of the geometric midpoint. For a delta wing, MAC is approximately two-thirds of the root chord.

### Why chord matters for UAV design

Chord interacts with airspeed to determine the local Reynolds number (Re = V × c / ν), which in turn determines [boundary layer](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/boundary-layer.md) behavior and [airfoil](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/airfoil.md) performance. Small UAVs with short chords (50–150 mm) operate in the [low-Reynolds-number regime](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/low-reynolds-number-aerodynamics.md) (Re < 200,000) where airfoil selection is critical and surface finish matters more than at larger scales.

Chord also determines the internal volume available for structure — a longer chord provides more depth for a [spar](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/spar.md) at a given airfoil thickness ratio, and more room for [infill](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/infill-density.md) in [3D-printed](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/additive-manufacturing-in-uav-airframes.md) wings. This is one reason delta [planforms](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wing-planform-selection-for-uavs.md) with their deep root chords can use simpler internal structure than high-[aspect-ratio](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/aspect-ratio.md) wings with narrow chords.

The [center of gravity](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/applied-sciences/technology/aerospace-engineering/archive/center-of-gravity.md) is conventionally expressed as a percentage of MAC measured from the leading edge — typically 25–30% MAC for active flight. Moving the CG aft of this range reduces [stability](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/applied-sciences/technology/terms/stability.md); moving it forward increases stability but requires more [elevator](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/elevator.md) deflection for [trim](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/trim.md), adding trim drag.
