The ratio of aircraft weight to wing area, the single most consequential parameter in airframe sizing.

Wing loading is the ratio of an aircraft&rsquo;s weight to its wing planform area, expressed in kg/m² or lb/ft². It is the single number that most directly determines an aircraft&rsquo;s flight characteristics: stall speed, turn radius, gust response, takeoff and landing distance, and cruise efficiency all follow from it.

Wing loading varies enormously across the UAV spectrum:

Higher wing loading means faster cruise, less sensitivity to gusts, and smaller wing area (cheaper, simpler structure) — but also higher [stall](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/stall.md) speed, longer takeoff distance, and worse loiter performance. The choice is driven by mission: a long-endurance surveillance platform wants low wing loading; an [expendable](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expendable-airframe-design.md) strike drone launched by catapult wants high wing loading.
