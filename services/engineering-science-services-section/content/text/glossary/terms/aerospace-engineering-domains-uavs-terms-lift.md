name: "Lift"
date-created: 2026-03-06

The aerodynamic force perpendicular to the freestream velocity, generated by pressure differences across a wing surface.

Lift is the component of aerodynamic force acting perpendicular to the direction of flight. It is produced by pressure differences between the upper and lower surfaces of a wing: air accelerates over the curved upper surface, reducing static pressure (per Bernoulli&rsquo;s principle), while the lower surface experiences higher pressure. The net upward force is lift.

The more complete explanation is circulation-based: a wing deflects the oncoming airflow downward (the &ldquo;downwash&rdquo;), and by Newton&rsquo;s third law, the air pushes the wing upward. The Kutta-Joukowski theorem formalizes this — lift per unit span equals air density × freestream velocity × circulation around the wing section. The Bernoulli and circulation explanations are not competing theories; they are two descriptions of the same physics.

Lift is calculated as:

L = ½ × ρ × V² × S × C_L

where ρ is air density, V is airspeed, S is [wing planform area](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wing-planform-selection-for-uavs.md), and C_L is the lift coefficient (a dimensionless number determined by [angle of attack](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/angle-of-attack.md), [airfoil](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/airfoil.md) shape, and [Reynolds number](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reynolds-number.md)).

For level flight, lift must equal weight. This relationship is the starting point for all wing sizing: given a target gross weight and cruise speed, the required wing area follows directly from the achievable lift coefficient. [Wing loading](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wing-loading.md) (weight/area) is the ratio that captures this relationship.

The lift equation reveals two ways to increase lift: increase speed (lift scales with V²) or increase the lift coefficient (by increasing [angle of attack](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/angle-of-attack.md) or deploying high-lift devices). Both have limits — speed is constrained by available [thrust](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/thrust.md), and C_L is bounded by [stall](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/stall.md). The maximum lift coefficient an airfoil can sustain before stalling determines the minimum speed at which the aircraft can fly at a given [wing loading](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wing-loading.md) — this is the stall speed, and it sets the lower bound of the flight envelope.

For UAVs, the practical consequence is that a heavier aircraft (higher wing loading) must fly faster to generate enough lift, which increases [drag](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/drag.md), which increases power consumption, which reduces [endurance](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/applied-sciences/technology/aerospace-engineering/archive/endurance.md). Every gram of structural weight that could have been saved translates directly into reduced mission capability through this chain of dependencies.
