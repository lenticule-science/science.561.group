A class of UAV operating above 15,000 m altitude for 24&#43; hours, used for strategic reconnaissance and wide-area persistent surveillance.

HALE (high-altitude long-endurance) is a classification for UAVs designed to operate above 15,000 m (50,000 ft) for 24+ hours — and in some solar-powered variants, for weeks or months continuously. HALE platforms provide strategic-level surveillance, communications relay, and atmospheric research from altitudes above weather and most air defenses.

HALE engineering is dominated by the challenge of efficient flight in thin air. At 18,000 m, air density is roughly 7% of sea level. Since [lift](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/lift.md) is proportional to air density, a HALE platform needs either enormously more wing area, much higher speed, or much higher lift coefficient than a sea-level aircraft of the same weight.

This drives HALE design toward:

- Very high [aspect ratios](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/aspect-ratio.md) (25–35) — Global Hawk&rsquo;s 40 m wingspan approaches sailplane territory

- Light [wing loading](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wing-loading.md) (200–350 kg/m²) — large wing relative to weight

- Turbofan or turboprop [propulsion](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/uav-propulsion-systems.md) optimized for altitude — or solar cells with electric motors for indefinite-endurance variants

- Specialized high-altitude [airfoils](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/airfoil.md) that perform at [Reynolds numbers](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reynolds-number.md) comparable to much smaller aircraft at sea level (Re ~1–3 million at altitude, vs. ~20–30 million at sea level for the same wing)

- Extreme structural efficiency — carbon [composite](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/composite.md) throughout, with weight management rivaling sailplane construction

The solar-powered HALE category (Zephyr, Odysseus) operates at a different engineering frontier: wing area is sized for solar panel capacity as much as for aerodynamics, structural weight must be minimized to near-zero margins for night flight on battery power alone, and [fatigue](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/fatigue.md) from daily thermal cycling at extreme altitude becomes a defining structural concern.
