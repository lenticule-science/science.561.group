---
name: "Span"
date-created: 2026-03-06
---

The tip-to-tip distance of a wing, the primary dimension governing induced drag and structural bending loads.

Span (or wingspan) is the distance from one wingtip to the other, measured perpendicular to the aircraft&rsquo;s longitudinal axis. Together with [chord](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/chord.md), it defines the wing planform area (S ≈ span × mean chord) and [aspect ratio](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/aspect-ratio.md) (AR = span² / S).

Span is the most structurally consequential dimension of a wing. Bending moment at the root scales with span squared (for a given wing loading), meaning that doubling the span quadruples the root bending load. This is why high-span wings require heavy, stiff spars — and why [expendable airframes](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/expendable-airframe-design.md) favor short spans that reduce structural requirements at the cost of aerodynamic efficiency.

Span also determines [induced drag](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/induced-drag.md): longer spans produce weaker tip vortices for a given amount of [lift](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/lift.md), reducing the energy lost to the wake. This is the fundamental reason that high-endurance UAVs (MQ-9 Reaper: 20 m span; RQ-4 Global Hawk: 40 m span) use long, narrow wings.

Practical span limits for UAVs include: build volume of [3D printers](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/additive-manufacturing-in-uav-airframes.md) or foam-cutting equipment, vehicle transport width, launch rail or catapult dimensions, and storage container size. Many UAV designs use folding or detachable wings to decouple flight span from transport span.
