title: "Monocoque"
type: "term"
date-created: 2026-03-06

A structural approach where the outer skin carries the primary loads, minimizing or eliminating internal framework.

Monocoque (French: &ldquo;single shell&rdquo;) is a structural design philosophy where the outer [skin](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/skin.md) of a structure carries all or most of the loads — bending, torsion, and shear — without a separate internal skeleton. A pure monocoque has no internal frame; a **semi-monocoque** adds internal stiffeners ([spars](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/spar.md), [ribs](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rib.md), stringers, bulkheads) to prevent the skin from buckling under compression, while the skin still carries a significant share of the load.

Most UAV airframes above the hobby scale are semi-monocoque: the fuselage is a skin tube stiffened by bulkheads and longerons, and the wing is a skin-covered torque box formed around one or two spars with periodic ribs.

[3D-printed](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/additive-manufacturing-in-uav-airframes.md) UAV components can approach true monocoque construction. A printed fuselage tube with 3–4 perimeter walls and moderate [infill](../../../../services/aerospace-engineering-science-services-section/services/uavs-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/infill-density.md) is effectively a monocoque — the &ldquo;skin&rdquo; (perimeter walls) and &ldquo;structure&rdquo; (infill) are printed as a single continuous part. This eliminates the assembly step of attaching skin to frame and is one of the key advantages of additive manufacturing for airframe production.

Foam-core composite construction is another near-monocoque approach: the foam core defines the shape and resists compression, while the fiberglass or carbon skin carries tension and shear. This is the primary construction method for the Shahed-136 and similar expendable UAV families.
