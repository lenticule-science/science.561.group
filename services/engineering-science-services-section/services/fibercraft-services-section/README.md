# Fibercraft Services Section

This section studies **how fiber, fabric, garment geometry, construction operations and actual wear** relate. [ObsmetrologiaFibercraft](lib/obsmetrologia-fibercraft) is the house's specific formal program for weave drafts and lenticle crossings, derived downstream of Obsmetrologia; **it is not synonymous with clothing design**. The existing [textile-mathematics survey](../../../../services/information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/06/26/2026-06-26-survey-of-textile-mathematics.md) covers distinct weave, knit, braid and material regimes without proving their physical interchangeability.

## Three different engineering questions, one shared physical receiver

1. **Woven/cut-and-sewn apparel:** given bodily dimensions, movement, intentional ease and silhouette, what **piece geometry**, grain direction, seam relationships, notches, allowances, closures, lining/trim and assembly order construct a wearable garment in a declared cloth? Treat a 2D pattern, graded nested patterns, 3D drape simulation, sample tech pack, actual sample and accepted production pack as **different** evidence. The object is neither an STL nor an illustrated fashion concept.
2. **Constructed textiles and knitwear:** given fibers, thread/loop structure, gauge and local operations, what textile is made and how does it behave after blocking/washing/load? [Knitting Science](services/knitting-science-services-section/README.md), including [CITY LOOPS](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/issues/117), owns its specialized hypotheses and executable knit/test terminology. It is **one** clothing path, not the limit of fashion.
3. **Print-on-demand graphic apparel:** given a **selected physical blank and manufacturer**, where can accepted original artwork be placed and printed, in which size/color variants, with what method, care properties and actual unit costs? Graphics and print placement do not manufacture a garment pattern. [Fine Print Mercantile](https://github.com/causeway-services/public-services-section/tree/main/services/public-resources-services-section/services/public-consumer-resources-services-section/services/public-print-on-demand-services-section) owns an existing 36-design / 12-raster-art product corpus; [Red Cup print-design Engineering](https://github.com/red-cup-engineering/print-design-engineering-services-section) owns its extracted reusable SVG/raster maker; [Causeway's Printify draft adapter](https://github.com/causeway-services/public-media-services-section/blob/main/src/pod-printify.mjs) remains a distinct proposed-to-accepted provider border. None of those is an accepted catalog listing or evidence of merchant demand.

These are **scientific and manufacturing distinctions**, not three new offices, competing product catalogs, universal CAD classes or an assertion that generic regularized meshes explain a living body's fit.

## Research-to-model requirements for clothing

For every named **non-knit** garment hypothesis, record in the original owning Work/source:

- **Observed signal and rights:** dated, situated primary fashion/usage/maker source, observer, physical feature actually seen, source rights; where marketplace metrics exist, separate views, saves, purchases, complaints and reorders.
- **Garment/receiver:** real wearer/user purpose; creative silhouette, intended layering and movement; anthropometric dimensions, intended finished sizes, ease and comfort envelope; distinguish unspecified measurement from zero.
- **Construction:** woven/knit/nonwoven material and actual mechanical behavior; pattern piece identity, seamline and CUT outline in **declared units**, allowances, notches, grain lines, pairwise seam-edge compatibility, required facings/trims and stitching order. Unlike a knitting chart, a sewing pattern's joins relate edges of different 2D panels.
- **Outputs/limits:** editable 2D construction source, production DXF/SVG as applicable, graded nest *only when size rules have been established*, bill of materials, cutter/marker layout, tech pack with finish/care/label requirements, and a distinct physically worn sample. An annotated visual or SVG alone is not an approved production pattern.
- **Independent tests:** for a positive sample and a contrasting material/fit or refusal, measure pattern dimensions, seam mismatch, cut/mark accuracy, washer and dryer response as permitted by actual care, shrinkage, drape, stress under movement, body coverage, wearer comfort, error correction and labor/cost. A virtual fitting is useful but not physical acceptance.
- **Feedback:** preserve rejected designs and the actual changed geometry/fit/production parameters with their source revisions. Refuse protected designer-copy requests, missing production rights, unsupported variants, guessed sizes, positive demand claims from likes, and oversized model releases with no cut/fit evidence.

**External comparators (observed capabilities, not adopted products or certified conformance):** [Seamly2D](https://seamly.io/) supports measurement-driven pattern drafting; [CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export) imports/exports pattern DXF with declared units and separate AAMA/ASTM modes; [Browzwear Tech Packs](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/13065820-editing-tech-pack-properties) include multi-size pattern layouts, seam allowances, fabric/trim BOM, artworks and DXF. The historical ASTM **D6673-10 was withdrawn in 2019**, not a currently warranted compliance certificate: https://store.astm.org/d6673-10.html. A plain DXF candidate may still be practically inspectable without claiming ASTM compatibility.

## Custody and first operational recipient

**Science** owns the research claims, physical/mathematical assumptions and design-validation protocol. **Red Cup** owns native reusable software, executable pattern generators and export/verification machinery (separate requirements → specification → architecture → design → fabrication). **Coesite** owns reusable operating/production methods and independently received actual material samples. **561 Group / Causeway** owns each *particular* clothing product, garment master, merchandising/pricing rights, customer/wearer and eventual shop submission. The Group's corporate receiving comes before Causeway public delivery; no downstream office inherits publication or capital-spend authority from a style note.

The **first bounded cut-and-sew design study** is the distinct non-knit sample model **Transit Layer**: a boxy open-front woven overlayer using measured source-supported oversized/layering proportions without copying an existing garment. Its consumer/source is under [Causeway Public Consumer Resources](https://github.com/causeway-services/public-services-section/tree/main/services/public-resources-services-section/services/public-consumer-resources-services-section), not in Knitting Science. Required first reception: real pattern pieces with unit-declared cut and seam lines, matching shoulder/side seam lengths, candidate grain and a printable calibration square; **then independent muslin, fit and care review**. The model is explicitly **not graded and not a saleable garment**. Compare [CITY LOOPS contemporary style research](services/knitting-science-services-section/research/2026-10-12-city-loops-urban-china-knitwear-research.md) for historical inspiration while not repurposing its knitwear product or scientific measurements.

**Product-making priority:** this source only selects a scientific/technical method; actual new physical models, garments or sales must be produced and received in the original product owners. No aesthetic or code stage is automatically discharged by updating this README.
