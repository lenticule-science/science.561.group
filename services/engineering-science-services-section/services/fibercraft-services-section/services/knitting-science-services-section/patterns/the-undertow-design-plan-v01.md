# THE UNDERTOW

**A sideways-knitted, asymmetrical, grown-on Aran sweater**  
Design development specification v0.1 — 12 October 2026  
**Status:** Design plan and testable core cable swatch. Not a finished, test-knitted sweater pattern.

## The garment

A generously sized, slightly cropped pullover in warm undyed oatmeal wool: sculptural but easy to wear with denim and boots. One very large braided cable starts at the **wearer's left cuff**, runs along the front-facing part of that sleeve, sweeps *through the same knitted fabric* onto the left upper chest, turns diagonally downward, and divides into thinner wandering strands across the front. The back has mostly quiet reverse stockinette and just one restrained ghost cable. The right sleeve is quieter still. Ribbed fitted cuffs, a 5 cm picked-up hem, and a softly folded crew neck give the piece polish.

**The clothing test:** The sweater must be flattering when the arms hang normally, comfortable to move in, and attractive even to someone who has never heard of Obsmetrologia. No floating tubes, enormous seams, loose trapped strands, or visible gimmickry.

### Design decisions

- **Construction:** flat, sideways, one grown-on sleeve-to-body-to-sleeve piece, worked from left wrist to right wrist; the neckline opens temporarily by separating front and back stitches; seams are on the *undersides* of sleeves and sides of torso. This avoids trying to transfer cable stitches across an already-separated armhole.
- **Silhouette:** wide but not balloon-shaped body; drop/dolman shoulder, modestly tapered sleeves, slightly cropped length; approximately 20 cm positive ease at chest.
- **Yarn:** a light, springy, crisp non-superwash wool in natural oatmeal (Aran / heavy worsted is a starting point). Avoid slippery, limp fibers until the cable holds its shape.
- **Target gauge, BLOCKED:** background 18 stitches and 26 rows over 10 cm. This is a *design target*, not an achieved result. Reverse-stockinette and cable widths must be measured independently.
- **Starting needles:** around 5 mm main and 4 mm ribbing, adjusted to blocked gauge.
- **Prototype size:** finished chest 120 cm, shoulder-to-hem including 5 cm ribbing 56 cm, upper sleeve circumference 40 cm, cuff circumference 22 cm, underarm-to-cuff 43 cm including 6 cm cuff, neck opening approximately 22 cm wide, front neck lower than back.
- **Yarn reservation:** provisionally ~1,500–1,800 meters for the 120 cm prototype, plus 10–15% before purchasing for swatches, revisions, and matched dye lot. Re-estimate by weighing blocked swatches and working prototype.

## The hero cable

**Undertow Braid:** three visually distinct 6-stitch cords, presented as an 18-stitch knit braid on reverse-stockinette ground, with generous purl space either side. The braid should be bulky and legible but not hard enough to make a ridge press uncomfortably against the arm. Large crossings are spaced 12 rows apart; two staggered crossing positions give the rope its rhythm.

The front is not covered in cables. The main braid dominates; one 4-stitch traveling cable and one 2-stitch twisted-stitch path branch off on the chest and gradually disappear. A small, low-relief countercurrent on the back balances the weight without competing with the front.

On the sleeve, place the braid on its *front-facing upper sector*, not on its underside seam. As the piece grows across the body, the band travels toward the hem far enough to avoid the front neckline opening. The main rope remains in the front stitch section when neckline stitches are temporarily separated. Its long axis is continuous from sleeve into the body.

**Crucial open design point:** A wearable panel that travels diagonally, without broken-looking cable columns or skewed fabric, must be charted and swatched. The 18-stitch core braid below is knit-ready; the full travel-and-branch chart is not yet validated. We will not silently fill that gap with instructions that merely say “make it travel.”

## Unrolled construction: 120 cm prototype

The working direction follows **the width of the wearer**, not the vertical length of the body. The main garment is one long piece with a front side and a back side that fold together at the shoulder line.

1. **Left wrist:** Cast on **40 stitches**. Work **16 flat rows of 2x2 rib** (~6 cm). Place a permanent front/underside orientation marker.
2. **Left sleeve:** Switch to the main fabric. Work **96 rows** (~37 cm) while increasing **one stitch at each edge on 16 evenly spaced increase rows**; under the target gauge, a paired increase about every 6th row gives **72 stitches** at the upper arm. Start the hero braid after the cuff, on the front-facing sector, clear of the future underarm seam.
3. **Expand for body:** At the underarm, cast on **56 additional stitches at each side** of the 72-stitch row, on successive row ends, to reach **184 stitches**. These extensions become the front and back lengths from underarm toward hem. The original 72 sleeve stitches occupy the shoulder/upper-body zone. Mark the fold between the front 92 and back 92 stitches and note which face will be outside.
4. **Left body:** Work **50 body rows** (~19.2 cm across the chest). Bring the main braid diagonally farther down the front, and ensure that the entire live braid is below the proposed front neckline shaping. Do not start the neckline until the cable corridor and the 2 cm safety margin are demonstrated in the test swatch.
5. **Neck opening:** Separate the front and back stitch groups at the shoulder fold, work them **independently for 56 rows** (~21.5 cm across). For a first cloth mockup only, remove about **14 stitches at the top of the front** (~7.8 cm neck depth) and **5 at the top of the back** (~2.8 cm) as the opening begins; restore these at the far side. Final neck curves will use staged cast-offs/short rows and a trial neckline, not abrupt square edges.
6. **Right body:** Rejoin front and back to the full **184 stitches**; work the remaining **50 rows**. The big braid has by now split into smaller branches and gradually terminated or settled into the ground.
7. **Right sleeve:** Bind off the 56-stitch extensions at the corresponding two row ends, returning to **72 stitches**. Work **96 sleeve rows**, decreasing one stitch at each edge on 16 evenly spaced decrease rows to return to **40 stitches**. Keep this sleeve substantially plainer than the left.
8. **Right cuff:** Work **16 rows of 2x2 rib**, then bind off elastically.
9. **Finish:** Block the unrolled main piece to measurements. Fold along the shoulder line, mattress-stitch the two underarm seams and both side seams (or close them as two continuous lines), and check mobility and cable placement. Pick up the hem and knit **5 cm 2x2 rib**; pick up the neckline and knit a flexible folded crew collar, approximately **7 cm of rib folded to 3.5 cm**. Finish and wet-block again.

**Row-boundary caveat:** The two edge extensions are made/removed on consecutive flat rows, so the final written instructions must account for those row transitions. The numbers above are the interior working budgets, not an instruction to add both edge extensions simultaneously without turning.

## Core cable swatch — ready to knit now

**Materials:** the proposed main yarn, 5 mm needles to start, cable needle. **Flat swatch, 34 stitches × 48 rows**, then wash and lay flat without stretching. The finished measurement determines whether the yarn and the size of the braid work aesthetically.

**Cast on 34 stitches.** The layout is: 3 garter border / 5 reverse-stockinette stitches / 18-stitch braid / 5 reverse-stockinette stitches / 3 garter border.

- **Default odd/right-side row:** K3, P5, K18, P5, K3.
- **Default even/wrong-side row:** K3, K5, P18, K5, K3.
- **Row 7:** K3, P5, **C12F**, K6, P5, K3.
- **Row 19:** K3, P5, K6, **C12B**, P5, K3.
- Rows 1–24: default instructions except rows 7 and 19. Repeat rows 1–24 once more to complete rows 25–48 (thus also cross on rows 31 and 43).
- **C12F:** Slip next 6 stitches to a cable needle and hold **in front**. Knit next 6, then knit the 6 held stitches.
- **C12B:** Slip next 6 stitches to a cable needle and hold **in back**. Knit next 6, then knit the 6 held stitches.

Every cable crossing consumes and produces 12 stitches; the swatch always has **34 stitches**. This is a defined test of the *braid*, not yet of the diagonal travel mechanism. Check the hand-feel and drape. A rope that digs into the arm or creates a ridge too bulky to bend fails the design even if it looks good lying flat.

## Make it as real clothing: design-and-test schedule

**Stage 1 — Knit-ready cable swatch (first).** Knit the exact 34-stitch swatch above, block it, and measure its width, row length, elasticity, relief height, and tendency to bias. Photograph from front and side in diffuse light. Reject thick rigid crossings; try 4-over-4 rather than 6-over-6 if needed.

**Stage 2 — Traveling-cable study.** Knit an approximately 45-stitch-wide, 70-row swatch that displaces the braid 10 stitches diagonally. Compare two *chartable* methods: (a) knit/purl boundary reassignment with careful edge detailing, (b) genuine 1x1 or 2x2 knit-purl stitch crossings. Evaluate which actually gives continuous-looking traveling cables without distorted gauge. Don't assume a purl-to-knit transition moves a wale.

**Stage 3 — Half-scale, sideways shoulder/neck mockup.** In spare yarn, practice the body-edge cast-ons, the front/back neckline split, the fold, and the underarm seam. Confirm that the braid can occupy the front region without meeting neck cast-offs. Mark actual front/back orientation before shaping.

**Stage 4 — Wearable 120 cm prototype.** Knit the first full sweater to the blocked-size numbers; try on before joining seams, then after seaming and before attaching the collar. Adjust underarm mobility, collar width, hem length, and cable placement. If the shoulder-to-chest transition looks forced, redraw it; aesthetics are a design requirement, not an afterthought.

**Stage 5 — Make a publishable pattern.** Produce a scaled front/back/sleeve schematic, a separate left-sleeve/body cable chart (with every crossing and migration marked), written row instructions, 6 graded sizes, full stitch counts, measured yardage, photos, technique key, clear RS/WS orientation, and independent tech editing/test knitting in at least three sizes.

**Stage 6 — Optional Obsmetrologia research, behind the scenes.** Use the formal port-growth and FOUR-valued constraint systems to enumerate different cable arrangements and reject combinations that cannot be knitted with the chosen operations. Record loop-support/crossing order, preservation of stitch count, and later measured fabric response. Compile to human chart and machine-specific knitout only when scheduling and machine capability checks pass. STL may depict the surface, but it does not describe how to knit the loops.

## Go/no-go checks

- Main cable can be followed from left sleeve into front body through continuous knitted fabric, with **no applied-on cable and no shoulder seam cutting it**.
- The neck opening stays at least **2 cm clear** of the traveling cable and its purl gutters after blocking.
- The 120 cm sample actually blocks within **±3 cm of the 120 cm chest target** and **±2 cm in body length**; otherwise revise gauge and counts before grading.
- Underarm seams do not force the sleeve to twist. The wearer can lift arms comfortably and bend elbows without the large braid digging in.
- The main braid is unmistakably visible with arms relaxed; the back and right sleeve don't fight for attention.
- No unspecified maneuver appears in the final knitting chart, including branch splits, neckline restoration, and stitch movement.
- A knitter not involved in designing the pattern can finish a sample without consulting the mathematics.
