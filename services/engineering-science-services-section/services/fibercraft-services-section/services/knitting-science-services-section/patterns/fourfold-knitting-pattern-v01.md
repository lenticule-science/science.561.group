# FOURFOLD - The Obstruction Bloom

Version 1.0 · 12 October 2026

An original experimental knitting pattern for emsenn, drawing on Obsmetrologia. **Not physically test-knit.** The prior generated picture is a concept image, not a forecast of the finished fabric.

## What you are making

Four rectangles share one long-edge spine. Their short ends are sewn cyclically A→B→C→D→A. This produces a four-page branched mapping torus with one free rim, not an ordinary torus surface. The seams make the topology; the purl motifs mark it.

## Materials, gauge, size

Use four distinguishable smooth yarns of the same weight and construction, or one yarn and durable labels. DK yarn with 3.5–4 mm needles is a starting suggestion. You also need a blunt tapestry needle, waste yarn, and locking markers. No measured yarn requirement is available. Work a 24-stitch × 48-row swatch and budget 7.5 times its yarn consumption per panel (six swatch-lengths plus 25%); use four times that for comparable yarns, keeping seam yarn in reserve.

Reference gauge across the chart: 24 stitches × 32 rows per 10 cm. Each 24-stitch × 288-row panel is then about 10 × 90 cm before seam take-up. A circular spine is approximately 29 cm across, and the spread sculpture approximately 49 cm across; these are geometric estimates, not test-knit measurements.

Block a swatch before choosing your repeat count. Panel length should be at least eight times panel width. Add complete 24-row repeats to every panel equally if needed. Equal dimensions, row counts, and aligned seam stations are more important than this gauge.

## Chart conventions

Work flat and turn after every row. RS = designated right side, WS = reverse. Mark RS after row 1. Cast-on does not count as a chart row.

- In the RS view, column 1 is at the RIGHT, next to the spine; column 24 is at the LEFT, next to the free rim.
- Columns 1–3 and 22–24 are garter borders. Knit all six edge stitches on every row; do not slip edge stitches. No extra edge stitches.
- Read odd/RS rows right to left and even/WS rows left to right; charts read from bottom upward.
- `.` or an empty square: knit on RS, purl on WS.
- `-` or a horizontal dash: purl on RS, knit on WS.
- Written rows below are already in working order. K = knit, P = purl, following number = stitch count. Do not reverse written rows.

## Make one of each panel

Cast on 24 stitches with a flexible cast-on. Work the appropriate chart through rows 1–24, then repeat the entire chart twelve times: 288 body rows. Mark the spine edge after each 24-row repeat. Bind off knitwise without tightening after row 288; that bind-off pass is not one of the 288 rows. Block all panels to equal width and length. Label START (cast-on) and FINISH (bind-off) on every panel. Keep labels until assembly checks pass.

The low fields mark two bits: token in columns 6–9 and claim in columns 16–19 on rows 3–6. A smooth field is 0; a purl field is 1. These are texture blocks, not holes. The tree motif is relief decoration, not a cable or stitch-count change.

## Assembly I: the shared spine

First rehearse these identifications with four narrow scrap strips and removable seams.

1. Align all START ends and all FINISH ends. Match markers after rows 24,48,…,288. Only column-1 long edges meet; all column-24 long edges stay free.
2. Fan panels in cyclic order A,B,C,D around one common spine. The cross-section is a four-spoke star, not a square tube. Give all RS faces the same rotational sense about the spine.
3. Using separate seam yarn, catch matching outermost edge bumps at the same garter ridge: A(k)→B(k)→C(k)→D(k)→A(k). Draw all four edges together without strangling the fabric, then advance one ridge. Every 24 rows gives 12 edge ridges. Work through all 144 body ridges, matching every repeat marker. Catch the START and FINISH corners at the ends. Never offset A(k) to B(k+1).
4. Use manageable seam-yarn lengths; secure changes within the seam. Leave it flexible. At several points, spread all four pages: each must extend directly from the same spine to its free edge. If you have sewn a longitudinal tube, undo it.

This four-way lacing is experimental. Ordinary two-edge garter seaming is documented by Purl Soho; that reference does not establish this four-way seam as test-knit.

## Assembly II: cyclic short-end closure

5. Bend the spine into a large ring. Keep the pages in cyclic order and distribute a quarter-turn of the four-spoke arrangement so the specified finish-to-start pairs meet. Do not half-twist each panel.
6. Baste these four joins, preserving column number exactly:

| FINISH / bind-off | START / cast-on | Column pairing |
|---|---|---|
| A | B | 1→1, 2→2, …, 24→24 |
| B | C | 1→1, 2→2, …, 24→24 |
| C | D | 1→1, 2→2, …, 24→24 |
| D | A | 1→1, 2→2, …, 24→24 |

Match spine corner to spine corner and rim corner to rim corner. Sew corresponding cast-on/bind-off loops one to one with a flexible visible whip seam, catching both legs where accessible. Do not gather. This is not an invisible graft; a ridge is acceptable.

7. Trace the still-free long edge before making joins permanent. Starting near A-START, travel along A to A-FINISH, cross to B-START, travel B, then C, then D, and only then return to A. You must have exactly one free rim.
8. Secure all short seams after that check passes. Close the common spine START/FINISH corner junction without a slit. Weave in ends and let the object relax. The result need not stand in the pose depicted in the original poster.

Four rim loops usually mean same-panel end joins; two mean a permutation with two cycles. A rim that enters the spine indicates a reversed end or an incorrectly captured free edge. Puckering suggests tight seams, unmatched gauges, or shifted rows.

## Mathematical specification and proof

This is a coarse-grained fabric-and-seam model, not a claim about the topology of the individual yarn centerline.

Let X be four rectangles [0,1]_t × [0,1]_r indexed j∈Z/4, subject to (t,0,j)∼(t,0,k) for all j,k and (1,r,j)∼(0,r,j+1). The common r=0 locus is the spine. The r=1 locus is the free rim B.

X is the mapping torus of a four-spoke star under quarter-turn rotation, equivalently the mapping cylinder of the covering z↦z⁴ on S¹. Contracting r↦(1-s)r respects the relations and retracts X to its spine. Therefore H₀(X;Z)=Z, H₁(X;Z)=Z, all H_k for k≥2 vanish, and χ(X)=0.

The cyclic end relation puts all four free edges into one circle B. Going once around B goes four times around the spine. Thus H₁(B;Z)→H₁(X;Z) is multiplication by 4, and the long exact sequence gives **H₁(X,B;Z)=Z/4Z**. This is relative torsion, not ordinary H₁ torsion. A transverse cut at the spine has four arms, so X is not a surface.

For an explicit ideal embedding choose R>w>0, α=π(j+t)/2, and set

F(t,r,j)=((R+wr cos α)cos(2πt), (R+wr cos α)sin(2πt), wr sin α).

The end values match the gluing, and positive-radius pages do not intersect. A flexible rectangular knit will not have this exact metric: wrinkling and stretch are expected.

## Obsmetrologia interpretation without overclaim

The archived articulation labels are potential 00, active 10, absurd 11, and stable 01. The selected seam transport is τ(a,b)=(1-b,a), a Gray-code four-cycle. This is an original geometric design choice, not the canonical bilattice negation and not a knowledge-order automorphism. The status tiles use both bits without treating the both-articulated state as a knitting mistake.

At fixed r>0 the four-sheet covering has no section: any locally chosen page transports to the next page after one spine circuit. The full star bundle does have the central spine as a section. Linearizing the cyclic permutation gives P and D=P-I; DᵀD=2I-P-Pᵀ has eigenvalues 0,2,2,4. Its invariant vector is the equal-weight sum of all pages. This is a precise small transport example inspired by the corpus, not a formalized theorem about the whole theory.
