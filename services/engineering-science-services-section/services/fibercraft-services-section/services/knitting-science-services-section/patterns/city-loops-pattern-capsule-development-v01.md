# CITY LOOPS — knitter-facing capsule development, v0.1

**Intent:** three pieces that someone would want to wear without needing a lecture on mathematics or fashion theory. Inspired by early-2020s urban Chinese fashion *as documented in specific sources*, not imitations of any designer's product. Research/provenance: [CITY LOOPS science brief](../research/2026-10-12-city-loops-urban-china-knitwear-research.md).

**This is not a working pattern.** No cast-on counts, shaping, yarn yardage, sizing or chart below is certified. Do not market or hand it to knitters as finished directions. Finish each design as an actual knit and tech-edited, test-knit pattern.

## A. PLATFORM CARDIGAN — the pilot

**What a knitter sees:** a genuinely good-looking oversized cardigan: relaxed square body, prominent structured front bands, weighty rib cuffs that hold the sleeves above the wrist, a clear drop shoulder and subtly different-length front/back hems. Wear it with a white shirt, denim or wide-leg black trousers. It should look deliberate hanging open. No fake calligraphy, logos or token motifs.

- **References:** city-boy layering and relaxed proportion, the 2023 cleanfit shift, and precise Shanghai knitwear finishing.
- **Construction proposal:** two flat front pieces and one back with shaped/graded shoulders; picked-up and separately tensioned front bands; drop-shoulder sleeves knitted flat and seamed. Favor a stable shoulder seam for weight control over a flashy unsupported "seamless" claim. Optional integrated pocket only after testing sag.
- **Fabric:** DK/light-worsted smooth or softly heathered wool/wool blend, low-pill preference, handwash-compatible options documented. Gauge exploration **18–22 stitches per 10 cm**, washed/blocked, in intended stockinette/rib. This is a trial range, not a target established by measurements.
- **Colorways for prototypes:** graphite/stone with a slim cobalt accent; or muted moss/cream. Limit deliberate accents so a modern garment is visible from across the street.
- **Fit draft:** 16–24 cm positive chest ease as a hypothesis; a size series must be derived from finished body measurements and graded shoulder/armhole/lengths, not simply multiplied stitch counts. Check both relaxed/open and buttoned (if closures selected).
- **Signature surface:** an off-center narrow broken-rib or twisted-rib track running from one front band to hem. Prefer knit/purl texture and a crisp selvage. Do not call an optical curve a migrated live cable.
- **First tests:** (1) smooth stockinette + 2x2 rib gauge and curling sample; (2) directional rib + seam/band pickup sample; record blocked gauge, fabric weight, fall, stretch, and how quickly the band distorts.
- **Publication skill:** intermediate. A complete pattern must specify cardigan length, band pickup rate, shoulder seam sequence, sleeves, sizes, closures if any, and finishing.

**Styling/content treatment:** walking shots with cuffs pulled up; side/three-quarter hanging silhouette, one flat schematic, one close-up of real textured stitches. Shoot an actual sample; never use an AI render as "test-knit proof."

## B. SIGNAL VEST — sporty layering

**What a knitter sees:** a slightly cropped, roomy V-neck knit vest, broad armhole ribs, confident alternating bands, tidy lower hem and a well-resolved shoulder. Wear over an oversized shirt or thin turtleneck. References: Y2K revival, blokecore and mix-and-match city styling on Chinese platforms in 2022–2023; not a clone of a sports shirt.

- **Construction proposal:** bottom-up body in the round to armholes, front and back separately to shaped shoulders; neck V worked with marked decreases, rib bindings and tidy join at center front.
- **Colorwork:** broad stripes or intarsia patches in two-three contrasting colors; choose a method knitters can execute without long floats. No team crests/brand graphics.
- **Fit:** generous ease and enough armhole depth for the real shirt layer; test that the hem does not roll or expose a section accidentally when wearer lifts arms.
- **First tests:** color-block tension across joins; 1x1/2x2 V-neck rib on an intentionally angled sample; wash tests for color bleed.
- **Publication skill:** beginner–intermediate once shaped V neck instructions are reliable.

**Styling/content treatment:** two outfit versions—wide trousers + collared shirt and skirt + long-sleeve base layer—without assigning gender or claiming these represent all Chinese youth fashion.

## C. RIPPLE SHELL — sculptural urban evening layer

**What a knitter sees:** a compact, rippling knit top or short sweater with a strongly sculpted surface, designed for layering over or under another garment. It evokes experimental Shanghai street-style silhouettes rather than copied runway details.

- **Construction proposal:** start with flat panels showing structured short-row curves and migrating raised-rib columns; move to shaping after real swatches. If negative-space windows survive testing, bind and stabilize every cutout edge as specified operations. Otherwise use closed ripple texture.
- **Fabric:** responsive fingering/sport wool or wool-blend preferred for expressive fine relief; alternatives and skin sensitivity need separate wear tests.
- **Risks:** accidental ladders, load-bearing cutout distortion, undue cling, body exposure without consent, unintended horizontal bias, uncomfortable raised seams, shaping whose gauge changes unpredictably.
- **First tests:** one 30–40 stitch ripple panel, one reinforced/open-edge comparison, blocked measurements and stretch recovery. Have a knitter review the *actual steps* before calling this knittable.
- **Publication skill:** advanced, with large-print and written alternatives to all charts.

**Styling/content treatment:** daylight fabric movement and nighttime styling with genuinely wearable underlayers; no exoticism or imitation "Chinese" symbolism.

## Release program (proposal, no publication performed)

1. **Fashion research chapter:** an illustrated *licensed/original* source analysis distinguishing 2022 Shanghai knit experimentation from 2023 cleanfit/city-boy/Y2K currents. Chinese-language credits and review.
2. **PLATFORM development diary:** yarn sampling, ordinary/after-block swatch comparisons, model/fit comparison and a short tutorial on clean front-band finishes.
3. **PLATFORM pattern release:** accessible chart + row instructions + size schematic + yarn budget + corrections version log; no release until technical editor and independent test knitters finish.
4. **SIGNAL and RIPPLE:** publish as separate tested editions; collect actual knitter photos with explicit consent and attribution.
5. **Lookbook/video:** outfits in motion, close-up of real knitted construction, and the voices of designers/makers credited for contributed work.

## Typed handoff and gates

Science accepts the fashion sources and hypotheses. Engineering owns executable stitch charts and compilation/formatting. Operations owns independent test knitting and fit evidence. Public Services owns media rights, art direction, publication and promotion. The project ID `city-loops-2021-2024` links these; its science registry record describes actual maturity, not aspirational release status.

Before **any** pattern is called publishable, provide: fiber and gauge after blocking; dimensioned sizes with documented ease; all row/round instructions; schematics; techniques; assembly and corrections; technical editing; independent complete garments in multiple sizes; rights-cleared real photography; content accessibility and license. Document rejected concepts rather than confusing failure with completion.

**Comparison principle:** "Is it clothing a knitter wants to wear?" precedes "Does the mathematical construction look clever?" The knit science exists to make those garments more expressive, reproducible and structurally sound.
