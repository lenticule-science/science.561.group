# PLATFORM CARDIGAN — first two swatches, v0.1

These are **actual stitch-by-stitch experimental swatches**, not the full cardigan pattern. No one has yet knit or independently tech-edited them. One tests a picked-up, clean vertical cardigan band; the other tests whether a visually shifting rib reads well as contemporary minimal knitwear. They are designed to be ordinary knitted cloth, not topological sculpture.

Use the yarn/needle combination considered for the cardigan: start with DK/light worsted wool-blend and needles around 4.5–5 mm, **adjust to suit your yarn**. You need approximately 10–30 g per swatch depending on yarn, a tapestry needle, ruler, optional row markers and blocking supplies; **this is an unmeasured estimate**. Work flat. K = knit, P = purl, K1tbl = knit one through back loop, RS = right side, WS = wrong side. Count the actual test samples, not a predicted final garment gauge.

## Swatch A — the vertical front-band trial

**Question:** Can a 2×2 rib front band picked up along an ordinary cardigan edge sit flat, look crisp and not contract the body unpleasantly?

1. Cast on **36 stitches**, using a method suitable for your yarn. Place a removable RS marker. Knit flat.
2. **Rows 1–48:** On **odd RS rows**, K36. On **even WS rows**, K3, P30, K3. This creates a 30-stitch stockinette field with three garter edge stitches each side. Count: **3 + 30 + 3 = 36 stitches** on every row.
3. Bind off knitwise on the next RS row, loosely. Keep the 48 completed fabric rows distinct from this bind-off.
4. With the **RS facing**, along **one 48-row side edge** and using an appropriately sized needle, pick up and knit **36 stitches**: pick up three stitches for every four fabric rows, consistently across all 12 consecutive four-row groups (12×3=36). Mark the edge into four-row groups first if this helps. Do not treat the 36 picked-up stitches as extra body rows.
5. Turn. Work **10 flat rib rows**, both RS and WS: repeat **K2, P2** nine times on **every row**. All 36 stitches are worked. Because the count is divisible by four and the piece is turned each row, this maintains 2×2 rib columns.
6. Bind off loosely in the visible rib pattern on the next row, avoiding a tight edge. Weave ends without over-stretching.
7. Measure the **unblocked** panel and band separately. Wash/block as yarn allows, lay flat without pulling the band into shape, dry and remeasure. Check rippling, scalloping, edge tension, the angle at which the band meets stockinette, and whether the band looks like polished clothing.

**Alternative to compare in the next revision, not part of this executable v0.1:** a 7-of-10 or 4-of-5 pickup ratio; change only one variable after a documented first sample.

## Swatch B — the off-center stepping rib

**Question:** Does a subtle stepped directional texture read as a controlled visual accent on a garment, or just a messy/discontinuous line?

1. Cast on **36 stitches** and mark the RS. Work flat for **48 rows**, using the segment table below.
2. On each **odd RS row** in a segment, work: **K3, P(a), [K1tbl, P1] four times, P(b), K3**.
3. On each **even WS row** in that segment, work from the other edge: **K3, K(b), [K1, P1] four times, K(a), K3**.
4. Use the segment's `a` and `b` stitch counts, not the number of the row, in those formulas:

| Rows | a (first RS ground) | b (last RS ground) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1–12 | 6 | 16 |
| 13–24 | 7 | 15 |
| 25–36 | 8 | 14 |
| 37–48 | 9 | 13 |

For every row, the stitch count is **3 + a + 8 + b + 3 = 36**, because **a + b = 22**. The RS track itself always has eight stitches. The corresponding WS repeats reverse the sequence so the same columns are knit or purled on their correct sides.

5. Bind off on the next RS row without tightening the raised track.
6. Photograph the unblocked surface flat and with a fold. Wash/block without forcing a bias; dry and measure. Check whether the track moves visually by about three stitch columns across 48 rows, whether boundaries look broken, and whether it twists or pinches. Measure stockinette/reverse-stockinette gauge in a ground section, **not over the track**.

**Important physical claim boundary:** the track steps because the knit/purl boundary changes at rows 13, 25 and 37. This is **optical reallocation**, not actual transfer of live knitted wales. If it looks jagged or reads as a mistake, reject this method or replace it with a separately charted crossing/transfer. Do not say Obsmetrologia proves its drape.

## A knitter's outcome record

For **each** swatch record: yarn maker/fiber/lot, actual grams consumed, needle size, exact cast-on and bind-off, any altered stitches or failed operations, row and stitch counts, unblocked and blocked dimensions, band width, fabric/track appearance at arm's length, spring and hang, skin feel, wash method, photos with ruler (RS and WS) and one short actual-work video if possible.

**Pass/fail in ordinary language:** Is the band clean? Does the direction look intentional? Would somebody choose to wear this cardigan over a shirt? If not, revise the garment before expanding into a full pattern.
