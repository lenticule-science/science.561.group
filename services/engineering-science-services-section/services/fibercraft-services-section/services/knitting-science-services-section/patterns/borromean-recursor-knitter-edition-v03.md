# Borromean Recursor

**A hollow knitted body with three large looped handles. Each large handle has a bulge carrying three smaller looped handles.** You make the tubes first, braid them while their ends are still free, then join each tube back to its own starting body or bulge.

The word **handle** means a tube bent into an arch and attached at both ends, like a teapot handle. It does not mean an extra piece to sew onto the surface.

[Diagram: Left: the body has A/B/C finish openings above A/B/C start openings. Three long tubes hang from the start openings, with a bulge near each base and free ends. Right: one tube bends back to its matching finish opening, forming a handle. Crossings are omitted here; page 8 gives them.]

### Work in this order

| Step | What you do | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Knit the body, leaving six waste-yarn sections. | 5 |
| 2 | Knit three long tubes from the lower openings. Each gets a bulge. | 6 |
| 3 | Make three short tubes at the matching return openings. | 7 |
| 4 | Braid the long tubes, fit their return lengths, then sew the ends to the short tubes. | 8–10 |
| 5 | Add a set of three smaller handles to each bulge, using the same method. | 11 |

> This is an experimental construction, not a test-knit pattern. Use the small technique sample on page 4 before committing to the full object. No mathematical vocabulary is needed to work these instructions.

This edition replaces the earlier PDF. It keeps the branching construction and eight crossings. It replaces the vague circular grafts with ordinary sewn, open-ended joins and adds an actual return-length fitting step.

# Tools, labels and stitch language

**Suggested starting yarn:** smooth DK-weight yarn. Use matching yarn weights throughout. A separate color for each of the three main tubes can help, but labels are required even when colors differ. Avoid fuzzy yarn for the first trial.

**Needles and other tools:** double-pointed needles or a long circular needle for small-circumference knitting; begin around 3.5–4 mm and adjust to the fabric. Have smooth contrasting waste yarn, a blunt sewing needle, locking markers, spare yarn for holders, a row counter, measuring tape and paper labels. You will have several sets of stitches on hold, but knit only one tube at a time.

### Gauge and size

Planning gauge: **24 stitches and 32 rounds = 10 cm / 4 in** in stockinette worked in the round. This is a reference, not a measured sample. Knit a circular swatch. Use firm enough fabric to show the openings, but not fabric so stiff that the tubes resist bending.

| Part | At the planning gauge, before shaping and blocking |
| --- | --- |
| Main body | About 40 cm / 15¾ in around its widest part; about 22 cm / 8¾ in from cast-on to gathered end. |
| Large tube | 10 cm / 4 in around the narrow section. The first 254 rounds are about 79 cm / 31 in long; add the return length required on page 9. |
| Small tube | 5 cm / 2 in around. The first 112 rounds are about 35 cm / 13¾ in long; add the return length required on page 9. |

**Yarn quantity and finished overall size are not yet established.** The return lengths depend on your actual braid. Do not buy an exact kit on the basis of the old loop-count estimate. The one-handle sample checks the technique, not the full project’s yarn requirement.

### Abbreviations used in the round tables

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| k12 | Knit 12 stitches. |
| kfb | Knit into the front and then the back of the next stitch: one increase. |
| k2tog | Knit the next two stitches together: one decrease. |
| (k2, kfb) 12 times | Repeat “knit 2, then kfb” a total of 12 times. |
| On hold | Transfer every live stitch to waste yarn and secure its ends; do not draw the stitches together. |

All knitting is in the round; do not turn at the end of a round. Place a beginning-of-round marker and slip it every round. “Pickup round” is the pickup circuit on page 3; it is **not** numbered round 1. Restart the round counter for each new body, long tube or short tube. Abbreviations follow ordinary U.S. knitting usage [3].

SPECIAL TECHNIQUE · USED FOR EVERY HANDLE

# Make an opening, then grow a tube

### A. Put a waste-yarn section into the fabric

When told to make an **11-stitch waste-yarn section**: drop the main yarn without cutting it. With a separate scrap of contrasting yarn, knit the next 11 stitches. Slip those same 11 stitches purlwise back to the left needle, keeping their order. Pick up the main yarn again and knit the 11 stitches. Continue the round as instructed. Leave both waste-yarn ends accessible and label the section immediately [1].

For a **5-stitch section**, do exactly the same thing over 5 stitches. Do not cast on, bind off, cut the main fabric or add stitches. The round’s stitch count stays unchanged.

### B. Open ONE section after the surrounding fabric is finished

With the outside of the fabric facing you, put the main-yarn loops directly below the waste yarn on one needle, and the loops directly above it on another. Catch **11 on each needle for a large opening, or 5 on each for a small opening**. Only then unpick the waste yarn [2].

[Diagram: A five-stitch waste-yarn section lies between five secured main-yarn loops above and five below. One additional stitch is picked up at each side after the waste yarn is removed. This gives twelve stitches around one hole.]

Join the tube yarn and knit the lower needle’s stitches. Pick up and knit 1 stitch at that side of the hole. Rotate the work to reach the upper needle, knit its stitches, then pick up and knit 1 stitch at the other side. Distribute the stitches for working in the round and place a beginning-of-round marker [2].

| Opening | Stitches after the pickup round |
| --- | --- |
| 11-stitch waste-yarn section | 11 + 1 side stitch + 11 + 1 side stitch = **24** |
| 5-stitch waste-yarn section | 5 + 1 side stitch + 5 + 1 side stitch = **12** |

> The two rows of recovered stitches belong to ONE hole. Knit around them as a single tube. Do not knit two separate flaps, and do not close the hole like a sock toe.

The two side stitches are kept in this design. Do not decrease them away. A missed main-yarn loop must be recovered rather than compensated for with an extra pickup. Sources [1] and [2] include technique demonstrations; they do not validate this sculpture.

TECHNIQUE SAMPLE · NOT A REPLACEMENT DESIGN

# First, make one small handle

This sample tests the two uncertain handwork steps before you invest in the sculpture: opening a waste-yarn section and joining two open tubes. It uses the same 12-stitch tubes as the nine small handles. It does **not** test the three-tube braid or prove the full object will drape well.

### Knit the sample’s base

Cast on 24 stitches and join in the round without twisting. Place a beginning-of-round marker. Do not stuff the sample.

| Round | Instruction | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–6 | Knit every stitch. | 24 |
| 7 | Make a 5-stitch waste-yarn section (page 3), then k19. Label it START. | 24 |
| 8–18 | Knit every stitch. | 24 |
| 19 | Make a 5-stitch waste-yarn section, then k19. Label it FINISH. | 24 |
| 20–24 | Knit every stitch. | 24 |

Bind off loosely around the base’s rim. Leave both ends of this base tube open; it is only a practice piece.

### Make the long part and the short return

**Long part:** open START by the method on page 3. You have 12 stitches after the pickup round. Knit 36 further rounds, then hold the 12 stitches securely on waste yarn.

**Short return:** open FINISH in the same way: 12 stitches. Knit 4 further rounds, then hold those 12 stitches. The base now carries one long tube and one short tube.

### Join the two open ends

Bend the long tube into a loose arch so its open end meets the short return. They should meet without pulling the base out of shape. Add plain rounds to the long tube if necessary. Bind off both rims loosely without gathering them. Follow the sewing instructions on page 10.

> You should now have an arch attached to the base at both ends. There is a hole under the arch. The two knitted tube openings have become one continuous passage, not two closed tips sewn together.

### Check the sample

There should be no dropped stitches at either pickup, and the join should bend without puckering. The sewing must not catch the opposite wall of the tube. A small pinhole beside a pickup can be closed with its yarn tail without closing the main opening.

If the pickup or seam is unclear, stop at this sample rather than guess across twelve handles. Record the exact step and stitch count. This is a test of the instructions, not a test of the knitter.

THESE ARE SMALL OPEN TUBES ON THE MAIN BODY

# 3. Make the three short returns

A return is simply the short tube to which a long tube will be joined. It is not a row worked back across the long tube.

| Make this short tube | Where it begins | What to knit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A-finish | Body section labelled A-finish | Open the 11-stitch section: 24 stitches after the pickup round. Knit 4 further rounds. Hold all 24 stitches. |
| B-finish | Body section labelled B-finish | Open to 24 stitches. Knit 4 further rounds. Hold all 24 stitches. |
| C-finish | Body section labelled C-finish | Open to 24 stitches. Knit 4 further rounds. Hold all 24 stitches. |

Join a fresh length of yarn for each return; cut it with a 15 cm / 6 in tail when finished. Keep the labels attached to the short tubes. They remain open. The body now has three long tubes and three short tubes attached to it.

### Prepare to braid

**Secure every live stitch on flexible waste yarn.** Move knitting needles out of the way. You are about to move whole tubes, not transfer stitches on a cable needle.

Lay the work on a table with the body at the far side and the three long tubes pointing toward you. Turn the body until the long tubes read **A, B, C from your left to right**. Check the names at the bases, not just the movable ends. Keep the three bulges beside the body, above the braid area.

Put the short return tubes underneath and behind the area where you will braid, with A behind the A lane, B behind B, and C behind C. Their paths must remain uncrossed. Support the body so it cannot roll as you move the long tubes. A removable sheet of paper beneath the braid can help separate the front braid from the back returns; do not fasten anything through the knitting.

> Finish all three long tubes before starting the braid. Do not join any long end to a return until all eight crossings are placed and checked.

### What “over” means on the next page

When the chart says **A over B**, lift A and lay it across B so the two swap left-to-right positions. A lies nearer your eyes at the crossing. C stays in its lane. Secure that crossing loosely, then make the next crossing farther along the tubes, toward their free ends. Do not undo the crossing you just made.

Tube letters never change, even when their positions change. The numbered fabric markers are spacing guides, not stitches to work. Keep the bulges out of the braid and leave enough slack to bend the tubes.

ASSEMBLY CHART · READ FROM TOP TO BOTTOM

# 4. Braid the three long tubes

Start A–B–C at the attached ends. Make crossing 1 near fabric marker 1, crossing 2 near marker 2, and continue toward the free ends. At a crossing, a gap in a line means that tube passes **under** the uninterrupted line. No knitting occurs during these eight moves.

[Diagram: Read top to bottom. 1 A over B -> B A C. 2 B over A -> A B C. 3 B over C -> A C B. 4 C over B -> A B C. 5 B over A -> B A C. 6 A over B -> A B C. 7 C over B -> A C B. 8 B over C -> A B C. Each crossing swaps the indicated adjacent tubes; other crossings remain in place.]

> Stop with the free ends in A–B–C order. Keep all eight crossings in place. Ends returning to their original order does not mean you should straighten out the braid.

Use loose temporary ties around crossing pairs if needed, without cinching the knitted tubes. Remove ties only after the joins are complete. If the order differs at any stage, undo to the last correct crossing rather than adding a new one.

DO THIS BEFORE BINDING OFF OR SEWING

# 5. Fit the plain return sections

The free ends must travel back to the three short return tubes without passing through the front braid. The original pattern did not establish how much length that takes. **The return length is fitted here, on the actual work.** There is no fixed final round count.

[Diagram: Side view of one handle route. A large body is at the top. The long tube descends in front, curves around the bottom, and returns behind to the short tube on the body. The eight crossings are in the front section; the return is plain and must not cross other returns.]

**1.** Leave the braid on the table. Bring each free end around the near edge of the braid and underneath it. Return A to A-finish, B to B-finish and C to C-finish. The three plain returns run side by side behind the braid. Do not thread an end through a knitted tube, through a finished handle, or between the front crossings.

**2.** Measure the intended route from **marker 8** to the matching short return rim. Include the curve around the braid and a little slack. This measurement is the required length of the plain section after marker 8, not the total tube length.

**3.** Compare that route with the actual knitted section after marker 8. Add plain rounds to that long tube, keeping **24 stitches**, until the section reaches comfortably; allow about 2 cm / ¾ in of ease. Rejoin yarn as needed. Hold the stitches again, put the return in position and recheck. Fit each tube separately; do not stretch it into place.

**4.** Only when all three pairs meet comfortably, bind off each long rim and its matching short rim loosely, knitwise, **around the circle**. Do not pull yarn through all the stitches. Keep the A/B/C labels attached.

> Binding off finishes a circular edge but leaves the hole open. Gathering the stitches closes the hole. You need the first operation, not the second.

The figure is a routing diagram, not a rigid form or a forecast of drape. A folded, curved braid may need less return length than a straight one. The test is whether all six open rims meet without pulling, twisting the braid or adding crossings. Record extra rounds for each tube.

OPEN-RIM JOIN · NOT A SOCK-TOE CLOSURE

# 6. Sew each handle closed into a loop

Join the long A tube to **A-finish**, B to **B-finish**, and C to **C-finish**. Work one pair at a time without changing the braid or the return paths. This edition deliberately uses a sewn join rather than claiming a seamless circular graft.

[Diagram: Two open circular rims, one from the long tube and one from its matching short return, each marked at top, bottom, left and right. Bring the rims together end to end. Sew only one adjacent edge from each rim, all around, without catching either tube’s opposite wall.]

**1. Align the ends.** Keep both tubes right side out. Butt their open ends together as though joining two pieces of hose. The inside passages must meet. Do not push one tube inside the other.

**2. Mark four matching points.** With the rims held together, match top to top, bottom to bottom, and the two sides. Use removable markers. On a 24-stitch rim the points are 6 edge stitches apart; on a 12-stitch rim they are 3 apart. Follow the physical rim around; do not reverse its order or give a tube a full twist.

**3. Sew the first quarter.** Thread a blunt needle with matching yarn, about 4 times the rim circumference. Secure it beside one marker. Take the needle under the two strands of one bound-off edge stitch on the long tube, then under the two strands of the directly adjoining edge stitch on the short tube. Pull through gently. Repeat at the next pair, making a simple overcast seam. Catch only the adjacent rims, never the walls across either hole.

**4. Continue all the way around.** Sew 6 pairs between quarter markers for a large join, or 3 for a small join. Count 24 paired edge positions in total for a large join, or 12 for a small one. The last bind-off junction counts as one edge position. Keep the seam flexible, not cinched. Secure and weave in the ends without closing the passage.

> A correct join joins TWO tube openings into ONE longer passage. It does not flatten a mouth, and it does not join the front and back of the same tube.

This is a structural rim-to-rim sewing instruction, not a claim of an invisible finish. Check it on the sample first. A knitter already confident in live-to-live circular grafting can use that technique instead, but would need to keep both rims live; do not mix the two methods partway through a join. This edition’s default is the sewn join above.

### Problems with a specific remedy

**Opening count is wrong:** check the recovered upper and lower loops and the one pickup at each side. Recover dropped loops before proceeding. **A seam puckers:** unpick it and resew more loosely; do not try to block out a closed passage. **A return does not reach:** add plain rounds before binding off. **Tube order differs from the braid chart:** undo back to the first wrong crossing. **A smaller set catches another handle:** undo its joins and route it beside its own bulge, not through the neighboring work.

### Status and changes from the old PDF

The fixed-round stitch arithmetic and the eight labelled crossing moves have been rechecked against the underlying specification. **No one has physically test-knit this design for this edition.** Tube flexibility, practical hand access, seam comfort, finished appearance and yarn requirement remain unverified. The changing return lengths are deliberately measured on the work, not falsely presented as a tested fixed count.

Changes: all openings are called start or finish; every part has an explicit work order and checkpoint; the crossings read top to bottom; the sample isolates the pickup and sewing techniques; sewn joins replace underspecified grafts; and return length is fitted after braiding. This edition does not claim to reproduce a generated image or to embody a proved theorem of Obsmetrologia.
