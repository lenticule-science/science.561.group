# Design decisions and rejections

## FOURFOLD / The Obstruction Bloom — retained as rejected garment direction

**Original concept:** four rectangular panels representing phases/charges and cyclically joined at a common spine. Each panel was 24 stitches by 288 rows in the written source, with four choices of texture and one continuous outer free rim after joining.

**Scientific interest:** a branched surface and a relative topological obstruction constructed from identified edges. **Design failure:** all interesting topology came from the seams; the individual knitting was simply rectangles. Initial output was an image rather than a chart. User explicitly rejected “four panels.” The PDF and machine specification are archived as educational/failed prototypes, not as apparel.

**Validation status:** digital specification only, not knitted. Render does not demonstrate physical shape.

## BORROMEAN RECURSOR — retained as a topology experiment, not the target sweater

**Original concept:** hub with six ports; three primary tubular handles; each primary handle sprouts three daughter handles; total twelve handles. Braid routing uses an eight-crossing commutator-like arrangement. Original packaging claimed counted rounds, braid algebra checks and circular grafts.

**Feedback:** a knitter could not interpret the original directions, especially the exact joining/needle manipulation. The revised v0.3 instructions use human craft terms and open-rim sewn tube joins rather than asserting seamless knitting; they introduce a practice handle and measured rather than prescriptive return lengths.

**Design failure:** a topological octopus does not answer the desire for compelling clothing. Image generation emphasized sculptural knots, not wearable styling. The mathematics may have independent scientific use, but the chart has not been physically tested.

**Validation status:** formal/checker claims in source archive require independent rerun; no knitted physical evidence. An illustrative image is not a topology certificate.

## Aesthetic pivot: THE UNDERTOW

**Source user instruction:** “right like a WILD cable knit sweater right? stop giving math vibes think what actual clothing and aesthetic would want lol. answer more like a knitter than mathematician.”

**Desirable look:** natural oatmeal, dramatic oversized fisherman/Aran, sculptural high-relief cables, generous negative space, oversized but wearable, casually styled with jeans and boots; forest/rivers/root/currents language as internal design inspiration. Not a logo-covered or deliberately “mathy” costume.

**Core direction:** one fat asymmetrical braided rope begins on left cuff, climbs front of sleeve, remains continuous across the shoulder/body knit, and migrates diagonally across the chest, eventually splitting into fine traveling cables and twisted-stitch paths. Reverse stockinette background, low-relief back, quieter right sleeve, deep ribbing, folded crew.

**Additional named directions (not production-ready):**

- **The Snarl:** deliberately clashing, raised knots; concentrated cable density.
- **The Mycelium:** tiny twisted columns from hem inflate into branching structures.
- **The Impossible Aran:** perceived over/under/vanish/reappear through layered knit structures, not merely a false topology diagram.

These are future garments only after a knitter-rated design brief, counts, swatches and independent test knitting.

## Decisions that can and cannot move forward

**Decided:** wearer-first visual standard; sideways grown-on arm-to-body construction for Undertow first prototype; a quiet ground; one dominating cable; begin with a defined 34-stitch/48-row core swatch.

**Not decided or demonstrated:** exact traveling-cable split/migration chart, neckline shaping at every row, test-knit gauge, final yarn/yardage, sizing/grade validation, wear testing, physical viability of any Borromean graft or return route, use of any STL as a knitting machine instruction.

**Failure-prevention rule:** never deliver another image as “the knitting chart”; never claim a pattern executable if another knitter cannot identify all stitch manipulations and hand positions.
