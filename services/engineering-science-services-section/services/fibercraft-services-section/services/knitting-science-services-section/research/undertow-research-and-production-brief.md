# THE UNDERTOW — from fashion brief to tested knitting pattern

## Garment proposition

An oversized, gently cropped, high-relief Aran pullover, undyed oatmeal wool, shoulder-friendly volume, fitted deep rib cuffs, 5 cm rib hem, folded crew. **One thick braided cable** moves without an applied-on seam from the wearer's left wrist up the front-facing sleeve and onto the left upper chest; it branches into smaller lines. Back and right sleeve remain subordinate to this feature. The main feature must be legible with the wearer's arms at rest, not just on a layout table.

Clothing criteria: attractive and comfortable for someone with no mathematical context; works over jeans, boots and everyday layers; not stiff or poking at skin; mobility when lifting and folding arms; finished sizing truthful.

## Construction selected for prototype

One flat, sideways grown-on piece, working from **left cuff → left sleeve → left torso → front/back neck opening → right torso → right sleeve → right cuff**. Underarm and side seams on underside; separately picked-up hem and neck ribbing. Actual technical plan [source](../patterns/the-undertow-design-plan-v01.md).

120 cm finished-chest prototype at about 20 cm positive ease; finished body length ~56 cm; target background gauge 18 stitches/26 rows per 10 cm (blocked), 5 mm main/4 mm ribbing as initial trial; Aran/heavy-worsted non-superwash springy wool; ~1,500–1,800 m *provisional, unmeasured*. First body working budget ~184 stitches after growing sleeve extensions; this is not a validated garment-wide stitch count.

Provisional finished chest family: **104, 112, 120, 128, 136, 144 cm**. These are target dimensions not test-knit sizes.

## Knitter-facing first swatch (actually specified)

Flat, **34 stitches × 48 rows**, arranged garter border 3 / reverse stockinette 5 / central 18-stitch braid / reverse stockinette 5 / garter border 3.

Default odd right-side row: K3, P5, K18, P5, K3.
Default even wrong-side row: K3, K5, P18, K5, K3.

Within each 24-row repeat, **row 7** is K3, P5, C12F, K6, P5, K3; **row 19** is K3, P5, K6, C12B, P5, K3. Repeat the whole 24-row block once (48 rows total). C12F/B means hold six stitches front/back, knit six, knit held six. Increases and decreases: none; all rows consume and produce 34 stitches.

This swatch measures braid bulk, stiffness, curvature, width contraction, row gauge and bias. It is **not** the as-yet-unwritten moving cable. Work and block it before selecting final yarn or body stitch counts.

## Design uncertainty — needs new tested techniques

Travel an 18-stitch high-relief braid diagonally without broken wale appearance, excessive short-side tension, neckline crossing, or accidental leaning. Contrast:

1. deliberate knit-purl boundary reallocation for optical travel; and
2. actual small stitch-crossing transfers with localized increases/decreases and cable orders for structural travel.

Swatch both. Do not confuse an apparent raised track with physically transferring the live stitches to a new course position. Run a local loop-graph legality check only after actual operations are specified.

Also validate the front/back split for a real crew neck, with shaping (not abrupt blocked squares), final seam positions and capacity to wear sleeves without cable rotation.

## Development gates

1. **Material gate:** agree yarn, gauge, blocking, yarn weight and actual braid cross-section; reject painful/rigid rope.
2. **Swatch gate:** independently knit and measure the 34×48 hero braid; document blocked/unblocked size and yarn mass.
3. **Travel gate:** a ~45-stitch ×70-row swatch demonstrating ten-stitch diagonal motion in a specified, repeatable knit operation program; compare alternatives.
4. **Construction gate:** half-scale sleeve-to-body-to-neck cloth mockup; check fold direction, two-sided underarm seams, neck corridor clearance.
5. **Prototype gate:** one wearable 120 cm example; target tolerance after blocking chest ±3 cm, length ±2 cm; room at underarm, arms-up movement, cable visible at rest.
6. **Publication gate:** tech-edited chart, written row-by-row instructions, all neck transitions and sizes; three independent test knits in distinct sizes; no math needed to read pattern.
7. **Formalization gate (independent):** encode verified patterns as typed loop graphs/ops and, where available, separate Lean statements from empirical comfort and mechanics.

**Immediate next step:** hand-knit the defined 34×48 braid swatch and conduct a traveling-cable technique study. Do not start full-body production based on unvalidated row budgets.

## What to record at each physical test

Needle sizes, actual yarn/fiber/lot, dry yarn weight, swatch photo with ruler, RS/WS conventions, number of live stitches after each operation, blocking method, stitch and row gauge, cable relief height, shrinkage, stretch and drape, needle access, subjective skin feel, observed cable torque, and whether an independent knitter could follow the directions.

If the swatch looks ugly, the science objective failed the design requirement even if the mathematical program passes a formal check.
