# CITY LOOPS — research brief

## Proposition and boundary

Develop a **media series and original, knittable garment patterns** informed by documented urban Chinese fashion during approximately 2021–2024, initially using 2022–2023 evidence. Start with the clothing: proportion, layering, surface, movement, material and how people style knitwear. Use Obsmetrologia and ObsmetrologiaFibercraft where they help formulate checkable loop operations; do not lead a clothing audience with speculative topology.

This is **not** a claim that there is one homogeneous "Chinese street style," nor a commission to reproduce a specific designer's garment, photo, print, logo, cultural garment, or pattern. Separate (a) a dated, attributed observation, (b) our original design interpretation, and (c) a tested knitting instruction. Chinese urban scenes differ by city, age, gender expression, and platform. Terms such as "city boy" have transnational histories; do not rebrand them as Chinese inventions.

**Project maturity:** evidence collected and design program specified. No CITY LOOPS garments, swatches, stitch charts, test-knit results, photographic assets, or published pattern files have yet been produced. A visual concept is not knitting instructions.

**Evidence level:** qualitative editorial accounts, fashion-week program material and a design essay. These sources establish that the styles were discussed, not how prevalent they were across all Chinese cities. Photography must be separately cleared or re-shot. Do not infer precise fabric gauge, fit or pattern construction from a photo.

**Research expansion needed:** sample contemporaneous Chinese-language street-style and maker commentary beyond the fashion-week/retail lens; compare Shanghai with at least one other city where sources permit; include the perspectives of Chinese makers/designers and, if commissioning interviews, retain consent and rights records. Distinguish knit fabric from hand knitting and industrial machine knitting.

## Design hypotheses: three distinct garments

The [CITY LOOPS capsule development source](../patterns/city-loops-pattern-capsule-development-v01.md) is the technical design handoff, not a finished pattern.

1. **PLATFORM CARDIGAN — quiet volume / city-boy × cleanfit.** Unisex boxy layering cardigan; roomy sleeves, deliberate contrast-facing seams, deep substantial rib bands, one modest directional knit rib that reads while worn. Styled over a collared shirt with wide trousers, dark denim or plain shirt. The attractive object must be the cardigan, not the brand mark. **Pilot; intermediate-level target**.
2. **SIGNAL VEST — athletic/color-blocked layering / Y2K × blokecore.** Slightly cropped relaxed V-neck vest with carefully proportioned graphic knitted bands and a strong armhole silhouette; viable over an oversized shirt. Avoid replicating a team jersey or trademarked badge. **Beginner–intermediate target** after colorwork swatch.
3. **RIPPLE SHELL — sculptural knit / 2022 Shanghai street-style experiments.** Close or semi-close-fit layered top with rippling columns, negative-space engineering or knit/purl relief rather than decorative mathematical diagrams. The shaped fabric should still move with the body; any cutouts require robust edge construction. **Advanced target**, and the highest failure risk.

These are complementary capsule propositions, not descriptions of garments physically present in the sources.

## Wearer-first technical questions

- How much positive ease produces intentional volume rather than a shapeless rectangle across different bodies and layer thicknesses? Measure both static silhouette and arms-forward movement.
- What gauge, yarn torsion and blocking method give crisp hems and falling folds without excessive weight, curling or growth?
- Can visual ribs travel or wave while each row has an explicit loop operation and admissible stitch count? Separate optical displacement from actual stitch transfer.
- Can an openwork/cutout edge withstand ordinary wear and washing without laddering, pinching, or unintended exposure?
- How do photographs of the same garment on different body shapes and with different layers change the style claim? Avoid one-size-fits-all or gender-coded fit assumptions.

## Research-to-pattern-to-media protocol

**Source observation (Science):** dated original source; city/platform; maker/designer attribution; observable clothing features; confidence and rights status.

**Design hypothesis (Science):** specific garment, silhouette, yarn/texture strategy, knit operation hypothesis, risks; preserve uncertainty.

**Executable construction (Red Cup Engineering / knitter):** all sizes, gauges, stitch definitions, shaping and row/round counts, chart and written instructions, finishing, accessibility alternatives.

**Independent sample and wear tests (Coesite Operations):** blocking measurements, reproducibility, comfort, ease, corrections, yarn budgets, fit on multiple bodies, photographed prototype. No pattern publication before real swatch and test knit.

**Public media (Causeway Public Services):** carefully credited article/mini-documentary, lookbook, instructional video, downloadable text-plus-chart PDF, accessible image descriptions and pattern metadata; original photography or independently licensed assets only.

**Feedback:** corrections and knitter outcomes produce new typed research questions and revisions to the canonical pattern version. Do not convert creative rhetoric into an asserted theorem or claim industrial hand/machine parity.

## Publication quality gates

1. Source sheet includes dated Chinese and international coverage, creditable references and a non-extractive rights plan. Specify Shanghai where that is all the evidence shows.
2. Each draft pattern has a complete stitch-level program, legible abbreviations, RS/WS conventions, shaping, assembly and scaled measurements; the reader need not know Obsmetrologia.
3. Independent knitter completes swatch/prototype; publication pattern gets technical editing and multiple completed test knits across size range. State all untested elements in the meantime.
4. Media demonstrates the clothes moving and being styled. Include blocked garment dimensions, ease, yarn alternatives, skill/time estimates, legible diagrams and accessible transcripts/captions.
5. Original works: no protected logo reuse, unlicensed fashion photos, copied designer charts or falsely attributed cultural patterns. Invite qualified Chinese fashion/maker review and paid collaboration before presenting commentary as definitive.
6. Every garment links its source notes to the knitting pattern revision and media version; broken links and unexplained changes block release.

## Immediate action and receiving

Start with the PLATFORM CARDIGAN construction and proportion study. Prepare sketches and **two actual 30–40 stitch swatches** of a structured rib + smooth ground (exact counts to be specified), then develop full stitch chart, graded size plan and a demonstrably comfortable cardigan. Source further Chinese-led visual criticism in parallel. Subsequent garments should reuse the tested publication pipeline.
