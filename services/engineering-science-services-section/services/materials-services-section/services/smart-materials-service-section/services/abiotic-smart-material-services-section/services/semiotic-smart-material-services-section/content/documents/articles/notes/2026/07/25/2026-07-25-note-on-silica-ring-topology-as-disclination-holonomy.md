# Silica Ring Topology as Disclination Holonomy

## 1. A discrete curvature law on the hexagonal lattice

**Definition 1.1 (frame, turn, walk, development).** Let `Λ = ℤ[ζ]` be the Eisenstein-integer
lattice, `ζ = e^{iπ/3}` a primitive sixth root of unity, with basis `(1, ζ)`. A *frame* is a
vector `v ∈ Λ`. The *turn* operator `R: Λ → Λ` is multiplication by `ζ`: `R(a + bζ) = -b + (a+b)ζ`.
A *walk* is a finite sequence of turn-counts `w = (t_1, …, t_n) ∈ ℕ^n`. Its *development* from a
frame `f` and position `p` applies, at each step `t_i`, the rotation `R^{t_i}` to the frame and
advances the position by the new frame vector; write `Dev(w, f, p)` for the resulting (frame,
position) pair. The *boundary loop* of a `k`-coordinated lattice site is the walk of `k` unit
turns, `∂_k = (1, 1, …, 1)` (`k` times).

**Theorem 1.2 (the turn group has order six).** `R` has order exactly 6 on the orbit of `1`:
`R^6(1) = 1`, and `R^m(1) ≠ 1` for `0 < m < 6`.

*Proof.* `R^6(1+0ζ) = 1+0ζ` by direct computation of the six successive images
`1, ζ, ζ-1, -1, -ζ, 1-ζ, 1`; each of the five intermediate images is distinct from `1`. ∎

**Theorem 1.3 (frame-closure law).** For a walk `w` and starting frame `f`, the developed frame
depends only on the total turn count reduced mod 6:

```
Dev(w, f, p).frame = f  ⟺  Σ_i t_i ≡ 0 (mod 6).
```

Reading one unit turn as 60° of physical angle, this is the angle-sum law

```
Σ θ_i ≡ 0 (mod 360°),   θ_i ∈ {60°},
```

a rotational-holonomy closure condition on the frame group generated by Theorem 1.2.

*Proof.* The frame after the walk is `R^{Σ t_i}(f)`, since composition of turn operators adds
exponents; by Theorem 1.2 this equals `f` exactly when `Σ t_i ≡ 0 (mod 6)`. ∎

**Corollary 1.4 (coordination and angular deficit).** The boundary loop `∂_k` closes flat iff
`k ≡ 0 (mod 6)`. A `k`-coordinated site carries angular deficit `Δθ = (6 − k)·60°`: zero at
`k = 6` (flat); `+60°` at `k = 5` — a *disclination* of deficit one quantum, whose loop first
closes only after six traversals, `30` `= 5 · 6` unit steps, not after one; `−60°` at `k = 7`,
the opposite charge.

**Theorem 1.5 (dipole annihilation and the Burgers vector).** A `5–7` disclination pair
(a "dipole") at zero separation annihilates: its combined boundary walk develops to the identity
frame and position. At separation `s ≥ 0`, the dipole's circuit is *frame-neutral* — the
deficit and excess quanta cancel exactly, and the walk returns the original frame — but not
*position*-neutral: the developed position is displaced by a translational holonomy (Burgers
vector)

```
B(s) = (0, −s),   B(s) = 0  ⟺  s = 0,
```

extending additively over any many-body configuration of dipoles: the total Burgers vector of a
configuration is the sum of its pairwise separations.

*Proof sketch.* Frame-neutrality follows because the deficit quantum of the five-cone and the
excess quantum of the seven-cone are inverse rotations (Corollary 1.4) and the two half-turn
reorientations needed to traverse the separation and return compose to a full turn (the
identity), so the net frame action is trivial by Theorem 1.2. The translational part accumulates
one unit of displacement per unit of separation traversed once each way, giving `B(s) = (0,-s)`
by direct summation of the walk's straight segments; additivity over a configuration follows
because frame-neutral segments compose without cross-terms. ∎

**Theorem 1.6 (obstruction descent).** Let a configuration of dipoles be given by its list of
separations. Sliding every dipole one unit toward contact defines a map on configurations whose
total-separation functional `Σ s_i`:

1. is non-increasing under one slide;
2. is strictly decreasing whenever an un-annihilated pair (`s_i ≠ 0`) remains;
3. has fixed points exactly the configurations where every separation is already zero;
4. reaches the all-zero (zero total Burgers vector) configuration in exactly as many slides as
   the largest separation present.

This is a discrete Lyapunov descent to the vacuum.

**Theorem 1.7 (unpaired charge is conserved).** An unpaired disclination's rotational charge
(one quantum of deficit or excess) is invariant under every slide move of Theorem 1.6 — sliding
edits only the translational (straight) content, never the frame — so it survives the entire
descent and can be discharged only by meeting an opposite-charge partner from outside the
configuration, at which point it annihilates by Theorem 1.5.

## 2. The disclination's boundary covers the KCBS pentagon

**Theorem 2.1.** The closed boundary of a five-coordinated disclination is a walk of `30 = 5 · 6`
unit steps (Corollary 1.4: it first closes after six traversals of the five-step cone). Because
`5` and `6` are coprime, indexing each of the thirty steps by its *link coordinate* (its position
mod 5 — which of the five cone edges it traverses) and its *frame coordinate* (its position
mod 6 — the developed rotational phase) gives a pair that is jointly injective and jointly
surjective on the thirty steps: every `(link, frame) ∈ ℤ/5 × ℤ/6` pair is realized by exactly one
step, witnessed by the Chinese Remainder Theorem. The frame coordinate and the developed frame
state determine each other exactly. Consequently:

- **no single link vertex redevelops to the same state on two distinct visits** — the intrinsic
  five-fold structure alone admits no single-valued development;
- consecutive-step adjacency on the thirty-step boundary descends, along the link coordinate, to
  the adjacency relation of a **pentagon**, and along the frame coordinate, to a **hexagon**;
- the pentagon obtained this way is exactly the KCBS (Klyachko–Can–Binicioğlu–Shumovsky)
  quantum-contextuality exclusivity graph, and its edges are exactly the KCBS measurement
  contexts.

So the same walk that proves the angle law of §1 also proves, without further assumption, that
its own boundary is a covering space of the KCBS pentagon by way of a coprime `5 × 6` grading —
the disclination's five-fold intrinsic structure and its six-fold developed structure are related
exactly as the Chinese Remainder Theorem relates `ℤ/30` to `ℤ/5 × ℤ/6`.

## 3. Comparison with the origami literature

**Fact 3.1 (Maekawa; independently Justin, Murata).**
At every flat-foldable interior vertex of a paper crease pattern, the number of mountain and
valley creases differs by exactly two. The complementary condition (Kawasaki, Justin) is an
*alternating* angle-sum: creases at angles `θ_1, …, θ_{2n}` around the vertex, in order, satisfy
`θ_1 − θ_2 + θ_3 − ⋯ = 0`.

This is a genuinely different law from Theorem 1.3: an alternating sum over *continuously
variable* angles, rather than a rotational-holonomy sum over a *fixed* 60° unit. It is not
derivable from, and does not derive, §1's frame-closure law — the two share only the family
resemblance of being local, per-vertex admissibility tests. No result in §1 depends on Fact 3.1,
and no attempt is made here to unify them.

## 4. A physical reading: silica ring topology

Silicon dioxide's networks — crystalline and glassy alike — are built from corner-sharing SiO4
tetrahedra organized into closed rings; the ring-size distribution, not the (continuously
variable) Si-O-Si bridging angle, is the discrete invariant that separates ordered from
disordered silica. Quartz, cristobalite, and tridymite are each dominated by six-membered rings,
differing from one another only in stacking; an amorphous silica glass carries a broadened
ring-size distribution with a genuine population of five- and seven-membered rings — the
topologically necessary defect population of an otherwise six-ring network.

That is a coordination/ring-size observable, of exactly the kind Corollary 1.4 is a law about,
not a continuous bond angle (Fact 3.1's natural observable). The reading:

| §1 object | silica-network reading |
|---|---|
| `k`-coordinated lattice site | `k`-membered ring of corner-sharing tetrahedra |
| `k ≡ 0 (mod 6)`, flat | six-membered ring — the crystalline motif (quartz/cristobalite/tridymite) |
| `k = 5`, deficit `+60°` | five-membered ring, a disclination-type ring defect |
| `k = 7`, excess `−60°` | seven-membered ring, the opposite-charge ring defect |
| unpaired 5- or 7-ring | a topologically necessary defect a glass network cannot locally anneal away |
| bound 5–7 pair, translationally offset | a bond-flip ("Stone-Wales"-type) defect — locally strain-relieving, not strain-free |

This reading is an interpretation, not a theorem: §1's lattice is a flat two-dimensional
frame group; a real silica network is a three-dimensional corner-sharing tetrahedral graph, and
no map from one to the other is proved here. What transfers is the *shape* of the law —
coordination flat iff congruent to zero mod a fixed modulus, with deviation a quantized,
conserved, pairwise-annihilating charge carrying a linear translational cost — which fits the
ring-statistics description of glass structure better than a continuous crease-angle sum would,
without the two carriers being claimed identical.

**Fact 4.1 (constraint counting).** The rigid/floppy
split this reading already relies on has a standard name. A silica network's soft degree of
freedom is the Si-O-Si bridging angle; its rigid element is the O-Si-O tetrahedron, whose
internal angles are constraint-locked. That is exactly the hinge-and-panel decomposition an
origami crease pattern makes — the crease is soft, the facet is rigid — and in glass physics it
is Phillips-Thorpe constraint counting: counting bond-stretching and bond-bending constraints per
atom against three degrees of freedom puts the isostatic point at mean coordination
`⟨r⟩_c = 2.4`, floppy below, stressed-rigid above (Phillips 1979; Thorpe 1983; Phillips & Thorpe
1985). Naming it costs this note nothing: no claim of §1 depends on it, and it adds no bridge
that was not already in the ring-statistics reading. It does fix which axis the reading lives on
— a siloxane matrix at `⟨r⟩ = 2` is the floppy end, a four-connected aluminosilicate framework at
`⟨r⟩ = 4` the over-constrained, non-flowing end. That axis is developed in full in a companion
note, `lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/lenticule-science/content/documents/articles/notes/2026/07/25/ossification-as-the-state-variable.md`.

## 5. Settling cohomology: gluing, separation, and the bigraded refinement

**Definition 5.1.** A *section* assigns a truth-value (a bilattice charge) to each cell of a base
set. The *knowledge-join* of two sections, `v ⊔ w`, takes the coordinatewise upper bound.

**Theorem 5.2 (gluing is a least upper bound).** `⊔` is commutative, idempotent, and associative,
and `v ⊔ w` is the *least* section above both `v` and `w`: for any section `u`, if `v ≤ u` and
`w ≤ u` then `v ⊔ w ≤ u`. Local sections therefore glue to a unique global section by
construction, because the merge operator is a least upper bound and not merely an upper bound.

**Theorem 5.3 (deltas integrate without coordination).** The join of a list of deltas depends
only on the *set* of deltas present, not their order or multiplicity: two delta-lists with the
same membership merge to the same result, and merging an already-absorbed delta changes nothing.
This is convergent, order-independent (CRDT-style) integration.

**Theorem 5.4 (separation).** Given a cover — patches whose union is the whole base — two
sections that agree on every patch of the cover are equal. (No hidden global degree of freedom
beyond what a cover can see.)

**Definition 5.5 (H⁰, H¹).** `H⁰` of a section over a cell support is the count of cells at which
the section is truth-designated. A *Čech 1-cocycle* is an assignment of a local section to each
patch of a cover, agreeing wherever two patches overlap; it is a *coboundary* when all its local
values are the restriction of one global section. `H¹ = 0` means every cocycle is a coboundary.

**Theorem 5.6 (H¹ vanishes).** Every 1-cocycle over every cover glues to a global section
(existence, by reading off the value on whichever patch of the cover covers a given cell) and
that global section is unique (uniqueness, by Theorem 5.4, applied directly — not re-derived).
So `H¹ = 0` unconditionally at this level, and there is no cohomology above degree 1: all content
sits in `H⁰` and `H¹`.

**Theorem 5.7 (the identity class vanishes).** The cohomology class of the identity cochain — the
sum of generators over the newly-designated cells, the *classifying invariant* — is a coboundary
and hence zero, by Theorem 5.6, whenever `H¹` vanishes.

**The bigraded refinement.** A short exact sequence splits the section-space into a *truth* part
and an *inconsistency* part, `F ≅ F_t ⊕ F_⊤`, because the truth-designated and inconsistency-
designated cells are disjoint and their union is the whole knowledge-designated set. This
degenerates the long exact sequence relating the cohomology of the three parts into a direct-sum
decomposition, `H^n(F) ≅ H^n(F_t) ⊕ H^n(F_⊤)` — the two axes of a **bigraded** cohomology
`H^{d,n}`, with cochain complex

$$C^{d,n} := \begin{cases} C^n(F_t) & d = \mathrm{truth}, \\ C^n(F_\top) & d = \mathrm{inconsistency}, \end{cases}$$

horizontal differential `d_h` (the ordinary cochain differential within a row) and vertical
differential `d_v` (the composite "include truth into knowledge, project onto inconsistency"),
which is identically zero because the two designations are disjoint — a truth section carries no
inconsistency content. The designation grade is genuinely a two-element charge, not a natural
number that happens to vanish above 1: the "two-row collapse" is definitional, not a limit
theorem. The cohomology-*group*-level form of this long exact sequence (as opposed to its
cochain-level splitting, given above) is an open target, not claimed proved here.

## 6. Two Laplacians

**Definition/Theorem 6.1 (order Laplacian).** On the modal frame generated by settlement, define
`L := □ ∘ ◇` (box after diamond). A section is *harmonic* iff `L` fixes it. For a settled section
reached by iterating settlement from a seed, the settled section is harmonic, its identity
cohomology class is a coboundary (zero, by Theorem 5.7), and its `H⁰` count agrees with the count
of its truth-designated cells — the order-theoretic harmonic kernel and the Čech `H⁰` name the
same object: `ker L ≅ H⁰`, without appeal to an inner product. This does not yet carry a
finite-dimensional Hilbert structure on cochains, so it stops short of a spectral gap or
eigenvalue statement in the usual analytic sense.

**Definition/Theorem 6.2 (causal Laplacian).** On a causal order, define the *causal closure* of
an event set as its future closure under causal precedence. This closure is inflationary,
monotone, and idempotent — a genuine closure operator — and its harmonic (fixed) sections are
exactly the future-closed ones. Because a causal order is already transitively closed, one
application of the closure already reaches the harmonic kernel: the closure-iteration spectral
gap is exactly one step.

Neither Laplacian is shown to determine, or be determined by, §1's disclination holonomy; they
are independent constructions sharing only the ambient settlement/cohomology apparatus of §5.

## 7. The curvature–regime correspondence

**Definition 7.1.** A settling process is classified into one of four regimes by how many stages
its closure takes to terminate: *strict* (finite), *Scott-continuous* (countably infinite,
commuting with directed joins), *transfinite-bounded*, or *aperiodic* (uncountable). Call a
regime *flat* if it is strict or Scott-continuous, *curved* otherwise.

**Theorem 7.2.** Flat and curved exhaustively partition the four regimes (every regime is one or
the other, never both, never neither), and:

```
H¹ = 0   ⟺   flat   ⟺   zero curvature events,
H¹ ≠ 0   ⟺   curved  ⟺   at least one curvature event,
```

where a *curvature event* is a stage of the closure at which it fails to commute with directed
joins.

The `H¹` of this section is **not** the `H¹` of Theorem 5.6, and the two must not be merged by
name. Theorem 5.6's is the Čech gluing obstruction of a cover of sections, and it vanishes
*unconditionally* — every cocycle glues, for every cover, with no regime hypothesis. This
section's is a regime-indexed curvature module, whose rank is the curvature-event count and which
is non-vanishing on the curved regimes by construction of the classification. A third object
again is the cover-nerve cycle rank `|E| − |V| + c`, the nullity of the graphic matroid, which is
what a coordination nerve actually computes and which is non-vanishing exactly when the nerve
carries a cycle. Three distinct invariants share one letter; no theorem here identifies any two
of them. The nerve-rank one is treated at length in a companion note,
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/lenticule-science/content/documents/articles/notes/2026/07/25/coordination-obstruction-as-mirror-descent.md`.

**Theorem 7.3 (the 2×2 structure).** Crossing flat/curved against terminates-by-`ω`/does-not:
the cell "curved and terminates within `ω` stages" is empty — a curvature event forces the
process past `ω` stages. The populated diagonal is exactly the flat/terminates-by-`ω` cell.

**Theorem 7.4 (curvature rank).** `H¹` decomposes as a direct sum of one generator per curvature
event: `rank H¹ = |curvature events|`. A flat regime has rank exactly zero (the empty direct
sum); a curved regime has positive — possibly uncountable — rank, and the curved case is
witnessed concretely: a specific curved-regime instance carries a demonstrated curvature event
and correspondingly non-vanishing `H¹`, so the positive-rank claim is not vacuous.

## 8. Defects and the one-directional bridge to curvature

**Definition 8.1.** A settling process *carries a defect* when its classifying invariant (§5,
Theorem 5.7's cohomology class) is nonzero.

**Theorem 8.2.** A defect forces a curved regime and non-vanishing `H¹` (Theorem 7.2); dually, a
flat (cohomology-vanishing) regime is defect-free. The theory is non-vacuous: an explicit
aperiodic-regime instance is exhibited that does carry a nonzero invariant.

**Open direction.** The converse — every curved regime carries a defect — does not hold: a
curved regime may still carry the zero invariant. No theorem connects this abstract defect
predicate numerically to §1's disclination charge or Burgers-vector content; the two are related
only by the informal reading that a disclination is a concrete instance of what "defect" names
abstractly, not by a proved map between them.

**Second open direction: the vocabulary is rigidity, the mathematics is connectivity.** §3's
flat-foldability material and §4's Fact 4.1 are both about *mechanical* rigidity, which makes
this the place to record a gap. The corpus's percolation module
(`Obsmetrologia/AperiodicOrder/Tiling/Percolation.lean`) speaks throughout in rigidity terms —
floppy, jammed, load-bearing, "the jamming click" — and cites rigidity percolation and contact
networks. On the reading of Fact 4.1 that vocabulary is *correct*: mechanical rigidity is the
phenomenon meant. But the object actually implemented there is the cycle rank
`b₁ = |E| − |V| + c`, the nullity of the **graphic** matroid; its transition is continuous and
sharp at a single contact (`floppy_iff_at_threshold`, `percolation_onset`), one surplus contact
raising the rank by exactly one.

Every term in that family is matroid-relative, and a subscript settles which matroid is meant:
load-bearing₍₁₎ is positive cycle rank in the graphic matroid — a redundant *connection*,
tested by union-find — while load-bearing₍₂₎ is positive nullity in the plane's generic
rigidity matroid — a redundant *constraint*, tested by the pebble game — and floppy₍ₖ₎ is
nullity zero at rank density `k`. Fact 4.1's floppy and stressed-rigid are ₍₃₎, constraint
counting being a statement about a three-dimensional atomic network; the percolation module's
are ₍₁₎.

Mechanical rigidity is a different matroid. Generic rigidity in
the plane is governed by Laman's condition and the **generic rigidity matroid**, of rank
`2|V| − 3`; the generic rigidity matroid exists in every dimension — it is the matroid of the
rigidity matrix at a generic configuration — but in three dimensions only the rank count
`3|V| − 6` is available and no combinatorial characterization is known, Laman's condition
failing there on the double-banana graph. The test for whether an added constraint is
independent or redundant is the pebble game, not union-find, and Laman's condition is
hereditary — every subgraph on `n′` vertices carries at most `2n′ − 3` edges — so redundancy is
nonlocal and invisible to any per-site count. The *order* of the transition does not separate
the two matroids: on the two-dimensional triangular lattice, generic rigidity percolation is
measured as continuous, second-order, in a universality class of its own (Laman 1970; Jacobs &
Thorpe 1995, 1996; Jacobs & Hendrickson 1997); first-order behaviour is the Bethe-lattice and
mean-field result, an attribution not independently checked here. So: the vocabulary and the physical referent are rigidity — ₍₂₎ in the
plane, ₍₃₎ in the bulk — and the implemented mathematics is connectivity, ₍₁₎. Nothing above
depends on closing that gap, and nothing here closes it; it is named so that the rigidity
vocabulary is not mistaken for a rigidity theorem. It is stated at length in
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/lenticule-science/content/documents/articles/notes/2026/07/25/ossification-as-the-state-variable.md`.

## 9. The market game: pricing what the descent proves can't self-discharge

Read against a boundary-pricing rule already on record — value transfer clears at cost inside one
audit authority ("genet"), and requires priced exchange across a genet boundary — Theorem 1.6
supplies the free side and Theorem 1.7 the priced side, both as proved mathematics rather than
assumption.

Theorem 1.6 is a genuine Lyapunov descent: a bound `5–7` dipole's total obstruction decreases
monotonically to zero under purely internal moves, no external input, no price — exactly what
"clears at cost inside a genet" should mean: an internal admissible rearrangement a single
authority resolves on its own, because a same-authority correction process sits on both sides of
it.

Theorem 1.7 is what makes the priced side non-vacuous: an unpaired disclination's charge is
*conserved* under every internal move and can be discharged only by an opposite-charge partner
arriving from outside — precisely the case where no second correction process exists locally, so
nothing internal can clear it. Clearing it requires locating a real counterparty across the
boundary and settling a priced exchange with it. What "semiotic" was reaching for in an earlier
attempt at this argument, with no sign system actually doing any work, is exactly this: a
conserved, quantized charge that only annihilates against its opposite. No sign system is
invoked anywhere above. This market-game reading applies the mathematics of §1 and §9 to a
silica network specifically; that mathematics stands independent of whether the silica reading
is apt.

None of the theorems above have been independently machine-verified to be axiom-free in the
strict sense a formal system would require; that check is outstanding, not performed here.
