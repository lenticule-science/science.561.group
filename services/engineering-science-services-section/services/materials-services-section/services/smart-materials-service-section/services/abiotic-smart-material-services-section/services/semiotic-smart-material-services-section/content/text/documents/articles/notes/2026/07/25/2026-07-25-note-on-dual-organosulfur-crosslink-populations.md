## Abstract

*Ossification as the State Variable* put organosulfur chemistry into the matrix — thiol–ene
cure for the permanent network, disulfide metathesis for the exchangeable one — and then
left two things open. It flagged the aliphatic-versus-aromatic disulfide choice as the
parameter that must be settled before any other, and settled it nowhere. And its §5
excision of the block-copolymer framing removed the physical carrier of the σ-conjugate
adjoint bilattice without replacing it: the corpus kept claiming that the knowledge order
and the truth order are two faces of one object, while the object that was supposed to carry
them had just been deleted.

This note argues that the two sulfur crosslink populations are that carrier, and that they
carry it better than a diblock chain did. It then develops four consequences. **Thiol–ene is
step-growth**, and step-growth versus chain-growth is the difference between a market and a
monopoly, with the gel point as the measurable that distinguishes them. **Disulfide exchange
is associative**, and associative exchange is the author-exclusion law made chemical: no
disulfide can be remade without a third sulfur party to neither bond, and the network never
passes through a state in which the record is unheld. **The thiol/disulfide couple supplies
a second intensive variable**, a redox potential $E_h$ orthogonal to temperature, which
prices the membrane rather than the clock and gives the wit/tally distinction a physical
form: a wit is a thiol, a tally is a matched pair. And **the cysteine oxidation ladder is a
cost-semantics for irreversibility** — the open frontier the relational-physics note names
and does not close — which in turn names a new pathology, *oxidative exhaustion*, distinct
from and worse than premature ossification because nothing reverses it.

We settle the aliphatic choice, answer the note's own open screening question from hard–soft
acid–base grounds, and mark what is established chemistry, what is interpretive transfer,
and what is assumed.

**Keywords:** thiol–ene, disulfide exchange, associative vitrimer, redox potential,
sulfenic/sulfinic/sulfonic ladder, step-growth gelation, crosslink sulfur rank, inverse
vulcanization.

## 1. Background and open items

Two open items, both left by the same upstream note.

**The unsettled parameter.** §4 of *Ossification as the State Variable* states the
aromatic/aliphatic disulfide choice as a design tension, observes that it *is* the
topology-freezing temperature $T_v$, and says it "must be settled against operating
temperature before any other formulation parameter is fixed." It is not settled there.
Everything downstream — the write/erase windows of the thermal-cycle report, the
$120$–$150\,^\circ\text{C}$ write band, the $175\,^\circ\text{C}$ erase threshold — was
computed against a silyl-ether $T_v \approx 115\,^\circ\text{C}$ inherited from the
formulation §4 proposed replacing. Those numbers are orphaned until the sulfur choice is
made. §4 is the growth face.

**The orphaned carrier.** §5 instructs: "Delete the block copolymer self-assembly framing
entirely." That was correct, and it was the largest excision in the family. But the diblock
chain had been doing load-bearing conceptual work elsewhere: the covalent junction between a
glassy and a rubbery block was the stated physical instantiation of the σ-conjugate Galois
adjunction, the claim that the append-only log ($\le_k$) and the mutable state ($\le_t$) are
two faces of one object. Delete the chain and that claim has no carrier. The corpus has been
asserting a two-order structure over a substrate that, since the excision, is single-order.

These close together, because the fix for the second is already sitting inside the first.

## 2. The two crosslink populations

§4's formulation makes both crosslink populations out of one component. Formulate with
$\mathrm{SH}\!:\!\text{vinyl}$ above unity; thiol–ene consumes every vinyl group into
permanent thioether junctions; the residual thiols are then oxidised to disulfides, which
are the exchangeable population. One reagent, one stoichiometric coefficient, two bond
types.

Those two bond types have opposite dispositions, and the opposition is the bilattice.

| population | bond | reversibility | order |
|---|---|---|---|
| thiol–ene product | C–S–C thioether | none under operating conditions | knowledge order $\le_k$ |
| oxidised residue | C–S–S–C disulfide | exchanges, associatively | truth order $\le_t$ |

The thioether is a plain carbon–sulfur single bond formed by radical anti-Markovnikov
addition across a double bond. It has no exchange chemistry. Nothing in the operating
envelope un-makes it: it is not hydrolysable like an ester, not metathesisable like a silyl
ether, not homolysable at accessible temperature. Once a thiol has added across a vinyl,
that addition is history in the strict sense — a record that accumulates and does not
retract. That is monotone accumulation of evidence, and it is the knowledge order, carried
by a bond type rather than by a block.

The disulfide is the same element one oxidation step up, and it is *constitutionally*
revisable: thiol–disulfide exchange, radical metathesis, and thermal homolysis all rearrange
it, and §4 chose it precisely for that. It carries the current disposition. It is the truth
order.

**Why this is a better carrier than the diblock was.** A bilattice is two orders on *one*
set, not one order on each of two sets. The diblock chain gave two orders on two disjoint
segments joined at a point, and the adjunction had to be located at that single junction —
which is why the framing kept needing a $\chi N$ gate and a domain interface to say anything.
The sulfur network gives two orders on the *same* atoms: every sulfur in the system is a
member of the one set, and which order it currently sits under is a property of its bond
state, not of its position along a chain. The set is the sulfur; the two orders are its two
bond types; and the map between them is a redox step, which §5 will show is a genuine
adjoint move and not a relabelling.

The two populations also enter the rigidity count with different functionalities, which §4
noted: a tetrathiol junction has $f = 4$, a disulfide merely joins two chains at $f = 2$. So
mean coordination $\langle r \rangle = \sum_i x_i f_i$ and exchangeable fraction are partly
independent knobs. §5 re-sites that independence more usefully.

## 3. Network formation: thiol–ene step-growth kinetics

Thiol–ene is a radical *step-growth* polymerization, and this is the single most
consequential fact about it for the reading this corpus wants. The cycle alternates: a thiyl
radical adds across the ene to give a carbon radical; the carbon radical abstracts hydrogen
from another thiol, regenerating thiyl. Propagation and chain transfer strictly alternate,
so no chain runs away.

The consequences are not stylistic. They are measured, they follow from the mechanism, and
each one has an organizational reading that is not available to the chain-growth alternative:

**Delayed gelation.** Step-growth systems gel at high fractional conversion, at the
Flory–Stockmayer point $\alpha_c = 1/(f_{\text{avg}} - 1)$. Chain-growth acrylate systems gel
at low conversion, because a single initiation event builds a macromolecule immediately.
Organizationally: a step-growth settlement stays fluid and reconfigurable until nearly all
demand has been matched pairwise, and only then does a spanning structure appear. A
chain-growth settlement freezes its topology after the first few hires, and everything
subsequent has to attach to whatever those hires happened to build. This is exactly the
Levinthal/folding-funnel requirement stated elsewhere in the corpus — *do not commit to a
conformation early* — restated as a polymerization mechanism, and therefore for once
testable rather than exhorted.

**Network uniformity and low shrinkage stress.** Because gelation is late and every junction
is formed by the same pairwise step, thiol–ene networks are homogeneous, with narrow
relaxation distributions and little internal stress. Chain-growth acrylates build
heterogeneous networks with dense microgel regions in a loose surround, and carry the
shrinkage stress of having vitrified around their own early structure. Organizationally,
heterogeneity here is the hub-and-void pattern: a few over-attached nodes bearing everything,
a lot of loosely-held periphery, and residual stress everywhere from having formed around a
structure nobody chose.

**Oxygen tolerance by reservoir, not by exclusion.** §4 states the mechanism correctly: the
thiyl radical does react with O₂, but the resulting peroxy radical abstracts hydrogen from
another thiol and regenerates thiyl. The chain is *diverted, not terminated*. Note what pays
for that: a free thiol from the reservoir. Ambient interference costs a cycle and a
molecule, and does not kill the process, exactly as long as there is uncommitted thiol
around. §6 shows what happens when there is not.

**Initiation is what is addressed, not the reaction.** Radical initiation can be optical.
The reagents are everywhere in the film; only where light lands does anything happen. That is
the dispatch pattern in physical form — ambient capability, addressed execution — and it is
why §4 wanted it against a thin-film electrode geometry.

**One thiol, two sanctioned routes.** The same thiol undergoes radical thiol–ene under
photoinitiation and base-catalysed thiol–Michael addition to an activated ene in the dark.
Same functional group, two mechanisms, selected by catalyst rather than by reagent. The
repository's own law — a blocked instrument names its own sanctioned route — has a chemical
instance here: blocking the radical path does not remove the capability, it selects the
anionic one.

## 4. Network rearrangement: associative versus dissociative exchange

Aliphatic disulfides rearrange by three mechanisms, and the corpus should not treat them as
interchangeable, because they differ on exactly the axis its own execution law cares about.

**Thiol–disulfide exchange (associative, SN2).** A thiolate attacks one sulfur of an existing
disulfide, displacing the other as a new thiolate. The nucleophile is the *thiolate*, not the
thiol, so the rate is pH-dependent and rises with the deprotonated fraction. Crucially the
attacking sulfur is a **third party, member of neither of the two bonds being rearranged**,
and it is not consumed — it leaves as a thiolate, available to catalyse again. The transition
state has partial bonds to all three sulfurs: the new bond is forming before the old one has
broken.

**Radical disulfide metathesis (associative).** Thiyl radicals exchange into disulfides
under photochemical or thermal initiation. Aromatic disulfides do this at room temperature
without catalyst, which is the basis of the self-healing elastomers of Odriozola et al.

**Thermal homolysis (dissociative).** The S–S bond breaks to two thiyl radicals on heat
alone. No third party. Bond dissociation energies are substantially lower for aryl than for
alkyl disulfides, which is why aromatic systems exchange so readily. *(Exact BDE values are
not verified in this repository; the aryl < alkyl ordering is well established, the numbers
are not asserted here.)*

The first two are **associative**: network connectivity is preserved throughout, crosslink
density never drops, and the material does not lose load-bearing capacity while it
rearranges. The third is **dissociative**: the bond count falls before it rises, and the
network passes through a state of reduced connectivity. This associative/dissociative
distinction is the defining axis of the vitrimer literature — associative networks show
Arrhenius viscosity with no connectivity-loss drop; dissociative ones do not. *(Whether
"vitrimer" should be reserved for associative networks is genuinely contested in that
literature; nothing here turns on the naming.)*

**The reading.** Associative exchange is the author-exclusion law made chemical, and it is a
stronger form of it than the corpus currently has. A disulfide cannot be remade by its own
two sulfurs. It requires a third sulfur, party to neither, present and free — and that third
sulfur is not consumed, so it goes on to witness the next exchange. The bond that results was
brought into existence by a producer-identity-neutral, mechanically, with no gate to configure and no
rollback to trigger. And because the mechanism is associative, **there is no instant at which
the record is unheld**: the corpus's chaperone apparatus achieves the same guarantee by
staging changes and restoring the working tree if a gate fails, which is the *dissociative*
pattern with a compensating mechanism bolted on. The associative route does not need the
compensation, because it never breaks first.

Thermal homolysis is then precisely the unauthorized route: change by force, no third party,
the network dropping load while it happens. It is not merely inelegant — it is the failure
mode the whole apparatus exists to prevent, and it is available to any actor who can supply
heat.

### 4.1 Selection of aliphatic over aromatic disulfide

§4 stated the tension and deferred. It settles as follows, and the argument is not a
formulation preference.

Aromatic disulfides exchange at ambient temperature with no catalyst. A substrate whose
exchangeable population rearranges at ambient temperature has no memory: room-temperature
exchange is room-temperature creep, and the written state decays without anyone writing it.
The corpus already has a name for a region that rearranges freely and cannot be settled —
the mempool, the whirling dervish — and the entire architecture depends on that region being
a *localized protected defect* rather than the bulk condition of the material. An aromatic
disulfide network is a substrate that is dervish everywhere. It cannot hold a record, and
holding a record is what §3 of the upstream note says the device is for.

**Take aliphatic.** The barrier is higher, homolysis is suppressed, and the state holds. The
cost §4 named — "requiring heat or catalyst to write" — is on this reading not a cost but the
requirement. A write should require an act. A substrate that rewrites itself at ambient
temperature has no author to exclude, because it has no authors at all.

The catalyst requirement is the better half of the bargain. An aliphatic disulfide network
with a maintained free-thiol reservoir has the associative route open — third-party
witnessed, connectivity-preserving — and the dissociative route suppressed by the higher
barrier. That is the exact ordering of routes the execution law wants, obtained from the
choice of one substituent.

**What this does to the inherited numbers.** $T_v$ is now set by aliphatic disulfide
exchange under the catalysis actually present, not by acid-catalysed silyl ether metathesis.
The write and erase windows of the thermal-cycle report were computed against
$T_v \approx 115\,^\circ\text{C}$ from the superseded chemistry and do not transfer. They
should be re-derived or explicitly marked as inherited-and-unverified. Nothing in the
opposition signature or the ossification state variable depends on the number — both rest on
$\langle r \rangle = \sum_i x_i f_i$ and on the mere existence of a topology-freezing
temperature — but the specific bands do.

**And it re-sites the write head.** §4's resolution of the UV/aryl-disulfide conflict was to
cure thermally, oxidise, then use UV as a spatially addressed write head, exploiting aryl
absorption. Choosing aliphatic removes that absorption and with it that write mechanism.
Aliphatic writing goes back through heat or through catalyst delivery. This is a real loss
and should be recorded as one: optical write was the cleaner path, and it is being traded
for state retention. If optical addressing is required, the route is a photobase or photoacid
generator that delivers the *catalyst* optically rather than cleaving the bond optically —
addressing the third party rather than the record. That is also, notably, the more law-shaped
mechanism: light summons a witness, it does not break a bond.

## 5. Redox potential as a second intensive variable

The substrate as it stands has one intensive control: temperature, applied by Joule heating,
setting exchange rate through $T_v$. The thiol/disulfide couple supplies a second, and it is
orthogonal.

$$2\,\mathrm{RSH} \;\rightleftharpoons\; \mathrm{RSSR} + 2\mathrm{H}^+ + 2e^-$$

The position of this equilibrium is set by the redox potential $E_h$ of the surrounding
medium, not by temperature. So:

- **$T$ sets the rate** at which the exchangeable population rearranges. Kinetics. How fast.
- **$E_h$ sets the size** of the exchangeable population — how much of the total sulfur is
  bonded as disulfide at all, versus sitting free as thiol. Thermodynamics. How much.

These are independent in a way the $f=4$ / $f=2$ functionality argument of §2 only gestured
at. Mean coordination and exchange rate were said there to be "partly independent knobs";
the honest siting of that independence is here. Temperature is the clock. Redox is the
census.

**Biology already runs the compartment version of this, and it is the membrane law.** The
cytosol is held reducing — glutathione predominantly in its reduced form, $E_h$ around
$-240\ \text{mV}$ — and cytosolic proteins accordingly keep free thiols and do not carry
structural disulfides. The endoplasmic reticulum and the bacterial periplasm are held
oxidizing, $E_h$ around $-180\ \text{mV}$, and that is where proteins destined for *export*
acquire their disulfides. The redox potential is a property of the compartment, and the
compartment boundary is where the bonding disposition changes.

Read across: internal work stays reduced — free thiols, no locked commitments, everything
renegotiable at no cost, which is internal contracts at cost. Anything crossing the membrane
gets oxidized on the way out — disulfides installed at the border, the conformation locked
for a world that will not be maintaining it — which is market pricing at the border. The
Rendanheyi membrane is a redox boundary, and the observation that a micro-enterprise faces
exactly one user with zero distance is the statement that it has exactly one oxidizing face.

**This gives the wit/tally distinction a physical form**, and the fit is exact rather than
suggestive. The relational-physics note defines a tally as "the border-reconciled form of the
wit… a *matched pair* of counterparty receipts." A disulfide is literally a matched pair of
counterparty thiols. Neither sulfur can form it alone; the oxidation is a two-electron,
two-party event; and the bond that results records that both parties agreed to the same
single bond. A free thiol is one unmatched commitment — one witnessed distinction held by one
party, a wit. Oxidation is the reconciliation step, it happens at the border because that is
where the potential changes, and it is exactly two wits to the tally.

## 6. The cysteine oxidation ladder and graded reversal cost

*Relational Physics of Coordination* closes by naming, as an open frontier, a cost-semantics
for the quotient axiom: the statement that collapsing a distinction has a definite price. The
corpus has a height function on charges — $\perp \mapsto 0$, $\{t,f\} \mapsto 1$,
$\top \mapsto 2$ — but no price attached to descending it.

Sulfur has the ladder, and unlike the height function, its rungs carry *graded and unequal*
reversal costs:

| state | sulfur | reversal | price |
|---|---|---|---|
| thiol RSH | $-2$ | n/a — nothing to reverse | free |
| disulfide RSSR | $-1$ | thiol–disulfide exchange | one free thiolate, not consumed |
| sulfenic RSOH | $0$ | reduction, or condensation to disulfide | labile; resolves either way |
| sulfinic RSO₂H | $+2$ | one dedicated enzyme, ATP-dependent | one quantized unit, one specialist |
| sulfonic RSO₃H | $+4$ | none | terminal |

The sulfinic rung is the interesting one. In biology it is reversed by sulfiredoxin, at the
cost of ATP hydrolysis — a *priced* reversal, requiring a dedicated specialist that is not
the residue's own author. *(Scope note: sulfiredoxin's characterized substrates are the
2-Cys peroxiredoxins; it is not a general sulfinic reductase. The claim transferred here is
that a reversal exists at quantized cost and requires a dedicated producer-identity-neutral agent, which
holds; generality across all sulfinic sites does not.)*

So the proposal is: **the oxidation state of a sulfur site is the wit-price of undoing the
distinction it holds.** Free thiol, zero. Disulfide, one third-party thiolate — one wit, and
the witness is returned. Sulfinic, one quantized unit burned by an evidence-bound
specialist — one tally, spent. Sulfonic, no price, because there is no reversal: this is the
quotient axiom, the point at which a distinction is destroyed and no expenditure recovers it.
The ladder is not an analogy for the cost-semantics; it is an instance of one, with the
prices already measured by a discipline that was not trying to answer this question.

### 6.1 Oxidative exhaustion as a distinct failure mode

The ladder names a pathology the corpus does not currently have, and it is worse than the one
it does.

**Premature ossification** — the pathology of §2 of the upstream note — is thermal and
structural: driven to Deborah number $\mathrm{De} \gg 1$, the network crystallizes into an
over-constrained brittle state that cannot rearrange. It is bad, and it is *reversible*: the
erase pulse re-dissolves the rigid domains, and the thermal-cycle report demonstrates the
round trip.

**Oxidative exhaustion** is different. Push $E_h$ up, or simply let air do it over long
enough, and sulfur does not stop at disulfide. It goes on to sulfinic and sulfonic. A network
in this state still has its thioether skeleton and may still have most of its crosslinks —
mechanically it can look fine, and $\phi$ may read nowhere near threshold. What it has lost
is its **free-thiol reservoir**, and with it three things at once:

1. The associative exchange route closes. Thiol–disulfide exchange needs a free thiolate;
   there is none. The only remaining route to rearrangement is thermal homolysis — the
   dissociative, unwitnessed, connectivity-dropping one.
2. Oxygen tolerance is gone. §3's diversion mechanism regenerates thiyl by abstracting from a
   free thiol. Without the reservoir, peroxy radicals terminate, and ambient interference
   stops costing a cycle and starts costing the process.
3. The oxidized sites do not come back. Sulfonic is terminal; sulfinic costs a specialist and
   a burned unit.

The organizational statement follows without further interpretation: **a settlement that has
spent all of its uncommitted capacity locking commitments can no longer renegotiate any of
them by the sanctioned route.** The only way left to change anything is to break it by force.
And the diagnosis is invisible to the instrument currently installed, because rigidity
percolation reads *bond count* and this pathology is a loss of *unbonded reserve*. A composite
can sit safely below $\phi_c$ and be exhausted.

This suggests the reserve is a state variable in its own right, alongside $\phi$: the free
thiol fraction, the uncommitted portion of the total sulfur. Two independent quantities —
what is bound, and what remains available to witness a rebinding — and health requires the
second to be nonzero regardless of where the first sits. The corpus's rule that global
$H^1 > 0$ is the definition of a living organism is the same claim in the other instrument's
vocabulary: something must remain undischarged.

**What exhaustion does to the reaction graph, not just the rate.** Point 2 above says oxygen
tolerance is lost once the reservoir empties; the CRN note's typing of irreversibility (its
§6.1) makes precise what "lost" means structurally. While free thiol is available, the
peroxy-radical diversion step is a member of the propagation T-invariant — thiyl adds, O₂
intercepts, the peroxy radical abstracts from a fresh thiol, thiyl is regenerated, the cycle
closes every pass, at a price of one thiol from the reservoir. That interruption is the *same
elementary reaction* whether or not the reservoir is exhausted; its rate constant does not
change. What changes is that once no free thiol remains for the peroxy radical to abstract
from, the return edge of that cycle no longer exists, and the identical step is now terminal:
the radical is lost, not regenerated, and no expenditure recovers it. Exhaustion is therefore
not merely "the reserve hits zero" as a number crossing a threshold — it is the deletion of
the edge that made an otherwise-costly step read as free, reclassifying that step from priced
cyclic to terminal without touching its kinetics at all.

## 7. Phase behaviour of elemental and polymeric sulfur

The element brings a phase repertoire the corpus has not used, and it is shaped differently
from the transitions already in play.

**The λ-transition is a window, not a threshold.** Liquid sulfur undergoes a ring-opening
equilibrium polymerization with a floor temperature near $159.4\,^\circ\text{C}$: below it S₈
rings are stable and the liquid is mobile; above it the rings open into long chains and the
viscosity rises by several orders of magnitude over a few degrees. But it does not stay
risen. Past roughly $180$–$190\,^\circ\text{C}$ the equilibrium chain length falls again and
the liquid thins. Cold gives short rings; hot gives short chains; only a narrow band gives
the entangled polymer.

Every transition currently in the corpus is monotone in temperature — hotter is always more
mobile, or past a threshold always more something. This one is not. It says that a minting
temperature can have an *optimum* rather than a floor, and that overshooting it returns you to
disconnected monomers by a different road than undershooting. Against the corpus's existing
work on the minting temperature, that is a distinct and testable shape.

**Polymeric sulfur is metastable and reverts.** Long-chain sulfur is not the element's ground
state. Left alone, it depolymerizes back to S₈ rings over days to weeks. Holding a sulfur
polymer requires trapping it — the inverse-vulcanization route copolymerizes it with an
organic crosslinker at the ring-opening temperature, and the crosslinker's job is not to
contribute properties but to *prevent the chains from finding their way back to rings*.

This is the whirling dervish at the elemental scale, and it is a cleaner statement of it than
the disclination argument, because the refusal here is thermodynamic rather than topological.
A substance whose ground state is a small closed ring can be built into long chains only
kinetically. Anything you build of it is held above its own ground state by a trap, and the
trap is a separate component whose entire function is obstruction. Read across: the
structures the settlement builds out of its most reactive element are all metastable by
construction, and something whose only job is to prevent relaxation must be co-polymerized
in, or they return to rings.

**Crosslink sulfur rank, and how it ages.** Sulfur bridges between chains are not one thing.
They come as monosulfidic (C–S–C), disulfidic (C–S–S–C), and polysulfidic (C–Sₓ–C, x up to
about eight), and the rank governs behaviour. Polysulfidic bridges are labile: they rearrange
under stress, which gives stress relaxation and self-healing. Monosulfidic bridges are stable
and thermally robust, and they do not relax.

So a bond has a **rank**, and the rank is its remaining renegotiation capacity — how many
rearrangements it can absorb internally before it has none left. That is a richer bond model
than the corpus currently uses, where a bond is present or absent.

And the rank *decays*. In sulfur vulcanizates, ageing at temperature drives desulfuration:
polysulfidic bridges shorten toward disulfidic and monosulfidic. Concurrently, some
crosslinks are destroyed outright, with main-chain modification — the reversion that softens
natural rubber on overcure. Both happen together, and the honest statement is the one with
both terms: **an ageing network's surviving bonds become more rigid and less renegotiable,
while the total number of bonds falls.** Not a hardening and not a softening — a
stiffening-of-the-remainder alongside a thinning-of-the-whole. That is a specific and
recognizable shape of institutional ageing, and it comes out of rubber chemistry rather than
out of anyone's theory of institutions.

## 8. Predicted thiolate–filler interaction and a revised screening protocol

§4 flagged one unknown for experimental screening: Na-LTA zeolite carries exchangeable Na⁺,
thiolate is a competent ligand, so thiol may ion-exchange onto the zeolite surface and
quietly consume the cure stoichiometry — "not answerable from the literature," to be settled
by titrating free thiol against filler loading.

Hard–soft acid–base grounds give a prediction, and it redirects the experiment.

Thiolate is a soft base — polarizable, large, low charge density. Na⁺ is a hard cation. The
pairing is mismatched, so simple exchange of thiolate onto hard alkali-cation sites should be
weak, and the feared stoichiometric drain from Na⁺ specifically should be small.

The real exposure is elsewhere and is larger. Thiolate binds *soft* electrophiles avidly:
transition-metal centres, heavy-metal contaminants, and residual noble metal. Any soft metal
in the framework — adventitious iron, any copper or silver present, and above all any
platinum surviving from a hydrosilylation route the formulation is supposed to have abandoned
— will scavenge thiol far more effectively than sodium will. Thiolate poisoning of platinum
is not a marginal effect.

**So the screen should titrate free thiol against soft-metal content, not against sodium
content or bare filler loading.** Concretely: run the titration across filler lots
characterized for trace transition metals, and include a deliberately Pt-spiked control. If
consumption tracks soft-metal content and not surface area, the mechanism is metal
scavenging, the fix is filler purity rather than surface grafting, and §4's proposed
titration-against-loading would have returned a confounded answer — loading and metal content
covary across lots.

*(This is a prediction from established HSAB reasoning, not a measurement. It changes what
the experiment should vary; it does not substitute for running it.)*

## 9. Status of claims

**Established chemistry, transferred but not originated here.** Thiol–ene step-growth
mechanism, delayed gelation, network homogeneity, oxygen tolerance by chain transfer,
anti-Markovnikov regiochemistry. Thiol–disulfide exchange proceeding through thiolate as
nucleophile, and its associative character. The associative/dissociative distinction in
dynamic covalent networks. Room-temperature catalyst-free metathesis of aromatic disulfides.
Cellular compartment redox potentials and their correlation with structural disulfide
formation. The sulfenic/sulfinic/sulfonic oxidation ladder and its reversal asymmetry. The
sulfur λ-transition and the metastability of polymeric sulfur. Sulfur crosslink rank and
desulfuration on ageing. Flory–Stockmayer $\alpha_c = 1/(f_{\text{avg}}-1)$.

**Interpretive transfer, argued here and not proved.** Thioether-as-$\le_k$ and
disulfide-as-$\le_t$ as the carrier of the σ-conjugate adjunction. Associative exchange as
the author-exclusion law. $E_h$ as a second intensive variable and compartment redox as the
Rendanheyi membrane. Thiol-as-wit and disulfide-as-tally. The oxidation ladder as a
cost-semantics for the quotient axiom. Step-growth versus chain-growth as market versus
monopoly. Each is a mapping, and none is a theorem; the disulfide/tally correspondence is the
one that most nearly earns its keep, because both sides independently require a *matched pair
of counterparties* and neither was defined with the other in view.

**Assumed, and flagged.** That $T_v$ for the aliphatic disulfide population lands in a usable
band for the electrode geometry — asserted nowhere, and the inherited
$115/140/175\,^\circ\text{C}$ numbers do not transfer. That the free-thiol reservoir can be
maintained against ambient oxidation over device lifetime — the whole of §6.1 is a warning
that it may not be. That both populations can be formed at controlled ratio by stoichiometry
alone, per §4, without the oxidation step also over-oxidizing part of the population past
disulfide, which §6 says is the live risk. No BDE values are asserted; only the aryl-below-
alkyl ordering.

**Unbuilt.** Nothing here is formalized. The upstream note's §6 gap stands unchanged — the
implemented instrument counts the graphic matroid's cycle rank while the physical referent is
rigidity — and this note adds a second instrument gap: the free-thiol reserve of §6.1 is a
state variable with no counterpart in the formalization at all, and it is invisible to any
bond-counting measure.

## 10. Falsification tests

1. **The reserve is a second state variable.** Prepare samples at fixed $\phi$ and varying
   free-thiol fraction. Measure stress relaxation. If relaxation time is insensitive to
   reserve at fixed rigid fraction, the associative route is not rate-controlling, and §6.1's
   exhaustion pathology is not a distinct failure mode from ossification.
2. **Associative versus dissociative.** Measure crosslink density and connectivity through a
   relaxation event. Associative exchange preserves crosslink density throughout; dissociative
   homolysis shows a transient drop. If the aliphatic network shows the drop under the write
   conditions actually used, the third-party guarantee of §4 does not hold at operating
   temperature and the write mechanism is the unauthorized one.
3. **Redox orthogonality.** Vary $E_h$ at fixed $T$ and $T$ at fixed $E_h$, and measure
   exchange rate and disulfide fraction. The claim is that $T$ moves rate without moving the
   census and $E_h$ moves the census without moving the rate. Cross-coupling falsifies the
   two-knob reading of §5.
4. **The non-monotone window.** If any transition in this substrate is to inherit the
   λ-transition's shape, it must be measurably non-monotone in temperature. A monotone
   response across the full accessible band means §7's window is a property of elemental
   sulfur that did not transfer to this network, and should be dropped rather than kept as
   decoration.

Experiment 1 first: it is a preparation-and-rheology experiment on samples the opposition-
signature protocol already produces, and it tests the only claim here that both is new and
would change what the instrument must measure.
