# Yang & Pitchumani (2002) — *Healing of thermoplastic polymers at an interface under non-isothermal conditions*

Foundational paper applying polymer reptation theory (de Gennes 1971; Wool 1995) to inter-layer bond formation in non-isothermal processing — exactly the FFF case. Introduces the dimensionless healing-time integral $\theta = \int \mathbb{1}[T>T_g]/t_{\rm rep}(T) \, dt$ and shows that bond strength scales as $\theta^{1/4}$ until full healing.

**Why it matters here.** The grounding theory for [healing-time-determines-z-strength](./predication-healing-time-determines-z-strength.md) and the implicit basis of the [cooling-warping-tradeoff](../../../../../content/text/theories/predication-cooling-warping-tradeoff.md) — both encode the same chain-interdiffusion-during-cooling mechanism.

**Strengths.** Tight coupling of polymer physics (reptation, $\sigma \sim \theta^{1/4}$) to processing physics (cooling profile). Predictive, not just descriptive.

**Limitations.** Assumes amorphous polymer; semi-crystalline cases (PLA, PA, PEEK) need extension to handle crystallization-pinned chains. Bartolai et al. (2018) is the modern FFF-specific extension.
