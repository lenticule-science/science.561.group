# Chain scission

Breaking of polymer chains into shorter ones, reducing molecular weight and degrading mechanical properties. The dominant degradation mechanism in repeated thermal processing of thermoplastics. For FFF, chain scission happens at every melt-cool cycle, so it is the underlying mechanism that limits how many times a feedstock can be recycled.

## Mechanisms

1. **Thermal scission.** At elevated temperature, weak bonds in the polymer backbone (especially $\alpha$-H positions, peroxide adducts) break homolytically. Rate increases exponentially with temperature (Arrhenius). For PLA at 200°C the half-life of $M_w$ is on the order of hours; well above this temperature it drops to minutes. This is why long polymer residence in the FFF hot-end (incomplete purge after color change) produces visibly degraded extrudate.

2. **Hydrolytic scission.** Polyesters (PLA, PETG, PET, PC, PA) react with absorbed water at the ester linkage, splitting the chain. Even small water content (~0.3% by mass) is enough for substantial scission at melt temperatures. This is the deep reason behind [dry-hygroscopic-polymers-before-processing](./predication-dry-hygroscopic-polymers-before-processing.md): wet PLA does not just pop and steam; it loses molecular weight permanently.

3. **Oxidative scission.** Atmospheric oxygen attacks chains at radicalized sites generated by thermal or UV exposure. Rate depends on $\mathrm O_2$ partial pressure, temperature, and presence of antioxidant additives. Standard mitigation: hindered-phenol antioxidants in the feedstock formulation; commodity FFF feedstocks all contain ~0.1–0.5% antioxidant package.

4. **Mechanochemical scission.** Mechanical shear at the extruder gear and in the nozzle can break chains at very high shear rates. Negligible for normal FFF operation but significant for fiber-reinforced feedstocks where the fibers locally amplify shear.

## Quantification

For one melt-cool cycle of a commodity FFF feedstock at recommended temperature:

- *PLA, dry, antioxidant-stabilized:* $M_w$ loss 1–3% per pass.
- *PLA, wet (1% water):* $M_w$ loss 10–25% per pass.
- *PETG, dry:* $M_w$ loss 2–5% per pass.
- *PETG, wet:* $M_w$ loss 15–30% per pass.
- *ABS, dry:* $M_w$ loss <1% per pass; not strongly hygroscopic.

Cumulative across recycling cycles, this places the practical recyclate ceiling at 3–7 cycles for PLA before properties become unacceptably degraded.

## Mitigation

- **Drying.** Remove water before processing. The single most effective mitigation for hydrolytic-scission-prone polymers. See [drying](../../../services/additive-manufacturing-services-section/services/fused-filament-fabrication-services-section/content/text/theories/predication-drying.md).
- **Antioxidant package.** Standard in commercial feedstocks; can be replenished during reprocessing.
- **Inert-atmosphere processing.** Used in industrial polymer compounding; not feasible for FFF.
- **Chain extenders.** Reactive multifunctional additives (Joncryl ADR for PLA/PETG, isocyanate-functional for PA) that re-build $M_w$ during reprocessing by linking shorter chains. Standard practice for recycled-PLA filament. Effectiveness is partial: chain extenders raise $M_w$ but also broaden MWD and may introduce gels.
- **Lower processing temperature.** A nozzle temperature 10–20°C below the manufacturer ceiling extends in-hot-end residence-time tolerance by a factor of 2–4.

## Diagnostic signatures

- *Visual:* Discoloration (yellowing for PLA, browning for ABS) is a degradation indicator. Caused mostly by chromophore formation, but correlated with chain scission.
- *Mechanical:* Disproportionate loss of impact strength relative to tensile strength. Recycled material that is "still strong" but brittle is a chain-scission signature.
- *Rheological:* Lower MFI (apparent viscosity decrease).
- *Spectroscopic:* GPC for direct $M_w$; FTIR for end-group changes.
