Fabrication fidelity is the degree to which a fabricated thing satisfies the work order that governed it, measured by independent assay rather than by the fabricator's own report. The Central Dogma locates where fidelity is set: in the completeness of the work order. The kinetic-proofreading result locates what it costs: time and energy spent on an irreversible delay.

Fidelity is distinct from provider skill and from passing a checklist: it is bought, as in kinetic proofreading, by an irreversible reject path for a failed candidate and by additional passes that use genuinely independent information, and two passes by the same model with the same prompt and evidence are not two channels. A fabrication line that assays a candidate only against evidence its author supplied has one channel, and adding a second pass of the same kind does not raise its fidelity. The glass-sponge note's rule that independence is evidential, not organizational, is the test a second assay must pass to count.
