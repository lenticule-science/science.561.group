The Central Dogma of Fabrication is the structural claim that the fidelity of a fabricated change is set by the completeness of the work order handed to a provider, not by which provider was hired, because a fabrication provider — any hired inferential-computing process, any model tier — occupies the role a ribosome occupies in the cell: universal, interchangeable, and constitutionally blind to intent the message did not carry. A provider that fills a gap in an under-specified order with its own guess about intent has not merely skipped verification; it has performed translation without a template for the part it invented.

The Dogma names a distinct question from the fixation gate's author-exclusion rule. Author exclusion governs who may verify a candidate result; the Central Dogma governs who may specify one, arguing that a provider which supplies missing intent is indistinguishable from outside from a provider that merely guessed correctly, until a different provider guesses differently. The Dogma is also distinct from fabrication fidelity's own kinetic-proofreading account of how fidelity is bought through independent verification passes: the Dogma prices the input to the check, kinetic proofreading prices the check itself.

The engineering consequence is a procurement policy, not a model-selection policy: a defect traced to a gap in a work order is repaired by completing the work order, and the corrective is never a more capable provider — spending effort on a smarter provider gives it no more license to invent intent than a plainer one, and may make an invented guess more convincing rather than less.
