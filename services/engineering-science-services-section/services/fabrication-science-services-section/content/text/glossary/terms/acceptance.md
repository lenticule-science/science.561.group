Acceptance is a receiver's or owner's decision, made after the relevant evidence exists, that a fabricated thing is taken up for the purpose it was ordered for.

Acceptance differs from admission, which precedes the effect, and from a fabricator's own passing assay, which is evidence of a bounded check and not a decision about use; provenance binds evidence to a production history but does not establish the truth of the claim it concerns. A fabrication receipt is reported as evidence of the stated assays, never as acceptance, and the receiver keeps the authority to accept, reject, deploy, or induct what was made.
