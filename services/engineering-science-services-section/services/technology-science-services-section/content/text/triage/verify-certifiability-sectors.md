The closure rule that gates dispatch certification on the **K₄-equivariant cohomology sectors** of the proposed action's settling cobordism. Per [equivariant-cohomology-decomposition](../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/equivariant-cohomology-decomposition.md), the four isotypic sectors `(H¹_id, H¹_χ∼, H¹_χ−, H¹_χ∼∘−)` partition the curvature events of a settling. Two of the four are *safety-critical*: the polity refuses certification when either is non-zero.

## The two safety-critical sectors

Per the predicated-drone-swarm theory-gap question's certifiability-sector section:

- **`H¹_χ−`**: generated by ⊤-firings (consistency-failure events). Paraconsistent inconsistencies between the cells the cobordism touches.
- **`H¹_χ∼∘−`**: generated by mixed firings — both negation- and conflation-antisymmetric. Worst-case multi-mode failure where epistemic frames disagree *and* paraconsistent inconsistency is present.

Either of these being non-zero means the action's settling-trajectory contains *content the polity cannot honestly project to a classical $T_t$-internal logic*. Per [truth-knowledge-cohomology-long-exact-sequence](../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/truth-knowledge-cohomology-long-exact-sequence.md), the $T_t$-projection of the $T_k$-internal state is lossless iff the relevant inconsistency-cohomology vanishes — i.e., iff `H¹_χ−` and `H¹_χ∼∘−` are both zero.

The polity's civil-compliance posture (when applicable) requires classical-logic projection. The substrate-algebraic statement: **civil compliance is structurally a consistency claim** — the polity certifies that the swarm operated *consistently* in the certification window.

## Closure-rule structure

```
subject:    (workitem, dispatchable) — proposed dispatch pair
predicate:  dispatch-certifiable
object:     bilattice value in FOUR
```

For each subject `(w, d)`, the rule reads the **projected** K₄-grading of the settling cobordism the dispatch would produce. Per [six-way-intersection-composition](../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theories/agential-orchestration-platform/theories/predicated-drone-swarm/theorems/six-way-intersection-composition.md), the projected K₄-grading is the K₄-graded ordinal sum of components' gradings; the strict-regime layer's $H^1 = 0$ structural property makes the composite's safety-critical sectors zero whenever the layer is reached without inconsistency injection. The substrate-cell shape `projected-cohomology-sectors` carries the four-tuple `(rank H¹_id, rank H¹_χ∼, rank H¹_χ−, rank H¹_χ∼∘−)` for the proposed cobordism; the rule reads from this cell:

| H¹_χ− | H¹_χ∼∘− | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | `T` — certifiable |
| > 0 | 0 | `F` — refuse on consistency-failure |
| 0 | > 0 | `F` — refuse on mixed double-curvature |
| > 0 | > 0 | `⊤` — repair-required (worst case) |

The other two sectors `(H¹_id, H¹_χ∼)` are *not* gates; they may be non-zero without preventing certification. They carry information (truth-plus-falsity content; epistemic-frame disagreement that is resolvable by frame clarification) but are not safety-critical in the strict sense.

## Position in the dispatch graph

The closure rule fires in the eligibility-rule subsystem alongside the existing rules in [`pyfars.runtime.eligibility_rules`](../tools/platform/pyfars/src/pyfars/runtime/eligibility_rules.py). Per the dispatch graph's selection logic ([`pyfars.runtime.dispatch_graph.select_winners_node`](../tools/platform/pyfars/src/pyfars/runtime/dispatch_graph.py)), a `(w, d)` pair is selectable iff `eligible-by-capabilities(w, d) = T` AND no disqualification axis is `F` or `⊤`. This rule introduces a new disqualification axis: `dispatch-certifiable(w, d) ∈ {F, ⊤}` blocks selection.

The rule's *presence as a substrate record* is what authorizes its application — per [`pyfars.runtime.eligibility_rules.discover_active_rules`](../tools/platform/pyfars/src/pyfars/runtime/eligibility_rules.py), an `objects/<rule-slug>.md` file is the substrate-side authorization for the rule to fire. This record is the authorization for the polity.

## Why this is a boundary discipline, not a runtime guard

Per [`guard`](../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theories/agential-orchestration-platform/concepts/guard.md), a guard is the platform's admissibility-check operation kind. This rule realizes a guard at the cobordism-category level: it checks the cohomological structure of the *proposed cobordism* before the cobordism's enactment fires. Verdict-bearing, not action-bearing. The action — refusing the dispatch — is a separate substrate-mutation step downstream of the verdict.

## What this rule does *not* do

- It does not pre-block work that *might* produce inconsistencies; only work whose *projected* settling cohomology has non-zero safety-critical sectors. Pre-blocking is the wrong posture (per the operator's correction during this bootstrap project) — the rule realizes audit-as-refusal, not pre-restriction.
- It does not refuse work whose `H¹_id` or `H¹_χ∼` are non-zero. Those are information-bearing, not safety-critical; the polity admits work in those sectors and records the cohomological data for audit.
- It does not subsume `meaningful-human-control`. MHC is a four-condition test of human-attestation, planning, charter, and persistence; this rule is a two-sector test of cohomological consistency. They are orthogonal axes; both must pass for full certification.
