An **inquiry** is the closure-position corner where neither the output nor the method is fixed in advance — the work is open-ended evidence-gathering.
The agent explores: reads, asks questions, runs experiments, gathers cells into the witness log, and lets the σ-nucleus saturate as evidence accumulates.
The work terminates when the agent decides enough has been gathered, not when a target output appears.

Examples: research, exploratory analysis, debugging an unfamiliar system, planning a new project, the agentive-act of *learning* per [the agentive-triad study notes](../../../../../../../../../../../../doing/working/hacking/doing/working/planning/library/project/library/realigning-the-repository-layout/study-notes/agentive-triad.md).
The Tier 2 (vertical, meta) act in the agentive triad's two-tier structure — the learning-act that bridges cell-level evidence to theory-extension.

Inquiry is the entry point of the closure-position cycle: an inquiry may settle into a derivation (the rules-needed are discovered), then a procedure (the rules become repeatable), then a process (the procedure is fully bounded).
The four corners are stations on the path from unsettled to fully-settled work.
