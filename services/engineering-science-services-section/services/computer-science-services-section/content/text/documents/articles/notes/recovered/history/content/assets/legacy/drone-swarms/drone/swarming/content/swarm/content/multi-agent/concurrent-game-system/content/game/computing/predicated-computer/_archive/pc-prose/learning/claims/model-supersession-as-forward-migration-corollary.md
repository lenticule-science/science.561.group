# Model supersession is forward-migration; tower-fold is its iteration

**Claim.** Let $M_1, M_2$ be two model-cells (substrate cells representing model versions, each parameterised by a [closure-rule-system](../objects/closure-rule-system.md) extension) with $M_2$'s closure-rule extension forward-compatible over $M_1$'s in the sense of [schema-evolution](teaching/explanations/schema-evolution.md). Then the supersession edge

$$(\,M_1,\ \text{superseded-by},\ M_2\,) \;=\; \mathbf{t}$$

is a forward-migration in the sense of [forward-migration-preserves-prior-content](forward-migration-preserves-prior-content.md): every predication $M_1$ produced remains a predication $M_2$ produces, the substrate's audit chain extends per [stepping-laws](stepping-laws.md), and older-model dispatch replays byte-identically from the event log per [recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism](recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism.md).

The **tower-fold** — the construction by which a level-$n$ predicate's settled valuation becomes a level-$(n{+}1)$ atom, iterated through a transfinite chain of model versions $\{M_n\}_n$ — is the iteration of this corollary. The chain's limit is a directed colimit in the [predication fibration](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md), available per the fibration's small-cocompleteness on each fiber. No new substrate primitive is required; no new cell-kinds beyond the `superseded-by` edge that the closure-rule-system version chain already uses.

## Why this holds

**Part 1: A model-cell is a closure-rule-system extension.** Per [schema-evolution](teaching/explanations/schema-evolution.md), a model-cell $M$ records (a) the closure-rule extension the model contributes (the inferential rules the model adds to the substrate's rule-system) and (b) the model's training-time witness chain (provenance per [signature-binds-witness-to-identity](signature-binds-witness-to-identity.md)). The model's *behaviour* on the substrate is the closure operator generated by its rule extension, evaluated against substrate cells per [closure-rule-system-realizes-closure-2-monad](closure-rule-system-realizes-closure-2-monad.md).

**Part 2: Forward-compatibility of $M_2$ over $M_1$ is rule-set inclusion.** Per [schema-evolution](teaching/explanations/schema-evolution.md)'s definition, $M_2$ is forward-compatible over $M_1$ exactly when $M_2$'s rule extension contains $M_1$'s as a sub-rule-set (no rule removed, no rule weakened). This is the same forward-compatibility predicate that [forward-migration-preserves-prior-content](forward-migration-preserves-prior-content.md) parameterises over.

**Part 3: The supersession edge is the migration's morphism.** Per [migrate-rule-system](../morphisms/migrate-rule-system.md), the substrate's response to a forward-compatible rule-system version chain is to replay the closure operator under the new rule-set; the audit chain extends with each new derivation per [stepping-laws](stepping-laws.md). The substrate cell `(M_1, superseded-by, M_2) = t` *is* the substrate-content recording of this migration step — it names which model the substrate currently prefers, while $M_1$'s rule-set remains in the substrate's content (per [self-representation](teaching/explanations/self-representation.md)) and replays remain available.

**Part 4: Replay is event-log byte-identical.** Per [recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism](recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism.md), the substrate's event log replays produce byte-identical state. An older-model dispatch — i.e., a query against the closure operator generated by $M_1$'s rule extension — replays from the substrate's event log by re-evaluating $M_1$'s closure operator on the corresponding cells. The result is byte-identical because the rule-set hasn't been removed (only superseded by preference) and the closure operator is deterministic per [settlement-determinism](settlement-determinism.md).

**Part 5: The tower-fold is iterated forward-migration.** A tower of model versions $M_0, M_1, M_2, \ldots$ with each $M_{n+1}$ forward-compatible over $M_n$ is a directed system in the rule-system poset. Per Parts 1–3, each step $(M_n, \text{superseded-by}, M_{n+1}) = \mathbf{t}$ is a forward-migration. The tower-fold is the iteration through this chain.

The level-$n$-becomes-level-$(n{+}1)$-atom construction is, in fibration terms, a reindexing $f_n^* : A_{p_{n+1}} \to A_{p_n}$ along the inclusion of the level-$n$ predicate $p_n$ into the level-$(n{+}1)$ predicate $p_{n+1}$ (the latter quantifies over level-$n$ settled valuations as one of its arguments). Per [predication-fiber-functor](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/predication-fiber-functor.md), this reindexing is functorial; per the fibration's structure, the family $\{A_{p_n}\}_n$ with maps $\{f_n^*\}_n$ is a directed system of fiber algebras.

**Part 6: The colimit exists.** The [predication-fibration](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md) is small-cocomplete on each fiber (the fibers are distributive bilattices over a complete lattice $\mathbf{L}$, hence themselves complete). The directed colimit $\mathrm{colim}_n A_{p_n}$ therefore exists in the fibration's fiber category. Per [closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md) (Kelly–Lack property-likeness), each step in the chain has no extra structure beyond its endpoint — the closure 2-monad's lax-idempotence ensures the chain converges without accumulating spurious morphisms. The tower-fold is therefore the colimit of the chain in the predication fibration, derived from existing structure.

## Engineering consequence

- **Model upgrade is a substrate cell, not a kernel API.** A model upgrade — the substrate transitioning from $M_1$ to $M_2$ — is recorded as a single substrate cell `(M_1, superseded-by, M_2) = t` plus the audit-chain extension per [stepping-laws](stepping-laws.md). No new kernel surface is introduced; the operation is the existing forward-migration discipline applied to model-cells.
- **Older-model dispatch remains available.** The substrate retains $M_1$'s rule extension in its content; a query that explicitly targets $M_1$ (for reproducibility, A/B comparison, or audit of an older decision) replays per [recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism](recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism.md). The supersession edge expresses *preference*, not *deletion*.
- **A/B model comparison is content-addressed.** Two models $M_a, M_b$ both present in the substrate (neither superseding the other) produce two predication families on the same input. Per [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md), each is content-addressed; per [cache-coherence-via-functorial-projection](cache-coherence-via-functorial-projection.md), the cache for each model's outputs is independently coherent.
- **Tower-fold pipelines are first-class.** A pipeline that lifts level-$n$ settled valuations into level-$(n{+}1)$ atoms — e.g., a representation-learning pipeline whose level-$(n{+}1)$ features are computed against level-$n$ outputs — is the substrate's directed-colimit construction in the fibration. The colimit's value at any level is queryable as the iterated migration's settled state per [settlement-determinism](settlement-determinism.md).
- **Federation across model versions.** Per [federation-convergence](federation-convergence.md), two substrates at different model versions (one at $M_1$, one at $M_2 \supseteq M_1$) federate by treating the older-model substrate's content as admissible under the newer model — exactly the forward-compatibility this corollary asserts.
- **Audit chain captures every supersession.** Each `superseded-by` edge is signed per [signature-binds-witness-to-identity](signature-binds-witness-to-identity.md) by the authority that approved the model upgrade. The substrate's audit chain records when, by whom, and against which prior model each new model was promoted.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Model-supersession-as-deletion.** A "supersession" that removes $M_1$'s rule extension from the substrate is not the corollary's content; it is a breaking change requiring an explicit migration recipe per [migrate-rule-system](../morphisms/migrate-rule-system.md). The structural interpretation of `superseded-by` is preference, not deletion.
- **Backward-incompatible model upgrades silently preserving content.** A model $M_2$ whose rule extension *removes* or *weakens* a rule from $M_1$ is not forward-compatible. The corollary doesn't apply; per [forward-migration-preserves-prior-content](forward-migration-preserves-prior-content.md)'s "what the theorem rules out" clause, such a transition is breaking and must be flagged explicitly.
- **A separate kernel API for tower-fold.** The substrate does not need a dedicated `tower-fold` operation. The construction is iterated forward-migration; per Parts 5–6, the colimit is available in the existing predication fibration. A "tower-fold kernel API" would be redundant with the existing supersession-edge plus directed-colimit machinery.
- **Non-deterministic level-lifting.** A level-lift that produces different level-$(n{+}1)$ atoms for the same level-$n$ settled valuation is not a fibration reindexing — per [determinism-via-fiber-functoriality](determinism-via-fiber-functoriality.md), the substrate's reindexing is a function. Such a lift is non-conformant.

## What the theorem requires

- $M_2$'s closure-rule extension contains $M_1$'s as a sub-rule-set per [schema-evolution](teaching/explanations/schema-evolution.md)'s forward-compatibility predicate.
- The substrate records `(M_1, superseded-by, M_2) = t` as substrate content (per [self-representation](teaching/explanations/self-representation.md)) and retains $M_1$'s rule extension for replay.
- The substrate's event log is intact per [recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism](recovery-via-event-log-replay-determinism.md) (so older-model dispatch is replayable).
- For the tower-fold case: each level's reindexing $f_n^*$ is a fibration reindexing per [predication-fiber-functor](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/predication-fiber-functor.md); the chain is small (a set, not a proper class) so the colimit is among those the fibration's small-cocompleteness provides.

## Position

Together with [forward-migration-preserves-prior-content](forward-migration-preserves-prior-content.md) (the rule-system case) and [migrate-rule-system](../morphisms/migrate-rule-system.md) (the morphism implementing the migration), this corollary completes the substrate's discipline for *evolution-preserving content*. Closure-rule-system versions, model-cell versions, and tower-fold level chains are three engineering surfaces of the same structural phenomenon — directed forward-migration in the predication fibration with audit-chain extension.

The substrate's claim to *non-destructive model upgrade* is therefore structural: the upgrade is a substrate cell (the supersession edge), the audit chain extends, the older model's behaviour replays from the event log, the new model's content is monotone over the old's. A "model upgrade lost old answers" failure mode cannot occur in a substrate respecting the corollary, because the structural argument forbids it.

This corollary discharges the captain-question on whether voidmath F9 (tower/fold) requires a dedicated kernel surface: it does not. The substrate's existing forward-migration plus `superseded-by` substrate-cell pattern realise tower-fold without new primitives. F9 reduces to a documentation-naming question (whether to *name* model-supersession as an instance of forward-migration in the v0.9 authoring) rather than a new-primitive question.
