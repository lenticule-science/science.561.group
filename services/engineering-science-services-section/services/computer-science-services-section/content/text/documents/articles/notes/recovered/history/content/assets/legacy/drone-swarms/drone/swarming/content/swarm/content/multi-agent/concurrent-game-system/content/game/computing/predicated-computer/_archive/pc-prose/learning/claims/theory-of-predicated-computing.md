# The Theory of Predicated Computing

**Theorem.** Predicated computing — the engineering theory of computers whose primary substrate is the bilattice-valued $(s, p, o)$ predication cell — has a dual mathematical grounding: its value domain $\mathbf{FOUR}$ is derived from the computability theory ($\mathbf{Eff}$) via the double-negation nucleus, and its predication fibers are models of the multiplicative-additive linear logic fragment (MALL) of the linear-computation theory. The closure-rule system is a Medvedev-filter nucleus on $\mathbf{Eff}$. Predicated computing is the sub-theory generated by the intersection of $\mathbf{Eff}$ and MALL within $\mathbf{CGS}$.

**Proof.**

*The value domain $\mathbf{FOUR}$ from $\mathbf{Eff}$.* In the computability theory, the double-negation topology $j = \neg\neg$ on $\mathbf{Eff}$ defines an internal map $\rho: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$ given by $\rho(P) = (\neg\neg P, \neg\neg\neg P)$. The image of $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ under $\rho$ is the four-element structure $\mathbf{FOUR} = \{(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{f}), (\mathbf{f},\mathbf{t}), (\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}), (\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t})\}$ — verified, refuted, undecidable, and contradictory — which is the Belnap-Dunn four-valued bilattice (Belnap 1977, Dunn 1976) and simultaneously the twist product $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$ (Fitting 1991). The four-valued structure is not an external design choice; it is forced by Rice's theorem: non-trivial behavioral predicates of programs must land in $\mathbf{FOUR}$, not $\mathbf{2}$. The predicated computer's value domain is therefore $\mathbf{FOUR}$ as derived from $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$. This computation is entirely internal to $\mathbf{Eff}$.

*Predication fibers as MALL models.* In the linear-computation theory (MALL, the multiplicative-additive fragment of linear logic in $\mathbf{CGS}$), the multiplicative connectives $(\otimes, \parr, \mathbf{1}, \bot)$ and additive connectives $(\&, \oplus)$ operate without the exponential $!$ — resources are used exactly once. A predication fiber at predicate $p$ is a MALL algebra valued in $\mathbf{FOUR}$: the knowledge-order join $\sqcup_k$ corresponds to the additive conjunction $A \mathbin{\&} B$ (both predicates are known, choose which to provide), the truth-order join $\sqcup_t$ corresponds to the additive disjunction $A \oplus B$ (commit to the more strongly-supported assertion), and predication composition corresponds to $\otimes$ (two predication cells can be composed into a joint witness). The absence of $!$ is correct: predications are consumed when they enter a rule application (each $(s, p, o)$ triple is a resource, not freely copiable). The fiber algebra is therefore a MALL algebra over $\mathbf{FOUR}$.

*Closure operators as Medvedev-filter nuclei.* A predicated computer's closure-rule system generates a closure operator $j: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ that is a Lawvere-Tierney topology on $\mathbf{Eff}$: idempotent, $\mathrm{id} \leq j$, and meet-preserving. From the computability theory's subtheory lattice result (the nucleus lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$ is isomorphic to the filter lattice of the Medvedev lattice $\mathbb{M}$), every such nucleus $j$ corresponds to a filter $F \subseteq \mathbb{M}$ of mass problems. The closure rules of a predicated computer define such a filter: each rule schema is a mass problem (given witnesses for the body, produce a witness for the head), and the filter condition ensures that the rule system is closed under modus ponens and rule composition. The closure operator $j_F$ is the Lawvere-Tierney topology corresponding to $F$. The runtime settling of a predicated computer is the iteration of $j_F$ to its least fixpoint, which exists by Kleene's fixed-point theorem (internal to $\mathbf{Eff}$).

*The intersection of $\mathbf{Eff}$ and MALL.* The Girard decomposition (Seely 1989) embeds $\mathbf{Eff}$ into the $*$-autonomous world via the Dialectica construction: $\mathbf{Eff} \simeq \mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})})$, where $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$ is $*$-autonomous and $\mathbf{Eff}$ is recovered as its ILL Kleisli subcategory. Within $\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Eff})$, the MALL fragment — objects without the $!$ comonad — is a sub-$*$-autonomous-category. The predication fiber algebra lives in this MALL fragment: it uses $\otimes, \&, \oplus$ but not $!$ (predications are linear resources). The closure operators (Medvedev-filter nuclei) live in the Kleisli fragment (ILL level). Predicated computing occupies the intersection: it uses the MALL fiber algebra (from the linear fragment) AND the Medvedev-filter nucleus (from the Kleisli/ILL fragment). This intersection is the sub-theory generated jointly by MALL (from linear-computation) and the intermediate nucleus lattice (from computability). $\square$
