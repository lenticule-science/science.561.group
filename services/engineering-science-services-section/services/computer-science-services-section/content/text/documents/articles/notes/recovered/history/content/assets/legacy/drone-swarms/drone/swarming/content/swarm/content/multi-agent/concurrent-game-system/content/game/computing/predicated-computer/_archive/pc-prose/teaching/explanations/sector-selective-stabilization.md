# Sector-selective stabilization

*Sector-selective stabilization* is the structural feature of aperiodic-regime settlings whereby some $K_4$-equivariant sectors of the aperiodic-classifying-invariant stabilize (vanish from a countable stage $\tau_0$ onward) while the complementary sectors generically remain nonzero. The aperiodic regime *permits* this option; the strict and Scott-continuous regimes do not, because their cohomology vanishes globally and cannot distinguish stabilizing from non-stabilizing sectors.

The $K_4$-equivariant decomposition projects $H^1$ into four isotypic sectors indexed by the four irreducible characters of the Klein-four group $K_4 = \langle \sim, - \rangle$ (negation and conflation):

- $H^1_\mathrm{id}$ — symmetric under both involutions.
- $H^1_{\chi_\sim}$ — antisymmetric under negation; symmetric under conflation.
- $H^1_{\chi_-}$ — symmetric under negation; antisymmetric under conflation.
- $H^1_{\chi_{\sim\circ-}}$ — antisymmetric under both.

A polity in the aperiodic regime can have its joint settling's classifying invariant settle to zero in some sectors while remaining nonzero in others. The sector selection is governance-regime-specific.

## Why sector-selectivity is the regime's structural strength

A polity needs *both* halves of sector-selectivity simultaneously:

- *Some content must stabilize* — the cells whose stabilization licenses the polity to act. Without stabilization in this set, the polity has no settled state from which to dispatch.
- *Some content must not stabilize* — the cells whose ongoing settling constitutes the polity's aim-generative work. Without persistent non-stabilization in this set, the polity has saturated and ceased to be itself.

The strict regime cannot provide both. Strict-regime polities have $\kappa < \omega$ and their cohomology vanishes globally; they stabilize *everywhere* at a finite ordinal. Any aim-generative work would have to live outside the settling, which contradicts the polity being a substrate-resident object. The aperiodic regime is what allows simultaneous stabilization and non-stabilization in disjoint sectors of the same substrate.

This is also the structural reason aperiodic-regime polities exhibit *continuity-through-disruption*: the stabilizing sectors carry conduct that doesn't waver across disruption, while the non-stabilizing sectors carry the work that doesn't complete. Both are required for the polity's continuity.

## Governance-regime instantiations

- *Kyriarchical regime:* safety-critical sectors $H^1_{\chi_-}, H^1_{\chi_{\sim\circ-}}$ play the stabilizing role; vanishing in these sectors is what the dispatch certifiability gate consults to license action. Aim-generative sectors $H^1_\mathrm{id}, H^1_{\chi_\sim}$ remain live. See [`kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026`](../../../learning/surveys/kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026.md), §8.
- *Kin-relational regime (tiyóšpaye):* wolakȟota-stabilized sectors play the role; the four virtues' substrate-content predicates correspond to stabilizing cells whose specific cohomological identification is the subject of an open question ([`cohomological-articulation-of-wolakhota`](../../../learning/questions/cohomological-articulation-of-wolakhota.md)). The aim-generative sectors carry the tiyóšpaye's ongoing work.
- *Other regimes:* the structural fact of sector-selective stabilization holds; the specific sector mapping is regime-specific.
