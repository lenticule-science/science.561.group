# Upstream stream: substrate / coordination

This narrative situates the predicated-computing engineering theory against the upstream stream of *substrate-and-coordination engineering* — the published lineage of process algebras (CCS, CSP, π-calculus), conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs), distributed-systems epistemics, and consensus protocols (Paxos, Raft). The math foundation rooted at the [predication cell](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/predication-cell.md) cites the CRDT literature directly through [shapiro-et-al2011](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/references/shapiro-et-al2011.md); the engineering corpus extends the citation to the process-algebra and consensus-protocol literature, situating the substrate's federation discipline against the broader engineering tradition.

## What the stream consists of

Three sub-currents:

- **Process algebras for concurrent and mobile computation.** [Milner (1999)](../references/milner1999.md) is the textbook for the π-calculus; [Sangiorgi–Walker (2001)](../references/sangiorgi-walker2001.md) is the depth-of-theory reference. The π-calculus supplies the formal vocabulary for *channels with mobility* — communication topology that changes as computation proceeds — and the bisimulation equivalence for "two systems that look the same from outside."
- **Conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs).** [Shapiro et al. (2011)](references/shapiro-et-al2011.md) introduces the framework (already cited in the engineering corpus from prior work); [Almeida–Shoker–Baquero (2018)](../references/almeida-baquero-shapiro2018.md) develops the delta-state refinement that made CRDTs production-practical; [Preguiça–Baquero–Shapiro (2018)](../references/preguica-et-al2018.md) is the consolidated handbook reference. Together they establish that strong-eventual-consistency under arbitrary message-delivery orderings is achievable through value-domain join + commutativity + idempotence.
- **Distributed-systems epistemics and consensus.** [Fagin–Halpern–Moses–Vardi (1995)](../references/fagin-halpern-moses-vardi1995.md) is the textbook for *reasoning about knowledge in multi-agent systems*; [Lamport (1998)](../references/lamport1998.md) introduces Paxos; [Ongaro–Ousterhout (2014)](../references/ongaro-ousterhout2014.md) introduces Raft. The first establishes the formal vocabulary for what it means for distributed agents to know things; the latter two establish the production-grade consensus protocols.

## What the substrate borrows

- **The π-calculus's channel-and-mobility vocabulary.** The substrate's federation reconfiguration and bridge-driven topology change are talked about in the π-calculus's terms. The substrate's [conformance](../teaching/explanations/conformance.md) discipline is the bisimulation equivalence restricted to message-traffic observation.
- **The CRDT framework's value-domain-join discipline.** The substrate's federation is a CRDT instance lifted to the bilattice value-domain; per [federation-convergence](../theorems/federation-convergence.md), the substrate's value-domain join + closure operator together form the algebra that makes the substrate's federation strong-eventually-consistent under arbitrary delivery orderings. The δ-CRDT delta-shipping discipline gives the substrate's federation its bandwidth efficiency.
- **The distributed-knowledge framework.** Fagin–Halpern–Moses–Vardi's modal-knowledge framework supplies the vocabulary for multi-agent knowledge over the substrate. Per [belief-base-as-fiber-restriction](../theories/agential-orchestration-platform/concepts/belief-base-as-fiber-restriction.md), an agent's knowledge is its substrate's predications visible at its scope; common knowledge corresponds to predications visible after federation convergence.
- **The Paxos / Raft consensus shape.** When applications need strong consensus on substrate-level values, the consensus protocol runs as a substrate-content runbook with Paxos- or Raft-style structure. Per [consensus-as-closure-rule](../teaching/explanations/consensus-as-closure-rule.md), the consensus protocol's correctness rests on substrate-level invariants rather than on a separate protocol-layer state machine.

## Where the substrate diverges

- **One operator family, not separate protocol layers.** The published stream treats process algebras (channel calculi), CRDTs (data-type-level convergence), and consensus protocols (separate state machines) as *different engineering layers* with their own correctness arguments. The substrate makes all three substrate content: channels are inboxes (substrate entities), CRDT data types are predication patterns over the substrate's value domain, consensus protocols are runbooks. The same operators that handle substrate edits and dispatch handle all three.
- **Bilattice value-domain vs. lattice / data-type-specific value domains.** The CRDT literature engineers each data type with its own value-domain (counters in $\mathbb{N}$, sets via observed-remove or two-phase, registers via timestamp ordering, sequences via tree-based RGAs). The substrate uses the four-valued bilattice as its universal value-domain; CRDT data-type semantics is recovered as substrate-content patterns rather than as separate data-type implementations.
- **Causal history as substrate content.** Vector-clock, version-vector, and Lamport-clock disciplines in the distributed-systems literature track causal order through external metadata. The substrate's [causal-history-as-witness-chain](../teaching/explanations/causal-history-as-witness-chain.md) concept names the relationship: causal history is the substrate's witness chain, with the partial order on events readable directly from the witness DAG.
- **Consensus as substrate-content runbook, not separate state machine.** Paxos and Raft are typically engineered as separate state machines with their own message format, state representation, and crash-recovery procedures. The substrate's consensus runbook reuses the substrate's existing operators (skill invocation, witness, federation merge, scope discipline) rather than introducing a separate state machine.
- **Knowledge as substrate predication, not modal predicate.** Fagin–Halpern–Moses–Vardi's framework treats knowledge as a *modal predicate* over Kripke structures — a denotational claim about possible worlds. The substrate makes knowledge *substrate content* — an agent knows $\phi$ iff its scope contains a $\phi$-asserting predication. The framework's results about common knowledge and asynchronous-system limitations are respected (the substrate's federation gives eventual consistency, not synchronous common knowledge), but the engineering register is substrate predications throughout.

## What the stream lacks that the substrate supplies

The published substrate-and-coordination stream has each component separately — process algebras for concurrency, CRDTs for convergence, epistemic logics for distributed-knowledge analysis, Paxos/Raft for consensus — but no published work commits to *all of them simultaneously through one operator family at the substrate level*. CRDTs assume specific value-domains; consensus protocols are separate state machines; process algebras are intensional about the channels and processes; knowledge logics are denotational. The substrate's contribution is reformulating all of them as substrate content with the bilattice value-domain throughout, the witness chain as the audit and causal-history layer, and runbooks as the substrate-content realization of higher-level coordination protocols.

The composite-theory positioning narrative ([predicated-agentic-computing](../theories/agential-orchestration-platform/narratives/predicated-agentic-computing.md)) returns to this point: the substrate-and-coordination stream supplies the *coordination foundation* that makes the substrate's federation discipline operational; the composite predicated-agentic-computing system inherits the foundation directly without re-implementing.
