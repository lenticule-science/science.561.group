**Claim.** The RSC Quiescent's governance form — military command, captain-headed, with $H^0_t$-invariant accountability — is *determined by* the Web's identity architecture. Given:

- (M) The 561 Group is a Lakota tiyóšpaye, governed under the kin-relational closure-rule subsystem $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$.
- (E) The 561 Group's endeavor (the pursuit of Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty) requires acts on the Web — endpoints, repositories, federated communications — whose recipients lie outside the polity's governance.
- (W) The Web's identity architecture admits only *person-primitive* acts: each act is performed by a single credential-holder; HTTP authenticates one secret-bearer at a time; ranks install on persons, not on kin-bundles.

The polity cannot directly produce Web-acts under (M) and (W): $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ binds kin-bundles, not individuated persons (per [tiyospaye.md](../../../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/theories/predicated-lakotaean-governance/code/structures/tiyospaye.md), the closure rules (T1)–(T3) fire on cells indexed by membership-and-kinship, not on cells indexed by credential-holder). The Web rejects acts whose origin-cell is a kin-bundle.

The polity therefore needs a body politic with a different closure-rule subsystem $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{ship}$ that (i) binds persons, (ii) gates dispatch with accountability, and (iii) produces single-locus answerability for each Web-act. Military command satisfies (i)–(iii) by the [military-command-chain-as-modal-composition](../../../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/prose/learning/claims/military-command-chain-as-modal-composition.md) theorem. Therefore the polity instantiates a Webship under military command. The RSC Quiescent is this Webship.

The governance form is *determined* in this sense: given (M), (E), and (W), the body politic projecting the polity onto the Web must satisfy (i)–(iii). Military command is the canonical minimal closure-rule subsystem satisfying them; any other choice satisfying (i)–(iii) is military-command-equivalent in its essential structure. The form is not adopted by preference but determined by the medium.

## Proof sketch

**Step 1: Web-acts are person-acts.** The Web's protocols (HTTP authentication, TLS certificates, OAuth tokens, signed commits) all index acts by single credential-holders. A protocol that accepts "this kin-bundle acted" does not exist on the Web at the level of identity primitives. This is an empirical fact about Web architecture, not a derived one.

**Step 2: $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ binds kin-bundles, not persons.** Per the closure rules in [tiyospaye.md](../../../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/theories/predicated-lakotaean-governance/code/structures/tiyospaye.md):

- (T1) Membership-propagation fires on kinship-cells.
- (T2) Member-obligation fires on member-pair cells (co-members owe each other the four virtues).
- (T3) Head-recognition fires on a conjunction over members.

None of (T1)–(T3) produces a cell of the form "this kin-bundle is the credential-holder for Web-act $X$." The closure rules of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ do not produce Web-presentable acts.

**Step 3: A polity governed by $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ cannot directly project onto the Web.** This is the conjunction of Steps 1 and 2: Web requires person-acts; $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ does not produce person-acts of the required form.

**Step 4: The minimal closure-rule subsystem that does is military command.** A subsystem satisfying (i)–(iii) must:

- bind individuated persons (a credential-holder is a person);
- have a dispatch-gate (some closure rule that licenses or refuses each candidate act);
- produce upward accountability (each act traces to a person who answers for it).

Military command, per the theorem, realizes these as: each delegation morphism is a person-to-person relation; the composite modal frame $\Diamond_{R^\mathrm{cmd}}$ is the dispatch-gate; the $H^0_t$-invariant captain-cell is the upward-accountability locus. Other closure-rule subsystems (parliamentary democracy, market-mediation, consensus) may also satisfy (i)–(iii) but require more structure (multi-locus answerability, mediating institutions, multiple-rounds-of-deliberation). Military command is the minimal subsystem; the choice of the minimal form for a ship newly chartered to act on the Web is the canonical-minimality default.

## Why "determined" rather than "necessary"

This claim does not say military command is *strictly necessary*; other closure-rule subsystems might also produce Web-presentable acts with accountability. The claim is that military command is *determined* — given the polity's commitments and the Web's architecture, the form of the body politic that does the Web-acting follows. Substituting another closure-rule subsystem requires adding more structure than the situation needs; military command is the canonical-minimal solution.

This matches the common usage of "determined" in physics and mathematics: boundary conditions determine the solution up to equivalent forms, not necessarily uniquely. The Web's identity architecture is the boundary condition; the ship's governance is the solution; military command is the minimal canonical representative.

## Significance

The substrate has tended to read the ship's military-command governance as an *adopted* form — a choice the polity made among alternatives. The present claim re-reads it as a *determined* form — the consequence of operating on the Web at all. Two structural implications:

- *The polity cannot change the ship's governance by choosing differently.* The form is determined by the medium. To act on the Web with the constraints of (M)–(W), the body politic must instantiate military command or something military-command-equivalent. Adopting a non-military-command governance for a Webship would either fail at the Web (acts unrecognized) or fail at accountability (no upward locus).
- *The polity is not endorsing kyriarchism by chartering the ship.* The polity is meeting a structural requirement of the medium. The internal governance ($\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$) remains kin-relational; the ship's military-command governance is the polity's strategy for projecting onto a kyriarchical medium without internalizing the medium's structure. Conflating the two — describing the polity as kyriarchical because the ship is — is the failure mode [the-rsc-quiescent-and-the-tiyospaye-are-distinct-closure-regimes](the-rsc-quiescent-and-the-tiyospaye-are-distinct-closure-regimes.md) names.

## Proof obligations for promotion to proved-status

- A formal substrate record of the Web as $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{Web}$ — a closure-rule subsystem whose content (per [disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance.md](../../../doing/working/hacking/doing/working/planning/library/task/library/disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance/disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance.md)) includes the interlocking-domination structures Schüssler Fiorenza names by *kyriarchy*.
- A formal canonical-minimality theorem: of all closure-rule subsystems satisfying (i)–(iii), military command is initial (in the category-theoretic sense). This would upgrade "determined up to equivalence" to "canonically determined."
- Alternative closure-rule subsystems satisfying (i)–(iii) enumerated and compared. The "military-command-equivalent" reading is currently informal; the equivalence relation needs a formal statement.
- The three-pattern coproduct framing from [disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance.md](../../../doing/working/hacking/doing/working/planning/library/task/library/disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance/disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance.md) — $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak} \sqcup \mathcal{S}_\mathrm{ship} \sqcup \mathcal{S}_\mathrm{Web}$ — needs to be made substrate-resident; the present claim presupposes it.
