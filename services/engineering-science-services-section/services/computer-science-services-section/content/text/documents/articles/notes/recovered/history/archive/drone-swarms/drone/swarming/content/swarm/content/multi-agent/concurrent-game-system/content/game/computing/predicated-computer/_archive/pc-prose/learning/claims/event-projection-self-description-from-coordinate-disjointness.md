# A projection's payload must self-describe whenever its restricted coordinates are not preserved by the join-target projection

**Claim.** Let $\pi_A, \pi_B : \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}(c_A), \mathcal{F}(c_B)$ be two [knowledge-join](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/knowledge-join.md)-natural projections of the substrate's [presheaf-indexed fiber](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/presheaf-indexed-fiber.md) at distinct contexts $c_A, c_B \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{sub}}$. Write $\mathrm{coord}(c)$ for the set of context-tuple coordinates (stratum, peer, session, ord, …) that $c$ restricts (versus collapses across). Suppose $\pi_A$ is *consulted as a payload-bearing surface* (a reader expects $\pi_A$'s output to carry the per-atom values it cares about) and $\pi_B$ is the substrate's *cell-store projection* (the predicate-keyed surface that already carries those values). The substrate may legally implement $\pi_A$'s payload as either:

- **a self-describing payload**, in which $\pi_A$'s output records the per-atom values verbatim; or
- **a join-pointer payload**, in which $\pi_A$'s output records a pointer (cell id, content-hash) into $\pi_B$ and the reader joins through to $\pi_B$ for the values.

The choice is *forced by coordinate-preservation between the two projections*:

- $\pi_A$ MAY be a join-pointer payload iff $\pi_B$ preserves every coordinate $\pi_A$ restricts — formally, $\mathrm{coord}(c_A) \subseteq \mathrm{coord}(c_B)$. Concretely: $\pi_B$ already exposes every cell $\pi_A$ would point to, indexed on the same coordinates.
- $\pi_A$ MUST be self-describing whenever $\mathrm{coord}(c_A) \not\subseteq \mathrm{coord}(c_B)$ — i.e. there is a coordinate $\pi_A$ restricts that $\pi_B$ collapses. Most commonly: $\pi_A$ is time-ordered ($\mathrm{ord} \in \mathrm{coord}(c_A)$) and $\pi_B$ is predicate-keyed ($\mathrm{ord} \notin \mathrm{coord}(c_B)$). The reader of $\pi_A$ at context-restriction $c_A$ cannot reconstruct the value via a join through $\pi_B$ because $\pi_B$ does not preserve the missing coordinate.

The condition is on coordinate-preservation, not on coordinate-disjointness; we use the latter informally to mean "$c_A$ has a coordinate $c_B$ does not."

The asymmetry generalises: the [event log](../objects/event-log.md) is the time-ordered projection of the substrate's content, and the [predication-graph store](../objects/predication-graph-store.md) is the predicate-keyed projection. The event log MUST carry per-event payload information that the cell store does not preserve; it MAY skip information the cell store preserves under a key the event log retains.

## Why this holds

The argument is direct from naturality and projection composition.

**Part 1: A join-pointer payload requires the join target to preserve the joining key.** A reader of $\pi_A$ that follows a pointer into $\pi_B$ joins at some key $k$ — typically a cell id or content-hash. The join is well-formed iff $\pi_B$'s output contains an entry at key $k$ for every $k$ $\pi_A$'s pointers reference. Per [projection-merge-naturality](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/projection-merge-naturality.md), $\pi_B$'s output at key $k$ is $\pi_B(\mathcal{F}|_k)$ — the projection's restriction to atoms with that key. The join is well-formed iff $\pi_B$'s context restriction $c_B$ does not exclude the atoms $\pi_A$ at $c_A$ pointed to.

The condition $\mathrm{coord}(c_A) \subseteq \mathrm{coord}(c_B)$ (read: every coordinate $c_A$ restricts is also restricted by $c_B$, or equivalently, $c_B$ preserves every coordinate $c_A$ restricts) is sufficient — every atom $\pi_A$ retains at $c_A$, $\pi_B$ also retains at $c_B$, and the join is exact on those coordinates.

**Part 2: When $\mathrm{coord}(c_A) \not\subseteq \mathrm{coord}(c_B)$, the join target loses the missing coordinate.** Suppose $c_A$ restricts a coordinate $\delta$ — e.g., event-ordinal $\delta = \mathrm{ord} = n$ — that $c_B$ does not (the cell-store projection collapses across event-ordinal: a cell's value at $\mathrm{ord} = n$ and at $\mathrm{ord} = n+5$ both project to "the cell-store value"). Then $\pi_B$ is *not* injective on the $\delta$-axis: two atoms differing only in $\delta$ map to the same $\pi_B$-output. Following a pointer from $\pi_A$ into $\pi_B$ recovers $\pi_B$'s collapsed value, *not* the $\pi_A$-specific value at coordinate $\delta = n$. The join is unsound.

The substrate's response is: $\pi_A$ MUST carry the $\delta$-restricted information in its own payload — it cannot delegate to $\pi_B$. This is the self-description requirement.

**Part 3: The condition is sharp.** When $\mathrm{coord}(c_A) \subseteq \mathrm{coord}(c_B)$, $\pi_B$ preserves every coordinate $\pi_A$ restricts; the join is exact and the join-pointer payload is admissible. When $\mathrm{coord}(c_A) \not\subseteq \mathrm{coord}(c_B)$, $\pi_B$ collapses on the missing coordinate; the join is unsound and the self-description payload is mandatory. There is no intermediate regime.

## Engineering consequence

- **The event log's per-event payload must record what the cell store collapses across event-ordinal.** The cell store is the predicate-keyed projection at $c_{\mathrm{cell}} = (\mathrm{stratum} = \mathrm{settled}, \mathrm{peer} = \mathrm{self}, \mathrm{ord} = \infty)$ — collapsed across event-ordinal. The event log is the time-ordered projection at $c_{\mathrm{event}} = (\mathrm{stratum} = \star, \mathrm{peer} = \star, \mathrm{ord} = n)$ — restricted on event-ordinal. Per Part 2 ($\mathrm{ord}$ in $c_{\mathrm{event}}$, not in $c_{\mathrm{cell}}$), the event log MUST carry per-ordinal information the cell store cannot preserve: the $(s, p, o, v)$ tuple at the $\mathrm{ord} = n$ moment, the witness chain at that moment, the closure-rule version at that moment, the per-stratum atoms emitted at that moment.
- **A pure-pointer event payload is admissible only on retraction-stable predicates.** When the predicate $p$ is retraction-stable — its cell-store projection at $\mathrm{ord} = \infty$ contains the same value as it did at $\mathrm{ord} = n$ for every $n$ since the cell was authored — the event log MAY carry a pure pointer (cell id) and the reader joins through to the cell store. Most substrate predicates are not retraction-stable: a retraction event at $\mathrm{ord} = m > n$ collapses the predicate's cell-store value to $\bot$ or $\top$, and a follower reading the event at $\mathrm{ord} = n$ via a pointer would see the post-retraction value, not the at-$n$ value.
- **The audit-conformance test discipline is structurally determined.** A test that asserts "the event at ordinal $n$ records the $(s, p, o, v)$ tuple of the cell change" is testing the substrate's self-description discipline; the test passes iff the event payload preserves the at-$n$ information per Part 2. A test that asserts "the event at ordinal $n$ records a pointer to the cell store's *current* value of $(s, p, o)$" is testing a join-pointer discipline; the test passes only on retraction-stable predicates. Mixing the two in a single test surface is the structural hazard the failing-test signal in the dev question reports.
- **Federation receive that consumes the event log directly requires self-describing payloads.** A federation peer that reconstructs the sender's history by replaying the sender's event log cannot resolve pointers into the sender's cell store (the receiver does not have the sender's cell store as a queryable surface; the receiver has the sender's events). Per Part 2, the event log's payload must carry every value the receiver needs to reconstruct the sender's by-context projections. Per [cross-peer-stratum-projection-via-naturality](cross-peer-stratum-projection-via-naturality.md), the receiver's by-stratum projection of the merged history requires per-event $(s, p, o, v, \mathrm{stratum}, \mathrm{ord})$ — exactly the self-description payload.
- **Codifying a join-discipline helper is equivalent to electing the join-pointer payload.** A spec that says "event bodies are pointers; readers go through an `audit-query` helper that joins to the cell store" is, by Part 1, an election of the join-pointer payload that is valid only when $c_{\mathrm{event}} \subseteq c_{\mathrm{cell}}$. Per Part 2, this election fails on retraction-stable / non-retraction-stable mixed surfaces; the helper does not change the structural condition, it only relocates where the join is performed.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Pure-pointer event payloads on non-retraction-stable predicates.** A substrate that ships event bodies as pure cell-id pointers, and whose readers are expected to join through to the cell store for the $(s, p, o, v)$ tuple, fails the at-ordinal-$n$ readability requirement on any predicate that has been retracted or superseded. The substrate's response is to either (a) restrict the pure-pointer discipline to retraction-stable predicates (with an explicit retraction-stability cell on each predicate), or (b) carry self-describing payloads on every event.
- **Self-describing event payloads that disagree with the cell store.** Once the event log is self-describing, the substrate has two presentations of the same content: the per-event payload and the cell store's projection. Per [two-store-coherence](teaching/explanations/two-store-coherence.md), the pair must be in the d-frame's $\mathrm{con}$ relation — disagreement surfaces as a $\top$-witness. The substrate's discipline is to detect divergence (e.g., periodic re-derivation of the cell store from the event log per [source-of-truth](teaching/explanations/source-of-truth.md)) and to repair via the canonical event-log-is-authoritative direction.
- **Predicate-key digests that pretend to be self-description.** A scheme that adds a sorted-keys SHA-256 digest to event bodies (rather than the per-event $(s, p, o, v)$ tuple) is a *pointer* with extra integrity-checking metadata — it does not preserve the at-$n$ value. Per Part 2, this is not self-description and does not satisfy the federation-receive requirement above; it is admissible as a tamper-detection augmentation of a pure-pointer payload, not as a substitute for self-description on non-retraction-stable predicates.
- **Audit-log fibers as substitute for event-payload self-description.** A separate cell-store fiber that records audit-relevant per-event information (e.g., a `audit-log` predicate on the changed entity) is a *third* projection $\pi_{\mathrm{audit}}$, not the event log itself; per Part 2, it must satisfy its own self-description condition with respect to whatever projection a reader joins through to. The substrate may elect a per-stratum split (event-payload self-describing for security-critical strata; pure-pointer for routine strata), but the choice is per-predicate-stratum and recorded as substrate content.

## What the theorem requires

- The two projections $\pi_A, \pi_B$ are presented with explicit context-coordinate signatures (per [presheaf-indexed-fiber](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/presheaf-indexed-fiber.md)).
- The cell-store projection's coordinate signature is fixed for the period the event-payload election is in force; a schema change at the cell-store projection retires the prior election.
- The substrate's event log and cell store are coherent under [two-store-coherence](teaching/explanations/two-store-coherence.md) (the d-frame $\mathrm{con}$ holds whenever both projections express the predicate).
- For pure-pointer payloads: every referenced predicate carries a retraction-stability cell that the substrate's audit discipline can consult.

## Position

This theorem refines [storage-as-projection](storage-as-projection.md) by addressing the multi-projection case. The substrate's content can be projected on many context coordinates — by predicate (the cell store), by event-ordinal (the event log), by peer (federation per-peer projections), by session (in-flight per-session projections). Each projection's payload-completeness is structurally determined by which coordinates the *other* projections preserve. The theorem turns "should this projection's payload duplicate what another projection already records?" from a stylistic-redundancy question into a structural one: duplication is forbidden when the other projection preserves every coordinate this one restricts; duplication is mandatory when it does not. Composes with [source-of-truth](teaching/explanations/source-of-truth.md) (the event log's authority over derived stores requires its payload to carry whatever the derived stores will need to reconstruct), with [two-store-coherence](teaching/explanations/two-store-coherence.md) (the d-frame structure on the event-log–cell-store pair is the substrate-level statement of the agreement requirement when both projections are payload-bearing), and with [section-materialisation-trichotomy](section-materialisation-trichotomy.md) (the materialisation regime A/B/C choice for a projection composes with its self-description election: a materialised A projection that is a join-pointer to another A projection inherits this theorem's coordinate-disjointness condition).
