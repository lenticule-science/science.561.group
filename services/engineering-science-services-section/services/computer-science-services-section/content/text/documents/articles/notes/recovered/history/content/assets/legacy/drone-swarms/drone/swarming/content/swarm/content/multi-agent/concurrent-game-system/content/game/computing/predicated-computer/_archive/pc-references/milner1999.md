## Source

R. Milner. *Communicating and Mobile Systems: The π-Calculus*. Cambridge University Press, 1999.

## Form

Textbook. Milner's mature exposition of the *π-calculus*, the canonical process algebra for concurrent and mobile systems with first-class channel-passing.

## Content summary

Develops the π-calculus: a process algebra for concurrent computation in which the primitive is the *channel* (a typed name on which processes can synchronize) and the distinguishing feature is *channel mobility* — channels can be passed as values along channels, so the communication topology between processes can change dynamically as the computation proceeds. The textbook covers: the calculus's reduction and labeled-transition semantics; bisimulation as the equivalence of processes that cannot be distinguished by external observation; type systems for channel use (linearity, session types, polymorphism); the calculus's relationship to lambda calculus (encodings in both directions); applications to concurrent objects, distributed systems, and security protocols. The π-calculus became the canonical reference for *the algebra of concurrency with mobility* and the mathematical underpinning of session-typed languages, channel-based programming systems, and the extensive process-algebraic literature that grew from CCS, CSP, and ACP.
