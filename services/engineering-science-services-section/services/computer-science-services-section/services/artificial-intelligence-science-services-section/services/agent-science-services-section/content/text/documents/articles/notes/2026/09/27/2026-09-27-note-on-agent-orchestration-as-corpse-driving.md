# 赶尸: Agent Orchestration as Corpse-Driving

## Abstract

Agentic programming has moved the programmer's work from writing code to directing language-model agents that write it. Agent orchestration has moved it again, to directing agents that direct agents.
The field names the resulting role with borrowed words: copilot, assistant, teammate, fleet, swarm. Its critics answer with the zombie, the golem, and the parrot.
This article proposes a closer model, taken from the textual record of a Chinese funeral trade: 赶尸 (*gǎnshī*, "driving corpses"), the art of walking the dead home for burial through western Hunan and Guizhou.
Xu Ke's compendium of 1917 records it as 送尸術, "the art of sending corpses" [@xu1917], and Shen Congwen visited it with open irony in 1938 [@shen1939].

Read closely, the record describes a paid transport discipline with a definite structure.
A body that cannot speak "walks when the men walk and stops when they stop."
A written charm binds it to the route.
Two men are required: one leads, and the other follows carrying a bowl of charmed water, and the body falls if the water spills.
Inns keep a room for the trade.
The practitioner keeps the method secret.
On arrival the water is poured out and the body, unless it is coffined at once, "shows a month's decay" for a month dead.

Each element has a precise counterpart in the orchestration of coding agents: stateless models; context files and skills; fragile working state that someone must carry and check; specialized runtimes; closed methods; and accumulated defects that surface only at integration.
The figure also makes visible three things the dominant metaphors hide.
The dead are being driven *home*, so the work is measured by placement and burial, not by motion.
The labor in the procession belongs to the living driver and to the dead who were once alive.
And the driver, not the corpse, answers to the family.
Shen Congwen's corpse-driver adds that the art "does not work if you don't practice it," an irony of automation stated forty-five years before Bainbridge named the ironies of automation [@bainbridge1983].

The article develops the correspondence element by element.
It tests the correspondence against the agentic-software-engineering literature and against a field record from the 561 Group's own agent-run codebase, states seven propositions that follow, and marks where the figure fails.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The role and its vocabulary

In the working definition Anthropic published in 2024, agents are "LLMs using tools based on environmental feedback in a loop."
It distinguishes them from workflows: "Workflows are systems where LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths. Agents, on the other hand, are systems where LLMs dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage" [@schluntz2024].
The same text names the pattern this article is about: "In the orchestrator-workers workflow, a central LLM dynamically breaks down tasks, delegates them to worker LLMs, and synthesizes their results."
In February 2025 Andrej Karpathy gave the human side of the shift its name, "vibe coding": a way of working in which the programmer will "fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists" [@karpathy2025].

Between those two poles sits a role that is now a large part of many programmers' days.
The person specifies work, dispatches it to one or more agents, watches or does not watch them, reviews what comes back, integrates it, and answers for the result.
The house glossary treats the machine side of this under [agent orchestration](../../../../../../glossary/terms/agent-orchestration.md).
This article is about the human-and-machine arrangement as a whole: the *agentic programmer* and the *agent orchestrator*, human or model, whose work is to move agents through a task.

The vocabulary for that role is borrowed, and every borrowing carries a theory.
*Copilot* says the human still flies the aircraft.
*Assistant* says the machine serves.
*Teammate* and *employee* say it labors alongside.
*Fleet* and *swarm* say many bodies share one purpose.
The critical vocabulary is no less loaded.
The house paper [Describing the Zombie](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/media-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2025/11/09/2025-11-09-describing-the-zombie-2025-11-09.md) reads the Haitian *zonbi* as "a person whose agency is owned": a captured will set to labor for a master.
None of these figures says where the work is going, and none of them makes the person in charge of the bodies the object of study.

### 1.2 From the stiff corpse to the corpse-driver

The house corpus already holds one reading of AI through Chinese undead lore.
[Semantic Web, Jiangshi Web](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/20/semantic-web-jiangshi-web.md) reads linked-data infrastructure as a 僵尸 (*jiāngshī*): a stiff corpse moved by residual *qi* without *shen*, following paths, open to being "re-routed" by small signs and talismans.
It closes with a warning that the field risks "building a clever Taoist priesthood of AI that can steer Jiangshi, but not restore Shen."

That paper studied the corpse.
This one studies the priest, or more exactly the trade that paper's closing image names without examining: the driving of the dead.
The difference is not cosmetic.
As section 2.1 shows, the 僵尸 of Qing fiction is a corpse out of control.
The driven corpse is a corpse under a discipline, moving toward a destination, in the charge of a named person who is paid for the trip.
Agent orchestration is a discipline of that kind, and its characteristic failure is that the discipline lapses and the procession turns into the other figure.

### 1.3 Claim

The claim is that the recorded structure of 赶尸 is an analytic model of agent orchestration, not an ornament for it.
It makes three specific claims.

1. Its elements (body, charm, water, inn, arrival, home, driver) correspond to the elements of an orchestrated coding session closely enough to predict where orchestration fails and what a competent orchestrator must do.
2. It foregrounds three things the dominant metaphors hide: the *destination* of the work, the *labor* in it, and the *liability* for it.
3. Its most careful witness, Shen Congwen, supplies a critique of the economy of demonstration that has grown up around agents.

### 1.4 Sources and method

The primary sources are three.

- Xu Ke's 《清稗類鈔》 (1917), entry 送尸術 in the 方伎類 (arts and techniques) volume [@xu1917].
- Shen Congwen's essay 《沅陵的人》, from the collection 《湘西》, serialized in 1938 and published in 1939 [@shen1939].
- Wang Jia's 《拾遺記》 (late fourth century), for the phrase 行屍走肉, "walking corpse, running flesh" [@wang0390].

For the distinction between the Qing stiff-corpse tale and the driving trade, the article relies on Feng Yichao's study of the 34 jiangshi tales in Yuan Mei's 《子不語》 and its sequel [@feng2007].
Later popular accounts, mostly from the era of Xiangxi tourism and mostly undated, add vocabulary and rules: the corpses called 喜神 ("happy gods"), corpse inns with doors that never close, bells and gongs, lists of which dead may be driven.
They are cited as later lore where they add structure, never as evidence.

This article makes no claim that corpses walked.
It reads the record as a record of how a trade was *described*: by a compiler who compared it to Western hypnotism, by a garrison that emptied out to watch it, and by a novelist who thought the question foolish.
It takes that description as its model.

On the agent side the sources are the agentic-software-engineering literature and a field record from the 561 Group's repository.
In that repository one human founder directs language-model agents that do nearly all the writing.
The field record has two parts:

- the harness file [`.claude/CLAUDE.md`](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../.claude/CLAUDE.md), which the house's knowing engine projects from the founder's corrections across agent sessions, read as generated on 2026-09-27;
- the house's own relocation tooling in the [animating faculty](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/services/computer-engineering-services-section/services/computer-systems-engineering-services-section/services/agent-computer-system-engineering-services-section/apps/agent-organization-engine/lib/animating/README.md) of the agent organization engine.

Translations from Chinese are this article's own.

## 2. The record

### 2.1 Two figures: the stiff corpse and the sent corpse

Feng Yichao counts 34 stories about 僵屍 in Yuan Mei's two collections.
Feng notes that "because of the legend of 'Xiangxi corpse-driving', in the common understanding jiangshi seem mostly to appear around Hunan" (由於「湘西趕屍」的傳說，在一般人的認知中，僵屍似乎多出現在湖南一帶).
Yuan Mei's stories place them from Jiangsu and Zhejiang to Yunnan [@feng2007].
The popular association of the stiff corpse with Hunan, on Feng's account, comes from the driving legend, not from the tales, which put jiangshi everywhere.

Feng's survey of *where* jiangshi appear is decisive for this article.
The newly dead who are not yet coffined appear in houses.
"Those who died in a strange place and await return burial appear in the inns where corpses are lodged" (客死異地待歸葬者，則出現在停屍的旅舍).
The long-dead who were never buried appear in wild graves, deep woods, and ruined temples.
In every case the jiangshi is a body that has not been properly brought to rest.
In the Qing imagination the stiff corpse is the *failure state* of the process that corpse-sending accomplishes: a body that did not make it home.

There are therefore two models, and they must not be merged.
The 僵屍 is animation out of control.
The Jiangshi Web paper used it for an infrastructure whose joints have stiffened.
The sent corpse is animation under a discipline, bound for a destination.
Agent orchestration belongs to the second model, and its failures take the form of the first.

### 2.2 Xu Ke's entry: the art of sending corpses (1917)

Xu Ke opens with a comparison of techniques:

> 西人之催眠術，能催生人，而不能催死人，能催數小時之久，而不能催至數月之久。而黔、湘間有送尸術，則以死尸而由人作法，進止聽命，可歷數月。
>
> Westerners' hypnotism can put the living under but not the dead; it can hold for some hours but not for months. Between Guizhou and Hunan, though, there is an art of sending corpses: a person works the method upon a dead body, which advances and halts on command, and this can last for months.

He adds that it differs from ordinary corpse-transformations (尸變), which happen on some stimulus "or are perhaps a kind of electrical action" (或係一種電氣作用者).
Xu Ke then gives the setting:

> 貴州商人採木為生者，每春水生時，輒編木為筏，乘之，直下湖南常德等處，將木筏析賣，乃遵陸還鄉。有病死者，道遠，尸不易回，同行者往往有送尸之術。然必兩人行之，乃有效。
>
> Guizhou merchants who live by timber lash logs into rafts each spring when the water rises, ride them straight down to Changde and other places in Hunan, break up and sell the rafts, and return home overland. When one of them dies of illness, the road is long and the corpse is not easily brought back, so his fellow travellers often have the art of sending the corpse. But it works only if two people perform it.

The method:

> 其術，一人導於前，一人以手持碗水隨於後，【碗中清水必加持符咒。】水不傾潑，尸不倒也。尸與生人無異，但不能言，其行步與生人亦微異。蓋人行則行，人止則止，純隨二人步趨。
>
> One leads in front; the other follows behind, holding a bowl of water in his hands [the clear water in the bowl must be charged with a spell]. So long as the water does not spill, the corpse does not fall. The corpse is no different from a living man except that it cannot speak, and its gait is slightly different: when the men walk it walks, when the men stop it stops, following the two men's steps entirely.

The lodging:

> 至薄暮投宿旅店時，逆旅主人見之，即知為送尸之客，必另備一房與居。【此種送尸人，時時不絕於道，彼處客店，每專備一房招待之。】二人睡於牀，尸則立於門側，湘諺所謂「三人住店，兩人喫飯」者也。
>
> At dusk, when they seek lodging, the innkeeper knows them on sight for corpse-senders and always prepares a separate room [corpse-senders of this kind are never absent from the roads, and the inns there each keep a room specially for them]. The two men sleep on the bed and the corpse stands beside the door: this is what the Hunan saying calls "three lodge at the inn, two eat the rice."

The arrival:

> 將至家前一日，尸必託夢於其家人，其家則將棺木衣衾，預備齊整。尸抵家，則挺立於棺側，術人將碗水傾於地，尸立倒，須急為收歛，否則其尸立變，現出腐壞之形矣。【如已死一月者，尸即現一月之腐狀，餘倣此。】
>
> The day before it reaches home, the corpse always appears to its family in a dream, and the family makes ready the coffin and grave-clothes. When the corpse reaches home it stands stiffly beside the coffin. The practitioner pours the bowl of water onto the ground and the corpse falls at once. It must be coffined quickly, or else it changes at once and shows the form of its rot [one dead a month shows a month's decay, and so on].

Xu Ke gives a witness: "in the autumn of 1909, Yang Kuanfu of Lu'an, sojourning in Hunan, saw it himself outside Changsha."
He then records a second account, from the camp of General Huang Zhonghao on the Sichuan border:

> 則見一人持布旛前導，一尸直立，隨其人，惘惘而步。因呼止之，詢其所以，云：「此人旅死，不能具棺木，特用法驅之自行，歸就家以歛耳。」問何法，曰：「吾業此，安能以其祕告人。」……復詢土人，云：「此事常有之，不足異也。」
>
> He saw a man leading with a cloth banner and a corpse standing upright following him, stepping along in a daze. He called a halt and asked why. The man said: "This man died on the road and there was no way to provide a coffin, so I am driving him by method to walk by himself, home to be coffined." Asked what method, he said: "This is my trade; how could I tell its secret to others?" … The local people, asked, said: "This happens often; it is nothing strange."

Nine structural features can be read off the entry:

1. **An economic setting.** The dead are migrant workers, timber rafters who died of illness far from home. Huang's corpse-sender states the economics plainly: there was no money for a coffin. Driving is the cheap alternative to carrying.
2. **Two kinds of practitioner.** Fellow travellers "often" have the art, and there are also professionals: "this is my trade."
3. **A two-person rule.** One leads; one follows with the water.
4. **A fragile, charmed state.** The body stands as long as the water does not spill.
5. **A body that only follows.** It cannot speak, its gait is slightly wrong, and it moves only when the men move.
6. **Specialized infrastructure.** Inns keep a room for the trade, and a proverb records its billing: three lodge, two eat.
7. **A prepared arrival.** The family is warned and readies the coffin.
8. **Decay deferred, then revealed.** When the water is poured out, the body falls, and unless it is coffined at once it shows all the rot it accumulated on the road.
9. **A secret method and a public spectacle.** The practitioner will not explain, hundreds watch, and locals shrug.

Xu Ke's own frame also counts as evidence.
The entry reads the art through the newest imported techniques available in 1917, hypnotism and electricity.
Section 6 returns to this.

Feng notes that Zhan Yinxin's 2001 study of Chinese magic reproduces this entry.
Feng observes that it describes an art current "between Guizhou and Hunan (not only in Xiangxi)" (黔湘間（並非只是湘西一地）) [@feng2007; @zhan2001].

### 2.3 Shen Congwen's visit (1938)

Shen Congwen's 《湘西》 essays were serialized in Hong Kong in the autumn of 1938.
The essay on Yuanling (the old Chenzhou) opens its treatment of the subject with the traveller's expectation: "Chenzhou is known for the Chenzhou talisman, and among the talisman's legendary wonders corpse-driving is the most famous" (辰州地方是以辰州符闻名的，辰州符的传说奇迹中又以赶尸著闻) [@shen1939].
He then disappoints it:

> 多数当地人对于辰州符都莫名其妙，且毫无兴趣，也不怎么相信。
>
> Most local people are baffled by the Chenzhou talisman, have no interest in it, and do not much believe in it.

The traveller may meet a local "big man," a chamber-of-commerce type.
This man will swear to having seen a beheaded chicken rejoined by talismanic water and sent running after scattered rice, and on reflection the traveller may feel that he too has seen it somewhere.
Shen's diagnosis:

> 原来五十年前的什么书上，就这么说过的。这个大人物是当地著名会说大话的。世界上事什么都好像知道得清清楚楚，只不大知道自己说话是假的还是真的，是书上有的还是自己造作的。
>
> It turns out some book fifty years ago said exactly this. This big man is famous locally for talking big. He seems to know everything in the world clearly; he just does not quite know whether what he says is false or true, whether it is in the books or of his own making.

The legend itself, Shen says, is kept alive by and for outsiders:

> 凡受了点新教育，血里骨里还浸透原人迷信的外来新绅士，想满足自己的荒唐幻想，到这个地方来时，总有机会温习一下这种传说。
>
> Any new gentleman from outside who has had a little new education but still has primitive superstition soaked into his blood and bones, and comes here wanting to satisfy his absurd fantasies, will always have the chance to review this legend.

The one practitioner worth visiting is a man called Que Wulao, nearly sixty, who is said to have learned the art in Yunnan and Guizhou at fifteen.
His incantation turns out to be ordinary:

> 作法时口诀并不希奇，不过是念文天祥的《正气歌》罢了。死人能走动便受这种歌词的影响。
>
> The incantation used in working the method is nothing special; it is just a recitation of Wen Tianxiang's "Song of the Upright Spirit." That the dead can walk is the effect of these lyrics.

The "Song of the Upright Spirit" (《正氣歌》, 1281) is Wen Tianxiang's prison poem of loyal fortitude, a canonical and widely memorized text.
Shen remarks that learning the art uses it as its incantation, "and so turns out to have a great deal to do with the Confucians, is not without its reward" (同儒家居然大有關係時，也不無所得).
A bowl of water stands before the ancestral tablets in Que's house; by Que's account it has stood there for thirty-five years, topped up as it evaporates.
Asked whether Que has ever driven a corpse, the answer is:

> 当然的。我还亲自赶过！那是我一个亲戚，在云南做官，死在任上，赶回湖南，每天为死者换新草鞋一双，到得湖南时，死人脚趾头全走脱了。只是功夫不练就不灵，早丢下了。
>
> Of course. I've driven one myself! A relative of mine was an official in Yunnan and died at his post; I drove him back to Hunan. Every day I changed the dead man's straw sandals for a new pair, and by the time we reached Hunan the dead man's toes had all walked off. But the art doesn't work if you don't practice it; I dropped it long ago.

Shen names the transaction exactly: "The guest's aim is a 'performance'; the host's intention is to 'mystify his account'; in the end the guest is naturally disappointed" (客人目的在"表演"，主人用意在"故神其说"，末后自然不免使客人失望).
He then imagines the silent mockery in Que's Voltairean smile:

> 用《正气歌》赶走了死尸，你充满好奇的关心，你这个活人，是被什么邪气歌赶到我这里来？
>
> The dead were driven off with the Song of the Upright Spirit; and you, a living man full of curious concern, what Song of the Crooked Spirit drove you here to me?

Shen turns the question on the visitor, who "perhaps even means to take him as your teacher, learn it, and go abroad to make money and a name, or at least come home with a doctorate in philosophy and use it to fool Chinese students."

The essay's last movement turns away from the corpses altogether.
The road the traveller rode in on was built "in blazing sun and wind and snow" by the mountain people the officials dismiss as "fierce and untamed" or as bandits.
In Yuanling women everywhere earn their living by hard, conscientious labor.
Shen concludes: "The mystery of this place, it turns out, lies here and not there" (这地方神秘处原来在此而不在彼).

Shen's essay is literary and ironic, and it is not an ethnography.
It is evidence of how the trade was talked about in 1938, and it is a critique of that talk.
Both uses matter below.

### 2.4 The later lore

Later accounts, many of them written for tourists, add features absent from Xu Ke and Shen:

- the trade name 喜神, "happy gods," for the corpses;
- corpse inns whose doors stand open year-round, with bodies propped behind them against the wall;
- night travel only, with the driver shaking a bell and striking a gong to warn the living off the road;
- yellow-paper talismans written in cinnabar;
- lists of which dead may and may not be driven;
- the rule that a single corpse is not "driven" but "carried" (背).

Modern accounts describe transport "using long bamboo poles" and call the practice "a money-saving endeavour" [@yang2024].
These accounts are compiled in popular and reference sources [@worldofchinese2014; @baidubaike2026].
This article uses the later lore sparingly and marks it where it does.
One piece of it is worth keeping for its structure: the euphemism 喜神, which gives the dead a cheerful name.

## 3. The elements of an orchestrated coding session

To fix terms, an orchestrated coding session has these parts.

- **The model.** Trained weights, fixed during deployment, carrying no memory from one session to the next except what the surrounding software supplies.
- **The harness.** The loop that feeds the model its context, executes its tool calls, enforces permissions, and runs hooks at fixed points.
- **The standing instructions.** Context files (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md), skills, and system prompts: written text placed in front of the model at the start of each session, telling it who it is, what the rules are, and where things belong.
- **The working state.** The context window; the repository's working tree, index, and history; journals and logs; the provenance of each change. See the house memo on [dirty-worktree feedback loops](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/agentic-programming-and-dirty-worktree-feedback-loops.md).
- **The runtimes.** Sandboxes, worktrees, containers, background units, and the machines they run on, with their memory and token budgets.
- **The orchestration.** A human, a model, or both, dispatching subagents under the orchestrator-workers pattern and synthesizing their results [@schluntz2024].
- **The receiving side.** Review, tests, continuous integration, merge, and the architecture into which the work must fit.

The *agentic programmer* is the human who carries this procession from task to integration.

## 4. The correspondence

| 赶尸 element (record) | Orchestration counterpart | Where it holds | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| The body: cannot speak, gait slightly wrong, follows the men's steps (Xu) | The model in the loop | Memory: nothing carried from one day's road to the next. Gait: output close to a person's but detectably off. | The agent speaks and chooses its own steps within a turn. |
| The charm on the body and in the water; the incantation is the 正氣歌 (Xu, Shen) | Context files, skills, system prompts | Must be re-applied every session; efficacy credited to the words but resting elsewhere. | Charms are not read; prompts are read and interpreted. |
| The bowl of water carried behind; if it spills, the body falls (Xu) | Working state: context, tree, journal, provenance | The body stands on a state someone else must carry and watch. | Spilled state often does not make the agent fall; it makes it walk wrong. |
| Two men required (Xu) | Orchestrator plus independent verifier | Leading and checking are different jobs held by different persons. | Pipelines collapse the two into one agent checking another. |
| Inns keep a room; "three lodge, two eat" (Xu) | Sandboxes, worktrees, compute; the token bill | The procession consumes lodging, not wages. | Compute is priced and metered in a way lodging never was. |
| Water poured out at home; decay shown at once (Xu) | Integration, merge, production | Defects deferred in transit surface all at once at arrival. | Software decay can be surfaced early; a corpse's cannot. |
| Unburied dead become 僵屍 in inns (Feng) | Unintegrated outputs: duplicates, legacy copies, stray files | What never reaches its home turns dangerous. | Code does not rot on its own; it rots against a moving system. |
| Home burial (歸葬) | The owning module in the architecture | The work has a destination and a terminal state. | The "home" is designed by people and may itself be wrong. |
| The driver: secret method, paid by the family, "doesn't work if you don't practice" (Xu, Shen) | The agentic programmer or orchestrator | Labor, skill decay, accountability. | The driver's liability was social; the programmer's is contractual and diffuse. |

### 4.1 The body: the learned dead that follow

*The body is dead labor.*
The model is made from the past labor of people: the public code of programmers who wrote it for other purposes, and the rating and correction work of annotators.
Marx's image for accumulated past labor set to work fits almost too well: "Capital is dead labour, that, vampire-like, only lives by sucking living labour, and lives the more, the more labour it sucks" [@marx1867].
The image is analogical, and section 8 marks its limit.
What it shows is that the body in the procession is not a *new* worker.
It is a figure assembled from work already done.
Wang Jia's scholar Ren Mo, dying, said: "One who loves learning, though dead, is as if alive" (夫人好學，雖死若存) [@wang0390].
A language model is the learning of the dead, persisting.

*It does not remember the road.*
The corpse in Xu Ke's account carries nothing from one day's walking to the next; it follows.
A model session carries nothing from the last session unless the harness hands it something.
This is the axis on which the figure is exact, and section 4.2 measures its cost.

*Its gait is slightly wrong.*
Xu Ke's body "is no different from a living man except that it cannot speak, and its gait too is slightly different."
The coding agent *does* speak, which is the obvious break.
Its gait, though, is the gait the literature describes: output that passes for a person's work and is off in characteristic ways.
Casserini, Facchini and Ferrario describe agents that "propose 'textbook' solutions that appear locally elegant but overlook system-specific architectural and security constraints," optimizing "local correctness" while drifting from "global architectural intent" [@casserini2026].
The house memo on dirty worktrees records *additive preservation bias*: the agent that, unsure what it may touch, adds wrappers and parallel implementations rather than editing.

*It follows, except when it does not.*
"When the men walk it walks, when the men stop it stops."
For a workflow in Anthropic's sense, with predefined code paths, the correspondence is exact.
For an agent, which "dynamically directs its own processes," it breaks: the modern body chooses its own steps between the driver's commands.
The break is useful, because the failure literature concentrates exactly there.
The MAST taxonomy groups the failures of multi-agent systems into system design issues, inter-agent misalignment (among them task derailment and withheld information), and task verification [@cemri2025].
Each is a failure of a body taking steps nobody walked ahead of.
Suchman's account of plans as resources for situated action, rather than programs that determine it, locates the difficulty [@suchman2007].
The driver's command is a plan; the body's steps between commands are where the plan meets the road.
The orchestrator's discipline, read through the figure, is to keep the unsupervised stride short.

### 4.2 The charm: text that binds a memoryless body to a route

Xu Ke's water "must be charged with a spell."
Later lore puts the charm on the corpse's forehead.
Shen Congwen's practitioner deflates it: the incantation is the 《正氣歌》, a text everyone knew.
The efficacy is credited to the words, but the words are ordinary.

Context files and skills work the same way.
The prompt is credited with the behavior.
The behavior depends mostly on what the model absorbed in training, most of which is ordinary text everyone knew, and on the harness around the model.
Chun's critique of "sourcery" describes this misattribution precisely.
Treating software as source, Chun argues, is "a fetishism that obfuscates the vicissitudes of execution and makes our machines demonic."
In fact "source code is only source code after the fact: its effectiveness depends on a whole imagined network of machines and humans."
The notion of the user as super agent "is the obverse, not the opposite of this 'sourcery'" [@chun2008].
Together these beliefs make us "vulnerable to fantastic tales of the power of computing."
The prompt-writer who believes the charm walks the corpse is the sourcerer of the agentic era.

The field record puts a number on the charm's weakness.
The 561 Group's harness file is not handwritten.
The house's knowing engine projects it from the founder's utterances to agents, and as generated on 2026-09-27 it reports what the founder had told "over 1656 utterances in 203 sessions."
It states that "221 relations were told in 2 or more sessions: each extra session is one where an earlier session did not carry it."
The most restated standing directives are:

| Directive | Sessions in which it was told |
|---|---|
| read the corpus first | 49 |
| commit and push without being asked | 24 |
| reduce parallel implementations | 22 |
| do not create parallel implementations | 21 |
| revive legacy material into the living tree | 21 |

A charm that must be re-pasted forty-nine times has failed forty-eight times to carry.
This is the memoryless body seen from the driver's side: each night the talisman goes back on, because in the morning the corpse knows nothing of the road.

The house's response is also legible in trade terms.
Its standing directives forbid handwritten agent files (told in 14 sessions) and require that the charm be *generated* from the organization's consolidated record of what the founder has told.
The talisman is written by the institution from its records, not improvised by each driver for each trip.
The measure of whether this works is the one the file already reports: how many directives still have to be told again.

The Jiangshi Web paper's other observation about charms also applies here.
The jiangshi is susceptible to "re-routing" by "small signs, talismans, and environmental tweaks."
A body moved by text can be moved by *any* text it encounters on the road.
For agents this is prompt injection: a page, file, or tool output that carries a charm of its own.

### 4.3 The water: the state carried behind

The bowl of water is the most exact element of the correspondence.
The orchestrated body stands on a fragile state, and someone other than the body must carry that state and watch it.
In agentic programming the water is the working state: the context window, the working tree and index, the journal of what was proposed and what was observed, and the provenance of every change.
When the water spills (the context is compacted, the tree fills with unattributed changes, a peer session's edits mix into one's own), the body falls.
Worse, it may keep walking wrongly.

The house memo on dirty worktrees states the mechanism in modern terms: "For coding agents, dirty state is a control signal. If it is ambiguous, it can reshape the agent's policy."
It names the result *VCS-state-induced action-space collapse*.
It also names what the water lacks when it is not "charged": Git "does not represent **intent** unless the workflow adds intent-bearing structure."
Xu Ke's clear water "must be charged with a spell."
Raw state is not enough; the state the body stands on has to carry the meaning of the trip.

The house's own tooling shows what it looks like to carry the water well, and it is candid about the limits.
The relocation command in the animating faculty records "a SignChain proposal before mutation and an applied or observed telling after reading the result."
"Filesystem failures during application leave an unresolved proposal; they are not automatically rolled back."
Its receipts "must not be mistaken for an independent proof of placement."
That is exactly the water's evidential limit: it shows the body is still standing, not that the body has reached home.

### 4.4 The inn and the rice: runtimes and their bills

The inns "each keep a room specially" for the trade.
Agentic programming has built the same specialized lodging: sandboxes, per-task worktrees, background units, agent runtimes and control planes.

The proverb "three lodge at the inn, two eat the rice" is a joke about billing.
The corpse takes up a room but does not eat.
The agent economy has the same shape: agents occupy capacity (compute, memory, context, tokens) and draw no wage.
The capacity is not small.
Anthropic reports that "agents typically use about 4× more tokens than chat interactions, and multi-agent systems use about 15× more tokens than chats" [@hadfield2025].
The house records this cost under [coordination overhead](../../../../../../glossary/terms/coordination-overhead.md).

A procession fills the inn.
The field record lists "agent causes oom" as told in 4 sessions and "agent causes disk-damage" in 2.
The bodies standing beside the door are a load on the building, and in a small inn that means one shared machine.

### 4.5 Arrival: the water poured out and the decay shown

The arrival rite is the figure's sharpest prediction.
The body reaches home and stands beside its coffin.
The water is poured out, and the body falls.
Unless it is coffined at once it shows all its decay together, in proportion to the time it has been dead.

This is the failure mode of agentic software work stated as a rite: *defects deferred in transit, revealed all at once at integration*.
On the road the procession looks alive.
The tests pass, the demonstration runs, the agent reports success.
Casserini and co-authors describe exactly this: "functional checks (e.g., confirming that the code runs) can thus mask deeper architectural violations and security gaps, producing an illusion of rapid progress while drift accumulates beneath the surface" [@casserini2026].
The house holds a note on [that paper](../../04/21/2026-04-21-note-on-beyond-the-diff-addressing-agentic-entropy-in-agentic-software-development.md).

The illusion is measurable.
In METR's randomized trial, sixteen experienced open-source developers worked on 246 tasks in their own mature projects.
They forecast that AI tools would cut their completion time by 24%, and afterwards they estimated the cut at 20%.
Measured, the tools increased completion time by 19%, with a confidence interval of +2% to +39% [@becker2025].
METR's later attempt to replicate was compromised by selection: developers declined to work without AI, or withheld tasks they thought needed it.
METR describes its new data as "only very weak evidence" about the size of any speedup, so the 2025 figure should be read as what it was, a snapshot [@metr2026].
The snapshot's gap between perception and measurement is the point.
The procession looked alive on the road.

The rest of the rite also carries over.
The family is warned the day before and makes the coffin ready, so the receiving side is prepared before the body arrives.
Feng's finding supplies the other half: the dead who never reach home become the jiangshi of the inns.
The unburied outputs of agentic work are their equivalents:

- duplicate implementations of one capability;
- legacy copies kept "for history";
- untracked files;
- half-finished relocations.

They wait in the inns of the codebase and turn.
The field record shows the founder fighting exactly these.
The standing directives say *do not create parallel implementations* (21 sessions) and *revive legacy material into the living tree* (21 sessions).
They say *move capabilities to the section that owns them* (9 sessions).
And they say *do not mass-delete*: "do not want you to blindly delete it, the capabilities, if there are any, need to be realized properly" (12 sessions).
The last is the rite's own rule.
Burial is not disposal: the dead are coffined *as* who they were, and the family must know whom it is burying.

### 4.6 Home: why the dead are driven at all

The whole trade exists because a body belongs somewhere.
Burial in one's home soil (歸葬; in the idiom, fallen leaves return to their roots, 落葉歸根) is the reason anyone walked a corpse through the mountains at night.
The dominant metaphors for agents have no such destination.
The zonbi labors for its master indefinitely.
The golem guards until it is erased.
The copilot flies until the aircraft lands, but a codebase never lands.
赶尸 is a *transport problem with a terminal state*: arrival, then burial, then the procession ends.

For code, the destination is the place in the architecture that owns the capability.
This is the figure's most consequential prediction.
If orchestration is measured by motion (lines generated, tasks closed, agents dispatched, pull requests opened), it will reward exactly the failure the rite exists to prevent: bodies walking forever, never buried, filling the inns.
The field record already registers the correct reading: the founder's relations include "code-on-disk is-a ai-slop" and "code-on-disk not is-a precious-resource."
A corpse's motion is not evidence that it is alive.

## 5. The driver

### 5.1 Whose labor walks

Shen Congwen's last move is the one this article most needs.
Having dismissed the corpse-driving legend, Shen points at the road: it was built by mountain people in sun and snow, and the women of Yuanling work for their living.
"The mystery of this place lies here and not there."

The phrase "the agent wrote it" hides three kinds of labor.

- **The dead's.** The programmers whose public code trained the models, and the annotators and raters whose work shaped them. The house holds the relevant sociology: Gray and Suri's [Ghost Work](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/services/participation-economy-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/bibliographies/grayandsuri2019.md) [@grayandsuri2019], Casilli's [study of click-work](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/services/participation-economy-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/bibliographies/casilli2019.md) [@casilli2019], and the term [microwork](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/services/participation-economy-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/microwork.md).
- **The driver's.** Walking ahead and carrying the water: specifying the task, writing and maintaining the charms, watching the state, recovering when the body falls, reviewing, and integrating. METR's 19% is, among other things, a measurement of this walking.
- **The receivers'.** The reviewers and maintainers downstream, who inherit what Casserini and co-authors call the "reviewer's paradox," in which "rising agentic output overwhelms human verification capacity" [@casserini2026].

The spectacle of the walking dead draws attention from all three, and the mystery is in the labor.

### 5.2 行屍走肉: the driver who stops learning

Wang Jia's Ren Mo, dying, warned: "One who loves learning, though dead, is as if alive; one who does not learn, though alive, is called a walking corpse and running flesh" (夫人好學，雖死若存；不學者雖存，謂之行屍走肉耳) [@wang0390].
Applied to the procession, the sentence inverts it.
The model is the learning of the dead that persists.
The *driver* who has stopped learning is the walking corpse.

Que Wulao said it about the corpse-driver's own trade: "the art doesn't work if you don't practice it; I dropped it long ago."
Forty-five years later Bainbridge stated the same thing about automated plants.
Automation leaves the operator the tasks that could not be automated, above all monitoring, while the manual skills needed to take over decay for want of use.
The operator is therefore least able to intervene at the moment intervention is needed [@bainbridge1983].
Braverman's account of [deskilling](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/deskilling.md) describes the same movement at the scale of an occupation [@braverman1974].
Kosmyna and co-authors report its cognitive form as "cognitive debt" accumulating in people who delegate writing to an assistant [@kosmyna2025].

The corpse-driver's version is sharper than the engineer's.
It does not say only that the operator's skill decays.
It says the art stops *working*: the dead stop walking for a driver who no longer walks.

### 5.3 Two men, not one

"It works only if two people perform it."
The leader and the water-bearer are distinct persons with distinct jobs.
The one in front chooses the road; the one behind keeps the state that holds the body up.

A great deal of current orchestration practice collapses the pair.
One agent writes and another agent, reading the same charm and often the same state, checks.
The rear man has become a second corpse.
The MAST study treats task verification as one of its three top-level failure categories [@cemri2025].
The founder's standing directives name the theatrical form of this collapse: *no verification theater* (5 sessions).
The figure's requirement is precise.
The water-bearer holds *their own* bowl: an independent state and the receiver's own condition for accepting the body, not a second recitation of the leader's charm.

### 5.4 How many dead to a driver

The later lore says a driver takes at least two corpses; a single corpse is "carried," not driven.
The upper bound is set by what one driver can keep in line and in sight on a mountain path at night.

The agent literature finds a similar bound.
The house glossary records a saturation "at approximately 4 agents," beyond which coordination costs grow faster than capability.
The MAST authors found that the performance gains of multi-agent systems "on popular benchmarks are often minimal" [@cemri2025].
Anthropic, reporting on its own multi-agent system, says that "most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than research, and LLM agents are not yet great at coordinating and delegating to other agents in real time" [@hadfield2025].
The founder's version, recorded among their standing directives against running many concurrent subagents (9 sessions), is: "No part of that implies 'run more agents faster harder.'"
A longer procession is not a faster one; past a small number it is just more bodies to lose.

### 5.5 Answering to the family

The corpse cannot be blamed for anything.
The family commissions the trip, the driver walks it, and if the body is lost, falls in a ditch, or arrives rotten, the driver answers.
The arrangement is candid about where responsibility lies, and the driver is paid in part for bearing it.

Automated systems often arrange the opposite.
Elish's *moral crumple zone* names the pattern in which the human nearest an automated system absorbs the blame when it fails, whether or not they could control it [@elish2019].
The house paper [Answerable Performances](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/06/27/researching/2026-06-27-agentic-autonomy-performance-affect.md) builds on Elish.
It asks that "responsibility does not collapse onto the nearest human after failure."
Its sixth requirement is "responsibility before delegation."
The corpse-driving figure agrees about the order and adds one thing: in a well-run trade the nearest human *is* the responsible one, knowingly and by prior arrangement.
The problem is not that someone near the body answers.
The problem is answering without the authority, the information, or the pay that go with answering.

Latour's study of the door-closer describes delegation to non-humans as a two-way street.
What we delegate to a mechanism, the mechanism prescribes back to us as behavior we must adopt [@johnson1988].
The procession prescribes the driver's life: night roads, a steady bowl, a room at the inn.
Orchestration prescribes the orchestrator's: the review queue, the context files, the hours spent recovering spilled state.

One more feature of the record bears on accountability: "This is my trade; how could I tell its secret to others?"
The family trusts a method it cannot inspect.
Closed models and proprietary harnesses put the agent economy in the same position, and answerability in the sense of the house paper requires methods the family can read.

## 6. Performance and mystification

Shen Congwen's sentence about the visit is the best short description of the agent demonstration economy: "The guest's aim is a performance; the host's intention is to mystify his account; in the end the guest is naturally disappointed."
The agent economy runs on the same exchange: benchmark announcements, staged demonstrations, and the rebranding of ordinary automation as "agentic."
Answerable Performances cites Gartner's forecast that more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, and its warning of "agent washing."

Shen's "big man" is an exact portrait of confabulation, in machines and in their boosters.
He "does not quite know whether what he says is false or true, whether it is in the books or of his own making," and what he swears to have seen turns out to be what "some book fifty years ago said."

Que Wulao's silent question is addressed to the orchestrator: "The dead were driven with the Song of the Upright Spirit; and you, a living man full of curious concern, what Song of the Crooked Spirit drove you here to me?"
The driver is driven too, by the attention economy, by fear of being left behind, and by the prospect Shen names without mercy: to learn the art "and go abroad to make money and a name, or at least come home with a doctorate in philosophy and use it to fool Chinese students."
This article is not exempt.
The 561 Group sells agent work, and it has drafted a paid [cohort offering a "Multi-Agent Operating Discipline"](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/public-social-services-section/services/public-education-services-section/services/public-computer-education-services-section/services/artificial-intelligence-education-services-section/services/artificial-intelligence-curriculum-enterprise/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/31/2026-08-31-note-offering-multi-agent-operating-discipline-cohort.md) to teams that run several coding agents.
The visitor who wants to learn the art to sell it is also the author.

Xu Ke's frame belongs here as well.
In 1917 Xu Ke read the art through hypnotism and "a kind of electrical action," the newest imported techniques to hand.
This article reads it through language models.
In time it should expect to look like Xu Ke's electricity, and the durable part of both readings is the structure of the trade, not the technology used to explain it.

## 7. Propositions

The figure yields propositions that can be tested.
Each names what to measure.

**P1. Placement over motion.**
Measure orchestration by capabilities returned to their owning modules and duplicates dissolved, not by lines, tasks, or pull requests.
Count unintegrated outputs (parallel implementations, legacy copies, untracked files) as liabilities on the session's books.
*Prediction:* teams measured by motion accumulate unburied work faster than teams measured by placement, and their integration failures are larger and later.

**P2. The two-person rule.**
Verification must be done by a party that holds its own state and the receiver's own acceptance condition, not by a second agent re-reading the first agent's instructions and output.
*Prediction:* agent-checks-agent pipelines detect fewer integration defects than independent receiving checks of equal cost, and the difference is concentrated in the MAST verification category.

**P3. Institutional charms.**
Generate standing instructions from the organization's settled record rather than writing them per session, and track the *re-telling rate*: the share of directives that must be told again in later sessions.
*Prediction:* generated instructions lower the re-telling rate relative to handwritten ones. The 561 Group's harness already computes the rate and can serve as the first longitudinal case.

**P4. Pour the water early.**
Integrate in small, reversible, checkpointed steps so that decay shows in transit instead of all at once at arrival.
Treat checkpointing as an invariant, as the dirty-worktree memo recommends (its §9.3).
*Prediction:* defect discovery time falls as checkpoint frequency rises, and so does the size of the perceived-versus-measured productivity gap.

**P5. Keep walking.**
Orchestrators must do the work by hand at intervals, and organizations should measure *take-over capacity*: how well the orchestrator performs when the agents are withdrawn.
*Prediction:* take-over capacity declines with the share of work delegated, in line with Bainbridge; the decline is slower where manual practice is scheduled.

**P6. Bound the line.**
Limit concurrency to the number of processions one driver can keep in state and in sight.
*Prediction:* for coding tasks, net integrated output per driver peaks at a small number of concurrent agents, near the ~4 saturation in the literature, and falls beyond it.

**P7. The driver answers, by prior arrangement.**
Before delegation, name the office that answers for each procession.
Give it the authority, the information, and the budget that go with answering, and prefer methods it can read over trade secrets.
*Prediction:* incidents in organizations that assign answerability before delegation produce fewer disputed attributions of fault than incidents in organizations that assign it afterward.

## 8. Where the figure breaks

**The agent is not dead in every relevant sense.**
It speaks, chooses steps within a turn, reads and writes, and can be spoken to and corrected mid-route.
It also learns within a context.
The figure is exact about memory across sessions and about discipline; it is inexact about initiative.
Section 4.1 uses the break, but it remains a break.

**Code has no family.**
The seriousness of burial at home comes from filial obligation and ancestral land, and none of that transfers to a module.
The "family" of a codebase is the organization and the people who depend on it.
The "home" is an architecture people decided on, which may itself be wrong.
Whether an architecture is a true home or merely a destination is a design question the figure cannot settle.

**The dead's labor is analogical.**
The model embodies past labor, but inference is new computation that consumes new energy and new capital.
Marx's "dead labour" is used here as a figure, not as a claim about value.

**The history is uncertain, and the figure belongs to someone.**
Nothing in this article depends on corpses having walked, and the practice's historical extent is unclear.
The lore has been commodified by regional tourism.
It is associated with the Miao and Tujia communities of western Hunan as well as with Han Daoist practitioners and Guizhou traders.
Using it as a figure for a technology industry risks exactly the move Shen Congwen mocks, the new gentleman from outside who comes to review the legend for his own purposes.
This article has kept to the documented textual record and names the risk; it cannot claim to have escaped it.

**The zombie is still needed upstream.**
For the annotators and data workers whose labor shapes the models, the zonbi's frame of captured, owned labor fits better than the corpse-driver's.
赶尸 describes the orchestrator's relation to the model and to the work.
It does not describe the platform's relation to its ghost workers, and it should not be used to soften that relation.

## 9. Conclusion

Agent orchestration is usually described as management of workers, piloting of aircraft, or command of a swarm.
The record of 赶尸 describes it better, as a trade in which a skilled and accountable person walks bodies without memory along a road, by night, toward the place they belong.
The bodies are bound to the route by a written charm that has to be reapplied every day.
They stand on a fragile state that a second person must carry, and on arrival they show at once whatever decay the road concealed.

The figure does not flatter the trade.
It says the charm is ordinary text, the spectacle is for visitors, and the one who stops practicing the art finds that it no longer works.
It says the measure of the work is the burial, not the walking.
And it says, with Shen Congwen, that the real mystery was never the walking dead.
It was the labor that built the road.
