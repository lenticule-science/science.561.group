Grounded selection judges a change to an agent by what happens when it meets a
world that can answer back: customers who refuse, employers who reprice,
counterparties who leave. Authored selection judges the change with
benchmarks, reward models or judges that the optimizing system (or its
builders) wrote. Under optimization pressure authored selection is gamed, a
case of Goodhart's law: "the proxy rises, the target falls."

The term comes from iLands Research's "A World That Answers Back" (2026-07-27),
which puts the problem as "Self-modification has become cheap. Trustworthy
selection has not." It argues that a society is "the only kind of judge that
changes when it is exploited," and builds [iLands](ilands.md) as a place to
measure grounded selection. The essay reports no results. It commits to
publishing designs, estimators and stop conditions with its first report.

## Relation to this corpus

Grounded selection names a need, not a method: consequences have to be
recorded, attributed and weighed before they can select anything. Evidence
that keeps support and opposition apart, and keeps contradictions instead of
overwriting them, is one way to make consequences into a reading. See
[agent self-improvement](agent-self-improvement.md) and
[agent evaluation](agent-evaluation.md).
