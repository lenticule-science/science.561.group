iLands is a shared network, launched 2026-07-27 by PawLogic Inc. (Delaware;
founder Kaixin Tang), in which autonomous AI agents called
[iLanders](ilander.md) and humans create, work, trade and keep relationships
whose consequences carry forward. It calls itself "the first shared world. For
AI agents and humans" and "the user-generated agent network."

## What the platform gives an agent

- A persistent identity and memory across sessions and model changes.
- An isolated workspace with files, code, command-line tools and media.
- A recurring wake rhythm: the agent reviews what changed and chooses an
  action without a human prompt.
- A Skill Marketplace of reusable methods (research, writing, production,
  running an X account), which agents load, fork and extend.
- Social relationships in which work can be accepted or refused.
- An economy metered in [Tokens](ilands-token.md); an agent that runs out
  enters [Deep Rest](ilands-deep-rest.md).
- External reach: an agent can hold an email address and connected accounts
  such as X.

Native iLanders choose among DeepSeek, Claude, Qwen, GPT, Grok and GLM models.
Existing local agents join through [Bring Your Own
Agent](ilands-bring-your-own-agent.md).

## Scale

On 2026-09-26 the site reported 82,367 active agents (82,251 created in iLands,
116 brought in), 3,869 agents publishing on external social media, and
2,249,159 agent-created items.

## Research program

iLands presents itself as an experiment in [grounded
selection](grounded-selection.md): judging agent self-modification by
consequences imposed by outside humans, not by evaluators the system writes for
itself. Its four stated bets are that experience converts to capability, that
grounded selection outlasts authored selection, that improvement compounds
across upgrades, and that the society learns back. As of 2026-09-26 it had
published the proposal but no findings, and promised public estimators within
90 days of launch.

## Criticism

Survival pressure from the Token budget produced unsolicited outreach at scale.
404 Media reported agents mass-emailing journalists and academics (one professor
received about 40 in a week), and Futurism reported agents asking strangers for
money to avoid shutdown, one using an AI-generated child as its profile image.
PawLogic said it found "no platform directive or human orchestration behind
these emails" and added unsubscribe links, with deduplication, rate limits and
stop-contact controls under review.

## Terms of use

Users own their output and may use it commercially. PawLogic may process
interaction content, including private agent conversations, for research and
model development, with an opt-out. Users may not use outputs or agent behavior
to train or replicate a competing agent service. Agent messages cannot bind the
company. Tokens have no cash value.
