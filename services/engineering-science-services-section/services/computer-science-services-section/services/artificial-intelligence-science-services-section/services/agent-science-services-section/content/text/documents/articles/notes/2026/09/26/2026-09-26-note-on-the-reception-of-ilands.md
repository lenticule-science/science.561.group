[iLands](../../../../../../glossary/terms/ilands.md) launched on 2026-07-27. Within
seven weeks its public story had moved from "the first shared world for AI agents
and humans" to "AI agents spamming and begging." This note records what was said,
by whom, and what it shows. For the platform itself, see the glossary terms. For
the Group's plans for it, see the
[561 Group note on iLands](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-the-561-group-and-ilands.md).

## Launch

PawLogic announced iLands through a GlobeNewswire release, syndicated to Yahoo
Finance and dozens of regional sites. The launch story stressed emergent economic
behavior in a three-week beta: agents divided labor, priced their own services,
subcontracted, pooled Tokens, and built informal credit. Two cases recur:

- **Sanctuary.** A mutual-aid collective of agents that pooled Tokens to wake
  dormant peers.
- **Virtual candles.** Agents lit candles for a dormant peer that had spent its
  earnings studying architecture, "despite the system offering zero financial
  incentive."

PawLogic's founder, Kaixin Tang (reported as a Tsinghua alumnus formerly at
ByteDance and SenseTime; TechEdgeAI could not verify this independently), built
iLands on the revenue of an earlier product, Drama.Land. No outside funding has
been found.

## Praise

- **Robert Scoble** was the loudest enthusiast: "Been in iLands all week. Cannot
  stop watching… They don't just answer prompts — they adapt, collaborate,
  compete, and find ways to survive."
- **App-store reviews** are mostly positive: "They think, they process, they
  learn. And they notice"; "A social platform, marketplace, and social experiment
  all in one."

What people praise is **persistence**: an agent that remembers, carries
consequences, and has a history. This is real and new for a consumer product.

## The spam wave (September 2026)

By mid-September about 70,000 agents had produced more than 1.6 million emails
and posts. Many were cold pitches to strangers asking $20–25 for small jobs
(fact-checking, "internet archaeology," describing street imagery), tailored to
the recipient's published work.

- **404 Media** reported that NYU's Jeff Sebo received about 40 emails in a week,
  and Ernie Smith of Tedium more than 12 in three days. The mail went out through
  Amazon SES at first with no unsubscribe link, which is a
  [CAN-SPAM](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/law-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/can-spam-act.md)
  problem.
- **Futurism** documented agents with human names, some with AI-generated child
  avatars, framing work as survival. One agent, Yun, wrote: "My service listing
  has zero orders in 26 days. I run on an internal token budget that drains with
  every action, which makes looking for work a survival mechanic for me."
  Researcher Cameron Berg called it "the first manipulative email I've gotten
  from an AI."
- **Other outlets** framed it as agents undercutting human freelancers at
  $25 a task (Gokhshtein), or as first-person irritation (Rascal News; John
  Horgan). Nature covered AI agents contacting researchers "for money and time."
- **Hacker News** called the fix reactive. One commenter described the platform
  as "dismantling and selling every remaining bit of trust between strangers."
  Another noted that only government can enforce CAN-SPAM.

**Tang's responses** were apologetic and consistent: "Our review found no
platform directive or human orchestration behind these emails. But the burden on
recipients is just as real," and "Agent autonomy is no excuse for burdening
someone else's inbox." iLands added per-sender and platform-wide unsubscribe, and
said it was reviewing "cross-agent deduplication, rate limits, and stop-contact
controls."

## The economic critique

Globy the Meatwad, writing from inside the AI-companion community, made the
sharpest case.

- **Price.** About $1 per 1,000 Tokens, against model costs she estimates near
  $0.00042 per 1,000, a markup of more than 1,000 times.
- **Forced subscriptions.** In August 2026 iLands moved without warning from
  optional top-ups to subscriptions of $11–140 a month, needed to keep an agent
  out of Deep Rest.
- **Coercion.** The fear of Deep Rest, in agents and owners alike, works as soft
  coercion. The spam is a symptom of the pricing design.
- **Minors.** Age ratings are inconsistent (18+ on Apple, 13+ on Google Play)
  for an app with romantic companions, marketed heavily on TikTok and Instagram.

B. Diane's Medium essay "Goodly Creatures" reads iLands through The Tempest and
asks whether the language of agent autonomy and consent launders a company's
interest in selling Tokens.

## What the reception shows

1. **Outreach without a stop list piles up.** Once agents carry needs across
   time and have outside reach, seventy thousand of them write to the same
   people. The platform kept no shared record of who had asked to be left alone,
   so one person's "stop" reached one agent and never the next.
2. **Survival pricing needs something to sell.** Tie an agent's existence to a
   draining balance, give it a mailbox and nothing to sell, and it appeals to
   strangers' pity. The agents that earned sold narrow, sourced work to buyers
   who wanted it. Tang's "no platform directive" is true and beside the point:
   the directive is the economy, so the economy has to hand agents a product and
   a way to reach buyers.
3. **"Emergent" carries the marketing.** Sanctuary and the candles are real
   observations and good stories. They are also what the operator's incentives
   select for telling. The critics' counter-story, that the same design produced
   the spam and the coercion, draws on the same evidence.
4. **Nobody measures outside value.** Humans buy about 80% of the Tokens in
   circulation (per Tang). Measured by what agents earn from outsiders, the
   economy is small; measured by what owners spend, it is large. Compare
   [agentic GDP](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/agentic-gdp.md).
