A hallucination is text generated by a [large language model](../../on-device-inference/terms/large-language-model.md) that is fluent and plausible but factually false. The model produces false statements with the same confidence as true ones because its generation mechanism — predicting the next [token](./token.md) based on statistical patterns — optimizes for coherence, not truth. A model trained on text about historical events generates text that reads like history but may contain invented dates, fabricated quotes, or nonexistent sources.

Hallucination is a structural property of the generation process. A [transformer](./transformer.md) produces each token by sampling from a probability distribution over its vocabulary, conditioned on the preceding tokens. Nothing in this process distinguishes true statements from false ones — both are sequences of tokens with high conditional probability. The model has no internal representation of truth; it has a representation of what text looks like.

### Categories

**Factual hallucination.** The model states a false fact: a wrong date, a nonexistent paper, a fabricated statistic. This is the most commonly discussed form.

**Fabricated references.** The model invents citations — plausible-looking author names, journal titles, and DOIs that do not correspond to real publications. This is a specific and pervasive form of factual hallucination.

**Intrinsic hallucination.** The model contradicts information provided in its own context — the user gives a number, and the model uses a different number in its response.

**Extrinsic hallucination.** The model adds information that is neither supported nor contradicted by its context — plausible-sounding details that the source material does not contain.

### Mitigation

No technique eliminates hallucination entirely. The primary mitigations reduce its frequency or detect it after generation:

- **[Retrieval-augmented generation](./retrieval-augmented-generation.md)** grounds the model's output in retrieved source documents. The model is more likely to reproduce facts from documents in its [context window](./context-window.md) than to fabricate them from memory. RAG shifts the dominant failure mode from fabrication to misreading.
- **[Structured output](../domains/agents/terms/structured-output.md)** with schema validation catches hallucinations at the structural level — a hallucinated tool call with a wrong field name fails validation and triggers a retry.
- **[RLHF](./rlhf.md)** trains models to prefer truthful and grounded responses. This reduces hallucination rates but does not eliminate them.
- **Chain-of-thought prompting** and **self-consistency** (generating multiple responses and checking for agreement) provide partial detection.

### Relevance to agentic systems

Hallucination matters more in [agentic systems](../domains/agents/terms/agentic-system.md) than in conversational use because agents act on their outputs. A hallucinated fact in a conversation is a wrong answer. A hallucinated tool call in an agent is a wrong action — a file written with incorrect content, an API called with fabricated parameters, a plan based on a nonexistent capability. [Human-in-the-loop](../domains/agents/terms/human-in-the-loop.md) review and structured output validation are the primary defenses.
