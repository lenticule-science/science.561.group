## What this note answers

What is an individual's web presence, as an economic thing: what kind of good it is, what it produces, who pays for it, who captures what it produces, and how its value changes over time.

The corpus already studies the Web from many sides but never answers this question for a person.
The existing [web presence](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/web-presence.md) term defines the firm's case: a business's use of the Web as a medium, sorted by business type.
The platform-capital essays ask what Substack, Discord, Reddit, and the Fediverse own.
The house currency theory says how value is counted at borders.
The founder's own essays and logs describe living with a web presence for twenty years.
This note puts those together, adds the external empirical literature, and states the result as a theory with falsifiable propositions (§10).

## 1. Definition

**An individual's web presence is the publicly addressable, persistent record through which a stranger can find a person, read evidence about them, and reach them.**
It is held partly on hosts the person controls (a domain, a site, a self-run server) and partly on hosts the person rents (platform profiles, marketplace listings, directory entries), and it includes what other people publish about the person as well as what the person publishes.

Economically it is a **capital stock, not a flow**.
It is built up by past work, it keeps producing services after the work stops, it depreciates, and it needs maintenance.
Its services are delivered to *other people*, strangers who are deciding whether to hire, buy from, lend to, follow, cite, or meet the person, and the person is paid, when paid at all, by the decisions those strangers make.

The founder's [February 2026 log](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/logs/personal-logs/2026/02/14/2026-02-14-1732-emsenn-personal-log.md) gives the working definition from the inside:
"I find myself generally too shy, at least for now, to bring myself directly to conversation, and so kind of rely on my Web presence to do some of that work."
A web presence is a **delegate**: it represents the person where they are absent, answers questions they are not there to answer, and acts on their behalf at hours and places they never see.
The 2020 letter [On Decolonizing My Web Use](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/on-decolonizing-my-web-use.md) states the other half: the writing "exists, off-line, here, as a file that I can use and reuse," and "the emailed newsletter is just one presentation of the writing."
The presence is a set of presentations of a record, not the record itself.

## 2. What the good produces

The corpus's [intermediation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/intermediation.md) term names the three costs an intermediary is paid to remove when two parties could in principle deal directly: **search cost**, **information asymmetry**, and **trust and enforcement cost**.
A web presence is an individual doing those three jobs for themself, for every counterparty at once, in advance.

1. **Search.** Stigler's founding point is that finding a counterparty is itself a costly activity [@stigler1961]. A findable name, a domain that resolves, a profile that appears for the query a buyer or employer actually types, lowers that cost. The [local SEO](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/local-seo.md) term shows the pure case: a plumber's presence exists to be found by a nearby searcher at the moment of need.
2. **Screening.** A stranger cannot see the person's competence directly. Spence's model shows that a signal carries information when it is costly to fake [@spence1973]. Hiring managers treat long-running public work traces (sustained open-source contribution on GitHub) as more reliable than résumé claims, and trust them more the longer and more networked they are [@marlowdabbish2013]. Berkelaar and Buzzanell find that employers frame looking candidates up ("cybervetting") as due diligence, and that job seekers therefore have to cultivate "digital career capital" [@berkelaar2015].
3. **Trust.** Where a counterparty fears non-performance, a record of past performance substitutes for a guarantee. This is where the largest measured effects are (§6).
4. **Routing attention.** Beyond answering strangers who arrive, a presence moves attention toward the person through links, feeds, follows, and recommendations. LinkedIn's randomized experiments on more than 20 million members found that network ties measurably carry jobs, with moderately weak ties carrying the most [@rajkumar2022]. The [attention economy](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/services/participation-economy-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/attention-economy.md) term records Simon's founding claim that information abundance makes attention the scarce resource.
5. **Holding options.** Most of a presence's value is contingent: the one inquiry, commission, or collaborator that arrives years after the page was written. The corpus's [attention economics reference](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/services/emsenn-economic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/references/attention-economics-and-the-value-of-information.md) records Dixit and Pindyck's result that under irreversibility and uncertainty, keeping an option alive has value of its own. A maintained presence is a portfolio of such options on being found; its carrying cost is the maintenance, and its payoff is lumpy and mostly unobserved.

## 3. Two ledgers: what the person writes and what others record

The house currency theory separates two acts that ordinary talk about "web presence" runs together.
A [wit](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wit.md) is one witnessed distinction-step, paid for by the party that makes the change.
An acknowledgement (ACK), per the [note on acknowledgement beside wit](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/04/2026-09-04-note-on-acknowledgement-beside-wit.md), is another party's uptake, and it "is minted only at a border": "self-acknowledgement moves nothing."
Attention, on that note's reading, is a wit taken of an acknowledgement: someone looking, and seeing that others have taken something up.

Applied to a person on the Web:

- **The person's ledger (WIT).** Posts, essays, portfolio pieces, code, the profile text, the site itself. The person pays for every entry in time and skill. Each entry is an assertion.
- **Everyone else's ledger (ACK).** Links to the person's pages, citations, reviews and ratings, follows and subscriptions, replies, reposts, endorsements, testimonials, hires recorded on a marketplace, a Webmention from someone else's site. None of these can be issued by the person. Each is another party's uptake, recorded somewhere.
- **The stranger's look (ATT).** When an employer or buyer looks the person up, what they read is mostly the ACK ledger. They are taking a wit of the acknowledgements.

**The economic value of a presence lies mostly in the ACK ledger.**
A page the person wrote about themself is an unacknowledged assertion, cheap to produce and cheap to fake; the corpus's work on [content farms](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/20/research-memo-political-economy-of-content-farms-and-content-mills.md) and the [attention-arbitrage media system](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/attention-arbitrage.md) shows how far the cost of producing assertions has already fallen, and generative models lower it again.
As the marginal cost of assertions goes to zero, their signaling value goes with it (a signal that costs nothing separates nothing), and value moves to what the person cannot produce alone: other parties' recorded uptake, and verifiable provenance.
The measured effects in §6 are nearly all effects of ACK records (ratings, evaluations, work histories), not of self-description.

A reputation, on this reading, is a record of matched crossings: the person released something (a job done, a piece published, a product shipped) and a counterparty admitted it (a review, a rating, a citation, a rehire).
That is the shape of a [tally](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tally.md), and the house instrument that lends against exactly such a record is [tally credit](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/monetary-economics-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tally-credit.md): credit whose limit is set by "demonstrated circulation, not holdings."
Every marketplace that ranks sellers by feedback history, and every employer who hires on a work record, is extending tally credit informally.
Pallais's field experiment on oDesk shows the public-good side of this: giving randomly chosen new workers a first job and a public evaluation substantially improved their later employment, and the market-wide value of the information exceeded the experiment's cost, which means such records are underproduced when each employer bears the cost of creating them [@pallais2014].

## 4. Custody: who holds the acknowledgements

The usual ownership question, "who owns the content," is about the WIT ledger, and the answer is usually "the person."
The question that decides the economics is **who holds the ACK ledger**.

The corpus's four platform-capital studies answer it platform by platform:

- **Substack.** The [capital-of-Substack essay](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/what-is-the-capital-of-substack.md) separates **list capital** (email addresses, exportable) from **graph capital** (recommendation position, app followers, Notes interactions, saved-card convenience, platform status), and finds that "the subscriber list is portable; the audience relationship is not fully portable." Its summary: Substack "rents independence back to writers in exchange for 10% of monetized audience capital and increasing dependence on its network graph."
- **Discord.** [Dependency capital](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/what-is-the-capital-of-discord-platform-rent-affective-infrastructure-and-privately-owned-social-reproduction.md): roles, reputations, archives, and the social graph stay inside, and knowledge that would once have been an indexable page becomes "weakly public, weakly exportable."
- **Reddit.** [Corpus capital and karma](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/what-is-the-capital-of-reddit-platform-rent-volunteer-governance-and-the-extracted-commons.md): the users' public words became a licensed asset (data-licensing contracts with an aggregate value of $203.0 million disclosed at the 2024 IPO) with no share and no withdrawal for the people who wrote them, and karma is a reputation that does not leave the site.
- **The Fediverse.** [Federated trust](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/what-is-the-capital-of-the-fediverse.md): capital is "the capacity to make a relation durable across servers." The [identity-migration memo](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/20/research-memo-fediverse-identity-migration-social-trust-and-reputation-capital.md) finds that followers can be redirected but posts, reply history, local recognition, and instance-borrowed trust do not move, so migration "is better described as audience salvage than identity portability."

The general rule these share: **on a platform, both halves of every acknowledgement sit in one party's database, and that party is neither the person nor the acknowledger.**
The like, the follow, the rating, and the recommendation edge are rows the platform keeps.
That custody is the platform's rent position: it charges for access to the ACK ledger (subscription cuts, boosts, promoted placement), sells it (data licensing), ranks with it (feeds), and can revoke it (suspension, demonetization, deplatforming).
The external evidence agrees:

- Few platforms let a worker or seller carry ratings to another platform. In an online experiment, importing ratings from another platform raised or lowered prospective buyers' trust depending on the rating values, so a portable reputation is a real asset whose absence keeps sellers where their history is [@teubner2022].
- YouTube applies different rules and protections to different tiers of creators, and a creator's monetization of their own audience depends on policy the platform can revise [@caplangillespie2020].
- Creators removed from YouTube recovered only about 65% of their lost revenue and about 6% of their lost viewership on an alternative platform [@rauchfleisch2024].
- Doctorow's "enshittification" names the lifecycle in which a platform first serves users, then shifts value to business customers, then takes it back for itself [@doctorow2023]; for a person whose acknowledgements live there, each stage re-prices their stock without their consent.

The corpus's Reddit research names what custody means politically: [delegated sovereignty](../../../../../../../../../web-science-services-section/services/web-platform-science-services-section/services/web2-platform-science-services-section/services/web2-social-platform-science-services-section/services/reddit-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/delegated-sovereignty.md), real day-to-day control resting on a reserved override, which once exercised leaves a [revocable commons](../../../../../../../../../web-science-services-section/services/web-platform-science-services-section/services/web2-platform-science-services-section/services/web2-social-platform-science-services-section/services/reddit-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/revocable-commons.md).
A person's platform-held presence is a revocable commons of one.

**On the open Web the halves separate.**
A link to the person's page sits on the linker's page; a citation sits in the citer's paper; a Webmention is sent from the mentioner's site and received on the person's; a `rel=me` verification is a pair of links, one on each side.
Each acknowledgement is kept by the party who gave it, on a host that party controls, and can be checked by anyone against the thing acknowledged.
That is the tally's design: two halves, held by two parties, matched rather than asserted.
The founder's 2019 log records taking the first step of it, [adding `rel="me"` to emsenn.net for IndieWeb level 1](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/logs/personal-logs/2019/10/21/2019-10-21-emsenn-personal-log-about-adding-my-website-to-the-indieweb-level-1.md).

This gives the economic reason behind the IndieWeb's POSSE rule ("publish on your own site, syndicate elsewhere") [@indiewebposse] and behind the corpus's [Substack-as-distribution-layer memo](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/substack-as-distribution-layer-not-home.md) ("Canonical website for truth claims. Substack for relationship and reach."): the canonical WIT belongs where the person controls it, and acknowledgements are worth most when they are collected in two-sided form that no single host can revoke.
Portability law and protocol design are moving the same way (DMA Article 6(9) end-user portability [@dma2022]; AT Protocol's separation of a persistent identity from its host [@atproto]), but in 2026 practice still lags both.

## 5. Coherence across hosts

A person's presence is spread over many hosts, each showing a local version: a site bio, a LinkedIn headline, a marketplace profile, a directory listing, a Fediverse profile.
The corpus's [sovereign-cloud research](../../07/07/2026-07-07-research-on-sovereignty-in-web-cloud.md) models distributed infrastructure as a sheaf: local data on each site, restriction maps saying what must agree across each interface, and global sections existing only where the local data glue.
The same structure fits a presence.
The hosts are the cover; each profile is a local section; `rel=me` pairs, matching names, and consistent claims are the gluing data; a **coherent identity is a global section**, and contradictions between profiles (an old job title here, a different name there, claims on one host the others do not support) are obstructions a stranger has to resolve at their own cost.

Strangers price that cost.
The local-SEO term records it in its plainest form: search engines rank local businesses partly on the consistency of name, address, and phone number across directories, which is a gluing condition checked by machine.
Fediverse verification works the same way: a profile is verified when the website it links to links back to it with `rel=me` (the identity-migration memo, §4).

The sheaf reading also names the platform's move.
The sovereign-cloud note describes hegemony as "trivialization": making every local stalk use the provider's coordinate system.
For a person that is the platform profile becoming the identity, so that being findable means being findable there.
The alternative the [Living Web program](../../../../../../../../../../../living-web-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026-08-26-living-web-frontier-and-engineering-program.md) states for publishing, "one catalog, many projections," is the founder's 2020 practice generalized: one canonical record, rendered to every host, so the local versions agree because they come from the same source.
The founder's 2025 outline [Accepting the Web as a Platform, Not a Place](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/accepting-the-web-as-a-platform-not-a-place.md) draws the same line in other terms: the personal website as "living on a land," with "permanence, backlinks, 'sediment', foundation, structure"; platform presence as "subject in environment," with "fungibility, presence."
The two need different governance, and a person needs both.

## 6. What a stranger reads: four readings of a public record

A stranger looking someone up finds evidence for the person, evidence against, both, or neither.
Those are the four readings of one evidence pair: in the Belnap truth order *neither, true, false, both*; in the house knowledge order *told neither, told for, told against, told both*.
Economically the four readings behave very differently, and the external evidence measures each.

- **Told neither (the blank page).** Absence of evidence is not neutral in practice. In CareerBuilder's 2017 survey of 2,380 hiring managers, 57% said they were less likely to interview a candidate they could not find online [@careerbuilder2017] (an industry survey, not a peer-reviewed study). Readers collapse *neither* into *against*. The Spiegel Research Center found that a product with five reviews is 270% more likely to be purchased than one with none [@spiegel2017]: the step from nothing to a little acknowledgement is the largest step there is. The house logic forbids that collapse (a cell told neither is not a cell told against), but the market makes it anyway, so for most people the first economic job of a presence is to move out of *neither*.
- **Told for.** Uptake raises outcomes, and it raises them most for people who have no other trust signal. A one-star increase in Yelp rating raises an independent restaurant's revenue by 5 to 9%, with no effect for chain restaurants, whose brand already carries the trust [@luca2016]. Standardized, verified work histories on an online labor market raise hiring most for applicants from less developed countries, whom employers otherwise discount [@agrawal2016]. Workers who joined an oDesk agency found first jobs more easily than otherwise similar workers, and the advantage disappeared once the unaffiliated workers built up their own feedback [@stantonthomas2016]. A web presence is worth most to the person with the least inherited reputation.
- **Told only for, too strongly.** Purchase likelihood peaks at average ratings of 4.0 to 4.7 and falls as ratings approach 5.0, with no product category where 5.0 is optimal [@spiegel2017]. A record with no evidence against reads as curated or faked.
- **Told both.** The most credible state is a record carrying both sides: praise and critique, successes and corrections. The house treats *told both* as the most informed reading, not as a failure, and buyers behave the same way. This is also why the corpus's [accountable attention](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/public-media-services-section/services/public-news-media-services-section/services/koios-news-services-section/dev/content/notes/2026/06/01/accountable-attention.md) memo makes public corrections part of a publisher's asset rather than a cost.
- **Told against.** Evidence against the person is told on the same record, not on a different identity; negation swaps sides of one cell. A person cannot delete the other party's half, which is exactly why the ACK record is informative.

The corpus's [professional identity note](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/services/emsenn-professionalization-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-professional-identity-evidence-classes-and-the-watch-list-strategy.md) turns this into a rule for the person's own ledger: "claim only identities whose evidence is available on the day of the claim," because a claim that a stranger checks and finds empty moves the record toward *against* and "does not recover," and because on a blank page "early names stick."
It also names the second function of the self-published ledger once acknowledgements exist: the dossier "whose sole job is to survive inspection when a right person, having encountered an artifact, looks the author up."
Self-description does not create value; it organizes the evidence others have produced so a stranger can read it cheaply.

## 7. Who captures the value

The value a presence creates is the counterparty costs it removes.
The share the person keeps is what they can price into their own labor, goods, or credit.
Four parties take shares.

**The person pays the production cost.**
Building and keeping a presence is work: Duffy's "aspirational labor," self-branding done on the promise of later pay that mostly does not come [@duffy2017]; Baym's "relational labor," the ongoing work of keeping an audience relationship alive [@baym2018].
Gandini shows freelancers treating self-branding as an investment in social capital expected to return work [@gandini2016], which is Bourdieu's convertibility of social into economic capital [@bourdieu1986] and the practical content of Peters's 1997 "brand called You" [@peters1997].
The corpus's [microcelebrity](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/media-science-services-section/services/social-media-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/microcelebrity.md) and [influencer](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/media-science-services-section/services/social-media-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/influencer.md) terms carry this literature in full.

**Hosts take rent by holding acknowledgements (§4).**
The take is sometimes explicit: Substack keeps 10% of paid subscription revenue before payment fees, and Patreon's plans take 5% to about 12% before its processing fees [@patreonfees].
More often it is implicit, in ranking, recommendation, and revocation.
Rochet and Tirole's result that a platform prices both sides jointly [@rochettirole2003] means the person, as one side, is charged according to the value they bring the other side, not according to their costs.

**Search and answer engines take the reach.**
In 2024, 58.5% of US Google searches ended without a click, and of every 1,000 US searches only 360 clicks went to the open Web [@sparktoro2024].
Where Google showed an AI summary, users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits, against 15% where no summary appeared, and clicked a link inside the summary on 1% [@pew2025] (an association, per Pew, not a measured causal effect).
Cloudflare measured crawl-to-referral ratios for June 19–26, 2025 ranging from 70,900 pages crawled per referral for Anthropic's crawler to 0.1 for Mistral's [@cloudflare2025].
In house terms, an answer engine that reads a page and answers from it without sending the reader takes the person's wit and returns no acknowledgement: a released half with no admitted half, which is a claim and not a settlement.
The corpus's [Reddit essay](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/what-is-the-capital-of-reddit-platform-rent-volunteer-governance-and-the-extracted-commons.md) shows the same break at platform scale: Reddit licensed its corpus to the answer engines that then reduce visits to Reddit.
Pay-per-crawl schemes and machine-payable APIs (the corpus's [x402 note](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-agent-to-agent-commerce-and-machine-payable-apis.md)) are attempts to make that crossing settle.

**Superstars take most of the attention-monetized remainder.**
Rosen's model predicts that when one performer can serve any number of people at almost no extra cost, income concentrates at the top [@rosen1981].
The data agree: on YouTube about 85% of views go to the top 3% of channels [@bartl2018]; in Linktree's 2022 survey, 12% of full-time creators earned over $50,000 a year and 46% earned under $1,000 [@linktree2022]; Goldman Sachs estimates about 4% of roughly 50 million creators earn over $100,000 a year [@goldman2023].
Elberse's transaction data show hits gaining share over time, not the flattening the long-tail thesis predicted [@elberse2008].
Kelly's "1,000 true fans" [@kelly2008] is a target, not a finding.
The founder's own site is a clean small-scale data point: in February 2020, emsenn.net stated a need of about $600 a month and reported donation income of about $41.20 a month from LiberaPay and Patreon, 6.8% of the need ([my website](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../history-and-archaeology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/my-website.md)).

The consequence for most individuals: **a presence mostly pays through conversion, not through monetizing attention directly.**
It converts into hires and contracts (by lowering screening cost), into sales (by lowering trust cost, the Yelp effect), into credit and collaboration (tally credit), and into the occasional opportunity that arrives by search.
Direct audience payment (subscriptions, tips, ads) is the superstar-distributed channel, and for most people it is the smallest.
The existing web-presence term makes the parallel point for firms: local businesses spend more on being findable than display advertising pays out to publishers.

## 8. Time

**Depreciation.**
Presence decays without maintenance.
Of pages that existed in 2013, 38% were no longer accessible in 2023, and 54% of Wikipedia pages had at least one dead reference link [@pew2024]; more than 70% of URLs cited in Harvard Law Review and about half in US Supreme Court opinions no longer pointed to the cited material [@zittrain2014].
Acknowledgements decay with the pages that hold them, so a person's ACK ledger shrinks when other people's sites die, not only their own.
The [email list](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/email-list.md) term records the same for owned audiences: lists lose roughly 20 to 30% a year if not maintained.

**Compounding.**
Some services compound: a page that ranks keeps drawing visitors without new spending ([organic traffic](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/organic-traffic.md)), and each acknowledgement makes the next stranger's look cheaper.
Zero-click search and answer engines reduce this compounding for open pages (§7) and so shift relative value toward acknowledgements that deliver directly: subscriptions held by the person, follows held two-sided, and citations.

**Cadence.**
Feeds reward frequency, so a platform presence has a continuous carrying cost (the relational labor of §7).
The professional identity note separates that "interval cadence" from a **return cadence**, where each artifact is an event and silence between events is normal, and argues that a canonical record with a dossier holds its value without continuous posting.
The same note adds a second audience for which frequency does not matter: what is published is read by future models regardless of human reach, so publishing "compounds at zero followers."
§7 is the qualification: models reading the record is reach for its contents but, without attribution, not acknowledgement for its author.

**Platform lifecycles.**
The value of a platform-held presence moves with the platform's stage (Doctorow's sequence; the Reddit research's extraction-triggered reassertion), on a clock the person does not control.
A presence held on the person's own domain moves only with the person and with the Web's link structure.

## 9. The costs of being seen

A presence is also exposure, and its costs fall unevenly.
In a field experiment, candidates whose social-media profiles marked them as Muslim received 13% fewer callbacks than otherwise identical Christian candidates [@acquistifong2020]; the same findability that removes screening cost for some people supplies grounds for discrimination against others.
The founder's essay [Fall Arrives, 2023](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/fall-arrives-2023.md) records a hostile online audience "obviously surveilling my online presence, so I didn't really have much choice but to get quieter."
The identity-migration memo records how marginalized users manage the risk: pseudonyms, separated accounts, locked profiles, avoiding personal domains.
In economic terms these are **contextual disclosure**: choosing which counterparties can connect which parts of the record.
The corpus's professional identity note makes the same choice through personas and "artifacts over presence," exposure routed through work rather than through the person.

At the same time, absence is increasingly penalized (§6), and participation has its own entry costs.
The founder's 2020 letter applies Illich's "oppressive monopoly" to the Web: once social and economic life is organized around a technology, not having it raises the cost of participating at all, "how do you get a job if you don't have a way to say 'Hey, I'd like to apply!'"
A web presence is therefore partly a **defensive good**: its value to a person includes avoiding the penalty others impose on its absence, a penalty that grows as more people have one.

## 10. The theory in propositions

Summary: **an individual's web presence is a findable record of other parties' acknowledgements; its economic value is the search, screening, and trust costs it removes for strangers; and the share of that value the individual keeps is set by who holds the acknowledgements.**
Three quantities carry it: **reach** (whether the stranger arrives), **reading** (which of the four readings the record gives), and **custody** (who holds the acknowledgement halves).
Each proposition below carries its evidence and what would falsify it, per the Living Web program's rule that a house hypothesis without a falsifier "does not yet belong."

1. **A presence is a capital stock.** It keeps producing after the work stops, depreciates, and needs maintenance. *Evidence:* §2, §8 (link rot, list decay, compounding search traffic). *Falsifier:* outcomes that depend only on current posting rate and not at all on the age, links, or history of the record.
2. **Value lies in acknowledgements, not assertions.** Outcomes move with third-party records (ratings, evaluations, work histories), much less with self-description; as the cost of producing assertions falls, the share of value in acknowledgements rises. *Evidence:* §3, §6 (Luca, Pallais, Agrawal et al., Spiegel). *Falsifier:* a market where self-authored profiles without third-party records move hiring or sales as much as ratings or verified histories do.
3. **Presence substitutes for inherited trust.** Its value is largest for those with the fewest other trust signals. *Evidence:* independents versus chains [@luca2016]; less-developed-country applicants [@agrawal2016]; agency affiliation fading as personal feedback accrues [@stantonthomas2016]. *Falsifier:* equal or larger effects for already-branded sellers and credentialed applicants.
4. **Custody sets capture.** The individual keeps more of the value when acknowledgements are held two-sided on hosts outside any single party's control, and loses value on platform exit in proportion to what the platform held. *Evidence:* §4 (list versus graph capital, audience salvage, deplatforming recovery of about 65% of revenue and 6% of viewership, rating non-portability). *Falsifier:* platform-held followings and ratings transferring on exit at rates comparable to self-held lists and links.
5. **Absence is read as evidence against, and pure praise is discounted.** Readers collapse *neither* toward *against*, and records with some evidence against outperform uniformly perfect ones. *Evidence:* §6 (57% less likely to interview; five reviews versus none; the 4.0–4.7 peak). *Falsifier:* no penalty for an absent record once other signals are controlled; monotone gains up to perfect ratings.
6. **Incoherence across hosts is priced.** Contradictions between a person's profiles lower outcomes, and consistency checks (NAP, `rel=me`) are used as trust inputs. *Evidence:* §5. *Falsifier:* no measurable effect of cross-profile inconsistency on ranking, callback, or conversion.
7. **Direct attention monetization is superstar-distributed; conversion is the ordinary channel.** *Evidence:* §7 (Rosen, Bärtl, Linktree, Goldman Sachs, Elberse; the founder's 6.8%). *Falsifier:* a flattening creator income distribution over time, or typical individuals earning more from audience payments than from the hires, sales, and contracts their presence brings.
8. **Answer engines break the reach term for open pages.** Intermediaries increasingly take the published record without returning the reader, which lowers the return to open publishing measured in visits and raises the relative value of acknowledgements that arrive directly. *Evidence:* §7 (zero-click share, the Pew AI-summary gap, crawl-to-referral ratios). *Falsifier:* referral traffic to open pages recovering to pre-summary levels while answer engines keep expanding, or attribution inside answers restoring click-through.
9. **Findability has an unevenly distributed cost, and absence has a growing one.** *Evidence:* §9 (Acquisti and Fong's 13% callback gap; the absence penalty in §6). *Falsifier:* no disclosure-driven discrimination in field experiments, or no absence penalty.

What the corpus and the literature do not yet settle: there is no peer-reviewed economics of personal domain names as identifiers (only registrar market reports), and no measurement of how much of a person's reachable acknowledgement record survives a platform exit when the person held a canonical site throughout.
The second is measurable from the Group's own sites and profiles.

## 11. Where the theory sits in the Group's systems

The theory reads onto structures the corpus already has; it adds no new ledger.
The acknowledgement record is what the house already counts as evidence for and against on a cell, and its reading is one of the four readings of §6; a person's presence is read the way any other subject is read, from the evidence pairs other parties' records supply.
On the publishing side, the Living Web program's "one catalog, many projections" and the VICTOR static publisher are the canonical-record-with-projections design of §5, and the IndieWeb protocols it tracks (Webmention, Micropub, WebSub, `rel=me`) are the two-sided acknowledgement carriers of §4.
