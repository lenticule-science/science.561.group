Censorship-resistant storage gathers the structural primitive that makes independent redundancy possible ([content addressing](../terms/content-addressing.md), located by a [distributed hash table](../terms/distributed-hash-table.md)), the two redundancy mechanisms built on it ([replication](../terms/replication-factor.md) and [erasure coding](../terms/erasure-coding.md)), the reliability properties redundancy is engineered to raise ([data availability](../terms/data-availability.md) and [data durability](../terms/data-durability.md), which are not the same property and are not raised by the same mechanism), the economic and legal mechanisms that keep redundant copies alive over years rather than weeks ([pinning](../terms/pinning.md), [incentivized storage](../terms/incentivized-storage.md), [web archiving](../terms/web-archiving.md), [BitTorrent distribution](../terms/bittorrent-distribution.md), [git mirroring](../terms/git-mirroring.md)), and the attacks that bound what a resistance claim can honestly mean ([Sybil](../terms/sybil-attack.md) and [eclipse](../terms/eclipse-attack.md) attacks), all read against [censorship resistance](../terms/censorship-resistance.md) as a property defined relative to one named removal mechanism rather than resistance in the abstract.

It sits at this level, under distributed-systems science rather than folded into the blockchain section, because the mechanisms it studies are broader than any one chain: BitTorrent swarms, WARC-based web archives, and plain git mirrors are all censorship-resistant-storage mechanisms with no blockchain involved, and the two mechanisms in this area's material that do run on a blockchain — Filecoin's proof-of-replication market and Arweave's endowment-funded permanent storage — are read here as storage guarantees with a stated cost and durability tradeoff, not as instances of chain consensus, which is the blockchain section's own concern.
