# Glossary {.unnumbered}

The chapter in brackets is where each term is taught.

**Accessibility.** People with disabilities can perceive, understand,
navigate and interact with a product. [1, 6]

**Affinity diagram.** Observations grouped by similarity to reveal themes.
[5]

**Affordance; signifier.** What an object makes possible; what shows where
and how to act. [1]

**Assistive technology.** Tools such as screen readers, magnifiers, voice
control and switches. [1]

**Atomic design.** Building interfaces from atoms, molecules, organisms,
templates and pages. [6]

**Breakpoint.** A width at which a responsive layout changes. [7]

**Competitive audit.** A structured review of competitors' products. [3]

**Crazy Eights.** Sketching eight ideas in eight minutes. [3]

**Deceptive patterns.** Interface designs that trick people; once called dark
patterns. [4]

**Design critique.** A structured review of work in progress against its
goals. [6]

**Design fixation.** Reproducing the first idea or example seen, flaws
included. [1, 3]

**Design sprint.** A five-day process: map, sketch, decide, prototype,
test. [1]

**Design system; design tokens.** Shared components and standards; named
values for color, type and spacing. [6]

**Design thinking.** Empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test. [1]

**Empathy map.** What a user says, thinks, does and feels. [2]

**Equity-focused design.** Design that works with the people most affected.
[1, 8]

**Fidelity.** How closely a prototype resembles the finished product. [4]

**Fitts's law; Hick's law.** Pointing time grows with distance and falls
with target size; choice time grows with the number of options. [2]

**Gestalt principles.** Proximity, similarity, common region, continuity,
closure, figure-ground. [4]

**Graceful degradation; progressive enhancement.** Keeping a full design
working when features are missing; building up from a core that works
everywhere. [8]

**Handoff.** Giving engineers what they need to build a design. [6]

**Heuristic evaluation.** Judging a design against usability heuristics.
[3]

**How might we.** A question that reframes a problem as an open
opportunity. [3]

**Information architecture.** How content is organized, labeled and
connected. [4, 7]

**Insight.** What a research theme means for the design, and why. [5]

**Journey map.** A persona's actions, emotions, pain points and
opportunities, stage by stage. [2]

**Mobile first.** Designing the small screen first. [7]

**Mockup.** A static, high-fidelity image of a screen. [6]

**Pain point.** A problem that frustrates or stops a user. [2]

**Persona.** A research-based profile of a type of user. [2]

**Problem statement.** A user, their need, and the insight behind it. [2]

**Prototype.** An early model people can interact with. [4, 6]

**Responsive web design.** Fluid grids, flexible images and media queries.
[7]

**Sitemap.** A diagram of a product's pages and how they connect. [4, 7]

**Storyboard.** Panels showing a user's experience with a solution. [4]

**System Usability Scale (SUS).** A ten-item usability questionnaire scored
0 to 100. [5]

**Usability.** Effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction for specified
users, goals and context. [1]

**Usability study.** Watching people try realistic tasks with a design. [5]

**User-centered design.** Design based on users' needs, with users involved
throughout. [1]

**User story.** "As a user, I want an action so that a benefit." [2]

**Wireframe.** A basic outline of a screen's layout. [4]
