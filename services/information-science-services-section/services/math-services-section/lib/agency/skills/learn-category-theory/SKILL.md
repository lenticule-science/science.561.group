# Learn Category Theory

The basic structural language: objects connected by morphisms with identity and composition (a category), structure-preserving maps between categories (functors), and structure-preserving maps between functors (natural transformations).

## What you will be able to do

- Given a structure, decide whether it forms a category and identify objects, morphisms, identities, and composition.
- Define a functor between two categories explicitly: object-mapping, morphism-mapping, verification of identity and composition preservation.
- Recognize a natural transformation as a family of morphisms with a coherence square, and check the coherence.
- Read formal categorical statements (universal properties, limits, adjunctions at an introductory level) without getting lost in the notation.

## Reference

- `library/domains/humanities/domains/math/domains/categories/` — the categories domain in the library.

## Prerequisites

None strict. Comfort with manipulating formal definitions and following symbolic reasoning is the only real requirement.

## Verification

Take a structure you know (sets and functions, groups and homomorphisms, posets and order-preserving maps). State explicitly: what are the objects, what are the morphisms, what is identity, what is composition, and verify that composition is associative and identity is a unit. Then exhibit a functor from this category to another and write out one natural transformation between two functors of your choice.
