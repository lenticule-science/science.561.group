# Learn Topology

A topology on a set is the abstract data of which subsets are "open" — closed under finite intersection and arbitrary union, with the empty set and the whole space included. From this minimal data, continuity, compactness, connectedness, and the rest of point-set topology follow.

## What you will be able to do

- Given a set and a candidate family of subsets, decide whether it forms a topology and verify each axiom.
- State continuity in terms of preimages of open sets, and verify continuity for a concrete map.
- Recognize compactness (every open cover has a finite subcover) and connectedness, and identify them in standard examples.
- Distinguish the topological structure (which sets are open) from the metric/uniform structure that may have generated it.

## Verification

State the topology on $\mathbb{R}$ generated by open intervals, and verify the topology axioms. Define a map $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and verify or refute its continuity using the preimage definition. Construct a topological space that is *not* metrizable (the Sierpinski space, the cofinite topology on an infinite set) and explain why.
