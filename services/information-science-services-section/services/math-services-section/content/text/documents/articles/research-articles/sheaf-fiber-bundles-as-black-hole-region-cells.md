# Sheaf or Fiber-Bundle Data over Black-Hole-Region Cells

## Research question

Does the literature define a sheaf, cellular sheaf, fiber bundle, Hilbert bundle, net, or cohomological assignment whose **base is cellularly decomposed into hole-regions or black-hole-regions**, with a stalk, fiber, Hilbert space, algebra, or cohomological object attached over each such cell?

The target is **not** an ordinary field, sheaf, or algebra over spacetime points or open spacetime regions. It is also not merely a causal-diamond Hilbert-space assignment. The target construction would be one level up: the cells or base-points of the indexing structure would themselves be hole-regions / black-hole-regions / causal cuts.

## Inclusion criteria

A positive hit would need all of the following:

1. A base, poset, cell complex, stack, moduli space, or indexing category whose cells/objects are black-hole regions, hole-regions, trapped regions, event-horizon-bounded regions, or causal-past complements such as \(M \setminus J^-(R)\).
2. A sheaf/fiber-bundle-like assignment of data to those cells: stalks, fibers, Hilbert spaces, algebras, cohomology groups, state spaces, or operator structures.
3. Incidence, restriction, transition, embedding, or compatibility maps between those hole-region cells.
4. The construction must not merely be a sheaf on an ordinary spacetime manifold, a field on the black-hole exterior, or a net of algebras over generic open sets.

## Result

I found **no real, checkable citation** that satisfies the target construction.

The nearest literatures provide formal pieces of the desired machinery, but they do not use **hole-regions / black-hole-regions as the cells of the base**. The requested object appears to be unexplored, or at least not indexed under the tested terminology.

## Near matches

### 1. Cellular sheaf theory supplies the right formal template, but not black-hole-region cells

Hansen and Ghrist define cellular sheaves over regular cell complexes and describe the assignment of vector spaces to cells, with compatibility maps along incidences. This is the formal pattern that would support the proposed construction. In particular, the theory treats a cell complex as a base over which data such as vector spaces and maps are assigned.

However, the cells in this literature are ordinary combinatorial/topological cells of a regular cell complex. They are not black-hole regions, hole-regions, trapped regions, or causal-past complements.

**Assessment:** Formal technology match; physical/geometric base mismatch.

### 2. Holographic-space-time Hilbert bundles assign Hilbert data to causal-diamond structures, but the base is not a hole-region cell complex

Banks reformulates holographic-space-time models as Hilbert bundles over the space of timelike geodesics on a background manifold. The framework uses nested causal diamonds and Hilbert-space data associated with causal-diamond screens.

This is one of the closest structural near-matches because it combines Hilbert bundles, causal diamonds, and holographic screens. But the base of the bundle is the space of timelike geodesics, not a cellular decomposition whose cells are black-hole regions. The causal diamonds appear as nested subsystems along geodesics, not as the base cells themselves.

**Assessment:** Hilbert-bundle and causal-region match; base-object mismatch.

### 3. Algebraic QFT assigns algebras to regions, but not to hole-region cells in a cellular base

Witten’s “Algebras, Regions, and Observers” discusses the ordinary QFT practice of associating algebras of observables to spacetime regions, while emphasizing that in gravity this notion becomes problematic and may be replaced by observer-worldline algebras.

This is relevant because it is explicitly about region-indexed algebras, including gravitational settings. But it is not a sheaf or cellular sheaf whose base cells are black-hole regions. It remains a region-algebra discussion over spacetime regions/worldlines, not a hole-region-cellular base.

**Assessment:** Region-indexed algebra match; not a cellular sheaf/bundle over black-hole-region cells.

### 4. Giddings uses causal diamonds and Hilbert-space networks, not hole-region base cells

Giddings discusses local observables associated with causal diamonds in algebraic local QFT and moves toward a quantum-first Hilbert-space-network perspective for black-hole information. This work is relevant to “data associated to causal regions,” especially causal diamonds and Hilbert factors.

But the indexing objects are causal diamonds, tensor factors, local observers, and Hilbert-space networks. The construction does not define a sheaf or bundle over a cell complex whose cells are black-hole regions.

**Assessment:** Causal-region/Hilbert-factor near match; no black-hole-region cellular base.

### 5. Horizon-detection work may involve sheaf-like language or Lie-algebraic localization, but the horizon is the detected object, not a base cell

McNutt and Schneider use relative differential invariants associated with Lie algebras of vector fields to detect horizons of symmetric black holes. The work is close to the horizon/black-hole vocabulary, and it involves algebraic/differential-geometric structures on manifolds.

However, the black-hole horizon is a geometric locus to be detected. It is not a cell in the base of a cellular sheaf, and there is no stalk/fiber assignment over hole-region cells.

**Assessment:** Horizon match; sheaf/fiber-cell construction absent.

### 6. ADG and finitary sheaf approaches to Schwarzschild singularities are sheaf-theoretic but not hole-region-cellular

Raptis’s finitary-algebraic treatment of the inner Schwarzschild singularity uses Abstract Differential Geometry, finitary posets, incidence algebras, and finitary spacetime sheaves. This is a serious sheaf-theoretic approach to a black-hole/singularity-adjacent problem.

But the base spaces are finitary substitutes, posets, or arbitrary topological localization scaffolds. The construction is not a cellular decomposition whose cells are black-hole regions or hole-regions, and the stalks are not assigned over black-hole-region cells.

**Assessment:** Sheaf-theoretic black-hole/singularity near match; base-cell mismatch.

### 7. Black-hole entropy work using sheaf cohomology lives on moduli/resolution spaces, not on black-hole-region cells

Dorey and Zhao relate BPS states to twisted Dolbeault cohomology and equivariant sheaf cohomology on resolved hyper-Kähler spaces, with growth relevant to black-hole entropy. This is a real instance of black-hole-adjacent sheaf cohomology.

But the sheaf cohomology is on an instanton/moduli/resolution space, not on a base whose cells are black-hole regions. The black hole enters through entropy/BPS-state interpretation, not as the cell-type of the sheaf base.

**Assessment:** Black-hole/sheaf-cohomology phrase match; wrong base and wrong role of black holes.

## Synthesis

The searched literature separates into several non-overlapping strands:

| Strand | What it contributes | Why it does not answer the target |
|---|---|---|
| Cellular sheaves | Data/stalks/fibers over cells, restriction maps, cohomology | Cells are generic topological/combinatorial cells, not hole-regions |
| Holographic-space-time Hilbert bundles | Hilbert bundles, causal diamonds, holographic screens | Base is timelike geodesics; diamonds are nested subsystems, not base cells |
| Algebraic QFT / region algebras | Algebras assigned to spacetime regions | Region poset/open-set indexing, not cellular decomposition into hole-regions |
| Hilbert-space networks | Hilbert factors associated with causal diamonds/local observers | Network/factor structure, not a sheaf over black-hole-region cells |
| Horizon detection | Black-hole horizons as geometric/differential-invariant loci | Horizon is object detected, not the cell of a sheaf base |
| ADG/finitary sheaves | Sheaf-theoretic gravitational/singularity machinery | Base is a finitary substitute/localization scaffold, not hole-region cells |
| Black-hole entropy sheaf cohomology | Sheaf cohomology in black-hole-adjacent physics | Sheaves live on moduli/resolution spaces, not on hole-region cell bases |

## Conclusion

The requested construction appears to be **unexplored** in the checked literature.

A plausible new construction would need to explicitly define a base category or cell complex such as:

\[
\mathcal{H} = \{\text{hole-regions / black-hole-regions / causal cuts}\}
\]

with an incidence or specialization relation between regions, and then define a functor/sheaf-like assignment

\[
\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{H}^{op} \to \mathbf{Vect}, \mathbf{Hilb}, \mathbf{Alg}, \mathbf{C^*Alg}, \mathbf{Ch}, \ldots
\]

or a cellular sheaf over the face poset of such a region-complex. I did not find an existing paper that takes this step.

The best phrasing for the negative result is:

> Existing work has sheaves over cell complexes, Hilbert bundles over spaces of timelike geodesics, and algebras/Hilbert factors associated with spacetime regions or causal diamonds. I found no literature that uses black-hole regions or hole-regions themselves as the cells of a base complex carrying stalks, fibers, Hilbert spaces, algebras, or cohomological data.
