A formal context $(G,M,I)$, a Chu space $(A,r,X)$, a bilattice, and a linear-logic model arise from related structure rather than from unrelated metaphors. Pratt showed that formal contexts are Chu spaces over $\{0,1\}$; Chu spaces form a $*$-autonomous category, the categorical setting for linear logic. Jakl (2025) identifies $\mathrm{Chu}(H,0)$ over a Heyting algebra $H$ with the twist construction producing bilattices.

Under this identification, linear-logic tensor ($\otimes$) is bilattice fusion ($\odot$), linear implication ($\multimap$) is the twist-product residual ($\Rightarrow$), and the adjunction $\otimes\dashv\multimap$ is the same residuation that governs bilattice-valued predication. A key-value repository or many-valued formal context can therefore be read simultaneously as data, a Chu space, and a resource-sensitive logical model.

The interpretation is especially useful for conflicting evidence: extending by an affirmation and a denial produces a contest value rather than erasing one assertion. The evidence has participated in a resource-sensitive composition and cannot be treated as if it had never been used.
