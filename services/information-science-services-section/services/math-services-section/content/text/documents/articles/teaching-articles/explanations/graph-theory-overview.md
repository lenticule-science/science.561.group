Graph theory is the mathematics of vertices and edges — the formal language of discrete-object networks. Founded by Euler's 1736 analysis of the [seven bridges of Königsberg](../examples/seven-bridges-of-konigsberg.md), it is the substrate underneath [partial orders](../../../../glossary/terms/partial-order.md), [categories](../../../../glossary/terms/category.md), state machines, and [dependency systems](../../../../glossary/terms/dag.md). This directory carries graph theory as a working library, not a textbook: a disciplined population of typed notes that earns the theory's load-bearing claim by being explicitly cited from the corpus's predication-substrate spec for the typed-DAG `class:` relation.

## Position relative to math

Graph theory sits *under* [order](../order/) and [categories](../categories/) — every poset and every category is, structurally, a graph with extra equations — and *alongside* [topology](../topology/) and [algebra](../algebra/) as a peer math theory. It supplies discrete combinatorial objects the rest of mathematics builds on: a partial order is a transitive acyclic digraph; a small category is a directed graph closed under composition; a state machine is a directed graph with labelled transitions; a [predication fibration](../../../../glossary/terms/predication-fibration.md) reindexes along a directed graph of predicate generators.

## Load-bearing role

Graph theory is cited explicitly by the predicated-markdown-vault-knowledge-system's frontmatter-relations spec, whose typed-DAG `class:` relation rests on the corpus being a [DAG](../../../../glossary/terms/dag.md) under `depends-on:`, and by the [predication-fibration](../../../../glossary/terms/predication-fibration.md) object, whose four-generator reindexing acts along a [directed graph](../../../../glossary/terms/directed-graph.md) of predicate morphisms. The [dependency-soundness-iff-dag](../../research-articles/dependency-soundness-iff-dag.md) theorem makes the load-bearing claim explicit: a `depends-on:` set produces a sound topological reading order iff its underlying graph is a DAG. The [partial-order](../../../../glossary/terms/partial-order.md) object now carries an explicit `depends-on: [directed-graph]` edge, replacing the prior silent coupling.

## Deferred sub-areas

The following sub-areas of graph theory are *named-as-deferred*: they are recognized as part of the textbook external theory but are not written here, and SHOULD become child theories under `graph-theory/theories/` when the corpus needs them.

- **Algorithmic graph theory** — BFS, DFS, shortest paths, max-flow, matching algorithms. Belongs under `engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/` or a dedicated `algorithms` theory; cited via `see-also:` here, not authored.
- **Spectral graph theory** — eigenvalues of adjacency and Laplacian matrices, expander graphs.
- **Random graphs** — Erdős–Rényi, configuration models, threshold phenomena.
- **Extremal graph theory** — Turán-type results, Ramsey theory.
- **Hypergraphs** — graphs with edges of arbitrary arity. Distinct enough from ordinary graphs to merit a separate theory.
- **Lean formalisation** — graph theory has live mathlib coverage; five proved theorems in this directory now carry initial `lean:` frontmatter bindings against mathlib's `SimpleGraph` and `Quiver` namespaces. Verification against a pinned mathlib commit, plus the remaining bindings, is tracked in [questions/lean-binding](../../research-articles/questions/lean-binding.md).
