# What is a Lawvere algebraic theory, derived from predication-categorical primitives?

## Pin: the target (standard literature)

A **Lawvere algebraic theory** is a small category $L$ with strict finite products and a distinguished object $x \in L$ such that every object of $L$ is isomorphic to a finite power $x^n$ for some $n \geq 0$, and the products are generated by $x$ (i.e., $L$ is the closure under finite products of $\{x\}$). A **model** of $L$ in a category $\mathcal D$ with finite products is a finite-product-preserving functor $L \to \mathcal D$; the category of models is $\mathrm{Mod}(L, \mathcal D)$. The morphism set $L(x^n, x)$ is the set of $n$-ary operations of the theory; $L(x^n, x^m)$ is the set of $m$-tuples of $n$-ary operations; equations among operations are encoded by the equality of arrows in $L$. For $\mathcal D = \mathbf{Set}$, $\mathrm{Mod}(L, \mathbf{Set})$ is the category of $L$-algebras and is equivalent to the category of algebras for a finitary monad on $\mathbf{Set}$ (Linton, Lawvere).

Enriched variants exist (Power; Lucyshyn-Wright); graded variants exist (Smirnov et al.). The pin above is the strict Set-enriched finitary case, sufficient to fix the target.

This is **not** the same as:

- **Lawvere comprehension** (the right adjoint to extension in a hyperdoctrine), which is already in the corpus and is a different Lawverian construction.
- **Predication-theory** (the corpus's $(\mathbf L, \mathcal C, E, \mathrm{Cl})$ notion), which is a different "theory" object — carrying bilattice values and an explicit closure, neither of which a Lawvere theory carries.

## The question

In the predication-categories corpus, the primitives are: an entity set $E$; a residuated lattice $\mathbf L$; a predicate category $\mathcal C$ with the four generator classes (specialization, converse, subject-projection, object-projection); the predication fibration $\mathcal E \to \mathcal C$ with fiber $\mathcal E_p \cong \mathbf L^{\bowtie E^n}$; the predication hyperdoctrine refinement (quantifier adjoints, Frobenius, Beck–Chevalley, comprehension); the closure 2-monad $\mathrm{Cl}$, lax-idempotent; the predication theory $(\mathbf L, \mathcal C, E, \mathrm{Cl})$ with transfinite settling iteration to a quiescent state $v^T_\infty$; and the bigraded diamond tower stratifying settling by truth/knowledge axes.

The question is: starting from *those* primitives, what structure crystallizes that, viewed externally, is a Lawvere algebraic theory? — not as a translation of Lawvere's definition into predication vocabulary, but as a structure the predication-categorical apparatus produces naturally, which afterwards turns out to satisfy Lawvere's axioms.

## Working the derivation

### Move 1: the entity set $E$ provides the sort

A Lawvere theory has one sort (the distinguished object $x$, generating all $x^n$). The predication-categorical apparatus has one entity set $E$ on which all predication values are defined, with fiber algebra $\mathcal E_p = \mathbf L^{\bowtie E^n}$ over an $n$-ary predicate $p$. So $E$ is the candidate sort.

This already differs structurally from a Lawvere theory: $E$ is a *set of entities*, not the categorical object "$x$" inside the theory category. The theory category $L$, if one is to crystallize, must have its $x$ be *something other than the bare set $E$* — it must be an object in some category derived from the predication apparatus, whose elements correspond to entities (or, more generally, to behavior consistent with being "an element of the sort").

### Move 2: arity stratification of predicates

The corpus has $n$-ary predication ([`objects/n-ary-predication.md`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/n-ary-predication.md)) for every $n \geq 0$. So the predicate category extends to an arity-stratified family $\{\mathcal C^{(n)}\}_{n \geq 0}$, each fibered by $\mathbf L^{\bowtie E^n}$. This is the candidate site for finite-product structure: a product $x^n$ should live in arity $n$.

But the corpus's $\mathcal C^{(n)}$ are categories of *predicates*, not categories of *operations*. A Lawvere theory's $L(x^n, x^m)$ is operations, not predicates. So predicates per se are not the morphisms of the sought $L$; some operation-like structure must be extracted.

### Move 3: functional cells are operations

A bilattice-valued cell $v \in \mathbf L^{\bowtie E^{n+1}}$ is **functional in its last coordinate** when, for each $(e_1, \ldots, e_n) \in E^n$, there is a unique $e \in E$ with the cell's value at $(e_1, \ldots, e_n, e)$ classically true ($\mathbf t$ in $\leq_t$), and the cell is classically false ($\mathbf f$) elsewhere. The functional cells form a subset

$$\mathrm{Fun}_n(E) \;\subseteq\; \mathbf L^{\bowtie E^{n+1}}$$

canonically isomorphic to $E^{E^n}$, the set of functions $E^n \to E$. (For $n = 0$, $\mathrm{Fun}_0(E) \cong E$: the constants.)

This is the move that converts predication-categorical data into Lawvere-theoretic data. The conversion is not an encoding because functional cells are an *internal* notion in the predication apparatus: they are exactly the $\leq_t$-classical, $\{(\mathbf t, \bot), (\mathbf f, \bot)\}$-valued cells whose $\mathbf t$-region projects bijectively onto $E^n$.

In the corpus's vocabulary: $\mathrm{Fun}_n(E)$ is the image of the [Lawvere-comprehension classical](../../../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/lawvere-comprehension-classical.md) right adjoint $\{p : -\}_\mathbf t$ applied to those $\psi \in \mathcal E_p$ that are *functional graphs* — characteristic functions of functions $E^n \to E$. Lawvere comprehension picks out the object representing "where $\psi$ holds"; restricting to functional graphs picks out the object representing a function.

### Move 4: clone-closed predication theories

Let $\mathrm{Adm}(T) := \bigcup_{n \geq 0} \mathrm{Adm}_n(T)$ where $\mathrm{Adm}_n(T) := \{f : E^n \to E \mid \text{the functional cell of } f \text{ is at } v^T_\infty\}$ — the set of $T$-admissible functions, indexed by arity. By Move 3, $\mathrm{Adm}_n(T)$ is a subset of $\mathrm{Fun}_n(E) \cong E^{E^n}$.

Whether $\mathrm{Adm}(T)$ forms a **clone** on $E$ — meaning it contains all coordinate projections $\pi_i^n : E^n \to E$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$ and is closed under composition $(f, g_1, \ldots, g_n) \mapsto f \circ (g_1, \ldots, g_n)$ — is the relevant structural condition. Call $T$ **clone-closed** when $\mathrm{Adm}(T)$ is a clone.

This is a condition on the closure operator $\mathrm{Cl}$: it must contain the coordinate-projection cells at the settled state, and it must drive $T$ to settle the composition-of-admissibles cell whenever the constituent cells are settled. Such closures are *not* the trivial $\mathrm{Cl} = \mathrm{id}$ — that admits only what's in the initial $v_0$ — but they exist whenever the closure-rule-system includes "if cells of $f$ and $g_1, \ldots, g_n$ are admissible, then so is the cell of $f(g_1, \ldots, g_n)$" together with the basic projection rules.

The condition is structurally analogous to the corpus's [Frobenius reciprocity](../../../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-hyperdoctrine.md) requirement on $\wedge_t / \wedge_k$ — it asks the closure to interact correctly with a composition structure. The corpus does not currently axiomatize clone-closure as a named condition; introducing it is part of the proposed extension.

Note: this is *not* a stability condition on the four predicate-category generator-reindexings ($\mathrm{Sym}$/converse/projection/specialization). Those move *between* cells of different arities and shapes in $\mathcal E$; functional cells are *not* generally stable under them — converse turns a function-graph into the relational converse, which is functional only for bijections; subject- and object-projection drop arity. The four generators are bookkeeping inside $\mathcal C$; clone-closure is a condition on the *closure dynamics on cells*, which is a different operation.

### Move 5: the Lawvere theory of $T$

Define, for a clone-closed predication theory $T$ over entity set $E$, the **Lawvere theory of $T$**, denoted $L(T)$, as follows:

- **Objects:** for each $n \geq 0$, one object $[n] \in L(T)$. Set $x := [1]$.
- **Morphisms:** $L(T)([n], [m]) = \mathrm{Adm}_n(T)^m$ — $m$-tuples of $T$-admissible functions $E^n \to E$.
- **Composition:** function composition on $E$ — well-defined by clone-closure: $\mathrm{Adm}(T)$ contains projections and is closed under composition.
- **Products:** $[n] \times [m] = [n + m]$ with the obvious projection cells (which are $T$-admissible by the projection axiom of clone-closure).

**Claim (working).** $L(T)$ is a Lawvere algebraic theory in the standard sense. The construction is an instance of the classical correspondence *clones on $E$ ↔ Lawvere theories with a faithful model in $\mathbf{Set}$ at carrier $E$* (Lawvere; Linton); the predication-categorical content is the construction of the clone $\mathrm{Adm}(T)$ from the predication-theory data $T$.

### Why this is a derivation, not an encoding

Each ingredient in $L(T)$ is a primitive of the predication apparatus:

- $[n]$ is the *arity stratum* — a primitive of $n$-ary predication.
- A morphism $[n] \to [1]$ is a *functional cell* — a fiber element whose classical truth-locus is the graph of a function. Functional cells are intrinsic to the bilattice; the bilattice's $\leq_t$ classical sub-order singles them out.
- Composition is *predication-cell composition* restricted to functional cells, which is well-defined inside the predication hyperdoctrine because the predicate category's projections and the hyperdoctrinal $\exists_f^t / \forall_f^t$ adjoints handle the bookkeeping.
- The single-generator property is *the existence of a single entity set $E$* in the predication theory.
- The finite-product structure is *the arity-product structure* of $n$-ary predications, $E^n \times E^m = E^{n+m}$.

No Lawvere-axiom was translated. The structure $L(T)$ is what one *finds* by taking the functional, $\leq_t$-classical fragment of a functionally closed predication theory's settled state, arity-stratified. That this fragment satisfies Lawvere's axioms is a *theorem*, not a *definition*.

### Where the bigraded diamond tower enters

The corpus's bigraded diamond stratifies settling by (truth, knowledge). The construction above uses the $\leq_t$-classical fragment — projecting away the knowledge axis. A **bigraded Lawvere theory** of $T$ is the obvious refinement: stratify $L(T)$ by knowledge level $k$, taking only those functional cells whose settled knowledge value is at least $k$. This yields a graded Lawvere theory in the sense of Smirnov et al., with grading monoid the knowledge axis of the bilattice. The bigraded diamond is the natural source of grading; no extra structure is needed.

### What this is *not*

- $L(T)$ is **not** the predication theory $T$ itself. $T$ has bilattice values and closure data; $L(T)$ is set-valued (after collapsing $\leq_t$-classical to two-valued) and has only finite-product structure. $T \mapsto L(T)$ is a forgetful transit: it discards the knowledge axis, the closure operator (after using it to define $T$-admissibility), and the non-classical fiber values.
- $L(T)$ is **not** Lawvere comprehension. Lawvere comprehension produces objects in $\mathcal C/p$ from predicates in $\mathcal E_p$; $L(T)$ produces a *category* from $T$. Lawvere comprehension *participates* in defining $L(T)$ (it gives the right adjoint that picks out functional graphs) but is not equal to it.
- $L(T)$ is **not** a sub-predication-theory or subtheory of $T$. Subtheories are induced by type-embeddings of predicate categories; $L(T)$ is a quotient by the functional-classical fragment, not a sub-embedding.

## Crystallized claim

**For a clone-closed predication theory $T$, the assignment $T \mapsto L(T)$ produces a Lawvere algebraic theory in the standard sense, with $L(T)$ realizing the clone $\mathrm{Adm}(T)$ on $E$ of $T$-admissible functions extracted from the functional sub-cells of the settled state $v^T_\infty$.**

The construction uses only predication-categorical primitives:

- **functional cells** — the $\leq_t$-classical, $\mathrm{graph}(f)$-shaped sub-poset of the fiber algebra (Move 3);
- **$T$-admissibility** — being part of the settled state $v^T_\infty$ (Moves 1, 4);
- **clone-closure** — a structural condition on $\mathrm{Cl}$ asking it to admit projection cells and to settle composition cells whenever the constituent cells are settled (Move 4);
- **arity stratification** — already present in the corpus as $n$-ary predication (Move 2).

Lawvere–Linton's classical correspondence (clones on $E$ ↔ Lawvere theories with faithful $\mathbf{Set}$-model at $E$) gives the equivalence; the predication-categorical content is the construction of the clone.

The bigraded diamond tower yields a refinement: grading the admissibility by the knowledge axis of the bilattice gives a graded Lawvere theory in the sense of Smirnov et al.

This is the precise claim to lift to a claim file once the verification obligations below are discharged against the corpus's axioms.

## Verification obligations

To promote the working claim to *proved*:

1. **Arity stratification of the predicate category.** Verify that the corpus's $n$-ary-predication structure ([`n-ary-predication.md`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/n-ary-predication.md)) supplies $\{\mathcal C^{(n)}\}_{n \geq 0}$ consistently for $n \in \{0, 1\}$ (the corpus is binary-primary; unary and nullary cases are sketched but not fully axiomatized).
2. **Functional sub-cells are well-defined.** Functional cells $\mathrm{Fun}_n(E) \subseteq \mathbf{L}^{\bowtie E^{n+1}}$ are picked out by a $\leq_t$-classical sub-poset condition. Verify this condition is preserved by the closure 2-monad's unit/multiplication on the $\leq_t$-classical fragment — i.e., the unit $\eta$ sends $\leq_t$-classical cells to $\leq_t$-classical cells. (Stability under the *predicate-category generators* — converse, projections, specialization — is *not* required and would in fact fail.)
3. **Clone-closure as a closure-rule-system condition.** State clone-closure precisely as a closure-rule schema; identify which of the four regimes ([`four-regime-dichotomy.md`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/four-regime-dichotomy.md)) admit clone-closed closures. Strict regime is the obvious candidate (finite settling under finitely many composition rules); the aperiodic regime may host clone-closures with transfinite closure ordinal.
4. **Composition closure ⇒ category.** Show that under clone-closure, $L(T)$'s composition is associative with identities (the projection cells $\pi_1^1 = \mathrm{id}_E$ act as identities) — this is automatic from $\mathrm{Adm}(T)$ being a clone.
5. **Finite-product universality.** Show $[n] \cong [1]^n$ in $L(T)$. The projection cells $\pi_i^n$ are in $\mathrm{Adm}_n(T)$ by clone-closure; the tuple-pairing $\langle f_1, \ldots, f_m \rangle : [n] \to [m]$ is the $m$-tuple $(f_1, \ldots, f_m) \in \mathrm{Adm}_n(T)^m = L(T)([n], [m])$ by definition.
6. **Graded refinement.** Define the knowledge-graded admissibility $\mathrm{Adm}^{\geq k}(T) := \{f \mid \text{the cell of } f \text{ has } \leq_k\text{-value} \geq k \text{ at } v^T_\infty\}$ and verify it forms a $\mathbf K$-graded clone (where $\mathbf K$ is the knowledge axis monoid). This realizes $L(T)$ as a graded Lawvere theory in the sense of Smirnov–Katsumata (2020).
7. **Enriched-theoretic placement.** Identify $L(T)$ as an enriched Lawvere theory in the sense of Lucyshyn-Wright (2017, 2023). The base of enrichment is plausibly $\mathbf{Set}$ at this level (after the $\leq_t$-classical collapse); a $\mathbf L^\bowtie$-enriched refinement using non-classical functional cells (relational graphs with bilattice weights) is an open direction (see follow-up below).

## Open follow-ups

- **Models of $L(T)$ versus settled states of $T$.** The standard models-as-product-preserving-functors story: how does $\mathrm{Mod}(L(T), \mathbf{Set})$ relate to the predication-models category of $T$ (corpus's [`predication-model.md`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-model.md))? Conjecturally: $\mathrm{Mod}(L(T), \mathbf{Set})$ embeds faithfully into predication-models of $T$ restricted to the functional-classical fragment.
- **Reverse direction.** Given a Lawvere algebraic theory $L$, can we construct a clone-closed predication theory $T(L)$ with $L(T(L)) \cong L$? Take $E$ = a sufficient carrier (e.g., the free $L$-algebra on a generator), build the predicate category $\mathcal C$ from $L$'s arities, define $\mathrm{Cl}$ from $L$'s composition. If this works, $T \mapsto L(T)$ is right adjoint to (or part of an equivalence with) the embedding $L \mapsto T(L)$.
- **$\mathbf L^\bowtie$-enriched Lawvere theory.** Relax functionality: allow morphisms $[n] \to [1]$ to be arbitrary cells $E^{n+1} \to \mathbf L^\bowtie$, not just functional graphs. This yields a *bilattice-enriched* Lawvere-style theory — closer in spirit to Power's enriched Lawvere theories and to Lucyshyn-Wright (2023). The classical $L(T)$ is its $\leq_t$-classical fragment. Open whether the result is genuinely enriched-Lawverian or requires further structure.
- **What's lost in $T \mapsto L(T)$.** Quantify the information discarded — paraconsistent cells, non-functional cells, closure-rule structure beyond clone-closure — as a cohomological obstruction, possibly an $H^1$ class in the corpus's settling cohomology. The bigraded refinement (obligation 6) is one direction; obstruction theory is another.
