# Appel & Haken 1977 — Every planar map is four colorable

The original proof of the [four-colour theorem](../research-articles/four-colour.md): every finite [planar graph](../../../glossary/terms/planar-graph.md) admits a proper vertex-colouring with four colours. The first major theorem in mathematics whose proof relied essentially on computer verification of a finite list of cases — about 1900 "unavoidable, reducible" configurations — that no human can check by hand.

## Why this is cited

This is the canonical proof citation in the [four-colour-theorem](../research-articles/four-colour.md) note, which carries `status: sketch` because the corpus does not reproduce the proof. The reference exists so the four-colour theorem can be stated honestly: proved, machine-assisted, and pointed at the source rather than reasserted on faith.

## Notable claims

- The four-colour theorem (every finite planar graph is 4-colourable). Original computer-assisted proof.
- The reduction from the four-colour theorem to a finite list of unavoidable, reducible configurations is itself a substantial combinatorial result, independent of the computer verification.
- The proof's reliance on machine computation raised foundational questions about what counts as a mathematical proof — a discussion the corpus inherits when citing this work.

## Status

The 1977 publication remains the original. A subsequent revision and refinement is [Robertson, Sanders, Seymour & Thomas 1997](robertson-sanders-seymour-thomas1997.md). A complete formal proof in Coq was given by Gonthier (2008).
