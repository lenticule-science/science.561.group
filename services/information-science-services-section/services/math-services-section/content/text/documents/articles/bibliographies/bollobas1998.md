# Bollobás 1998 — Modern Graph Theory

A graduate textbook with strong coverage of the analytic, probabilistic, and extremal sides of graph theory: spectral graph theory, random graphs, extremal results (Turán's theorem and its descendants), and the algebraic perspective. Distinct in emphasis from the standard structural texts ([Diestel 2017](diestel2017.md), [Bondy–Murty 2008](bondy-murty2008.md)).

## Why this is cited

Bollobás is the corpus's go-to reference whenever the *deferred* sub-areas of graph theory (spectral, random, extremal — see the _index) are touched on by passing remark, even though no theorems in the deferred areas are authored here. Cited mostly via `see-also:` links from notes that gesture at those sub-areas. Also serves as a counterpoint to the structural texts on questions of generality (Bollobás takes multigraphs as primary in many chapters; cf. [questions/multigraph-first-class](../research-articles/questions/multigraph-first-class.md)).

## Notable claims

- Ch. III — extremal problems and Turán's theorem.
- Ch. VII–VIII — random graphs and threshold phenomena.
- Ch. VIII–IX — spectral graph theory and eigenvalue bounds.

## Status

The 1998 Springer edition is the standard reference. Bollobás's earlier *Extremal Graph Theory* (1978) and *Random Graphs* (1985) cover specific subareas in greater depth.
