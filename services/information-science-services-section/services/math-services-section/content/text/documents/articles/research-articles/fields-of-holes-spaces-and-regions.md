## Question

The target was a field in the physics/differential-geometry sense: an assignment of a scalar, vector, tensor, operator, Hilbert space, algebra, or related value to every point of a base space. The special requirement was that each base-space point should itself be a **hole-region**, **black-hole region**, horizon-region, or causal-cut-like region, not an ordinary spacetime point.

This excludes ordinary fields on a black-hole spacetime, such as electromagnetic, scalar, Yang--Mills, or metric perturbation fields defined at spacetime points near or across a horizon. It also excludes a fiber bundle over a Cauchy slice or spacetime manifold merely because that manifold contains a black hole.

## Bottom-line result

I found **no exact literature match** for a field whose base space has points that are themselves black-hole regions or hole-regions in the strict sense above.

The closest positive family is not black-hole-specific: **kinematic-space / causal-diamond field theory**, where the base points are causal diamonds, intervals, geodesics, or codimension-two extremal surfaces. The strongest black-hole-specific family is **black-hole moduli-space geometry or quantum mechanics**, where points are black-hole solutions or multi-black-hole configurations. These two streams do not appear to meet in the requested construction: a field defined over a base of black-hole regions or hole-regions themselves.

So the result is best reported as:

> Established: field-like observables on kinematic spaces whose points are intervals, causal diamonds, geodesics, or extremal surfaces.  
> Established: moduli spaces and phase spaces of black-hole solutions/configurations.  
> Not found: a field, section, or local field theory whose base points are black-hole regions/hole-regions-as-regions.

This means the requested construction mostly collapses into the adjacent sheaf/fiber/stalk search: if one assigns a stalk, fiber, Hilbert space, algebra, or operator datum to each hole-region cell, that is essentially the categorical/sheaf-theoretic version of the same level shift, not a separately established differential-geometric field theory literature.

## Criteria used

A work counted as an exact hit only if it satisfied all three conditions:

1. There is a base space or indexing space.
2. Points of that base space are whole regions: black-hole regions, hole-regions, horizon-regions, trapped regions, causal cuts, or comparable region-objects.
3. A field/section/observable/algebraic value is assigned over that region-space, rather than over ordinary spacetime points or over ordinary CFT insertion points.

A work did **not** count if:

- the field is merely defined on a spacetime containing a black hole;
- the “moduli” are scalar fields of a supergravity theory evaluated near a horizon;
- the base point is a black-hole solution, metric, charge vector, or multi-center configuration rather than a region-object;
- the construction uses causal diamonds or intervals but not black-hole/hole-regions;
- the construction is a sheaf/fiber assignment over hole-region cells, which belongs to the adjacent sheaf search rather than a distinct “field over region-space” construction.

## Near-hit A: kinematic-space fields and causal-diamond moduli

This is the most serious positive match to the **form** of the requested construction.

De Boer, Haehl, Heller, and Myers explicitly study observables on the **moduli space of causal diamonds**, equivalently the space of pairs of timelike-separated points. They construct, for a primary CFT operator, an observable on this space by smearing one-point functions over causal diamonds; for small perturbations, these observables obey equations of motion on the moduli space of causal diamonds. In two dimensions this moduli space is a product of two two-dimensional de Sitter spaces, and the paper discusses the possibility of interacting theories on this moduli space [@deBoer2016CausalDiamonds].

Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, Mosk, and Sully describe OPE blocks as **fields in kinematic space**. Here kinematic space is the space of pairs of CFT points; in holographic AdS3/CFT2 settings it is also the space of boundary intervals, boundary causal diamonds, or oriented bulk geodesics. Their OPE blocks satisfy a Klein--Gordon equation on kinematic space [@Czech2016Stereoscopic].

The earlier integral-geometry formulation by Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, and Sully defines kinematic space as an auxiliary Lorentzian geometry organizing CFT entanglement and, in holographic theories, as the space of bulk geodesics. This is a region/object-indexed geometry, but the objects are geodesics/intervals rather than black-hole regions [@Czech2015IntegralGeometry].

Penna and Zukowski generalize/clarify kinematic space as the space of codimension-two spacelike extremal surfaces in AdS, with a coadjoint-orbit interpretation [@Penna2019Orbit]. This moves the base points closer to extended geometric objects, but again not to black-hole regions.

Cresswell and Peet study kinematic space for conical defects. They show that partial OPE blocks can be related to bulk fields integrated over individual geodesics, including non-minimal geodesics [@CresswellPeet2017Conical]. This is a useful near-hit because it shows kinematic-space fields in a nontrivial quotient geometry, but it is still not a black-hole-region base.

**Assessment:** This is the closest established literature to “field one level up.” It is a real region/object-indexed field construction, but its base points are causal diamonds, intervals, geodesics, or extremal surfaces, not black-hole regions/hole-regions.

## Near-hit B: black-hole moduli spaces and solution spaces

Black-hole moduli-space literature is genuinely black-hole-specific, but the objects being parametrized are solutions, charges, centers, metrics, scalar moduli, or initial data, not black-hole regions as base-space points.

Maloney, Spradlin, and Strominger study quantum mechanics on the moduli space of multiple supersymmetric Reissner--Nordström black holes. A wavefunction on such a moduli space is field-like in the weak sense of a function on a space of configurations, but the base points are multi-black-hole configurations, not hole-regions [@Maloney2002MultiBlackHole].

Gibbons, Papadopoulos, and Stelle analyze HKT and OKT geometries on soliton black-hole moduli spaces; Gutowski and Papadopoulos give universal moduli metrics for classes of four- and five-dimensional supersymmetric black holes [@Gibbons1997HKTOKT; @Gutowski2000ModuliSpaces]. These are geometries of solution/configuration spaces, not fields over region-cells.

Ferrara, Gibbons, and Kallosh study black holes and critical points in moduli space, especially the stabilization of scalar moduli near supersymmetric black-hole horizons [@Ferrara1997CriticalPoints]. This is a central black-hole/moduli reference, but the “moduli” here are scalar-field values or parameters of compactification/supergravity, not a base space of hole-regions.

Suvorov gives a superspace approach to distances between black-hole spacetimes, restricting the space of metrics to black-hole geometries and measuring closeness between them [@Suvorov2020Superspace]. This is a space of black-hole metrics/spacetimes, but the construction is a metric/distance on solution-space, not a field whose argument is a black-hole region.

Katona and Lucietti construct/classify a large moduli space of five-dimensional supersymmetric black-hole and soliton spacetimes with several horizon topologies [@KatonaLucietti2023Axial]. Angelopoulos, Kehle, and Unger describe an infinite-dimensional moduli space of dynamical spherically symmetric Einstein--Maxwell--scalar solutions and identify the black-hole threshold inside it [@AngelopoulosKehleUnger2026Threshold]. Both are important “spaces of black-hole spacetimes,” but neither defines the requested field over black-hole regions-as-points.

**Assessment:** These works are black-hole-specific and moduli-theoretic, but they do not provide the required region-indexed field. They are better classified as black-hole solution-space geometry, moduli dynamics, or quantum mechanics on configuration space.

## Near-hit C: isolated/dynamical horizons and horizon fields

The isolated/dynamical horizon program defines quasi-local horizon structures and assigns geometric and physical data to horizons and their cross-sections. Ashtekar and Krishnan review isolated horizons, dynamical horizons, balance laws, angular momentum, energy fluxes, and black-hole entropy applications [@AshtekarKrishnan2004Horizons].

This could be mistaken for the target because it involves data associated with horizons. But the base is not a moduli/cellular space whose points are whole hole-regions. Rather, the constructions are fields, forms, fluxes, connections, or charges **on** horizon hypersurfaces or cross-sections, or structures in a phase space of gravitational fields.

**Assessment:** This is not the requested one-level-up construction. It remains horizon-local or phase-space-based, not a field over a space of horizon-regions.

## Near-hit D: holographic holes and differential entropy

The “holographic holes” literature is relevant to the word “hole,” but it does not define a field over hole-regions.

Headrick, Myers, and Wien show that the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy of certain closed bulk surfaces can be interpreted as differential entropy of a family of boundary intervals or strips [@Headrick2014Holes]. Myers, Rao, and Sugishita extend related holographic-hole constructions to higher dimensions [@Myers2014HigherHoles].

These papers encode a closed bulk surface or hole-like bulk surface by a family of boundary regions/intervals. The base is not a space whose points are black-hole or hole-regions carrying field values.

**Assessment:** Relevant vocabulary and adjacent geometry; not a field over hole-region space.

## Near-hit E: causal/light-cone cuts

Kozameh and Newman define light-cone cuts of future null infinity as intersections of light cones from interior points with future null infinity, and show how the set of such cuts can reconstruct the interior conformal metric [@KozamehNewman1983Cuts]. This is a real “cuts as geometric data” tradition.

However, the cuts are not black-hole regions or hole-regions used as base-space points for a field. The construction reconstructs spacetime geometry from characteristic/cut data; it does not assign a field value to each hole-region.

**Assessment:** Useful as an order/causal-cut analogue, but not an exact hit.

## Negative-result statement

A careful statement for use in a memo:

> I found no literature defining a physics/differential-geometric field whose base space consists of black-hole regions or hole-regions themselves. The closest established analogue is holographic kinematic space, where OPE blocks and smeared CFT observables behave as fields on spaces of intervals, causal diamonds, geodesics, or extremal surfaces. Separately, there is substantial work on moduli spaces of black-hole solutions and multi-black-hole configurations. I found no work merging these into a field theory over a base whose points are black-hole regions or hole-regions.

## How this relates to the prior sheaf/fiber/stalk search

The field-search does not appear to reveal a distinct construction beyond the sheaf/fiber/stalk search. If one were to define a base category or cellular complex whose objects/cells are hole-regions, and then attach a stalk, fiber, algebra, Hilbert space, or cohomological object to each one, that would already be the sheaf/cellular-sheaf version of the desired assignment.

The present search suggests that the ordinary differential-geometric vocabulary of “field,” “section,” or “bundle” has not been developed for hole-regions/black-hole-regions as base points. The closest vocabulary in the literature is either:

- “fields on kinematic space,” for causal diamonds/intervals/geodesics; or
- “quantum mechanics/geometry on black-hole moduli space,” for black-hole solution/configuration spaces.

## Research gap

A genuinely new construction would likely need to define:

- a base object \(\mathcal H\) whose points are black-hole regions, trapped regions, causal complements, horizon-bounded regions, or hole-region cells;
- a topology, smooth structure, order structure, or cellular structure on \(\mathcal H\);
- a bundle, sheaf, or field assignment \(\Phi : \mathcal H \to E\), where \(\Phi(H)\) is a scalar, tensor, operator, algebra, Hilbert space, entropy datum, cohomological datum, or state space attached to the region \(H\);
- locality, covariance, restriction, gluing, or dynamics with respect to inclusions/intersections/causal ordering of the regions.

The existing kinematic-space literature supplies the closest model for the first three items when \(H\) is replaced by causal diamonds or intervals. The black-hole moduli literature supplies black-hole-specific solution spaces. The missing piece is the strict region-as-point base.
