A Chu space (Barr 1979, developed by Pratt 1999) over a value set $L$ is a triple $(A, r, X)$ where $A$ is a set of states (or objects), $X$ is a set of attributes (or observations), and $r: A \times X \to L$ is an evaluation function.

Pratt (1999) proved that a [formal context](../../../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/_archive/category/scripts/tensoring/content/monoidal-category/scripts/chu-constructing/content/chu-space/formal-context.md) $(G, M, I)$ is a Chu space over $\{0, 1\}$, with $G$ as states, $M$ as attributes, and $I$ as evaluation. Many-valued formal contexts are Chu spaces over the value set $L$.

Chu spaces form a $*$-autonomous category — the categorical structure underlying [linear logic](../../../../../../glossary/terms/linear-logic.md). Tensor in linear logic corresponds to the tensor product of Chu spaces.

Jakl (2025) proved the key connection: the Chu construction $\mathrm{Chu}(H, 0)$ over a Heyting algebra $H$ is exactly the [twist construction](../../../../../../../../services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/residuated-lattice/content/twist-product/twist-product.md) that produces [bilattices](../../../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/_archive/category/scripts/tensoring/content/monoidal-category/scripts/chu-constructing/algebra/objects/bilattice.md). Under this identification:

- Tensor ($\otimes$) in linear logic = fusion ($\odot$) in the twist product = extending
- Linear implication ($\multimap$) = twist-product implication ($\Rightarrow$) = restricting

This means every bilattice-valued formal context is simultaneously a Chu space, a model of linear logic, and a [concept lattice](../../../../../../glossary/terms/concept-lattice.md) with [Lambek calculus](../../../../../../glossary/terms/lambek-calculus.md) structure.
