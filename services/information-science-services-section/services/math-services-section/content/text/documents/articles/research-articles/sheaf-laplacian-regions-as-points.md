## Research memo: causal regions as moduli points vs. sheaf Laplacians on such moduli

### Bottom-line finding

There **is** substantial literature in which the basic object is not a point-event but an extended geometric object: a 3-geometry, a causal diamond, a boundary interval/domain, a spacetime region, or an entire black-hole solution. The clearest match to your formulation is **CFT/holographic kinematic space**, especially de Boer et al.’s “generalized kinematic space,” where the **moduli space of causal diamonds** is explicitly treated as a space on which observables obey differential equations. ([pure.uva.nl](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/10006136/Entanglement_holography_and_causal_diamonds.pdf))

There is also a mature literature on **cellular sheaf Laplacians**, especially Hansen–Ghrist’s spectral theory of cellular sheaves, but that literature defines Laplacians over a **fixed graph, cell complex, simplicial complex, network, or filtration**, not over a moduli/configuration space whose points are causal diamonds or black-hole regions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01513))

I did **not** find an established literature where these two threads are already joined: a Hansen–Ghrist-style cellular/sheaf Laplacian whose base complex is itself a moduli space of causal diamonds, black-hole regions, or causal-set regions. The nearest established intersections use ordinary differential/spectral operators on kinematic space, Hilbert bundles or operator algebras indexed by causal diamonds, or sheaf-theoretic quantum-gravity formalisms, but not a cellular sheaf Laplacian on the region-moduli space.

My assessment: **the exact construction you describe appears to be genuinely underdeveloped or unexplored, not an established subfield**.

---

## 1. Literature where “points” are extended spaces, regions, or causal diamonds

### 1.1 Wheeler superspace and “spaces of spaces”

The oldest and broadest version is Wheeler–DeWitt geometrodynamics. In DeWitt’s canonical quantum gravity, the configuration variable is not a spacetime point but a 3-geometry; the relevant infinite-dimensional space is the space of Riemannian 3-geometries modulo diffeomorphism. DeWitt explicitly frames this as an infinite-dimensional manifold of 3-geometries, while later reviews identify Wheeler’s “superspace” as the arena of geometrodynamics. ([link.aps.org](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRev.160.1113?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This is a clear “space of spaces,” but it is not specifically a moduli space of causal diamonds. It is more global: its points are spatial geometries, not causal subregions. Rainer’s work on the moduli space of local homogeneous 3-geometries is a finite-dimensional subcase of this general superspace idea. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9607067?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Relevance:** strong precedent for configuration spaces whose points are entire geometries.  
**Limitation:** not specifically causal diamonds or black-hole regions; spectral/sheaf-Laplacian structures are not the central object.

---

### 1.2 Holographic kinematic space: causal diamonds as points

The closest direct match is the holographic/CFT literature on **kinematic space**. Czech et al. describe kinematic space in AdS\(_3\)/CFT\(_2\) as the space of boundary causal diamonds, equivalently pairs of timelike-separated boundary points or oriented bulk geodesics. ([inspirehep.net](https://inspirehep.net/files/7412b683da7e542ab450bc899132851d))

The more direct higher-dimensional formulation is de Boer et al., *Entanglement, holography and causal diamonds*. They explicitly say that CFT degrees of freedom can be reorganized into observables living on the **moduli space of causal diamonds**, or equivalently the space of timelike-separated point pairs. They call this the **generalized kinematic space** and identify it with a coset of the conformal group. They also define a metric and dynamics on that causal-diamond moduli space. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP08%282016%29162))

A particularly important point for your question: this literature already connects **spectral/differential equations** to the moduli of regions. The relevant operators are not cellular sheaf Laplacians; they are continuous wave/Casimir/Klein–Gordon-type operators on the smooth homogeneous space of causal diamonds. de Boer et al. state that the corresponding observables obey two-derivative equations of motion on the space of causal diamonds. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP08%282016%29162))

**Relevance:** very high. This is the cleanest established “causal diamonds as points of a moduli space” literature.  
**Limitation:** the spectral theory is ordinary smooth/differential spectral theory on kinematic space, not cellular sheaf Hodge theory.

---

### 1.3 Banks–Fischler / Banks holographic space-time

Banks’s holographic space-time program also treats causal diamonds as fundamental organizing objects. In Banks’s review, Lorentzian geometry is recast in terms of timelike trajectories and their associated causal diamonds; the entropy of a causal diamond is tied to the area of its holographic screen, and constraints relate quantum information in overlapping diamonds. ([frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2020.00111/full))

Later Banks papers phrase the framework in terms of **Hilbert bundles** over spaces of timelike geodesics, with finite-dimensional subsystem algebras associated to causal diamonds. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07038?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Relevance:** high conceptually. It assigns Hilbert spaces/operator algebras to causal diamonds and overlaps.  
**Limitation:** I found no Hansen–Ghrist-style cellular sheaf Laplacian, sheaf cochain complex, or sheaf Hodge operator on a causal-diamond moduli space in this literature.

---

### 1.4 Causal sites, causal-set geometry, and domains of intervals

Christensen and Crane’s **causal sites** are another close mathematical analogue. Their proposal replaces an underlying point set by a categorical structure of regions, causal relations, and inclusions; the abstract explicitly frames causal sites as a setting for quantum geometry without fundamental point-events. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0410104))

Martin and Panangaden’s domain-theoretic work studies domains of spacetime intervals and shows how spacetime structure can be reconstructed order-theoretically from interval/causal data. ([cs.mcgill.ca](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~prakash/Pubs/relativity.pdf))

This is not exactly a smooth moduli space of causal diamonds, but it is very close to your “regions/domains as primitive” direction. The causal-set literature itself often starts from locally finite posets of elementary events, but the causal-site/domain-theoretic variants move closer to region-based foundations. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11544?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Relevance:** high for region-based causal geometry.  
**Limitation:** I did not find an established cellular sheaf Laplacian on the poset/category/domain of regions in these sources.

---

### 1.5 Finite causal diamonds as gravitational subregions

There is also a subregion phase-space literature around finite causal diamonds. Chandrasekaran and Prabhu study the covariant phase space of vacuum general relativity at the null boundary of causal diamonds, including charges, fluxes, and corner symmetries. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.00017))

More recent work in three-dimensional gravity studies the charge algebra of causal diamonds and quantization of a reduced phase space localized to the diamond corner. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03014v2))

**Relevance:** strong for “entire causal diamond as a gravitational object.”  
**Limitation:** these works study phase spaces and boundary symmetries of individual causal diamonds or classes of such diamonds; they do not define a cellular sheaf Laplacian on the moduli space of diamonds.

---

### 1.6 Black-hole moduli spaces

There is a separate, real black-hole moduli-space literature. For example, work on supersymmetric multi-black-hole solutions studies moduli spaces whose coordinates include black-hole positions, with metrics governing low-energy dynamics. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0201111))

Breunhölder and Lucietti study moduli spaces of five-dimensional supersymmetric soliton and black-hole spacetimes, including black-hole solutions with nontrivial topology. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.07092))

However, much of the classic “black-hole moduli space” literature concerns either the moduli of **solutions** or the scalar/vector multiplet moduli of a supergravity/string compactification, not a configuration space whose points are black-hole interiors or black-hole regions as causal-domain objects. Ferrara–Kallosh–Strominger’s attractor-moduli literature, for example, is about black holes and critical points in moduli space, not a moduli space whose points are black-hole regions. ([cds.cern.ch](https://cds.cern.ch/record/320328/files/9702103.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Relevance:** moderate to high, depending on whether “black-hole object” means whole solution/lump or causal region/interior.  
**Limitation:** I found no sheaf/cellular Laplacian on such black-hole-region moduli spaces.

## 2. Literature on cellular/sheaf Laplacians

The central reference is Hansen and Ghrist’s *Toward a Spectral Theory of Cellular Sheaves*. They define a Hodge Laplacian for cellular sheaves of vector spaces over regular cell complexes, generalizing the graph Laplacian and relating spectral data to sheaf cohomology. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01513))

The basic form is the expected cellular sheaf Hodge operator:
\[
\Delta^k_{\mathcal F}
=
\delta^{k-1}_{\mathcal F}\delta^{k-1,*}_{\mathcal F}
+
\delta^{k,*}_{\mathcal F}\delta^k_{\mathcal F},
\]
where the coboundaries are those of the cellular sheaf cochain complex. Hansen–Ghrist’s contribution is to make this into a spectral theory for sheaves on cell complexes, including harmonic cochains, eigenvalue interlacing, and relationships with cohomology. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01513))

There are also extensions and applications: persistent sheaf Laplacians over filtrations, Tarski/Laplacian constructions for lattice-valued sheaves, discourse sheaves and diffusion dynamics, and sheaf neural networks. These all remain, in the sources I found, based on a given combinatorial base such as a graph, cell complex, simplicial complex, network, poset-like structure, or data filtration. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.10906v4))

**Relevance:** high for the Laplacian technology.  
**Limitation:** the base space is not, in the cited literature, a moduli/configuration space of causal diamonds or black-hole regions.

## 3. Where the two threads nearly intersect

### 3.1 Kinematic space already has spectral geometry, but not sheaf Hodge theory

The CFT kinematic-space literature is the closest positive case. It has:

- a moduli space of causal diamonds;
- a conformally invariant geometry on that moduli space;
- wave/Casimir-type equations for observables living on that space.

But the operators are continuous geometric/differential operators, not cellular sheaf Laplacians over a sheaf cochain complex. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP08%282016%29162))

So the statement “spectral theory on the moduli of regions” is already true in kinematic space. The stronger statement “cellular sheaf Laplacian on a discretized/cellular moduli space of causal diamonds” does not appear to be established.

---

### 3.2 Holographic space-time has Hilbert bundles, but not cellular sheaf Laplacians

Banks’s holographic space-time framework assigns quantum systems to causal diamonds and imposes consistency conditions between overlapping diamonds. Later Hilbert-bundle formulations make the bundle language even more explicit. ([frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2020.00111/full))

That is structurally reminiscent of sheaf data: local Hilbert spaces/operator algebras, restrictions/overlaps, and consistency. But I found no paper in that literature defining a cellular sheaf cochain complex, sheaf cohomology, or Hansen–Ghrist Laplacian on the causal-diamond indexing space.

---

### 3.3 Causal sites are region-based, but not spectral-sheaf constructions

Causal sites already supply a category/order of regions rather than point-events. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0410104)) A cellular sheaf on such a poset/category is mathematically plausible, and Hansen–Ghrist-style sheaf theory is compatible with combinatorial bases such as cell complexes and related incidence structures. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01513))

But I found no checkable established source that actually carries out the construction:
\[
\text{causal regions/diamonds as cells or vertices}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal F
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\Delta_{\mathcal F}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{spectral invariants of region moduli}.
\]

---

### 3.4 Black-hole moduli spaces have metrics/dynamics, not sheaf Laplacians

The black-hole moduli-space literature does connect geometry of moduli spaces to dynamics, especially for supersymmetric multi-black-hole systems. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0201111)) But I found no established work defining a sheaf Laplacian over a cell complex whose vertices/cells are black-hole regions or whole black-hole spacetimes.

---

## 4. Assessment of novelty

The following pieces are established:

| Component | Status |
|---|---|
| Spaces whose points are geometries | Established in Wheeler superspace/geometrodynamics. ([link.aps.org](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRev.160.1113?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) |
| Spaces whose points are causal diamonds | Established in holographic kinematic space/generalized kinematic space. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP08%282016%29162)) |
| Region-first causal geometry | Established in causal sites/domain-theoretic interval approaches. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0410104)) |
| Black-hole solution/lump moduli spaces | Established, especially in supersymmetric settings. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0201111)) |
| Cellular sheaf Laplacians | Established by Hansen–Ghrist and follow-up work. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01513)) |
| Sheaf Laplacian on causal-diamond / black-hole-region moduli | I found no established literature. |

My best reading is that the proposed object is a **natural but apparently missing synthesis**: take a region-moduli space such as generalized kinematic space, causal-site region poset, interval domain, or black-hole solution moduli; choose a cellular/discrete model of it; place a sheaf of local observables, Hilbert spaces, charges, entanglement data, algebras, or cohomological data over that base; then study the Hansen–Ghrist sheaf Laplacian spectrum as a spectral invariant of the **moduli of regions** rather than of a fixed spacetime base.

The closest existing phrase would not be “sheaf Laplacian on causal diamonds,” but something like:

> **cellular sheaf spectral theory on region moduli spaces**

or, in the holographic setting,

> **sheaf-Hodge theory on causal-diamond kinematic space**.

I would treat that as **research-program language**, not as the name of an already established field.
