# Directed graphs and the predication substrate

The corpus's predication-categories directory is built on a substrate $(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E)$ — a residuated lattice, a predicate category, an entity set — and the predicate category $\mathcal{C}$ is itself generated by a [quiver](../../../../glossary/terms/quiver.md) of four edge classes: specialization, converse, subject-projection, object-projection. The [predication fibration](../../../../glossary/terms/predication-fibration.md) then acts on $\mathcal{C}$ by reindexing along this quiver. The graph-theoretic content underneath the fibration is a [directed graph](../../../../glossary/terms/directed-graph.md), and most of the structural commitments the predication substrate makes are commitments about the shape of that graph.

The same pattern shows up at a different scale in the corpus's own bookkeeping. The renderer takes a corpus of typed notes and treats their `depends-on:` declarations as edges of a directed graph $D$ on the vertex set of all notes. To produce a linear reading order, the renderer needs a topological sort of $D^{\mathrm{op}}$ — and by the [dependency-soundness-iff-dag](../../research-articles/dependency-soundness-iff-dag.md) theorem, that topological sort exists iff $D$ is a [DAG](../../../../glossary/terms/dag.md). The corpus's commitment to a sound reading order is therefore a commitment to the `depends-on:` graph being acyclic. The [dependency-graph-of-this-corpus](../examples/dependency-graph-of-this-corpus.md) example walks through what this graph looks like at the corpus level: roughly $10^3$–$10^4$ vertices, comparable edge count, sparse, layered, and required-by-construction acyclic.

The two scales — the small quiver of predicate-category generators, and the corpus-wide `depends-on:` graph — are the same kind of object, used the same way: a finite directed graph whose acyclicity (or whose specific cycle structure, in the predicate-category case where cycles do exist but carry semantic content) controls what downstream constructions are sound. The graph-theoretic claims this directory makes — [dag-topological-sort](../../research-articles/dag-topological-sort.md), [dependency-soundness-iff-dag](../../research-articles/dependency-soundness-iff-dag.md), the [transitive-closure morphism](../../../../glossary/terms/transitive-closure.md), the [topological-sort morphism](../../../../glossary/terms/topological-sort.md) — are exactly the claims those uses depend on. Without them, the substrate's commitments float; with them, the substrate's commitments are typed against a graph-theory directory that proves exactly the structural facts the substrate quietly relies on.

There is a third use of the same substrate worth pointing at: state machines. A state machine is a [directed labelled graph](../../../../glossary/terms/labelled-graph.md) (states as vertices, transitions as edges, labels on edges) — see the [state-machine-as-labelled-digraph](../examples/state-machine-as-labelled-digraph.md) example. State machines do not have to be DAGs (loops back to earlier states are typical), but the [reachability](../../../../glossary/terms/reachability.md) relation on a state machine still answers operationally important questions — which states are reachable from the start state, which are co-reachable to the accept state. The [transitive-closure morphism](../../../../glossary/terms/transitive-closure.md) is what computes reachability. The corpus's own state machines (the various lifecycle FSMs in the FARS implementation: task-state transitions, ASR plan phases, build pipelines) are concrete instances of this third use.

The structural takeaway from these three uses is the same. Whenever the corpus reasons about *what edges exist between what objects, and which compositions of edges encode which structural facts*, the underlying object is a directed graph, and the cleanest statements of the structural facts are graph-theoretic. The substrate sits on top of that layer; the layer is not just the bottom of the substrate but the layer the substrate's soundness arguments actually invoke. Promoting graph-theory from a three-file staging area to a populated theory is therefore not just a courtesy to the discipline — it is what gives the predication substrate's structural arguments somewhere to land.

What this directory does *not* do is reprove the substrate-level theorems in graph-theoretic terms. The predication-categories theorems carry their own depths (closure-driven settling, the closure 2-monad, the cohomological-rank computations) that are not graph-theory's province. Graph theory's job is the layer below: supplying the directed-graph object, the DAG specialization, the reachability and topological-sort machinery, and the soundness theorem for `depends-on:`. The substrate's deeper theorems sit on top of *this* layer plus several others ([order](../../order/), [algebra](../../algebra/), [categories](../../categories/), [topology](../../topology/)). The contribution of this directory is to make the bottom layer load-bearing in name as well as in fact.
