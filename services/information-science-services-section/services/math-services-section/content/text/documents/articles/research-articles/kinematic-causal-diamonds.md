# Causal-Diamond Kinematic Space

## Plain-English summary

The guiding idea is simple but powerful: **build a geometry whose points are regions rather than events**. In ordinary spacetime geometry, a point is an idealized event: “here, now.” In causal-diamond kinematic space, the elementary object is instead an extended region: the overlap of the future of one event with the past of another. Such a region is called a **causal diamond** or **Alexandrov interval**.

This idea has three established pillars. First, Lorentzian geometry supplies the continuum notion of causal diamonds, regions determined by pairs of causally related events. Second, holography supplies **kinematic space**, an auxiliary space whose points can stand for boundary intervals, bulk geodesics, or causal diamonds, and whose geometry encodes entanglement and reconstruction data. Third, causal set theory supplies a mathematically clean discrete analogue: a causal diamond becomes a finite **order interval** in a locally finite partially ordered set.

The established literature goes far in continuum holography, especially in the work of Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, Sully, and collaborators on integral geometry and kinematic space, and in the work of de Boer, Haehl, Heller, and Myers on the moduli space of causal diamonds in conformal field theory. The literature does **not** yet establish a mature “causal-set kinematic space dynamics” in which the set of intervals in a growing causal set is itself treated as a developed dynamical geometry. That remains an open, underexplored direction.

## 1. The hook: a geometry whose points are regions

The phrase **causal-diamond kinematic space** names a shift in what one treats as basic. Instead of beginning with event-points and then building regions out of them, one begins with a collection of regions and asks whether that collection has its own structure: adjacency, inclusion, order, metric, measure, topology, or dynamics.

A causal diamond is not a generic spatial region. It is a **causally defined spacetime region**: it is determined by two events, an earlier “bottom tip” and a later “top tip.” In a relativistic spacetime, it consists of the events that can be reached from the bottom tip and can still influence the top tip. In a causal set, it consists of the elements ordered between two comparable elements.

Kinematic space adds a second layer. It is not usually the physical spacetime itself. It is an **auxiliary space of geometric data**. In the best-developed holographic examples, a point of kinematic space represents a boundary interval, a bulk geodesic anchored to that interval, a ball-shaped boundary region, or a causal diamond. The “geometry” of kinematic space is then a geometry of how those objects vary, overlap, nest, or encode entanglement.

This is the central conceptual point: **regions can be coordinatized as points of another space**. Once this is done, questions about many regions in spacetime can be translated into geometric questions in the auxiliary space.

## 2. Causal diamonds and Alexandrov intervals

### 2.1 Continuum definition in Lorentzian geometry

A relativistic spacetime is modeled as a Lorentzian manifold: a smooth manifold equipped with a metric that distinguishes timelike, null, and spacelike directions. The causal structure determines which events can influence which other events.

For an event $p$, let:

- $I^+(p)$ denote the **chronological future** of $p$: events reachable from $p$ by future-directed timelike curves.
- $J^+(p)$ denote the **causal future** of $p$: events reachable from $p$ by future-directed timelike or null causal curves.
- $I^-(q)$ and $J^-(q)$ denote the corresponding pasts of an event $q$.

When $p$ causally precedes $q$, the corresponding causal diamond is commonly written as

$$
D(p,q)=J^+(p)\cap J^-(q).
$$

The open Alexandrov interval is often written as

$$
A(p,q)=I^+(p)\cap I^-(q).
$$

The difference is boundary convention: $J$ includes null boundary points and endpoints, while $I$ uses timelike reachability and gives an open region. Both versions express the same organizing idea: a region determined by a pair of causally related events. Standard Lorentzian geometry texts treat such causal futures, pasts, and intervals as basic tools for studying global causal structure [@beem1996global].

The term **Alexandrov interval** emphasizes that the interval is built from causal order. The terminology fits the causal-space tradition in which one abstracts from spacetime to an event set equipped with causal and chronological relations [@kronheimer1967causal]. It also fits later results showing how much geometry is encoded by causal structure: in sufficiently well-behaved spacetimes, causal relations are closely tied to topology and conformal geometry [@hawking1976topology].

### 2.2 What causal diamonds do

Causal diamonds are useful because they are both local and covariant. They are local because small diamonds can probe small spacetime neighborhoods. They are covariant because they are defined by causal relations rather than by a preferred spatial slicing.

Several features matter for the present memo.

First, a causal diamond is determined by **two events** rather than one. The resulting region has duration, spatial extent, null boundaries, and sometimes a preferred “waist” or maximal spatial cross-section, depending on the spacetime and slicing.

Second, causal diamonds express the idea of a **causally complete region**. In flat spacetime, the domain of dependence of a ball-shaped spatial region is a diamond-shaped spacetime region. This is why ball-shaped regions in a conformal field theory can be equivalently discussed through their causal diamonds: the algebraic or entanglement data of the spatial ball is naturally associated with its causal development.

Third, causal diamonds are order-theoretic. The definition $J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$ depends only on the relation “can causally influence,” plus boundary conventions. This order-theoretic character is what makes them portable between continuum relativity, holography, and causal set theory.

### 2.3 Discrete analogue: order intervals in causal set theory

Causal set theory begins from the hypothesis that the fundamental structure of spacetime is a locally finite partially ordered set, or **causal set**. The partial order represents causal precedence; local finiteness encodes discreteness [@bombelli1987spacetime; @surya2019causal].

A causal set is usually written as $(C,\preceq)$, where $x\preceq y$ means that $x$ causally precedes $y$. Local finiteness says that for any $x\preceq y$, the interval

$$
[x,y]=\{z\in C\mid x\preceq z\preceq y\}
$$

contains finitely many elements.

This finite set is the discrete analogue of a causal diamond or Alexandrov interval. In a continuum approximation, where a causal set is faithfully embedded into a Lorentzian manifold by a random sprinkling at fixed density, the number of elements in such an order interval approximates the spacetime volume of the corresponding continuum causal interval [@surya2019causal].

This is an established bridge: **continuum causal diamond $\leftrightarrow$ finite order interval**. What is not established is a full holographic-style kinematic-space theory built on the set of all such order intervals in a causal set. The interval itself is standard. A mature dynamics of a “space of intervals” is not.

## 3. Kinematic space in holography

### 3.1 What kinematic space is

In holography, the term **kinematic space** refers to an auxiliary space whose points label geometric or field-theoretic objects. The objects vary by context:

- in two-dimensional conformal field theory, a point of kinematic space may label a boundary interval;
- in static $\mathrm{AdS}_3/\mathrm{CFT}_2$, the same boundary interval labels a bulk geodesic anchored to its endpoints;
- in higher-dimensional conformal field theory, a point may label a ball-shaped region or its associated causal diamond;
- in OPE-block constructions, a point may label a pair of boundary points or the diamond determined by them.

The point is not that these objects are literally pointlike in spacetime. They are not. The point is that a family of extended objects can itself be organized as a space.

### 3.2 Entanglement as the reason regions matter

Holography relates certain gravitational theories in a bulk spacetime to quantum field theories on a lower-dimensional boundary. The Ryu–Takayanagi formula says, in its original static form, that the entanglement entropy of a boundary region is computed by the area of a corresponding minimal surface in the bulk [@ryu2006holographic]. The Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi proposal extends the idea covariantly to time-dependent settings using extremal surfaces [@hubeny2007covariant].

This immediately makes regions central. Entanglement entropy is not assigned to a single event; it is assigned to a region or subalgebra. Bulk reconstruction likewise often asks how bulk geometric information can be recovered from entanglement data associated with many boundary regions. Ball-shaped regions are especially important in conformal field theory because the vacuum reduced density matrix for a ball has special symmetry properties and can be related by conformal transformations to thermal physics on hyperbolic space [@casini2011towards]. Perturbations of entanglement entropy for all boundary balls lead to gravitational constraints in the bulk, including the linearized Einstein equations in the holographic setting [@faulkner2014gravitation].

This is the physical reason kinematic space is natural: the data one wants to organize are already region-based.

### 3.3 The Czech–Lamprou–McCandlish–Sully program

The most influential kinematic-space line begins with the work of Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, and Sully on **integral geometry and holography** [@czech2015integral]. Their construction focuses especially on static $\mathrm{AdS}_3/\mathrm{CFT}_2$, where an interval on the boundary corresponds to a geodesic in the bulk. The set of all such oriented intervals can be treated as a two-dimensional kinematic space.

In this setting, a point of kinematic space can be labeled by the two endpoints of a boundary interval. The same point can also be viewed as labeling the bulk geodesic anchored at those endpoints. The auxiliary geometry of kinematic space is tied to entanglement: the kinematic-space metric or measure can be built from second derivatives of boundary entanglement entropy, equivalently from conditional mutual information in an infinitesimal limit [@czech2015integral].

The power of this construction comes from integral geometry. In ordinary integral geometry, one studies a space of submanifolds—such as lines or geodesics—and uses integrals over that space to recover geometric quantities in the original space. In the holographic version, lengths of bulk curves can be computed by integrating a Crofton form over the set of geodesics intersecting the curve [@czech2015integral]. Thus, the geometry of the bulk is partly encoded in the geometry of the space of boundary intervals.

This is a precise realization of the hook: **the points of the auxiliary geometry are extended objects in the original geometry**. The extended objects are boundary intervals and bulk geodesics, not point-events.

The same group extended this viewpoint in several directions. In work on tensor networks, Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, and Sully argued that the MERA tensor network resembles a discretization of kinematic space—the space of intervals or bulk geodesics—rather than a direct discretization of a spatial bulk slice [@czech2016tensor]. This matters because it shifts the interpretation of network geometry: network sites can be read as encoding scale- and interval-dependent entanglement data.

In “A Stereoscopic Look into the Bulk,” Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, Mosk, and Sully connected kinematic space to **OPE blocks**, nonlocal CFT objects that package the contribution of a conformal family in an operator product expansion [@czech2016stereoscopic]. They showed that OPE blocks can be treated as fields on kinematic space and, in holographic settings, are related to integrals of bulk fields over geodesics or higher-dimensional extremal surfaces. This broadened kinematic space from an integral-geometric tool for lengths to a framework for organizing nonlocal CFT observables and aspects of bulk reconstruction.

### 3.4 Differential entropy as a close precursor

Kinematic space also inherits ideas from differential entropy. In holographic settings, families of boundary intervals can be used to compute areas or lengths of bulk curves and surfaces. Headrick, Myers, and Wien showed that the differential entropy of a family of intervals or strips can equal the gravitational entropy associated with a bulk surface, under appropriate conditions [@headrick2014holes]. Czech, Dong, and Sully extended reconstruction ideas for more general bulk surfaces [@czech2014reconstruction].

These works are not identical to causal-diamond kinematic space, but they are part of the same conceptual environment: reconstruct geometry by organizing and integrating over families of boundary regions or their associated extremal surfaces.

### 3.5 de Boer–Haehl–Heller–Myers: causal diamonds as kinematic space

The most direct source for **causal-diamond kinematic space** is the work of de Boer, Haehl, Heller, and Myers, “Entanglement, Holography and Causal Diamonds” [@deboer2016diamonds]. Their central move is to reorganize CFT data using observables living on the **moduli space of causal diamonds**, equivalently on the space of pairs of timelike-separated points.

For a $d$-dimensional conformal field theory, a causal diamond can be specified by two timelike-separated boundary points, its past and future tips. The corresponding moduli space has dimension $2d$, because each diamond is labeled by two $d$-dimensional points subject to causal separation. A point in this kinematic space is therefore not an event in the CFT spacetime; it is an entire causal diamond.

The paper constructs CFT observables by smearing local primary operators over causal diamonds. Entanglement entropy of vacuum perturbations and related higher-spin generalizations appear as examples of such diamond-associated observables [@deboer2016diamonds]. In holographic CFTs, these observables can be represented by integrals over bulk extremal surfaces associated with the boundary diamonds, connecting the construction back to Ryu–Takayanagi-type geometry.

A striking result is that small perturbations around the vacuum obey wave equations on the space of causal diamonds. In two-dimensional CFT, the causal-diamond kinematic space factorizes into two copies of two-dimensional de Sitter space; in higher dimensions, related de Sitter-like structures appear for ball-shaped regions and causal diamonds [@deboer2016desitter; @deboer2016diamonds].

This is established, not speculative: the moduli space of causal diamonds is a developed tool in CFT/holography. But its scope should be stated carefully. It is a kinematic and reconstructive framework for CFT observables, entanglement perturbations, OPE-related structures, and holographic extremal surfaces. It is not a claim that physical spacetime is replaced by this auxiliary space, nor that every spacetime theory has a fully dynamical causal-diamond kinematic space.

### 3.6 A current frontier: causal diamonds and OPE blocks

Recent work continues to use causal diamonds as organizing regions in conformal field theory. Haehl and Huang’s 2026 paper on Virasoro OPE blocks studies bilocal OPE-block structures using integrals over nested causal diamonds associated with timelike-separated insertions, with applications in higher-dimensional CFT [@haehl2026virasoro].

This belongs to the current frontier of the established CFT literature: causal diamonds remain useful as regions over which nonlocal operator data can be organized. It should not be confused with a causal-set dynamics of kinematic space. The 2026 work is a continuum CFT/OPE-block development, not evidence that a causal-set interval kinematic dynamics already exists.

## 4. Regions-as-points: the conceptual center

### 4.1 What changes mathematically

Replacing event-points with regions changes the mathematical viewpoint in at least four ways.

**First, the basic coordinates are relational.** A causal diamond is identified by two causally related endpoints. In continuum notation, a diamond is labeled by $(p,q)$ with $p\preceq q$. In a causal set, an interval is labeled by a comparable pair $(x,y)$ with $x\preceq y$. The auxiliary space therefore begins not as the original event set, but as a space of pairs satisfying a causal relation.

**Second, inclusion becomes geometric data.** Diamonds can nest: one diamond may lie inside another. They can overlap, share tips, share null boundaries, or intersect in complicated ways. In a kinematic-space viewpoint, these relations between regions can become adjacency, order, incidence, or measure relations in the auxiliary space.

**Third, size becomes a coordinate.** In CFT examples, the “scale” of a boundary interval or causal diamond is one of the natural coordinates of kinematic space. This is why kinematic space often has a Lorentzian signature: changing the center of an interval is different from changing its size. In the $\mathrm{AdS}_3/\mathrm{CFT}_2$ example, the space of boundary intervals is two-dimensional de Sitter space in the vacuum [@czech2015integral]. In the ball-region construction, the radius of the ball plays the role of an emergent de Sitter time coordinate [@deboer2016desitter].

**Fourth, fields can become nonlocal fields on region space.** OPE blocks, smeared operators, and entanglement perturbations are not ordinary point-local fields in the original spacetime. But they can behave like fields on the auxiliary space whose points are intervals or causal diamonds [@czech2016stereoscopic; @deboer2016diamonds].

### 4.2 Why this matters for holography

Holography is a theory of encoding. Boundary quantum data encode bulk gravitational data. But the encoding is not naturally organized event by event. Entanglement entropy is attached to regions; modular Hamiltonians are attached to regions; extremal surfaces are anchored to regions; entanglement wedges are associated with regions.

Kinematic space gives a way to organize this region-indexed data geometrically. In static $\mathrm{AdS}_3/\mathrm{CFT}_2$, the set of boundary intervals simultaneously labels bulk geodesics. This lets one translate questions about the bulk into questions about the space of intervals. Crofton-type formulas convert integrals over kinematic space into bulk lengths [@czech2015integral]. OPE-block constructions convert certain nonlocal CFT data into fields on kinematic space [@czech2016stereoscopic]. Causal-diamond constructions convert diamond-smeared observables and entanglement perturbations into wave equations on a moduli space of diamonds [@deboer2016diamonds].

The advantage is not merely linguistic. It changes what counts as local. An OPE block may be nonlocal in the original CFT spacetime, because it is associated with a pair of points or an interval. But it can be local as a field on kinematic space. A boundary interval is extended on the boundary, but it is a point in the auxiliary geometry. This is the precise sense in which kinematic space is a “geometry whose points are regions.”

### 4.3 Why this matters for causal structure

Causal diamonds are especially natural because their definition is causal from the start. They do not require choosing a preferred time slice. A diamond packages together duration, spatial extent, and causal accessibility.

This matters in relativity because causal order is not secondary. In many settings, causal structure strongly constrains the conformal geometry of spacetime. It matters in holography because boundary causal domains and bulk extremal surfaces are tightly related to entanglement and reconstruction. It matters in causal set theory because causal order is the fundamental structure, not a derived one.

The common thread is this: **causal diamonds are extended but not arbitrary**. They are extended regions selected by the causal order itself.

## 5. The discrete bridge: causal sets and finite intervals

### 5.1 What is established

Causal set theory offers a legitimate discrete setting for causal diamonds. Its basic object is a locally finite poset. The order relation encodes causal precedence, and local finiteness ensures that every order interval is finite [@bombelli1987spacetime; @surya2019causal].

Given comparable elements $x\preceq y$, the order interval

$$
[x,y]=\{z\in C\mid x\preceq z\preceq y\}
$$

is a finite causal diamond. It is the direct discrete counterpart of $J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$. The number of elements in the interval is a discrete volume proxy in manifoldlike causal sets [@surya2019causal]. Other combinatorial features of intervals—such as chains, links, and subinterval counts—are used throughout causal set theory to probe dimension, locality, action principles, and continuum approximation, although the details vary by construction.

There is also an established literature on recovering continuum topology from causal sets, including work using thickened antichains and related structures [@major2007recovering; @surya2008topology]. This shows that causal set theory already studies how continuum-like topological information may be recovered from order-theoretic data.

Classical sequential growth models provide an established stochastic dynamics for generating causal sets element by element under principles such as discrete general covariance and causality conditions [@rideout2000classical]. Surya’s review discusses these models as part of the broader causal set program [@surya2019causal].

### 5.2 What should not be overclaimed

The established causal set literature does **not**, by itself, establish a full “causal-set kinematic space” in the holographic sense. From any causal set $C$, one can certainly define the set of all comparable-pair intervals,

$$
\mathrm{Int}(C)=\{[x,y]\mid x,y\in C,\;x\preceq y\}.
$$

This is a natural mathematical object. It can be ordered by inclusion, decorated by cardinality, studied through overlaps, or represented as a combinatorial structure. But defining this object is not the same as showing that the causal set literature has developed a kinematic-space geometry with a metric, measure, field equations, reconstruction map, or dynamics analogous to the CFT/holographic constructions.

The responsible statement is narrower:

1. **Established:** causal set intervals are finite order intervals and are the discrete analogue of Alexandrov intervals.
2. **Established:** classical sequential growth dynamics generate causal sets under specific stochastic rules.
3. **Open or underexplored:** whether the induced collection of intervals in a growing causal set should itself be treated as a kinematic space with its own topology, order, metric, combinatorics, or dynamics.

This distinction is essential. The discrete bridge is real, but the fully developed bridge to holographic-style kinematic space remains a research opportunity, not an established field.

## 6. How the continuum and discrete pictures line up

The following comparison captures the legitimate common structure without overstating the analogy.

| Continuum Lorentzian geometry | Holographic/CFT kinematic space | Causal set theory |
|---|---|---|
| Event $p$ | Endpoint of interval or diamond | Element $x$ |
| Causal relation $p\preceq q$ | Pair of timelike-separated CFT points | Partial order $x\preceq y$ |
| Causal diamond $J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$ | Point in the moduli space of causal diamonds | Finite order interval $[x,y]$ |
| Volume of diamond | Entanglement-, measure-, or smearing-related data, depending on construction | Cardinality of interval as volume proxy in manifoldlike cases |
| Family of all diamonds | Kinematic space / moduli space | Mathematically definable set of all order intervals |
| Dynamics of spacetime or fields | Wave equations for certain observables on kinematic space in CFT examples | Sequential growth dynamics for causal sets; no established full interval-space dynamics |

The alignment is strongest at the level of order intervals. It is also strong in the continuum CFT setting, where causal-diamond kinematic space has been explicitly developed. It is weakest—and therefore most frontier-like—at the level of a dynamical causal-set kinematic space.

## 7. Frontier and open questions

### 7.1 Established results

Several statements can be made confidently from the existing literature.

**Causal diamonds are standard Lorentzian objects.** They are defined by causal futures and pasts and are central to the study of spacetime causal structure [@beem1996global; @kronheimer1967causal].

**Kinematic space is established in holography.** In the Czech–Lamprou–McCandlish–Sully line of work, the space of boundary intervals or bulk geodesics carries an auxiliary geometry whose measure is related to entanglement and integral geometry [@czech2015integral]. This framework supports bulk reconstruction and connects to tensor-network and OPE-block ideas [@czech2016tensor; @czech2016stereoscopic].

**Causal-diamond kinematic space is established in CFT/holography.** De Boer, Haehl, Heller, and Myers explicitly study observables on the moduli space of causal diamonds, equivalently pairs of timelike-separated points, and connect them to entanglement, OPE structures, and holographic extremal surfaces [@deboer2016diamonds].

**Causal set intervals are established discrete causal diamonds.** In a locally finite poset, order intervals are finite and play the role of Alexandrov intervals [@bombelli1987spacetime; @surya2019causal].

**Sequential growth dynamics exist for causal sets.** Rideout and Sorkin developed classical sequential growth models for causal sets [@rideout2000classical]. These are dynamics for causal sets themselves, not automatically a developed dynamics for the space of all intervals.

### 7.2 Underexplored directions

The following questions are natural, but they should be presented as open questions rather than established conclusions.

**What would it mean to build a kinematic space whose points are intervals in a growing causal set?**  
One could begin with $\mathrm{Int}(C)$, the set of all finite order intervals in a causal set $C$. But to call this a kinematic space in the stronger holographic sense, one would need to specify what additional structure it carries: inclusion order, overlap graph, interval cardinality, chain-count functions, a metric or distance, a topology, a measure, fields on intervals, or reconstruction maps back to the underlying causal set or to a continuum approximation.

**Does such a kinematic space itself grow under classical sequential growth dynamics?**  
A growing causal set automatically changes its set of intervals: adding a new element can create new intervals and enlarge old ones. But this induced change is not yet the same as a formulated sequential growth law for interval space. A real theory would need to specify the state space, transition rules, covariance conditions, and observables for the interval-space process, and then relate them to known causal set dynamics.

**Could one study topology, order, metric structure, or combinatorics of the space of causal diamonds?**  
Yes, as an open mathematical program. There are established tools for recovering topology from causal sets and for studying continuum kinematic spaces, but a systematic theory of topology, order, metric structure, or combinatorics on the interval space of arbitrary or dynamically grown causal sets is not, based on the cited literature, an established subfield.

**Could continuum causal-diamond kinematic space guide causal-set constructions?**  
Possibly, but cautiously. The continuum holographic constructions use entanglement entropy, conformal symmetry, OPE blocks, and extremal surfaces. A generic causal set does not come equipped with those structures. Any discrete analogue would have to identify which ingredients are order-theoretic and which depend on continuum QFT or holography.

### 7.3 Where the frontier currently sits

A careful frontier statement is therefore:

> The established literature supports causal diamonds in Lorentzian geometry, kinematic spaces of intervals/geodesics/diamonds in holography, and finite order intervals in causal set theory. It does not yet support the claim that there is a mature causal-set analogue of holographic causal-diamond kinematic space with its own accepted dynamics.

That is not a weakness of the idea. It is exactly where the boundary between established research and underexplored research lies.
