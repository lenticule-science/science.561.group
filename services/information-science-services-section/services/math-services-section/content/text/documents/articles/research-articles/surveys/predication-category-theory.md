# A Map of Predication Category Theory

*A territory map of the present body of mathematics, read carefully but with the understanding that the theory continues beyond its written form.*

This document is a map of what is there, not a complete theory. The corpus's own state-of-the-theory narrative records ~178 theorems, 95 examples, 58 questions, 69 narratives, and a substantial Python toolkit; what follows is an external reader's reconstruction of the architecture from the inside out, organized around what is structurally load-bearing.

The objects of central study — *predication fiber algebras*, *predication settlings*, the *bitopological settling site*, the *aperiodic classifying invariant*, the *four-regime dichotomy* — are not in the public mathematical literature. The closest neighbors (Belnap–Fitting bilattices, Jung–Rivieccio duality, hyperdoctrines, cellular sheaf cohomology, Anderson–Putnam direct limits, CRDT convergence) supply ingredients but not the combination.

## 0. Orientation

The theory is the categorical study of **bilattice- and quantale-valued predication structures**: predicate-categorical relation cells fibered over a small predicate category, refined into predication hyperdoctrines, dualized through Priestley correspondence onto bitopological sites, and stratified by the cohomology and moduli of the resulting *settling dynamics*. The driving application is **spatiotemporal knowledge** — modeling how relations between entities settle over space and time, with paraconsistent disagreement as first-class content rather than an error condition.

What makes the theory novel is not any single ingredient, but the *combination*: bilattice values (paraconsistency-native), settling dynamics (transfinite-temporal), bitopological / d-frame duals (pointfree spatial structure), and cohomological invariants of the resulting site. The four-regime classification of settling behavior — and the moduli theory built on top — is the corpus's distinctive content.

## 1. The base: $(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E, \lambda)$

Every construction begins from four parameters, organized into a 1-category $\mathbf{B} = \mathrm{ResLat} \times \mathrm{PredCat} \times \mathbf{Set} \times \mathbf{Ord}$ — the **parameter base category**:

- $\mathbf{L}$ — an integral commutative **residuated lattice**. The value domain.
- $\mathcal{C}$ — a small **predicate category** with finite limits. Objects are predicates; morphisms are generated by four canonical classes (see §3). The indexing of the theory.
- $E$ — an **entity set**. Elements are entities (subjects/objects of predication).
- $\lambda$ — an ordinal **settling bound**. The temporal axis.

The ordered triple $(s, p, o) \in E \times \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}) \times E$ is a **predication cell** — the atomic locus at which knowledge is held. Cells form the **predication cell set** $\Phi = E \times \Pi \times E$.

This four-tuple is enough to instantiate the entire algebraic apparatus that follows. A morphism in $\mathbf{B}$ is a 4-tuple $(\ell, e, \pi, \phi_\lambda)$ — a base change in each axis. The base category has finite limits, computed componentwise; it serves as the base of the settling moduli stack.

## 2. Bilattices and twist products: the value layer

The value algebra is the **twist product** $\mathbf{L}^{\bowtie}$ on the residuated lattice $\mathbf{L}$. Elements are pairs $\langle a_+, a_- \rangle \in L \times L$, where $a_+$ tracks **positive evidence** (information for an assertion) and $a_-$ tracks **negative evidence** (information against). Two orders coexist on the same carrier:

- **Truth order** $\leq_t$: raises positive, lowers negative. $\mathbf{f} = \langle 0, 1\rangle \leq_t \mathbf{t} = \langle 1, 0\rangle$.
- **Knowledge order** $\leq_k$: raises both. $\bot = \langle 0, 0\rangle \leq_k \top = \langle 1, 1\rangle$.

Specializing $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{2}$ recovers the four-element Belnap bilattice $\mathbf{FOUR} = \{\bot, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$. The four states are the canonical evidence configurations: no evidence, only-positive, only-negative, both (contradictory). The point $\top$ — "told both" — is *designated* rather than degenerate. This is the paraconsistent stance.

The twist product carries a **negation** $\sim\langle a, b\rangle = \langle b, a\rangle$ that swaps evidence kinds, a **conflation** $-$ when $\mathbf{L}$ is de Morgan, and a residuated **fusion–residual** pair $\odot \dashv \Rightarrow$. Restricted to consistent values $\{a_+ \wedge a_- = 0\}$, the residuated structure is exactly the $\otimes \dashv \multimap$ of classical multiplicative linear logic on the Chu space $\mathrm{Chu}(\mathbf{L}, 0)$. The twist product is therefore a **paraconsistent extension** of the Chu-space linear-logic model — the inconsistent point $\top$ sits outside the standard Chu space but is hosted as a legitimate value, without trivializing the multiplicatives.

## 3. The predication fiber algebra and the four generators

For each predicate $p \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$, the **predication fiber algebra** is the pointwise twist product over the entity-pair index:

$$A_p \;:=\; \mathbf{L}^{\bowtie\, E \times E}.$$

An element $v \in A_p$ assigns to every cell $(s, o)$ a four-state value $v(s, o) \in \mathbf{L}^{\bowtie}$. As an abstract algebra, $A_p$ is the same for every predicate; the subscript records which predicate's values are held.

The **predicate category** $\mathcal{C}$ has its morphisms generated by exactly **four classes**:

1. **Specialization** $f : q \to p$ — restriction of the cell-domain along an inclusion $\mathrm{dom}(q) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{dom}(p)$, with off-domain cells valued $\bot$.
2. **Converse** $f : p^c \to p$ — the swap $(s, o) \mapsto (o, s)$.
3. **Subject-projection** $(s, o) \mapsto (s, s)$ — diagonal collapse on the subject coordinate.
4. **Object-projection** $(s, o) \mapsto (o, o)$ — diagonal collapse on the object coordinate.

Under a $\mathrm{Sym}(E)$-equivariance hypothesis with $|E| \geq 3$, **predicate-generator exhaustiveness** asserts that these four classes generate every natural bilattice homomorphism between fiber algebras. This four-generator structure is constitutive — much of the theory is the tracking of these four classes' actions through every layer.

The assignment $p \mapsto A_p$ extends to a contravariant functor

$$F : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{DBiLat}(\mathbf{L}),$$

the **predication fiber functor**, into the category of distributive bilattices over $\mathbf{L}$. This is the static spatial structure on which the dynamics will run.

## 4. The hyperdoctrine refinement

The fiber functor's Grothendieck transpose is a cloven Grothendieck fibration $p : \mathcal{E} \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{C}$ — the **predication fibration** — whose fibers are canonically isomorphic to the $A_q$ and whose reindexings are bilattice-residuated homomorphisms preserving $\wedge_t, \vee_t, \wedge_k, \vee_k, \sim, \odot, \Rightarrow$ pointwise. Equivalently, by Emmenegger's lax-idempotent 2-comonad theorem, a predication fibration is a pseudo-coalgebra for the $\mathbb{N}$-comonad on $\mathbf{Cat}/\mathcal{C}$.

A **predication hyperdoctrine** is a predication fibration that additionally satisfies (B1)–(B4):

- **(B1)** Quantifier adjoints $\exists_f \dashv f^* \dashv \forall_f$ in *both* the knowledge and truth orders.
- **(B2)** Frobenius reciprocity for $\wedge_k$ and $\wedge_t$.
- **(B3)** Beck–Chevalley coherence on pullbacks, in both adjoint systems.
- **(B4)** Comprehension — extension functors $\mathrm{Ext}^t, \mathrm{Ext}^k : \mathcal{C}/p \to \mathcal{E}_p$ with right adjoints.

By Kelly–Lack property-like-ness, (B1)–(B4) are properties of a fibration, not added structure: a fibration either has them or doesn't.

**Two parallel adjoint systems.** Both $\leq_k$- and $\leq_t$-quantifier adjoints are present. This yields two derived equality predicates (classical and paraconsistent) and is structurally distinctive — no public hyperdoctrinal framework carries this dual adjoint architecture.

**Frobenius for $\odot$ fails.** The fusion $\odot$ has its monoidal unit at $\top$ (not $\mathbf{t}$) and absorber at $\mathbf{f}$ (not $\bot$); $\leq_k$-Frobenius for $\odot$ fails for three of the four generator classes. The residuation $\odot \dashv \Rightarrow$ survives at the fiber level but does not lift to a hyperdoctrinal Frobenius for the multiplicatives.

**Not a tripos.** The corpus has a deliberate narrative on this point. Triposes have Heyting fibers, preorder truncation, a generic predicate, and a 1-categorical base. Predication hyperdoctrines have distributive-bilattice-with-residuation fibers, *genuine 2-categorical fiber structure* (needed for the KZ-diamond of the temporal 2-category), no generic predicate, and a 2-categorical base. The tripos-to-topos construction does not directly apply. The cohomology proceeds through Priestley dualization, *because* the primal side is not a topos.

## 5. The settling: dynamics

A **predication valuation** is a function $v$ assigning to each predicate $p$ an element $v(p) \in A_p$. A **predication settling** is a transfinite family $v = (v_\tau)_{\tau < \lambda}$ of valuations satisfying **settling monotonicity**:

$$\sigma \leq \tau \;\Rightarrow\; v_\sigma(p) \leq_k v_\tau(p) \qquad \text{cellwise.}$$

Knowledge accumulates in $\leq_k$; nothing is retracted. Conflicts pile up at $\top$ rather than trivializing. Three associated regularity conditions are named:

- **Settling continuity:** at limits, $v_\mu(p) = \bigvee_k\{v_\sigma(p) : \sigma < \mu\}$.
- **Settling quiescence:** stabilization at some $\tau^*$ — values stop changing.
- **$\mathcal{C}$-naturality (predicate-category naturality):** $f^*(v_\tau(p)) = v_\tau(q)$ for every morphism $f : q \to p$. Pre-closure settlings need not satisfy this; a fully $\mathcal{C}$-closed settling is equivalently a functor $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \lambda \to \mathbf{DBiLat}_\ast(\mathbf{L})$.

A settling is **closure-driven** when each successor step takes the form $v_{\tau+1}(p) = \mathrm{cl}_p(v_\tau(p) \vee_k u_{\tau+1}(p))$, where $u_{\tau+1}$ is the **increment** (new evidence) and $\mathrm{cl}$ is a **closure operator**. The closure operator is generated by a **closure-rule system** $\mathcal{S}$ of conditional cell updates; rules are written in a formal **closure-rule language** with positive antecedents (single $\forall$-block over entities, possibly preceded by signature literals) and a single cell-update consequent.

## 6. The four-regime dichotomy

The most consequential structural fact of the theory. Every closure-driven settling has a **closure ordinal** $\kappa$ — the least ordinal at which $\mathrm{cl}$ stabilizes the orbit. The four regimes partition settlings by where $\kappa$ falls relative to $\omega$ and $\omega_1$:

| Regime | $\kappa$ | $H^1$ | Closure | KZ 2-cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Strict** | $< \omega$ | $0$ | strict-idempotent | trivial |
| **Scott-continuous** | $= \omega$ | $0$ | strict at $\omega$ | trivial at $\omega$ |
| **Transfinite-bounded** | $\omega < \kappa < \omega_1$ | non-zero, $\bigoplus \mathbb{Z}$ over NSC firings | genuinely lax-idempotent up to $\kappa$ | non-trivial in bounded range |
| **Aperiodic** | $\geq \omega_1$ | direct-limit of countable truncations | genuinely lax-idempotent throughout | non-trivial throughout |

Equivalence with combinatorial conditions:

- *Strict* iff $\mathbf{L}, E, \mathcal{C}$ are all finite.
- *Scott-continuous* iff $\mathrm{cl}$ is Scott-continuous and at least one of $\mathbf{L}, E, \mathcal{C}$ is infinite.
- *Transfinite-bounded* iff $\mathrm{cl}$ is non-Scott-continuous and the state-space cofinality is countable.
- *Aperiodic* iff $\mathrm{cl}$ is non-Scott-continuous and the state-space cofinality is $\geq \omega_1$.

The mathematical content lives in the *non-Scott-continuous firing ordinals* $L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\mathcal{S})$ — limit ordinals where $\mathrm{cl}(\bigvee_k v_\sigma) \neq \bigvee_k \mathrm{cl}(v_\sigma)$. By the transfinite-bounded $H^1$-generator theorem, *each non-Scott-continuous firing contributes one $\mathbb{Z}$-generator* to $H^1$. The aperiodic classifying invariant is the sum of these generators across all such firings. This is the corpus's analogue of the tile-substitution direct-limit cohomology of Anderson–Putnam, but indexed by closure-rule firings instead of tile-substitution iterations.

## 7. Priestley duality and the bitopological settling site

The whole structure dualizes. **Jung–Rivieccio duality** sends a distributive bilattice $A$ to a **paired Priestley space** $\mathrm{Spec}(A)$ — a pair of Priestley spaces with a swap involution corresponding to negation. Knowledge accumulation $\leq_k$ on the algebraic side corresponds to filter-tightening on the dual side: "knowledge grows" is dual to "possibility space shrinks."

The **bitopological settling site** $(\mathbf{T}^*, J)$ assembles the dualized fibers across the predicate category and the settling axis:

- **Objects:** triples $(q, \tau, U)$ with $q \in \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$, $\tau < \lambda$, and $U$ a pairwise-Stone clopen of $X_q^\tau = \mathrm{Spec}(A_q)$ at stage $\tau$.
- **Morphisms** $(q', \tau', U') \to (q, \tau, U)$: $\mathcal{C}$-morphism + ordinal advancement + pairwise-Stone clopen inclusion.
- **Topology $J$:** generated by pairwise-Stone covers at fixed $(q, \tau)$ — fiber-level only, no temporal covers.

Cross-ordinal cohomological content is extracted at the cohomology level via direct limits along settling morphisms, not via covers in the topology. The site is 1-truncated as a category but the underlying **temporal 2-category** $\mathbf{T}(\mathcal{C}, \lambda)$ carries non-trivial KZ-2-cells in the transfinite regimes, requiring the **KZ-coherent sheaf** descent condition to handle them at the sheaf level.

## 8. Cohomology — and four parallel frameworks

This is where the architecture is genuinely incomplete and the corpus is honest about it. There are **four cohomological frameworks** running in parallel; they should agree where they overlap; **agreement has been verified concretely in only one case**.

### Framework 1 — Substitution-matrix Anderson–Putnam (computational)
Replace the underlying paired-Priestley space with a substitution dynamical system's tile space; compute classical Čech cohomology of the Anderson–Putnam direct limit $\Omega_\omega$. Implementation in Python. Coverage: a benchmark suite of seven substitutions (Fibonacci, Tribonacci, Tetranacci, Padovan, Thue–Morse, …). Status: working.

### Framework 2 — Tarski-Laplacian on discrete-points fiber (proved within scope)
Treat each fiber as a 0-dimensional discrete topological space; cochain group $C^0$ is the product of stalk values, $C^n = 0$ for $n \geq 1$. Value category: d-frames. Coverage: the **strict-regime and Scott-continuous-regime** d-frame cohomology theorems are proved end-to-end here. Does *not* extend to the transfinite regimes.

### Framework 3 — Cellular sheaf on order complex of clopen lattice (Q-enriched, parallel-pair)
Take the order complex of the lattice of pairwise-Stone clopens of $X_q^\tau$. For the Belnap fiber, this is $\Delta^1$. Compute cellular sheaf cohomology in the parallel-pair convention; Curry equivalence on the cosheaf side via Lopez 2024 fuzzy adjoints. Coverage: per-fiber Curry on graphs and 2-dim degree-0 — proved; CW-complex extension and Grothendieck-site assembly — sketch.

### Framework 4 — Lawvere-Laplacian on Grothendieck-site (Q-enriched, weighted-meet)
Compute Q-enriched cohomology with weighted-meet coboundary directly on the Grothendieck site, without per-fiber factoring. Specializes to Lawvere-Laplacian fixed-point cohomology in degree 0. Status: scaffolding only; not concretely computed.

**The agreement question.** Frameworks 2/3/4 give the same headline results on overlapping spaces by *structural argument*, not by computational verification. The strong-MLD theorem and the higher-degree-vanishing theorem implicitly assume framework agreement. Only one cross-framework agreement is concretely verified: Framework 2 ↔ Framework 3 on the standard Belnap fiber, $H^0$ component, via a five-pass worked example. Frameworks 1 ↔ 2, 1 ↔ 3, 3 ↔ 4 — none has a single verified concrete agreement.

This is the **largest unresolved tension in the theory**.

## 9. Moduli, automorphisms, classifying invariants

Settlings over a fixed base form a 2-category, with three distinct morphism notions:

- **Predication-settling preorder morphism** — same-base $\leq_k$-pointwise inequality. Hom-sets singletons or empty. The morphism class of the **predication settling category** $\mathbf{PS}(\mathbf{L}, \mathcal{C}, E, \lambda)$, which has all small limits and colimits (computed pointwise), with $\mathbf{0}$ = constant-$\bot$ initial and $\mathbf{1}$ = constant-$\top$ terminal.
- **Predication-settling base-morphism** — cross-base, with bilattice-hom families, residuated-lattice homomorphism, entity map, predicate-category functor, ordinal map.
- **Settling-morphism** — same-base strict notion: state *equality* with ordinal shift, plus settling-endofunctor commutation. The 1-cells of the 2-category whose biequivalences classify aperiodic settlings.

### Equivalence hierarchy

$$\text{strict iso} \;\subsetneq\; \text{biequivalence} \;=\; \text{MLD} \;\subseteq\; \text{cohomological}.$$

- **Strict isomorphism** — singletons; degenerate.
- **Biequivalence in the 2-category** = **mutually-locally-derivable (MLD)** equivalence. Captures settling-shift and structural closure-pattern matching, à la Sadun's MLD for substitution tilings.
- **Cohomological equivalence** — sheaf-iso on $\mathcal{F}_U$ with ordinal shift. Always implied by biequivalence; the reverse holds *regime by regime* (biequivalence-cohomological collapse).

### Visible vs invisible automorphisms

For each settling $v$, the automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}^\simeq(v)$ acts on the settling-associated sheaf; the kernel of that action is the **invisible automorphism subgroup** $N(v)$. The quotient $\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{vis}}(v) := \mathrm{Aut}^\simeq(v) / N(v)$ is the **visible automorphism quotient** — the cohomologically-meaningful part.

Moduli points stratify accordingly:
- **Rigid** — $\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{vis}}_{\mathrm{nat}} = \{1\}$. The transfinite-induction settling on $E = \omega_1$ is rigid.
- **Orbifold** — $\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{vis}}_{\mathrm{nat}} \neq \{1\}$. Sources: fiber-swap, entity-permutation stabilizer, predicate-automorphism stabilizer.

The **settling moduli prestack** lives over the parameter base $\mathbf{B}$ with its Grothendieck topology; the moduli stack theorem assembles the per-stratum data.

### Classifying invariants

For aperiodic settlings, the **aperiodic classifying invariant**

$$\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}(v) \;=\; [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{F}_U}] \;\in\; H^1(\mathbf{T}^*_{\leq \kappa}, \mathcal{F}_U)$$

is a *complete* invariant for MLD-equivalence (per the aperiodic-regime classification theorem). Settlings $v \sim_{\mathrm{MLD}} v'$ iff their classes agree.

For substitution-recurrence settlings, the classifying invariant refines via the **step-$n$ parameter** to a $\mathbb{Z}^n$-valued **step-$n$ classifying invariant** of rank exactly $n$ — the post-quotient Anderson–Putnam Čech rank.

## 10. Symmetry: Klein-four and internal $\mathbb{Z}/2$ gauge

Negation $\sim$ and conflation $-$ generate a **Klein-four group** $K_4 = \langle \sim, - \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$. The four elements act distinctly: $\mathrm{id}$, $\sim$ (preserves $\leq_k$, reverses $\leq_t$), $-$ (preserves $\leq_t$, reverses $\leq_k$), $\sim \circ -$ (reverses both). Including the order-swap involution extends to a dihedral $D_4$.

The non-trivial element of the **internal $\mathbb{Z}/2$ gauge** is $\sim$ alone. On the Priestley dual it realizes as the swap exchanging the $+$- and $-$-components of the paired space. The gauge fixes cell positions and acts only on values, so it is automatically in the invisible automorphism subgroup $N(v)$ whenever it stabilizes $v$ — gauge does not contribute to visible automorphism for the basic $\mathcal{F}_U$ sheaves.

The Klein-four orbits on $\mathbf{FOUR}$:
- $\{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}\}$ — 2-orbit under $\sim$, fixed by $-$.
- $\{\bot, \top\}$ — 2-orbit under $-$, fixed by $\sim$.

Each orbit pairs the *extremes of one order* with the *incomparable middle of the other*.

## 11. The realizability program

The moduli classifying function $\mathcal{I}_B$ is injective on each regime stratum; **realizability** asks which classes are realized by some MLD-class. The program decomposes along two layers:

### First layer — regime decomposition

- *Strict & Scott-continuous regimes:* $H^1 = 0$. $\mathcal{I}_B$ is a bijection onto the $H^0$ image; realizability is essentially trivial.
- *Transfinite-bounded & aperiodic regimes:* the realizable $L_{\mathrm{ns}}$-subsets are exactly the **initial segments** of countable limit ordinals, in the bare $\Pi_1$+$<$ rule language.

### Second layer — three orthogonal axes inside the non-trivial regimes

1. **Bigraded value-type axis** — $\mathbf{t}$-cells (truth-side) vs $\top$-cells (inconsistency-side). On discrete $\mathcal{C}$, the two are independently realizable as a 2-parameter family of pairs of initial segments.
2. **$\mathcal{C}$-naturality coupling axis** — when $\mathcal{C}$ has non-trivial morphisms, each of the four predicate generators (specialization, converse, subject-projection, object-projection) imposes its own coupling on per-predicate firing patterns.
3. **Rule-language fragment axis** — bare $\Pi_1$+$<$, named-ordinal entity-equality, ordinal arithmetic, $\Pi_2$-schemas, second-order. Each enrichment has its own realizability characterization; only the lower fragments are formally proved.

The realizability cardinality bound caps everything at $2^{\aleph_0}$; the per-regime and per-axis theorems give tighter characterizations within scope. Once realizability is fully characterized, $\mathcal{I}_B$ becomes a bijection onto a structurally explicit image — the moduli classification is then complete on each stratum.

This is the **cleanest research-program structure** in the corpus: a layered set of progressively finer invariants, each with its own theorem set, organized around extending the rule-language fragment.

## 12. The step-$n$ structural unifier

A genuinely surprising structural feature: the corpus has an internal **step-$n$ hierarchy** in which the same parameter $n \geq 2$ controls *multiple* seemingly-independent quantities, all incrementing together:

| Level | Quantity at step-$n$ |
|---|---|
| Substitution recurrence | $n$-term recurrence |
| Substitution alphabet | $n$ letters |
| Cohomological rank | rank-$n$ Anderson–Putnam invariant |
| Growth-rate algebraic degree | degree-$n$ algebraic number (Pisot-type) |
| Rule-language fragment depth | $\Pi_{n-1}$ |
| Bilattice-structure correspondence | $n$-element bilattice substructure |

Concrete instances:
- $n = 2$ (Fibonacci, $a \mapsto ab, b \mapsto a$): 2-term recurrence, growth-rate $\phi$, rank-$2$ cohomology, alphabet ↔ $\{\leq_k, \leq_t\}$.
- $n = 3$ (Padovan): 3-term recurrence, growth-rate plastic number $\rho$, rank-$3$, alphabet ↔ $\{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$.
- $n = 4$ (Tetranacci): 4-term recurrence, rank-$4$, alphabet ↔ full bilattice $\{\bot, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$.

Some of the propagation is inherent to substitution-matrix theory (Perron–Frobenius). What is *distinctive* to the corpus is the further "+1" propagation to the bilattice-structure alignment and (speculatively) the closure-monad coherence depth. The step-$n$ parameter is the corpus's first internal *renormalization-style* structural parameter; the step-shift morphism (forthcoming) is intended to act as RG flow on the parameterized family.

## 13. The distributed-systems and agentic strand

A second-order layer reads the apparatus through distributed-systems theory:

- **CRDT convergence theorem** — the predication-settling preorder-morphism $\vee_k$-merge is the structural CRDT operation. Convergence is proved.
- **Replica covers and Mayer–Vietoris** — under Jung–Rivieccio duality, knowledge-merge $v_1 \vee_k v_2$ corresponds to clopen-union $U_1 \cup U_2$. Two replicas form a 2-element pairwise-Stone cover; the **replica Mayer–Vietoris sequence** is the cohomological face of CRDT merge.
- **Cohomological CALM** — the obstruction-vanishing reformulation of the Hellerstein–Alvaro CALM theorem (consistency without coordination iff monotone).
- **Network-sheaf-on-agent-graph** specialization — the corpus's CRDT/MV/CALM trio specialized to $O = \hat{G}$ for an agent-graph $G$. Five-claim package proved.
- **Bilattice extension of network-sheaf consensus** — the d-frame-valued analog. Six-claim package proved, with paraconsistency-from-disagreement captured natively at the d-frame level via $\mathrm{con}$-failure at $\top$-witness cells.
- **Hansen–Ghrist spectral engagement** — a structural finding: the bilattice / d-frame value category *cannot* host the spectral-Laplacian apparatus of Hansen–Ghrist 2019. No canonical inner product on $\mathbf{B}$, no canonical adjoint of d-frame morphisms, no $\Delta_F$-style operator on heterogeneous d-frame components. Both sides agree on the consensus space ($\Gamma = \ker L_F = H^0$), but characterize it via complementary, structurally non-interchangeable machinery.
- **Felber task-sheaf d-frame translation** — Felber's task sheaves and the corpus's settling-associated sheaves share $\mathbf{Frm}_\vee$ as a common value category, opening a unified-distributed-systems-cohomology direction.

This strand is the corpus's most rigorously-grounded recent expansion — every theorem reduces componentwise to either the proved CRDT machinery or the proved abelian-shadow theorems, with d-frame extension via the proved d-frame cohomology comparison theorem. No new sketch dependencies were introduced.

Each of these is a chapter that *consumes* the central apparatus:

## 16. The conceptual frontier — where the explanation ends

The user emphasized that what is written is *not the complete theory* but only the start of an explanation. Reading the corpus carefully, the frontier of what is named-but-not-developed sits in several places:

1. **Above hyperdoctrines.** The theory deliberately distinguishes itself from triposes and from Lawvere–Seely hyperdoctrines. What sits *above* the predication hyperdoctrine in the hierarchy of categorical doctrines — i.e., what 2-monad on $\mathbf{Cat}/\mathcal{C}$ has predication hyperdoctrines as its pseudo-algebras — is not given. The Emmenegger fibration 2-comonad describes the underlying fibration; the additional structure (B1)–(B4) and its dual adjoint architecture is property-like, but the full doctrine has no name yet.

2. **The dual adjoint system.** The presence of *both* $\leq_k$- and $\leq_t$-hyperdoctrinal structure is structurally distinctive. The two comprehension schemas yield two derived equality predicates. This dual architecture appears nowhere in the public hyperdoctrine literature — and the corpus has named it but not exhausted its consequences. What would a "dual hyperdoctrine" be in the abstract? The corpus does not yet say.

3. **The KZ-diamond.** The temporal 2-category $\mathbf{T}(\mathcal{C}, \lambda)$ carries two KZ-lax-idempotent structures: $\mathbb{M} \dashv \mathbb{N}$ from Emmenegger and $\mathrm{Cl}$ from the closure 2-monad. The "two disentangled KZ structures" narrative records the existence of both; the *interaction* of the two — and what the diamond's universal property is — remains outside the present layer.

4. **Beyond $H^1$.** Settling cohomology vanishes above degree 1. The "exceptional higher cohomology" question asks when this might fail (e.g., on richer value categories or richer sites). The corpus's $H^1$ classification is complete on its scope; what would $H^2$ classify if it were non-zero is genuinely open.

5. **Continuum / Lorentzian limits.** The "continuum-Lorentzian limit" question asks how the discrete-ordinal settling structure might admit a continuum limit; whether the apex / void / cone structure of paraconsistent variants admits a Lorentzian-causal interpretation. These are gestured at in narratives; the math is not in place.

6. **Probabilistic and continuous-$L$ extensions.** The base parameter $\mathbf{L}$ being an integral commutative residuated lattice is general but discrete-flavored; passing to continuous-valued or measure-theoretic $\mathbf{L}$ is named as a question, not pursued.

7. **Higher arity.** Cells are $E \times E$ — binary. $n$-ary predications, with cells $E^n$, are anticipated but the four-generator structure does not directly transfer.

8. **Six-functor formalism.** A six-functor formalism for settling cohomology would assemble the various pushforward / pullback / tensor / hom adjunctions into a Grothendieck-style package. The pieces are present (the predication fiber functor, the dualized closure 2-comonad, the bitopological-site morphisms), but the assembly is open.

9. **Realizability of cohomology classes — full characterization.** The realizability program's higher fragments and cross-axis interactions remain the corpus's largest *internally-named* open frontier.

10. **The agentic-swarm direction.** The recent network-sheaf-on-agent-graph and bilattice-extension theorems are the corpus's bridge to multi-agent / distributed-systems mathematics. Five further directions (Brewka–Eiter multi-context-systems, Halpern–Moses common knowledge, Dung argumentation, Sznitman mean-field, Fagin–Halpern–Moses–Vardi epistemic logic) are positioned but not pursued in depth.
