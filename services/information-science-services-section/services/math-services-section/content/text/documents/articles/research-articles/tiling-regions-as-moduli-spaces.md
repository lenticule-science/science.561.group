# Tilings and covers of causal-diamond / region-moduli spaces

## Executive finding

The guess that tiling would be "even more unexplored" is only partly right.

If the target is a **general theory of tilings of moduli spaces whose points are causal diamonds, black-hole regions, causal-set regions, or compact Lorentzian diamonds**, then the literature is sparse and fragmented. I did not find an established field with that name, nor a general-purpose theory parallel to ordinary tilings of manifolds, triangulations, or CW decompositions.

But if the target includes **AdS\(_3\)/CFT\(_2\) kinematic space**, where a point of the kinematic space represents a boundary interval, oriented bulk geodesic, or causal-diamond datum, then tiling is **not empty**. There is a small but real literature on:

- MERA and tensor-network discretizations of kinematic space [@czech2016tensor];
- causal patterns in kinematic space made of causal diamonds [@levay2020scanning; @levay2021associahedron];
- associahedra, Tamari lattices, and cluster mutations organizing those patterns [@levay2020scanning; @levay2021associahedron; @arkani2019causal];
- BTZ black-hole analogues where entanglement patterns are organized by a cyclohedron and a type-\(C\) cluster algebra [@boldis2022btz];
- fundamental regions in the kinematic spaces of BTZ black holes and multiboundary wormholes [@zhang2017kinematic].

The strongest conclusion is therefore:

> **Tiling/cellular-cover ideas already exist for kinematic space, especially in the AdS\(_3\)/CFT\(_2\) entanglement-pattern literature. What appears genuinely missing is a sheaf-Hodge or Hansen--Ghrist-style cellular sheaf Laplacian placed on those tilings/covers, with spectral theory interpreted as spectral geometry of the moduli of regions.**

That is a narrower but more interesting gap than "nobody has tiled such spaces." The better statement is: **the tiling thread exists in a small holographic/cluster-algebra corner, while the sheaf-Laplacian-on-that-tiling thread does not appear to have been developed.**

## What counts as "tiling in this space"?

There are at least four different meanings that have to be kept separate.

First, one can tile **ordinary spacetime** with causal diamonds, null polytopes, Alexandrov intervals, or related causal cells. That is not the main target here, because the base space is still spacetime itself.

Second, one can tile **kinematic space**, especially AdS\(_3\)/CFT\(_2\) kinematic space. This is much closer to the target. In this setting, a point of kinematic space represents a boundary interval, an oriented geodesic, or a causal-diamond datum. A tiling of kinematic space is therefore a tiling of a space whose points are themselves extended region-like objects.

Third, one can build a **cover, site, Grothendieck topology, or domain-of-dependence structure** on regions. This is not always called a tiling, but it is adjacent to tiling because it describes how region-data glue.

Fourth, one can build a **cell complex, graph, exchange graph, associahedron, cyclohedron, Tamari lattice, or cluster polytope** whose vertices or cells encode region-patterns. This is not always a geometric tiling in the Euclidean sense, but it is often the right combinatorial object for spectral or sheaf-theoretic constructions.

The positive literature mostly occupies the second and fourth meanings. The sheaf-theoretic near-misses mostly occupy the third. The older quantum-gravity discretization literature mostly occupies the first.

## 1. The central positive case: kinematic-space tilings and causal patterns

The strongest region-moduli base space is **kinematic space**. In holography, kinematic space is the space of boundary intervals or, equivalently in AdS\(_3\), oriented bulk geodesics. In a broader conformal-field-theory setting, de Boer, Haehl, Heller, and Myers explicitly describe observables on the **moduli space of causal diamonds**, or equivalently the space of timelike-separated point pairs [@deboer2016entanglement]. This gives a well-defined sense in which the base points are not point-events but extended causal regions.

Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, and Sully then argue that MERA should be viewed not as a discretization of the bulk geometry itself, but as a discretization of **kinematic space**, i.e. the space of bulk geodesics / CFT intervals [@czech2016tensor]. This is an important near-tiling result: it does not necessarily give a tile set in the classical tessellation sense, but it gives a discrete network model for the region-moduli space.

The most direct tiling-like result comes from Lévay and Boldis. In the AdS\(_3\)/CFT\(_2\) setting, they study how boundary entanglement patterns correspond to triangulations of geodesic \(N\)-gons. These triangulations are mapped to **causal patterns in kinematic space**, and those causal patterns are made from causal diamonds [@levay2020scanning]. For fixed \(N\), the family of all such causal patterns is related to the associahedron \(K_{N-3}\), with cluster mutations giving a dynamics on the patterns.

Lévay's later note makes the same structure especially explicit: AdS\(_3\) kinematic space \(K\) is the space of geodesics and can be identified with \(dS_2\) equipped with the Crofton form; triangulations of geodesic polygons give causal patterns of \(K\) made of causal diamonds; the labels on those diamonds encode conditional mutual information; and the fixed-\(N\) space of causal patterns is related to the associahedron [@levay2021associahedron].

This is the closest existing literature to "tiling in the space of causal diamonds." The tiles are not causal diamonds of the original spacetime. Rather, they are causal diamonds **inside kinematic space**. Since points of kinematic space already encode boundary intervals/geodesics/causal-diamond data, this is a second-order tiling: causal diamonds in a moduli space of causal-diamond-like objects.

## 2. Cluster polytopes, Tamari lattices, and the combinatorics of the tilings

The Lévay--Boldis story also shows that these tilings are not just pictorial decompositions. They come with a nontrivial combinatorial and algebraic organization.

For fixed \(N\), the different causal patterns form a finite family related to the associahedron \(K_{N-3}\) [@levay2020scanning; @levay2021associahedron]. The partial order on these patterns is related to the Tamari lattice, and moves between patterns are given by cluster mutations. The region labels are tied to conditional mutual information, so the cells/diamonds are not merely geometric; they carry entanglement-theoretic content.

Arkani-Hamed, He, Salvatori, and Thomas provide a neighboring amplitudes/cluster-polytopes perspective. Their paper on causal diamonds, cluster polytopes, and scattering amplitudes abstracts a causal evolution or "walk" in a kinematic spacetime and connects it to finite-type cluster polytopes [@arkani2019causal]. This is not primarily a tiling paper about causal-diamond moduli spaces, but it reinforces the existence of a broader cluster-polytope/casual-diamond/kinematic-space interface.

For the question at hand, the key lesson is that the available "tiling" objects are likely to be **combinatorial tilings/cellulations/exchange graphs**, not merely periodic tessellations. The natural base complexes for further work would be associahedra, Tamari lattices, cluster exchange graphs, cyclohedra, or nerves of causal-diamond covers.

## 3. Black-hole and wormhole kinematic-space cases

The black-hole version is also not empty, but it is narrower than the AdS\(_3\) vacuum case.

Zhang and Chen study kinematic space for BTZ black holes and multiboundary wormholes. They emphasize that a point in kinematic space corresponds to a geodesic / causal-diamond datum, that hyperbolic transformations defining BTZ black holes correspond to timelike geodesics in kinematic space, and that one can discuss **fundamental regions in the kinematic space** for BTZ black holes and multiboundary wormholes [@zhang2017kinematic]. This is a genuine decomposition-of-kinematic-space result with black-hole/wormhole content.

Boldis and Lévay give a more algebraic BTZ-specific analogue of the entanglement-pattern story. For a thermal CFT dual to a high-temperature static BTZ black hole, they partition the boundary into \(N\) subsystems and find an underlying type-\(C_{N-1}\) cluster algebra. The fixed-\(N\) polytope organizing the patterns is the cyclohedron \(\mathcal C_{N-1}\), and the patterns can be represented in BTZ kinematic space using a Zamolodchikov \(Y\)-system with boundary conditions involving the BTZ entropy [@boldis2022btz].

This gives a meaningful black-hole-adjacent answer: there is a small literature on **black-hole kinematic-space patterning/decomposition**, especially for BTZ. However, I did not find an analogous theory for general black-hole interior regions as points, nor for a moduli space whose points are entire black-hole regions in a Wheeler-superspace-like sense.

The positive black-hole result is therefore specific:

> **BTZ kinematic space has fundamental-region and cyclohedron/cluster-pattern literature. General black-hole-region moduli tilings do not appear to be an established topic.**

## 4. de Sitter causal-diamond tessellations: close, but not exactly the same target

Niermann and Osborne introduce holographic tensor networks associated with tilings of \((1+1)\)-dimensional de Sitter spacetime [@niermann2022holographic]. Their construction uses causal-diamond tessellations and connects the resulting network capacity to the cosmological constant.

This is close for two reasons. First, pure AdS\(_3\) kinematic space is itself \(dS_2\). Second, the tiles in the Niermann--Osborne construction are causal diamonds. So at the level of raw geometry, their de Sitter tilings look relevant to tilings of kinematic space.

But the intent is different. Niermann and Osborne are primarily tiling de Sitter spacetime as a spacetime, not explicitly treating it as a moduli space of intervals or causal diamonds. Thus this is best classified as a **transferable near-hit** rather than a direct instance of tiling a region-moduli space.

Still, if one wanted to build a discrete base for sheaf-Hodge theory over AdS\(_3\) kinematic space, these de Sitter causal-diamond tessellations would be among the natural geometric candidates.

## 5. Spacetime tilings by null cells and causal diamonds: nearby but not the target

There is also a separate literature on causal or null tilings of spacetime itself. Neiman's "causal cells" paper studies spacetime polytopes with null hyperfaces, including null-faced 4-polytopes and a "most regular" 4-parallelotope whose copies tile spacetime as a lattice with null edges [@neiman2012causal].

This is relevant as causal discretization technology, but it is not a tiling of a moduli space whose points are regions. The base is still spacetime. It might supply inspiration for a second-level construction, but it is not itself that construction.

Causal-set and Alexandrov-interval literature gives another near miss. In causal-set theory, Alexandrov intervals are central order-theoretic regions, and one can consider covers or nerves built from such intervals [@surya2019causal]. Rideout's thesis also cautions that covering causal sets with Alexandrov sets does not behave like ordinary spatial tiling, because the sets can be extremely null-shaped and do not cover regions in the intuitive Euclidean way [@rideout2002dynamics].

This matters because it prevents an overly naive translation from Euclidean tiling to Lorentzian/causal tiling. In causal geometry, a "tile" may be better modeled as a cover element, interval, order ideal/filter intersection, nerve simplex, exchange-graph vertex, or site covering family rather than as a rigid polygonal tile.

## 6. Region covers, causal sites, and sheaf-adjacent structures

Christensen and Crane's causal sites are important because they replace point-set spacetime with a region-based categorical structure [@christensen2005causal]. This is not tiling in the geometric sense, but it is a framework where regions and their causal/inclusion relations are primitive. It is therefore a plausible base for cover, nerve, or sheaf constructions.

A more recent and very relevant development is Heunen and van der Schaaf's work on **causal coverage** in ordered locales and spacetimes [@heunen2025causal]. They introduce a point-free/topological notion of causal coverage, connect causal coverages to generalized Grothendieck topologies, and interpret the sheaf condition as a kind of deterministic time evolution.

This is the strongest sheaf-adjacent tiling/covers result I found. But it is still not a Hansen--Ghrist-style cellular sheaf Laplacian. It gives a site/coverage/sheaf-condition framework for causal structures, not a cellular Hodge Laplacian on a tiling of kinematic space.

So the sheaf side currently looks like this:

- causal sites and causal coverages give region-based cover structures [@christensen2005causal; @heunen2025causal];
- cellular sheaf Laplacians exist for sheaves on regular cell complexes [@hansen2019spectral];
- persistent sheaf Laplacians extend the idea to filtrations/point-cloud settings [@wei2025persistent];
- but I found no source that takes a kinematic-space causal-pattern tiling, associahedron, cyclohedron, BTZ fundamental-region decomposition, or causal-site coverage and defines a cellular sheaf Laplacian on it.

## 7. Compact causal-diamond spaces as a mathematical base

Barrera, Montes de Oca, and Solis study the space of compact causal diamonds in Lorentzian length spaces and endow such a space with Lorentzian length-space structure [@barrera2024compact]. This is very close to the earlier "regions as points" question: it is a mathematically explicit space whose points are causal diamonds.

For the present tiling question, however, I found no follow-up literature on tilings, tessellations, nerves, cellular decompositions, or sheaf Laplacians of this compact-diamond space. This looks like one of the most direct mathematical bases for future work, precisely because it is not merely a physics heuristic: it is a defined geometric space of causal diamonds.

A possible research bridge would be:

1. start with a Lorentzian length space;
2. form its compact-diamond space;
3. construct finite covers, nerves, or cellular approximations of that diamond space;
4. put sheaves of geometric, causal, quantum, or entropy data over those covers;
5. study the resulting cellular sheaf Laplacian.

The literature I found establishes step 2 and establishes general technology for step 5, but not the combined construction.

## 8. Sheaf/cellular Laplacians: what is already available and what is missing

Hansen and Ghrist define a spectral theory of cellular sheaves by lifting the graph Laplacian to a Hodge Laplacian on cellular sheaves over regular cell complexes [@hansen2019spectral]. This gives the formal machinery one would need once a tiling/cell complex of a region-moduli space has been chosen.

Persistent sheaf Laplacians extend this machinery to filtrations and data settings, where spectra can encode both geometric and non-geometric labels [@wei2025persistent]. This is suggestive for region-moduli spaces because causal diamonds, boundary intervals, and black-hole kinematic sectors naturally carry non-geometric labels: entanglement entropy, conditional mutual information, OPE-block data, charges, fluxes, Hilbert-space dimensions, or modular-Hamiltonian data.

But the search did not turn up a paper doing any of the following:

- a Hansen--Ghrist cellular sheaf Laplacian on Lévay--Boldis causal patterns;
- a sheaf Laplacian on the associahedron/Tamari lattice of kinematic-space causal patterns;
- a sheaf Laplacian on the BTZ cyclohedron or type-\(C\) cluster exchange graph;
- a sheaf Laplacian on Zhang--Chen BTZ/wormhole kinematic-space fundamental regions;
- a sheaf Laplacian on Niermann--Osborne de Sitter causal-diamond tessellations;
- a sheaf Laplacian on causal sites or causal coverages;
- a sheaf Laplacian on Barrera--Montes de Oca--Solis compact-diamond spaces.

This negative result should be read carefully. It is not a proof of nonexistence. It is a literature-search result: the natural keywords, known references, and citation-neighborhoods do not reveal an established construction.

## 10. What seems genuinely unexplored

The following research directions appear genuinely underdeveloped rather than merely renamed established fields.

### 10.1 Cellular sheaves on kinematic-space tilings

The Lévay--Boldis causal patterns and associahedra are natural finite combinatorial bases. A cellular sheaf could assign entanglement, conditional mutual information, OPE-block, or Crofton-form data to the cells and encode compatibility under cluster mutations or causal-pattern refinements.

The missing object would be a sheaf Laplacian whose spectrum measures coherence, obstruction, synchronization, or diffusion of those data across the causal-pattern tiling.

### 10.2 Sheaf Laplacians on BTZ cyclohedra and black-hole kinematic sectors

The BTZ cyclohedron construction is especially promising because it already contains black-hole entropy in the boundary condition of the relevant \(Y\)-system [@boldis2022btz]. A sheaf-theoretic version could try to encode how black-hole entropy behaves as a boundary condition or defect in a sheaf over the cyclohedron/exchange graph.

I found no existing paper doing this.

### 10.3 Nerves of causal-diamond covers

Given a covering of a kinematic space or compact-diamond space by causal diamonds, one can form a nerve. This would produce a simplicial complex suitable for cellular sheaves. The idea is very natural from the perspective of topological data analysis and sheaf theory, but I found no established construction specialized to causal-diamond moduli spaces.

### 10.4 Causal coverage plus Hodge theory

Heunen and van der Schaaf's causal coverage framework connects causality, sites, Grothendieck topology, and sheaf conditions [@heunen2025causal]. Hansen--Ghrist spectral sheaf theory supplies Laplacians for cellular sheaves [@hansen2019spectral]. The obvious missing bridge is a discrete/cellular approximation of causal coverage that supports a Hodge Laplacian.

This looks like a clean mathematical research seam.

### 10.5 Compact-diamond-space tilings

Barrera--Montes de Oca--Solis provide a geometric space of compact causal diamonds [@barrera2024compact]. I found no tiling theory, cellular approximation theory, or sheaf-Laplacian theory on that space. This is likely the most direct purely mathematical gap.

## 11. Concise answer to the original hunch

The answer is:

> **Tiling is less unexplored than expected in AdS\(_3\)/CFT\(_2\) kinematic space, because there is real work on causal-diamond patterns, associahedra, cyclohedra, cluster mutations, MERA discretizations, and black-hole/wormhole fundamental regions. But tiling of general causal-diamond or black-hole-region moduli spaces is not a broad established field, and I found no literature putting Hansen--Ghrist-style cellular sheaf Laplacians on those tilings.**

So the refined novelty claim should not be "nobody tiles region-moduli spaces." That would be too strong. The safer and more accurate claim is:

> **A narrow holographic literature studies tiling-like structures in kinematic space. The apparently unexplored territory is sheaf-Hodge/spectral theory on those tilings, especially when the tiling base is interpreted as a moduli space of regions rather than as a fixed spacetime.**
