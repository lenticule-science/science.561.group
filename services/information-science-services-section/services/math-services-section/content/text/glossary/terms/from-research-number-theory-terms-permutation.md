A permutation of a set is an arrangement of its elements in a definite order. The number of permutations of $n$ distinct objects is n!n! (n factorial).

Permutations can also be viewed as bijective [functions](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/concepts/function/index.md) from a set to itself. Under [composition](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/objects/categories/ordinary-categories/terms/composition.md), the permutations of a set form a [group](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/objects/groups/terms/group.md) — the **symmetric group** SnS_n. This is one of the most important examples of a non-[abelian](abelian-group.md) group (for $n \geq 3$).

A kk-permutation of $n$ objects is an ordered selection of $k$ objects from $n$, numbering n!(n−k)!\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}.
