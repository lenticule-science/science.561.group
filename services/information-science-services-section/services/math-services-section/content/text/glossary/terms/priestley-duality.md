Priestley duality (Priestley, 1970) gives a contravariant equivalence between bounded distributive lattices and Priestley spaces: compact topological spaces equipped with a compatible partial order and the Priestley separation property. A lattice is sent to its ordered space of prime filters; a Priestley space is sent to its lattice of clopen upsets. The constructions are inverse up to isomorphism.

This duality translates algebraic properties into geometric ones. For distributive bilattices, the dual is the coproduct (disjoint union) of the Priestley duals of the two lattice components (Mobasher, Pigozzi, Slutzki, and Voutsadakis, 2000). For the four-valued bilattice $\mathbf 2 \otimes \mathbf 2$, each component has a one-point dual, so the result is a discrete two-point space. Jung and Rivieccio (2012) refine the bilattice dual by adding an order-reversing homeomorphism representing negation.
