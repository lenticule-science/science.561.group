Kinetic proofing is repeated, economically accountable challenge of a candidate result through structurally independent evidence before settlement, purchased only when its expected reduction in acceptance risk exceeds its expected cost, and never a ceremonial fixed checklist. The name generalizes Hopfield and Ninio's kinetic proofreading, the biological result that a single-read discrimination step cannot beat the equilibrium error floor set by binding-energy difference alone; beating that floor costs real free energy spent on an irreversible intermediate step. Kinetic proofing buys the same multiplicative error reduction only under two conditions: a failed candidate must enter an effectively irreversible reject path rather than being silently readmitted, and the additional pass must use genuinely independent information or a fresh draw.

Independence here is evidential, not organizational: two passes by the same model reading the same prompt and evidence are not two channels, and two nominal agents using the same derivation may still be one channel. This distinguishes kinetic proofing from a checklist, which fixes its items in advance regardless of what each item's marginal reduction in risk actually costs to obtain, and from same-model double-checking, which looks like a second opinion but supplies no fresh evidence.

The consequence for operation: there is a fidelity-throughput trade, since error can fall rapidly while time and energy costs rise, and kinetic proofing must expose this trade rather than setting maximum verification as an unpriced goal — the system should record which channel caught which defect, not only count reviewers.
