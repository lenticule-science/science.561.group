An involution is a function $f$ satisfying $f(f(x)) = x$ for all $x$. Applying it twice returns to the starting point.

On the four-valued [bilattice](./bilattice.md), there are two involutions:

- **Negation** $\mathord{\sim}$: swaps $t \leftrightarrow f$, fixes $\bot$ and $\top$. In the [twist product](../../../../services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/residuated-lattice/content/twist-product/twist-product.md): $\mathord{\sim}(a, b) = (b, a)$.
- **Conflation** $-$: swaps $\bot \leftrightarrow \top$, fixes $t$ and $f$. In the twist product: $-(a, b) = (1-b, 1-a)$.

Together they generate the Klein four-group $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, the symmetry group of the bilattice.

An involution on a [lattice](./lattice.md) that reverses the ordering ($x \leq y$ implies $f(y) \leq f(x)$) produces a [De Morgan algebra](./de-morgan-algebra.md). Kalman's (1958) twist product construction generates De Morgan algebras from involutions on bounded distributive lattices.
