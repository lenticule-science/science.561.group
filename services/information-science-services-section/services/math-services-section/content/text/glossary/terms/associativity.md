A [binary operation](./binary-operation.md) $\ast$ is associative if grouping does not matter:

$$(a \ast b) \ast c = a \ast (b \ast c)$$

for all $a$, $b$, $c$. When an operation is associative, you can write $a \ast b \ast c$ without parentheses — the result is the same either way.

Addition is associative: $(2 + 3) + 4 = 2 + (3 + 4) = 9$. Function composition is associative: $(h \circ g) \circ f = h \circ (g \circ f)$. Subtraction is not: $(7 - 3) - 1 = 3$ but $7 - (3 - 1) = 5$.

Associativity is the first axiom of nearly every algebraic structure: [semigroups](./semigroup.md), [monoids](./monoid.md), [groups](./group.md), [rings](./ring.md), [categories](./category.md), [residuated lattices](./residuated-lattice.md). It is what makes sequential combination well-defined — you can chain operations without ambiguity.
