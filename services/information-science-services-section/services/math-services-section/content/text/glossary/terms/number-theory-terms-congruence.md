Two integers $a$ and $b$ are congruent modulo $n$ (written $a \equiv b \pmod{n}$) if $n$ [divides](divisibility.md) $a - b$.

Congruence modulo $n$ is an equivalence [relation](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/concepts/relation/index.md) on $\mathbb{Z}$. It is compatible with [addition](addition.md) and [multiplication](multiplication.md): if $a \equiv a' \pmod{n}$ and $b \equiv b' \pmod{n}$, then $a + b \equiv a' + b' \pmod{n}$ and $ab \equiv a'b' \pmod{n}$. This compatibility is what makes the [quotient group](quotient-group.md) $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ a [ring](ring.md).
