The word **prealgebra** has two senses in the corpus, and the glossary keeps them apart.

1. **Legacy sense.** A set with an operation, before laws such as associativity or identity are imposed. The legacy archive uses it for magmas, semigroups and monoids.
2. **Order sense.** An algebra-like structure on a preorder, whose operations are characterized by universal properties and so hold their laws up to mutual entailment, not equality. The quotient by mutual entailment is the algebra. `heyting-prealgebra` is the case with Heyting operations.

The two senses share the prefix and the direction of the move: a prealgebra is the structure before the laws are imposed strictly, and the algebra comes from imposing them. They differ in which laws are relaxed (all of them in the first sense, equality in the second). If the Leader's use is only the second sense, this entry should be reduced to it and the legacy sense recorded as a distinct term under the magma entry.
