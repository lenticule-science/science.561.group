The operadic nerve generalizes the [nerve](./nerve.md) of a [category](./category.md) from chains to [trees](./tree.md). Where the ordinary nerve presents a category as a [simplicial set](./simplicial-set.md) — a presheaf on $\Delta$, the category of finite linear orders — the operadic nerve presents an [operad](./operad.md) as a *dendroidal set*, a presheaf on $\Omega$, the category of finite rooted trees.

$\Omega$ has one object for each finite rooted tree $T$ (a corolla with a single vertex represents an arity, and the linear trees — chains with one leaf per vertex — form a full subcategory equivalent to $\Delta$), and morphisms generated by face maps (contracting an inner edge, or removing an outer vertex) and degeneracy maps (inserting a unary vertex), satisfying tree-shaped analogues of the simplicial identities. For an operad $\mathcal O$, the operadic nerve is the dendroidal set

$$N\mathcal O(T) := \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Operad}}(\Omega(T), \mathcal O),$$

where $\Omega(T)$ is the free operad generated by the tree $T$ — an $N\mathcal O(T)$-element is a labeling of $T$'s vertices by operations of $\mathcal O$ matching each vertex's arity, compatible under grafting. This is exactly the tree-shaped analogue of an $n$-simplex of $NC$ being a chain of $n$ composable morphisms of $C$: a $T$-shaped simplex of $N\mathcal O$ is a $T$-shaped composable diagram of operations of $\mathcal O$.

Because linear trees embed fully faithfully in $\Omega$, restricting $N\mathcal O$ along $\Delta \hookrightarrow \Omega$ recovers an ordinary simplicial set — and when $\mathcal O$ is a one-colored operad concentrated in arities $0$ and $1$ (equivalently, an ordinary category $C$), this restriction recovers the classical nerve $NC$. The operadic nerve is thus a strict generalization: every category's nerve is the linear-tree slice of that category's associated operad's operadic nerve.

Just as inner-horn-filling simplicial sets ([quasi-categories](./quasi-category.md)) model [$\infty$-categories](./infinity-category.md), inner-horn-filling dendroidal sets model $\infty$-operads. The theory was introduced by Ieke Moerdijk and Ittay Weiss in "Dendroidal Sets" (2007) and "On Inner Kan Complexes in the Category of Dendroidal Sets" (2007), with the operadic analogue of the nerve theorem — that $N$ is fully faithful from operads into dendroidal sets — proved there. Subsequent work of Cisinski, Moerdijk, and Heuts developed the homotopy theory of dendroidal sets as a model for $\infty$-operads, parallel to Joyal and Lurie's quasi-category model for $\infty$-categories.

The trees making up $\Omega$ carry no ordering on the children of a vertex — an $n$-ary vertex's inputs form a set, permuted by the $S_n$-action of the [symmetric operad](./operad.md) being nerved. Restricting to trees whose vertices carry a fixed left-to-right ordering of children gives the smaller **planar tree category** $\Omega_p$, the correct indexing category for the operadic nerve of a [non-symmetric operad](./operad.md); $\Omega_p$ embeds in $\Omega$, with the quotient by the residual $S_n$-actions recovering the general (symmetric) case. The two settings are not interchangeable in practice: some structural theorems proved for $\Omega_p$-indexed (planar) dendroidal sets — the [dendroidal Dold–Kan correspondence](./dold-kan-correspondence.md) among them — are not currently known to extend to $\Omega$-indexed (symmetric) ones.
