The Dold–Kan correspondence is an equivalence of categories between simplicial [abelian groups](./abelian-group.md) (or, over a field, simplicial [vector spaces](./vector-space.md)) and non-negatively graded [chain complexes](./chain-complex.md) of abelian groups. It is the precise bridge between combinatorial, shape-indexed data — a [simplicial set](./simplicial-set.md)'s worth of information, [linearized](./linearization.md) into an abelian category — and the differential-algebra data of a chain complex.

Given a simplicial abelian group $X_\bullet$, the normalized chain complex $N(X)_n := \bigcap_{i=1}^{n} \ker(d_i: X_n \to X_{n-1})$ takes, in each degree, the elements killed by every face map except the last; the remaining face map $d_0$ (restricted to this subgroup) serves as the differential, and $d_0 \circ d_0 = 0$ follows from the simplicial identities. Equivalently, the *Moore complex* takes $M(X)_n := X_n / (\text{sum of images of the degeneracy maps})$, the quotient by all degenerate simplices, with the alternating sum of face maps descending to a well-defined differential on the quotient; $N(X)$ and $M(X)$ are canonically isomorphic. The correspondence's inverse, $\Gamma$, reconstructs the simplicial object from a chain complex via an explicit combinatorial (shuffle) formula, and $N$ and $\Gamma$ are mutually inverse equivalences.

The correspondence preserves the invariant that matters: $\pi_n(X_\bullet) \cong H_n(N(X))$, the homotopy groups of the simplicial abelian group agree with the homology of its normalized chain complex, for every $n$. This is what licenses treating "simplicial abelian group" and "non-negatively graded chain complex" as two presentations of the same mathematical content, freely interchanging whichever is more convenient — the simplicial picture for constructions that are naturally combinatorial (products, mapping spaces), the chain-complex picture for constructions that are naturally algebraic (tensor products via the Eilenberg–Zilber theorem, spectral sequences).

The theorem is due independently to Albrecht Dold ("Homology of symmetric products and other functors of complexes," 1958) and Daniel Kan ("Functors involving c.s.s. complexes," 1958). Its generalization from $\Delta$-shaped (simplicial) data to tree-shaped ([operadic/dendroidal](./operadic-nerve.md)) data is established, but for a narrower and differently-shaped target than one might guess by analogy. Gutiérrez, Lukács, and Weiss ("Dold–Kan correspondence for dendroidal abelian groups," *J. Pure Appl. Algebra* 215 (2011), 1669–1687) prove a genuine adjoint equivalence $N \dashv \Gamma$ between dendroidal abelian groups on the *planar* tree category $\Omega_p$ — presheaves valued on trees built from non-symmetric operads, with no symmetric-group action — and a purpose-built category of *[dendroidal complexes](./dendroidal-complex.md)*, not ordinary non-negatively graded chain complexes: their own stated reason for introducing a new target category is that "the category of chain complexes is not big enough" to receive the normalization of a general dendroidal abelian group, since a tree carries more than one kind of face map and a single integer grading cannot record that. Two things this does *not* settle: whether the equivalence extends from $\Omega_p$ to the full (symmetric) tree category $\Omega$ used for ordinary symmetric operads, and — separately — whether a dendroidal complex can be *totalized* into an ordinary chain complex (the way a double complex is totalized) in a way that preserves homology, which is not part of what Gutiérrez–Lukács–Weiss prove and would need to be established independently before other chain-complex-only machinery (a Hodge Laplacian, for instance) could be applied to the result.
