A **formal term** is a well-formed syntactic expression generated by the
formation rules of a specified formal language. In a first-order signature,
variables and constants are terms and an operation symbol applied to terms
forms a new term. In lambda calculus, variables, abstractions, and applications
generate the lambda terms [@barendregt1984].

The qualifier *formal* matters. There is no single grammar shared by every
formal language, and a formal term is not defined by being a word used for a
concept. It is an expression whose admissibility and operations—such as
substitution or reduction—are fixed by a formal system. The separate
terminological sense of [term](../../../../../communication-science-services-section/services/glossary-science-services-section/services/term-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/term.md)
belongs to glossary science.

In logics that distinguish terms from formulas, terms denote individuals while
formulas bear truth conditions. In typed lambda calculi a well-formed term may
also require a typing derivation. Curry–Howard–Lambek relates typed terms,
proofs, and morphisms at the level of formal structure; that correspondence
does not define words, names, glossary entries, or terminological concepts.
