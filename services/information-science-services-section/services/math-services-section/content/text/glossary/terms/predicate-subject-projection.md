A **predicate subject-projection** is a morphism in the [predicate category](predicate-category.md), denoted $f : q \to p$, induced by the collapse of the entity-pair index to the subject diagonal:

$$(s, o) \mapsto (s, s).$$

The induced fiber-algebra map $f^* : A_p \to A_q$ acts cellwise:

$$f^*(v)(s, o) = v(s, s).$$

The resulting predicate $q$ has values that depend only on $p$'s subject-diagonal content: at every cell $(s, o)$, $q$ inherits whatever value $p$ takes at $(s, s)$. The object coordinate $o$ becomes irrelevant — it doesn't enter the definition of $q$'s value at all. Subject-projection is one of the four canonical generator classes of the [predicate category](predicate-category.md).

## What subject-projection encodes

Subject-projection is the formal source of *unary predicates* derived from binary ones. A unary predicate — one that depends only on a single entity — is, in the framework's binary-base structure, a binary predicate whose value happens to be insensitive to one of its coordinates.

The subject-projection morphism makes this precise: $q$'s value at $(s, o)$ is determined entirely by $p$'s subject-diagonal value at $(s, s)$. The framework's binary-base structure (every cell is $(s, p, o)$) does not need to admit unary predicates as primitives; they appear as the subject-projection (or [object-projection](predicate-object-projection.md), or both) of binary predicates with appropriate diagonal content.

Examples:

- A predicate *is-tall* (unary, of an individual) appears as the subject-projection of *taller-than-self* — formally, *taller-than* applied to the subject diagonal.
- A predicate *is-employed* (unary) appears as the subject-projection of *employs-self*, where the diagonal content of *employs* records who employs whom only when subject and object coincide.

## The structural source of "unary predicates"

The four-generator structure of the predicate category exhausts the natural maps under $\mathrm{Sym}(E)$-equivariance. The two projection generators (subject-projection and [object-projection](predicate-object-projection.md)) are what allow apparently-unary predicates to appear within the binary base. Without them, unary predicates would need a separate categorical structure; with them, they arise as morphisms within the binary-base predicate category.

The two projections are duals: subject-projection collapses to the subject diagonal $(s, s)$; object-projection collapses to the object diagonal $(o, o)$. Whether a derived unary predicate captures *the subject* or *the object* of the original binary predicate depends on which projection generates it.

## Composition with converse

Subject-projection composed with [converse](predicate-converse.md) yields object-projection: swapping subject and object converts collapse-onto-subject-diagonal into collapse-onto-object-diagonal. Symbolically, $f_{\mathrm{conv}} \circ f_{\mathrm{sp}} = f_{\mathrm{op}}$ at the level of generator classes (with appropriate composition signs). This gives the predicate category a clean four-generator structure where each pair of generators interacts predictably with the others.
