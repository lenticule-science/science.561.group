A **predicate category**, denoted $\mathcal{C}$, is a small category whose objects are predicates and whose morphisms record the structural relationships between them. The morphisms are not freely chosen: under a $\mathrm{Sym}(E)$-equivariance hypothesis on the entity set $E$ (with $|E| \geq 3$), every natural map between predication fiber algebras is generated by exactly four canonical classes. That the generator set is *exactly four classes* is a theorem about equivariance, not a stipulation.

The four-generator structure is the mathematical articulation of the structural lopsidedness that the relationality framework commits to philosophically. It is where the four-cornered shape of [predication](predication.md) — the four positions a settlement can occupy across the knowledge and truth orders — gets its canonical content.

## The four canonical generator classes

Each generator class is induced by a specific manipulation of the entity-pair index $E \times E$ that underlies the [predication cell](predication-cell.md):

- **Specialization** $f : q \to p$ — a sub-support inclusion $\mathrm{dom}(q) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{dom}(p)$. The morphism encodes that $q$ is a sub-predicate of $p$, with $q$'s values inheriting from $p$'s on the smaller domain and extending by $\bot$ outside. The induced fiber-algebra map $f^* : A_p \to A_q$ is restriction along the inclusion.
- **Converse** $f : p^c \to p$ — induced by the swap $(s, o) \mapsto (o, s)$ on the entity-pair index. Encodes the relational converse: "$p$ holds of $(s, o)$" becomes "$p^c$ holds of $(o, s)$." Asymmetry is what allows the converse to produce a *distinct* predicate; in the symmetric case where a predicate equals its own converse, the generator collapses.
- **Subject-projection** $f : q \to p$ — induced by the diagonal collapse $(s, o) \mapsto (s, s)$. The resulting predicate $q$ depends on $p$ only through its subject-diagonal content: $q$'s value at $(s, o)$ is $p$'s value at $(s, s)$. This is the formal source of *unary* predicates as derived structures.
- **Object-projection** $f : q \to p$ — the dual of subject-projection, induced by $(s, o) \mapsto (o, o)$.

These four classes are residuation-natural: the [twist-product](../../../../services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/residuated-lattice/content/twist-product/twist-product.md) fusion and right-residual on the [bilattice](bilattice.md) fiber algebras are preserved by each reindexing.

## What the structure means

The predicate category is the indexing base of the predication fibration. Reindexing along its morphisms is how predicate-relating-to-predicate translates into fiber-algebra-relating-to-fiber-algebra. Specialization gives sub-predicates; converse gives directional inversion; the projections give unary derivations. These four are *the* ways predicates structurally relate to one another, and the category is the structure that holds them together.

## Why exactly four

The four-generator exhaustiveness theorem is structurally important because it explains why the framework's four-cornered logic and four-position settlement taxonomy are not coincidences. The four corners of a bilattice (true-only, false-only, both, neither), the four positions of settlement (settled-affirmed, settled-denied, both, neither), and the four generator classes of the predicate category all share a common origin: they reflect, at different layers, the structural ways a directed triadic predication can stand. None of them is a free parameter; each is a structural consequence of taking the directed triple as primitive.
