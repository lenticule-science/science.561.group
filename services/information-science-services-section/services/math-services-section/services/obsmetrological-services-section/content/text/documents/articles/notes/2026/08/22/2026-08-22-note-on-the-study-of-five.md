## 1. The restriction, confirmed against the world

The corpus proves in Lean that `d = 5` is the first order violating the crystallographic bound `N ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6}`. The external literature says the same thing the same way: the crystallographic restriction theorem admits only 1-, 2-, 3-, 4-, 6-fold rotations for a periodic lattice, with the standard proof an infinite-descent/shrinking argument — an assumed 5-fold lattice yields a strictly smaller 5-fold lattice, contradicting minimal spacing (Wikipedia; MathWorld). The historical weight of the exclusion is worth keeping: Kittel's solid-state textbook stated the orthodoxy — "We cannot find a lattice that goes into itself under other rotations, such as by 2π/5 radians or 2π/7 radians. … We can make a crystal from molecules which individually have a five-fold rotation axis, but we should not expect the lattice to have a five-fold rotation axis" — and Shechtman quoted exactly this passage in his 2011 Nobel lecture, having found tenfold electron diffraction in an Al–Mn alloy in 1982 and published in 1984 after initial rejection (verified verbatim against Au-Yang & Perk, arXiv:1306.6698). De Bruijn's two equivalent global constructions — the pentagrid (five families of parallel lines at 36°/72°, dualized) and cut-and-project from a periodic **five-dimensional** cubic lattice through an irrationally oriented slab — are confirmed in the same source. Fivefold order is periodic upstairs in 5D and aperiodic in the plane: the whirl is a shadow of a lattice one can't live in. (That last sentence is a gloss, not a theorem.)

## 3. √5 as discriminant

Binet's formula `Fₙ = (φⁿ − ψⁿ)/√5` comes from the characteristic equation `x² − x − 1 = 0` of the Fibonacci recurrence; its discriminant is `1 + 4 = 5`, so √5 is literally the square root of the discriminant, appearing both as the root gap `φ − ψ = √5` and in the denominator (PlanetMath; MathWorld; elementary). Since `|φ| > 1 > |ψ|`, growth is governed by φ alone. The corpus's five-tile gap satisfies `x² − 6x + 4 = 0` for the doubled quantity (`tile5_gap_char_root`) — a different quadratic in the same field, discriminant `36 − 16 = 20 = 4·5`. Same square-free core, 5. That both quadratics ramify at 5 is not a coincidence — it is what `ℚ(√5)` means — but no further identity is claimed here beyond membership in the field; the golden-ladder identities (`φ⁻³ = √5 − 2`, `φ⁻² = 2 − φ`, `√5 = φ + φ⁻¹`) remain, per the dervish note, proposed and not yet proved in `GoldenFieldBridge`.

## 4. Five alive: what the verified literature says

The phyllotaxis convergents `1/2, 1/3, 2/5, 3/8, 5/13, …` — Fibonacci mediants — converge to the golden angle `360°(1 − 1/φ) ≈ 137.5°` (Okabe, *Sci. Rep.*, PMC4607949; Adler, Barabé & Jean 1997). Douady & Couder (1992) showed the golden angle emerging as the dynamical attractor of a repulsion-plus-growth process in a physical analog, with divergence sliding from 180° to 137.5° as the growth parameter falls. Two corrections to the popular story, both verified verbatim:

- **The golden angle is not the fitness peak.** Strauss, Lempe, Prusinkiewicz, Tsiantis & Smith (*New Phytologist* 2020): "the golden angle cannot really be seen as 'the' global maximum in terms of LCE, but is rather one of many optimal angles" — the Lucas angle (≈99.5°) and others do as well; what selection acts on is the iterative positioning mechanism, not the angle.
- **Why five-part flowers is not answered.** Pentamery's dominance in core eudicots correlates with the gamma whole-genome triplication, and the dynamical model of Kitazawa & Fujimoto (*PLOS Comput. Biol.* 11(4):e1004145, 2015 — citation corrected during verification from a misattributed paper) finds 4- and 5-organ whorls dominating parameter space, while stating plainly that the underlying developmental mechanism "is unclear."

In the stem rather than the flower: Larson's *Populus deltoides* work (see the botany companion for full citations) shows the vascular system realizing the phyllotactic fraction's **denominator** as its sympodium count — five discrete bundle-chains in the 2/5 zone — which is what the cottonwood pith star is a cross-section of, if the botany note's flagged inference holds. And the serious literature connecting phyllotaxis to aperiodic order (van Iterson 1907; Levitov 1991; Rivier/Sadoc/Charvolin; Maciá's 2022 review) treats spiral lattices as aperiodic-crystal-**adjacent** — their own object, with locally quasicrystal-like grain boundaries between parastichy domains — not literally Penrose tilings. The honest bridge is family membership in aperiodic order, not identity.

**The rhyme, graded as rhyme.** The KCBS reading in the dervish note is "value realized only in circulation around the pentagon" — fivefold order with no rest frame. Phyllotaxis is fivefold order that likewise never sits in a lattice: the 2/5 spiral exists only on a growing cylinder, its five realized as circulation of the generative front, its limit angle irrational. The tree carries its five where the cottonwood carries its star: in the circulatory architecture, visible only in cross-section, never as a tiling. This is corpus-grade rhyme in exactly the sense the dervish note uses for its own cross-domain readings — an invitation to formalization (is there a `Der₅` readout in the cylinder-lattice/van Iterson setting? Levitov's energy-minimizing cylindrical lattices would be the place to look), not a claim.
