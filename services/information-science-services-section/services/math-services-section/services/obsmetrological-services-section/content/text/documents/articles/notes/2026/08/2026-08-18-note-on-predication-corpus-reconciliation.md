This note is the canonical index for the mathematical material recovered from a
flattened Windows archive. The source contains several generations of the same
predication-category research, so the individual drafts remain historical while
their mathematical commitments are represented here and in the active
obsmetrologia design.

## Stable structure

The corpus treats a predication cell as a subject–predicate–object relation whose
value lives in a bilattice or quantale-enriched fiber. Predication fibers are
refined into a hyperdoctrine, then dualized through Priestley/bitopological
constructions. Closure and settling are ordinal processes: Scott-continuous
segments behave like smooth directed-colimit flow, while non-Scott-continuous
firings are the proposed singular events at which genuinely new content appears
at a limit stage.

The cellular extension plan asks how the Tarski/Lawvere Laplacian machinery for
cellular sheaves extends to the predication corpus's bitopological settling site.
The higher-categorical direction records possible lifts from the working
2-categorical backbone (temporal 2-category, closure 2-monad, and settling
moduli) to an $(\infty,1)$-categorical setting. These are research directions,
not settled theorems.

## Open questions retained as questions

- What hypotheses make the closure-flow/singularity analogy a theorem rather
  than a metaphor?
- Can the per-fiber Curry construction be made precise for clopen lattice fibers,
  q-enriched sites, and the proposed two-dimensional degree terms?
- Under what assumptions does the forward trajectory induced by a Galois
  adjunction preserve the intended settling structure?
- Which stability statements survive deformation, and which depend on the
  chosen regime or continuity hypothesis?

The source also contains finite/infinite relational-mathematics drafts and
quasicrystalline hypertensor-topos specifications. They are preserved as
historical specifications because they contain implementation-dependent claims;
the active pages should cite and test those claims before promoting them to
definitions or theorems.
