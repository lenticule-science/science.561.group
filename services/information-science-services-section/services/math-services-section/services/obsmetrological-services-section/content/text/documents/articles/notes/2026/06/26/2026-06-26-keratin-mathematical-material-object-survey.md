# Frontier survey: keratin as a mathematical material object

## 1. Executive map

Keratin is best modeled not as a generic “fiber,” but as a **multiscale directed material system**: a chemically crosslinked protein composite; a morphogenetic record of follicle growth; a slender anisotropic rod with intrinsic curvature, torsion, swelling, damage, and viscoelastic memory; a cuticle-covered frictional surface; and, in wool/fur/hair textiles, a population of interacting directed rods whose contacts become yarns, felts, knits, braids, and woven cells.
The central mathematical tension is that the single-shaft level is naturally rod-like, while the textile/population level is naturally network-, complex-, knot-, and sheaf-like.

A useful high-level decomposition is:

| Scale | Keratin-specific structure | Existing mathematics | Underdeveloped mathematics |
|---|---|---|---|
| molecular / filament | α-keratin intermediate filaments, matrix proteins, disulfides, hydration-sensitive bonds | molecular dynamics, constitutive laws, composite mechanics | rigorous molecular-to-rod homogenization with humidity/damage variables |
| cell / shaft | cortex, cuticle, medulla, elliptic cross-sections, intrinsic crimp/curvature | nonlinear elasticity, viscoelastic rods, imaging morphometrics | anisotropic rod models whose material frame includes cuticle direction and growth history |
| surface / contact | directed overlapping scales, 18-MEA lipid layer, root-tip friction asymmetry | tribology, contact mechanics, AFM/SEM image analysis | directed contact complexes; frictional sheaves; irreversible locking models |
| population / yarn | staple length/diameter/crimp distributions, twist, drafting, hairiness | stochastic fiber models, yarn mechanics, finite elements, CT reconstruction | category-like “process maps” from fiber distributions to yarn/fabric states |
| textile / topology | felts, knits, weaves, braids, loops, defects, laddering | knot/link theory, textile topology, discrete rods, metamaterial mechanics | keratin-specific topology where cuticle direction, humidity, and damage change energy barriers |
| morphogenesis | follicle orientation fields, whorls, feather branching, coat patterns | reaction–diffusion, vector fields, index theory, PCP models | growth-to-material encodings: maps from developmental fields to later mechanical response |

## 2. What keratin is mathematically

At the molecular scale, mammalian hair, wool, cashmere, yak, camel, alpaca, angora, horn, hoof, claw, and nail are primarily **α-keratin systems**: coiled-coil intermediate-filament proteins embedded in a matrix, with cystine/disulfide crosslinking and water-sensitive secondary interactions.
Mechanically, this makes α-keratin neither a simple elastic rod nor a simple polymer fiber; it is a hydrated, crosslinked, semi-crystalline/amorphous composite whose response changes with humidity, strain rate, temperature, chemistry, and damage.
Human hair, for example, has reported tensile strength around 150–270 MPa and marked strain-rate and humidity dependence.
([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28183593/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

At the shaft scale, a mammalian hair or wool fiber has at least three geometrically distinct zones: **cuticle**, **cortex**, and often **medulla**.
The cuticle is a layered directed surface of overlapping scales; the cortex contains cortical cells and aligned keratin filament/matrix material; the medulla, when present, introduces voids or cellular inclusions that alter bending, opacity, density, thermal properties, and processing behavior.
Wool scale morphology can be parameterized by SEM/image-analysis features such as scale perimeter per unit length and scale perimeter index, and these parameters differ among sheep breeds.
([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-45689-w?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Feathers are not merely “keratin fibers.” They are branching keratin appendages whose rachis, barbs, barbules, cilia, and hooklets form a multilevel graph/rod/shell architecture.
Modern terminology treats the old “β-keratin” category more carefully: reptiles and birds contain **corneous beta-proteins** or β-keratin-like sauropsid structural proteins, with feather morphology depending on combinations of α- and β-type keratin expression across appendage regions.
([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30637919/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Species differences matter mathematically because they change the probability distributions and constitutive parameters of the material ensemble.
Cashmere, mohair, camel, yak, alpaca, llama, vicuña, guanaco, and angora rabbit fibers are difficult to distinguish chemically because they are all animal keratin fibers, so textile metrology relies heavily on microscopy, image analysis, NIR spectroscopy, proteomics, and statistical classification.
Yak wool has been reported to have denser scales and stronger frictional effect than cashmere and camel hair in comparative studies; camel hair medullation varies with coat color, sex, age, and body region; mohair quality control treats medullation as a significant measurable defect.
([mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6439/11/8/67?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

A mathematically faithful object is therefore something like:

\[ \mathcal H = (\gamma, R, A, \Omega_0, C, M, \Sigma, \mu^\pm, E(H,D), \eta(H,D), \chi), \]

where \(\gamma(s)\) is the centerline, \(R(s)\in SO(3)\) is a material frame, \(A(s)\) is cross-section geometry, \(\Omega_0(s)\) is intrinsic curvature/torsion, \(C(s)\) is cortical composition, \(M(s)\) is medulla state, \(\Sigma(s,\theta)\) is cuticle scale morphology, \(\mu^+,\mu^-\) are direction-dependent friction laws, \(E,\eta\) are humidity/damage-dependent elastic and viscous moduli, and \(\chi\) is chemical state: disulfide density, oxidation, lipid layer, dye/bleach/thermal history.

## 3. Existing mathematical models of keratin fibers

### 3.1 Rod mechanics

The natural first model for a single hair or wool fiber is a Kirchhoff/Cosserat rod: a slender curve with a moving material frame, bending, twisting, stretching, and possibly shear.
This is already standard in computer graphics and soft-matter mechanics for hair and yarn-like objects, and recent “stable Cosserat rod” solvers explicitly target hair, yarn-level cloth, and high-stiffness rod simulations at interactive rates.
([cosseratrods.org](https://www.cosseratrods.org/cosserat_rods/case-studies/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

For keratin specifically, the rod energy should not be written as the generic isotropic form alone.
A more honest first-order energy is

\[ E_{\rm shaft} = \int_0^L \frac12(\Omega-\Omega_0)^{T}B(H,D,s)(\Omega-\Omega_0) + \frac12(\epsilon-\epsilon_0)^T S(H,D,s)(\epsilon-\epsilon_0) \,ds + E_{\rm surface} + E_{\rm contact}. \]

Here \(B\) and \(S\) depend on humidity \(H\), damage \(D\), cortical type, medulla, and cross-section ellipticity.
The intrinsic curvature \(\Omega_0\) should encode follicular growth, crimp, asymmetric cortex, thermal/chemical setting, and permanent deformation.
Human hair mechanics studies already support humidity- and strain-rate-sensitive constitutive laws, while wool stretching studies show structural rearrangements under industrial stretching.
([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28183593/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 3.2 Nonlinear elasticity, viscoelasticity, and hygromechanics

Keratin is strongly hydration-sensitive.
Water plasticizes amorphous matrix regions and changes hydrogen-bond networks, lowering stiffness and altering relaxation.
This makes hair/wool distinct from glass fibers and many synthetic filaments; it also differs from cotton/flax cellulose, where the molecular architecture is polysaccharide microfibrillar rather than keratin intermediate-filament/matrix/disulfide.
The relevant mathematical model is not a dry elastic rod, but a **hygro-viscoelastic anisotropic rod with internal variables** for bound/free water, crosslink state, and damage.
Hydration sensitivity is repeatedly emphasized in keratin mechanics reviews and in hoof/hair mechanical modeling.
([scholars.cityu.edu.hk](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/keratin-structure-mechanical-properties-occurrence-in-biological-/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

A useful constitutive family is:

\[ \sigma(t) = \mathcal C(H,D,\lambda,\dot\lambda)\,\varepsilon(t) + \int_0^t G(H,D,t-\tau)\,\dot \varepsilon(\tau)\,d\tau + \sigma_{\rm plastic}(D,\chi), \]

with \(\chi\) tracking chemistry: bleaching, dyeing, oxidation, reduction, heat, UV, lipid loss, or disulfide modification.
Recent oxidative-damage studies combine SEM and AFM nanomechanical mapping to resolve cuticle/cortex damage; repeated dyeing and other cosmetic treatments alter hair morphology, mechanics, and chemistry.
([onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/VIW.
20250079?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 3.3 Molecular-to-fiber multiscale modeling

At smaller scales, the frontier is molecular dynamics and intermediate-filament assembly, with some 2026 preprint work comparing nanomechanical descriptors across hair keratins.
The hard problem is homogenization: how to pass from keratin dimers, intermediate filaments, matrix proteins, disulfides, and water to a rod whose bending/torsion/stretching tensors are measurable.
Public work exists on keratin structure and molecular dynamics, but a rigorous keratin-specific derivation from molecular bonds to Cosserat rod parameters remains open.
([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29797268/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 3.4 Horn, hoof, claw, and nail: not rods only

Horn, hoof, claw, and nail are better modeled as **laminated/tubular keratin composites, shells, and impact/abrasion structures**, not as long free rods.
Bovidae horn is studied as a lightweight keratin biocomposite with high energy absorption; equine hoof is treated as a hierarchical energy-absorbing polymer composite with orientation- and rate-dependent response; recent reviews also frame horse hoof trimmings as a renewable keratin resource.
([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9412671/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The mathematical distinction is important: hair/wool need rod-contact-population mechanics; horn/hoof need shell/composite/fracture/impact mechanics; feather needs branching rod-shell mechanics; nail/claw sit between plate/shell fracture and keratin laminate mechanics.

## 4. Surface scales, anisotropic friction, and directed morphology

The cuticle is the most keratin-specific surface feature.
It is a **directed, overlapping, scale-like geometry** whose root-to-tip orientation changes contact, brushing, combing, felting, shrinkage, and tactile feel.
Human hair friction increases with weathering largely because the protective 18-methyleicosanoic acid monolayer is lost; hair also shows anisotropic friction because protruding cuticle edges can interlock when sliding toward the root.
([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40782659/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

For wool, the classical **directional frictional effect** is central to felting: the coefficient/frictional resistance differs with and against the scale direction, giving preferential migration and irreversible entanglement under water, heat, and agitation.
Older wool felting papers already formulated the problem in terms of different friction directions; current processing papers still measure static and dynamic directional friction effects and relate them to anti-felting treatments.
([publications.tno.nl](https://publications.tno.nl/publication/34616231/mrlPp5/vegt-1955-study.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

A mathematical surface model should represent the cuticle by a directed scale field

\[ \Sigma: (s,\theta)\mapsto (h,\alpha,\ell,\rho,r,\kappa_{\rm edge},\chi_{\rm lipid}), \]

where \(h\) is scale height, \(\alpha\) scale angle, \(\ell\) scale length, \(\rho\) scale density, \(r\) roughness, \(\kappa_{\rm edge}\) edge curvature, and \(\chi_{\rm lipid}\) surface chemistry.
The friction law should be asymmetric:

\[ \mu_{\rm with} \neq \mu_{\rm against}, \qquad {\rm DFE}=\mu_{\rm against}-\mu_{\rm with}, \]

and should depend on humidity, normal load, damage, lipid layer, and fiber-fiber angle.
AFM fiber-probe work shows that hair friction anisotropy depends on cuticle alignment and damage, so a scalar Coulomb coefficient is too poor.
([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23565816/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

A promising next model is a **directed contact graph** whose vertices are shaft patches and whose edges store relative cuticle orientation.
Two hairs are not just “in contact”; they have a compatibility relation:

\[ e=(p_i,p_j): \quad (\text{scale direction}_i,\text{scale direction}_j,\text{relative sliding}) \mapsto (\mu, \text{lock probability}, \text{damage increment}). \]

This is precisely where graph/sheaf descriptions become natural: contact compatibility is local, directed, history-dependent, and not reducible to a single global scalar.

## 5. Hair populations, drafting, spinning, yarn, and textiles

A fleece, fur coat, or bundle of hair is not a bundle of identical rods.
It is a **staple-fiber population** with distributions of length, diameter, ellipticity, crimp amplitude, crimp wavelength, curvature, medulla fraction, cuticle scale geometry, pigmentation, stiffness, damage, and surface chemistry.
Processing transforms distributions, not just fibers.

For wool and fine animal fibers, textile identification already treats morphology statistically: microscopy and image analysis compare scale patterns, diameters, medulla features, and other fiber signatures across wool, cashmere, mohair, camel, vicuña, guanaco, llama, alpaca, yak, and angora rabbit.
([mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6439/11/8/67?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

A mathematical process chain should be written as maps between measures:

\[ \mu_{\rm fleece} \longrightarrow \mu_{\rm sorted} \longrightarrow \mu_{\rm sliver} \longrightarrow \mu_{\rm roving} \longrightarrow \mu_{\rm yarn} \longrightarrow \mu_{\rm fabric}. \]

Each measure lives on a morphology-state space \(X_{\rm fiber}\), and each processing step is a stochastic transformation plus a mechanical alignment/twist/contact operation.
Drafting changes alignment and length correlations; combing changes length distribution and removes noils; spinning adds twist and radial packing; finishing changes humidity, damage, friction, and fiber migration.
Yarn hairiness is a population-level boundary phenomenon: fibers protrude from the yarn body, alter tactile handle, pilling, shedding, abrasion, and fabric appearance, and are modeled statistically in textile-engineering work.
([journals.sagepub.com](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0040517514538692?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Keratin-specific textile modeling must include:

\[ (\text{length}, \text{diameter}, \text{crimp}, \text{medulla}, \text{scale density}, \text{DFE}, H, D) \]

rather than just “fiber stiffness” and “fiber radius.” Felting propensity, for example, is not only a function of contact density; it depends on cuticle directionality, crimp, fineness, bending rigidity, elasticity, and medullation.
([researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237560461_A_Comparative_Study_on_the_Felting_Propensity_of_Animal_Fibers?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## 6. Knitting, weaving, felting, and spinning as higher-level keratin transformations

Recent physics and applied-math work treats textiles as mechanical metamaterials with topology.
A 2026 review frames twisted yarn, woven patterns, and knitted fabrics in terms of symmetry, topology, knots, links in thickened tori, yarn-level geometry, dissipation, and defects.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09005?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Knitting turns a one-dimensional yarn into a two-dimensional fabric whose nonlinear elasticity is programmed stitch by stitch.
Recent work combines experiments and simulations to connect stitch topology with emergent elasticity; geometric models predict three-dimensional self-folding from knit/purl patterns; laddering is now explicitly studied as propagation of a topological defect after a broken thread or dropped stitch, with experiments and discrete rod simulations.
([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-46498-z?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

But most textile-topology models are **yarn-generic**.
They treat the yarn as a continuous strand with bending, stretching, twisting, friction, and contact.
Keratin yarns add extra structure:

1. Wool/cashmere/yak/alpaca yarns are assemblies of directed cuticle-bearing staple fibers, not monofilaments.
2. Humidity changes modulus and friction.
3. Cuticle direction and scale density affect inter-fiber migration and felting.
4. Crimp changes bulk, elasticity, warmth, and packing.
5. Damage and surface chemistry change pilling, shedding, handle, and relaxation.

Felting is the most keratin-specific higher-level transformation.
Unlike weaving or knitting, felting is not primarily a prescribed topological path of one yarn; it is an **irreversible contact-locking process** in a population of directed scale-covered rods.
A minimal model is a growing directed contact complex \(K_t\) where edges are added when fibers touch under agitation and promoted to locked edges when local cuticle/friction/humidity conditions pass a threshold.
The limiting felt is a percolated, frictionally locked, history-dependent complex.

## 7. Topology of hair, fur, whorls, tangles, and loops

A hair coat is a vector or line field on a surface plus a family of emerging rods.
Hair whorls, cowlicks, part lines, and fur direction fields are singularities or defects in follicle orientation.
Mammalian planar cell polarity genes regulate hair follicle orientation, and scalp hair whorl direction has recent genetic association work connecting whorl direction to loci likely acting through follicle cell polarity and cranial development.
([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3257556/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Mathematically, a scalp or animal surface \(S\) carries a follicle orientation field

\[ v:S\setminus \Sigma \to TS, \]

with singular set \(\Sigma\).
On closed surfaces, index constraints analogous to the hairy ball theorem/Poincaré–Hopf force defects for nonvanishing vector fields; on scalp patches with boundary, the relevant invariant is a boundary-corrected index.
A cowlick is then not a nuisance morphology but a topological singularity whose index, location, and local winding interact with growth direction, grooming, and rod mechanics.

Hair tangles and mats invite braid/knot theory, but with an important caveat: topological entanglement alone does not determine mechanical behavior.
A knot in clean smooth nylon, in wool under humidity, and in damaged hair with raised scales can have the same topological type but different energy barriers and frictional hysteresis.
Thus the topological state should be decorated by keratin morphology:

\[ (\text{knot/link type},\ \text{contact graph},\ \text{scale orientation},\ \mu^\pm,\ H,\ D). \]

Knitted-loop topology is similarly incomplete without material decoration.
The topology of a knit constrains possible deformations and failures, but keratin yarn morphology changes locking, relaxation, pilling, and laddering thresholds.
Recent work on topology-defined textile computation and laddering makes clear that topology can control information flow or defect propagation, while mechanics determines whether the topological operation actually executes.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14062?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## 10. Proposed “keratin sheaf Laplacian” framework

### 10.1 Base space

Let \(X\) be a multiscale cell complex built from keratin material organization.

At the biological level:

- vertices \(v_i\): follicles/root points on a skin surface \(S\);
- directed 1-cells \(h_i\): hair shafts as discretized curves;
- 2-cells or patches: local scalp/skin/fur regions;
- singular cells: whorls, cowlicks, part lines, damaged zones.

At the bundle/textile level:

- vertices: shaft segments, yarn cross-section positions, loop nodes, contact points;
- edges: fiber-fiber contacts, twist adjacency, drafting neighbor relations, stitch crossings;
- 2-cells: knitted loops, woven unit cells, felt patches, braid crossings, yarn cross-sections;
- higher cells: yarn bundles, fabric repeat cells, felted clusters, pilling aggregates.

Thus \(X\) is not fixed once and for all.
It evolves under grooming, drafting, felting, pilling, shedding, fracture, and humidity cycling.

### 10.2 Stalks

A stalk \(\mathcal F(\sigma)\) over a cell \(\sigma\) stores local keratin state.
For a shaft segment:

\[ \mathcal F(h_i^k) = \{ d, A, e, \kappa_1,\kappa_2,\tau, \phi_{\rm crimp}, \Sigma, C_{\rm cortex}, M_{\rm medulla}, \mu^\pm, B,S, H,D,\chi \}. \]

Here \(d\) is diameter, \(A\) cross-section shape/ellipticity, \(e\) taper, \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\tau\) curvature/torsion, \(\phi_{\rm crimp}\) crimp phase, \(\Sigma\) cuticle-scale data, \(C_{\rm cortex}\) cortical type, \(M_{\rm medulla}\) medulla state, \(\mu^\pm\) directed friction, \(B,S\) stiffness tensors, \(H\) humidity/water state, \(D\) damage, and \(\chi\) chemistry.

For a contact edge \(e=(p_i,p_j)\), the stalk stores:

\[ \mathcal F(e) = \{ \text{relative orientation}, \text{normal load}, \text{sliding direction}, \text{contact area}, \text{adhesion}, \text{lock state}, \text{wear increment} \}. \]

For a yarn or textile cell, the stalk contains local twist, packing density, loop topology, fiber orientation distribution, porosity, permeability, heat-transfer parameters, hairiness, and pilling/shedding variables.

### 10.3 Restriction maps

For an ordinary vector-space cellular sheaf, restriction maps

\[ \rho_{\sigma\to\tau}:\mathcal F(\sigma)\to \mathcal F(\tau) \]

encode compatibility between higher and lower cells.
In keratin:

- follicle-to-shaft restrictions map follicle orientation/growth conditions to shaft intrinsic curvature and taper;
- shaft-segment restrictions enforce continuity of centerline, material frame, curvature, and damage;
- contact restrictions map two shaft states to a contact state;
- drafting restrictions map local fiber orientation/length distributions to sliver alignment;
- twist restrictions map fiber positions to yarn helical angle and radial packing;
- stitch restrictions map yarn state to loop curvature, crossing forces, and contact;
- felting restrictions map contact history plus DFE to lock state;
- pilling restrictions map surface hairiness and fatigue to pill nucleation.

For a contact edge \(e=(i,j)\), a linearized sheaf coboundary would be:

\[ (\delta x)_e = \rho_{i,e}x_i-\rho_{j,e}x_j. \]

The sheaf energy is then:

\[ E_{\mathcal F}(x)=\|\delta x\|^2, \qquad L_{\mathcal F}=\delta^\ast\delta. \]

Low energy means local morphology/contact states are mutually compatible; high energy marks incoherence, unstable contact, damage mismatch, incompatible twist, poor drafting, or impending pilling/felting/shedding.

### 10.4 What the Laplacian measures

Depending on the stalks and restrictions, a keratin sheaf Laplacian could measure:

- mismatch of neighboring crimp phase;
- incompatibility of adjacent intrinsic curvature fields;
- high-energy contact orientation likely to lock or felt;
- local humidity-driven swelling inconsistency;
- mismatch between yarn twist and fiber migration;
- loop-contact frustration in knit structures;
- pilling-prone surface incoherence;
- whorl/follicle direction defects;
- damage gradients along a hair shaft.

Global sections are coherent material states: all local morphology, contact, and textile constraints agree.
Harmonic representatives identify least-incompatible states when exact global consistency is impossible.

### 10.5 What is new mathematically

The linear cellular-sheaf formalism already exists.
What is new is the keratin-specific modeling choice:

1. **Directed frictional restrictions.** Standard sheaf Laplacians are symmetric once inner products are chosen.
  Keratin contact is direction-dependent and hysteretic.
2. **Hybrid continuous/discrete stalks.** Some variables are vector-valued; others are ordered labels, intervals, probability distributions, or damage lattices.
3. **Dynamic base complexes.** Contacts appear/disappear; felting turns temporary contacts into locked cells; pilling creates new aggregate cells; shedding removes cells.
4. **Morphogenetic restrictions.** Follicle/growth fields constrain later rod geometry.
5. **Humidity filtrations.** A filtration parameter could be humidity, strain, time, agitation, or damage, not only spatial scale.

The rigorous extension likely needs **nonlinear sheaves**, **lattice-valued/Tarski sheaves**, **directed or non-self-adjoint Laplacians**, and **persistent sheaf Laplacians over processing histories**, not just ordinary graph Laplacians.

## 12. Open problems and proof-agent tasks

### Problem A. Formalize a decorated keratin rod

**Definition task.** Define a keratin shaft as a tuple

\[ (\gamma,R,A,\Omega_0,\Sigma,C,M,H,D,\chi) \]

with regularity assumptions and physical units.

**Invariant candidates.**

- integrated crimp energy;
- cuticle direction index;
- medulla fraction;
- humidity sensitivity coefficient;
- damage-weighted bending spectrum;
- scale-density Fourier modes along \(s\).

**Toy theorem.** If two decorated rods have identical centerline geometry but opposite cuticle orientation fields, then they are topologically equivalent as curves but not tribologically equivalent under directed sliding. **Sketch.** Ambient isotopy ignores \(\Sigma\); frictional contact energy depends on orientation through \(\mu^\pm\), so the decorated energy is not invariant under reversal.

### Problem B. A sawtooth cuticle contact model

**Model.** Represent cuticle scales as periodic asymmetric sawteeth with height \(h\), angle \(\alpha\), spacing \(\ell\), and normal load \(N\).

**Theorem target.** Under a Coulomb-plus-geometric-interlocking law, rootward sliding has higher expected resistance than tipward sliding when \(h\sin\alpha\) exceeds a load-dependent threshold.

**Needed data.** Paired SEM/AFM topography and fiber-on-fiber friction for the same hair/wool fibers, measured root-to-tip and tip-to-root across humidity and damage states.

### Problem C. Keratin contact graph and felting threshold

**Data structure.** A time-dependent directed graph \(G_t=(V,E_t)\), with each vertex a fiber segment and each edge storing scale-orientation mismatch, normal load, humidity, and agitation history.

**Invariant candidates.**

- directed lock number;
- contact-cycle rank;
- irreversible-edge fraction;
- percolating felt cluster size;
- DFE-weighted Betti number.

**Theorem target.** In a random directed contact graph with lock probability \(p=p(\rho_{\rm scale}, \mu_{\rm against}-\mu_{\rm with},H,A)\), there is a percolation threshold for macroscopic felting. **Near-miss literature.** Wool felting work identifies DFE, scale morphology, crimp, fineness, and medullation as relevant, but does not formulate a directed percolation/contact-complex theorem.
([researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237560461_A_Comparative_Study_on_the_Felting_Propensity_of_Animal_Fibers?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Problem D. Follicle orientation fields and whorl index

**Definition task.** Let \(S\) be a scalp or animal body patch with boundary and \(v\) a follicle orientation line field.
Define whorl index for singularities of \(v\).

**Theorem target.** Under smooth follicle-field deformation without birth/death of singularities in the interior, total index is conserved up to boundary flux.

**Experimental need.** High-resolution follicle direction maps over scalp/fur surfaces, with whorl, part-line, and cowlick annotations.

### Problem E. Crimp as intrinsic curvature

**Model.** Treat wool crimp as periodic intrinsic curvature/torsion:

\[ \Omega_0(s)= (a_1\sin ks,\ a_2\cos ks,\ \tau_0+\tau_1\sin(k s+\phi)). \]

**Inverse problem.** Given relaxed 3D fiber centerlines from imaging, estimate \(\Omega_0\), anisotropic bending tensor \(B\), and humidity response.

**Proof-agent task.** Formalize identifiability conditions: when do centerline observations under multiple humidity/load states determine intrinsic curvature up to frame gauge?

### Problem F. Yarn drafting as a stochastic functor

**Definition task.** Let \(\mathsf{KerPop}\) be a category whose objects are probability measures on fiber morphology state spaces and whose morphisms are processing operations: sorting, washing, carding, combing, drafting, twisting, finishing.

**Theorem target.** Under moment-bounded drafting kernels, mean fiber alignment increases monotonically while length variance transforms by a computable contraction/selection operator.

**Experimental need.** Open datasets containing raw fleece/fiber distributions before and after each processing stage.

### Problem G. Keratin-specific knit energy

**Definition task.** A knitted keratin yarn is a loop complex plus a yarn-sheaf stalk containing twist, hairiness, fiber migration, humidity, and DFE.

**Theorem target.** Two yarns with identical loop topology and identical dry elastic constants can have different laddering thresholds if their frictional/damage stalks differ.

**Near-miss literature.** Recent knitting mechanics connects stitch topology to elasticity and laddering, but not to keratin cuticle direction, crimp, humidity, or staple-fiber hairiness.
([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-46498-z?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Problem H. Linear keratin sheaf Laplacian

**Definition task.** Build a cellular sheaf over a contact graph with vector stalks:

\[ x_i=(d_i,\kappa_i,\tau_i,\mu_i^+,\mu_i^-,D_i,H_i) \]

and edge restrictions predicting contact compatibility.

**Theorem target.** For linear restrictions over real inner-product spaces, \(\ker L_{\mathcal F}\) equals the space of global sections. **Sketch.** This is the standard cellular-sheaf Hodge argument; the work is to instantiate physically meaningful stalks and restrictions.
([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41468-019-00038-7?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Problem I. Lattice/Tarski keratin sheaf

**Definition task.** Let stalks be finite lattices for damage, medullation class, cuticle integrity, pilling state, and felt-lock state.

**Theorem target.** A monotone Tarski Laplacian iteration converges to a fixed point representing a globally compatible processing/damage state under appropriate Galois-connection restrictions.

**Why useful.** Damage and felting are often ordinal and irreversible; forcing them into vector spaces is artificial.
Tarski sheaves are a plausible better fit.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04099?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Problem J. Persistent humidity sheaf Laplacian

**Filtration.** Let \(X_H\) be the contact complex at humidity \(H\), or the subcomplex of contacts activated below a load threshold at humidity \(H\).

**Invariant.** Persistent sheaf spectrum

\[ \lambda_k(H_1,H_2) \]

measures stability of coherent material states across humidity changes.

**Theorem target.** If restrictions vary Lipschitz-continuously with humidity and the base filtration changes by controlled interleavings, then low sheaf eigenvalues vary stably.

**Near-miss literature.** Persistent sheaf Laplacians already encode physical labels on point clouds, but not keratin humidity/contact data.
([aimsciences.org](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/fods.
2024033?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Problem K. Dataset specification

A useful open dataset would include, for the same fibers:

- SEM cuticle images along root-to-tip position;
- AFM topography and friction maps;
- Raman/FTIR/SHG orientation and chemistry;
- tensile/bending/torsion tests under controlled humidity;
- diameter/ellipticity/taper;
- medulla segmentation;
- species/breed/body-region metadata;
- processing history;
- fiber-fiber friction for controlled relative orientations;
- µCT of yarn/fabric/felt made from the same population.

This dataset would make it possible to learn restriction maps rather than invent them.
