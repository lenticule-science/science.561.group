abstract: "This survey maps the active frontiers of mathematical physics as of June 2026. Its organizing thesis is that the leading edge has shifted from the classical problem of solving equations on a given spacetime toward the structural problem of defining, constructing, comparing, and computing physical theories themselves. The central programs are rigorous quantum field theory, probabilistic Euclidean QFT, algebraic and factorization approaches to locality, quantum-information approaches to spacetime emergence, celestial holography, generalized and non-invertible symmetries, mixed-state and noisy topological order, positive geometry for amplitudes and cosmological correlators, geometric Langlands/mirror symmetry, random geometry and singular stochastic PDE, black-hole stability and cosmic censorship in mathematical relativity, and machine-checked formalization of mathematical physics. The survey emphasizes the conceptual convergence among these programs: local-to-global structures, defect algebras, categorical and operator-algebraic invariants, monotone/preorder classifications, reconstruction from boundary or asymptotic data, and rigorous control of renormalization."keywords:

# Mathematical Physics at the 2026 Frontier

*A research survey of quantum fields, quantum spacetime, categorical symmetry, positive geometry, random geometry, and the mathematics of emergence.*

**Date of survey:** 26 June 2026 **Orientation:** This survey is written for a mathematically strong reader who wants the actual conceptual shape of the frontier, not a press-release digest.
It assumes comfort with abstract algebra, topology, category theory, PDE, probability, and quantum theory, but tries to keep the hierarchy of problems legible.

## Executive thesis

The frontier of mathematical physics in 2026 is not one frontier.
It is a web of frontiers organized around a single pressure: **modern physics no longer merely asks for solutions of equations on a fixed spacetime; it asks for mathematically precise definitions of physical theories themselves, and for ways to compare, construct, compute, deform, and extract observables from those theories.**

That pressure appears in different dialects.

In rigorous quantum field theory, the question is still: *What is a four-dimensional interacting quantum field theory as an actual mathematical object?* The Yang-Mills mass gap remains the iconic target [@clay_yang_mills], while perturbative algebraic QFT, constructive QFT, stochastic quantization, and factorization algebras supply partial but increasingly sophisticated answers [@brunetti_fredenhagen_rejzner_2025_paqft; @simons_probabilistic_paths_qft_2025; @costello_gwilliam_factorization].

In quantum gravity, the question becomes: *Can spacetime be reconstructed from entanglement, boundary data, asymptotic symmetry, quantum error correction, and complexity?* Holography, celestial holography, and quantum-information approaches now supply the live mathematical laboratory [@takayanagi_2025_emergent_holographic_spacetime; @simons_celestial_2026; @zhu_2026_celestial_bottom_up].

In symmetry, the question has changed most radically: *What replaces group symmetry when the symmetry operators are defects, interfaces, higher-form operators, or non-invertible categorical objects?* This is now a major organizing program across high-energy theory, condensed matter, topology, and category theory [@simons_categorical_symmetries_2025; @schafer_nameki_2023_lectures; @shao_2023_noninvertible].

In topological phases and quantum information, the frontier is no longer only classification of pure gapped phases up to equivalence.
It is increasingly about **mixed states, noise, quantum channels, monotones, preorders, operator-algebraic locality, and computable categorical invariants** [@ogata_2025_mixed_state_operator; @ogata_2026_invariants_mixed; @sohal_2025_noisy_topological_order].

In amplitudes and cosmology, the frontier asks whether Feynman diagrams are the wrong primitive.
Positive geometry, cluster algebras, symbol bootstrap, Landau analysis, and cosmological correlators suggest that locality and unitarity may be emergent consequences of deeper geometric/combinatorial structures [@ranestad_sturmfels_telen_2025_positive_geometry; @raman_2026_positivity_amplitudes; @lam_2025_combinatorial_geometry; @paranjape_2026_cluster_bootstrap_cosmological].

In geometry and representation theory, QFT continues to act as a concept generator.
Geometric Langlands, mirror symmetry, Fukaya categories, sheaf theory, derived geometry, and noncommutative geometry are not peripheral decorations: they are now among the deepest languages through which physical duality becomes mathematical theorem [@gaitsgory_raskin_2024_glc1; @gaitsgory_raskin_2024_glc5; @kapustin_witten_2006_geometric_langlands; @simons_hms_site].

In probability and statistical mechanics, the frontier is the renormalization of randomness: singular stochastic PDEs, Liouville quantum gravity, Schramm-Loewner evolution, Gaussian multiplicative chaos, KPZ universality, active matter, and turbulence all confront the problem of extracting universal structures from noisy, scale-coupled, non-equilibrium systems [@holden_sun_2025_lqg; @clay_spde_recent_trends; @corwin_2011_kpz; @scgp_turbulence_2025].

In mathematical relativity, the frontier remains a PDE/geometric analysis program around black-hole stability, cosmic censorship, gravitational collapse, apparent horizons, Penrose inequalities, and the nonlinear global behavior of Einstein's equations [@klainerman_2025_black_hole_stability; @van_de_moortel_strong_cosmic_censorship; @an_he_2025_kerr_black_hole_formation].

Finally, a new meta-frontier has appeared: **formalized mathematical physics**.
Machine-checked formalizations of quantum field theory and quantum information in Lean are still modest compared with the ambitions of the subject, but they signal a real change in how extended arguments in mathematical physics may eventually be audited, reused, and composed [@douglas_hoback_mei_nissim_2026_formal_qft; @meiburg_lessa_soldati_2025_quantum_stein; @ren_li_qi_2026_merlean].

The compressed thesis is this:

> **Mathematical physics is becoming the study of physically meaningful local-to-global systems under quantum, probabilistic, categorical, geometric, and information-theoretic constraints.**

The rest of the survey unpacks that thesis.

## 1. The old central problem has not gone away: construct QFT

The deepest unresolved mathematical problem at the center of physics is still the construction and classification of quantum field theories, especially interacting four-dimensional gauge theories.
The Clay problem on Yang-Mills and the mass gap is not just a prize problem; it is a precise emblem of the larger failure of mathematics to give a fully rigorous nonperturbative definition of the theories physicists actually use [@clay_yang_mills].

There are several different meanings of “construct QFT.” They are not equivalent in practice, and the frontier partly consists in translating among them.

### 1.1 Wightman/Osterwalder-Schrader/constructive QFT

The classical constructive program seeks actual fields, correlation functions, Hilbert spaces, operator-valued distributions, or Euclidean measures satisfying axioms such as Wightman or Osterwalder-Schrader.
It achieved profound successes in low-dimensional scalar and constructive models, but four-dimensional nonabelian gauge theory remains beyond reach.

The obstacle is not merely technical.
Quantum fields are distributional, nonlinear products require renormalization, gauge redundancy complicates state spaces, and the physical observables of gauge theory are often nonlocal or extended.
The Yang-Mills action formally suggests a measure

\[ \mathrm{d}\mu(A) \sim \exp\left(-\frac{1}{4g^2}\int \operatorname{Tr}(F_A \wedge *F_A)\right)\mathcal{D}A, \]

but this expression is not a measure in the naive sense.
The problem is to make a continuum probability or operator-theoretic object out of it, remove cutoffs, maintain gauge covariance, prove locality/reflection positivity, and extract a mass gap.

The recent stochastic quantization program attacks this by replacing the impossible direct construction of the measure with a stochastic dynamics whose invariant measure should be the desired Euclidean field theory.
Work on stochastic Yang-Mills and Yang-Mills-Higgs in two and three dimensions shows how singular SPDE methods, regularity structures, gauge covariance, and renormalization counterterms can be combined [@chevyrev_2022_stochastic_yang_mills; @chandra_chevyrev_hairer_shen_2022_yang_mills_higgs].
Recent work on uniqueness of gauge-covariant renormalization strengthens the relation between the stochastic dynamics and canonical/gauge-invariant limits [@chevyrev_shen_2025_gauge_covariant_renormalisation].
A 2026 tree-free regularity-structure approach to 3D Yang-Mills Langevin dynamics is a sign that the analytic technology is still being actively rebuilt [@sevostyanov_2026_tree_free_ym].

The frontiers here are:

1. **From 2D/3D to 4D.** 2D Yang-Mills is much better controlled; 3D is singular but tractable in pieces.
  Four dimensions remain the realistic and brutal case.
2. **Gauge-covariant renormalization.** It is not enough to renormalize; one must renormalize compatibly with gauge symmetry.
3. **Measure construction versus dynamics.** Stochastic quantization gives a Markov process.
  Turning that into a full Euclidean QFT satisfying reconstruction axioms is a separate problem.
4. **Wilson loops and extended observables.** Gauge theory is naturally expressed through holonomies and line/surface observables, not only point fields.
5. **Mass gap and confinement.** Even if a measure is constructed, proving exponential decay/mass gap and confinement is another layer.

The frontier is therefore not “somebody might prove Yang-Mills tomorrow.” It is that the analytic technology for singular random gauge fields is becoming precise enough to approach lower-dimensional analogues of the problem in a way that did not exist a generation ago.

### 1.2 Algebraic QFT and perturbative algebraic QFT

Algebraic quantum field theory begins from observables rather than fields.
A theory assigns to spacetime regions \(O\) algebras \(\mathcal{A}(O)\), satisfying locality/isotony/covariance conditions.
In this view, the field is secondary; the local algebra of measurements is primary.

Perturbative algebraic QFT, as reviewed by Brunetti, Fredenhagen, and Rejzner, combines Haag-Kastler-style locality with perturbative renormalization and works naturally on globally hyperbolic curved spacetimes [@brunetti_fredenhagen_rejzner_2025_paqft].
Its distinctive strength is conceptual: it does not require a preferred vacuum or a global Poincaré group.
It is therefore a rigorous language for QFT in curved spacetime.

This matters because quantum gravity needs QFT in curved spacetime not as a heuristic but as a controlled limiting regime.
Hawking radiation, semiclassical Einstein equations, local covariance, and perturbative interactions all demand an algebraic formulation that does not collapse outside Minkowski space.

The frontier problems are:

- constructing nonperturbative nets of algebras for interacting theories;
- connecting perturbative formal power series to genuine analytic objects;
- understanding states and superselection sectors in curved backgrounds;
- integrating gauge theories, gravity, and BV/BRST methods into algebraic locality;
- relating algebraic QFT to factorization algebras, conformal nets, and operator-algebraic approaches to phases of matter.

AQFT’s importance is that it says: **locality is not merely geometric adjacency; locality is algebraic inclusion and commutation.** That shift is one of the main bridges to modern topological phases and categorical symmetry.

### 1.3 Factorization algebras and BV geometry

Factorization algebras package local observables in a way reminiscent of sheaves, but adapted to disjoint unions and operator products.
Roughly, a factorization algebra assigns cochain complexes or spaces of observables to open sets, together with multiplication maps for disjoint opens embedded in a larger open.
It is a local-to-global machine for quantum observables [@costello_gwilliam_factorization].

This is one of the most important mathematical languages for perturbative QFT because it naturally accommodates:

- BV formalism;
- derived critical loci;
- observables as cochains;
- deformation theory;
- descent/local-to-global gluing;
- connections to \(E_n\)-algebras, chiral algebras, and vertex algebras;
- perturbative quantization as obstruction theory.

Factorization algebras are not “just another formalism.” They identify QFT with a kind of **structured cosheaf of observables**, and that makes QFT legible to homological algebra, derived geometry, and higher categories.

A 2026 paper on states and infrared divergences in factorization algebras signals one of the important pressure points: QFT is often locally well-behaved but globally or asymptotically subtle [@ptep_2026_ir_factorization].
Infrared divergences, choice of state, asymptotic sectors, and long-range gauge/gravitational fields resist clean finite-region descriptions.
This connects factorization methods directly to the celestial/asymptotic program.

### 1.4 The conformal bootstrap as nonperturbative constraint geometry

Conformal field theories are fixed points of renormalization group flows and often serve as landmarks in the space of QFTs.
The conformal bootstrap treats consistency conditions—crossing symmetry, unitarity, conformal symmetry, positivity—as enough to constrain or solve the data of a CFT.

The modern numerical conformal bootstrap turned this philosophy into a computational program.
Its achievements include high-precision determinations of critical exponents and operator data in models such as 3D Ising and \(O(N)\) models [@poland_rychkov_vichi_2018_bootstrap; @rychkov_su_2023_numerical_bootstrap].
Recent historical and conceptual surveys emphasize the bootstrap as a central example of unity across strong interactions, critical phenomena, CFT, holography, and numerical convex optimization [@rychkov_2025_polyakov_bootstrap].

The deep frontier is not only better numerics.
It is the geometry of the space of consistent QFTs.
The bootstrap suggests that the possible spectra/OPE coefficients of a theory form constrained convex or semialgebraic regions.
It converts “existence of a QFT” into “existence of a point satisfying infinitely many positivity and crossing constraints.”

Open frontiers include:

- defect and boundary bootstrap;
- mixed correlator bootstrap;
- Lorentzian inversion and analytic bootstrap;
- bootstrapping nonunitary and logarithmic CFTs;
- connecting numerical bounds to rigorous existence/nonexistence theorems;
- relating CFT data to lattice regularizations such as fuzzy sphere methods;
- extending bootstrap logic to de Sitter/cosmological correlators and scattering amplitudes.

In short: the bootstrap is a **consistency-first** approach to QFT.
It does not build fields out of bare Lagrangians; it asks what correlation data can consistently exist.

## 2. Probabilistic QFT and random geometry: renormalizing randomness

One of the clearest current signals is the establishment of the Simons Collaboration on Probabilistic Paths to Quantum Field Theory, whose aim is a unified probabilistic foundation for Euclidean QFT, bringing together stochastic analysis, random geometry, and structural QFT [@simons_probabilistic_paths_qft_2025; @simons_probabilistic_paths_qft_2026_meeting; @probabilistic_qft_site].
This is a real frontier marker.

The reason is straightforward: Euclidean QFT often wants to be a probability measure on distributions.
But the random objects are singular, nonlinear functions of them are ill-defined, and renormalization is required to make sense of the limiting object.
Probability theory, SPDE, and random geometry are now providing tools that look structurally comparable to what functional analysis and distribution theory did for earlier generations of physics.

### 2.1 Singular SPDE and stochastic quantization

Singular SPDE theory, including regularity structures and paracontrolled distributions, gave rigorous meaning to equations that were previously formal because they involved products of distributions.
The KPZ equation is the canonical example, but the relevance to QFT is direct: stochastic quantization equations for \(\Phi^4\), sine-Gordon, Yang-Mills, and related theories are singular SPDEs [@bauerschmidt_2024_qft_spde; @clay_spde_recent_trends].

The Parisi-Wu idea of stochastic quantization turns a Euclidean field theory into the invariant measure of a Langevin dynamics:

\[ \partial_t \phi = -\frac{\delta S}{\delta \phi} + \xi, \]

where \(\xi\) is space-time white noise.
This is beautiful because it turns a static measure-construction problem into a dynamical problem.
It is brutal because \(\xi\) is too rough, and the nonlinear terms must be renormalized.

For scalar models in two dimensions, stochastic quantization can provide clean constructions satisfying Osterwalder-Schrader axioms [@duch_2025_p_phi].
For gauge theories, stochastic quantization must also respect gauge covariance, producing a much subtler state-space and quotient-space problem [@chevyrev_2022_stochastic_yang_mills; @chandra_chevyrev_hairer_shen_2022_yang_mills_higgs].

The core mathematical issue is this:

> **Renormalization is no longer merely a physicist's subtraction recipe.
> It is a construction of a canonical object in a category of singular stochastic/geometric structures.**

### 2.2 Liouville quantum gravity and random surfaces

Liouville quantum gravity (LQG), Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE), random planar maps, Brownian maps, Gaussian free fields, and Gaussian multiplicative chaos form one of the deepest probability/math-physics interfaces.
A 2025 review by Holden and Sun describes LQG as both the random conformal geometry of scaling limits of random planar maps and as connected to Liouville conformal field theory [@holden_sun_2025_lqg].

This field is a model of what success can look like in mathematical physics:

1. Physics predicts universal scaling limits of random discretized surfaces.
2. Probability constructs continuum random surfaces and curves.
3. Complex analysis supplies conformal invariance.
4. CFT supplies correlation functions and operator insertions.
5. Discrete models converge to continuum objects under appropriate embeddings.

The frontier is not merely “2D quantum gravity.” It is a rigorous theory of random geometry as a continuum limit.
The mathematical question is how discrete combinatorial surfaces, random maps, random curves, and conformal field theories become one object.
The physical question is how much of this paradigm can survive outside two dimensions.

### 2.3 KPZ universality and stochastic growth

KPZ universality describes a broad class of random growth, directed polymers, exclusion processes, last-passage percolation, and stochastic PDE.
The classic survey by Corwin already framed KPZ as a universal object connecting stochastic PDE, integrable probability, and random matrices [@corwin_2011_kpz].
A 2026 workshop devoted to KPZ universality and related topics describes the field as connecting random growing interfaces, exclusion processes, directed polymers, last passage percolation, random matrix theory, stochastic PDE, combinatorics, and integrable systems [@brin_kpz_2026].

The frontier here is not only exact solvability.
It is the relation among:

- exact integrable formulas;
- universality beyond exactly solvable models;
- singular SPDE methods;
- multi-dimensional generalizations;
- non-equilibrium scaling limits;
- experimental realizations.

KPZ is a prototype for how mathematical physics now studies universality: not by solving every microscopic model, but by proving convergence to a continuum stochastic object.

### 2.4 Active matter, non-equilibrium physics, and pressure without equilibrium

Active matter—systems whose components consume energy to move—is not a solved branch of statistical mechanics.
It is an assault on equilibrium assumptions.
A 2025 review on pressure in active matter emphasizes that pressure, effective temperature, swim pressure, and equation-of-state ideas become fragile outside equilibrium [@yu_et_al_2025_active_pressure].

For mathematical physics, active matter matters because it forces extensions of statistical mechanics beyond Gibbs measures.
The frontier objects are:

- entropy production;
- large deviations for non-equilibrium steady states;
- hydrodynamic limits with active stresses;
- motility-induced phase separation;
- fluctuation-response violations;
- stochastic thermodynamics;
- field theories with broken detailed balance.

This area is not as algebraically glamorous as geometric Langlands or categorical symmetry, but it is foundational: most physical systems are not at equilibrium.
A mathematics of non-equilibrium collective order is necessary if mathematical physics is to describe living, driven, open, and computational matter.

### 2.5 Turbulence and wave turbulence

Turbulence remains one of the most famous mathematical-physics frontiers.
The SCGP 2025 program on turbulence emphasized new experimental/numerical data and progress in rigorous proofs of validity domains of wave kinetic equations and weak turbulence theory [@scgp_turbulence_2025].

Wave turbulence has become especially important because it offers a more tractable route to deriving kinetic equations from Hamiltonian wave systems.
The frontier is to justify statistical closures, cascade laws, resonant interactions, and kinetic limits from deterministic or weakly random microscopic dynamics.

The mathematical themes are:

- derivation of kinetic equations;
- control of resonances;
- long-time limits;
- singular measures and cascades;
- anomalous dissipation;
- universality of spectra;
- numerical-data-driven conjecture formation.

This frontier is conceptually aligned with QFT renormalization: both ask how many-scale nonlinear systems generate effective laws.

## 3. Quantum gravity as quantum information and reconstruction

Quantum gravity remains a landscape of programs rather than a single theorem-proving agenda.
But the most mathematically active frontier is clear: **spacetime emergence from quantum information**.

Holographic duality describes gravitational theories in terms of lower-dimensional quantum systems.
In the modern formulation, entanglement entropy, tensor networks, quantum error correction, modular flow, complexity, and relative entropy are not metaphors; they are technical tools for reconstructing geometry [@takayanagi_2025_emergent_holographic_spacetime; @bousso_dong_et_al_2022_black_holes_emergence].

### 3.1 Entanglement as geometry

The Ryu-Takayanagi and quantum extremal surface paradigms suggest that geometric area is computed by entanglement entropy.
This transformed a geometric problem into an information-theoretic one.

The rough form is:

\[ S_A = \frac{\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4G_N} + S_{\text{bulk}} + \cdots, \]

where \(S_A\) is boundary entanglement entropy and \(\gamma_A\) is an extremal surface in the bulk.
The formula’s mathematical force is not just that entropy computes area.
It is that bulk geometry can be constrained or reconstructed from entanglement data.

The open problems include:

- deriving smooth bulk locality from finite-dimensional quantum systems;
- understanding finite-\(N\) corrections;
- classifying which quantum states have semiclassical geometric duals;
- making quantum error correction exact enough to describe black hole interiors;
- extending holography beyond AdS;
- understanding time, complexity, and pseudo-entropy;
- relating quantum information inequalities to energy conditions and gravitational dynamics.

### 3.2 Quantum error correction and bulk reconstruction

The quantum error-correction interpretation of holography says that bulk information is redundantly encoded in boundary degrees of freedom.
This explains why bulk operators may be reconstructed on different boundary regions and why locality behind horizons is subtle.

Mathematically, the frontier asks for a precise relation among:

- von Neumann algebras;
- code subspaces;
- operator-algebra quantum error correction;
- modular inclusions;
- entanglement wedge reconstruction;
- approximate recovery;
- gravitational dressing.

This is not merely applied quantum information.
It is a candidate for the algebraic architecture of emergent spacetime.

### 3.3 Black holes, islands, and the information paradox

Recent black-hole information work showed how island formulas can reproduce Page-curve behavior in semiclassical gravity models [@bousso_dong_et_al_2022_black_holes_emergence].
This is one of the biggest conceptual changes in quantum gravity: semiclassical gravitational path integrals, including replica wormholes, seem to know about unitary evaporation.

The open mathematical problems remain severe:

- What is the Hilbert space of quantum gravity?
- What is the exact status of replica wormholes?
- Are islands derived from an underlying microscopic theory or a rule of effective gravity?
- How does one define local algebras in a theory with diffeomorphism constraints?
- What is the black-hole interior as an operator-algebraic object?
- How do these ideas extend to realistic cosmology?

The frontier is now less “does information come out?” and more “what exact mathematical structure makes that statement meaningful?”

### 3.4 Celestial holography

Celestial holography is one of the sharpest current frontiers because it attempts holography in asymptotically flat spacetimes rather than AdS.
The Simons Collaboration describes its goal as finding realizations of the holographic principle for quantum gravity in nearly flat spacetimes, using top-down string/twistor theory and bottom-up soft theorem/asymptotic symmetry methods [@simons_celestial_2026; @celestial_collaboration_events].

The basic idea is to recast the four-dimensional gravitational \(S\)-matrix as correlation functions of a putative two-dimensional conformal theory on the celestial sphere [@pasterski_pate_raclariu_2021_celestial].
A 2026 review emphasizes symmetries, celestial CFT ingredients, twistor theory, and relations to AdS/CFT [@zhu_2026_celestial_bottom_up].

The frontier objects are:

- BMS symmetry and extensions;
- soft theorems as Ward identities;
- memory effects;
- celestial amplitudes via Mellin transforms;
- asymptotic charges;
- \(w_{1+\infty}\)-type algebras;
- Carrollian and twistor structures;
- celestial operator product expansions;
- flat-space limits of AdS/CFT.

The key mathematical problem is whether the celestial dictionary defines a genuine theory, not merely a rewriting of amplitudes.
The key physical problem is whether this theory captures nonperturbative quantum gravity in flat space.

### 3.5 de Sitter and cosmology

A glaring weakness of standard holography is that our universe is not asymptotically AdS. de Sitter holography and cosmological correlator programs are attempts to derive observables appropriate for inflationary or late-time cosmological spacetimes.

The central problem is that de Sitter lacks the same kind of spatial boundary and global timelike Hamiltonian structure as AdS.
Observables are subtle.
The cosmological bootstrap and wavefunction-of-the-universe methods try to compute boundary correlators using symmetry, singularity structure, unitarity-like constraints, and analytic continuation.

This is where quantum gravity, amplitudes, positive geometry, and cosmology begin to merge.

## 4. The new symmetry frontier: groups are not enough

The conceptual revolution in symmetry is that “symmetry” no longer means only a group acting invertibly on local fields.
Modern quantum systems possess symmetries implemented by topological defects, higher-dimensional operators, fusion categories, higher categories, and non-invertible interfaces.

The Simons Collaboration on Global Categorical Symmetries frames this explicitly as studying generalized symmetries in QFT, especially topological symmetries implemented by extended operators, using higher categories, fusion/braided categories, homotopy theory, and topological/higher-categorical dualities [@simons_categorical_symmetries_2025; @global_categorical_symmetries_site].

### 4.1 Higher-form symmetries

Ordinary symmetries act on pointlike local operators and have codimension-1 topological defect operators.
Higher-form symmetries act on extended objects: line operators, surface operators, branes.
A \(p\)-form symmetry has charged \(p\)-dimensional operators and symmetry operators of codimension \(p+1\).

These symmetries are essential in gauge theory because Wilson lines, ’t Hooft lines, and surface operators are often more natural than point fields.
Higher-form symmetry constrains confinement, deconfinement, anomalies, and phases.

The mathematical objects include:

- higher groups;
- cohomology operations;
- generalized global symmetries;
- topological operators;
- extended TQFT;
- higher-categorical representation theory.

### 4.2 Non-invertible symmetries

A non-invertible symmetry is implemented by a topological operator/defect that cannot be undone by another operator.
Instead of group multiplication, defects fuse by rules such as

\[ D_a \otimes D_b \cong \bigoplus_c N_{ab}^c D_c. \]

This is category-like rather than group-like.
The fusion coefficients \(N_{ab}^c\) are not merely representation-theoretic decoration; they determine dualities, selection rules, anomalies, and possible phases.

Lectures by Schafer-Nameki and Shao survey how non-invertible symmetries appear across spacetime dimensions and connect high-energy theory, condensed matter, quantum gravity, and category theory [@schafer_nameki_2023_lectures; @shao_2023_noninvertible].
Bhardwaj’s work on non-invertible higher-categorical symmetries shows how gauging ordinary symmetries can produce higher-categorical non-invertible structures [@bhardwaj_2022_higher_categorical].

The frontier questions include:

- What is the correct general definition of symmetry in arbitrary dimension?
- Which non-invertible symmetries can be gauged?
- How do anomalies generalize?
- How do defects act on local operators?
- Which non-invertible symmetries are intrinsic rather than secretly ordinary after extension?
- How do non-invertible symmetries classify or constrain RG flows?
- How are they realized on lattices and in experiments?

Recent work on anomalies of non-invertible symmetries in \((3+1)d\) uses higher-dimensional inflow TQFTs and defect data to constrain symmetric phases [@cordova_hsin_zhang_2023_anomalies].
A 2026 paper on the action of non-invertible symmetries on local operators in \((3+1)d\) argues that, under certain conditions, their local action decomposes through invertible action plus a gauging interface [@putrov_radhakrishnan_2026_noninvertible_local_operators].
Work on non-Abelian topological orders in three dimensions constructs non-invertible higher-form symmetries directly from conserved currents in twisted \(BF\) theories and studies mixed anomalies [@zhang_huang_wang_ye_2026_currents].

### 4.3 SymTFT and topological holography

The symmetry topological field theory, or SymTFT, approach encodes the symmetry data of a lower-dimensional theory in a topological theory one dimension higher.
Instead of treating symmetry as external structure, one builds a bulk TQFT whose boundaries correspond to theories with that symmetry.

This is a profound reframing:

- The symmetry is not a group; it is a bulk topological order.
- Boundary conditions become different realizations of the symmetry.
- Gauging and duality become changes of boundary condition.
- Anomalies become obstructions to trivializing the bulk.
- Non-invertible symmetries become natural, not exceptional.

SymTFT connects directly to condensed matter notions of topological holography and to high-energy generalized symmetry.
It also makes the “space of theories with a given symmetry” into something navigable by categorical/topological operations.

### 4.4 Why this matters

The generalized-symmetry revolution is not terminology inflation.
It changes what a phase diagram is.
It changes what a conservation law is.
It changes what anomaly matching means.
It changes how confinement is characterized.
It gives a new interface between lattice models, continuum QFT, tensor categories, and higher algebra.

The older Landau paradigm classified phases by symmetry breaking.
The topological-order paradigm classified phases by long-range entanglement and anyonic data.
The current categorical-symmetry paradigm aims to unify both by treating symmetries, dualities, defects, phases, and transitions inside one extended topological/categorical calculus.

## 5. Topological phases, mixed states, and the shift from equivalence to preorder

Topological phases of matter are a central mathematical-physics frontier because they require topology, operator algebras, tensor categories, homotopy theory, quantum information, and many-body physics simultaneously.

### 5.1 Pure-state topological order

In \((2+1)d\), topological order is often described by modular tensor categories or unitary braided fusion categories.
Anyons, braiding, fusion, topological spins, and modular \(S,T\) matrices encode robust long-range data.

Mathematically, one wants to relate:

- lattice Hamiltonians;
- ground-state spaces;
- superselection sectors;
- DHR categories;
- tensor-network constructions;
- TQFTs;
- operator-algebraic nets;
- categorical/homotopy classifications.

The Harvard CMSA 2025 workshop on QFT and topological phases via homotopy theory and operator algebras explicitly focused on operator algebras, homotopy theory, higher category theory, the Kitaev conjecture, and the long-standing goal of a proper mathematical formulation of QFT [@cmsa_qft_topological_phases_2025].
That is a remarkably good snapshot of the frontier: the communities are converging because the objects are the same from different angles.

### 5.2 Local topological order and boundary algebras

Jones, Naaijkens, Penneys, and Wallick introduced local topological order axioms and boundary algebras for quantum spin systems, giving a mathematically precise algebraic description of a holographic dual of bulk topological order [@jones_naaijkens_penneys_wallick_2023_boundary].
Their later work on bulk-boundary local topological order extends these axioms to systems with topological boundaries and constructs boundary algebra structures for Levin-Wen and Walker-Wang systems [@jones_naaijkens_penneys_2025_bulk_boundary].

This line is important because it gives a local/operator-algebraic route from lattice models to categorical topological invariants.
It also resonates with holography: bulk topological order is remembered by boundary representation theory.

The frontier problems include:

- proving Haag duality for broad classes of tensor-network states;
- classifying cone von Neumann algebras;
- relating DHR categories to unitary braided fusion categories;
- extending boundary algebra methods to chiral phases;
- connecting lattice local topological order to continuum TQFT;
- understanding defects and gapped boundaries categorically.

### 5.3 Mixed states and noisy topological order

The most striking conceptual shift is the move from pure states and unitary circuits to mixed states and quantum channels.
Pure-state topological phases are often classified up to finite-depth local unitaries or quasi-local automorphisms.
But noisy physical systems are mixed, and physical transformations include decoherence and dissipation.

Ogata’s operator-algebraic approach to mixed-state topological order associates braided \(C^*\)-tensor categories to mixed states satisfying an approximate Haag duality condition, and studies how these categories behave under finite-depth quantum channels [@ogata_2025_mixed_state_operator].
A 2026 note states the key conceptual point: mixed-state classification requires indices that behave monotonically under finite-depth quantum channels, so the structure is naturally a **preorder**, not merely an equivalence relation [@ogata_2026_invariants_mixed].

This is a major philosophical and mathematical turn.

For pure phases, the question is often:

\[ A \sim B ? \]

For mixed/noisy phases, the question becomes:

\[ A \succeq B ? \]

Can state \(A\) be degraded, decohered, or transformed into state \(B\) by allowed local quantum channels?
Which invariants can only decrease?
Which pieces of topological order are robust under noise, and which are not?

This connects topological phases to resource theories, quantum information, thermodynamics, and order theory.

### 5.4 Non-semisimple TQFT and topological quantum computation

Another live frontier is the relaxation of semisimplicity assumptions.
A 2025 Nature Communications paper proposes universal quantum computation using Ising anyons from a non-semisimple TQFT.
Standard Ising anyons are not universal by braiding alone; the non-semisimple extension introduces new anyon types that can enable universal computation [@iulianelli_kim_sussan_lauda_2025_ising_anyons].

The mathematical lesson is broad: structures previously discarded as negligible in semisimple settings may carry computationally meaningful information once the categorical framework is enlarged.

This intersects with:

- logarithmic CFT;
- non-semisimple modular tensor categories;
- modified traces;
- quantum groups at roots of unity;
- topological quantum computation;
- categorical representation theory.

The frontier is not only engineering fault-tolerant devices.
It is understanding what kinds of categorical/topological data can support computation.

### 5.5 Stable homotopy and the Kitaev conjecture

The Kitaev conjecture relates invertible phases of matter to generalized cohomology/stable homotopy objects.
Recent work aims to provide operator-algebraic realizations of these ideas for infinite quantum spin systems, including construction of spectra whose homotopy groups classify smooth homotopy classes of invertible gapped systems [@kubota_2025_kitaev_conjecture].

The slogan is:

> **The classification of gapped phases is becoming stable homotopy theory constrained by locality.**

This is another place where mathematical physics is not borrowing a tool from topology; it is creating a physical interpretation of deep homotopical structure.

## 6. Positive geometry, amplitudes, and the reconstruction of locality

The scattering-amplitudes frontier asks whether the traditional Feynman-diagram expansion is a computational artifact rather than the natural mathematical form of QFT.

Positive geometry arose from the discovery that certain scattering amplitudes can be encoded as canonical forms of geometric objects such as the positive Grassmannian, amplituhedron, associahedron, and cosmological polytopes.
A 2025 introduction describes positive geometry as an interdisciplinary subject inspired by particle physics and cosmology, where observables such as scattering amplitudes and cosmological correlators are recovered from geometric objects [@ranestad_sturmfels_telen_2025_positive_geometry].

### 6.1 Canonical forms as observables

A positive geometry is roughly a semialgebraic space \(X_{\ge 0}\) equipped with a canonical differential form \(\Omega(X)\) with logarithmic singularities on its boundaries.
In favorable cases,

\[ \Omega(X) = \text{scattering amplitude or integrand}. \]

This is conceptually radical because physical singularities, factorization, and locality may arise as boundary behavior of geometry.

The amplituhedron program began in planar \(\mathcal{N}=4\) super Yang-Mills, but positive geometry has expanded into a broader mathematical field involving polytopes, Grassmannians, cluster algebras, total positivity, and algebraic geometry [@lam_2025_combinatorial_geometry; @moerman_2023_positive_geometries].

### 6.2 Positivity beyond amplituhedra

Raman’s 2026 lecture notes on positivity properties of scattering amplitudes emphasize completely monotone and Stieltjes structures in QFT and their ability to constrain analytic behavior and extract global information from perturbative data [@raman_2026_positivity_amplitudes].
This is a different but related positivity frontier: positivity as analytic control.

There are several interacting positivity notions:

- positivity of Grassmannians and amplituhedra;
- positivity bounds from unitarity/causality/analyticity;
- complete monotonicity and Stieltjes functions;
- positive geometries of cosmological correlators;
- semidefinite positivity in conformal bootstrap;
- positivity in effective field theory constraints.

The frontier lies in unifying these.
Are they projections of one deeper positivity principle, or a family of related but distinct constraints?

### 6.3 Symbol bootstrap and Landau geometry

Loop amplitudes are not just numbers; they are functions with intricate singularity and branch-cut structures.
Symbol alphabets, cluster adjacency, Landau singularities, and polylogarithmic functions form a bridge between algebraic geometry and perturbative QFT.

Recent work on geometric Landau analysis and symbol bootstrap uses positive geometry to constrain integrated amplitudes by associating Landau diagrams to geometric boundaries and distinguishing physical from spurious singularities [@chicherin_henn_mazzucchelli_trnka_yang_zhang_2025].

The frontier problems include:

- identifying symbol alphabets geometrically;
- understanding elliptic and more general functions beyond polylogs;
- controlling multi-loop analytic structure;
- connecting local integrand geometry to integrated amplitudes;
- extending beyond planar/supersymmetric theories;
- connecting with number theory, motives, and periods.

### 6.4 Cosmological correlators and the cosmological bootstrap

Cosmological observables are not ordinary scattering amplitudes because cosmological spacetimes do not have the same asymptotic in/out structure.
But cosmological correlators still obey constraints from locality, unitarity, symmetry, and singularity structure.

A 2026 cluster bootstrap paper finds cluster-variable structures in cosmological wavefunction coefficients for chain and loop graphs in de Sitter scalar theory [@paranjape_2026_cluster_bootstrap_cosmological].
A 2026 work on cosmological correlator discontinuities relates discontinuities of cosmological correlators to cuts of flat-space Feynman diagrams using auxiliary propagators, yielding sum-rule constraints [@cosmological_correlator_discontinuities_2026].

The frontier is therefore a three-way interface:

1. scattering amplitudes;
2. cosmological wavefunctions/correlators;
3. positive/cluster/algebraic geometry.

This is mathematically physics at its sharpest: the birth of the universe becomes a problem about analytic functions, singularities, and geometry.

## 7. Geometric Langlands, mirror symmetry, and QFT as theorem engine

Mathematical physics does not only seek rigorous foundations for physics.
It also produces mathematics.
The most spectacular recent example is geometric Langlands.

### 7.1 Geometric Langlands after the Gaitsgory-Raskin proof

The geometric Langlands correspondence relates categories of sheaves on moduli stacks of \(G\)-bundles to quasi-coherent sheaves on stacks of local systems for the Langlands dual group \(G^\vee\).
The proof series by Gaitsgory, Raskin, and collaborators constructs the functor and proves the conjecture in characteristic-zero settings [@gaitsgory_raskin_2024_glc1; @gaitsgory_raskin_2024_glc5].
Quanta’s 2024 report notes that the proof may also sharpen function-field Langlands directions [@quanta_2024_geometric_langlands].

For mathematical physics, geometric Langlands matters because of the Kapustin-Witten interpretation via \(N=4\) super Yang-Mills, electric-magnetic duality, branes, and topological twisting [@kapustin_witten_2006_geometric_langlands].
The physics did not prove the theorem, but it supplied a conceptual architecture for why such a duality should exist.

The frontier after proof is not “done.” It includes:

- ramified and local geometric Langlands;
- quantum geometric Langlands;
- Betti/de Rham/Dolbeault variants;
- relation to Fargues-Fontaine and arithmetic Langlands;
- categorified traces and sheaf-function dictionaries;
- physical interpretations of the proof’s constructions;
- extended TQFT and boundary-condition reformulations.

The lesson is that QFT can act as a machine for producing equivalences between categories that pure mathematics then has to make exact.

### 7.2 Mirror symmetry and homological mirror symmetry

Mirror symmetry began as a string-theoretic duality between sigma models on mirror Calabi-Yau manifolds.
Kontsevich reframed it as homological mirror symmetry: an equivalence between the derived category of coherent sheaves on one side and the Fukaya category of the mirror symplectic manifold or Landau-Ginzburg model.
The Simons Collaboration on Homological Mirror Symmetry summarizes this history and its ongoing role [@simons_hms_site].

The frontier includes:

- wrapped Fukaya categories;
- microlocal sheaves;
- nonarchimedean SYZ;
- perverse schobers;
- cluster varieties;
- mirror symmetry for singular/noncompact spaces;
- 3D mirror symmetry and Coulomb branches;
- categorical wall-crossing;
- relations to Langlands duality.

Work such as homological mirror symmetry at large volume shows how local-to-global principles, coherent sheaf descent, and Fukaya-category restrictions can be matched through mirror symmetry [@shende_gammage_2021_hms_large_volume].
Current programs and conferences emphasize the continuing expansion of HMS into enumerative geometry, noncommutative geometry, birational geometry, and the Langlands program [@homological_mirror_2025_miami; @ranestad_2025_functorial_hms].

### 7.3 Derived and shifted symplectic geometry

Modern QFT is naturally derived.
Gauge theories have quotient stacks; moduli spaces are singular; equations of motion define derived critical loci; BRST/BV formalisms encode ghosts, antifields, and cohomological reductions.

Shifted symplectic geometry provides a language in which moduli of fields can carry \((-1)\)-shifted symplectic structures, quantization becomes deformation of derived critical data, and boundary conditions become Lagrangians in derived stacks.
This is not an optional abstraction: it is the natural geometry of constrained field theories.

This frontier connects:

- BV quantization;
- derived algebraic geometry;
- deformation quantization;
- moduli of local systems;
- factorization algebras;
- topological field theories;
- categorical representation theory.

The unresolved question is how much of perturbative and nonperturbative physics can be recovered from derived/categorical structures alone.

## 8. Mathematical relativity: black holes as nonlinear geometry

Mathematical relativity is sometimes treated as separate from QFT-centric mathematical physics, but it remains one of the central rigorous frontiers.
Here the objects are Lorentzian manifolds satisfying Einstein equations; the tools are nonlinear hyperbolic PDE, geometric analysis, microlocal analysis, causal geometry, and global analysis.

### 8.1 Black-hole stability

The black-hole stability problem asks whether the Kerr family of rotating black-hole solutions is dynamically stable under small perturbations.
Klainerman’s 2025 survey describes the problem and recent results on the stability of slowly rotating Kerr [@klainerman_2025_black_hole_stability].

This is one of the great achievements and frontiers of nonlinear PDE.
The difficulty is that Einstein’s equations are gauge-invariant, nonlinear, dispersive, and geometrically delicate.
One must control gravitational radiation, horizon behavior, null infinity, decay estimates, gauge choices, and nonlinear interactions simultaneously.

The frontier problems include:

- stability of the full subextremal Kerr family;
- Kerr-Newman and charged/rotating black holes;
- stability with matter fields;
- nonlinear stability in alternative gravity theories;
- relation to cosmic censorship;
- formation of black holes from generic initial data;
- precise asymptotics at null infinity.

### 8.2 Cosmic censorship

Cosmic censorship asks whether singularities produced by gravitational collapse are generically hidden behind horizons and whether deterministic evolution remains valid.
Strong cosmic censorship concerns inextendibility of maximal Cauchy developments; weak cosmic censorship concerns visibility of singularities from infinity.

Modern work has shown that naive formulations can fail, forcing refined versions.
Reviews of strong cosmic censorship emphasize advances using modern PDE techniques [@van_de_moortel_strong_cosmic_censorship].
The frontier is not a single yes/no conjecture but a family of regularity-dependent and matter/model-dependent formulations.

The central mathematical tension is between:

- singularity formation;
- horizon stability;
- extendibility at low regularity;
- deterministic PDE evolution;
- physical genericity.

This is a place where foundations of physics become foundations of PDE: what regularity counts as a physically meaningful spacetime?

### 8.3 Gravitational collapse, apparent horizons, and Penrose inequalities

Recent work on Kerr black-hole formation with complete apparent horizon connects scale-critical short-pulse methods, Kerr stability, apparent horizon analysis, and Penrose inequalities [@an_he_2025_kerr_black_hole_formation].
This is frontier mathematics because it attempts to pass from “trapped surfaces form” to a more complete dynamical picture of black-hole formation, final state, and geometric inequalities.

The Penrose inequality relates mass and horizon area, expressing a mathematical version of cosmic censorship and black-hole thermodynamics.
Dynamical, nonsymmetric versions are hard because horizons are not simple static surfaces; they emerge and evolve inside nonlinear spacetime.

Mathematical relativity therefore remains a domain where geometry, analysis, and physics are inseparable.

## 9. Formalized mathematical physics: proof assistants enter the field

A new frontier is the machine-checking of mathematical physics.
This is not yet a replacement for theoretical physics, but it is a meaningful change in the infrastructure of rigor.

### 9.1 Formalization of QFT in Lean

Douglas, Hoback, Mei, and Nissim’s 2026 paper formalizes the construction of the free bosonic quantum field theory in four-dimensional Euclidean spacetime and proves that it satisfies Glimm-Jaffe/Osterwalder-Schrader-style axioms in Lean 4 [@douglas_hoback_mei_nissim_2026_formal_qft].
The result is not an interacting QFT, and it does not solve the foundation problem.
Its significance is different: it shows that extended arguments in mathematical physics can be translated into machine-checked proof using existing libraries and AI-assisted workflows.

The frontier questions are:

- Which parts of functional analysis, distribution theory, probability, and operator algebra are mature enough in proof assistants?
- Can renormalization arguments be formalized?
- Can constructive QFT examples be turned into reusable libraries?
- Can formalization reveal hidden assumptions in physics proofs?
- Can AI-assisted theorem proving scale to real mathematical physics rather than toy examples?

### 9.2 Formalizing quantum information

The Lean formalization of the generalized quantum Stein’s lemma is important because it handles a theorem in quantum hypothesis testing/resource theory and required topology, analysis, and operator algebra [@meiburg_lessa_soldati_2025_quantum_stein].
It also reportedly corrected or clarified imprecisions in the informal proof process.

This matters for mathematical physics because quantum information is now central to black holes, holography, thermodynamics, condensed matter, and computation.
If quantum information lemmas become formalized, they can serve as verified infrastructure for more ambitious formal physics.

### 9.3 Agentic autoformalization

MerLean, a 2026 agentic framework for autoformalization in quantum computation, extracts statements from LaTeX, formalizes them into Lean, and translates verified code back into human-readable LaTeX [@ren_li_qi_2026_merlean].

The frontier is not merely “AI solves physics.” The realistic frontier is:

1. AI helps translate informal proofs into formal ones.
2. Proof assistants check the translated structure.
3. Libraries accumulate reusable definitions and lemmas.
4. Researchers discover where informal mathematical physics hides gaps.
5. Eventually, formalized structures become computational objects.

This is likely to matter most in areas with heavy categorical and analytic bookkeeping: tensor categories, operator algebras, quantum information, constructive QFT, and perturbative renormalization.

## 10. The cross-cutting mathematical structures

Across all these frontiers, the same structural motifs recur.
The important point is not that every field uses “category theory” or “geometry.” It is that several distinct mathematical needs are converging.

### 10.1 Local-to-global structure

QFT, sheaf theory, factorization algebras, AQFT, boundary algebras, holography, Langlands, and random geometry all ask how local data determines global structure.

Examples:

- AQFT assigns algebras to regions.
- Factorization algebras glue observables from opens.
- Sheaves assign data to spaces with descent.
- Boundary algebras recover bulk topological order.
- Holography reconstructs bulk from boundary data.
- Celestial holography reconstructs flat-space physics from null-infinity data.
- Geometric Langlands relates sheaf categories on moduli stacks to spectral local systems.

This suggests that mathematical physics is increasingly a science of **structured locality**.

### 10.2 Defects and interfaces

Defects are now central objects.
They are not perturbations; they are carriers of symmetry, duality, boundary condition, phase transition, and functorial action.

Examples:

- non-invertible symmetry defects;
- domain walls between phases;
- line and surface operators in gauge theory;
- branes in mirror symmetry;
- boundaries in topological phases;
- interfaces in SymTFT;
- defect CFTs and defect bootstrap;
- temporal defects from decoherence in mixed-state topological order.

A modern physical theory is increasingly understood through its defects as much as through its fields.

### 10.3 Categorical invariants

Tensor categories, higher categories, derived categories, Fukaya categories, \(C^*\)-tensor categories, fusion categories, braided categories, and categories of sheaves all occur because physical systems have compositional structure.

Categorification appears when:

- particles/anyons fuse;
- defects compose;
- boundary conditions form categories;
- symmetries are non-invertible;
- observables form algebras up to homotopy;
- dualities are equivalences of categories;
- phases are classified by higher-categorical data.

The old invariant might be a number.
The new invariant is often a category, functor, or higher category.

### 10.4 Positivity and order

Positivity appears everywhere:

- Hilbert-space positivity;
- reflection positivity;
- bootstrap positivity;
- positive geometry;
- entropy inequalities;
- monotonicity under quantum channels;
- energy conditions;
- complete monotonicity and Stieltjes functions;
- resource-theoretic monotones.

The mixed-state topological order frontier is especially important because it shifts classification from equivalence to preorder [@ogata_2026_invariants_mixed].
This is likely a broader trend.
Physics often cares not only whether two structures are the same, but whether one can be transformed into another under allowed operations.

### 10.5 Reconstruction

Many frontiers are reconstruction problems:

- reconstruct QFT from correlation functions;
- reconstruct Lorentzian theory from Euclidean theory;
- reconstruct bulk from boundary entanglement;
- reconstruct flat-space scattering from celestial CFT;
- reconstruct amplitudes from singularities and positivity;
- reconstruct phases from boundary algebras;
- reconstruct global sheaves from local systems;
- reconstruct continuum limits from random discrete models.

This is why mathematical physics now feels less like “solve equations” and more like “infer hidden structure from consistency data.”

## 11. The most important open problems and “red nodes”

This section lists high-value frontier nodes: problems where progress would reorganize several fields at once.

### 11.1 A mathematically complete definition of QFT flexible enough for physics

The field has multiple partial definitions: Wightman axioms, Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction, Haag-Kastler nets, functorial TQFT, factorization algebras, conformal nets, constructive measures, perturbative BV, and lattice-continuum limits.

The frontier problem is not to choose one and declare victory.
It is to understand translations among them and their domains of validity.

A successful framework would explain:

- perturbative and nonperturbative QFT;
- gauge theory;
- curved spacetime;
- extended operators;
- defects/boundaries;
- renormalization;
- states and sectors;
- anomalies;
- continuum limits;
- computational observables.

This may require a pluralistic definition: QFT as a network of equivalent or adjoint formalisms rather than one universal object.

### 11.2 Nonperturbative 4D Yang-Mills

The Yang-Mills mass gap remains central [@clay_yang_mills].
But the likely route may not be a direct proof from the classical action.
It may require combining:

- stochastic quantization;
- lattice gauge theory limits;
- Wilson-loop analysis;
- gauge-covariant renormalization;
- constructive probability;
- operator algebras;
- categorical/topological symmetries;
- confinement criteria;
- computer-assisted proof.

A proof would not merely solve a problem; it would validate a method for constructing realistic QFT.

### 11.3 The exact mathematical status of generalized symmetry

Generalized symmetry is powerful but still in conceptual motion.
A mature theory would answer:

- What is a symmetry in arbitrary dimension?
- What is the universal algebraic object of defects?
- How do anomalies classify obstructions?
- Which categorical symmetries are gaugeable?
- How do generalized symmetries constrain RG flows?
- How do they act on local and extended operators?
- What is their lattice realization?
- How are they detected experimentally?

This is one of the highest-payoff areas because it touches QFT, condensed matter, quantum gravity, and category theory simultaneously.

### 11.4 Mixed-state and open-system phases

Real quantum systems are noisy.
The pure-state classification program must be extended to density matrices, channels, Lindbladians, measurement, and decoherence.

The key red node is the preorder structure:

\[ \rho \succeq \sigma \quad\text{if}\quad \sigma = \mathcal{E}(\rho) \]

for an allowed finite-depth local quantum channel \(\mathcal{E}\).
Which invariants are monotone?
Which topological data survive?
Can there be intrinsically mixed-state phases with no pure-state analogue?
How do categorical symmetries operate on density matrices?

This is mathematically deep and physically urgent.

### 11.5 Flat-space holography

Celestial holography would be revolutionary if it becomes a complete dual formulation of flat-space quantum gravity.
The red nodes are:

- identifying the celestial CFT or generalized celestial theory;
- understanding unitarity and locality in celestial variables;
- including massive particles and loops;
- handling infrared divergences;
- making contact with observables;
- reconciling twistor/top-down and soft-theorem/bottom-up approaches;
- establishing nonperturbative content.

The reason this matters is simple: AdS is mathematically beautiful, but nearly flat spacetime is closer to the world we inhabit [@simons_celestial_2026].

### 11.6 Positive geometry beyond toy theories

Positive geometry is powerful in planar supersymmetric theories.
The red node is extension:

- beyond planar limits;
- beyond maximal supersymmetry;
- to QCD-like theories;
- to gravity;
- to cosmology;
- beyond polylogarithms;
- to nonperturbative data.

If locality and unitarity can be derived from geometry in broader settings, the conceptual foundations of QFT shift.

### 11.7 Quantum gravity in cosmological spacetime

Holography is best understood in AdS, but cosmology asks for de Sitter-like or more general spacetimes.
Mathematical problems include:

- defining observables;
- understanding Hilbert spaces;
- formulating de Sitter entropy;
- deriving cosmological correlators;
- connecting inflationary data to QFT constraints;
- understanding time emergence.

This is one of the hardest frontiers because it lacks the clean asymptotic structures that make AdS and flat-space scattering manageable.

### 11.8 Rigorous non-equilibrium statistical mechanics

Equilibrium statistical mechanics has a powerful Gibbsian framework.
Non-equilibrium systems do not.
The red nodes are:

- entropy production;
- fluctuation theorems;
- hydrodynamic limits;
- active matter;
- turbulence;
- stochastic thermodynamics;
- non-equilibrium phase transitions;
- driven open quantum systems.

This is the mathematical physics of the actual messy world.

### 11.9 Formalized renormalization

Formalizing free QFT is a start [@douglas_hoback_mei_nissim_2026_formal_qft].
The deeper red node is formalizing renormalization, perturbative expansions, distribution products, and constructive limits.

A formalized renormalization library would be transformative because it would create reusable verified infrastructure for:

- perturbative QFT;
- stochastic PDE;
- Feynman integrals;
- regularity structures;
- BV formalism;
- effective field theory;
- constructive QFT.

This is not close, but the direction is visible.

## 12. A synthesis: what mathematical physics is becoming

The frontier is not best understood as a list of subfields.
It is a change in ontology.

Classical mathematical physics was often organized around differential equations on manifolds:

\[ \text{space} + \text{fields} + \text{equations} + \text{symmetry group}. \]

The 2026 frontier increasingly looks like:

\[ \text{local data} + \text{gluing} + \text{defects} + \text{categories} + \text{states} + \text{observables} + \text{allowed transformations}. \]

The primitive object is no longer necessarily a manifold, field, or equation.
It may be:

- a net of algebras;
- a factorization algebra;
- a category of boundary conditions;
- a braided tensor category;
- a stochastic distribution-valued process;
- a positive geometry with canonical form;
- a space of conformal data satisfying bootstrap constraints;
- a quantum error-correcting code;
- a boundary algebra reconstructing a bulk;
- a derived stack of fields;
- a preorder of phases under channels.

This does not mean equations are obsolete.
It means equations are no longer the only privileged syntax of physical law.

### 12.1 Locality is being generalized

Locality once meant fields at spacelike separation commute.
Now locality also means:

- algebraic inclusion of observables;
- factorization over disjoint regions;
- descent/gluing of sheaves;
- entanglement-wedge reconstruction;
- boundary representation of bulk order;
- causal factorization in perturbative AQFT;
- locality of finite-depth circuits/channels;
- topological invariance of defects.

Locality is becoming a family of compatibility conditions.

### 12.2 Symmetry is becoming defect algebra

Symmetry once meant a group.
Now it is often a category of topological operators.
Invertibility is optional.
Dimensionality is variable.
Fusion replaces multiplication.
Anomalies become bulk obstructions.
Gauging becomes a boundary operation.

This is not a minor generalization.
It changes the fundamental grammar of physical invariance.

### 12.3 Classification is becoming transformation theory

The move from pure topological phases to mixed-state topological order shows a broader pattern.
Physical classification is often not “same/different” but “convertible/nonconvertible under allowed operations.” This introduces resource-theoretic and preorder structures into mathematical physics.

Likely future examples include:

- phases under noisy channels;
- entanglement under LOCC-like operations;
- QFTs under RG flow;
- symmetry data under gauging/condensation;
- spacetimes under coarse-graining;
- effective theories under decoupling limits.

### 12.4 Computation is becoming part of the theory

The frontier is computational in several distinct senses:

- numerical bootstrap as convex optimization;
- amplitudes as symbolic/algebraic computation;
- tensor networks and quantum codes;
- machine-checked proofs;
- lattice simulations;
- stochastic quantization algorithms;
- topological quantum compilation;
- AI-assisted formalization.

Mathematical physics is becoming a field where a theorem, an algorithm, and a physical observable are often three faces of the same structure.

## 14. Concluding picture

The most honest answer to “what is the frontier of mathematical physics?” is:

> **The frontier is the attempt to make quantum physical theories into rigorous, computable, compositional objects while discovering that the necessary mathematics is categorical, probabilistic, geometric, operator-algebraic, and information-theoretic at once.**

The field’s center of gravity is shifting from *equations of motion* to *structures of consistency*.

The classical dream was to derive everything from a few equations.
The current frontier often works differently.
It asks:

- What local data can be consistently glued?
- What defects can exist, fuse, and end?
- What observables are reconstructible from boundary/asymptotic data?
- What transformations are allowed, and what decreases under them?
- What positivity constraints carve out the possible theory space?
- What random continuum objects arise from discrete models?
- What categorical equivalences express physical duality?
- What proofs can be machine-checked?

This is why mathematical physics in 2026 feels unusually alive.
The foundational problems are old, but the available languages are new enough that the old problems no longer look frozen.
They look like interfaces.

The deepest frontiers are precisely those interfaces:

- stochastic analysis with gauge theory;
- operator algebras with topological phases;
- category theory with symmetry;
- quantum information with gravity;
- positive geometry with scattering and cosmology;
- sheaf/derived geometry with duality;
- proof assistants with formal QFT;
- preorders/resource theories with noisy quantum matter.

The next generation of mathematical physics will likely be built not by choosing among these languages, but by making them talk to one another.
