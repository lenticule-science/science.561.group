Moduli theory studies *spaces of mathematical structures*. A moduli space is a parameter space whose points are isomorphism classes of objects of a fixed kind — Riemann surfaces, vector bundles, representations, metrics, Hodge realizations of cohomology — with whatever topology, algebraic, or differential-geometric structure naturally reflects how those objects deform, degenerate, and stratify.

The recurring questions are: when does a moduli space exist? What is its dimension? How is it stratified by invariants of the parametrized objects? What does its closure look like — what limit objects appear at the boundary? When the space carries a group action (gauge, automorphism, change-of-basis), what is the quotient — orbifold, stack, GIT quotient — and how do its strata correspond to invariants of the orbits?
