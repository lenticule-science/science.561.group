A cover (or covering family) of an object U in a [site](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/site.md) is a family of [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md) {Uᵢ → U} that the Grothendieck topology declares to be "enough to determine U locally."

The covering family says: to understand U, it suffices to understand the pieces Uᵢ and how they overlap. The [sheaf condition](../../higher-topos-theory/terms/sheaf-condition.md) then asks that any compatible local data on the pieces [glues](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/glue.md) to unique global data on U.

In classical topology, a cover of an open set U is a collection of open subsets {Uᵢ} whose union is U. In a Grothendieck topology, covering families are declared axiomatically and need not come from open sets — they can be étale maps, flat maps, or any class of [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md) that satisfies the covering axioms (identity covers, stability under pullback, transitivity).

What qualifies as a cover determines the entire character of the [sheaf theory](../index.md). A finer topology (more covering families) imposes stronger gluing conditions and produces fewer [sheaves](./sheaf.md). A coarser topology imposes weaker conditions and admits more sheaves. Choosing a topology is choosing what "local" means.

In the [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe, the set of [fragments](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/fragment.md) F(H) covers the [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) H: every element belongs to some fragment, and the fragments together determine the global algebra. Fragment-preserving operations respect these local pieces.
