# Tensor product of settlings

The corpus's [Q-enriched closed-monoidal structure theorem](../content/bitopological-settling-site/content/quantale-enriched-sheaf/references/q-enriched-sheaf-closed-monoidal-structure.md) gives a monoidal structure on Q-enriched sheaves (tensor and internal Hom).
Lifting this to *settlings* — the closure-stable subcategory — is non-trivial: the sheaf-tensor of two settlings is generally **not** itself a settling.
The natural settling-level operation is **sheaf-tensor + closure**: tensor at the sheaf level, then close under the union of the closure rules.

This theorem makes that precise and verifies the resulting operation is a well-behaved monoidal structure on the category of *settlings-with-rules* (rather than on settlings simpliciter).

## Statement

**Theorem (settling-tensor product).** *Let $\mathrm{Settlings}^{\mathrm{rule}}_B$ be the category of settlings-with-rules over base $B$ — pairs $(\mathcal{S}, v)$ where $\mathcal{S}$ is a closure-rule system and $v$ is a closure-stable settling under $\mathcal{S}$.*

*Define the **settling-tensor product**:*

$$(\mathcal{S}_1, v_1) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} (\mathcal{S}_2, v_2) \;:=\; (\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2,\; T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}(v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2))$$

*where $\otimes_{\mathrm{Q}}$ is the [Q-enriched sheaf tensor](../content/bitopological-settling-site/content/quantale-enriched-sheaf/content/q-enriched-sheaf-tensor/q-enriched-sheaf-tensor.md), $T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}$ is the closure operator generated by the union of the rule systems, and the closure ordinal of the result is computed in the union system.*

*Then $(\mathrm{Settlings}^{\mathrm{rule}}_B, \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}}, \mathbf{1})$ is a symmetric monoidal category, with unit object $\mathbf{1}$ the trivial-rule settling on the trivial-base.*

## Proof

**Three things to check.**

### Step 1: sheaf-tensor is well-defined

By the [Q-enriched closed-monoidal structure theorem](../content/bitopological-settling-site/content/quantale-enriched-sheaf/references/q-enriched-sheaf-closed-monoidal-structure.md), the Q-enriched tensor $v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2$ is a Q-enriched sheaf, computed pointwise then sheafified.
Both $v_1$ and $v_2$ are presheaves on the same parameter base, so their tensor is a presheaf on that same base.

### Step 2: sheaf-tensor of two settlings is not always a settling

A settling is a closure-stable sheaf: $T(v) = v$ for the appropriate closure operator $T$.
The sheaf-tensor $v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2$ is generally **not** closure-stable.

*Counter-example.* Let $\mathcal{S}_1 = \mathcal{S}_2 = \emptyset$ (no closure rules); then any presheaf is a settling under either rule system.

Take $v_1$ with $v_1(P)(0, 0) = \mathbf{t}$, all else $\bot$.
Take $v_2$ with $v_2(P)(0, 0) = \mathbf{f}$, all else $\bot$.

Both are settlings under the empty rule system (trivially closure-stable).
The Q-enriched tensor at $(0, 0)$: combining $\mathbf{t}$ and $\mathbf{f}$ via knowledge-join gives $\top$.
So $(v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2)(P)(0, 0) = \top$, all else $\bot$.

Now suppose we have a closure rule $\sigma_{\top \mathrm{prop}} : v(P)(s, o) = \top \implies v(P)(s, o') \geq_k \top$ for adjacent $o'$ (paraconsistency propagates).
With $\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2 = \{\sigma_{\top \mathrm{prop}}\}$ (or some non-empty rule), the rule fires on $v_1 \otimes v_2$ but didn't fire on $v_1$ or $v_2$ alone.

So $T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}(v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2)$ has new content beyond $v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2$ — the tensor is not closure-stable in the union rule system.

### Step 3: post-closure tensor is a settling

The closure operator $T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}$ is inflationary, monotone, idempotent (per the [closure 2-monad theorem](../morphisms/closure-2-monad.md)).
Applied to any presheaf $v$, it produces a closure-stable presheaf.
Applied specifically to $v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2$:

$$T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}(v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2)$$

is closure-stable under $\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2$ — i.e., it's a settling for that rule system.
The pair $(\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2, T(\cdot))$ is a settling-with-rule, well-defined by the construction.

### Step 4: monoidal axioms

- **Associativity.** $(\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2) \cup \mathcal{S}_3 = \mathcal{S}_1 \cup (\mathcal{S}_2 \cup \mathcal{S}_3)$ trivially.
  Closure operators distribute over union, and the sheaf-tensor is associative pointwise.
  So $((\mathcal{S}_1, v_1) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} (\mathcal{S}_2, v_2)) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} (\mathcal{S}_3, v_3)$ has the same closure-rule and the same closed presheaf as $(\mathcal{S}_1, v_1) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} ((\mathcal{S}_2, v_2) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} (\mathcal{S}_3, v_3))$.

- **Symmetry.** Sheaf-tensor is symmetric (per the closed-monoidal structure theorem); rule-system union is commutative.
  So $\otimes_{\mathrm{settle}}$ is symmetric.

- **Unit.** The trivial settling-with-rule $(\emptyset, \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Q}})$ — empty rule system, Q-enriched tensor unit — acts as identity:

$$(\emptyset, \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Q}}) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} (\mathcal{S}, v) = (\mathcal{S}, T_\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Q}} \otimes v)) = (\mathcal{S}, T_\mathcal{S}(v)) = (\mathcal{S}, v)$$

(since $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Q}} \otimes v = v$ pointwise and $v$ is closure-stable). $\square$

## Closure ordinal of the tensor product

The closure ordinal of $T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}(v_1 \otimes v_2)$ depends on:

- The individual closure ordinals $\kappa(v_1), \kappa(v_2)$.
- The interaction between the rule systems on the tensor's combined content.

**Bound**: $\kappa(v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} v_2) \geq \max(\kappa(v_1), \kappa(v_2))$ — the tensor's closure can't be reached faster than either factor's.

**Upper bound**: depends on whether new firings appear in the union system.
If $\mathcal{S}_1 = \mathcal{S}_2 = \mathcal{S}$ (same rule system) and $v_1, v_2$ are both already closure-stable, then $T(v_1 \otimes v_2)$ might fire additional rules due to the *bilattice-join paraconsistency* in the tensor — producing $\top$-content where $v_1$ and $v_2$ disagreed.
The closure ordinal of the tensor can therefore be *larger* than either factor's, depending on how rules interact with $\top$-content.

**Worst case**: if $v_1$ is in the strict regime ($\kappa = n_1$) and $v_2$ is in the strict regime ($\kappa = n_2$), the tensor's closure ordinal is bounded by $n_1 + n_2 + \text{(number of rules cascading on $\top$-content)}$ — finite, but potentially much larger than either factor.

If $v_1, v_2$ are aperiodic ($\kappa = \omega_1$), the tensor is also aperiodic — there's no "regime promotion" beyond aperiodic in the four-regime hierarchy.

## What this gives

Three structural consequences:

**1.
Settlings have a well-defined monoidal structure.** The corpus's category of settlings is symmetric monoidal under $\otimes_{\mathrm{settle}}$, with explicit unit and associative composition.

**2.
Tensor produces paraconsistent content from disagreement.** When two settlings disagree (one says $\mathbf{t}$, one says $\mathbf{f}$ at the same cell), their Q-enriched tensor has $\top$-content there.
The settling-tensor inherits this — and any closure rule that propagates $\top$-content will fire. **The settling-tensor structurally produces the corpus's distinctive paraconsistent classification from disagreement.** This is the same phenomenon as the [multi-observer disagreement benchmark](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/benchmark-multi-observer-disagreement.md) viewed at the categorical level.

**3.
Tensor as agential composition.** Two settlings represent two agents' / observers' / processes' settled content.
Their tensor represents their *joint* content with conflicts surfaced as $\top$.
The closure operator on this joint content propagates and resolves the conflicts (or keeps them as $\top$-content if no resolution rule applies). **The settling-tensor is structurally the categorical operation of "combining two agents' knowledge."**

## Connection to other corpus structures

- The [multi-observer system definition](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/multi-observer-system.md) builds on the same idea: multiple settlings combined via natural categorical operations.
  The tensor is the binary case; multi-observer systems generalize to arbitrary $|O|$-many.
- The [strong-MLD orbit type](../theorems/strong-mld-via-composed-duality.md) extends compositionally: the orbit type of a tensor is determined by the orbit types of the factors plus the union-rule's interactions.
- The [conflation-strong-MLD theorem](../theorems/conflation-strong-mld.md): if $v_1$ and $v_2$ are both conflation-self-dual (every firing in $\{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}\}$), is $v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} v_2$?
  Generally no — the tensor produces $\top$-content from disagreement, breaking conflation-self-duality.
  Tensor doesn't preserve the conflation stratum.
