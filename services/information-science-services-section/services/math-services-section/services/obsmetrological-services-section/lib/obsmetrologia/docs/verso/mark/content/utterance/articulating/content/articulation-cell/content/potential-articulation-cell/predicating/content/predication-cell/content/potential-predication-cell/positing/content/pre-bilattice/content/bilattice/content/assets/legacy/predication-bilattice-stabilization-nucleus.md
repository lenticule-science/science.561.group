# Bilattice stabilization nucleus

The **bilattice stabilization nucleus** is a *parametrized* family of cell-local closure operators on the twist-product algebra $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie$, obtained by iterating the [mixed step operator](../theorems/four-bilattice-mixed-orbit-convergence.md) $T_a$ to its fixed point.

For each fixed **background element** $a \in \mathbf{L}^\bowtie$, define the unary operator

$$T_a : \mathbf{L}^\bowtie \to \mathbf{L}^\bowtie, \qquad T_a(x) \;:=\; a \Rightarrow (a \odot x),$$

where $\odot$ is the [twist-product fusion](../morphisms/twist-product-fusion.md) and $\Rightarrow$ is the [twist-product right residual](../morphisms/twist-product-right-residual.md). The **$a$-stabilization nucleus** is the iteration-to-fix

$$T_a^* : \mathbf{L}^\bowtie \to \mathbf{L}^\bowtie, \qquad T_a^*(x) \;:=\; \lim_{n} T_a^n(x),$$

provided the limit exists.

## Existence and termination

For $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{2}$ (so the algebra is $\mathbf{FOUR}$), the limit exists and is reached at $n = 1$ for every $a \in \mathbf{FOUR}$ by [four-bilattice mixed orbit convergence](../theorems/four-bilattice-mixed-orbit-convergence.md): $T_a(T_a(x)) = T_a(x)$ for every $x$, so $T_a^* = T_a$. For richer residuated lattices $\mathbf{L}$, existence depends on $\mathbf{L}$'s convergence-regime properties; existence is automatic in the strict regime, may require ordinal stages in the transfinite-bounded regime, and may fail in the aperiodic regime per the [four-regime dichotomy](../../../../../../valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/references/four-regime-dichotomy.md).

## Closure operator on the truth order

For each fixed $a \in \mathbf{FOUR}$, the operator $T_a^*$ is a [closure operator](../morphisms/closure-operator.md) on the [truth order](../../../../truth-order/truth-order.md) $(\mathbf{FOUR}, \leq_t)$. The three closure-operator axioms hold:

1. **Inflationary in $\leq_t$:** $x \leq_t T_a^*(x)$ for every $x \in \mathbf{FOUR}$. Verified by direct enumeration against the value table in [four-bilattice mixed orbit convergence](../theorems/four-bilattice-mixed-orbit-convergence.md): each row's outputs sit at-or-above the corresponding $x$ in the truth order $\mathbf{f} \leq_t \{\bot, \top\} \leq_t \mathbf{t}$.
2. **Idempotent:** $T_a^*(T_a^*(x)) = T_a^*(x)$. Immediate from the definition as iteration-to-fix; on $\mathbf{FOUR}$, $T_a^* = T_a$ and $T_a \circ T_a = T_a$ per the convergence theorem.
3. **Monotone in $\leq_t$:** $x \leq_t x' \Rightarrow T_a^*(x) \leq_t T_a^*(x')$. Verified by enumeration: for each $a$, the value table preserves the $\leq_t$-order on inputs.

The closure-operator structure is on the **truth order**, not the knowledge order. This reflects the underlying algebraic adjoint: $\odot$ and $\Rightarrow$ are by [twist-product residuation](../../../../../../residuated-lattice/content/twist-product/references/twist-product-residuation.md) the truth-order fusion-residual pair on $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie$. The knowledge order $\leq_k$ is *not* preserved by $T_a$ in general — for instance, on $\mathbf{FOUR}$ with $a = \mathbf{f}$, $T_\mathbf{f}$ is constant $\mathbf{t}$, which is incomparable to $\mathbf{f}$ in $\leq_k$, so $T_\mathbf{f}$ is neither $\leq_k$-inflationary nor $\leq_k$-monotone.

## Distinction from the rule-saturation closure operator

The bilattice stabilization nucleus is operationally and structurally distinct from the rule-saturation [closure operator](../morphisms/closure-operator.md) $\mathrm{cl}$ generated by a [closure-rule system](teaching/explanations/closure-rule-system.md):

| Feature | Rule-saturation closure $\mathrm{cl}$ | $a$-stabilization nucleus $T_a^*$ |
|---|---|---|
| Structural axis | Knowledge order $\leq_k$ | Truth order $\leq_t$ |
| Domain | Predication fiber algebra $A_p$ as a whole | Single cell value in $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie$ |
| Drives | Cells producible by rule conclusions | A single cell's value to its $T_a$-fixed point |
| Operator shape | $A_p \to A_p$ | $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie \to \mathbf{L}^\bowtie$, parametrized by $a \in \mathbf{L}^\bowtie$ |
| Information added | New cells (positions in $E \times E$ get values) | Existing cell's value gets refined or reset |
| Termination on $\mathbf{FOUR}$ | $\leq |\text{rules}| \cdot |\text{seed}|$ layers (regime-dependent) | $1$ composite step per $(a, x)$ pair |
| Affects predicate-category coherence | Yes, via the rule system's quantification | No — purely cell-local for fixed $a$ |

Both operators arise in the substrate's settlement engine; their interaction is what produces the joint [σ/Δ pass decomposition](../../../../../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/concepts/sigma-delta-pass-decomposition.md). The rule-saturation closure $\mathrm{cl}$ is a single operator on the entire fiber algebra; the $a$-stabilization nucleus is a *family* of cell-local operators indexed by background values, applied per-cell to the cells the rule pass moves.

## On the cell-level vs. fiber-level lift

To extend $T_a^*$ from a single cell's $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie$ to the entire predication fiber algebra $A_p = \mathbf{L}^{\bowtie\, E \times E}$, the substrate must specify, for each cell $(s, o) \in E \times E$, a background value $a_{s, o}$. The fiber-level operator is then

$$N_\Delta : A_p \to A_p, \qquad N_\Delta(v)(s, o) \;:=\; T_{a_{s, o}}^*\bigl(v(s, o)\bigr).$$

The choice of background-value function $(s, o) \mapsto a_{s, o}$ is a substrate-policy decision, not a structural feature of the algebra. Common choices:

- **Self-background:** $a_{s, o} = v(s, o)$, i.e. the cell's current value serves as its own background. This collapses the operator to $v(s, o) \mapsto T_{v(s, o)}^*(v(s, o))$, which on $\mathbf{FOUR}$ produces non-trivial value transitions (e.g. $\mathbf{f} \mapsto T_\mathbf{f}^*(\mathbf{f}) = T_\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{f}) = \mathbf{t}$). Self-background is *not inflationary in $\leq_k$* and is therefore not a knowledge-order closure on $A_p$. Substrates that use this policy are performing truth-axis stabilization, not knowledge-monotone settling.
- **External-background:** $a_{s, o}$ is supplied by the rule that moved the cell (the rule's conclusion value, the merge-source value, or an authority value). The fiber-level operator's properties then depend on the rule's choice; per-cell, each $T_{a_{s, o}}^*$ is a truth-order closure operator on $\mathbf{FOUR}$.
- **Constant background:** $a_{s, o} = a$ for a fixed $a \in \mathbf{L}^\bowtie$. The fiber-level operator is $v \mapsto T_a^* \circ v$, the post-composition of $v$ with the fixed cell-local operator. This is a closure operator on the *truth-axis pointwise* order on $A_p$.

The fiber-level operator's properties are policy-dependent; the cell-level operator $T_a^*$ for fixed $a$ is unambiguously a truth-order closure operator on $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie$.

## Why "nucleus" and not "closure operator"

The term *nucleus* signals that this operator is the bilattice-side closure complementary to the rule-saturation closure — it is a closure operator on a different axis (truth) and a different domain (single-cell value rather than entire fiber). The nominal distinction tracks the operator's role in the joint-fixpoint discipline, not a structural distinction in the closure axioms; on the cell-level, $T_a^*$ for fixed $a$ satisfies the standard closure axioms on $(\mathbf{L}^\bowtie, \leq_t)$.

## Position

The bilattice stabilization nucleus is the math object that the engineering [Δ-pass](../../../../../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/concepts/sigma-delta-pass-decomposition.md) realizes per moved cell: each Δ-pass per-cell stabilization picks the cell's background value (per the substrate's background-value policy) and applies $T_a^*$ to the cell's seed value. The Δ-pass termination condition is exactly that every moved cell has reached its $T_a^*$-fixed point. Together with the rule-saturation [closure operator](../morphisms/closure-operator.md) (the σ-pass), the two closure structures are the substrate's settled-state operator.

The truth-order vs. knowledge-order distinction matters: the σ-pass's rule-saturation closure operates on the knowledge order $\leq_k$ (it adds cells, increasing knowledge); the Δ-pass's bilattice stabilization operates on the truth order $\leq_t$ at each moved cell (it refines the cell's truth-component value). The two passes therefore preserve different structural axes, and the joint fixed point is a fiber that is simultaneously $\leq_k$-saturated under rules and $\leq_t$-saturated per cell under stabilization.
