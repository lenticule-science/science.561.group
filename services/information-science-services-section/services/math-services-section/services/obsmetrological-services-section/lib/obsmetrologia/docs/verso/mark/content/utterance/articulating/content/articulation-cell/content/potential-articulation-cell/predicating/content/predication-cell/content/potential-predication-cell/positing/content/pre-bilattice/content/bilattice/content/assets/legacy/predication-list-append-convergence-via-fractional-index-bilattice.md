# Concurrent appends to a fractional-index list converge to a deterministic order via the bilattice cellwise join on author-graphs

**Claim.** Let $L$ be a [list-record](../objects/list-record.md) using fractional-index positioning, edited concurrently by authors $\{u_1, \ldots, u_n\}$ via [append-to-list](../morphisms/append-to-list.md) (or arbitrary fractional-index insertion). The substrate distinguishes two layers:

- *Substrate state* — the cellwise bilattice join over author replicas of the list's underlying *primitive* predications: element-record cells (one per item, recording identity and content-hash) and position cells (one per item, recording fractional-index). The substrate stores only these two cell types; $\top$-valued cells may appear at genuine disagreement loci on either.
- *Display projection* — derived deterministically from the substrate state by the rule "items in ascending fractional-index, ties broken by canonical-content-hash." From this total order on items the display layer materializes the `next-element` chain, the length, and head/tail pointers; these are projection artifacts, not substrate-stored cells whose convergence would have to be argued separately.

For every replica of $L$ across the federation, after federation propagation completes:

1. **Substrate-state convergence (cellwise $\vee_k$).** The list's element-record cells and item-keyed position cells converge as the cellwise bilattice $\vee_k$ join over author replicas, exactly as [bilattice network-sheaf consensus](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/bilattice-network-sheaf-consensus.md) gives for the author-graph multi-observer system. When fractional-indices are distinct (the typical case) the join is union-on-disjoint-cells. When two distinct items $i_1 \neq i_2$ from different authors carry position cells with the same fractional-index value $q$, the *primitive join output* admits both item-keyed cells $(i_1, \mathrm{position}, q)$ and $(i_2, \mathrm{position}, q)$ into substrate state, and the *derived audit witness* — a separate audit-layer record — flags index $q$ as a collision locus per [audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology.md). The two are distinct: primitive join outputs are item-keyed position cells (never collapsed); the audit witness is the index-keyed top-marker derived from the multiplicity at $q$. The substrate state is byte-identical across replicas — including all colliding item-keyed cells and their derived audit witnesses — once federation propagation completes.

2. **Display-projection determinism.** The display projection — the rendered sequence of items, the materialized `next-element` chain, the materialized length and head/tail pointers — is computed from the substrate state by the rule above. Because the rule is a total order on items (fractional-index is a rational; ties broken by content-hash, which is unique by content per [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md)), the display is deterministic across replicas without coordination. The chain and length materializations are derived from the displayed sequence, not stored as independent substrate cells whose convergence would have to be argued separately.

3. **Substrate state and display are not coextensive.** The substrate retains both the colliding item-keyed position cells and the derived audit witness for any contested fractional-index; the display projection makes a deterministic *choice* in rendering. An auditor reading substrate state sees the collision (multiple item-keyed cells at one $q$, plus the index-keyed audit witness); an end user reading the display sees an ordering. The two layers are independently meaningful: the display-layer determinism does not erase the substrate-layer disagreement, and the substrate-layer disagreement does not destabilize the display-layer order.

The list-edit case is a structurally distinct application of the parent network-sheaf consensus theorem from the document-edit case — different primitive cell types (element-records and positions, with chain/length/head/tail derived in projection) and different conflict topology (rare same-index collisions vs. routine same-span overlaps) — but the same theorem applies once the author-graph and the per-author list valuations are exhibited as a multi-observer system on the author-graph.

**Cell-keying convention.** Throughout this theorem, position cells are keyed *by item identity* (one position cell per item, of the form $(i, \mathrm{position}, q)$ where $i$ is the item's content-hash identity and $q$ its fractional-index rational). A "same-index collision" between authors $u_1$ and $u_2$ thus means: two *distinct* items $i_1 \neq i_2$ each carry a position cell with the same $q$ — i.e., $(i_1, \mathrm{position}, q)$ and $(i_2, \mathrm{position}, q)$ are *separate* cells with equal value. The cellwise $\vee_k$ join admits both into substrate state. The display-projection's tiebreaker on `(q, content-hash)` then orders $i_1$ and $i_2$ deterministically by content-hash. (Any "collision at index $q$" claim throughout this theorem refers to: multiple item-keyed position cells share value $q$ as primitive substrate content, and a separate audit-layer witness indexed by $q$ records the multiplicity per [audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology.md). The two layers are distinct; neither collapses into the other.) This keying choice is what makes the convergence argument unambiguous: the cellwise join is over item-keyed cells, the display projection is over the resulting (item, position) pairs, and ordering is total because content-hashes are unique by content.

## Why this holds

**Setup.** Each author $u_i$ runs a substrate-replica per [federation-convergence](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/federation-convergence.md). For the list $L$, each author's replica holds a settling $V_{u_i}(L)$ of $L$'s primitive predication cells — element-record cells (one per item, recording identity and content-hash) and position cells (one per item, recording fractional-index). The chain, length, and head/tail pointers are derived view state at each replica, materialized on-demand from the primitive cells per the display projection rule; they are not separately stored substrate cells whose convergence would have to be argued. Edges in the author-graph $G_{U_L} = (\{u_1, \ldots, u_n\}, E_{U_L})$ are the federation channels.

The collection $V_{G_{U_L}}(L) : \hat G_{U_L} \to \mathrm{Settlings}_B$ — taking each author's primitive-cell settling — is a [multi-observer system](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/multi-observer-system.md) with one observer per author.

**Append is monotone admission of primitive cells.** Per [append-to-list](../morphisms/append-to-list.md), appending an item admits two primitive cells into substrate state: the element-record cell and the position cell. Both are admission-only — per [settling-monotonicity](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-monotonicity.md), the list's primitive-cell set grows in the knowledge order. The append's effect on the chain, length, and tail-pointer is computed at display projection time from the primitive cells, not stored as additional substrate cells. Two concurrent appends from different authors produce *disjoint* element-record cells (each author's item has a distinct content-hash and identity per [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md)) at *distinct fractional-indices* (each author allocates `next-after-current-tail` from their local view, but the indices are rationals whose denominators expand on collision per the fractional-index allocator). The display projection at each replica re-materializes the chain, length, and head/tail from the post-merge primitive cells.

> **Model note.** Older prose in [list](teaching/explanations/list.md) and [append-to-list](../morphisms/append-to-list.md) describes the chain (`next-element` predications), length, and head/tail as substrate-stored predications. This theorem supersedes that prose: under the present model, the chain/length/head/tail are display-projection materializations from the primitive (element-record, position) cells, not independent substrate cells. The substrate's primitive storage is leaner than the older prose suggested; concurrent-author convergence is provable on the primitive cells alone, with all derived structure inheriting determinism via the projection rule.

**Substrate-state cellwise $\vee_k$ converges.** Per [bilattice network-sheaf consensus](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/bilattice-network-sheaf-consensus.md) claim 2, the cellwise $\vee_k$ on the author-graph multi-observer system gives:

- *Element-record cells.* Disjoint across authors; $\vee_k$ is the union — all items appear in all replicas. Byte-identical convergence is direct.
- *Position cells.* Item-keyed (per the cell-keying convention above): one cell $(i, \mathrm{position}, q_i)$ per item. When all items have distinct fractional-indices (typical), $\vee_k$ is the union — each item has its assigned position. When two distinct items $i_1 \neq i_2$ from different authors collided on the same fractional-index $q$, $\vee_k$ admits both cells $(i_1, \mathrm{position}, q)$ and $(i_2, \mathrm{position}, q)$ into substrate state; the audit layer flags index $q$ as a collision locus. Byte-identical convergence (including both cells of any colliding pair) is the network-sheaf claim.

The `next-element` chain, length, and head/tail-pointer cells are *not* asserted to converge as independent substrate cells; the substrate stores only element records and position cells, with chain/length/pointers as the display projection's materialization.

**Display-projection materialization.** From the converged substrate state, the display layer computes:

- *Total ordering on items.* Per [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md), each item has a canonical-content-hash unique by content. The display rule "ascending fractional-index, ties broken by canonical-content-hash" is a total order on the merged item set: distinct fractional-indices induce a total order on positions; same-fractional-index items are ordered by content-hash, also total. This total order is well-defined on the substrate state under collisions because each colliding item carries its own item-keyed position cell with the shared $q$; the projection reads each item's $(i, \mathrm{position}, q)$ pair, and the content-hash tiebreaker on $i$ resolves the order. The derived audit witness at $q$ is consulted by the audit layer, not the display projection.
- *`next-element` chain.* Materialized by walking the totally-ordered item set; each item's `next` pointer is the next item in the order. Chain materialization is deterministic because the order is.
- *Length and head/tail.* Length is the cardinality of the item set; head and tail are the first and last items in the materialized chain. Both deterministic for the same reason.

The display projection is a *function* of the substrate state, not an independent substrate-stored object whose convergence would need separate argument. Because the substrate state converges byte-identically (claim 1) and the projection is a deterministic function of it (this paragraph), the display projection is byte-identical across replicas (claim 2).

**State-vs-display separation.** Both the colliding item-keyed position cells and the derived audit witness for any contested $q$ are *not erased* by the display projection — they remain in substrate state, queryable by audit per [audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology.md). The display projection makes a deterministic rendering choice; the substrate retains the disagreement. A platform that drops one of the colliding item-keyed cells (keeping only the projection-winner) — or that suppresses the audit witness — has collapsed substrate state into the display projection, losing audit content.

**Mayer–Vietoris vanishing (CALM).** Per [bilattice network-sheaf consensus](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/bilattice-network-sheaf-consensus.md) claim 4, $\delta^n_\pm = 0$ on the author-graph cover for all $n \geq 0$. List-append is therefore *coordination-free*: appends do not require any global agreement, only federation propagation. Per the substrate's [federation-convergence](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/federation-convergence.md), propagation is eventually-converging.

## Engineering consequence

- **Concurrent appends never lose items.** Per the cellwise $\vee_k$ being union on disjoint cells, no append from any author is silently dropped at merge. All items appear in all replicas after propagation.
- **Coordination-free collaborative lists.** Per the CALM-vanishing, two clients can append to a shared list while disconnected from each other, then re-connect, and merge produces a single converged list with all appends. This is the substrate's "offline-first lists" property.
- **Append is O(1) per author per replica.** Per [append-to-list](../morphisms/append-to-list.md) with a tail-pointer, append cost is constant per author. Per the cellwise convergence, each replica's per-event cost remains constant under federation; merging $n$ authors' appends costs O(n) substrate operations, not O(n²) coordination steps.
- **Collisions surface as substrate content.** Per claim 3, fractional-index collisions are recorded as multiple item-keyed cells at the same $q$ plus a derived audit witness, not silently broken. An auditor can ask "where did fractional-index collisions occur?" and get a structural answer; per [cycle-holonomy-as-federation-disagreement](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/cycle-holonomy-as-federation-disagreement.md), persistent collisions across federation rounds indicate fractional-index allocator drift between substrates that the substrate can detect and report.
- **Specializations (sequence, stack, queue) inherit convergence.** Per [list](teaching/explanations/list.md)'s specializations, sequence (append-only), stack (LIFO push/pop), and queue (FIFO enqueue/dequeue) all reduce to fractional-index list operations. The theorem applies to all four; their concurrent-modification semantics is the same convergence theorem, with operation-specific allocator rules.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Integer-index lists with concurrent insertion.** Per [list](teaching/explanations/list.md), integer-indexed lists require O(n) re-indexing on insert. Concurrent integer-index inserts produce cellwise *contradictions* on the position cells (two authors both claim "position 5"), not commutable disjoint admissions. Per the parent [bilattice network-sheaf consensus](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/bilattice-network-sheaf-consensus.md) claim 2, this produces $\top$ at every contested position cell — the entire tail of the list becomes inconsistency-content. The substrate's discipline for integer-indexed lists is *single-author or coordinated*; concurrent multi-author integer-index lists are not convergent under this theorem.
- **Last-write-wins on position collisions.** A platform that resolves fractional-index collisions by timestamp (most-recently-allocated index wins) drops the loser's item-keyed position cell and suppresses the derived audit witness. Per [bilattice network-sheaf consensus](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/bilattice-network-sheaf-consensus.md) claim 2, disagreement is content; suppressing it loses information about allocator drift.
- **List operations producing position cells outside the bilattice.** A list whose position cells are non-bilattice values (e.g., opaque server-allocated UUIDs whose ordering is server-determined) cannot apply the cellwise $\vee_k$. Per the theorem, such lists are not convergent without coordination; the substrate's claim of coordination-free lists is restricted to bilattice-valued (fractional-index, content-hash) position schemas.
- **Unbounded fractional-index expansion without garbage collection.** Repeated insertions between adjacent items expand the fractional-index denominator unboundedly. Per [compaction-via-cosettling-equivalence-class](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/compaction-via-cosettling-equivalence-class.md), the substrate's compaction discipline normalizes fractional indices when their denominator exceeds a threshold; without compaction, indices grow without bound. The theorem assumes the substrate's compaction discipline is in effect; long-running collaborative lists without compaction become arbitrarily expensive.

## What the theorem requires

- The list uses fractional-index positions per [list](teaching/explanations/list.md) (not integer indices).
- Items have canonical-content-hash per [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md).
- The substrate's federation propagates per-cell updates per [federation-convergence](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/federation-convergence.md).
- The substrate's display rule is "ascending fractional-index, ties by canonical-content-hash."
- The substrate's compaction discipline operates per [compaction-via-cosettling-equivalence-class](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/compaction-via-cosettling-equivalence-class.md) to bound fractional-index expansion.

## Position

This theorem is the *list* analogue of [collaborative-edit-convergence](collaborative-edit-convergence.md) (which covers documents). The two theorems together cover the subtheory's two collaborative-mutable-content disciplines:

- *Documents.* Cellwise $\vee_k$ on the user-graph for document text-spans. Conflicts at the same span produce $\top$-content surfaced as conflict.
- *Lists.* Cellwise $\vee_k$ on the author-graph for item-keyed list-position cells. Conflicts at the same fractional-index are retained as multiple item-keyed cells plus a derived audit witness in substrate state; the display projection orders the colliding items deterministically by content-hash without erasing the substrate disagreement.

Both theorems specialize the same parent ([bilattice network-sheaf consensus](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/bilattice-network-sheaf-consensus.md)) to two different content shapes; the structural mathematics is one theorem, applied twice. Together with [document-canonicalization-via-settling-quiescence](document-canonicalization-via-settling-quiescence.md) (forward fixed-point) and [cursor-as-cosettling-fiber-with-staleness-as-pre-image-collapse](cursor-as-cosettling-fiber-with-staleness-as-pre-image-collapse.md) (off-trajectory presence state), the subtheory's collaborative-content discipline is grounded throughout in the predication-categories' closure / cosettling / network-sheaf apparatus.
