## Content summary

Surveys the bilattice framework and its use as a value-domain for logic programming and knowledge representation. A *bilattice* carries two complete lattice orders on the same carrier — a *truth* order $\leq_t$ (how true) and a *knowledge* order $\leq_k$ (how much we know) — with appropriate interaction laws (negation reverses $\leq_t$ but preserves $\leq_k$; conflation reverses $\leq_k$ but preserves $\leq_t$). Fitting develops the bilattice semantics for logic programs: a program's denotation is a fixed point of an operator on the bilattice-valued model space, and the four-valued ($\mathcal{FOUR}$) and nine-valued bilattices give well-founded and active-model semantics naturally as bilattice fixed points. The chapter consolidates the case for bilattices as the right value-domain for systems that must reason under partial information, contradictions, and recursive definitions.
