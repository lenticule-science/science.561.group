# Cosettling

The **backward-dynamics counterpart** of settling. For a closure-operator `T` on the predication-presheaf category `KP(B)` over a base `B`:

$$T^{-1}(w) \;:=\; \{\, v \in \mathrm{KP}(B) \,:\, T(v) = w \,\}.$$

This is a **multi-valued** operation — multiple presheaves typically close to the same state. The cosettling is the **backward-dynamics** counterpart to the forward closure operator: where `T` asks "what does `v` become under one closure step?", `T^{-1}` asks "what could have produced `w` in one step?".

## Iterated cosettling

The full backward-orbit:

$$T^{*-1}(w) \;:=\; \bigcup_{k \geq 0}\, (T^{-1})^k(w) \;=\; \{\, v : T^k(v) = w \,\text{ for some } k \geq 0 \,\}.$$

This collects ALL presheaves that, after some finite number of closure-steps, produce `w`. The iterated cosettling is the **ancestry** of `w` under `T`.

## Why multi-valued

A closure-operator `T` is typically **NOT injective** — different inputs can produce the same output. Concretely:

- `T(⊥) = ⊥` (trivial closure of empty input).
- `T(v) = v` for any `v` that's already at fixed point.
- `T(v_1) = T(v_2) = w` for two valuations that differ only on cells that closure pushes to identical values.

So `T^{-1}(w)` contains ALL presheaves that close to `w` — not just one. The cardinality of `T^{-1}(w)` varies: for `w = ⊥`, it contains everything; for highly-restrictive `w`, it may be a singleton.

## Forward vs backward dynamics

| Direction | Question | Notation |
|---|---|---|
| Forward | "What does `v` become?" | `T(v) = w` |
| Backward | "What could have produced `w`?" | `T^{-1}(w) ∋ v` |

Forward dynamics is deterministic (one input, one output). Backward dynamics is multi-valued (one output, many possible inputs).

This is the **standard discrete-dynamical-systems shape** — forward is a function, backward is a relation. Cosettling makes this shape operational for the substrate.

## Operational use

Two main uses:

**Reachability analysis**: Given a target state `w` (a desired settled valuation), what initial-presheaf classes can reach it? `T^{*-1}(w)` answers — it's the **basin of attraction** of `w` under `T`.

**Counterfactual reasoning**: Given a settled state, what alternative pasts could have produced it? Each `v ∈ T^{-1}(w)` is a counterfactual past whose one-step closure is `w`.

Both uses extend naturally to settling-tier analyses where one wants to invert the orbit `(v_τ)_τ` to find its possible origins.

## Why this lives at settling tier

Cosettling is **operationally part of settling** — it's the backward-direction of the same closure-2-monad action. While the forward direction produces the settled orbit, the backward direction `T^{-1}` produces its preimages. Both directions inhabit settling tier.

The placement `finite-settlement-algebra/hacking/library/cosettling/` records this: cosettling is a settling-tier multi-valued operation defined on the same closure-operator that settling uses forward.
