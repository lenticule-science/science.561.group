### Definition — The Transfer Nucleus $\Delta_t$ {#transfer-nucleus}

For each step $s \in \Sigma$ independent of $t$ (written $s \perp t$), let
$i_{s,t} : t \to s \star t$ be the one-step extension morphism in $T$. The
**transfer-stable sub-algebra** is the intersection of all restriction images
from one-step extensions:
$$
\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)
\;:=\;
\bigcap_{\substack{s \in \Sigma \\ s \perp t}}
\mathrm{image}\!\bigl(H(i_{s,t}) : H_{s \star t} \to H_t\bigr).
$$
An element $a \in H_t$ is **transfer-stable** when it lies in every such image —
it was already present in every possible next step. The **transfer nucleus** is
then the reflection onto this layer:
$$
\Delta_t(a) \;:=\; \bigwedge\!\bigl\{\, b \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t) \mid b \geq a \,\bigr\}.
$$
This is the smallest transfer-stable element above $a$; it exists because $H_t$ is
finite and $\top_t \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$.

When no $s \perp t$ exists (i.e., every step is dependent on $t$), the intersection
is empty and $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t) = H_t$: every proposition is vacuously
transfer-stable, so $\Delta_t = \mathrm{id}$.

#### Nucleus Properties {#transfer-nucleus-nucleus}

$\Delta_t$ is a nucleus on $H_t$ (§nucleus). Each $\mathrm{image}(H(i_{s,t}))$ is
a sub-Heyting-algebra of $H_t$ (§nuclear-heyting-algebra, image lemma); an intersection of
sub-Heyting-algebras is again one (§nuclear-heyting-algebra, intersection lemma). So
$\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ is a sub-Heyting-algebra and $\Delta_t$ is the reflection
onto it — a nucleus by standard locale theory (Johnstone, *Stone Spaces* §II.2.2). ✓

#### Fixed Points {#transfer-nucleus-fixed-points}

An element is **transfer-stable** — $a \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ — exactly when for
every $s \perp t$ there exists $b \in H_{s \star t}$ with $H(i_{s,t})(b) = a$: it arises
as the restriction of some proposition in every possible one-step extension. See
§delta-linking for the canonical-transfer characterization and lifting lemma.

The sub-algebra $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ is the **extension-witnessed layer** of $H_t$;
its internal structure is developed in §transfer-stable-fiber.

The **Transfer** operator of FiberNuclearLanguage ([fiber-nuclear-language](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/predicated-agentic-orchestration-platform/library/relational-hyperverse-repository/math/fiber-nuclear-language.md)) is the internal-language name for $\Delta_t$:
$\mathsf{Transfer}(a)$ denotes $\Delta_t(a)$. The gap $a < \Delta_t(a)$ is the space of
propositional possibility (§possible) that remains open at $t$.

In the saturation–transfer dyad (§saturation-transfer-dyad), $\Delta_t$ is the
forward-facing nucleus — oriented toward the committed future (one-step extensions).

#### Connection to the Commutation Topology {#transfer-topology-connection}

The definition of $\Delta_t$ and the commutation topology $J$ (§commutation-topology)
share the same independence structure: both are generated by $I \subseteq \Sigma \times \Sigma$.
The relation $\{s \in \Sigma : s \perp t\}$ appears

- **algebraically** in $\Delta_t$: as the set of steps contributing one-step
  independent extensions $s \star t$, whose restriction images are intersected to
  produce $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$;
- **geometrically** in $J$: as commutation covers by step-set-disjoint sub-histories.

The Axiom of Locality (§locality) — that $H$ is a sheaf for $J$ — is what makes
the intersection in $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ coherent: the sheaf condition ensures
that restriction images from independent one-step extensions combine consistently,
and the fiber product decomposition (§locality, Corollary) gives the precise shape
of each image. The open theorem that $J$ is canonically determined by the transfer
nucleus structure of the fiber doctrine is an open theorem (§commutation-topology).
