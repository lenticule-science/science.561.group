# Step-n cohomological rank theorem

**Statement.** *Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a primitive [step-$n$ substitution rule](../objects/step-n-substitution-rule.md) over a base $B$ with $E = \omega_1$, with substitution matrix $M \in \mathrm{Mat}_{n \times n}(\mathbb{N})$ and $\det M \neq 0$. Let $v_\mathcal{S}$ be a settling generated by $\mathcal{S}$. Then the post-settling-shift-quotient classifying invariant*

$$\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)}(v_\mathcal{S}) \;:=\; \mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}(v_\mathcal{S}) \big/ \sim_{\mathrm{settling\text{-}shift}}$$

*is a $\mathbb{Z}$-module of rank exactly $n$. When $\det M = \pm 1$ (the unimodular case),*

$$\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)}(v_\mathcal{S}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}^n$$

*with no torsion, and the $n$ generators correspond to the $n$ substitution-letters of $\mathcal{S}$.*

## Proof

The proof has three parts: (1) pre-quotient identification, (2) settling-shift orbit count equals $n$, (3) reduction to the Anderson-Putnam direct limit.

### Part 1: Pre-quotient invariant

By the [aperiodic-regime cohomology via direct limit theorem](aperiodic-regime-cohomology-via-direct-limit.md),

$$H^1(\mathbf{T}^*_{\leq \omega_1}, \mathcal{F}_v) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{\mu \in L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\omega_1)} \mathbb{Z}$$

with one generator per non-Scott-continuous firing ordinal. The pre-quotient classifying invariant is uncountable-rank.

### Part 2: Settling-shift orbits on $L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\omega_1)$

The settling-shift relation $\sim$ on firing ordinals is generated by ordinal translations $t : \omega_1 \to \omega_1$ that preserve $v_\mathcal{S}$. For a substitution-recurrence rule, the autoequivalences of $v_\mathcal{S}$ are generated by powers of the substitution itself: $\sigma^k$ acts on positions by $p \mapsto \sigma^k(p)$ and on substitution stages by $\tau \mapsto \tau + k$.

**Letter type.** Each firing $\mu \in L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\omega_1)$ corresponds to a substitution stage $\tau(\mu)$ and a position $p(\mu)$ in the substitution sequence at that stage. The position carries a letter type $\ell(\mu) \in \{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}$ — the letter the substitution rule writes at that position.

**Letter type is settling-shift invariant.** $\sigma^k$ maps an $a_i$-position to an $a_i$-position, by the deterministic letter-rewriting structure of the substitution rule. Hence $\mu \sim \mu' \implies \ell(\mu) = \ell(\mu')$.

**Letter type is the unique invariant.** By primitivity of $M$, for each letter $a_i$ the orbit of $a_i$-positions under iterated substitution is dense in the substitution sequence — every position eventually carries $a_i$ infinitely often as $\tau \to \infty$. So for any two firings $\mu, \mu'$ with $\ell(\mu) = \ell(\mu') = a_i$, there exists $k$ and an ordinal-translation $t$ generated by $\sigma^k$ such that $t(\mu) = \mu'$. Hence $\ell(\mu) = \ell(\mu') \implies \mu \sim \mu'$.

**Orbit count.** Combining: $L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\omega_1)/\sim$ is in bijection with the alphabet $\{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}$, so the orbit count is exactly $n$.

### Part 3: Reduction to the Anderson-Putnam direct limit

The post-quotient invariant is the $\mathbb{Z}$-module spanned by the $n$ orbit-classes:

$$\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)}(v_\mathcal{S}) \;=\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^n \mathbb{Z} \cdot [e_{a_i}],$$

with the substitution endomorphism inducing the action of $M^T$ on $\mathbb{Z}^n$ (the $i$-th generator $e_{a_i}$ is sent to $\sum_j M_{ij} e_{a_j}$, since the substitution rule writes $M_{ij}$ copies of letter $a_j$ at each $a_i$-position).

Taking the direct limit under iterated $M^T$ gives:

$$\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)}(v_\mathcal{S}) \;\cong\; \varinjlim_k \,(\mathbb{Z}^n \xrightarrow{M^T} \mathbb{Z}^n \xrightarrow{M^T} \cdots).$$

This is exactly the [Anderson-Putnam direct-limit cohomology](../objects/anderson-putnam-direct-limit.md) of the substitution tiling space $\Omega_\sigma$:

$$\check{H}^1(\Omega_\sigma) \;\cong\; \varinjlim_k \,(\mathbb{Z}^n, M^T).$$

By [@anderson-putnam1998], for a primitive substitution this is rank $n$.

### Rank computation

For non-singular $M$ (i.e. $\det M \neq 0$), $M^T$ is invertible over $\mathbb{Q}$ and the direct limit embeds in $\mathbb{Q}^n$ as the increasing union of $\mathbb{Z}$-lattices

$$\varinjlim_k (\mathbb{Z}^n, M^T) \;\cong\; \bigcup_{k \geq 0} (M^T)^{-k}(\mathbb{Z}^n) \;\subset\; \mathbb{Q}^n.$$

This is a $\mathbb{Z}$-submodule of $\mathbb{Q}^n$ containing $\mathbb{Z}^n$, so it has $\mathbb{Z}$-rank exactly $n$.

When $\det M = \pm 1$ (unimodular), $(M^T)^{-1} \in \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{Z})$, so $(M^T)^{-k}(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \mathbb{Z}^n$ for all $k$, and the direct limit is $\mathbb{Z}^n$ exactly. When $|\det M| = d > 1$, the direct limit is $\mathbb{Z}[1/d]$-localised in the appropriate $n$-tuple structure — still rank $n$, but with denominators allowed.

In either case, $\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)}(v_\mathcal{S})$ has rank exactly $n$. $\square$

## Verification at concrete $n$

Computational verification via the [Anderson-Putnam cohomology tool](../tools/anderson-putnam-cohomology/compute.py), using SymPy:

| Substitution | $n$ | $\det M$ | $\check{H}^1(\Omega_\sigma)$ | Smith invariants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fibonacci ($\sigma : a \mapsto ab, b \mapsto a$) | 2 | $-1$ | $\mathbb{Z}^2$ | $(1, 1)$ |
| Padovan ($\sigma : a \mapsto b, b \mapsto c, c \mapsto ab$) | 3 | $1$ | $\mathbb{Z}^3$ | $(1, 1, 1)$ |
| Tribonacci ($\sigma : a \mapsto ab, b \mapsto ac, c \mapsto a$) | 3 | $1$ | $\mathbb{Z}^3$ | $(1, 1, 1)$ |
| Tetranacci ($\sigma : a \mapsto ab, b \mapsto ac, c \mapsto ad, d \mapsto a$) | 4 | $-1$ | $\mathbb{Z}^4$ | $(1, 1, 1, 1)$ |
| Pentanacci (analogous) | 5 | $1$ | $\mathbb{Z}^5$ | $(1, 1, 1, 1, 1)$ |

All canonical step-$n$ substitutions in the corpus are unimodular ($|\det M| = 1$), giving $\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)} = \mathbb{Z}^n$ exactly without torsion factors. The Smith invariants $(1, 1, \ldots, 1)$ confirm the matrices are integrally equivalent to the identity, so no torsion is picked up by the direct limit.

## What this gives

Two structural payoffs:

**1. The corpus recovers Anderson-Putnam.** The post-quotient rank is correctly $n$ for $n$-letter primitive substitutions; the corpus's transfinite-cellular framework reproduces the established $\check{H}^1(\Omega_\sigma) \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$ result.

**2. The corpus's pre-quotient invariant is strictly richer.** The pre-quotient $\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}$ has uncountable rank (one generator per firing ordinal in $L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\omega_1)$); the post-quotient $\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)}$ has rank $n$. The "+ uncountable" content lost in the quotient is the corpus's structurally-new information beyond Anderson-Putnam — corresponding to the choice of *which* settling-shift orbit representative is canonical.

## Position in the program

Foundational theorem of the step-$n$ family. Pairs the structural parameter $n$ with the cohomological rank, establishing the central correspondence the family is built on. Consumed by the [step-n meta-stratification theorem](step-n-meta-stratification.md), the [step-shift functoriality theorem](step-shift-functoriality-theorem.md), and all the L5 sub-lineage examples computing classifying invariants.

The non-singular hypothesis $\det M \neq 0$ is satisfied by all canonical step-$n$ substitutions; the structurally degenerate $\det M = 0$ case (M not full-rank) gives a strictly smaller rank and is excluded from the standard step-$n$ family.
