# d-frame-valued sheaf condition

The [d-frame-valued settling sheaf](../../../../../../../../../../../../valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/d-frame-valued-sheaf/d-frame-valued-sheaf.md) $\mathbb{F}_U$ satisfies the sheaf condition on the [bitopological settling site](../../../../../../../../../../../../valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/bitopological-settling-site/bitopological-settling-site.md): for every covering family $\{(q_i, \tau_i, U_i) \to (q, \tau, U)\}_i$ in the topology $J$, sections over $(q, \tau, U)$ agree with compatible families of sections over the $(q_i, \tau_i, U_i)$.

Concretely, given d-frame-valued sections $\mathbb{F}_U(q_i, \tau_i, U_i)$ that agree on pairwise pullbacks, there exists a unique d-frame $\mathbb{F}_U(q, \tau, U)$ whose pullbacks reproduce the family.

## Proof

The site morphisms in the bitopological settling site are tuples $(f, h, j)$ — a predicate-category morphism $f$, an ordinal map $h$, and a clopen inclusion $j$.
By the [bitopological settling site axioms](../../../../../../../../../../../../valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/bitopological-settling-site/content/assets/legacy/bitopological-settling-site-axioms.md) the topology $J$ is generated by *fiber covers* — covers where $f$ is the identity and $h$ is the identity, varying only the clopen $j : U' \hookrightarrow U$.
So it suffices to prove the sheaf condition for fiber covers; the predicate- and settling-axes contribute via 2-functoriality of the site, not via descent.

For a fiber cover $\{U_i \hookrightarrow U\}_i$ at fixed $(q, \tau)$, write $\mathbb{F}_i := \mathbb{F}_U(q, \tau, U_i)$ and $\mathbb{F}_{ij} := \mathbb{F}_U(q, \tau, U_i \cap U_j)$.
The four components of the d-frame must each satisfy the sheaf gluing condition:

**$L_+$ component (powerset of newly-truth-positive cells).** For each $i$, $L_+^{(i)} = \mathcal{P}(\Delta^{+, *}_\tau(q) \cap U_i)$.
The assignment $U \mapsto \mathcal{P}(\Delta^{+, *}_\tau(q) \cap U)$ is the *powerset frame on a subset functor* applied to the cell-set restriction.
Powerset on subset is a sheaf for any topology where covers are jointly surjective on cells: a section over $U$ (a subset $A \subseteq \Delta^{+, *}_\tau(q) \cap U$) is determined by its restrictions $A_i := A \cap U_i$, and any compatible family $(A_i)_i$ glues to $A := \bigcup_i A_i$.
The fiber covers in $J$ are jointly surjective on the cell index by the [bitopological settling site axioms](../../../../../../../../../../../../valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/bitopological-settling-site/content/assets/legacy/bitopological-settling-site-axioms.md), so $L_+$ glues. $\checkmark$

**$L_-$ component (powerset of newly-falsity-positive cells).** Same argument as $L_+$, applied to $\Delta^{-, *}_\tau(q)$. $\checkmark$

**Consistency relation $\mathrm{con}$.** A pair $(A, B) \in L_+ \times L_-$ is consistent on $U$ iff $A$ and $B$ don't co-witness a $\top$-cell — equivalently, there is no $(s, o) \in U$ such that the cell first acquires its $\mathbf{t}$-component and its $\mathbf{f}$-component at the same stage $\tau$ within both $A$ and $B$.
This is a **local property**: it can be checked at each cell separately.
Hence $(A, B)$ consistent on $U$ iff $(A_i, B_i)$ consistent on each $U_i$ for any cover $\{U_i\}$, where $A_i = A \cap U_i$ and $B_i = B \cap U_i$.
So $\mathrm{con}$ is preserved by covers in both directions: a globally consistent pair restricts to locally consistent pairs, and locally consistent pairs glue to a globally consistent pair. $\checkmark$

**Totality relation $\mathrm{tot}$.** A pair $(A, B)$ is total on $U$ iff $A \cup B \supseteq$ (settled cells of $U$ at $\tau$).
This is also local: a cell is settled iff it's in any $A_i \cup B_i$.
So $A \cup B$ covers settled cells of $U$ iff each $A_i \cup B_i$ covers settled cells of $U_i$. $\checkmark$

The four components glue independently and consistently.
The unique gluing is

$$\mathbb{F}_U(q, \tau, U) \;=\; \bigl(L_+, \,L_-, \,\mathrm{con}, \,\mathrm{tot}\bigr)$$

with $L_+ = \bigcup_i L_+^{(i)}$, $L_- = \bigcup_i L_-^{(i)}$, and the relations as the joint extension of the local relations. $\square$

## Reading

The sheaf condition is the foundational well-definedness check for the d-frame-valued setting.
With it established, $\mathbb{F}_U$ is a genuine $\mathbf{dFrm}$-valued sheaf on the bitopological settling site, not merely a presheaf, and downstream cohomological theorems are at least *statable* on a well-defined object.

The proof structure is **component-wise reduction**: the d-frame's four pieces ($L_+$, $L_-$, $\mathrm{con}$, $\mathrm{tot}$) each glue independently, and the joint gluing is forced.
The argument uses:

- *Powerset on subset is a sheaf.* Standard.
- *Joint surjectivity of fiber covers on the cell index.* Inherited from the bitopological settling site axioms.
- *Locality of consistency and totality.* These relations are checkable cell-by-cell, hence preserved by covers.

The proof does **not** use any d-frame-specific homological-algebra machinery — it's purely the sheaf gluing axioms applied to the four-component structure.
This makes it the cleanest available foundation for the bilattice-valued cohomology development.

## Position in the program

First proved theorem of the [bilattice-valued cohomology chapter](../narratives/bilattice-cohomology-opener.md).
With the sheaf condition established, the [d-frame ↔ $\mathbf{Ab}$ comparison](d-frame-cohomology-comparison.md)'s section-level part is automatic from the definitions, and the cohomological development can begin.

Notably, this theorem is **independent of the choice of derived-functor framework** for d-frame cohomology — whether the chapter ultimately works in d-modules ([@jakl2018]), takes cohomology by fiat as the comparison target, or chooses a non-abelian derived setting, the d-frame-valued sheaf $\mathbb{F}_U$ is well-defined as a sheaf on the bitopological settling site.
So this theorem is a stable foundation regardless of how subsequent framework choices unfold.
