Let $X$ be a set.

**Definition.** A *topology* on $X$ is a collection $\tau \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ satisfying:

1. $\emptyset \in \tau$ and $X \in \tau$.
2. If $\{U_i\}_{i \in I} \subseteq \tau$, then $\bigcup_{i \in I} U_i \in \tau$ (closure under arbitrary unions).
3. If $U_1, \ldots, U_n \in \tau$, then $U_1 \cap \cdots \cap U_n \in \tau$ (closure under finite intersections).

The pair $(X, \tau)$ is a *topological space*. Members of $\tau$ are called [open sets](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/open-set.md).

**Proposition.** *On any set $X$, there is a coarsest topology $\tau = \{\emptyset, X\}$ (the indiscrete topology) and a finest topology $\tau = \mathcal{P}(X)$ (the discrete topology). The collection of all topologies on $X$, ordered by inclusion, forms a complete lattice.*

*Proof sketch.* An arbitrary intersection of topologies on $X$ is a topology on $X$ (each axiom is preserved by intersection). The lattice join of a family of topologies is the coarsest topology containing all of them, which exists because the intersection of all topologies containing the family is itself a topology. $\square$

**Proposition.** *The open sets $\tau$ of $(X, \tau)$, ordered by inclusion, form a [frame](../../../objects/posets/terms/lattice.md): a complete [lattice](../../../objects/posets/terms/lattice.md) in which finite [meets](../../../objects/posets/terms/meet.md) (intersections) distribute over arbitrary [joins](../../../objects/posets/terms/join.md) (unions). Equivalently, $\tau$ is a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../objects/universes/semiotic-universe/terms/complete-heyting-algebra.md) with [Heyting implication](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/heyting-implication.md) $U \Rightarrow V = \bigcup\{W \in \tau : U \cap W \subseteq V\}$.*

**Proposition.** *Every metric space $(M, d)$ carries a canonical topology whose open sets are the unions of open balls $B(x, \varepsilon) = \{y \in M : d(x, y) < \varepsilon\}$.*

**Proposition.** *The [category](../../../objects/categories/ordinary-categories/terms/category.md) $\mathbf{Top}$ of topological spaces and [continuous maps](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/continuous-map.md) admits arbitrary products and coproducts, as well as subspace and quotient constructions.*

**Examples.**

1. **Standard topology on $\mathbb{R}$.** Generated by open intervals $(a, b)$. Equivalently, the metric topology for $d(x, y) = |x - y|$.
2. **Discrete topology.** Every subset of $X$ is open. Every function out of a discrete space is [continuous](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/continuous-map.md).
3. **Indiscrete topology.** Only $\emptyset$ and $X$ are open. Every function into an indiscrete space is continuous.
4. **Cofinite topology.** The open sets are $\emptyset$ and all subsets whose complement is finite.

---

*Remark (categorical and algebraic perspectives).* Viewing $(X, \tau)$ as a [category](../../../objects/categories/ordinary-categories/terms/category.md) whose objects are open sets and whose [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md) are inclusions is the starting point of [sheaf theory](../../sheaf-theory/index.md): a [presheaf](../../sheaf-theory/terms/presheaf.md) assigns data to each open set, and a [sheaf](../../sheaf-theory/terms/sheaf.md) is a presheaf whose local data glues uniquely. The frame description of $\tau$ generalizes to [locales](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/site.md) (pointless topology) and to the [closure operators](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) studied in order theory.
