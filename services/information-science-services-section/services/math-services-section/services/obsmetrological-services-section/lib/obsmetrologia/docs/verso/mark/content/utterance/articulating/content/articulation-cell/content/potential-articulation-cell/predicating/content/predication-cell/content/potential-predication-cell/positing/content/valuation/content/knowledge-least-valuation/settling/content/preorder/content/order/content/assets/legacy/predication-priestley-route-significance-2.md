# Why the Priestley route

The Priestley side of an [predication hyperdoctrine](../../objects/predication-hyperdoctrine.md) was already on offer at the bilattice level via [Jung–Rivieccio duality](../../theorems/jung-rivieccio-duality.md), and the [predication settling Priestley dual](../../objects/predication-priestley-dual.md) flagged it as an alternative computational representation. [Priestley dualizability](../../theorems/priestley-dualizability.md) elevates that fiber-level fact to the full hyperdoctrine: the entire tuple $(\mathcal{E}, p, \mathbb{M}, \mathbb{N}, \mathrm{Cl}, \mathbf{T})$ has a coherent dual $(\mathcal{E}^*, p^*, \mathbb{M}^*, \mathbb{N}^*, \mathrm{Cl}^*, \mathbf{T}^*)$ — the [dual predication hyperdoctrine](../../objects/dual-predication-hyperdoctrine.md).

## What the dual buys that the primal does not

The four [regimes](../../theorems/four-regime-dichotomy.md) are not symmetric in what their *primal* invariants reveal. The strict and Scott-continuous regimes stabilize at fixed points, and Knaster–Tarski / Cousot–Cousot machinery on the primal side already extracts everything one wants. The transfinite-bounded regime stabilizes only transfinitely, but stabilizes; primal fixed-point arguments still apply with patience. The aperiodic regime — [aperiodic settlings](../../teaching/explanations/aperiodic-settling.md) — has *no* primal fixed point in the working universe. The orbit is strictly $\leq_k$-ascending forever, and there is nothing for a primal fixed-point theorem to grasp.

The dual side carries cohomology. Sheaves on the dual paired Priestley space, equipped with the dual settling algebra structure, admit derived-functor cohomology $H^n(\mathbf{T}^*, F)$. In the aperiodic regime, those cohomology classes detect orbital invariants that have no primal counterpart: the aperiodic orbit's "shape" is recorded as a class in the dual cohomology, not as a primal limit.

This is why the Priestley dualization is structural rather than ornamental. It reroutes the entire program through Stone-dual space precisely so that the cohomologically rich regime — the one that fixed-point methods cannot touch — becomes accessible.

[Priestley dualizability](../../theorems/priestley-dualizability.md) is the bridge: it makes the dual side a fully structured object on which the cohomological apparatus of subsequent layers has somewhere to live.
