\section{Chromatic carriers: colouring as factor map}

This subgraph exposes the chromatic carrier that the textile and causal
surfaces already use in prose.  The Lean settlement is not a new primitive
definition in the Blueprint: the incidence claim is that proper colouring,
factor maps, FOUR-valued palettes, and complete-graph obstructions are the
same carrier read at different profiles.

\begin{proposition}[Proper colouring is a factor map]\label{thm:proper-colouring-factor-map}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.properColoring_iff_hom}\leanok\uses{thm:cover-nerve}
A proper colouring of a graph is exactly a graph homomorphism into the complete
graph on its palette.  This is the chromatic settlement of the slogan
``colouring is factor map'': local adjacency constraints are carried by a map
to a finite colour shift, not by an external decoration.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Colourings pull back along factor maps]\label{thm:colourings-pull-back}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.ProperColoring.comp}\leanok\uses{thm:proper-colouring-factor-map}
If a factor map sends one graph carrier into another and the target is properly
coloured, the source inherits a proper colouring by pullback.  This is the
functorial weld behind sofic covers, MLD mediators, and every place the proof
tree says a local finite rule is read through a simpler chromatic carrier.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Bool-colouring is bipartiteness]\label{thm:bool-colouring-bipartite}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.bipartite_iff_colorableBool}\leanok\uses{thm:proper-colouring-factor-map}
The old two-colour graph layer is exactly the Bool-valued instance of proper
colouring.  Plain weave, bipartite causal splitting, and two-phase alternation
therefore sit inside the same carrier rather than forming a separate rule.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Odd closed walks obstruct two-colouring]\label{thm:odd-cycle-obstructs-two-colouring}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.not_bipartite_of_odd_closed_walk}\leanok\uses{thm:bool-colouring-bipartite}
An odd closed walk blocks Bool-colouring.  In the incidence graph this is the
chromatic obstruction fiber: the first place where a local alternation rule
cannot be globally combed into two phases.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[FOUR is the four-colour palette]\label{thm:four-colour-palette-is-four}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.fourColouring, Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.fourColouring_iff_hom}\leanok\uses{thm:four-bilattice, thm:proper-colouring-factor-map}
A Charge-valued proper colouring is a four-colouring whose palette is the
Belnap FOUR.  The textile over/under charges and the complete-graph
four-colour shift are one alphabet seen through the bilattice and chromatic
profiles of the same claim-site.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Complete graphs force distinct colours]\label{thm:complete-graph-forces-distinct-colours}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.completeGraph_colorable, Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.proper_completeGraph_injective}\leanok\uses{thm:proper-colouring-factor-map}
The complete graph on a palette is coloured by the palette itself, and every
proper colouring of a complete graph is injective on vertices.  This is the
finite chromatic lower-bound carrier: if every pair conflicts, no two vertices
can share a colour.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$K_4$ requires the FOUR]\label{thm:k4-requires-four-charges}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.K4_four_colorable, Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.K4_requires_FOUR}\leanok\uses{thm:four-colour-palette-is-four, thm:complete-graph-forces-distinct-colours}
The complete graph on Charge is colourable by Charge, and every proper
colouring of it uses the four charges injectively.  Thus ``four colours'' is
not a loose metaphor in this proof tree: the extremal chromatic carrier forces
the Belnap FOUR as its palette.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$K_4$ has \(4! = 24\) proper FOUR-colourings]\label{thm:k4-proper-colouring-count}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.properK4Colourings_count}\leanok\uses{thm:k4-requires-four-charges}
The finite information face of the same obstruction is the permutation count:
proper FOUR-colourings of \(K_4\) are exactly the assignments that put the four
charges on the four vertices without repetition, so the concrete list of proper
colourings has length \(24\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Realized tile-adjacency chromatic numbers are bounded and attained]\label{thm:realized-tile-adjacency-chromatic-numbers}\lean{Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.orthogonal_chromaticNumber_bounded_attained, Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.triangular_chromaticNumber_bounded_attained, Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.king_chromaticNumber_bounded_attained, Obsmetrologia.Combinatorics.corpus_planar_tile_patches_four_colorable}\leanok\uses{thm:k4-requires-four-charges, thm:complete-graph-forces-distinct-colours}
The realized finite tile-adjacency conventions in the corpus have exact
chromatic witnesses: orthogonal adjacency is attained at two colours,
triangular adjacency is attained at three colours, and king adjacency is
attained at FOUR.  Consequently every planar tile patch represented by these
realized convention carriers is FOUR-colourable.  The quantifier here is the
finite corpus carrier \texttt{CorpusTileAdjacencyConvention}; it is not an
unproved appeal to a general planar four-colour theorem.
\end{theorem}
