## What this node is

A **bounded lattice** is a [lattice](../../lattice.md) with two distinguished
elements: a **top** $\top$ that is above everything ($a \le \top$ for all $a$) and
a **bottom** $\bot$ that is below everything ($\bot \le a$ for all $a$). It is the
lattice with its two extreme elements named — nothing more is added to the order,
only the demand that the order *have* a greatest and a least element.

The two bounds are the nullary cases of the lattice operations: $\top$ is the
[meet](../meet/meet.md) of the empty set (vacuously, every element is a
lower bound of nothing, and the greatest such is $\top$) and equally the
[join](../join/join.md) of the whole carrier; $\bot$ is the join of the
empty set and the meet of the whole carrier. By [order](../../../../order.md)'s
antisymmetry each is unique.

## Generated from the lattice

The lattice gives a meet and join for every *pair*. Extend that demand to the
empty collection and the two bounds appear: a top and a bottom. This is the first
refinement because it adds no new kind of operation — it only closes the existing
operations under the nullary case. Every finite lattice is already bounded, and
every [complete lattice](../complete-lattice/complete-lattice.md) is bounded,
since arbitrary meets and joins include the empty one.

Settling needs both bounds. The monotone graded chain settling ascends *begins*
at a least element — the value carrying no commitment, $\bot$ — and the closure
can never carry a cell past $\top$. The floor and ceiling of the ascent are
exactly the two bounds this refinement names; without a $\bot$ there is no
starting point for the iteration, and without a $\top$ no guarantee the ascent
stays inside the order.

## What it generates

Boundedness is the substrate the higher refinements assume:

- a [distributive lattice](../distributive-lattice/distributive-lattice.md) that
  is also bounded is the input to the residuated implication, hence to the
  [Heyting algebra](../distributive-lattice/content/heyting-algebra/heyting-algebra.md),
  whose pseudo-complement $\lnot a = a \to \bot$ is defined *from* $\bot$;
- a [complete lattice](../complete-lattice/complete-lattice.md) is bounded as a
  consequence, so completeness is the stronger sibling that produces this one for
  free.

The four-valued [bilattice](../../../../../../../../../../../../pre-bilattice/content/bilattice/bilattice.md)
is bounded under both of its orders at once: on the knowledge axis $\bot$
(told nothing) sits below and $\top$ (told both) above; on the truth axis $f$
below and $t$ above.

## Orientation

Generated by [lattice](../../lattice.md), generated in turn by
[order](../../../../order.md) and [settling](../../../../../../../../settling.md).
Its strengthening to all-subset bounds is the
[complete lattice](../complete-lattice/complete-lattice.md); its combination with
distributivity and implication is the
[Heyting algebra](../distributive-lattice/content/heyting-algebra/heyting-algebra.md).
