# Positing-closure commutation defect

This example shows why commutation must be tested as a predication-valued calculation, not assumed from the names of the operations.

Fix two cells $\phi$ and $\psi$ over $\mathbf{FOUR}$. Let $v$ be the all-$\bot$ valuation. Let

$$P := \mathrm{posit}_{(\phi,\mathbf{t})}$$

and let $C$ be the closure operator generated by the single rule

$$\phi=\mathbf{t} \Longrightarrow \psi=\mathbf{t}.$$

Concretely, $C(x)$ preserves $x$ and additionally joins $\mathbf{t}$ into $\psi$ whenever $x(\phi)$ contains positive evidence.

## Two composites

First posit, then close:

$$C(P(v))(\phi)=\mathbf{t}, \qquad C(P(v))(\psi)=\mathbf{t}.$$

First close, then posit:

$$P(C(v))(\phi)=\mathbf{t}, \qquad P(C(v))(\psi)=\bot.$$

The composites agree at $\phi$ but not at $\psi$. Therefore

$$C \circ P \neq P \circ C$$

at $v$.

## Defect

The FOUR symmetric evidence difference is

$$\delta^{\oplus}_k(C(P(v)), P(C(v)))(\phi)=\bot$$

and

$$\delta^{\oplus}_k(C(P(v)), P(C(v)))(\psi)=\mathbf{t}.$$

The defect is not merely "different." It says exactly that the close-after-posit path contains positive evidence at $\psi$ which the posit-after-close path lacks.

## Calculator reading

This is the minimal calculator test for the operational ordering claim in the [positing morphism](../morphisms/positing-morphism.md): evidence injection before consequence derivation is not interchangeable with consequence derivation before evidence injection. The noncommutation defect is itself a predication valuation.
