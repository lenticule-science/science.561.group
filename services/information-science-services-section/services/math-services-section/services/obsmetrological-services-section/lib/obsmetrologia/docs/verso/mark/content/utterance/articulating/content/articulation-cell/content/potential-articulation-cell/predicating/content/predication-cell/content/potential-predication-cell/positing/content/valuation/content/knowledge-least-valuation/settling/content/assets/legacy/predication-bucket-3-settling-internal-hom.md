# Settling internal Hom

The corpus's [settling-tensor product theorem](../../../references/settling-tensor-product.md) gave a symmetric monoidal structure on settlings-with-rules. **This theorem closes the structure**: defining the settling internal Hom $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{settle}}$ such that $(\mathrm{Settlings}^{\mathrm{rule}}_B, \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}}, \mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{settle}}, \mathbf{1})$ is a closed symmetric monoidal category, with the standard tensor-Hom adjunction.

## Statement

**Theorem (settling internal Hom).** *Define the **settling internal Hom**:*

$$\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{settle}}((\mathcal{S}_1, v_1), (\mathcal{S}_2, v_2)) \;:=\; (\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2,\; T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_1, v_2)))$$

*where $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}$ is the [Q-enriched sheaf internal Hom](../../bitopological-settling-site/content/quantale-enriched-sheaf/content/q-enriched-sheaf-tensor/content/q-enriched-sheaf-hom/q-enriched-sheaf-hom.md), $T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}$ is the closure operator generated by the union of the rule systems.*

*Then $(\mathrm{Settlings}^{\mathrm{rule}}_B, \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}}, \mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{settle}}, \mathbf{1})$ is a closed symmetric monoidal category, with the natural adjunction*

$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Settlings}}((\mathcal{S}_1, v_1) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} (\mathcal{S}_2, v_2),\; (\mathcal{S}_3, v_3)) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Settlings}}((\mathcal{S}_1, v_1),\; \mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{settle}}((\mathcal{S}_2, v_2),\; (\mathcal{S}_3, v_3)))$$

*natural in all three arguments.*

## Proof

The proof structurally mirrors the [settling-tensor product theorem](../../../references/settling-tensor-product.md). Three pieces:

### Step 1: $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_1, v_2)$ is a sheaf

By the [Q-enriched closed-monoidal structure theorem](../../bitopological-settling-site/content/quantale-enriched-sheaf/references/q-enriched-sheaf-closed-monoidal-structure.md), the pointwise Q-enriched Hom $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_1, v_2)$ is a sheaf when $v_2$ is a sheaf (Step 2 of that theorem: pointwise Hom of a sheaf into a sheaf is automatically a sheaf, no sheafification needed).

So $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_1, v_2)$ is well-defined on the parameter base as a Q-enriched sheaf.

### Step 2: The Q-enriched Hom may not be closure-stable

A settling $v_2$ is closure-stable: $T_{\mathcal{S}_2}(v_2) = v_2$. The Q-enriched Hom $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_1, v_2)$ is a different presheaf — it's the Hom of $v_1$ into $v_2$, not $v_2$ itself. Whether this Hom is closure-stable depends on the closure-rule structure.

In general, applying the union closure operator $T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}$ to $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_1, v_2)$ may produce strict inflation: the rule system can detect Hom-content and fire on it, generating new content not in $\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_1, v_2)$ initially.

So we apply $T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}$ to obtain a closure-stable result.

### Step 3: Adjunction (the closed-monoidal property)

The adjunction $\otimes \dashv \mathcal{H}om$ at the Q-enriched sheaf level (per [the Q-enriched closed-monoidal structure theorem](../../bitopological-settling-site/content/quantale-enriched-sheaf/references/q-enriched-sheaf-closed-monoidal-structure.md)):

$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sh}}(F \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} G,\, H) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sh}}(F,\, \mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(G, H)).$$

Lifted to settlings via closure: a settling-morphism $(\mathcal{S}_1, v_1) \otimes_{\mathrm{settle}} (\mathcal{S}_2, v_2) \to (\mathcal{S}_3, v_3)$ is, at the underlying-presheaf level, a morphism

$$T_{\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2}(v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2) \to v_3$$

respecting the closure structures. Equivalently (since $v_3$ is closure-stable under $\mathcal{S}_1 \cup \mathcal{S}_2 \cup \mathcal{S}_3$, which is what the morphism's source domain demands), this corresponds to a Q-enriched sheaf morphism $v_1 \otimes_{\mathrm{Q}} v_2 \to v_3$ that closes consistently. By the Q-enriched adjunction, this is the same as a morphism $v_1 \to \mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{Q}}(v_2, v_3)$, which closes to a settling-morphism

$$(\mathcal{S}_1, v_1) \to \mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{settle}}((\mathcal{S}_2, v_2), (\mathcal{S}_3, v_3)).$$

The adjunction is preserved through closure, giving the closed-monoidal structure on settlings-with-rules. $\square$

## What the internal Hom represents

The Q-enriched internal Hom of two presheaves is "the presheaf of morphisms" at each stalk. Lifted to settlings:

$$\mathcal{H}om_{\mathrm{settle}}(v_1, v_2)(\sigma)$$

is the bilattice-valued *strength* of "how much $v_1$'s content at $\sigma$ implies $v_2$'s content at $\sigma$" — an internal-implication structure. For Boolean-bilattice-valued data:

- $v_1(\sigma) = \mathbf{t}$ and $v_2(\sigma) = \mathbf{t}$: Hom is "true implies true" = $\mathbf{t}$ (strong implication).
- $v_1(\sigma) = \mathbf{t}$ and $v_2(\sigma) = \bot$: Hom is "true implies bot" = $\bot$ (no implication).
- $v_1(\sigma) = \mathbf{f}$ and $v_2(\sigma) = \top$: Hom is "false implies paraconsistent" = $\top$ (vacuous + paraconsistent).
- Etc.

The internal Hom is the corpus's natural **internal implication operator** — settling the implication-strength between two settlings cell-by-cell.

## What this gives

Three structural consequences:

**1. Settlings form a closed symmetric monoidal category** — the corpus's strongest structural categorical statement so far. The framework supports tensor (joint information), internal Hom (implication), and identity (trivial settling) with a clean categorical algebra.

**2. Settling-of-settlings is well-defined.** The internal Hom $\mathcal{H}om(v_1, v_2)$ is itself a settling, hence has its own classification — closure ordinal, regime, cohomological invariant, Klein-four orbit type. **Meta-level reasoning about settlings is internalised** — we can talk about "the classification of settlings-as-implications" without leaving the framework.

**3. The framework supports an internal logic.** With tensor (×), Hom (→), unit (1), and the bilattice's truth structure, the corpus's settlings form a *rich logical category* in the categorical-logic sense. This is the natural home for an *internal-language* approach to spatiotemporal-knowledge dynamics — propositions are settlings, implications are internal Homs, conjunctions are tensors, and the whole framework supports first-order (or higher-order) logic internally.

## Connection to other corpus structures

- **The strong-MLD orbit type extends to internal Hom**: $\mathcal{H}om(v_1, v_2)$ has an orbit type determined by the orbit types of $v_1, v_2$ and the rule-systems' interaction. Concretely: if both inputs are in the conflation-self-dual stratum (truth-axis only), so is the Hom (since negation/conflation act on the bilattice values pointwise and preserve the truth axis).

- **Tunneling theory and internal Hom**: by the [no-tunneling theorem](cl-inv-locally-constant-on-paths.md), settling paths preserve $\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}$. So a settling path of internal Homs $\mathcal{H}om(v_1^t, v_2^t)$ along smooth deformations of $v_1, v_2$ stays within one MLD-class. **Implication structure is rigid under smooth evolution.**

- **The void cone and internal Hom**: the void cone of $\mathcal{H}om(v_1, v_2)$ tracks "cells where the implication is undetermined." This is the corpus's natural object for *open implications* — questions of the form "does $v_1$ imply $v_2$?" that haven't yet been resolved.
