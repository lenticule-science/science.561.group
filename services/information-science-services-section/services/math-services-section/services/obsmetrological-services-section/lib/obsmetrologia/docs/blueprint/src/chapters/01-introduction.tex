\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

This Blueprint is the formal proof plan for Obsmetrologia.  Its objects are
definition, theorem, lemma, proposition, corollary, and example nodes; its
morphisms are the \(\texttt{\textbackslash uses}\) edges between those nodes.
Green nodes name compiled Lean declarations with \(\texttt{\textbackslash lean}\)
and \(\texttt{\textbackslash leanok}\).  Red nodes are precise unproved
obligations.

The document is a reader-facing projection of the decorated proof tree.  In the
Lean library, finite local rules, sofic factors, closure regimes, condensates,
percolation thresholds, Bratteli towers, and phase invariants are not metaphors;
they are formal carriers.  The Blueprint therefore cannot be a single periodic
ladder of topics.  It is a sofic projection: a locally checkable claim graph
whose visible reading forgets some proof decoration while preserving the
admissible dependency structure.

The first region records the generators and local rules: marks, articulation,
predication, value, quantification, and the recurring FOUR.  The second region
records closure, settling, and sheaf formation: the machinery by which local
cells become fixed sections.  The third region records aperiodic order and phase
invariants: classifying invariants, dynamics, spectral gaps, Collatz settlement
projections, and other non-periodic structure visible after settling.

The keystone of the first region is the orbital theorem,
\cref{thm:orbital-exhaustive}: from four entities on, every naturally defined
reindexing of an oriented pair is one of four generators, so predication forces FOUR.
\Cref{thm:fregean-predication-four-chu} records the finite correspondence that follows:
evidence for and against, kept independently, is FOUR's two slots, and claim--valuation
evaluation presents the FOUR-valued Chu space.

The information region is split by the phases already present in Lean.
Tiles are finite-local-rule and sofic-factor cells.  Beads are lenticular
condensates: ported walks whose over-occupation becomes held
\(\top\), jamming, percolation, and mass.  Hair is the horizon-visible
invariant layer: diffraction, curvature, metallic growth, Bratteli levels, and
serial order.  Boundary sectors then project the same machinery into material,
liquid, glass, kinetic, plasma/fluid, dense-matter, quantum, gaming, and
spacetime surfaces.

The \(\texttt{\textbackslash uses}\) graph, not prose order, is the source of
truth.  Prose explains the projection; the graph carries the proof plan.
