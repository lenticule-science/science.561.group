# Exhaustiveness of the four predication cell complex site generators

The exact phrase "predication cell complex site" is not the dominant phrase in the current corpus.
The mathematics appears under the names **predicate category**, **predication cell set**, **predication fiber algebra**, **predication fibration**, and especially **predicate-generator exhaustiveness**.
This resource treats the predication cell complex site as the indexed predication structure whose cells are triples `(subject, predicate, object)` and whose site-like reindexings are generated by the predicate-category morphisms.

The claim is:

> For a binary predication structure over an entity set `E`, the four canonical generator classes -- specialization, converse, subject-projection, and object-projection -- exhaust the natural bottom-preserving bilattice homomorphisms between predication fiber algebras, under `Sym(E)`-equivariance and `|E| >= 3`.

Equivalently: once the system is required to respect entity-renaming symmetry, there are no additional natural ways to move predication values between binary fibers.
Every such movement is one of the four generators, or a finite composite of them, possibly with an invariant sub-support and a bounded bilattice endomorphism.

## First principles

A predication cell is an oriented assertion-position.
It has the form

```text
phi = (s, p, o)
```

where `s` and `o` are entities and `p` is a predicate.
The orientation matters: `(s, p, o)` is not the same cell as `(o, p, s)`.
The cell set is

```text
Phi = E x Pi x E
```

where `E` is the entity set and `Pi` is the predicate set.

For each predicate `p`, the fiber algebra at `p` is

```text
A_p = (L^bowtie)^(E x E)
```

An element of `A_p` assigns a bilattice value to every entity pair `(s, o)`.
The bilattice operations are pointwise: to combine two valuations, combine their values at each `(s, o)`.
This pointwise form is what makes the exhaustiveness proof work.

## The four generators

The predicate category `C` has predicates as objects.
Its four canonical generator classes act by simple maps on the entity-pair index `E x E`.

| Generator | Index behavior | Informal reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specialization | restrict to a structurally designated sub-support | `q` is a sub-predicate of `p` |
| Converse | `(s, o) -> (o, s)` | swap subject and object roles |
| Subject-projection | `(s, o) -> (s, s)` | collapse to the subject diagonal |
| Object-projection | `(s, o) -> (o, o)` | collapse to the object diagonal |

These are not four arbitrary operations.
They are the four entity-role patterns available to a binary oriented cell once entity identity is ignored and only structural role is allowed to matter.

## Why these four are exhaustive

Start with a natural bottom-preserving bilattice homomorphism

```text
phi : A_q -> A_p
```

between two predication fiber algebras over the same entity set `E`.

The proof has five parts.

First, the homomorphism is cell-local.
Because each fiber algebra is pointwise over `E x E`, evaluating `phi(v)` at a target pair `(s', o')` gives a bottom-preserving bilattice homomorphism from a product-like pointwise algebra into one bilattice value.
For each source coordinate `(i, j)`, let `delta_(i,j)^a` be the valuation supported only at `(i, j)` with value `a`.
If `(i, j) != (i', j')`, the knowledge-order meet of the two supported valuations is the everywhere-bottom valuation.
Since `phi` preserves knowledge meet and bottom, two distinct source coordinates cannot both contribute non-bottom content to the same target coordinate.
Thus each target coordinate can read at most one source coordinate, followed by a bilattice endomorphism.

So the homomorphism has the form:

```text
phi(v)(s, o) =
  psi(v(g(s, o)))  if (s, o) is in an invariant support D
  bottom           otherwise
```

Second, naturality makes the partial coordinate map structural.
For every entity renaming `sigma in Sym(E)`, naturality says:

```text
sigma* phi = phi sigma*
```

Equality for all valuations forces three constraints:

- the domain `D` of the partial coordinate map is `Sym(E)`-invariant;
- the coordinate map `g : D -> E x E` is `Sym(E)`-equivariant;
- the bilattice endomorphism `psi` is constant on `Sym(E)`-orbits of target cells.

Third, the invariant supports are only unions of the two `Sym(E)`-orbits of `E x E`:

- the diagonal `Delta = {(s, s)}`;
- the off-diagonal `Delta^c = {(s, o) : s != o}`.

So the possible supports are `empty`, `Delta`, `Delta^c`, and `E x E`.

Fourth, for `|E| >= 3`, the equivariant maps `E x E -> E x E` are exactly four.
On the diagonal, equivariance forces `(s, s)` to map to `(s, s)`.
On the off-diagonal, fix a representative `(s, o)` with `s != o`.
The stabilizer of `(s, o)` is `Sym(E \ {s, o})`, whose fixed points in `E` are exactly `s` and `o`.
Therefore `g(s, o)` must lie in `{s, o} x {s, o}`.
That gives four consistent global maps:

```text
(s, o) -> (s, o)  identity
(s, o) -> (o, s)  swap
(s, o) -> (s, s)  subject-collapse
(s, o) -> (o, o)  object-collapse
```

Fifth, these four maps are precisely the cell-index actions of the four generator classes.
Restriction to an invariant support is specialization.
Swap is converse.
Subject-collapse is subject-projection.
Object-collapse is object-projection.
Therefore every natural bottom-preserving bilattice homomorphism factors through the four generator classes, together with a bounded bilattice endomorphism on the value object.

The same classification can be read combinatorially.
The four-element index-map inventory is the basic relation inventory of the trivial coherent configuration induced by the action `Sym(E) -> E x E`.
In that frame, the theorem says the predicate-category generators are not chosen ad hoc: they are the orbitally available binary role transformations.

More explicitly, `Sym(E)` acts on `E x E` with two orbital relations:

- `Delta = {(s, s) : s in E}`;
- `Delta^c = {(s, o) : s != o}`.

For `|E| >= 3`, the off-diagonal stabilizer `Stab(s, o) = Sym(E \ {s, o})` has fixed-point set `{s, o}`.
This stabilizer-rigidity is what forces the four equivariant global choices above.
Thus the generator inventory is the equivariant function set `Hom_Sym(E)(E x E, E x E)` transported into the pointwise bilattice-fiber setting.

## Boundaries of the claim

The theorem is binary-specific.
It depends on cells being indexed by `E x E`.
For `n`-ary cells indexed by `E^n`, the symmetry group and possible coordinate maps change; the corpus treats `n`-ary exhaustiveness as a separate proof obligation, not as an automatic consequence.

The theorem also depends on bottom preservation.
Without bottom preservation, constant homomorphisms with non-bottom image can slip in.
Those maps preserve algebraic shape only by ignoring the predication support; they are not generated by the four structural role maps.
In the Boolean-base relaxed classification, dropping bottom preservation is still finite rather than open-ended: it adds orbit-coloring generators that paint one or more `Sym(E)`-orbits of `E x E` with a constant non-bottom value.
Those generators are best read as explicit inconsistency-injection or boundary-overlay operations, not as structural role maps.

The theorem also depends on naturality.
Without `Sym(E)`-equivariance, one can define ad hoc homomorphisms that inspect a particular named entity or a particular cell.
Those maps may be bilattice-algebraic, but they are not structural morphisms of the predication site.
Naturality is therefore the substantive restriction: it is what turns an unbounded inventory of entity-distinguishing bilattice maps into the finite orbital inventory above.

For `|E| = 2`, the stabilizer argument used above degenerates and the case should be handled by finite enumeration rather than by the `|E| >= 3` proof.
The global equivariance constraint still gives the same four index-map types, but the clean orbital-relation proof no longer explains the case.
The stable theorem statement therefore remains the `|E| >= 3` classification.
For `|E| <= 1`, the fiber algebra collapses to a trivial or degenerate case.

## Why this matters

The exhaustiveness theorem is the reason the predicate category is not an arbitrary graph of relations.
Its arrows are determined by the structural shape of binary predication itself.
This lets later constructions define functors, fibrations, closure rules, Beck-Chevalley squares, cohomology arguments, and substrate transformers by checking the four generators and extending by composition.

In site language: the four generator classes form the structural coverage of binary predication reindexing.
Any admissible, entity-symmetric, bottom-preserving movement of values between predication fibers is already generated by them.
