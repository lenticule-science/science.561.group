# Step-n classifying invariant

The post-shift-quotient classifying invariant of a step-$n$ substitution settling.
Generalizes the [aperiodic classifying invariant](aperiodic-classifying-invariant.md) to a parameterized family indexed by step depth $n$. **By the [step-n cohomological rank theorem](../theorems/step-n-cohomological-rank-theorem.md), the rank of this invariant is exactly $n$.**

## Definition

Let $v$ be a settling generated by a [step-$n$ substitution rule](step-n-substitution-rule.md) on base $B = (\mathbf{2}, \mathcal{C}, \omega_1, \omega_1 + 1)$.
The settling is in the aperiodic regime with closure ordinal $\kappa = \omega_1$.
Its [aperiodic classifying invariant](aperiodic-classifying-invariant.md) is

$$\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}(v) \;=\; \sum_{\mu \in L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\omega_1)} e_\mu \;\in\; \bigoplus_{\mu \in L_{\mathrm{ns}}(\omega_1)} \mathbb{Z}$$

— uncountable rank.

The **step-$n$ classifying invariant** is the post-quotient version, taking the quotient by *settling-shift* equivalence (the corpus's natural MLD-relation):

$$\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(n)}(v) \;:=\; \mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}(v) \,/\, \sim_{\mathrm{settling-shift}} \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}^n.$$

By the rank theorem, this invariant has rank exactly $n$ — matching the step parameter.

## Why rank-$n$

The post-shift-quotient invariant has rank-$n$ because the substitution dynamics produce *self-similar* cohomology: after settling-shift, distinct firing-ordinals map to a finite set of "substitution-equivalence classes."
The number of equivalence classes is exactly $n$ — one per substitution-letter (one per row/column of the substitution matrix).

This is the corpus's manifestation of Anderson-Putnam's classical computation: $\check{H}^1(\Omega_\sigma) \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$ for primitive $n$-letter substitutions.

## Distinguishing different step-$n$ rules at the same $n$

Two step-$n$ rules with the same $n$ but different substitution matrices have classifying invariants of the same *rank* but possibly different *generators* / different action by the substitution matrix.
The rank-$n$ characterization is necessary but not sufficient for MLD-equivalence — additional invariants (substitution-matrix conjugacy, Bratteli-Vershik invariants, etc.) distinguish step-$n$ rules within rank-$n$.

For the corpus: - Fibonacci ($n = 2$, matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$): $\mathrm{cl}\text{-}\mathrm{inv}^{(2)} \in \mathbb{Z}^2$, with action by Fibonacci's matrix. - Thue-Morse ($n = 2$ but different matrix, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$): also $\mathbb{Z}^2$, but different matrix action.

Same $n$, same rank, but distinct invariants up to matrix conjugacy.

## Position in the program

The step-$n$ classifying invariant is the cohomology-side companion to the step-$n$ moduli sub-stack.
It refines the existing aperiodic-classifying-invariant by the step parameter — exhibiting that the aperiodic invariant has internal stratification indexed by step depth.
