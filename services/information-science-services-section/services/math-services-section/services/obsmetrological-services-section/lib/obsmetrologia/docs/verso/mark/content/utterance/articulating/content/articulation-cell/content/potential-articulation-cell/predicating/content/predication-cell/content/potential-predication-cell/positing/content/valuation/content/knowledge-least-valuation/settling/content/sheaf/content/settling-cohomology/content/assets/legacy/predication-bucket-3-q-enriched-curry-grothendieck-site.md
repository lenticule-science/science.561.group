# Q-enriched Curry equivalence: Grothendieck sites with cellular fibers

The Grothendieck-site lift of the per-fiber Q-enriched Curry equivalence. Where the per-fiber theorems handle individual cell complexes, this theorem assembles the per-fiber equivalence across a fibered site whose fibers are cell complexes — the structural shape exhibited by the [bitopological settling site](../objects/bitopological-settling-site.md). The lift adds one new ingredient — base-change fuzziness — and one new structural verification — that fiber-level Curry maps assemble compatibly across base morphisms.

The theorem covers both **finite-cellular fibers** (using the [CW-complex theorem](q-enriched-curry-cw-complex.md) as per-fiber input) and **transfinite-cellular fibers** (using the [transfinite-CW theorem](q-enriched-curry-transfinite-cw.md) as per-fiber input). The proof structure is identical in both cases; only the cited per-fiber theorem and the per-fiber fuzziness profile differ.

## Setup: fibered cellular sites

A **fibered cellular site** is the data of:

- a small category $\mathbf{B}$ (the **base**) carrying the trivial Grothendieck topology;
- a small category $\mathbf{T}$ (the **total site**) with a Grothendieck topology $J$;
- a functor $\pi : \mathbf{T} \to \mathbf{B}$ (the **projection**);

subject to two structural conditions:

1. **Fiber-cellular condition.** For each $b \in \mathbf{B}$, the fiber $\mathbf{T}_b := \pi^{-1}(b)$ (with the induced category structure) carries the structure of a regular CW complex's face-incidence poset — either a [finite regular CW complex](../theorems/q-enriched-curry-cw-complex.md) or a [transfinite cell complex](../objects/transfinite-cell-complex.md) (countable cells indexed up to $\omega_1$). Write $\mathrm{cells}_k(b)$ for the $k$-cells.

2. **Fiber-only covers.** The covering families in $J$ at any object $(b, \sigma) \in \mathbf{T}$ are exactly the families generated by face inclusions within the fiber $\mathbf{T}_b$. There are no covering families crossing base objects.

The bitopological settling site $(\mathbf{T}^*, J)$ realizes this with $\mathbf{B} = \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$, $\mathbf{T}_{(q, \tau)}$ the face-incidence poset of the [paired Priestley space](../objects/paired-priestley-space.md) $X_q^\tau$, and the fiber-only cover restriction matching the corpus's [why-fiber-covers-only](../narratives/why-fiber-covers-only.md) reasoning.

## Q-enriched data over a fibered cellular site

A $\mathsf{Q}$-enriched cellular sheaf $F$ on $\mathbf{T}$ packages two pieces of data:

- **Per-fiber data.** For each $b \in \mathbf{B}$, the restriction $F_b := F|_{\mathbf{T}_b}$ is a $\mathsf{Q}$-enriched cellular sheaf on the cell complex $\mathbf{T}_b$ — the [finite version](../objects/quantale-enriched-sheaf.md) or [transfinite version](../objects/q-enriched-transfinite-cellular-sheaf.md) according to the fiber's cellularity. Stalks $F_b(\sigma)$ for $\sigma \in \mathrm{cells}(b)$, restrictions $f_{b, \sigma}^\tau$ along face incidences in $\mathbf{T}_b$.

- **Base-change data.** For each $\beta : b' \to b$ in $\mathbf{B}$ and each $\sigma \in \mathrm{cells}(b)$, a $\mathsf{Q}$-functor $F(\beta, \sigma) : F_b(\sigma) \to F_{b'}(\beta^*\sigma)$ where $\beta^*$ is the cellular pullback (the morphism in $\mathbf{T}$ over $\beta$).

The cosheaf $\check{F}$ is structured dually: per-fiber cosheaves $\check{F}_b$ via the [CW-complex theorem](q-enriched-curry-cw-complex.md)'s fuzzy adjoint extensions, and base-change *extensions* $\check{F}(\beta, \sigma) : \check{F}_{b'}(\beta^*\sigma) \to \check{F}_b(\sigma)$ — the **fuzzy right adjoints** of the sheaf-side base-change Q-functors, with quantale-graded fuzziness $q_\beta^\sigma \in \mathsf{Q}$.

## Global cohomology and homology

The site's fiber-only covers mean global cohomology decomposes as **base-presheaf cohomology of fiber cohomologies**.

**Sheaf cohomology.** Define the **fiber-cohomology presheaf**

$$\underline{H}^k(F) \,:\, \mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Q\text{-}Cat}_{\mathrm{cwl}}, \qquad b \mapsto H^k(F_b),$$

with restriction along $\beta : b' \to b$ given by the fiber-cohomology functoriality of the base-change $F(\beta, -)$. The global sheaf cohomology is then the **base-presheaf cohomology** of $\underline{H}^k(F)$:

$$H^n(\mathbf{T}, J;\, F) \,=\, \mathbb{H}^n(\mathbf{B},\, \underline{H}^\bullet(F)),$$

where $\mathbb{H}^n$ is the hypercohomology of the cochain complex $\underline{H}^\bullet(F) = (\underline{H}^k(F))_{k \geq 0}$ as a presheaf of cochain complexes on $\mathbf{B}$. Since $\mathbf{B}$ has trivial topology, $\mathbb{H}^n$ reduces to the limit-and-equalizer construction over $\mathbf{B}$ — the **functor cohomology** of the presheaf.

**Cosheaf homology.** Dually, define the **fiber-homology copresheaf**

$$\underline{H}_k(\check{F}) \,:\, \mathbf{B} \to \mathbf{Q\text{-}Cat}_{\mathrm{cwl}}, \qquad b \mapsto H_k(\check{F}_b),$$

with extension along $\beta : b' \to b$ given by the fiber-homology functoriality of the base-change *extensions* $\check{F}(\beta, -)$. The global cosheaf homology is the **base-copresheaf homology**:

$$H_n(\mathbf{T}, J;\, \check{F}) \,=\, \mathbb{H}_n(\mathbf{B},\, \underline{H}_\bullet(\check{F})).$$

Since $\mathbf{B}$ has trivial topology, $\mathbb{H}_n$ reduces to the colimit-and-coequalizer construction — the functor homology of the copresheaf.

## The theorem

**Theorem (Q-enriched Curry equivalence on fibered cellular sites).** Let $\pi : \mathbf{T} \to \mathbf{B}$ be a fibered cellular site, $F$ a $\mathsf{Q}$-enriched cellular sheaf on $\mathbf{T}$, and $\check{F}$ its associated cosheaf via per-fiber and base-change fuzzy right adjoints. Then for each degree $n$, there is a q-fuzzy equivalence

$$H^n(\mathbf{T}, J;\, F) \,\simeq_{q_n^{\mathrm{glob}}(F)}\, H_n(\mathbf{T}, J;\, \check{F})$$

with **global fuzziness profile**

$$q_n^{\mathrm{glob}}(F) \,=\, \Bigl(\bigwedge_{b \in \mathbf{B}} q_n(\mathbf{T}_b, F_b)\Bigr) \;\wedge\; \Bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta \in \mathrm{Mor}(\mathbf{B})} \prod_{\sigma \in \mathrm{cells}(b)} q_\beta^\sigma\Bigr),$$

where $q_n(\mathbf{T}_b, F_b)$ is the per-fiber fuzziness from the [CW-complex theorem](q-enriched-curry-cw-complex.md) and $q_\beta^\sigma$ is the base-change fuzziness on the stalk $F_b(\sigma)$.

The first meet is the **fiber contribution** (per-fiber Curry fuzziness, accumulated over base objects). The second is the **base-change contribution** (fuzziness from the assembly across base morphisms).

## Proof

The argument has three steps: per-fiber lift, base-presheaf compatibility, and global assembly via the trivial-topology cohomology decomposition.

**Step 1: per-fiber Curry equivalence.** For each $b \in \mathbf{B}$, the appropriate per-fiber theorem applies — the [CW-complex theorem](q-enriched-curry-cw-complex.md) for finite-cellular fibers, the [transfinite-CW theorem](q-enriched-curry-transfinite-cw.md) for transfinite-cellular fibers — giving

$$H^k(F_b) \,\simeq_{q_k(\mathbf{T}_b, F_b)}\, H_k(\check{F}_b)$$

for all relevant $k$. Write $\Phi_b : H^k(F_b) \to H_k(\check{F}_b)$ for the per-fiber forward map and $\Psi_b$ for the inverse, both established by the cited theorem. The fuzziness $q_k(\mathbf{T}_b, F_b)$ is finite-cellular when the fiber is finite, transfinite (a meet over finite sub-complexes) when the fiber is transfinite.

**Step 2: base-presheaf compatibility.** The per-fiber maps $\{\Phi_b\}$ assemble into a Q-natural transformation between the fiber-cohomology presheaf $\underline{H}^k(F)$ and the fiber-homology copresheaf $\underline{H}_k(\check{F})$ — *modulo* a base-change fuzziness term. Concretely, for $\beta : b' \to b$, the diagram

$$\begin{array}{ccc} H^k(F_b) & \xrightarrow{\;\Phi_b\;} & H_k(\check{F}_b) \\ \downarrow{\scriptstyle F(\beta)_*} & & \uparrow{\scriptstyle \check{F}(\beta)_*} \\ H^k(F_{b'}) & \xrightarrow{\;\Phi_{b'}\;} & H_k(\check{F}_{b'}) \end{array}$$

commutes up to fuzzy equivalence, with fuzziness bounded by $\prod_\sigma q_\beta^\sigma$ (the product of stalk-level base-change fuzzinesses).

The reason this is fuzzy and not strict: the base-change fuzzy right adjoint $\check{F}(\beta, -)$ is the fuzzy right adjoint of $F(\beta, -)$ pointwise, not the strict adjoint. Composing fuzzy adjoints with the per-fiber Curry maps incurs the same kind of multiplicative quantale-error as in the per-fiber proof, restricted to the base-change incidences.

**Step 3: global assembly.** Because $\mathbf{B}$ has trivial topology, base-presheaf cohomology / copresheaf homology of $\underline{H}^\bullet$ and $\underline{H}_\bullet$ are computed as limits / colimits over $\mathbf{B}$. By Step 2, the per-fiber forward maps $\{\Phi_b\}$ are a fuzzy-natural transformation $\underline{H}^k(F) \to \underline{H}_k(\check{F})$ of presheaves on $\mathbf{B}$ (the contravariant-vs-covariant clash is mediated by the fuzzy-adjoint base-change extensions on the cosheaf side).

Taking the limit on the left and the colimit on the right — and using that fuzzy-natural transformations between presheaves induce fuzzy maps on the limit/colimit sides — yields a fuzzy Q-functor

$$\Phi^{\mathrm{glob}} : \mathbb{H}^n(\mathbf{B}, \underline{H}^\bullet(F)) \to \mathbb{H}_n(\mathbf{B}, \underline{H}_\bullet(\check{F})),$$

with fuzziness bounded by the meet of (i) the per-fiber Curry fuzzinesses (controlling the per-object compatibility) and (ii) the base-change fuzzinesses (controlling the inter-object compatibility).

The inverse map $\Psi^{\mathrm{glob}}$ is constructed dually via the per-fiber $\Psi_b$ maps and the fuzzy *left* adjoints of base-change extensions (on the cosheaf side) ascending to fuzzy right adjoints on the sheaf side.

**Round-trip verification.** $\Phi^{\mathrm{glob}} \circ \Psi^{\mathrm{glob}}$ and $\Psi^{\mathrm{glob}} \circ \Phi^{\mathrm{glob}}$ are identity-up-to-$q_n^{\mathrm{glob}}$-fuzzy by composition of fuzzy units and counits, exactly mirroring the per-fiber round-trip argument from the CW-complex theorem with the additional base-change layer composed multiplicatively into the total error.

$\square$

## Specialization to the bitopological settling site

For the bitopological settling site $(\mathbf{T}^*, J)$ with $\mathbf{B} = \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$ and fibers the paired Priestley spaces $X_q^\tau$:

**Strict regime ($\kappa < \omega$).** Each fiber is a finite cell complex; the base $\mathbf{B}$ is a finite category. The CW-complex theorem applies per fiber; finitely many base-changes contribute to $q^{\mathrm{glob}}$. The fuzziness is a finite meet of finite-products, in $\mathsf{Q}$.

**Scott-continuous regime ($\kappa = \omega$).** Each fiber is finite (countable cell complex via Husainov acyclicity); base is countable. The CW-complex theorem applies; $q^{\mathrm{glob}}$ is a countable meet, well-defined in $\mathsf{Q}$ (which has all meets / joins).

**Transfinite-bounded regime ($\omega < \kappa < \omega_1$).** Fibers may be transfinite-cellular (with countable cell sets indexed by ordinals $< \omega_1$). The [transfinite-CW theorem](q-enriched-curry-transfinite-cw.md) supplies the per-fiber Curry equivalence; the global theorem assembles it across the base. The per-fiber fuzziness is itself a meet over finite sub-complexes of the fiber, and the global fuzziness is a meet of these per-fiber meets together with the base-change contribution. For the corpus's typical Boolean d-frame data, all per-finite-sub-complex fuzzinesses are 1, so the global fuzziness $q^{\mathrm{glob}} = 1$ — strict equivalence in this regime.

**Aperiodic regime ($\omega_1 \leq \kappa$).** Fibers are $\omega_1$-cofinal transfinite cell complexes. The same per-fiber transfinite-CW input applies; the base $\mathbf{B}$ now has $\omega_1$-many ordinal strata as well, but all base-changes (predicate-changes via $\mathcal{C}$, settling-advancements within $\lambda$) are themselves Boolean-projection Q-functors with strict adjoints in the corpus's typical setting. So $q^{\mathrm{glob}} = 1$ in the aperiodic regime too.

So the theorem now covers all four regimes of the bitopological settling site, with the per-fiber input switching between [finite-CW](q-enriched-curry-cw-complex.md) (strict, Scott-continuous) and [transfinite-CW](q-enriched-curry-transfinite-cw.md) (transfinite-bounded, aperiodic) as appropriate. The Verdier duality conjecture is reduced to "established at draft level across all four regimes" — a substantial completion of the cellular extension plan.

## What's open

**Internal to this theorem (Layer 2 specifics):**

1. **Step 2 verification — fuzzy-naturality of $\{\Phi_b\}$.** That the per-fiber forward maps give a fuzzy-natural transformation of presheaves on $\mathbf{B}$ is the core structural claim. The argument is: (i) $\Phi_b$ is constructed from per-fiber adjunctions plus path-chained fuzzy adjoints in the fiber, (ii) the base-change Q-functors are themselves stalk-level Q-functors, (iii) fuzzy adjunctions of stalk-level Q-functors compose multiplicatively. The full check requires bookkeeping but no new structural ideas beyond the CW-complex theorem.

2. **The colimit-of-fuzzy-natural-transformations construction.** When taking the limit of presheaves and colimit of copresheaves over $\mathbf{B}$, the resulting global Q-functor's fuzziness needs to be tracked. This is a routine $\mathbf{Q\text{-}Cat}_{\mathrm{cwl}}$ construction (Stubbe 2005, Lopez 2024 use related limit/colimit machinery), but the explicit fuzziness bound for the assembled map is part of what we owe.

**Beyond this theorem:**

3. **Non-Boolean value categories.** The Layer 1+2+3 chain has been verified in the corpus's typical Boolean d-frame setting; whether non-Boolean value categories (Lawvere-quantale-enriched, MV-algebra-enriched) yield non-degenerate transfinite fuzzinesses is open and depends on per-finite-sub-complex fuzziness behavior under the directed colimit (see the [transfinite-CW theorem](q-enriched-curry-transfinite-cw.md)'s non-degeneracy criterion).

## What this theorem gives

With this theorem in hand:

1. **The Q-enriched Curry equivalence on the bitopological settling site is established across all four regimes.** Strict and Scott-continuous via the [CW-complex theorem](q-enriched-curry-cw-complex.md); transfinite-bounded and aperiodic via the [transfinite-CW theorem](q-enriched-curry-transfinite-cw.md). All four regimes give $q^{\mathrm{glob}} = 1$ (strict equivalence) for the corpus's Boolean d-frame data.

2. **The [Verdier duality theorem](verdier-duality-bitopological-site.md) is established at draft level across all four regimes.** The conjecture's central structural claim — that sheaves and cosheaves on the bitopological settling site are Q-fuzzy-equivalent — is now a theorem with explicit fuzziness profile, in q-fuzzy form (specializing to strict for Boolean d-frame data).

3. **The [d-frame Verdier composition theorem](d-frame-verdier-composition.md) acquires concrete spatial-side ingredients across all regimes.** The two-dualities-composing structure (spatial × value-category-negation) can now be checked against specific fiber+base-change data in any regime.

4. **The aperiodic co-classification and strong-MLD classification refinement become accessible.** With Verdier in the aperiodic regime, the dual cosheaf-homology classifying invariant (future work, dual to the existing [aperiodic-regime classification](aperiodic-regime-classification.md)) and its composed-duality-equivariant refinement (future work on the [strong-MLD classification question](../questions/strong-mld-classification.md)) are now within reach.
