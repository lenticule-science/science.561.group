The **four-element bilattice**, denoted $\mathbf{FOUR}$, is the smallest non-trivial [bilattice](bilattice.md). Its carrier is

$$\{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \bot, \top\}$$

and its [truth order](../../../../../../truth-order/truth-order.md), [knowledge order](knowledge-order.md), and [negation](negation.md) make it a complete bilattice with all the structure voidmath uses.

This bilattice was introduced by Belnap (1977) as a four-valued logic for reasoning under incomplete and contradictory information, and recognized by Ginsberg (1988) and Fitting (1991) as the canonical small example of the bilattice structure later generalized.

## The two orders

The truth order $\leq_t$ is generated by:

$$\mathbf{f} \leq_t \bot \leq_t \mathbf{t} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{f} \leq_t \top \leq_t \mathbf{t}.$$

It runs from $\mathbf{f}$ (false) to $\mathbf{t}$ (true), with $\bot$ and $\top$ both at intermediate truth-axis positions — they are *neither* false nor true, on this axis.

The knowledge order $\leq_k$ is generated by:

$$\bot \leq_k \mathbf{f} \leq_k \top \quad \text{and} \quad \bot \leq_k \mathbf{t} \leq_k \top.$$

It runs from $\bot$ (no evidence) to $\top$ (full evidence in both directions), with $\mathbf{t}$ and $\mathbf{f}$ at intermediate knowledge-axis positions — they have full evidence in one direction but none in the other.

The two orders are independent. The four corners are the joint extremes: $\mathbf{t}$ (high truth, mid knowledge), $\mathbf{f}$ (low truth, mid knowledge), $\top$ (mid truth, high knowledge), $\bot$ (mid truth, low knowledge).

## As a twist product

$\mathbf{FOUR}$ is isomorphic to the [twist product](../../../../../../../../residuated-lattice/content/twist-product/twist-product.md) $\mathbf{2}^\bowtie$ of the two-element Boolean lattice $\mathbf{2} = \{0, 1\}$ with itself:

$$\mathbf{f} = \langle 0, 1 \rangle, \quad \bot = \langle 0, 0 \rangle, \quad \top = \langle 1, 1 \rangle, \quad \mathbf{t} = \langle 1, 0 \rangle.$$

The first component tracks positive [evidence](evidence.md) (1 = full positive, 0 = none); the second tracks negative evidence. Reading off the four canonical configurations:

| Value | $\langle a_+, a_- \rangle$ | Evidence reading |
|---|---|---|
| $\bot$ | $\langle 0, 0 \rangle$ | no evidence — *told nothing* |
| $\mathbf{t}$ | $\langle 1, 0 \rangle$ | only positive — *told true* |
| $\mathbf{f}$ | $\langle 0, 1 \rangle$ | only negative — *told false* |
| $\top$ | $\langle 1, 1 \rangle$ | both directions — *told both* |

Belnap's original epistemic interpretation of the four values: *told true, told false, told nothing, told both*. The bilattice structure makes this interpretation formally precise — the four corners are the four canonical evidence configurations, and the orders capture how evidence accumulates and tilts.

## Negation and the symmetries

[Negation](negation.md) on $\mathbf{FOUR}$ swaps positive and negative evidence:

$$\sim \mathbf{t} = \mathbf{f}, \quad \sim \mathbf{f} = \mathbf{t}, \quad \sim \bot = \bot, \quad \sim \top = \top.$$

It fixes the symmetric corners ($\bot$ and $\top$, which have equal evidence in both directions) and swaps the asymmetric ones ($\mathbf{t}$ and $\mathbf{f}$).

The full symmetry group of $\mathbf{FOUR}$ — generated by negation and [conflation](conflation.md) — is the Klein four-group $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$. Negation is the truth-axis flip; conflation is the knowledge-axis flip; their composition is the 180° rotation that fixes the diagonal and swaps the off-diagonal corners.

## Why FOUR is canonical

$\mathbf{FOUR}$ is the **free bounded distributive bilattice on one generator** (Fitting 2002) — the universal small example of the bilattice structure. Any four-cornered settlement structure of the form *true, false, both, neither* embeds into a bilattice that contains $\mathbf{FOUR}$ as a sub-bilattice, and the four canonical evidence configurations always appear in the embedded copy.

In voidmath specifically, $\mathbf{FOUR}$ is the smallest [bilattice](bilattice.md) on which a non-trivial [predication fiber algebra](predication-fiber-algebra.md) can be built. For larger or richer reasoning, larger bilattices (twist products of bigger residuated lattices) provide finer evidence levels — but the four-cornered structure is always preserved at the bilattice's extremes.
