# Conflation

An **optional** unary operator `−` on a bilattice satisfying:

- `a ≤_k b ⟹ −b ≤_k −a` — **antitone in the knowledge order**.
- `a ≤_t b ⟹ −a ≤_t −b` — **monotone in the truth order**.
- `−(−a) = a` — **involutive**.

Conflation is **optional** in the sense that a bilattice need not carry one; it is additional structure when present. When the substrate uses conflation (e.g., to model "knowledge-flip" operations alongside "polarity-flip"), the operator is part of the algebra.

## Comparison with negation

The two unary operators have **dual** axiomatic shapes:

| Axiom | `~` (negation) | `−` (conflation) |
|---|---|---|
| In `≤_k` | monotone | **antitone** |
| In `≤_t` | antitone | **monotone** |
| Involutive | yes | yes |

The dual roles are precisely interchanged. Negation reverses the truth-order, conflation reverses the knowledge-order. Negation preserves how-much-evidence (`≤_k`-monotone), conflation preserves which-side-is-more-true (`≤_t`-monotone).

## Concrete reading on FOUR

If negation swaps `t ↔ f` and fixes `⊥, ⊤` (the truth-axis flip), conflation swaps `⊥ ↔ ⊤` and fixes `t, f` (the knowledge-axis flip):

| `a` | `−a` |
|---|---|
| `⊥` | `⊤` |
| `t` | `t` |
| `f` | `f` |
| `⊤` | `⊥` |

On twist-product coordinates: `−⟨a, b⟩ = ⟨¬a, ¬b⟩` where `¬` is the underlying lattice's complementation (for the Boolean case `L = 2`, this is `0 ↔ 1`). So `−` flips BOTH coordinates simultaneously.

**Negation** flips coordinates **between each other** (swap). **Conflation** flips coordinates **inside themselves** (each negates). The two are genuinely different operations.

## Commutativity

When both `~` and `−` are present, they are typically required to **commute**:

$${\sim} \circ {-} \;=\; {-} \circ {\sim}.$$

Equivalently: `−~a = ~−a` for all `a`. The two flips can be applied in either order.

Commutativity is what makes the group generated by `{~, −}` Abelian — specifically, the Klein-four group.

## The Klein-four symmetry

The four operators `{id, ~, −, ~∘−}` form a group of order 4. Action table on FOUR:

| Operator | `⊥` | `t` | `f` | `⊤` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | `⊥` | `t` | `f` | `⊤` |
| `~` | `⊥` | `f` | `t` | `⊤` |
| `−` | `⊤` | `t` | `f` | `⊥` |
| `~∘−` | `⊤` | `f` | `t` | `⊥` |

Group structure:

- `~² = −² = (~∘−)² = id`. Every non-identity element has order 2.
- `~ ∘ − = − ∘ ~` (commutativity).
- The four elements are `{e, a, b, ab}` with `a² = b² = e` — exactly Klein-four `Z₂ × Z₂`.

The symmetry stratifies FOUR into orbits of the group's subgroups:

- **`⟨~⟩` orbits**: `{⊥}, {⊤}, {t, f}` (3 orbits — the truth-axis flip).
- **`⟨−⟩` orbits**: `{⊥, ⊤}, {t}, {f}` (3 orbits — the knowledge-axis flip).
- **`⟨~∘−⟩` orbits**: `{⊥, ⊤}, {t, f}` (2 orbits — the diagonal flip).
- **Full `Z₂ × Z₂` orbit**: `{⊥, t, f, ⊤}` is one orbit under the full group (transitive action on FOUR).

This is the formal sense in which the bilattice carries a `Z₂ × Z₂` gauge symmetry: the four values are equivalent under the group action, with subgroup-orbits revealing finer distinctions.

## Why a bilattice "might" carry conflation

The basic bilattice axioms only require negation. Conflation is added when:

- The substrate needs to distinguish **knowledge-direction** operations (`−`) from **polarity-direction** operations (`~`) — typical in paraconsistent logics that want both "not (knowledge-flip)" and "not (polarity-flip)" available.
- The full Klein-four symmetry is needed for invariance arguments — common in cohomological computations on the bilattice.
- The bilattice arises from `L^⋈` for `L` with a complementation — then conflation lifts naturally as componentwise complementation.

In settings where only one flip is needed (most classical-leaning bilattice work), negation suffices and conflation is omitted.
