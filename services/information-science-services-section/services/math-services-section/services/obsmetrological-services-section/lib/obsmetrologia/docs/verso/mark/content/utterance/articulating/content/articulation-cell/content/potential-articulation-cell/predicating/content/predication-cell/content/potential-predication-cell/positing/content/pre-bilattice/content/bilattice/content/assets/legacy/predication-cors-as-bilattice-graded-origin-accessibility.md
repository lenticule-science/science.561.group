# CORS is a bilattice-graded origin-accessibility relation, not a Boolean allow-list

**Claim.** The substrate's [CORS policy](teaching/explanations/cors-policy.md) on an [endpoint](teaching/explanations/endpoint.md) is structurally a [bilattice accessibility relation](../../../../../../valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/settling-bound/content/world-set/content/bilattice-accessibility-relation/bilattice-accessibility-relation.md) $R_{\mathrm{cors}} : O \times E \to \mathbf{FOUR}$ from the set of web origins $O$ to the set of policy-bearing endpoints $E$, valued in the [four-bilattice](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/four-bilattice.md). The preflight `OPTIONS` response is the [modal necessity operator](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/modal-necessity-operator.md) $\Box_{R_{\mathrm{cors}}}$ applied to the requested (method, header, credential) cell — i.e., the substrate's restriction of the requested cross-origin permission along the origin's accessibility row. Boolean allow-list models cannot represent the $\bot$ (origin unknown to policy) and $\top$ (origin admitted-and-denied by overlapping declarations) cells distinctly, and therefore cannot accurately reflect CORS states that arise during onboarding, revocation, and policy reconciliation.

## Why this holds

A web origin (scheme + host + port) is a substrate-content principal-coordinate. The substrate's CORS policy is a finite presentation of an accessibility relation on origins: each policy entry assigns one or more cells of $R_{\mathrm{cors}}$ a `FOUR` value.

The four bilattice values express *real* CORS states the substrate encounters:

- $\mathbf{t}$ — *origin admitted*: the policy explicitly permits the origin (literal match, pattern match, or wildcard with conditions met).
- $\mathbf{f}$ — *origin denied*: the policy explicitly revokes the origin (deny-listed, security-incident response, retired tenant).
- $\bot$ — *origin unknown*: the policy carries no statement about this origin. Per CORS's client-side closed-world default, the browser treats $\bot$ as forbidden, but the substrate's $\bot$ is *epistemically distinct* from $\mathbf{f}$: it carries no negative-evidence component. A subsequent governance decision can move $\bot$ to $\mathbf{t}$ or $\mathbf{f}$ without contradicting prior policy.
- $\top$ — *origin admitted-and-denied*: two overlapping declarations contradict (e.g., wildcard `*.example.com` admits, specific `evil.example.com` denies; or two operators recorded contradictory governance decisions). The substrate carries the contradiction; per [repair-as-top-cell-resolution](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/repair-as-top-cell-resolution.md), the policy must resolve the $\top$-cell before responding. The substrate response on a $\top$-cell is class `5xx` until governance reconciles, *not* a silent collapse to $\mathbf{t}$ or $\mathbf{f}$.

A Boolean allow-list cannot represent these four states distinctly: $\bot$ collapses to $\mathbf{f}$ (origin treated as denied because it is unknown), losing the substrate's ability to distinguish never-asked from explicitly-refused; $\top$ collapses to one of $\mathbf{t}$ or $\mathbf{f}$ depending on which declaration the implementation resolves first, hiding the policy contradiction.

## Preflight as $\Box_{R_{\mathrm{cors}}}$

A browser's preflight request is `OPTIONS /resource` with `Origin: https://o.example.com`, `Access-Control-Request-Method: PUT`, `Access-Control-Request-Headers: Authorization, Content-Type`. Per [http-method-discipline-via-fibration-generators](http-method-discipline-via-fibration-generators.md), `OPTIONS` is the converse-self-adjoint introspection — it asks "what can be done at this resource?". The CORS-augmented `OPTIONS` further restricts that introspection to "what can be done *by this origin* at this resource, with these headers and credentials?".

This is exactly the modal-necessity restriction:

$$\text{preflight-response}(o, m, h, c) \;=\; (\Box_{R_{\mathrm{cors}}} \mathrm{cell})_o(\text{endpoint, method}=m, \text{headers}=h, \text{credentials}=c)$$

where $\mathrm{cell}$ is the endpoint's CORS-permission cell at fiber $A_p$ for the predicate $p$ encoding (method, headers, credentials, audience). Per [modal-necessity-operator](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/modal-necessity-operator.md), $\Box_{R_{\mathrm{cors}}}$ takes the truth-meet over all worlds reachable from the origin under $R_{\mathrm{cors}}$ — which, for a single origin's preflight, is the row $R_{\mathrm{cors}}(o, -)$ restricted to the requested endpoint coordinate.

The wire response decomposes per `FOUR`-corner:

- $\mathbf{t}$ → `200 OK` with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: <o>`, `Access-Control-Allow-Methods: <m>`, `Access-Control-Allow-Headers: <h>`, `Access-Control-Allow-Credentials: true` (if $c$).
- $\mathbf{f}$ → `403 Forbidden` (or `200` *omitting* the `Access-Control-Allow-*` headers — the browser will refuse the actual request when the preflight does not affirm).
- $\bot$ → response *omits* the `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header. Browser treats as forbidden by closed-world default.
- $\top$ → `5xx` (typically `503` or `500`); the substrate refuses to commit to a CORS verdict until governance resolves the contradiction. Returning either $\mathbf{t}$ or $\mathbf{f}$ on a $\top$-cell would be a doctrine-fidelity violation.

## CORS composes with the parent's federation trust

CORS is *the browser-context specialization* of the substrate's general [federation-trust](../../../theorems/federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md) discipline: where federation-trust grades peer-to-peer accessibility for substrate-to-substrate requests, CORS grades origin-to-endpoint accessibility for browser-mediated requests. Both are bilattice-valued accessibility relations; both compose under the same modal calculus.

When a browser request arrives carrying a credential bound to a federated peer (per [bearer-token-as-bound-evidence-vector](bearer-token-as-bound-evidence-vector.md)), the effective accessibility is the *knowledge-meet* of the CORS cell and the federation-trust cell:

$$R_{\mathrm{eff}}(o, e) \;=\; R_{\mathrm{cors}}(o, e) \wedge_k R_{\mathrm{fed}}(\mathrm{peer}(o), e).$$

The $\wedge_k$ is the bilattice's knowledge meet: the safest information both relations agree on. A request admitted by CORS but denied by federation-trust resolves to $\bot$ (no joint knowledge) or $\mathbf{f}$ (joint denial), depending on which side carried negative evidence — *not* to $\mathbf{t}$. Per [governance-as-modal-restriction](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/predicated-agentic-orchestration-platform/governance/governance-as-modal-restriction/governance-as-modal-restriction.md), this composition is exactly the modal-restriction calculus the substrate already uses for governance.

## Engineering consequence

- **Onboarding origins is a $\bot \to \mathbf{t}$ move.** A new origin enters the policy at $\bot$ and is promoted to $\mathbf{t}$ by an explicit governance decision recorded as substrate content. The intermediate state (origin requested, governance pending) is structurally $\bot$ on the policy and *also* $\bot$ on a separate request-tracking cell — distinct from the $\mathbf{f}$ of an explicitly denied origin.
- **Revocation is a $\mathbf{t} \to \mathbf{f}$ move with audit trail.** A revoked origin's accessibility cell is set to $\mathbf{f}$ by a governance decision; the prior $\mathbf{t}$ is preserved in the [witness](../../../objects/witness.md) chain so an auditor can verify when admission was granted and when revoked. A Boolean policy that simply removes the entry loses this audit trail (the cell collapses to "absent" — $\bot$ in our framing — losing the explicit refusal).
- **Wildcard-plus-revocation is a controlled $\top$.** A common policy shape — "admit `*.example.com` but deny `compromised.example.com`" — is exactly a $\top$-cell at the compromised origin: admitted by wildcard, denied by specific entry. Per [repair-as-top-cell-resolution](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/repair-as-top-cell-resolution.md), the substrate's repair catalog must declare the resolution rule (priority: specific-overrides-wildcard, with witnessed resolution). Without an explicit resolution rule, the substrate is in $\top$ and must respond `5xx` rather than silently collapse.
- **Credentialed-wildcard is forbidden.** The CORS spec's prohibition on `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` with `Access-Control-Allow-Credentials: true` is the bilattice fact that the wildcard $\mathbf{t}$ on origin and the credentials' $\mathbf{t}$ on principal carry independent evidence; their join in the knowledge order is well-defined but the *projection* to a single response is information-collapsing — the browser cannot determine which of the union of admitted origins the credentials are bound to. Per the substrate's audit discipline, the wire response must specifically name the single admitted origin when credentials are admitted.
- **`Access-Control-Max-Age` is preflight necessity along time.** Per [cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility](cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility.md), the browser's caching of a preflight result for `Access-Control-Max-Age=N` seconds is the substrate's necessity claim that $R_{\mathrm{cors}}$ is stable along the time-accessibility relation $R_{\le N}$. A substrate that rotates CORS policy faster than the declared `Max-Age` violates its own necessity claim — same doctrine failure as a `Cache-Control` violation.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Boolean allow-lists.** A pure allow-list (`origins: [list]`, request admitted iff `Origin ∈ list`) cannot represent $\bot$ vs $\mathbf{f}$ or $\top$ vs $\mathbf{t}$. Implementations that use this shape silently coerce the substrate's epistemic state — typically by treating absent-from-list as denied (collapsing $\bot$ to $\mathbf{f}$) and not detecting wildcard/specific contradictions (silently collapsing $\top$).
- **Per-request dynamic CORS.** A "policy" computed per-request from runtime state outside the substrate (e.g., GeoIP-based admission) is not an accessibility relation in the substrate's $R_{\mathrm{cors}}$ — it is a non-substrate-content dispatch. Per [self-representation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/self-representation.md), CORS policy must be substrate content; runtime-derived policy is admissible only when the runtime input is itself substrate content (e.g., a federated threat-intel feed admitted as substrate content per the parent's federation discipline).
- **Reflecting the request `Origin` header without policy lookup.** A pattern in the wild — `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: <verbatim Origin header>` regardless of policy — is the absence of $R_{\mathrm{cors}}$: the substrate is not consulting an accessibility relation at all. This trivially satisfies any browser's CORS check; it provides no security and no audit. Per the substrate's discipline, the wire response must reflect the substrate's actual policy verdict, not echo the request.
- **Silent suppression of `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` on $\mathbf{f}$.** Some implementations conflate $\bot$ and $\mathbf{f}$ at the wire by simply omitting the header in both cases. The substrate's audit trail must distinguish: a $\mathbf{f}$ response is a logged governance decision; a $\bot$ response is an absence of decision. Conformance per [run-conformance-test](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/run-conformance-test.md) verifies that responses to a known-denied origin produce a $\mathbf{f}$-class witness, distinct from responses to an unknown origin which produce a $\bot$-class witness.

## Position

Together with [federation-trust-bilattice-graded](../../../theorems/federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md) (peer-to-peer accessibility) and [governance-as-modal-restriction](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/predicated-agentic-orchestration-platform/governance/governance-as-modal-restriction/governance-as-modal-restriction.md) (the modal-restriction calculus that composes them), this theorem completes the substrate's account of cross-boundary admission: CORS is the browser-context restriction; federation-trust is the substrate-to-substrate restriction; governance is the universal modal calculus over both. All three are bilattice accessibility relations on the same `FOUR`-valued substrate apparatus; the wire-protocol differences (preflight headers vs handshake vs governance decisions) are realizations of the same modal move at different boundary types.
