# Knowing as settling

The structural reading of *knowing* on the predicated substrate: to know is for a community's substrate-region to have **settled** under the community's closure-rule system to a state that is stable in the [knowledge order](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md). A community knows what its substrate has settled to; its uncertainty is the substrate's not-yet-settled cells; its disagreement is the substrate's $\top$-valued cells; its silence is the substrate's $\bot$-valued cells.

This is the substrate-side reading of the process-philosophical commitment named in [process-ontology-and-situated-learning](../texts/process-ontology-and-situated-learning.md): knowing is not the possession of finished facts but the [predication-settling](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) dynamic itself. The substrate's settled cells are the community's actually-held knowledge; the settlement *is* the knowing.

Three operational consequences follow:

- **A community's knowledge is bounded by its closure rules.** A predication that is not derivable from the asserted cells under the admitted closure-rule system cannot be known by the community using the substrate. A new closure rule expands the community's potential knowledge; restricting closure rules contracts it. The community's epistemic culture (per Knorr-Cetina) *is* its admitted closure-rule subset.
- **A community's knowledge is provisional.** Because settlement may be revisited as new evidence arrives or rules are revised, every $t$-valued cell carries the implicit qualifier "settled-given-current-rules-and-evidence." A change in either inputs or rules can re-settle the cell to a different value. The substrate's [substrate-state](../../../../../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/concepts/substrate-state.md) carries the history of settlements.
- **A community's knowledge is regime-classified.** The substrate's [four-regime classification](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/four-regime-dichotomy.md) determines whether the community's knowing is *bounded* (strict regime — settles in finite stages), *Scott-continuous* (settles in $\omega$ stages, each derivable from finite earlier stages), *transfinite-bounded* (settles within a fixed transfinite ordinal), or *aperiodic* (settles only at countably many irregular firing-ordinals). A community's regime is a substrate-level fact about its mode of knowing.

The gerund "knowing" is used deliberately; the predicated framing names the gerund's mathematical referent: knowing is the substrate's settling under the community's closure rules.
