# Internal Z/2 gauge

The internal gauge of a bilattice carrying negation `~` is the `Z/2`-action

$$\mathbb{Z}/2 \;\curvearrowright\; \mathbf{B}, \qquad 0 \mapsto \mathrm{id}, \;\; 1 \mapsto {\sim}.$$

The non-trivial element of `Z/2` acts as negation; the identity element acts as the identity. Negation's involutivity (`~~ = id`) is precisely what makes this a group action — `Z/2 × Z/2 → Z/2` with `1 + 1 = 0` corresponds to `~ ∘ ~ = id`.

## Action data on FOUR

For `B = FOUR`:

| `a` | `id(a)` | `~(a)` |
|---|---|---|
| `⊥` | `⊥` | `⊥` |
| `t` | `t` | `f` |
| `f` | `f` | `t` |
| `⊤` | `⊤` | `⊤` |

## Three structural properties of the action

**(1) Preserves knowledge order `≤_k`.** Negation is monotone in `≤_k` (bilattice axiom). So `Z/2` acts by `≤_k`-isomorphisms — both order directions preserved.

**(2) Reverses truth order `≤_t`.** Negation is antitone in `≤_t` (bilattice axiom). So `Z/2` acts by `≤_t`-anti-isomorphisms — order reversed under the non-trivial element.

**(3) Fixed and free orbits.**

- **Fixed points** (orbit size 1): the truth-neutral values. On `FOUR`: `⊥` and `⊤`. These satisfy `~a = a`.
- **Free 2-orbits** (orbit size 2): the truth-asymmetric values. On `FOUR`: `{t, f}`. The action exchanges them.

The fixed/free orbit decomposition is what makes the action "internal" — it organizes the bilattice into a `Z/2`-orbit structure intrinsic to negation.

## Dual realization on paired Priestley spaces

The paired Priestley dual of `FOUR` is the two-point space `{+, -}` with swap involution `σ: + ↔ -`. The `Z/2`-action on FOUR corresponds via Jung–Rivieccio duality to the swap involution `σ` on the dual side:

| Algebraic side (FOUR) | Dual side (paired Priestley {+, -}) |
|---|---|
| `id` | `id` |
| `~` | `σ` |

So the same `Z/2`-action appears on both sides of the duality, geometrically as "swap the two Priestley points." This makes "internal Z/2 gauge" a structurally meaningful name: it is the symmetry the bilattice carries via negation, dually realized on the topological side as the paired-Priestley swap.

## Why this matters for predication

Predicating's evidence-injection `v ↦ v ∨_k δ_{(φ, q)}` is `Z/2`-equivariant: injecting evidence and then negating gives the same result as negating and then injecting the negated evidence,

$$\sim(v \vee_k q) = \sim v \vee_k \sim q.$$

This equivariance is the structural reason positive and negative evidence are treated symmetrically by predicating. The substrate doesn't privilege one polarity; the `Z/2` symmetry says positive and negative are interchangeable under negation.

## Role in Klein-four

When the bilattice ALSO carries conflation `−`, the `Z/2` gauge extends to a Klein-four symmetry (sibling $thing `klein-four-symmetry`) by adjoining the `−`-generated `Z/2`. The negation-`Z/2` is one of the two `Z/2`-factors in the Klein-four `Z/2 × Z/2`.
