# Settling-associated sheaf

The **central object** of the substrate's cohomological development. An `Ab`-valued KZ-coherent sheaf

$$\mathcal{F}_U \;:\; \mathbf{T}^*_{\mathrm{site}} \to \mathbf{Ab}$$

on the bitopological-settling-site whose values count **newly-added designated cells** at each settling stage.

The sheaf is the operational link between the orbit data of a settlement (cells designated over time) and its cohomological data (`H^n(T*, F_U)` invariants).

## Construction

Fix a settlement `(E, c)` over `(L, C, E, λ)` with increment-sequence `U = (u_τ)_{τ<λ}`.

For each `(q, τ) ∈ C^op × λ^op`, the **set of newly-added designated cells** at stage `τ` for predicate `q` is:

$$\Delta^+_\tau(q) \;:=\; \{(s, o) \in E \times E \,:\, v_\tau(q)(s, o) = \mathbf{t} \,\text{ and }\, v_{\tau'}(q)(s, o) \neq \mathbf{t} \,\text{ for all }\, \tau' < \tau\}.$$

`Δ^+_τ(q)` collects cells that **first become designated** at stage `τ` — cells whose value transitions to `t` at `τ` and was not yet `t` at any earlier stage.

The sheaf `F_U` then assigns to each site object `(q, τ, U)`:

$$\mathcal{F}_U(q, \tau, U) \;:=\; \mathbb{Z}\langle\, \Delta^+_\tau(q) \cap U \,\rangle,$$

the free Abelian group generated by newly-designated cells at stage `τ` that lie in the pairwise-Stone clopen `U`.

Restriction to sub-clopens and sub-sites acts by restricting the indexing set.

## Why "newly-added designated"

The cohomological framework needs a sheaf whose values **track novelty** — what's new at each stage. Using the total designated set `{(s, o) : v_τ(q)(s, o) = t}` at each stage would double-count cells across stages (a cell designated at stage 1 would appear in the sheaf at stages 1, 2, 3, ... — non-sheaf-like).

Tracking only the **newly-added** designated cells gives a sheaf-coherent structure: each cell contributes to `F_U(q, τ, U)` at exactly one stage `τ` (the first stage at which it's designated). The total designated set at stage `τ` is recoverable by summing across `τ' ≤ τ`.

## KZ-coherent

`F_U` is a **KZ-coherent sheaf** — a sheaf that respects the Kock–Zöberlein structure of the closure-2-monad. Operationally: the sheaf's values are compatible with closure operations on the algebraic side (via Jung–Rivieccio duality).

KZ-coherence is what makes `F_U` produce **meaningful cohomology** — without it, the sheaf would have arbitrary values not connected to the settling's inferential structure.

## Cohomology

The substrate's central cohomological invariants are computed from `F_U`:

$$H^n(\mathbf{T}^*_{\leq \kappa}, \mathcal{F}_U) \quad \text{for } n \geq 0.$$

The `H^0` is the **rank**: total newly-designated cells (across all stages and predicates), counted in `Z`-modules.

The `H^1` is the **classifying class**: encodes the inferential structure that produced the orbit, in cohomological form.

Higher `H^n` for `n ≥ 2` typically vanish (by the cohomological-framework's higher-degree-vanishing claim, status sketch).

## What `F_U` captures vs misses

`F_U` captures:

- **Which cells get designated** (newly-`t` cells across `(s, o, q, τ)`).
- **When they get designated** (the `τ` index).
- **At which predicate** they get designated (the `q` index).
- **Where in the Priestley space** they sit (the `U` clopen index).

`F_U` does NOT directly capture:

- The exact closure-rule responsible for each designation (only the result is recorded, not the inferential cause).
- The values at cells other than newly-designated ones (only `t`-cells contribute; other values don't directly appear in the sheaf).

The omitted data is what cohomological-equivalence's "sheaf-iso" relaxation accepts: two settlements with the same `F_U` can have different inferential paths but are cohomologically equivalent.
