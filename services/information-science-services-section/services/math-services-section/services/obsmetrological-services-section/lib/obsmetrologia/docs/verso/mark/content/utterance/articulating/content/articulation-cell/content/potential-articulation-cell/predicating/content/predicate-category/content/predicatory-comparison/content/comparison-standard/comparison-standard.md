# Comparison standard

A **comparison standard** is the relation or predicate that determines what a comparison is checking.

The standard determines what counts as sameness, divergence, containment, truth-strengthening, joinability, or residual difference.
Without a standard, "compare these two settlings" is under-specified: equality, $\leq_k$ containment, $\leq_t$ strengthening, confluence, and equalization are different mathematical questions with different answers.

## Standard families

Predication categories currently need at least these families:

- **Exact standards:** equality of valuations, equality of composites, equalization loci.
- **Order standards:** $v \leq_k w$ for evidence containment; $v \leq_t w$ for truth-strengthening.
- **Residual standards:** what evidence must be added to move from one input to another when the direction is defined.
- **Confluence standards:** whether two branches admit a common successor by allowed operations.
- **Temporal standards:** whether comparison is at one stage, over an interval, or after closure.

The standard is part of the mathematical data of a comparison context.
Two comparison contexts with the same inputs but different standards define different comparison morphisms.
