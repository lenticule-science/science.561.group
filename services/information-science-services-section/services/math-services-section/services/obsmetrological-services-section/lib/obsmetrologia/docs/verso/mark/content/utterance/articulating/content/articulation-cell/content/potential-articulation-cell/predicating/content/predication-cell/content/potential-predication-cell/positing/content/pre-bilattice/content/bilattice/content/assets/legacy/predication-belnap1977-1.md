## Content summary

Motivates a four-valued logic $\mathbf{4} = \{\bot, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$ — *no information*, *told true*, *told false*, *told both* — for a computer reasoning over an open-ended supply of asserted information from possibly inconsistent sources. The four values form a bilattice under two orders: the *truth order* $\bot \leq \mathbf{f}, \mathbf{t} \leq \top$ in the wrong sense (Belnap actually uses two interleaved orderings — knowledge and truth) and a *knowledge order* in which $\bot$ is least (no opinion) and $\top$ is greatest (full information from both sides). Belnap argues that a real-world database receiving assertions from many independent sources must reason in $\mathbf{4}$, not $\mathbf{2}$: collapsing "told both" to either $\mathbf{t}$ or $\mathbf{f}$ throws away information the system has been given, and collapsing it to "false" via *ex falso quodlibet* trivializes the database on the first contradiction. The four-valued semantics gives a paraconsistent answer: contradictions are *recorded*, not exploded.
