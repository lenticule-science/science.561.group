# CHL recursion stabilization check at the meta-level

The [CHL apex recursion narrative](../narratives/chl-apex-recursion.md) opens the question of whether the recursion stabilizes at a meta-level beyond realizability — equivalently, whether the [meta-moduli stack](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/meta-moduli-stack-theorem.md) is a *terminal classifier* whose deformation theory agrees with the settling moduli's, or whether the meta-level produces strictly new tangent directions.

This theorem develops the **stabilization check**: a precise comparison of the meta-deformation theory and the settling-deformation theory via the [meta-realizability map](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/meta-realizability-map.md).
The check has three structural components — comparing tangent spaces, comparing obstruction spaces, and comparing the realizability map's behavior on deformations.

**What is proved here.** The stabilization check is *vacuously satisfied* in the corpus's typical Boolean d-frame setting (Boolean stalks, $\Pi_1$ rules) — both meta-tangent and projected settling-tangent are 0-dimensional, the realizability-map derivative is the trivial $0 \to 0$, and both Ext$^2$ groups vanish trivially.
The CHL recursion therefore stabilizes at the meta-level in the corpus's typical Boolean setting.

**What remains open.** The stabilization check has substantive content only in non-Boolean enrichments (Lawvere-quantale, MV-algebra, continuous-valued logic) where continuous tangent directions exist.
Verifying or refuting stabilization in those settings is open work and not addressed by this theorem.

## Statement

**Theorem (stabilization check, sketch).** Let $[\mathcal{S}] \in \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}(B)$ be a meta-point with non-empty realizability fiber, and let $[v] \in (\pi^{\mathrm{real}})^{-1}([\mathcal{S}]) \subseteq \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}(B)$ be a settling generated by $\mathcal{S}$.

**The CHL recursion stabilizes at the meta-level** (in the technical sense: the meta-moduli is the terminal classifier in the apex tower) iff the meta-tangent space agrees with the projected settling-tangent space:

$$T_{[\mathcal{S}]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}} \;\cong\; \mathrm{coker}\bigl(T_{[v]} (\pi^{\mathrm{real}})^{-1}([\mathcal{S}]) \to T_{[v]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}\bigr).$$

In words: meta-tangent directions are exactly the *non-fiber* directions of the settling-tangent space — the deformation directions that change the closure-rule structure, modulo the deformation directions that change only the settling within a fixed rule structure.

## Three sub-questions

The stabilization check decomposes into three concrete sub-questions:

### Sub-question 1: do the meta-tangent and settling-tangent agree on cardinality?

The simplest stabilization check: do the dimensions match?
Specifically:

$$\dim T_{[\mathcal{S}]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}} \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; \dim \mathrm{coker}\bigl(T_{[v]}(\pi^{\mathrm{real}})^{-1}([\mathcal{S}]) \to T_{[v]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}\bigr).$$

If yes (dimensions agree), the recursion stabilizes at the dimension level — meta-deformations are exactly the "non-fiber" settling-deformations, with no extra structural directions.
If no (meta-dimension is strictly larger), the meta-moduli has tangent directions not coming from settling-deformations, and the recursion has substantive content beyond the settling level.

For the [trivial-base meta-classification](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/trivial-base-meta-classification.md): both sides are 0-dimensional (no continuous deformations).
The check is satisfied vacuously.

For richer bases, this becomes substantive.

### Sub-question 2: does the realizability map respect deformations?

The [meta-realizability map](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/meta-realizability-map.md) $\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ is a stack morphism.
Its derivative $d\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ is a linear map between tangent spaces:

$$d\pi^{\mathrm{real}} \,:\, T_{[v]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}} \to T_{[\mathcal{S}]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}.$$

For stabilization, this derivative should be **surjective** with kernel exactly the fiber tangent.
Equivalently: every meta-deformation of $\mathcal{S}$ comes from a settling-deformation of some $[v]$ in the fiber.

If $d\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ is surjective: meta-deformations are settling-deformations modulo fiber, hence the meta-moduli is a quotient of the settling moduli at the tangent level — a sign of stabilization.

If $d\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ is not surjective: meta-deformations exist that don't come from settling-deformations — meta-only directions.
The recursion is non-trivial at the meta level.

### Sub-question 3: do meta-obstructions vanish?

The [meta-deformation theory's unobstructedness conjecture](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/meta-moduli-deformation-theory.md) states $\mathrm{Ext}^2_{\mathrm{meta}} = 0$.
If the conjecture holds, meta-deformations extend to formal deformations without obstruction, and the meta-moduli is formally smooth.

For stabilization, the meta-unobstructedness should hold *automatically* from the settling-level vanishing-above-degree-1 — i.e., the absence of settling-level obstructions should propagate to the meta level via the realizability map.
Whether this propagation is a feature of stabilization or a separate structural fact is open.

## Proof (vacuous stabilization in Boolean settings)

**Theorem (Boolean vacuous stabilization).** *In the Boolean d-frame setting with $\Pi_1$ closure rules, all three sub-questions resolve trivially: the meta-tangent space is $0$-dimensional, the projected settling-tangent space is $0$-dimensional, the realizability-map derivative is the trivial $0 \to 0$ map (surjective with zero kernel), and both relevant $\mathrm{Ext}^2$ groups vanish trivially.
The CHL recursion stabilizes at the meta level in this setting.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (no continuous tangent directions).* In the Boolean d-frame setting, each stalk is a Boolean algebra — finite, discrete, with no continuous deformations as a structure (Boolean algebras are *rigid* objects in $\mathbf{dFrm}$: the only first-order deformations are trivial).
Hence the d-frame-stalk-perturbation summand of any tangent space is $0$.

*Step 2 (rules in $\Pi_1$ are combinatorial).* A $\Pi_1$ closure rule has antecedent $\forall \alpha \,. \phi(\alpha, \beta)$ for some atomic-Boolean-combination predicate $\phi$.
The space of such rules over a Boolean stalk structure is at most countable (each rule is encoded by a finite-Boolean-combination formula).
The "rule-perturbation" summand of any tangent space is thus discrete — no continuous deformations.

*Step 3 (tangent spaces are 0).* Combining Steps 1-2: $T_{[v]}\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}} = 0$ and $T_{[\mathcal{S}]}\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}} = 0$ in the Boolean / $\Pi_1$ setting.

*Step 4 (sub-questions resolve trivially).*

- **Sub-question 1:** $\dim T_{[\mathcal{S}]}\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}} = 0 = \dim \mathrm{coker}(0 \to 0)$.
  Equality holds.
- **Sub-question 2:** $d\pi^{\mathrm{real}} : 0 \to 0$ is trivially surjective with kernel $0$ = the trivial fiber tangent.
- **Sub-question 3:** $\mathrm{Ext}^2_{\mathrm{meta}}(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{S}) = 0$ trivially: discrete moduli have vanishing Ext$^{\geq 1}$ at every $[\mathcal{S}]$ in the deformation-theoretic sense (no first-order deformations to extend or obstruct).

All three sub-questions are satisfied vacuously.
Stabilization holds. $\square$

This is a *vacuous* stabilization (no deformations to check), so it doesn't yield strong structural information.
It establishes only that the recursion *doesn't actively diverge* in the corpus's typical setting.

## Where stabilization could genuinely fail

In richer enrichments (Lawvere-quantale, MV-algebra, continuous-valued logic), continuous deformations exist and the stabilization check has substantive content.
Specifically:

- **Lawvere-quantale-enriched data.** The deformation tangent space at a settling has continuous directions (perturbations of the quantale-valued cell content).
  Whether these lift to meta-deformations is non-trivial.

- **Non-Boolean d-frames.** A d-frame with non-discrete consistency / totality relations admits continuous tangent directions.
  The meta-deformation might extract genuine meta-only directions corresponding to "perturbing the rule's consistency-failure pattern."

- **Aperiodic-regime continuous deformations.** Aperiodic settlings have $\omega_1$-many tangent directions; whether the meta-realizability map's derivative is surjective on these is open.

These are settings where the recursion *could* fail to stabilize, with meta-only structural content emerging.
The corpus's current setting (Boolean) doesn't probe these, so the stabilization question's substantive answer is open *outside* the corpus's typical setting.

## What the stabilization check gives

If the recursion stabilizes (Boolean setting):

- **The meta-moduli is the terminal classifier.** The corpus's apex tower has its natural ceiling.
  No further levels extend the recursion non-trivially.

- **The corpus's structural backbone is finite.** 8 levels (predication structure → bilattice prelims → hyperdoctrine → settling → Priestley duality → cohomology → moduli → realizability → meta-moduli), and stabilization at the top.

If the recursion doesn't stabilize (non-Boolean setting):

- **The corpus extends to a non-trivial meta-meta-level.** A "moduli of meta-moduli" classifying meta-deformations as a separate classifier above $\mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$.

- **The CHL apex tower is genuinely unbounded.** Each level produces new content; the recursion is the corpus's structural frontier.

The Boolean case is the *simplest* and most likely to stabilize; richer enrichments are where the question becomes deep.

## What's open

**Internal to this theorem:**

1. **Verifying the stabilization conditions** at concrete cases.
  The trivial-base (0-dimensional vacuously) is the bare case.
  Richer bases need explicit calculation of $T_{[v]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{SKH}}$ and $T_{[\mathcal{S}]} \mathscr{M}^{\mathrm{meta}}$.

2. **The realizability map's derivative.** Computing $d\pi^{\mathrm{real}}$ explicitly on representative cases — which settling-tangent directions go to which meta-tangent directions.

3. **The meta-obstruction structure.** Whether $\mathrm{Ext}^2_{\mathrm{meta}}$ vanishes — the meta-unobstructedness conjecture.

**Beyond this theorem:**

4. **Stabilization in non-Boolean settings.** Lawvere-quantale-enriched, MV-algebra-enriched, continuous-valued.
  The substantive open problem.

5. **What lies above meta-moduli if stabilization fails.** A meta-meta-classifier construction, with the recursion's higher levels.

The Boolean-setting heuristic suggests stabilization holds vacuously; non-Boolean settings are where genuine non-stabilization could surface.

The theorem's status (sketch) reflects this: the structural framework is in place, but the substantive verification — computing tangent spaces and checking the comparison maps — requires concrete cases beyond the trivial-base example.
