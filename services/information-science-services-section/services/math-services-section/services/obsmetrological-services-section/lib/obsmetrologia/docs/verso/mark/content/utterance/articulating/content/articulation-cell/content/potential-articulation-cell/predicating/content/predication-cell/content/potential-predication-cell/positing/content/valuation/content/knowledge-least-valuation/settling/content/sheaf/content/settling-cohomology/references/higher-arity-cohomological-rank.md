# Higher-arity cohomological rank

For [$n$-ary predication](../objects/n-ary-predication.md) settlings, the cohomological rank scales combinatorially with arity in two distinct regimes: **arity-collapse** for projection-only closures (rank stays at the binary value), and **arity-amplification** for genuine $n$-ary joins (rank grows up to $k^n$ for $k$-letter alphabets).

## Statement

**Theorem (higher-arity rank, two cases).**

Let $v^{(n)}$ be an $n$-ary substitution-flavored settling on a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ of size $k$, with closure operator $T^{(n)}$.
Define:

- *Projection-only closure*: $T^{(n)}$ is constructed entirely from projections $\pi_J : \mathbf{L}^{\Sigma^n} \to \mathbf{L}^{\Sigma^J}$ for $J \subset \{1, \ldots, n\}$, extensions $\iota_J$, and pointwise bilattice operations.
  No genuine multi-coordinate joins.

- *Genuine $n$-ary closure*: $T^{(n)}$ uses join-patterns combining content from multiple $n$-tuples non-projectively (e.g., transitive composition on the third coordinate combining triples differing in slot positions).

Then in the aperiodic regime ($\kappa = \omega_1$):

**Case A (projection-only).** *$H^1(\mathcal{F}_{v^{(n)}}) \cong H^1(\mathcal{F}_{v^{(2)}})$ where $v^{(2)}$ is the binary projection.
The rank does not scale with $n$.*

**Case B (genuine $n$-ary).** *$\mathrm{rank}\, H^1(\mathcal{F}_{v^{(n)}}) \leq k^n$ post-quotient under the settling-shift relation, with equality for "primitive non-singular" $n$-ary substitutions where the $k^n \times k^n$ image-count matrix has Perron-rank $k^n$.*

## Proof

### Case A: arity-collapse for projection-only closures

If $T^{(n)} = T_{\mathrm{proj}}$ uses only projections, extensions, and pointwise operations, then the closure factors through the binary structure:

$$T^{(n)} = \iota_n \circ T^{(2)} \circ \pi_2$$

(or an analogous factoring through the binary projection).
Iteration of $T^{(n)}$ is therefore equivalent to iteration of $T^{(2)}$ followed by trivial extension to higher arity — the additional coordinates carry no closure-relevant content.

Cohomology: per the [aperiodic-regime cohomology via direct limit theorem](../../../../aperiodic-settling/references/aperiodic-regime-cohomology-via-direct-limit.md), $H^1$ is generated by NSC firings.
For projection-only $T^{(n)}$, every NSC firing in the $n$-ary settling corresponds to an NSC firing in the binary projection (by projection-extension factoring).
The firing-ordinal sets are in bijection.
So $H^1(\mathcal{F}_{v^{(n)}}) \cong H^1(\mathcal{F}_{v^{(2)}})$ as $\mathbb{Z}$-modules. $\square$ (Case A)

### Case B: arity-amplification for genuine $n$-ary closures

For a genuine $n$-ary closure using non-projective joins, NSC firings can occur at $n$-tuples that don't correspond to single binary firings.
Each $n$-tuple $(a_{i_1}, \ldots, a_{i_n}) \in \Sigma^n$ is a candidate cell; the NSC firings at the cell space $\Sigma^n$ are bounded above by $k^n$ (the cell-count).

**Upper bound.** Per the aperiodic cohomology theorem, $H^1$ generators are indexed by NSC firings; each NSC firing is at a specific $n$-cell; there are at most $k^n$ cells; so $\mathrm{rank}\, H^1 \leq k^n$. $\square$ (upper bound)

**Achievability.** For a *primitive* non-singular $n$-ary substitution where the image-count matrix $M^{(n)}$ on $\Sigma^n \times \Sigma^n$ is primitive (some power has all positive entries), the cohomology is generated by all $k^n$ cell-types post-quotient under the settling-shift action of $M^{(n)}$.
The post-quotient rank equals the rank of $M^{(n)}$ acting on $\mathbb{Z}^{k^n}$ — generically $k^n$ when the $M^{(n)}$ is unimodular and primitive.

Concretely: pick a substitution $\sigma : \Sigma^n \to (\Sigma^n)^*$ (each $n$-tuple maps to a sequence of $n$-tuples).
The image-count matrix $M^{(n)}_{(a, b)}$ is the count of $n$-tuple $b$ in $\sigma(a)$.
If $M^{(n)}$ is primitive non-singular and unimodular, the post-quotient cohomology has rank $k^n$. $\square$ (Case B achievability)

Combining: for genuine $n$-ary primitive non-singular settlings, $\mathrm{rank}\, H^1 = k^n$.
The arity-collapse / arity-amplification dichotomy follows. $\blacksquare$

## Concrete instances

### Case A example: ternary projection of Fibonacci

Take Fibonacci binary substitution $a \mapsto ab, b \mapsto a$, lift to ternary by extending: a ternary predicate $\tilde R(s, o, m)$ where $m$ is a "modality" carrier that doesn't participate in closure.
Closure on the first two coordinates is the Fibonacci settling; the third coordinate is constant.

$H^1(\tilde R) \cong H^1(\mathrm{Fib}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ — same rank as binary.

### Case B example: ternary causal triples with mechanism-composition

Take $\Sigma = \{a, b, c\}$ ($k = 3$) and a ternary substitution where each triple $(s, o, m)$ has its "mechanism" $m$ composed with the next firing's mechanism.
The substitution matrix is $3^3 \times 3^3 = 27 \times 27$.

For a generic primitive non-singular case, $\mathrm{rank}\, H^1 = 27$ post-quotient — much larger than the binary $\mathrm{rank}\, H^1 = 3$.

The empirical case is computed in [the 3-ary causal-triple benchmark](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/benchmark-causal-triples-3-ary.md).

## Connection to existing theorems

This generalizes the [step-$n$ cohomological rank theorem](../../../../aperiodic-settling/content/step-n-substitution-recurrence/content/step-n-substitution-rule/references/step-n-cohomological-rank-theorem.md) (which gave rank $n$ for step-$n$ substitutions on $n$-letter alphabets) in a different direction: instead of *step-$n$* (refining to longer sequences in time), this is *arity-$n$* (refining to higher-tuple cells in space).

The two refinements interact: a step-$m$ arity-$n$ substitution has cohomology bounded by $m \cdot k^n$ (or $k^{m+n-1}$ for joint refinements, depending on the join-shape).
A unified theorem covering both is open.

## Position in the program

Establishes the cohomological scaling law for higher-arity settlings, with the clean dichotomy:

- *Projection-only $\Rightarrow$ arity-collapse* (no rank scaling).
- *Genuine $n$-ary joins $\Rightarrow$ arity-amplification* (rank scales as $k^n$).

This refines the "what's new at higher arity?" question concretely: **higher-arity is cohomologically novel iff the closure rule uses genuine $n$-ary joins**.
Datalog-flavored projection-extension closures don't gain rank from arity.

Pairs with: - The [$n$-ary predication](../objects/n-ary-predication.md) object — defines the underlying setting. - The [3-ary causal-triple benchmark](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/benchmark-causal-triples-3-ary.md) — concrete computation showing arity-amplification. - The [step-$n$ cohomological rank theorem](../../../../aperiodic-settling/content/step-n-substitution-recurrence/content/step-n-substitution-rule/references/step-n-cohomological-rank-theorem.md) — the *step-shift* analog (arity-time tradeoff).

Open whether the graded structure has a clean form. - *Variable-arity settlings*: predicates whose arity varies across cells.
Cohomology theory open.
