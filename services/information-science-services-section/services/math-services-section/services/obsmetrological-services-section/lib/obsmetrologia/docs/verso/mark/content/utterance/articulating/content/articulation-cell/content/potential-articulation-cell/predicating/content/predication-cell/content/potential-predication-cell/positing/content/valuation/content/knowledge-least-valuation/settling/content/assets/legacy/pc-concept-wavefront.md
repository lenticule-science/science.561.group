# Wavefront

The propagation pattern produced when applying a closure rule at one entity creates predications that match the premises of closure rules at neighboring entities, which in turn produce new predications matching rules at *their* neighbors, and so on. In the substrate's hyperdoctrine setting, a wavefront is not a queue-and-loop runtime construct — it is *the substrate-side runtime presentation of [closure-driven settling-monotonicity](../../settling-monotonicity/references/closure-driven-settling-monotonicity.md) executed locally on the predication graph*. Per [wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing.md), the wavefront's locality property — work bounded by affected cells, not graph size — follows structurally from the [Mayer-Vietoris vanishing](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/crdt-convergence-via-mayer-vietoris.md) of the substrate's settling-associated sheaf at the wavefront's cover boundaries.

The substrate's closure-driven advance is a wavefront of closure-rule applications proceeding through the predication graph until no further matches occur (quiescence) or the [fuel-bound](fuel-bound.md) is exhausted.

## Hyperdoctrinal grounding

Per [closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md), the closure 2-monad $\mathrm{Cl}$ is lax-idempotent; per [closure-driven-settling-monotonicity](../../settling-monotonicity/references/closure-driven-settling-monotonicity.md), the iteration is $\leq_k$-monotone. The wavefront is the substrate-side execution model of these structural properties:

- **Each step of the wavefront** is one application of $\mathrm{cl}$ at one entity, producing $v_{i+1} = \mathrm{cl}(v_i \vee_k u_{i+1})$ at that entity.
- **The wavefront's reach** is the entities whose cells are affected by the iteration — the structural transitive closure of "depends on a recently-changed cell."
- **The wavefront's termination** is the closure-iteration reaching its fixed point.

Per [wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing.md), the locality property is *structural*: per [crdt-convergence-via-mayer-vietoris](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/crdt-convergence-via-mayer-vietoris.md), the Mayer-Vietoris connecting homomorphism $\delta^0$ on the substrate's settling-associated sheaf is identically zero — the wavefront's effects don't propagate as cohomology classes through the substrate. The substrate is structurally local in $H^1$.

## Three properties make the wavefront the right computational primitive

- **Locality.** Each application is bounded by the [closure rule](closure-rule.md)'s match radius, not the graph size. Memory consumed by the wavefront is the wavefront width — the number of entities currently to be examined — not the total entity count. A graph of millions of entities can be settled with a wavefront width of dozens. Per [wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing.md), this is the engineering surface of $\delta^0 = 0$ on the settling-associated sheaf — a structural property of the substrate, not a runtime optimization.
- **Monotonicity.** Each application either inserts a new predication or raises an existing one's value in the [knowledge order](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md) (per [settling monotonicity](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-monotonicity.md)). The substrate's value-domain is upper-bounded, so each predication can be raised only finitely many times before it cannot rise further. This is what makes the wavefront terminating on substrates with bounded value-domains.
- **Confluence.** When two closure rules apply at the same predication, the [knowledge-join](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md) of their conclusions is the same regardless of application order. The wavefront is a sound implementation of the underlying [closure operator](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-operator.md) — it computes the same fixed-point as a globally-coordinated iteration would. Per the closure 2-monad's lax-idempotence, the order-independence is structural to the monad.

## Why locality is structural

A naive view: locality is achieved by the substrate's runtime carefully tracking which entities have changed. But that view doesn't explain *why* this tracking works — i.e., why the substrate's effect propagation doesn't require recomputing the global closure on every change.

The hyperdoctrinal answer: per [crdt-convergence-via-mayer-vietoris](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/crdt-convergence-via-mayer-vietoris.md), the substrate's settling-associated sheaf has trivial Mayer-Vietoris obstruction — every section on a cover-overlap extends canonically by zero outside the overlap. Engineering translation: a closure rule's effect at one entity composes additively with effects elsewhere, with no cross-entity correction term. The wavefront can therefore process effects locally, never needing to "go back and fix" entities outside the changed region.

Per [wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/wavefront-locality-via-cohomology-vanishing.md), this is not an engineering choice but a structural consequence of the substrate's value-domain (bilattice with $\vee_k$-join) and the closure operator's monotonicity. A substrate whose value-domain didn't satisfy these would have non-trivial $H^1$ obstructions and would *not* admit a wavefront-style runtime — it would require global re-settlement on every change.

## Position

The wavefront is the engineering content of "computation is local; the cumulative effect is global." A predicated computer's effective throughput is determined by the wavefront width and the closure rules' per-application cost, not by the size of the substrate it acts on. Per [wavefront-termination](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/wavefront-termination.md), the substrate's wavefront cost is bounded by content (affected cells), not by graph size — the structural cohomological vanishing is what makes this bound tight.
