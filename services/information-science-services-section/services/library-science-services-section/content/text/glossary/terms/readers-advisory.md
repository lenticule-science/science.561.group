Readers' advisory is the library service in which a reader receives recommendations of material suited to their own reading interests. Joyce Saricks, whose *Readers' Advisory Service in the Public Library* (first with Nancy Brown; 3rd ed., ALA Editions, 2005) shaped the practice, defined it as "patron-oriented library service for adult leisure readers"; the practice has since widened to nonfiction and to young readers.

Readers' advisory works from what a reader enjoys (a book's appeal: its pace, characters, story line, tone, and setting) rather than from subject alone, and produces conversations, booklists, and displays. In [youth services](youth-services-librarianship.md) the advisee is often an adult choosing for a child, so the advisor serves two readers at once.
