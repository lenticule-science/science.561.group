A digital library is a set of electronic resources and associated technical capabilities for creating, searching, and using information, constructed, collected, and organized by and for a community of users, whose functional capabilities support the information needs and uses of that community (Borgman 1999). The DELOS Manifesto restates this as three distinct layers: a Digital Library is a possibly virtual organization that comprehensively collects, manages, and preserves for the long term rich digital content, and offers to its user communities specialized functionality on that content, of measurable quality and according to codified policies; a Digital Library System is the software system that provides all functionality required by one particular Digital Library; and a Digital Library Management System is the generic software infrastructure used to produce and administer a Digital Library System (Candela et al. 2007).

A digital library is distinguished from a plain file archive or a bare search index over scattered files by codified policy and community-facing capability: a digital library's holdings are bounded by a stated collection policy, and its functions are offered as a service to a named community rather than merely stored on disk. amusewiki is a Digital Library Management System in the DELOS sense; theanarchistlibrary.org is one Digital Library System instance it runs, with its own collection policy and community, distinct from amusewiki.org itself or any other site the same management system operates.

Because a digital library is defined by codified policy and community-facing capability rather than by file format or hosting technology, a body of downloaded files with no stated collection policy, no discovery interface, and no preservation commitment is not yet a digital library under either definition — it is raw content a digital library could be built from.
