Youth services librarianship is the branch of librarianship that serves children and teens: it selects and weeds their collections, advises young readers and the parents, caregivers, and teachers who choose for them ([readers' advisory](readers-advisory.md)), and designs programs such as storytimes, reading clubs, and teen workshops. Library schools offer it as a concentration of the master's degree.

In the American Library Association the field long had two divisions: the Association for Library Service to Children, whose lists cover birth through age 14, and the Young Adult Library Services Association, whose lists covered ages 12 to 18. YALSA dissolved on 2026-08-31 and was reunified into ALSC on 2026-09-01, which now serves young people 0 to 18.

A youth services librarian's written work is characteristically the booklist, the annotated bibliography, the community resource guide, and the program plan; its institutional work is the children's and teen collection and the [collection policy](collection-policy.md) that governs it.
