A collection development policy — often shortened to collection policy — is a statement of long- and short-range needs, of acquisition priorities, and of collecting boundaries (Reed-Scott 1984, p. 26, as quoted in the SAA Dictionary of Archives Terminology). It states what a library or archive will and will not add to its holdings, so that selection decisions are consistent across time and across the people making them, and it typically addresses the breadth and depth of the collection's focus, whether that focus is geographic, subject, or periodic (Berner 1984, p. 248, as quoted in the same entry).

A collection policy is distinguished from a single selection decision: a selection decision applies criteria to one candidate item, while a collection policy states the criteria themselves, in advance, so that many independent selection decisions — made by different people, or by an open submission queue, over years — produce a coherent collection rather than an arbitrary one. amusewiki's own submission process rejects texts for being off-topic ('not anarchist' on one site), poorly marked up, or of unknown or unpublished authorship — an operating collection policy stated as moderation criteria, even where no single document names it as a policy.

Because a collection policy is what makes many independent selection decisions cohere, a digital library that accepts open or anonymous submissions cannot substitute a moderation queue for a stated policy: without a written policy the moderation queue's own criteria are unaccountable to anyone outside it, and a change in who moderates can silently change what the collection becomes.
