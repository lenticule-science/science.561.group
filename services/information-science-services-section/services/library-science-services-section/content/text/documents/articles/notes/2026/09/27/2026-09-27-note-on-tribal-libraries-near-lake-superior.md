# Tribal libraries near Lake Superior

## The libraries found

**Minnesota** (Minitex keeps the state's list: [minitex.umn.edu/tags/tribal-libraries](https://minitex.umn.edu/tags/tribal-libraries)):

- **Fond du Lac**: the Ruth A. Myers Library of Fond du Lac Tribal and Community
  College, Cloquet. The college is both a tribally chartered college and part of
  the Minnesota State system
  ([FDLTCC library](https://fdltcc.edu/academics/library-services/library-information/)).
- **Red Lake**: Medweganoonind Library in Red Lake Nation College, opened
  2015-08-15, also holding the tribal archives. Before it opened, the nearest
  public library was in Bemidji, 45 minutes away.
- **White Earth**: Maamigin Achigaazo ("the gathering place"), opened 2015
  within the tribe's childcare and early childhood program, aimed at early
  literacy.
- **Leech Lake**: the library of Leech Lake Tribal College.
- **Bois Forte, Grand Portage, Mille Lacs**: no standalone tribal library was
  found. That is a gap in the search, not a finding of absence; ask each nation's
  offices.

**Wisconsin**:

- **Red Cliff Band of Lake Superior Chippewa**: Ginanda Gikendaasomin ("we seek
  to learn"), opened 2015 after planning from 2008 with the TLAM (Tribal
  Libraries, Archives, and Museums) Project at the UW–Madison iSchool. It combines
  a library, a language immersion school and a gathering space
  ([TLAM Project](https://www.tlamproject.org/partnerships/red-cliff-library/)).
- **Bad River**: no tribal library was confirmed; ask the band.

## The support around them

- **ATALM**, the Association of Tribal Archives, Libraries, and Museums (founded
  2010), offers training and an annual conference, much of it funded by IMLS.
- **IMLS Native American Library Services**: Basic Grants, divided equally among
  all eligible tribes, and competitive Enhancement Grants. In the year of the
  release read, 188 tribes shared $1,960,416 in Basic Grants, and 21 tribes in
  nine states received $2,684,565 in Enhancement Grants
  ([IMLS](https://www.imls.gov/news/imls-invests-5-million-library-services-tribal-communities)).
  Minnesota and Wisconsin recipients should be checked by name at
  imls.gov/grants/awarded.

## The state's materials law does not apply

Minn. Stat. 134.51 defines the libraries it governs, and a tribal nation's
library is not among them. The Fond du Lac college library may be an exception
through the "public higher education" clause. See the law note on
[government data and a contracted library administrator](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/law-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/27/2026-09-27-note-on-government-data-and-a-contracted-library-administrator.md).
A tribal library's collection policy is the nation's own. The "read free"
framing of a Minnesota policy product is the wrong offer to bring to one.

## What a youth services consultant brings, and what not

- **The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance** (Collective benefit,
  Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics; Global Indigenous Data Alliance,
  2019; Carroll et al., *Data Science Journal*, 2020,
  [doi:10.5334/dsj-2020-043](https://datascience.codata.org/articles/dsj-2020-043))
  apply to data *about* a nation and its people: circulation and program data,
  recordings, photographs, oral histories, and any audit of the collection. The
  nation governs them. A consultant's report stays the tribe's, and it is not
  used as a case study or sales material without the nation's consent.
- **Local Contexts** Traditional Knowledge and Biocultural Labels (led by Jane
  Anderson and Kim Christen) are catalog-level notices of a community's
  protocols for a work: attribution, non-commercial use, seasonal or ceremonial
  restrictions. They matter as much to a non-tribal library that holds Ojibwe
  materials as to a tribal one.
- **A collection audit** of a tribal library is the tribe's to commission and to
  shape. The CREW method and Diverse BookFinder's categories are tools the
  library may choose, not a standard to apply to it. Diverse BookFinder covers
  trade picture books and tags tribal affiliation. It does not know
  community-published or language materials, which are often a tribal
  library's core.
- **Offers that fit**: grant writing help for IMLS Basic and Enhancement
  applications; family bookbinding workshops led with community knowledge
  keepers, on the community's terms; readers' advisory for Native-authored
  youth literature. Each is offered, not pitched, and follows the relationship.

The house's consulting on Indigenous data governance
(`561-group-operations-services-section/services/indigenous-data-governance-consulting-services-section`)
serves AI labs and technology companies. It is not the model for a library
relationship, which is local and service-first.
