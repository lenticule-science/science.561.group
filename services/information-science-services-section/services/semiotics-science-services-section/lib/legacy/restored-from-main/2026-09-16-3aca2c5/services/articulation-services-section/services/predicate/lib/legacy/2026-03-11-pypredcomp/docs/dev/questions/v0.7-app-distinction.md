> **Resolved 2026-05-03 (industry default).** Modern multi-package > projects default to the monorepo (Cargo workspaces, pnpm > workspaces, Bazel, the entire Google/Meta convention) for > exactly the reasons that already apply here: v0.6 SDK and v0.3 > bundled apps already live in this repo, atomic cross-cutting > refactors require it, and split-repo coordination cost is > non-trivial.
The current `scope.md` claim that v0.7 productivity > apps live "in their own project directories" is overruled — > they live under `pypredcomp/apps/<app>/` like every other > package.
Split out only when (a) a single app needs an > independent release cadence the substrate cannot accommodate, > or (b) external contributors need to depend on one app without > the substrate's full source.
Captain may overrule. ---


Per [milestones/v0.7-561-productivity-stack.md](../milestones/v0.7-561-productivity-stack.md), these apps come at v0.7:

- Tasks
- Knowledge
- Governance
- Dispatch
- Fleet
- Review

The current [scope.md](../texts/scope.md) says "user-stack apps that come at v0.7 *after* the OS exists.
The OS provides the substrate; the apps are written *on* the OS.
Per-app design happens in the app's project directory, not here."

But the v0.6 SDK is in this sprint repo.
The bundled-apps for v0.3 are in this sprint repo.
Why are the v0.7 productivity stack apps "out of this sprint repo"?

## Options

- **A.** v0.7 apps live in their own project directories under `operations/command/projects/<app-slug>/`.
  The sprint repo is OS-only.
- **B.** v0.7 apps live in `specs/v0.7-apps/` within the sprint repo.
  The sprint repo includes the captain's full operational stack.
- **C.** Hybrid: bundled-apps style stubs in the sprint repo; full feature design in per-app project directories.
  Captain has light specs here for reference; deep specs there for execution.

## Tradeoffs

- (A) keeps the sprint repo focused on OS spec; clean separation.
  (Current default.)
- (B) groups all 561 group OS work in one place; easier for the captain to navigate.
- (C) middle ground; some duplication but clearer interfaces.
