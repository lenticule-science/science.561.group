## Theory grounding

Row 6 of [theory-for-developers.md](../references/theory-for-developers.md) ("the respond primitive") plus [`single-respond-primitive`](../decisions/single-respond-primitive.md).
Every stdlib verb is `respond` against a different shape of payload.
The dispatch shell exists *only* to canonicalise the surface a developer types into a series of `respond` calls.
Per-verb fuel composes via [`cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale`](../../../../../../../../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale.md) (row 7), so `verb1 | verb2` replays byte-identically to its pipelined run only after v0.2a gate 6 closes.

## Procedural steps

Per [implementation/next-stretch.md §2C](../implementation/next-stretch.md#2c-stdlib-verb-specs-and-dispatch-shell):

1. Author one spec per verb under [specs/stdlib/](../specs/stdlib/) per [workflows.md recipe 4](../implementation/workflows.md#4-adding-or-extending-a-stdlib-verb).
  The section that distinguishes the verb is its `respond` reduction.
2. Author the per-verb fixture per the [_index appendix](../specs/stdlib/_index.md).
  The fixture proves the reduction is a well-typed `respond`.
3. Implement [`src/shell/repl.py`](../../../src/shell/repl.py) §dispatch as a one-screen function: parse the message-language token, route to `stdlib.<verb>`, return the `respond` result.
4. Land [pt-05](../tasks/engineering/pt-05-tier2-model-registry-lookup.md) and [pt-06](../tasks/engineering/pt-06-confidence-bilattice-calibration.md) for the model-registry and confidence-calibration verbs (most non-trivial reductions).

## Enables next

v0.3's bundled apps script their workflows in the dispatch shell.
Without the shell stable, any v0.3 work that touches a verb has to also fix the verb's reduction — a perpetual scope-bleed.
Per [next-stretch.md §3B](../implementation/next-stretch.md#3b-first-bundled-apps-as-driver--verb--dashboard-tuples), the cheapest first end-to-end app (terminal + calculator + ledger) is *literally* a thin wrapper over the v0.2c dispatch shell.
