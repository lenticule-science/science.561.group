## Grounding

Theory rows 7 (fuel composition and the Lawvere quantale) and 10 (federation merge as $\odot$) of [references/theory-for-developers.md](../../references/theory-for-developers.md).
Federation rate-limiting is structurally a *fuel-tensor coefficient* on the per-peer merge channel: the per-peer admissible-event-rate is a quantale-valued capacity, and exceeding it down-grades the channel's bilattice value rather than dropping packets.
Library grounding:

- [`federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity`](../../../../../../../../../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity.md) — the per-peer fuel coefficient distributes over $\odot$, so rate-limit composition across peers is well-defined.
- [`federation-path-metrics-multiplicative`](../../../../../../../../../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/federation-path-metrics-multiplicative.md) — rate budgets compose multiplicatively along federation paths; the spec must record this.
- [`transport-tolerance-of-federation`](../../../../../../../../../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/transport-tolerance-of-federation.md) — federation tolerates dropped/delayed transport; the rate limit is what *constrains* the tolerance, not what breaks under load.
- [`cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale`](../../../../../../../../../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale.md) — per-peer fuel composes under the Lawvere quantale; the spec inherits this composition law rather than inventing a new one.

## Description

`specs/network/federation-rate-limit.md` does not exist.
The Unix reflex would be "token bucket per peer with HTTP 429 backpressure" — wire-level mechanism baked in.
The substrate's discipline is that the rate budget is structurally a fuel-tensor coefficient that *modulates the bilattice value of merged events*, not a binary admit/reject.
A peer that exceeds budget produces events with degraded confidence, not silence; the captain can still see the over-budget contributions and make a policy decision.

This task creates the stub.
It names the algebraic shape (fuel-tensor coefficient per peer-channel, multiplicative composition along paths), names the deferred surface (enforcement mechanism, refill cadence, captain-facing override), and cites the deferral decision.
