## Grounding

Theory row 2 (adjunction → counit) of [references/theory-for-developers.md](../../references/theory-for-developers.md).
The hledger driver is the *faithful-counit* exemplar — a journal entry round-trips byte-identically through the substrate (cell ↔ hledger journal-line).
Per [`predicated-computing/theorems/doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor`](../../../../../../../../../theories/engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor.md): the hledger driver's counit ε is provably faithful and gates that property in [`task:t02-65`](t02-65-v0.2b-drivers-lossy-counit-discipline.md).

## Description

The hledger-driver spec is normative.
This task is the Python implementation that binds hledger journal files (text format: ledger-style accounting) to substrate cells.
The hledger driver is the v0.2b proof that *some* driver can be faithful — not lossy — across its adjunction.

**What this consumes from the rest of v0.2b.** The driver framework from [`task:t02-50`](t02-50-v0.2b-driver-model-and-three-drivers.md) + lifecycle from [`task:t02-53`](t02-53-v0.2b-driver-model-and-three-drivers.md).
The counit-fidelity discipline from [`task:t02-65`](t02-65-v0.2b-drivers-lossy-counit-discipline.md) gates this driver as `faithful`; the conformance test is the byte-identity round-trip below.

Watch for whitespace, comment-line preservation, and decimal-precision issues.
