Audience: someone who can read code and wants to know why this module treats
"evidence for" and "evidence against" as two separate things instead of one
switch that flips between true and false.

Learning goal: understand why a predication's charge is a pair of independent
bits, not one bit, and what that buys.

## The claim has no truth field

A predication fixes one oriented point: a subject, a predicate, and an
object — "the door is open," say. The claim itself does not carry a truth
value. Standing is supplied separately, by whoever observed it. Two people
can each make an independent observation about the same claim: one can
observe evidence *for* it, another evidence *against* it, and — this is the
part worth sitting with — observing the *converse* relation ("the door is
shut," or even just reversing subject and object) is not the same act as
observing evidence against the original claim. Converse is not denial.

This is proved formally in
`Obsmetrologia.Language.Predicating.FregeanFourChu` (Lean, at
`obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/Obsmetrologia/Language/Predicating/FregeanFourChu.lean`):
preserving the two independently observable acts about one predication —
evidence for it, evidence against it — gives exactly `Bool × Bool`, i.e. the
four-valued bilattice FOUR (⊥ neither observed, t only for, f only against,
⊤ both). The theorem also shows the canonical evaluation matrix
(`predication -> witness -> charge`) is exactly a Chu matrix over FOUR:
predications are points, witnessing parties are states, and the matrix entry
is the charge that party observed.

`independentEvidenceRow`/`independentEvidenceSide` in
`predicating/independent-evidence.mts` are the executable form of the Lean's
`independentEvidenceCharge : Bool × Bool -> Charge`: a row names which side
(`"positive"` or `"negative"`) one witness observed. `foldIndependentEvidence`
joins every row's contribution per predication point with
`@lenticule-science/metrologic/bilattice`'s `joinKnowledge` — evidence
accumulates, nothing is overwritten, so a point told both ways settles to ⊤
(held, not an error). `sectionSpace` is the executable form of the Lean's
`predicationChu`: it presents the folded fiber as an explicit Chu space
(`@lenticule-science/metrologic/chu`) with predications as points and
witnessing parties as states, so "who observed what about this claim" reads
as one row of the space instead of a hand-rolled witness index.

## Why this needs to be exactly four, not two

The same forcing shows up from a different angle in the firm's own
governance algebra. The 561-group note on PWS core systems design
(`561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/04/06/2026-04-06-note-on-pws-core-systems-design.md`)
reads the Curry-Howard-Lambek correspondence as exactly the "nuclear
quartet": a free carrier, `Fix(σ)` (saturation-stable, past-stable), `Fix(Δ)`
(transfer-active, future-active), and the doubly-stable fixed fiber where
both apply. The submonoid generated by two commuting, idempotent operators
has exactly four elements — the same reason FOUR and not some other count
shows up whenever two independent yes/no observations are preserved rather
than collapsed. It is a different derivation of the same shape this
module's evidence pairs live in: two independent axes, not reducible to one.

## Independence is evidential, not organizational

One more caution, from the glass-sponge note's discussion of kinetic
proofreading (§16.4,
`engineering-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/01/2026-08-01-note-on-the-content-addressed-glass-sponge-semantic-compute-complex.md`):
"Independence is evidential, not organizational." Two passes by the same
witness with the same evidence are not two independent observations, no
matter how many different `by` names or call sites route through them. This
module cannot enforce that on its own — `independentEvidenceRow` will accept
whatever `by`/`witness` a caller supplies — but the property it *does* hold
structurally, and that the FregeanFourChu independence test in
`test/independent-evidence.test.mjs` checks directly, is narrower and
mechanical: a row asserting a converse or otherwise-related predicate never
reaches the original predicate's negative coordinate. Only an evidence row
that names that exact predicate, with `side: "negative"`, does.
