# Jade as Polycrystalline Settling

This note investigates whether jade has been explored as glass or as
tessellation. The answer to the first question is the finding: jade is
not glass, and the way it is not glass is the most formally suggestive
fact about it. The mineralogy inquiry note filed today in
mineralogy-services-section carries the literature checks; its sourced
numbers are cited where used.

## What jade is made of

"Jade" names two distinct metamorphic rocks, both aggregates rather than
crystals. Nephrite is amphibole jade — tremolite–actinolite, a
calcium-magnesium double-chain silicate — occurring as a felted mass of
microscopic interlocking fibers, tangled in every direction like pressed
wool. Jadeite is pyroxene jade — a sodium-aluminium single-chain
silicate — occurring as a dense mass of interlocking granular crystals.
Both form under metamorphic pressure over long time, which is the fourth
regime's signature as the alchemical-line note fixed it. What that note
did not yet say is the microstructure: the fourth regime's material is
polycrystalline. Not one order; many small orders, locked.

## Toughness is not hardness

On Mohs' scale nephrite sits near 6–6.5: quartz scratches it. Jade is not
especially hard. What jade is, is tough — exceptionally resistant to
fracture. The one quantitative study located (Bradt, Newnham & Biggers
1973, *American Mineralogist* 58) measured fracture toughness around
7.7 MPa·m^1/2 for nephrite and 7.1 for jadeite — extraordinary values for
stone, and the reason Neolithic peoples across Eurasia, Mesoamerica, and
Aotearoa independently made jade into axes, adzes, and mere when harder
stones were available: the harder stones shattered under impact and the
jade did not. Those figures come from a single 1973 paper and want an
independent replication before anything leans on the exact numbers, but
the qualitative fact — jade as the toughest of the classical stones — is
robust across the literature.

The toughness comes from the polycrystallinity. A crack entering
nephrite meets a felt of fibers oriented every way; to advance it must
cut across fibers or follow their tangled interfaces, and either way its
energy is spent deflecting, branching, and pulling fibers out. Cracks die
at grain boundaries. There is no cleavage plane through the whole because
no single crystallographic orientation spans the whole.

## Three ways to be solid, three ways to fail

The finding sharpens against jade's two rivals:

The single crystal is one perfect global order — and it cleaves. A
monocrystal's atoms all share an orientation, so a flaw propagates along
the shared planes unimpeded; the crystal splits along its own perfection.
Diamond, the hardest material, is split with a mallet by anyone who knows
its planes.

Glass is no order at all — and it shatters. With no crystal planes there
is also nothing to arrest a crack; fracture runs conchoidally wherever
the stress field takes it. The estate's amorphous silicas — sponge
spicule, opal, tabasheer — live here; the glass sponge compensates
architecturally, laminating its brittle glass into layered spicules,
though the mineralogy inquiry's reading of the literature finds the
spicule lamination toughens far less than nacre's superficially similar
brick-and-mortar structure — a real disagreement in the biomimetics
literature and a useful test case below.

The felted polycrystal — jade — is many small orders interlocked, and it
endures. Each grain is locally perfect; no orientation is global; the
interfaces between grains, where two domains meet without sharing an
order, are where fracture energy goes to die.

## The formal reading: which obstructions toughen

Read in the estate's terms, the three solids are three regimes of
agreement. The monocrystal is one global section — total agreement,
everything glued — and its failure mode is that any flaw travels the
whole structure unimpeded; total coherence is a cleavage plane. Glass is
no sections at all — nothing settled, nothing to appeal to — and its
failure is unbounded, pathless fracture. Jade is many local sections that
do not glue globally, a dense population of small settlements whose
mutual misalignments are permanent — and this is the tough one. The grain
boundaries are obstruction made load-bearing.

This connects to standing estate material in two places. The bamboo
note's mautam warning — a monoculture cohort settling all at once floods
the ecosystem; staggered cohorts are the hedge — is the same claim one
level up: global synchrony is fragility. Jade states it at the
microstructural level: global orientation is fragility; the felted stone
is the anti-mautam. And the proved cohomology strand classifies
obstruction (H¹ generators, contradiction cells) while the game design
treats holes as things to repair — jade proposes a refinement the current
machinery does not draw: some non-gluings dissipate propagating failure
rather than constituting it. Under what conditions? The ossification
note already names the likely doorway: toughness is a rigidity
phenomenon, and rigidity percolation is governed by Laman's generic
rigidity matroid while the existing formalization counts the graphic
matroid's cycle rank. The question is now posed properly in the
obsmetrological section's note "Which Obstructions Toughen?", along with
one candidate conjecture (an obstruction dissipates failure when it is
rigidity-matroid-redundant) and the cheapest concrete test: run the
nacre-versus-spicule comparison through it, since those two structures
look alike and toughen very differently. Nobody has yet published a
quantitative crack-deflection study of nephrite's own microstructure;
that is the key missing measurement.

The social reading follows directly and matters to the estate's
politics: a community whose every member shares one orientation cleaves
along it; a community with no settled domains shatters; a community of
many small, internally-settled, mutually-misaligned domains, felted at
their interfaces, is the one that takes blows. The paraconsistent
substrate — hold the contested charge, never average it — is this
doctrine at cell scale; jade is the same doctrine at aggregate scale.

## The connectivity ladder, and what MUD is made of

Silicate mineralogy classifies by how the SiO₄ tetrahedron polymerizes:
isolated tetrahedra (olivine, garnet), pairs, rings, chains — single in
pyroxene, double in amphibole, which is to say both jades — sheets
(micas, clays), and full 3D frameworks (quartz, feldspar, zeolites).
This is a connectivity classification, not a temporal sequence — a
correction worth making explicitly, since the tetralogy's framing is
temporal and the two axes should not be conflated. Placing the estate's
silica objects on the connectivity ladder:

SEA's dissolved monosilicic acid is the unpolymerized monomer. Sponge
spicule, opal, and tabasheer are amorphous — polymerized without order.
The zeolite vitrimer substrate is tectosilicate — the complete
three-dimensional framework, whose function depends on its pores: maximal
connectivity holding open interior room, Laozi 11 in crystal form. JADE
is inosilicate — chains: nephrite is double chains felted, jadeite single
chains interlocked. And clay minerals are phyllosilicates — sheet
silicates, stacked two-dimensional layers held by weak interlayer forces,
which is why mud is plastic and endlessly re-formable. MUD, the base site
the sponge stands on, is mineralogically made of sheets — a fact recorded
here because the estate's base-site vocabulary and the sediment's actual
structure agree more literally than intended.

Two consequences. First, jade is made of fibers: the estate's central
formal object — the fiber algebra — and the fourth regime's composition
coincide, and not vacuously, since nephrite's virtue is precisely that
its fibers are felted: many one-dimensional strands, no shared
orientation, interlocked into something that cannot be cleaved. The
recut JADE expansion (Jacobian Arena Difference Engine) gains a
microstructural reading: the arenas are the grains, the engine reads the
differences at their boundaries, and the differences are what make the
aggregate hold. Second, the regimes now have two independent axes —
settling time and connectivity class — and whether the κ-regime ladder
and the polymerization ladder are secretly one ladder is an open
question, not an identification.

## The tradition read the stone this way first

The Confucian account of jade — the eleven virtues in the Liji, with
benevolence in its luster, wisdom in its sound, courage in that it may be
broken but not bent — is a tradition reading material properties as
conduct, and the virtues attach to jade's toughness and texture, not its
hardness: the tradition prized the polycrystalline properties three
millennia before microscopy could say why the stone behaved that way.
Re-deriving the eleven virtues as interface properties of the felted
aggregate is left as an exercise for whoever wants an afternoon of it.

## Open questions

An independent replication of the 1973 toughness figures. A quantitative
microstructural study of crack deflection in nephrite. The
toughening-obstruction question as posed to the mathematics, with the
nacre/spicule comparison as its first test. And whether connectivity
class and closure regime are one ladder or two.
