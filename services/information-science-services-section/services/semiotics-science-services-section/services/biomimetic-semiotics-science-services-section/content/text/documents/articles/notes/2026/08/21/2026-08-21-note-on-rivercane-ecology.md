# Rivercane Ecology

The bamboo note built the grove from Asian botany — *Phyllostachys*'
synchronized growth, mast semelparity, harvest into tools. North America
has one native bamboo genus, *Arundinaria*: rivercane (*A. gigantea*) of
the Southeastern bottomlands, switchcane (*A. tecta*) of wetter coastal
sites, and hill cane (*A. appalachiana*) of upland slopes, the last only
recognized as a distinct species in 2006. Its formation is the canebrake.
This note records where the American genus differs from the Asian ones
and what each difference does to the grove theory.

One boundary held deliberately: rivercane is Southeastern terrain, and
its peoples are the Cherokee and their neighbors — the basket-makers,
blowgun-makers, and fire-tenders whose material culture is woven from
this plant. The owner's kinship ground is Lakota. The two are not
interchangeable registers, and rivercane enters the bestiary as the
local standing relative of the owner's North Carolina watershed, its
actual peoples named. Where the two traditions' forms converge below,
the convergence is noted as convergence.

## The origin is a network

Woody bamboos did not arise by clean branching descent. Genome studies
(Guo et al. 2019, *Nature Plants*) indicate the woody lineages arose
through hybridization among ancestral diploid lineages, and the
temperate woody clade — *Arundinaria*'s — is allotetraploid: two
distinct ancestral genomes fused and held in every cell. At least one
ancestral donor lineage appears extinct as a free-living plant,
surviving only as a subgenome inside the hybrids. The clade's internal
phylogeny is notoriously resistant to tree methods — low divergence,
extensive hybridization — and requires network models.

For the theory: a reticulate phylogeny is a history with cycles, which
tree carriers cannot represent — the same lesson the estate's own
settled-object serialization failure taught about tree encodings, learned
by the genus first. An allotetraploid is two settled structures glued
and stably held as one viable organism over millions of years — the
jade note's question about load-bearing non-gluings, answered
affirmatively at lineage scale. And the ghost subgenome is the
tabasheer structure at genome scale: an extinct lineage persisting
solely as settled record carried inside a living relative.

## Flowering is sporadic, not synchronized

*Arundinaria* flowering is irregular, partial, and thinly documented:
stands flower in patches on no established clock, and death after
flowering is inconsistent rather than obligate. This is the sharpest
contrast with the masting genera the FLOWER doctrine came from, and it
matters because the bamboo note's own risk analysis — the mautam, the
monoculture cohort settling all at once, staggered cohorts as the hedge —
recommended as design advice exactly the life-history the American genus
runs natively: local, asynchronous, partial settlings instead of one
global event. The flowering literature is thin enough that this section
should be treated as provisional until the botany paper runs it down.

## Canebrakes are fire-maintained

The canebrake — historically vast monodominant stands along Southeastern
rivers — persists only under periodic fire: cane resprouts vigorously
from rhizome after burning, and fire suppresses the woody succession
that would otherwise overtop and shade it out. The historical fire
regime was substantially indigenous burning; the canebrake is in large
part an anthropogenic ecosystem, maintained by the peoples whose
material culture depended on it.

A canebrake is therefore not a stable attractor but a metastable state
held off the successional pathway by scheduled disturbance — and the
disturbance operator is a relationship with people. Remove the human
co-operator and the colony closes over into forest. This is the
strongest ecological instance the bestiary holds of a structure the
Lakota material also carries: periodic, obligatory, apparently
destructive acts, publicly performed with a necessary kin-partner, whose
performance is what keeps the whole alive.

## Hill cane sheds

*A. appalachiana* is deciduous — nearly unique among bamboos, it drops
its leaves each autumn and stands bare through winter dormancy. The
temperate regime, where light is seasonal rather than constant, forces a
cyclic rather than monotone growth pattern, and hill cane is its
visible carrier: scheduled whole-surface shedding, dormancy as
pausing-without-losing-state, regrowth each spring.

## The niche is the boundary

Rivercane's niche is the riparian buffer — the seam between flowing
water and land — where its documented functions are bank stabilization
and water filtration. The bamboo note placed the grove "on MUD at the
margin of the SEA" as idiom; the canebrake occupies the margin as its
actual address, and its ecological job is concentrating filtration at
the boundary.

## Loss and recovery

Over 98% of historical canebrake extent is gone, among the steepest
losses of any North American ecosystem type. The destruction was
directed, not incidental: cane signaled fertile bottomland ("cane land"
meant good land), so canebrakes were plowed first; grazing and fire
suppression finished the work. Bachman's warbler, a canebrake
specialist, is likely extinct with them.

Recovery runs through the substrate: with flowering rare and seedling
establishment rarer, rivercane spreads and is restored almost entirely
by transplanting living rhizome. A new canebrake is founded by moving a
piece of the old one — never recompiled from seed — which is why the
remaining fraction is irreplaceable. Current restoration work pairs
tribal artisans' need for mature cane with ecological recovery: the
craft tradition and the ecosystem now depend on each other.

## Summary of what the American register adds

Asian bamboo gave the theory synchrony, scale, and harvest. Rivercane
adds reticulation (held hybrid origin, ghost subgenome), asynchrony
(sporadic partial flowering), kinship-maintenance (fire as the operator
that keeps the colony alive), boundary-occupation (the riparian seam as
address), and recovery (substrate transplant as the only founding move,
after directed near-total destruction). The Cherokee double-weave — one
continuous weave carrying two independent patterns on its two faces —
is recorded here as an adjacency to the estate's two-sided charge
carrier and nothing more until someone shows the construction does
matching structural work.

## Open questions

The flowering ecology needs real literature. Whether *Arundinaria*
accumulates silica like the Asian genera, and whether it forms tabasheer
at all. The canebrake fire-dependence deserves its proper sources. All
of this is the brief for the rivercane ecology paper in
