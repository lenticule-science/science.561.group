name: defect-mediated growth
description: "The principle that sustained growth or morphogenesis organizes around a persistent, held structural defect rather than around a perfect, defect-free state — established in crystallography (Burton-Cabrera-Frank) and given a stronger, living instantiation in active-nematic defects and Hydra morphogenesis."
date-created: 2026-08-26

Defect-mediated growth names the observation, first made rigorous in
condensed-matter physics, that continued growth of an ordered structure often
depends on a persistent topological or structural imperfection rather than
occurring despite one. Burton, Cabrera & Frank (1951, *Philosophical
Transactions of the Royal Society A*) showed that a screw dislocation
intersecting a crystal surface provides a permanently self-regenerating step
edge (a growth spiral), letting the crystal grow continuously at
supersaturations far too low to sustain the two-dimensional nucleation a
dislocation-free face would require. In this precise sense, a perfect crystal
grows far more slowly than one held open by a defect it never heals.

The same theme reappears, considerably strengthened, in active matter and
developmental biology. Keber et al. (2014, *Science*) showed that topological
defects in active nematic liquid-crystal vesicles are not static flaws but
self-propelled, dynamical entities that actively drive the vesicle's shape
changes. Maroudas-Sacks et al. (2021, *Nature Physics*) went further: in
regenerating Hydra, +1 topological defects in the nematic order of
supracellular actin fibers are the actual organizing centers of the animal's
body plan — mouth, tentacles, and foot form at defect sites, with the
orientation field acting as what the authors call a "mechanical morphogen."
Here the defect is not merely tolerated; it is the organizing cause of the
growing structure's form, in a living, developing tissue rather than an inert
lattice.

Calibrated status: the crystallographic result is settled, well-confirmed
physics for inorganic growth from vapor or solution. Extending "growth
organizes around held defects" as a structural theme to living or social
growth is a defensible analogy, not an equation — and where a biological
register is wanted, the active-nematic and Hydra-morphogenesis literature is
a stronger, more current, and more literally apt anchor than the crystal
spiral, since it describes defects organizing growth in an actual living,
developing system rather than in an inert solid.
