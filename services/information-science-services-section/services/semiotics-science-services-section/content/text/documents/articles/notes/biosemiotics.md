Biosemiotics is the study of [sign](../terms/sign.md) processes in and among living systems. It extends [Peircean semiotics](./peircean-semiotics.md) beyond human communication to encompass all life, treating [semiosis](../terms/semiosis.md) — the production and interpretation of [signs](../terms/sign.md) — as a defining characteristic of living organisms. Its founding figures are Thomas Sebeok (1920–2001) and, retrospectively, the biologist Jakob von Uexküll (1864–1944).

## Methods and approach

Biosemiotics draws on [Peirce](../../../../../../../references/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)'s triadic sign model to argue that sign processes are not confined to language or culture but are coextensive with life. Wherever an organism interprets its environment — a bacterium detecting a chemical gradient, a bee performing a waggle dance, a cell responding to a hormone — [semiosis](../terms/semiosis.md) is occurring. The biosemiotic claim is ontological: life and [semiosis](../terms/semiosis.md) are coextensive.

### Key contributions

1. **Zoosemiotics** — Sebeok's term for the study of [sign](../terms/sign.md) processes in animal communication and behavior. Sebeok argued that all animals communicate through signs, and that the study of animal sign systems is a branch of [semiotics](../_index.md) rather than a separate discipline. He extended the Peircean classification ([icon](../terms/icon.md), [index](../terms/index-sign.md), [symbol](../terms/symbol.md)) to non-human organisms, showing that animal communication uses all three sign types.

2. **Umwelt** — von Uexküll's concept of the organism's subjective perceptual world. Each species inhabits its own Umwelt — a world constituted by the [signs](../terms/sign.md) it can perceive and the actions it can perform. A tick's Umwelt consists of butyric acid, warmth, and skin texture; a bat's Umwelt is constituted by echolocation. The Umwelt is the organism's sign-world: the totality of [signs](../terms/sign.md) that are meaningful for it.

3. **Functional cycle** — von Uexküll's model of the organism-environment relationship as a loop of perception signs (*Merkzeichen*) and action signs (*Wirkzeichen*). The organism perceives features of its environment as signs, interprets them, and acts; the action changes the environment, producing new signs. This is a biological instance of [semiosis](../terms/semiosis.md): the iterative process of sign-interpretation-sign.

4. **Endosemiotics** — the study of [sign](../terms/sign.md) processes within organisms: cell signaling, immune recognition, genetic expression. The cell's response to a hormone is a [semiotic](../_index.md) process: the hormone is a [representamen](../terms/representamen.md), the cellular receptor mediates the relation, and the cellular response is an [interpretant](../terms/interpretant.md). The genetic code itself — the mapping from nucleotide triplets to amino acids — is a [semiotic](../_index.md) system.

5. **The semiotic threshold** — biosemiotics challenges the assumption that [semiosis](../terms/semiosis.md) requires consciousness, intention, or language. The threshold of sign use is not the human mind but life itself. This expands the scope of [semiotics](../_index.md) to include domains traditionally claimed by biochemistry and molecular biology.

## Relation to Peircean semiotics

Biosemiotics is the most direct extension of [Peircean semiotics](./peircean-semiotics.md). [Peirce](../../../../../../../references/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) himself did not restrict [semiosis](../terms/semiosis.md) to human beings — his definition of the [sign](../terms/sign.md) as "something that stands for something else to some interpreter" places no species restriction on the interpreter. Biosemiotics takes this generality seriously and investigates its consequences for biology and the philosophy of life.

## Active research

Since Hoffmeyer's death in 2019, biosemiotics has continued to develop at the University of Tartu (Kalevi Kull, Timo Maran), in the journal *Biosemiotics* (Springer), and at *Sign Systems Studies*. Kull's recent work explores the role of freedom and choice in organismic behavior and evolution ("Choices by Organisms," *Biological Journal of the Linnean Society*, 2023). The field has also engaged with AI through the topos-theoretic framework of Tohme, Gangle, and Caterina (2024), which shows that machine learning processes share the formal structure of semiosis.

## Related schools

- [Peircean Semiotics](./peircean-semiotics.md) — provides the triadic sign model that biosemiotics extends to all life
- [Ecosemiotics](./ecosemiotics.md) — studies sign processes specifically in ecological relations between organisms and environments
- [Social Semiotics](./social-semiotics.md) — shares the concern with sign-making beyond language but restricts its scope to human social practice
- [Cognitive Semiotics](./cognitive-semiotics.md) — attentive to qualitative differences between levels of sign use that biosemiotics sometimes flattens
