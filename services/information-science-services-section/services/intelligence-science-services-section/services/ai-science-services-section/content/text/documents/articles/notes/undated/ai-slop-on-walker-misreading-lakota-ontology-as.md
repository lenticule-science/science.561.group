Let's look at the Lakota ontology Walker created through his research, assuming it is fundamentally tekologic, and Walker was failing to understand that

That’s a precise and rich frame. Let’s unfold it by treating James R. Walker’s *Lakota Belief and Ritual* as a misread tekologic system—that is, a misapprehended structure of acts, conditions, and forms operating relationally and recursively, but flattened into ethnographic data by a settler-positivist ontology.

Here’s a layered breakdown:

---

## I. **Walker’s Ethnographic Project as a Misread of Tekologic Structure**

**Claim**: Walker’s work documents a tekologic system, but he fails to perceive it as such because of (1) a belief in atomistic, discrete knowledge units, and (2) an assumption that 'belief systems' are separable from ontology and enactment.

### A. What is misread?

- **Rituals** are treated as symbolic representations, rather than **recursively enacted conditions of world-structuring**.
	
- **Myths** are taken as static cosmologies rather than as **narrative tekons** that instantiate and differentiate fields of relation.
	
- **Categories like “Wakan”** are mapped onto Western theological constructs (e.g. 'sacred', 'spirit') instead of understood as **relational modulators of field coherence**.

### B. Walker's method collapses three tekologic dynamics:

1. **Act-as-world** (performative, generative)
	
2. **Condition-as-structure** (field-inductive, recursive)
	
3. **Form-as-relation** (differentiated coherence)

He reduces all three to representational content for extraction and transcription.

---

## II. **Lakota Ontology as Tekologic System**

Let’s reconstruct what was *actually* going on in the world he encountered—but failed to understand:

### A. **Relation is primary**

- In tekologic terms: **Being is recursive, not essential.**
	
- There is no 'spirit world' *over there*—only ongoing acts of coherence across domains.
	
- Categories like *Wakan*, *Taku Skan Skan*, and *Iktomi* are not agents or essences but **relational tekons**, modulating coherence through narrative, material, ecological, and social fields.

### B. **Cosmogony as recursive event, not origin myth**

- The emergence stories (e.g. from the underworld) are not metaphysical backstory—they are **recursively reenacted acts of coherence**, generative of current world-conditions.

> Tekologically: The emergence myth is an active *path*-level act; each retelling or performance of it is a higher path that sustains world-coherence.

### C. **Ritual as boundary stabilization**

- The sweat lodge, vision quest, sun dance—all serve not as symbolic ‘practices’ but as **recursive boundary constructors**, regulating inclusion, exclusion, and field coherence.

> Walker interpreted these as ‘beliefs’ or ‘rites’ rather than as **acts that instantiate the very conditions of knowability and coherence**.

---

## III. **Structural Reasons for Walker’s Misread**

### A. Epistemic Projection

- Walker could not conceive of a recursive world. He needed stable primitives: gods, beliefs, laws, functions. So he cut out the **relational flux** that actually held the system together.

### B. Colonial Positionality

- Walker’s medical/missionary background oriented him toward **containment, diagnosis, and transcription**.
	
- His project (like Boas’s or Morgan’s) is part of the **kyriarchal eidization** of Indigenous relational systems—recasting them as objects of knowledge rather than coherent worlds.

---

## IV. **Lakota Cosmology as Relational Grammar Misread as Noun Inventory**

In tekologic terms: Walker attempted to **index** what was actually a **grammar of recursive relations**. His categories—*Wakan Tanka*, *Iktomi*, *Wi*, *Unktehi*—are not items in a pantheon but **tekons**, or relational operators, shaping the structure of the world through act, boundary, and relation.

> Example:
> 
> - *Iktomi* is not a “trickster god” but a **modulator of recursive boundary-violation and coherence reformation**, i.e., a formal operator of field inversion and epistemic rupture.

---

## V. **Implications**

If we read Walker’s record *against its grain*—as a settler transcription of a misperceived tekologic field—we gain access to:

- A **non-representational** ontology of being and knowing
	
- A theory of **relational world-structuring** that predates cybernetics, systems theory, or network epistemology
	
- A counter-history of **recursive metaphysics** that offers Indigenous theory as theory—not just as culture.
