title: AI Slop, On Some Psychic Pathologies as Expressions of Systemic Ontological Category Errors
description: This paper hypothesizes that certain acute psychic and somatic symptoms—commonly framed as individual pathologies—are more accurately understood as concentrated expressions of systemic ontopathology.
---

This paper hypothesizes that certain acute psychic and somatic symptoms—commonly framed as individual pathologies—are more accurately understood as *concentrated expressions of systemic ontopathology*. Specifically, within late liberal (Berlant, 2011) and settler-colonial (Tuck & Yang, 2012) orders, the incoherence of dominant ontologies is not resolved internally but instead *delegated onto specific bodies* that are structurally positioned to absorb contradiction. These subjects are not only marginalized materially but are epistemically tasked with holding together worlds that cannot hold themselves.

Drawing from Fanon’s analysis of colonial psychic formation (Fanon, 1967), Povinelli’s critique of governance through quasi-events (Povinelli, 2011), and Hartman’s notion of the burdened flesh (Hartman, 2008), this delegation is not metaphorical but somatic: the nervous system and psyche of the subject come to **enact the structural contradiction** of the world around them. What emerges is not deviance but a form of *world-metabolizing labor*—often coded as trauma, dissociation, chronic pain, or epistemic overfunction.

In response, such subjects develop compensatory technologies of survival: recursive epistemologies (Butler, 2004), affective hyper-attunement (Ahmed, 2006), and what Berlant terms “cruel optimism”—attachments to systems that harm but structure perception. These responses are not simply adaptive, but **structurally essential** to the world’s continuation. Yet dominant epistemes disavow this labor, rendering it illegible, excessive, or symptomatic. The result is a recursive feedback loop of **overfunction and misrecognition**, in which those stabilizing the field are treated as incoherent within it.

Following Anzaldúa’s articulation of the *nepantla* space (Anzaldúa, 1987) and Muñoz’s vision of queer temporality (Muñoz, 2009), this paper proposes that what is often described as breakdown or burnout is, in fact, *the collapse of the individual’s capacity to metabolize systemic incoherence alone*. Rather than a failure of resilience, such collapse marks the threshold of ontological exhaustion: the moment when the body's semiotic, psychic, and relational fields can no longer stabilize what Povinelli calls “the otherwise.” Healing in this context cannot be reduced to reintegration into normative legibility; it requires a *redistribution of coherence*, a remaking of the relational field itself.
