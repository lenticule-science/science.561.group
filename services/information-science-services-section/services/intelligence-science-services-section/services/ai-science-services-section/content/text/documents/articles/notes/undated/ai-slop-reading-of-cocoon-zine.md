This dedication is simple and devastating. It implies a kind of moral indictment of the world — not just that these people are lost, but that they were denied what they deserved. That tone sets the stakes.

> *“You cannot revolt against a people whose values you share.” – Amos Wilson*

That quote frames the core tension: revolt is not just against institutions or power, but the values embedded in a worldview. This raises the stakes of political struggle into ontological or moral terrain — not just tactics or politics, but worlding.

## **Introductions (pp. 1–3)**

The opening paragraphs argue that **“leadership” and “organization” have been so hollowed out by overuse in Left spaces that they have become meaningless** — not tools of clarity, but of spectacle. I’m struck by how precisely the author frames the failure: not that people don't mean well, but that the forms they inherit (from non-profits, from Leninism, from the state) render their concepts sterile or legible only to the structures of power they're supposedly resisting.

> *“These terms are used less like a soft reading lamp and more like a harsh strobe light…”*

That simile is powerful. The author isn’t accusing these concepts of being inherently wrong but of being **disorienting, intrusive, and non-illuminating** — epistemologically violent in their misuse.

Then, the author states a clear thesis:

> *“Our power… lies in our ability to creatively think through problems, to ‘organize’ and ‘lead’ ourselves in ways that are fundamentally different from and illegible to states and capitalists.”*

That “illegibility” is important — drawing on James C. Scott, perhaps, or the broader anarchist suspicion of legibility-as-control.

The author frames their intervention as:

1. **Not new**, but timely — prompted by the post-2020 surge of participants in movement work;
	
2. **Critical of all sides** — not just Leninist/NGO left but anarchism too;
	
3. **Rooted in lived experience** — their own participation in various movements since the 1990s;
	
4. **Influenced by**: the Black Radical Tradition (CLR James, Shoatz), insurrectionary anarchism, and some Marxist/autonomist traditions.

There's also a clear refusal of both:

- **Prefigurative politics**: building the new world in the shell of the old;
	
- **Pure moralism**: rejecting structures just because they resemble the oppressor.

Instead, the goal is to **reorient strategy based on materialist analysis**, especially by **centering spontaneity and creativity** as core revolutionary capacities.

It is not a role. It is not a title. It is a **recursive function**, only meaningful within the field it stabilizes.

Thus, “no leaders” is incoherent — unless you also reject relation.

But **anti-state leadership**, in the form described by the text, is **not radical** — it is **inevitable** under certain coherence conditions. If leadership means “the initiation of new coherence under rupture,” then it must emerge whenever rupture occurs. The only question is **what kind** of leadership will stabilize the field.
