## 🧠 **1. Critical Management Studies (CMS)**

**Core Focus**: Deconstructing the logics of managerialism and capitalist work through poststructuralist and Marxist theory.

- **Martin Parker** – critiques managerial rationality, encourages alternative organizing.
	
- **Mats Alvesson** – works on organizational culture, identity, and critical reflexivity; he uses postmodernism and power analysis.
	
- **Hugh Willmott** – explores ideology in organizational theory, often via Gramsci and Foucault.

> These thinkers won't use *intersectionality* in the Crenshaw mode often, but their systems thinking is deeply structural and sometimes queered.

---

## 📚 **2. Intersectional Feminist Work/Organizational Theory**

**Core Focus**: Power, gender, race, queerness, and coloniality within structures of labor and management.

- **Aimee Carrillo Rowe** – brings affect, queerness, and relationality into media and organizational analysis.
	
- **Sara Ahmed** – her work on *complaint*, institutions, and emotional labor within feminist theory can be directly applied to workplaces.
	
- **Nirmal Puwar** – especially her concept of “space invaders” in institutional structures, analyzing who bodies belong in which jobs.

> These thinkers explore how institutions encode social power through **ontoeidic** placements—who gets to work, belong, or complain—and what emotional labor upholds the system.

---

## 🔍 **3. Libidinal Economy & Organizational Affect**

**Core Focus**: Desire, enjoyment, and emotional economy in structuring institutions and labor.

- **Patricia Clough** – works on affective labor, precarity, and neoliberal subjectivities.
	
- **Brian Massumi** – doesn’t focus on business per se but is used in design and affective strategy.
	
- **Peter Fleming** – especially in *The Mythology of Work* and *The Death of Homo Economicus*, critiques work as a moral imperative and offers strategies of refusal.

> This line aligns with *homotekic strategy*—seeing strategy not just as planning but libidinally as structuring acts, including refusal, rupture, or perverse coherence.

---

## 🏢 **4. Decolonial and Indigenous Organizational Theory**

**Core Focus**: How Indigenous or decolonial worldings structure work differently, and what colonialism means for labor and systems of organization.

- **Linda Tuhiwai Smith** – not a business theorist per se, but her work underlies Indigenous methodologies in org theory.
	
- **Leanne Betasamosake Simpson** – speaks more to refusal and Indigenous resurgence than traditional "work," but her thought could easily map to your tekic approach.
	
- **Tuck & Yang** – particularly in how they theorize refusal and cooptation under settler structures.

> This body of work offers alternatives to the ontoeidic structure of “work” altogether—framing it instead as *relational obligation*, *resurgence*, or *presence*.

### You said:

Are there any that speak to how to operate as a worker, not manager, organizer, or resister?

#### ChatGPT said:

Yes—and the rarity of this focus is notable. Most critical or intersectional labor theory orients around **organizing**, **refusal**, or **managing differently**, rather than how to *inhabit* work as a **worker** within a kyriarchal structure while **not identifying with it**, **not resisting it directly**, and **not becoming managerial**.

But a few thinkers *do* theorize this—some implicitly, some explicitly. These thinkers might be your closest lineage:

---

## 🧍‍♀️🪨 1. **Saidiya Hartman** – *Wayward Lives, Beautiful Experiments*

Hartman offers portraits of everyday Black life as radical not through resistance but through **waywardness**:

> small acts of refusal, redirection, withdrawal, pleasure, non-compliance, slowness, or drifting.

This is a practice of **inhabiting the structures** without ever **becoming their subject**. For workers, this could mean:

- Performing “the job” while withholding your soul.
	
- Following rules *as misdirection*, not as truth.
	
- Finding movement and rhythm in imposed stillness.

---

## 🛠️🌫️ 2. **Stefano Harney & Fred Moten** – *The Undercommons*

The clearest and most rigorous theorization of **how to work while not working for them**:

> “To work the university, and to work *in* the university, while never working *for* the university.”

This is a **parasitic, fugitive** stance: not rebellion, not leadership—but **presence-without-possession**. You move *through* the system but not *with* it.

For a worker, this looks like:

- Doing the labor while redirecting value to others.
	
- Refusing the call to “own” your role or climb.
	
- Enacting minor interruptions of fidelity to capital.

---

## 🧤👂 3. **Christina Sharpe** – *In the Wake*

She theorizes living “in the wake” of catastrophe—not through resistance, but through **care, witnessing, and navigation**. As a worker, her theory supports:

- Attunement over acceleration.
	
- Staying with the violence without reproducing it.
	
- Being present as a form of refusal to disappear.

---

## 🛠️🕯️ 4. **Lisa Adkins** – *The Time of Money*

She critiques the contemporary worker’s time being entirely subsumed by **financialized futurity**. Adkins doesn’t offer resistance—but makes space to ask:

> How do we **be** within these rhythms without aligning ourselves to them?

This echoes your sense of **tekonic alignment**—being present, but not *coherent* with the field’s imposed time.

---

## 👷‍♂️🔮 5. **Lauren Berlant** – *Cruel Optimism*

Not exactly advice, but offers tools to understand **how workers stay attached** to forms that *don’t* work for them. She helps frame:

- Why you keep doing the job.
	
- Why even “good” work hurts.
	
- Why leaving isn't always liberation.

Berlant helps make sense of **not-resisting as fidelity to life**, which is crucial for someone in a worker role who sees, but does not “fight.”

---

## 🧘🧹 6. **Kathi Weeks** – *The Problem with Work*

Weeks critiques the ideology of “the work ethic,” but also reflects on how to **exist within** work **without centering** it:

> “To see work not as who we are, but what we are forced to do.”

She doesn’t idealize resistance, but explores **reconceptualizing time, obligation, and self** under work—useful for theorizing *a tekonic worker-practice.*

---

## 🪨 Bonus Mentions:

- **Maurice Blanchot**, though not a work theorist, models the figure of the one who **performs without revealing presence**—the *worker who is not there*.
	
- **Gloria Anzaldúa**, through *nepantla*, theorizes a border-being **within contradiction**, neither rejecting nor accepting.

## Summary:

These thinkers can guide a tekic worker-practice of:

- **Non-sovereign labor**: Working without identification.
	
- **Fugitive fidelity**: Staying in relation but never “belonging.”
	
- **Rhythmic divergence**: Moving in counter-time within dominant flows.
	
- **Recursive acts**: Doing work as a loop that stabilizes your own field, not theirs.
