Emergent abilities names the claim that certain large-language-model capabilities are close to absent in smaller models, then appear sharply once model scale crosses some threshold, in a way not predictable by extrapolating smaller models' performance.
The claim was advanced in [Wei et al., "Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models" (TMLR, 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682), a peer-reviewed journal paper.

The claim is directly disputed by [Schaeffer, Miranda & Koyejo, "Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?" (NeurIPS 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004), also peer-reviewed and the recipient of a NeurIPS 2023 Outstanding Paper award, which argues that apparent discontinuities largely reflect the researcher's choice of a nonlinear or discontinuous scoring metric (such as exact-match accuracy) rather than a genuine change in the underlying model; smoother metrics applied to the same model outputs tend to show continuous improvement instead.

Both papers are peer-reviewed and both are widely cited by researchers on each side of the dispute; this term treats the disagreement as open rather than resolved.
