## **Supporting Threads to Follow:**

### 1. **Geographical-Historical Reorientation:**

- **Pre-Railroad Westward Expansion:** Mainly followed east-west corridors—wagon trails like the Oregon, California, and Santa Fe Trails.
	
- **Post-Railroad Expansion:** The Union Pacific/Central Pacific line ran roughly east-west (Omaha to Sacramento), completing the primary coast-to-coast axis.
	
- Once this axis was secured, **economic and military logistics turned Northward** into areas not yet "tamed": the Northern Plains and Rockies.
	
- **Indian Wars** in this period shifted North as well: e.g., Little Bighorn (Montana, 1876), Wounded Knee (South Dakota, 1890).

### 2. **Imaginary of the "True" Frontier:**

- The **"mythic" West** starts to crystallize in this era—in novels, dime-store tales, and later cinema.
	
- These are often **not** set in the already-railroaded West (California, Kansas), but in the **harsher, more rugged, less settled Northwest.**
	
- Consider how the **mountainous and arid landscapes of Montana, Idaho, Wyoming** become iconic backdrops for cowboy and Indian narratives, while Kansas or Missouri are often treated as thresholds.

### 3. **Cultural Cartography:**

- The shift to Northwest mirrors what **Benedict Anderson** might call an "imagined geography"—not strictly cartographic but ideological.
	
- This directional recalibration **coincides with whiteness fleeing itself**: the southern frontier was increasingly racialized (Reconstruction, border conflicts, etc.), and the western plains increasingly commodified.
	
- The Northwest thus becomes a **new stage for the performance of settler masculinity**, rugged individualism, and anti-modern nostalgia.

### 4. **Railroad as Temporal and Spatial Fold:**

- The railroad **collapsed space-time**, rendering many “western” spaces no longer “wild” but logistical.
	
- Thus, **wilderness had to move**—like a receding mirage—into the more remote Northwest.
	
- This could be read through **Harold Innis’** or **Paul Virilio’s** work on the speed-space relationship of empire-building media.

### 5. **Legibility in Film and Literature:**

- Later genre-defining westerns (e.g., *The Searchers*, *Shane*, *Dances With Wolves*) are often set in landscapes of the Northwest, not the literal west of, say, Nevada or California.
	
- The cowboy myth's spiritual home, in visual culture, is more **Montana and Wyoming** than New Mexico or Arizona (despite their important presence in other subgenres).
