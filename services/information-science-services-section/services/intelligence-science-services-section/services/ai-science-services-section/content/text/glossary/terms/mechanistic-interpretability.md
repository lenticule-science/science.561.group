Mechanistic interpretability treats a trained network's weights as a program to be read rather than a black box to be probed only from the outside, on the premise — argued in Olah et al.'s ["Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits"](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/zoom-in/) (Distill, 2020) — that features are the fundamental unit of the network and that circuits, the weighted subgraphs linking features across layers, are the right unit of explanation, with related features and circuits recurring ("universally") across otherwise-unrelated models.

The program's central obstacle is superposition: because a network can represent more sparse features than it has neurons or residual-stream dimensions, individual neurons often respond to several unrelated concepts at once (polysemanticity), which Elhage et al.'s ["Toy Models of Superposition"](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html) (2022) demonstrates and formalizes in small synthetic ReLU networks. The proposed remedy is dictionary learning via sparse autoencoders, which Bricken et al.'s ["Towards Monosemanticity"](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features/index.html) (2023) show can recover largely monosemantic — single-concept — features from a one-layer transformer's superposed activations, a result Templeton et al.'s ["Scaling Monosemanticity"](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/) (2024) extend to a production frontier model, Claude 3 Sonnet, recovering tens of millions of features. A parallel line of circuit-level work, Olsson et al.'s ["In-context Learning and Induction Heads"](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html) (2022), attributes a substantial share of transformer in-context learning to a specific two-head circuit pattern.

With the exception of the founding Distill paper, this arc runs almost entirely through Anthropic's Transformer Circuits Thread — an industry-lab publication venue without external peer review — a tier distinction this library treats as load-bearing rather than incidental, and one counterweighted by peer-reviewed methodological skepticism about what any sub-symbolic probing method (feature-based or classifier-based) can actually establish about a model's causal computation.
