**Formation-duration protective prioritization (FDPP)** names the proposed descriptive effect in which knowing that an item required longer to *form* increases the chance it will be selected for protection. The corresponding term **formation-duration protection bias** is reserved for a demonstrated case in which that cue causes a worse choice **relative to an explicit and legitimate decision objective**, with relevant historical value, replacement delay, rarity, hazard and other affected objects held appropriately in view.

This is an **untested candidate**, not a published diagnostic construct or a synonym of the sunk-cost effect. Formation duration differs from elapsed *age* after formation, the age of an item's lineage, labor expended in its making, time until replacement, and an original object's distinctive history.

A seed-and-rock case motivates the proposal: moving a reportedly water-sensitive rock away from a plate of drying seeds may protect the rock while unintentionally leaving the seeds vulnerable. This single report does not identify which mechanism drove the action or establish that any loss occurred. The key discriminator is whether experimentally varied formation duration affects protective choice after legitimate values and competing-hazard awareness are considered.

**Scientific source and experimental falsifiers:** [Formation Duration and Protective Choice](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/information-science-services-section/services/intelligence-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/10/16/2026-10-16-formation-duration-and-protective-choice.md), Lenticule Science working paper, 16 October 2026. The paper distinguishes existing evidence on effort heuristics, object history, rational irreversibility and goal shielding from its original hypotheses.
