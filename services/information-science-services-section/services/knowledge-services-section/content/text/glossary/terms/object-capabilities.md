An object capability is a reference that designates one object or move and carries the authority to use it. It cannot be forged, it can be narrowed (attenuated) and handed on (delegated), and it is the only authority a holder has: there is no ambient authority and no access-control list. The tradition runs from Dennis and Van Horn (1966) through Hydra, KeyKOS, EROS and seL4 to Miller's E and the OCapN protocol family that Spritely Goblins implements.

The Group realizes object capabilities as capability assertions (term `capability-assertion`, in the computer-science section): grant, attenuate, delegate, revoke and present records in a witnessed journal feed. A holder's standing for a grant has four values: held, refused, contested, unevidenced. Attenuation never widens a grant, delegation names who answers for it, revocation is an assertion read uncached at every authorization, and authority and the record of action are separate witnessed logs. CapTP, with sturdyrefs, sessions, vats, handoff gifts and pipelines, is the transport and is realized as pure values with statement logs in the networking section's object-capability module.

Federation between nodes carries these assertions inside ActivityPub++ (term `semioverse`, in the engineering section) activities; a peer is a holder like any other. The theory, the comparison with Goblins and the measurement of the running nodes are in the note of 2026-10-09 listed under sources.
