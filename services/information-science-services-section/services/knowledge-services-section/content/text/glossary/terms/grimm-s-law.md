***Grimm's Law***, also known as the First Germanic Consonant Shift, is a liguistic principle that describes the phonological changes that transformed [Proto-Indo-European language](../../../../../../../engineering-science-services-section/services/computer-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/proto-indo-european-language.md) into [Proto-Germanic language](proto-germanic-language.md).

First [described](../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/describing.md) by [Rasmus Rask](../../../../../communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rasmus-rask.md) and later formalized by [Jacob Grimm](jacob-grimm.md). \[cite:@Grimm1822\], Grimm's Law introduced the concept that pjhonological changes are regular and predictable.
