An [environment](./environment.md) constructed with [digital computer|digital computers](digital computer|digital computers) or through [imagination](imagination).

## Additional material from the Google Drive library

A **virtual environment** is an environment constructed through computation or imagination rather than through physical material. In computing, virtual environments are built with [digital computers](./digital-computer.md) — they range from text-based spaces like [MUDs](../../technology/disciplines/computing/topics/multi-user-dungeons/mud.md) to graphical simulations to the abstract environments of programming language runtimes. In imaginative practice, a virtual environment is any coherent space that exists through shared description: a tabletop role-playing game setting, a collectively imagined landscape, a fictional world sustained by narrative.

What makes an environment *virtual* is not that it is fake or lesser but that its structure is maintained by something other than direct physical causation. A virtual environment's walls hold because the system (or the shared fiction) says they hold, not because of material resistance. This gives virtual environments a flexibility that physical environments lack — they can be reshaped, forked, or suspended — but also a fragility: they exist only as long as something sustains them.
