Emma Goldman (1869–1940) was a Lithuanian-born American [anarchist](../../sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md), writer, and organizer whose work synthesized feminism, [labor](../../sociology/terms/labor.md) radicalism, anti-militarism, and [anarcho-communism](/sociology/terms/anarchism.md) into a comprehensive practice of resistance. She was deported from the United States in 1919 for her opposition to conscription and spent the remainder of her life in exile.

## Core ideas

- **Anarchism as lived practice**: Goldman insisted that [anarchism](../../sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md) was not a distant political goal but a way of living — the [refusal](../../sociology/terms/refusal.md) to reproduce domination in personal relationships, sexual life, education, and daily conduct.
- **Feminism within [anarchism](../../sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md)**: Goldman critiqued both the patriarchal structures of [the state](../../sociology/terms/the-state.md) and the patriarchal tendencies within the [anarchist](../../sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md) movement itself. She argued that women's liberation required not just legal rights but the transformation of the social relations that produce gender subordination.
- **Critique of suffrage and reformism**: Goldman opposed women's suffrage not from anti-feminism but from the [anarchist](../../sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md) position that the ballot cannot dismantle structures it is designed to maintain.
- **Free speech and political repression**: Goldman's career was defined by state repression — arrests, deportation, censorship — and her insistence on speaking and organizing regardless.

## Notable works

- *Anarchism and Other Essays* (1910)
- *Living My Life* (1931, autobiography)
- *The Social Significance of the Modern Drama* (1914)
