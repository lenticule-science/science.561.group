*Cannibal Metaphysics: For a Post-Structural Anthropology* (*Métaphysiques cannibales*, 2009; English translation by Peter Skafish, 2014) is a book by [Eduardo Viveiros de Castro](./eduardo-viveiros-de-castro.md) that synthesizes his program of using Amerindian thought as a tool for philosophy rather than an object of anthropological study. The "cannibal" of the title refers to the Amerindian practice of incorporating the enemy's perspective — and [Viveiros de Castro](./eduardo-viveiros-de-castro.md) proposes that anthropology should do the same with Indigenous concepts, allowing them to transform Western categories.

The book develops [perspectivism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/perspectivism.md) and [multinaturalism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/multinaturalism.md) into a broader philosophical program, engaging with Deleuze and [Guattari](./felix-guattari.md)'s concept of multiplicity, Lévi-Strauss's structural anthropology, and Latour's actor-network theory. [Viveiros de Castro](./eduardo-viveiros-de-castro.md) argues that Amerindian ontologies are not pre-philosophical but offer a different and in some respects more powerful [metaphysics](../../philosophy/terms/metaphysics.md) than the substance-based frameworks of Western philosophy — one in which relations are ontologically prior to terms, variation is prior to identity, and becoming is prior to being.
