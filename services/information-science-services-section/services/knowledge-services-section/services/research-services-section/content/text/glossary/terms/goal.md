A goal in a command context carries the library definition's predicates (direction, temporal-kind, owning-agent, success-conditions) plus operational predicates: its status, what scope it operates at (paradigm-level vs. operational), who the captain is, what ship it belongs to, and what plans/projects/objectives serve it.

The `scope` predicate distinguishes paradigm goals (ordering-first-computation) from system goals (system-architecture-refactor) from operational goals (operations-loop). Scope governs how goals are evaluated — paradigm goals have indefinite horizons and external success criteria (peer review, adoption); operational goals have near-term horizons and internal criteria (the system works).

Goals are the top of the work hierarchy within a ship. The endeavor governs the goal-set; the captain selects and prioritizes goals within the endeavor's scope.
