Thomas Hanna (1928–1990) was an American philosopher and movement educator who coined the modern usage of the term [somatics](/medicine/concepts/somatics/index.md) to name the field concerned with the body as experienced from within. Hanna studied philosophy at the University of Chicago under [Richard McKeon](richard-mckeon.md) and later trained with [Moshe Feldenkrais](./moshe-feldenkrais.md), whose work on movement and awareness shaped Hanna's approach to bodily education.

Hanna founded the Novato Institute for Somatic Research and Training in California and developed Clinical Somatic Education, a hands-on method for resolving chronic muscular pain through [pandiculation](../../../../../../../../../../natural-science-services-section/services/biological-science-services-section/services/medical-science-services-section/services/somatics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/pandiculation.md) — voluntary contraction followed by slow, controlled release. His central clinical concept, sensory-motor amnesia (SMA), describes the condition in which habitual muscular contraction drops below the threshold of conscious awareness. SMA isn't a muscular problem but a cortical one: the brain has stopped voluntarily controlling the contraction pattern.

Hanna identified three reflex patterns — the green-light reflex (posterior contraction from the Landau arousal response), the red-light reflex (anterior contraction from the startle response), and the trauma reflex (lateral contraction from injury or asymmetric use) — as the primary sources of SMA. His method addresses these patterns through guided [somatic awareness](../../../../../../../../../../natural-science-services-section/services/biological-science-services-section/services/medical-science-services-section/services/somatics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/somatic-awareness.md) and pandiculation.
