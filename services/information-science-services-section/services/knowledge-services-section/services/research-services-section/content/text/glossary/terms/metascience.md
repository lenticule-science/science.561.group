**Metascience**, also called **meta-research**, is the field that studies the methods, reporting, verification, evaluation, and incentive structures of scientific research using the same empirical tools that science applies to its other subjects.
The label was given its modern, discipline-naming form by Ioannidis, Fanelli, Dunne, and Goodman in ["Meta-research: Evaluation and Improvement of Research Methods and Practices"](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002264) (PLoS Biology, 2015), which announced Stanford's Meta-Research Innovation Center (METRICS) and grouped the field's concerns into methods, reporting and dissemination, reproducibility and verification, evaluation, and incentives.

The field's founding argument predates the label by a decade.
John Ioannidis's essay ["Why Most Published Research Findings Are False"](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124) (PLoS Medicine, 2005) used a model of study power, effect size, number of tested relationships, analytic flexibility, and field-wide bias to argue that, for many common research designs, a published claim is more likely to be false than true.
That argument, together with empirical work on [publication bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publication_bias) and on questionable research practices, motivated the large-scale replication projects and reform proposals that make up the bulk of the [replication crisis](./replication-crisis.md) literature - metascience is the broader field of study; the replication crisis is metascience's most publicized finding.

Not every metascientist accepts "crisis" as the right word for what the field has found: Daniele Fanelli's ["Is Science Really Facing a Reproducibility Crisis, and Do We Need It To?"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29531051/) (PNAS, 2018) argues the evidence is concentrated in particular fields and journals and may not generalize to science as a whole.
Two of the field's central reform proposals, [preregistration](./preregistration.md) and registered reports, and its central diagnostic category, [questionable research practices](./questionable-research-practices.md), are covered as sibling terms in this glossary.

This term draws on the library's [Study of Metascience and Replication](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-metascience-and-replication.md), which carries forward as a gap that the metascience label's own founding paper has four authors, not the three sometimes cited.
