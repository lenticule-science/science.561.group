Oliver Sacks (1933–2015) was a British-American neurologist and author whose clinical narratives documented the lived experience of neurological conditions — not as diagnostic categories but as transformations of the person's relationship to their own body and world. Sacks practiced neurology in New York and wrote extensively for both clinical and popular audiences.

Sacks' case studies are relevant to [somatics](/medicine/concepts/somatics/index.md) because they document what happens when the systems that somatic practices train — [proprioception](../../../../../../../../../../biological-science-services-section/services/medical-science-services-section/services/somatics-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/proprioception.md), [interoception](../../../../../../../../../../biological-science-services-section/services/medical-science-services-section/services/somatics-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/interoception.md), motor control — break down. His account of "the disembodied woman" (in *The Man Who Mistook His Wife for a Hat*) describes a patient who lost proprioception through peripheral neuropathy and had to visually monitor her own body to maintain posture and movement. The case demonstrates that proprioception isn't a background sense but a constitutive condition for inhabiting a body.

## Notable works

- *The Man Who Mistook His Wife for a Hat* (1985)
- *Awakenings* (1973)
- *An Anthropologist on Mars* (1995)
- *On the Move: A Life* (2015)
