Lisa Duggan is a historian and cultural analyst at New York University whose work connects queer theory to the analysis of [neoliberalism](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/services/political-economy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neoliberalism.md), political economy, and the history of sexuality in the United States.

## Core ideas

- **[Homonormativity](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/homonormativity.md)**: in "The New Homonormativity" (2002), Duggan coined the term to name a politics that does not contest dominant [heteronormative](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/heteronormativity.md) assumptions and institutions but upholds and sustains them, while promising the possibility of a demobilized gay constituency and a privatized, depoliticized gay culture anchored in domesticity and consumption. Homonormativity is not simply assimilation but the specific form assimilation takes under [neoliberalism](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/services/political-economy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/neoliberalism.md): the transformation of a liberation movement into a consumer market and a rights-bearing individual subject.
- **The twilight of equality**: in *The Twilight of Equality?: Neoliberalism, Cultural Politics, and the Attack on Democracy* (2003), Duggan analyzed how culture wars over sexuality functioned as a mechanism for advancing neoliberal economic restructuring — how battles over "family values" provided the emotional and political energy for policies of privatization, deregulation, and welfare retrenchment.

## Notable works

- *The Twilight of Equality?: Neoliberalism, Cultural Politics, and the Attack on Democracy* (2003)
- *Sapphic Slashers: Sex, Violence, and American Modernity* (2000)
