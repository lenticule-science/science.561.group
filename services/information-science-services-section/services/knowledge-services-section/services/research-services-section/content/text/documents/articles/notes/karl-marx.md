Karl Heinrich Marx (1818--1883) was a German philosopher, economist, and political theorist. His work analyzed [capitalism](../../sociology/terms/capitalism.md) as a historical system driven by [class struggle](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/class-struggle.md) and the extraction of [surplus value](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/surplus-value.md) from [labor](../../sociology/terms/labor.md). Marx's writings have been adopted, contested, and extended across traditions far beyond his own framework, and his legacy is inseparable from the critiques that followed -- particularly from anti-colonial, feminist, and [abolitionist](../../sociology/terms/abolition.md) thinkers who identified what his analysis excluded or distorted.

## Core ideas

- **[Historical materialism](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/historical-materialism.md)**: social structures, legal systems, and cultural forms arise from and change with the material conditions of production. History moves through the contradictions between productive forces and the social relations that organize them. Marx treated this as a method of analysis, not a law of inevitable progress, though many of his inheritors read it as the latter.
- **Surplus value and exploitation**: under [capitalism](../../sociology/terms/capitalism.md), workers produce more value than they receive in wages. The difference -- [surplus value](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/surplus-value.md) -- is appropriated by the owners of capital. This extraction is not incidental but structural: it is the mechanism through which capital accumulates.
- **Class struggle**: the history of existing societies is a history of conflict between classes defined by their relation to the means of production. Marx argued that the working class, by virtue of its position, had the capacity to abolish class society altogether.
- **Primitive accumulation**: the violent processes -- [enclosure](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/services/political-economy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/enclosure.md), colonization, enslavement, dispossession -- through which [capitalism](../../sociology/terms/capitalism.md) created the conditions for its own emergence. Marx treated these as historical preconditions; later theorists, notably [Rosa Luxemburg](./rosa-luxemburg.md) and [Silvia Federici](./silvia-federici.md), argued that such accumulation is ongoing rather than confined to [capitalism](../../sociology/terms/capitalism.md)'s origins. This critique connects to [racial capitalism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/black-radical-tradition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/racial-capitalism.md) and [settler colonialism](../../sociology/terms/settler-colonialism.md).
- **Critique of political economy**: Marx did not propose a rival economic theory so much as a critique of the categories economists used -- value, price, [labor](../../sociology/terms/labor.md), commodity -- showing that they naturalized historically specific social relations.

## In this vault

The vault's treatment of Marx draws on emsenn's essay "[From science to story, reading the late Marx as morality](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/from-science-to-story-reading-the-late-marx-as-morality.md)," which argues that Marx's late openness -- his ethnological notebooks, his interest in communal [property](../../sociology/terms/property.md) forms outside Western Europe -- has been converted from an unfinished research program into a moral narrative of redemption for Marxism. The essay calls for recovering the scientific questions Marx posed rather than celebrating his change of heart.

Marx is treated here as one contributor among many, not as the origin of radical thought. His framework has been critiqued and extended by [Antonio Gramsci](./antonio-gramsci.md) on [hegemony](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/services/gramscian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hegemony.md), by [Cedric Robinson](./cedric-robinson.md) on [racial capitalism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/black-radical-tradition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/racial-capitalism.md), by [Frantz Fanon](./frantz-fanon.md) on [colonialism](../../sociology/terms/colonialism.md), and by [anarchist](../../sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md) thinkers including [Mikhail Bakunin](./mikhail-bakunin.md) on [the state](../../sociology/terms/the-state.md).

- *Grundrisse* (written 1857--1858, published 1939--1941)
- *The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte* (1852)
