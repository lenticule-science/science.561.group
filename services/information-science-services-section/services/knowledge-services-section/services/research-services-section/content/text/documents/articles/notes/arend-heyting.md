Arend Heyting (9 May 1898 – 9 July 1980) was a Dutch mathematician and logician, a student of [L. E. J. Brouwer](./l-e-j-brouwer.md), who formalized [intuitionistic logic](../../../../../../../../math-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/teaching-articles/explanations/intuitionistic-logic.md) and introduced the algebraic structures now known as [Heyting algebras](../../mathematics/concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md). Where [Brouwer](./l-e-j-brouwer.md) had developed [intuitionism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/analytic-philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/mathematical-constructivism.md) as a philosophy of mathematics grounded in mental construction — and had resisted formalization on principle — Heyting provided the formal logical system that made intuitionism accessible to mathematical and logical study.

## Core ideas

- **Formalization of [intuitionistic logic](../../../../../../../../math-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/teaching-articles/explanations/intuitionistic-logic.md)**: Heyting's axiomatization (1930) defined a propositional calculus that captures the logical principles valid under constructive interpretation — all the laws of classical logic except the [law of excluded middle](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/analytic-philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/law-of-excluded-middle.md) and related principles. Intuitionistic implication, disjunction, and negation all acquire constructive meanings: a proof of "A or B" requires a proof of A or a proof of B; a proof of "A implies B" requires a method for transforming any proof of A into a proof of B.
- **BHK interpretation**: the [Brouwer](./l-e-j-brouwer.md)-Heyting-Kolmogorov interpretation gives meaning to the logical connectives in terms of proofs: a proof of a conjunction is a pair of proofs, a proof of a disjunction is a proof of one disjunct together with an indication of which, a proof of an implication is a construction that transforms proofs of the antecedent into proofs of the consequent.
- **[Heyting algebras](../../mathematics/concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md)**: the algebraic structures that model [intuitionistic logic](../../../../../../../../math-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/teaching-articles/explanations/intuitionistic-logic.md), generalizing Boolean algebras by replacing the complement with a pseudo-complement. Every [Heyting algebra](../../mathematics/concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) is a bounded distributive [lattice](../../../../../../../../math-services-section/services/order-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/lattice.md) with an implication operation satisfying: a ∧ x ≤ b if and only if x ≤ a → b.

## Significance for this research

[Heyting algebras](../../mathematics/concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) are the algebraic foundation of the [semiotic universe](../../mathematics/objects/universes/semiotic-universe/index.md). The complete [Heyting algebra](../../mathematics/concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) H on which the [semiotic](../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe is built provides the [lattice](../../../../../../../../math-services-section/services/order-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/lattice.md) of semantic values — the space of possible meanings. The choice of Heyting rather than Boolean algebras reflects the intuitionistic commitment: meaning is constructed through interpretive acts, not given as a pre-existing binary partition of truth and falsity. The implication operation of the [Heyting algebra](../../mathematics/concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) models the interpretive relation between [signs](../../../../../../../../communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md), and the three [closure operators](../../mathematics/concepts/closure-operator/index.md) (semantic, syntactic, fusion) are the formal mechanism by which construction achieves fixed-point stability.
