Lee Edelman (born 1953) is an American queer theorist and literary scholar at Tufts University whose work analyzes how the figure of the Child organizes political [discourse](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/foucauldian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/discourse.md) and forecloses alternatives to heteronormative futurity. His central concept, [reproductive futurism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/political-philosophy-services-section/services/queer-theory-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reproductive-futurism.md), names the [ideological](../../sociology/terms/ideology.md) framework in which the future is always figured as the Child — and in which any politics that refuses this figuration is rendered unthinkable.

## Core ideas

- **[Reproductive futurism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/political-philosophy-services-section/services/queer-theory-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reproductive-futurism.md)**: the political logic that organizes collective life around the image of the Child — the innocent, vulnerable figure for whose sake all politics must be conducted. Reproductive futurism is not about actual children but about the symbolic function of the Child as the guarantee of futurity. Every political position must justify itself in terms of its effects on "our children," and any position that refuses this justification is coded as anti-social, [nihilistic](../../philosophy/disciplines/nihilism/index.md), or monstrous.
- **The death drive and queer negativity**: drawing on Lacan, Edelman argues that queerness — as a structural position, not merely a sexual identity — names the [refusal](../../sociology/terms/refusal.md) of [reproductive futurism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/political-philosophy-services-section/services/queer-theory-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reproductive-futurism.md). The queer figure is consigned to the place of the death drive: the force that disrupts the symbolic order's fantasy of continuity. Rather than rehabilitating queerness within the framework of futurity (gay marriage, adoption, "we're just like you"), Edelman proposes embracing the negativity that the symbolic order assigns to queerness.
- **Sinthomosexuality**: Edelman's neologism for the queer figure who embodies the disruption of the social order's fantasy of wholeness — not a person who happens to be gay but the structural position of the one who refuses to participate in the reproduction of the social order through the figure of the Child.

## Significance for this research

Edelman's framework appears in emsenn's analysis of how political [discourse](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/foucauldian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/discourse.md) is organized around affective investment in futurity. [Reproductive futurism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/political-philosophy-services-section/services/queer-theory-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reproductive-futurism.md) is a form of [feeling rules](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/affect-theory-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/feeling-rules.md): it prescribes which political affects are legitimate (hope, protection, investment in the future) and which are illegitimate ([refusal](../../sociology/terms/refusal.md), negativity, the insistence that there is no future worth reproducing). In "On white-supremacist covid-eugenicist queers" (2025-09-23), emsenn connects Edelman's analysis to the question of how COVID-era public health [discourse](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/foucauldian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/discourse.md) organized itself around the protection of vulnerable populations while structurally abandoning them.
