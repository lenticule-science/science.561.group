Franco "Bifo" Berardi (born 1949) is an Italian autonomist philosopher, media theorist, and activist. He was a participant in the Italian autonomia movement of the 1970s and has since developed a body of work that connects [autonomist](../../sociology/schools/autonomism/index.md) political theory with media theory, [semiotics](../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md), and psychopathology.

## Core ideas

- **Semio-[capitalism](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/capitalism.md)**: Berardi's central concept names the economic system in which the production of [signs](../../../../../../../../communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) — information, images, affects, attention — is the primary source of value. In semio-[capitalism](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/anarchism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/capitalism.md), the commodity is not material but [semiotic](../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md): what is produced, circulated, and consumed is meaning itself. This transforms [labor](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md) from physical activity to cognitive and affective work, and transforms exploitation from the extraction of physical surplus to the capture of attention and creativity.
- **Cognitive [labor](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md) and exhaustion**: Berardi argues that the shift to cognitive [labor](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/labor.md) produces specific pathologies. When the mind itself becomes the factory, there is no outside to retreat to — no distinction between work time and life time. The result is not the revolutionary subject [autonomism](../../sociology/terms/operaismo.md) anticipated but collective depression, panic, and attentional collapse.
- **Slow cancellation of the future**: Berardi describes a cultural condition in which the future has been canceled — not because catastrophe has arrived but because the capacity to imagine alternatives has been exhausted. This is not pessimism but a diagnosis: the [semiotic](../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) saturation of the present has consumed the imaginative resources needed to think beyond it.
- **Insurrection and [poetry](../../writing/disciplines/poetry/index.md)**: against this diagnosis, Berardi proposes that insurrection is not a political program but a reactivation of the capacity for collective imagination. [Poetry](../../writing/disciplines/poetry/index.md) — understood not as a literary genre but as the excess of language over its informational content — is the mode in which this reactivation occurs.
