Scientometrics is the academic discipline concerned with quantitative study of science, scholarship, and their measurement -- encompassing citation analysis (often called bibliometrics), research-output counting, and the design and critique of indicators used to evaluate researchers, journals, and institutions.

## Founding citation-indexing work

Eugene Garfield's Garfield, E. (1955), "Citation Indexes for Science: A New Dimension in Documentation through Association of Ideas," *Science* 122(3159):108-111, proposed indexing scientific literature by citation relationships between papers rather than by subject classification alone.
Garfield founded the Institute for Scientific Information in 1960; its Science Citation Index made possible the calculation of the [journal impact factor](./h-index.md).

Derek de Solla Price is scientometrics' other widely cited founding figure: *Little Science, Big Science* (Columbia University Press, 1963); "Networks of Scientific Papers," *Science* (1965), an early empirical demonstration that citation networks follow power-law distributions; and "A general theory of bibliometric and other cumulative advantage processes," *Journal of the American Society for Information Science* (1976), formalizing preferential-attachment ("Matthew effect") citation dynamics.
[UNVERIFIED: this study confirmed these primary works and de Solla Price's strong secondary characterization as "founder" (posthumous 1984 ASIS Research Award, the field's eponymous journal *Scientometrics*), but did not verify one canonical peer-reviewed source explicitly naming him founder, as opposed to it being retrospective field consensus.]

## Metrics, their misuse, and reform

Garfield's infrastructure enabled the journal impact factor, which has a well-documented misuse literature: Seglen (1997) argued against its use for individual-researcher evaluation given wide within-journal citation variance; Rossner et al. (2007) documented its statistical inappropriateness as a mean of skewed data; and Wilhite and Fong (2012) documented editors coercing authors into self-citation.
Jorge Hirsch's [h-index](./h-index.md) (2005) was proposed partly as a correction to these distortions.
The resulting reform movement -- DORA (2012) and the Leiden Manifesto (Hicks et al. 2015, *Nature* 520:429-431) -- and Edwards & Roy's (2017) explicit Goodhart's-law critique of research-evaluation metrics are documented in the [study of research synthesis and scientometrics](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-research-synthesis-and-scientometrics.md), along with open scientometric-data infrastructure (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar) that now parallels Garfield's commercial model.
See also [FAIR data principles](./fair-data-principles.md) for the related but distinct data-infrastructure literature.
