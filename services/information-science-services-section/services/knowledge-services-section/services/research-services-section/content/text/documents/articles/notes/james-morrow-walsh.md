James Morrow Walsh (1840–1905; born Prescott, Ontario — biographical basics attested-secondary via the Dictionary of Canadian Biography lineage of sources) was an original officer of the North-West Mounted Police from its 1873 founding; Fort Walsh in the Cypress Hills carried his name. From 1877 he was the NWMP officer at Wood Mountain responsible for the several thousand Lakota who had crossed the Medicine Line after the Greasy Grass, and his working relationship with Sitting Bull — small detachments riding into a camp that outnumbered them twentyfold, rules stated and largely kept on both sides — became the celebrated image of the Canadian border as law rather than war ("Sitting Bull's Boss," in the later romance).

The corpus's treatment (see the exile note and [north-west-mounted-police](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agent-science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/social-history-science-services-section/content/glossary/terms/north-west-mounted-police.md)) uses Walsh as the test of that romance, and the record cuts both ways. The mutual respect appears genuine; the restraint was real; no cavalry charge ever came. And: Walsh administered a policy he did not set — no reserve, no rations, wait out the buffalo — and when Ottawa judged him too sympathetic to Sitting Bull he was **reassigned away from Wood Mountain in 1880** (attested-secondary), the personal relationship removed precisely because it was working as a relationship rather than as pressure. He resigned in 1883; his later public career included appointment as the first Commissioner of the Yukon Territory (1897–98) during the gold rush; he died in 1905.

## Significance for this research

Walsh individualizes the corpus's claim that the Canadian and American treatments of the Lakota differed procedurally, not structurally. The man on the ground could be honorable; the filing system he served was running a starvation clock, and it removed him when his honor became friction. He is the northern counterpart of the corpus's finding that exclusion-powers are institutional dispositions — no officer's character, however real, alters where the power points.
