José Esteban Muñoz (1967–2013) was a Cuban-American performance studies scholar at New York University whose work centered the cultural production and survival strategies of queers of color. His two major books — *Disidentifications* (1999) and *Cruising Utopia* (2009) — define two poles of queer-of-color thought: tactical engagement with a hostile present, and insistence on a future that has not yet arrived.

## Core ideas

- **[Disidentification](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/disidentification.md)**: in *Disidentifications: Queers of Color and the Performance of Politics* (1999), Muñoz described a mode of navigating dominant [ideology](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ideology.md) that is neither assimilation (identification with the norm) nor rejection (counter-identification) but a third path — working on and against dominant codes simultaneously, recycling and repurposing encoded meaning. The concept was developed through analyses of queer-of-color performers, video artists, and writers.
- **Queer utopia**: in *Cruising Utopia: The Then and There of Queer Futurity* (2009), Muñoz argued against [Lee Edelman](./lee-edelman.md)'s antisocial thesis, insisting that queerness is not the negation of futurity but an insistence on a futurity that has not yet arrived. Queerness is "not yet here" — a horizon, not a present state. This is not naïve optimism but a political demand: the insistence that the present is not enough, that another world is necessary and possible.
