**Preregistration** is the practice of publicly, and often time-stamped, recording a study's hypotheses, design, and planned analysis before any data are collected.
Brian Nosek and colleagues argue, in ["The Preregistration Revolution"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1708274114) (PNAS, 2018), that its value lies specifically in separating confirmatory hypothesis testing from exploratory analysis - the process, not the paperwork, is what should improve the credibility of a reported result.
A later PNAS exchange, ["The Preregistration Revolution Needs to Distinguish between Predictions and Analyses"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6233109/), argues that distinction is harder to enforce in practice than the 2018 paper implies, so preregistration is better described as a widely adopted but still contested tool than as a settled solution.

**Registered reports** apply the same idea at the level of the journal rather than the individual study: a journal peer-reviews and conditionally accepts a study's introduction, hypotheses, and method before data collection, then publishes based on the question and design rather than on the direction or significance of the eventual result.
Chris Chambers introduced the format in ["Registered Reports: A New Publishing Initiative at Cortex"](https://linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/S0010945212003735) (Cortex, 2013) and later reviewed its spread across journals in ["The Registered Reports Revolution: Lessons in Cultural Reform"](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1740-9713.2019.01299.x) (Significance, 2019).

Both practices are proposed remedies for the upstream causes of the [replication crisis](./replication-crisis.md) - specifically for the undisclosed analytic flexibility described as [questionable research practices](./questionable-research-practices.md) - and are grouped, alongside other reforms, in Munafo and colleagues' ["A Manifesto for Reproducible Science"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-016-0021) (Nature Human Behaviour, 2017).
They are part of the broader field of [metascience](./metascience.md).

This term draws on the library's [Study of Metascience and Replication](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-metascience-and-replication.md), which proposes a preregistration-like practice for this library's own study notes: a short program note, written before a study begins, stating the question it is trying to answer and what kind of source would settle it.
