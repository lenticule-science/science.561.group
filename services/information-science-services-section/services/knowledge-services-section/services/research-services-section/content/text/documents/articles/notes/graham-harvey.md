Graham Harvey is a British scholar of religious studies whose work has been central to rehabilitating [animism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/animism.md) as a serious philosophical and anthropological category. A professor of Religious Studies at the Open University, Harvey has argued against the colonial-era dismissal of [animism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/animism.md) as "primitive" belief, proposing instead that [animism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/animism.md) names a [relational ontology](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/metaphysics-services-section/services/ontology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/relational-ontology.md) in which personhood extends to other-than-human beings — not as a projection of human qualities onto objects but as a recognition that the world is populated by many kinds of persons, only some of whom are human.

## Core ideas

- **New [animism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/animism.md)**: a reclamation of [animism](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/animism.md) from its Tylorian definition ("belief in spirits") toward a relational understanding — animists are people who recognize that the world is full of persons and that life is lived in relationship with them.
- **Relational personhood**: personhood is not a fixed [property](../../sociology/terms/property.md) of a special category of beings but a relational capacity. What makes something a person is how it engages in relations of [reciprocity](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/marxism-services-section/services/political-economy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reciprocity.md), communication, and mutual obligation.
- **Critique of Western naturalism**: the reduction of other-than-human beings to objects, resources, or mechanisms is not neutral description but a specific [ontological](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/metaphysics-services-section/services/ontology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ontology.md) commitment — one that enables [extractivism](../../../../../../../../../../natural-science-services-section/services/biological-science-services-section/services/ecology-services-section/services/climate-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/extractivism.md).

## Notable works

- *Animism: Respecting the Living World* (2005; 2nd ed. 2017)
- *The Handbook of Contemporary Animism* (editor, 2013)
- *Food, Sex, and Strangers: Understanding Religion as Everyday Life* (2013)
