Giorgio Agamben (born 1942) is an Italian philosopher whose work spans political philosophy, aesthetics, and [ontology](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/western-services-section/services/metaphysics-services-section/services/ontology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/ontology.md). He is best known for his analysis of sovereignty, [the state](../../sociology/terms/the-state.md) of exception, and bare life — the reduction of human existence to biological survival stripped of political standing. His work extends [Michel Foucault](./michel-foucault.md)'s analysis of [biopolitics](../../sociology/terms/biopolitics.md) by showing how sovereign power and [biopower](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/foucauldian-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/biopower.md) converge in the production of life that can be killed but not sacrificed.

## Core ideas

- **Bare life (nuda vita)**: human life reduced to its biological minimum — excluded from the political community while remaining subject to sovereign power. Bare life is not a natural condition but a product of sovereignty: the sovereign decides who counts as a political subject and who is reduced to mere biological existence. The refugee, the detainee, the stateless person exist as bare life: alive but without political standing.
- **Homo sacer**: a figure from Roman law — one who can be killed without it constituting homicide but cannot be sacrificed in religious ritual. Agamben uses this figure to analyze the structure of sovereign power: the sovereign relates to bare life through the capacity to kill without legal consequence. This is not an archaic remnant but the hidden foundation of modern political order.
- **State of exception**: the suspension of legal order by sovereign authority, which Agamben argues has become the dominant paradigm of modern governance rather than an extraordinary measure. When [the state](../../sociology/terms/the-state.md) declares an emergency — war, pandemic, security threat — it suspends the law in the name of preserving the law. Agamben's claim is that this exception has become the rule: modern governance operates through permanent emergency.
- **The camp**: the spatial realization of [the state](../../sociology/terms/the-state.md) of exception, where bare life is produced and governed directly. Agamben argues the camp — from the Nazi concentration camp through contemporary refugee processing centers — is the fundamental political space of modernity, not the city. The camp is where the exception becomes the norm and human beings are reduced to bare life as a matter of routine administration.

## Significance for this research

Agamben's framework intersects with emsenn's analysis at several points. His concept of bare life connects to [Achille Mbembe](./achille-mbembe.md)'s [necropolitics](../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/black-philosophy-services-section/services/abolition-services-section/services/state-repression-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/necropolitics.md) — [Mbembe](./achille-mbembe.md) extends Agamben by showing how bare life is produced specifically through racialized and colonial structures, not merely through the abstract logic of sovereignty. Agamben's state of exception connects to emsenn's analysis of [harm governance](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/harm-governance.md): the permanent emergency in which political action must be administered to be legitimate, and care is confused with risk management.

The concept of the camp as the political space of modernity also connects to emsenn's analysis of [organized abandonment](../../sociology/terms/organized-abandonment.md): zones where populations are managed through the withdrawal of infrastructure and the reduction of political standing to biological survival. [Ruth Wilson Gilmore](./ruth-wilson-gilmore.md)'s analysis of California's prison system as organized abandonment is, in Agamben's terms, an analysis of the camp as the organizing principle of [the state](../../sociology/terms/the-state.md)'s relationship to surplus populations.
