A systematic review is a research-synthesis method that answers a predefined question by exhaustively and reproducibly searching, selecting, and appraising the existing literature, as distinct from an ordinary narrative literature review, which need not document its search strategy or apply consistent selection criteria.
It is often (but not always) paired with [meta-analysis](./meta-analysis.md) for quantitative pooling of the studies it identifies.

## Institutionalization and reporting standards

The Cochrane Collaboration, founded in 1993 under Iain Chalmers's leadership, institutionalized systematic review production for healthcare evidence at scale; its *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions* (currently version 6.5, 2024, senior-edited by Julian Higgins and James Thomas) is the field's principal methodological reference, covering study selection, bias assessment, and meta-analytic methods across 26 chapters.

Reporting of systematic reviews was itself found to be inconsistent enough to require a standard: Moher, D., Liberati, A., Tetzlaff, J., Altman, D.G., and The PRISMA Group (2009), "Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses: The PRISMA Statement," *PLoS Medicine* 6(7):e1000097, established a checklist and flow diagram now near-universally required by journals for systematic review submissions.
It was superseded by Page, M.J., McKenzie, J.E., et al. (2021), "The PRISMA 2020 statement: an updated guideline for reporting systematic reviews," *BMJ* 372:n71, which expanded the checklist to reflect methodological advances since 2009 (risk-of-bias tools, certainty-of-evidence assessment, search-automation reporting).
A citation to "the PRISMA statement" without a year should be read as ambiguous between these two versions.

## Living systematic reviews

Elliott, J.H., Turner, T., Clavisi, O., Thomas, J., Higgins, J.P.T., Mavergames, C., and Gruen, R.L. (2014), "Living Systematic Reviews: An Emerging Opportunity to Narrow the Evidence-Practice Gap," *PLoS Medicine* 11(2):e1001603, proposed continually updated reviews -- incorporating new evidence as published rather than being fixed at publication -- as a response to primary-research output outpacing periodic review-update cycles.
This is the founding citation for "living systematic review" as a named method.

## What a systematic review is not

A systematic review's rigor is about process (documented search, transparent selection, consistent appraisal), not about the resulting certainty of evidence, which the [GRADE framework](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-research-synthesis-and-scientometrics.md#4-grade-and-evidence-hierarchies) formalizes separately (no standalone GRADE glossary term exists yet; this is a gap), and not about where any given study sits in a simple design-based hierarchy, which has itself been the subject of methodological critique.
See the [study of research synthesis and scientometrics](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-research-synthesis-and-scientometrics.md) for the fuller literature review, including the parachute-paper critique of rigid evidence hierarchies and the [UNVERIFIED] gaps around DORA's exact release date and de Solla Price's founder citation.
