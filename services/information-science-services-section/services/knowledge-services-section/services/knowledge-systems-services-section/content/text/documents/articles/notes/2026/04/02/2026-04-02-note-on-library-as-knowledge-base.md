The library serves the endeavor by grounding its knowledge in real disciplines. Every term defined, every text written, every frontmatter relation added makes the knowledge graph richer — and critically, makes respond() compositions richer. The library is published on the web and should be useful to any reader, not just the repo's agents.

## Current state (2026-04-06)

**Scale:** 38K+ entities, 562K+ facts in the EAVT knowledge graph. 26+ domains spanning humanities (philosophy, sociology, linguistics), science (biology, ecology, medicine, math, information, relationality), and applied domains (domesticity, gardening, computing).

**Quality:** Foundational terms (thing, quality, state, act, container, relation) recently enriched to 15-44 predicates each. respond() tested on real fibers with 62 agreed predicates. The convergent enrichment plan is actively improving fiber richness from both bottom (foundational terms) and top (operational records).

**Gaps:** Domains needed by the charter but not yet grounded — land stewardship, logistics, legal/rights, education/pedagogy. See the domain-coverage objective.

## Mathematical grounding

The library IS the semantic fiber sheaf the system computes over. Each domain is a sub-topos; each term's frontmatter IS its fiber (the set of predicates known true of that concept). respond(term-A, term-B) produces non-⊥ only when both terms have rich enough fibers — meaning enough well-defined predicates with shared vocabulary. The convergent enrichment method (enrich from bottom and top, let the middle converge) is the strategy for making the library computationally useful, not just humanly readable.
