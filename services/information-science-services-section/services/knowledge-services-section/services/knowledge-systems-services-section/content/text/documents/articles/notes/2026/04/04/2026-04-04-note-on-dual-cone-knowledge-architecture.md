## Captain's prompt (2026-04-04)

> A bunch of our library is basically a cone built out from the apex of math. There's like, breaks and it's sloppy but generally, shit builds up from basic math (or maybe mereology) and goes from there, building more and more complex of things from those basic things. But the math stuff, here, isn't the bottom: it in fact then is supported by relationality, which builds its OWN cone of concepts out from its own basics and then into own "world" of shit.

## The idea

The library has two cones, not one:

**Cone 1: Standard disciplines.** Apex at foundational math/mereology, expanding outward through physics, chemistry, biology, ecology, sociology, governance, work, etc. Each layer builds on the one below. This is the bulk of `library/theories/` — it's a conventional knowledge hierarchy, sloppy at the edges but structurally sound.

**Cone 2: Relationality.** Its own apex (the claim that things exist only through acts of relating), its own foundational concepts (relational entity, relational operation, the dual nuclei, the nuclear quartet), building outward into its own "world" — the relational computer, the semiotic universe, the generative comonad. This is mostly in `operations/engineering/triage/content/relationality/` — NOT YET integrated into the library.

The cones aren't parallel — Cone 2 sits UNDER Cone 1. The standard disciplines assume entities exist and then relate. Relationality says: the relating is prior, entities are constituted by their relations. So the math that sits at the apex of Cone 1 is itself supported by the relational ontology of Cone 2.

This means integrating the triage's relationality material into the library isn't just "processing backlog." It's installing the foundation under the existing building. Every term in the library that says "X is a thing that..." implicitly depends on what "thing" and "is" mean — and relationality is where those are defined.
