A **trace** is the residual deformation left in a [field](./field.md) by a [praxon](./praxon.md). It is the mark of past modulation — the structural memory that shapes future recursive process.

Every praxon leaves a trace. Every trace modulates future [pressure](./pressure.md). Traces are subject to decay: they lose recursive force over time, shaped by field volatility and praxon structure. Decay determines how long a trace persists and how it reshapes the field. Through decay, traces enable reuse — old patterns fade enough for new ones to form. Through persistence, traces generate charge — accumulated readiness for further emission.

Traces are not records. They are active residues. They fold back into the field, conditioning where pressure accumulates, where paths form, where new praxons emerge. A trace-dense region has high narrative residue: every element carries the mark of prior modulation.

When traces overlap recursively, they create folds — new local field shapes with unpredictable charge. Folded regions may emit recursive praxons. When a trace recursively triggers similar praxons, a refold occurs — the loop-behavior of field-form becoming path again.

In the recursive grammar, meaning is a trace. Every meaning decays.
