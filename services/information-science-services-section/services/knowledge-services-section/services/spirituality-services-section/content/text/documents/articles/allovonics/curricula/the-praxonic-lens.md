This lesson introduces [praxonics](../concepts/praxonics.md) as a way of reading systems. Where the [foundations lesson](./foundations-of-recursive-modulation.md) introduced the structural vocabulary, this lesson shows how to apply it: how to see [pressure](../terms/pressure.md), [trace](../terms/trace.md), and drift in systems around you.

## Pressure first

The praxonic paradigm begins with a commitment: reality emerges from active tensions, not static elements. Meaning, identity, and action are effects of pressure. Before there are actors, there are gradients. Before there are choices, there are paths of least resistance. Before there are structures, there are deformations.

To read a system praxonically is to ask: where is the pressure? What gradients produce the tensions I can see? What is being strained, and what would relieve that strain?

## The praxon as act-form

A [praxon](../terms/praxon.md) is not an action chosen but a deformation that occurs. It is pressure times path — the act-form that emerges when unresolved tension finds a conditioned line through the [field](../terms/field.md). Every praxon leaves a [trace](../terms/trace.md), and every trace modulates future pressure.

This means systems carry memory: past praxons shape the field for future praxons. Trace-dense regions have high residue — every element carries the mark of prior modulation. This is not metaphorical. In biological systems, the trace is tissue scarring or learned behavior. In social systems, the trace is institutional memory or cultural habit. In narrative, the trace is what happened before.

## Coherence, torsion, and drift

When a praxon aligns with its field — when trace reduces friction — [coherence](../terms/coherence.md) holds. The system sustains recursive operation. When a praxon misaligns, torsion results: friction amplifies, instability grows, and the system may fracture.

But the praxonic paradigm does not seek resolution or control. It seeks expression, resonance, and the capacity to sense drift. Drift is not error — it is how meaning evolves. Every praxon shifts the field, and the field shifts the conditions for future praxons. What appears stable is always drifting. What appears fixed is always under pressure.

## Reading through the lens

To apply the praxonic lens to any system:

1. **Identify pressure.** What gradients, tensions, or unresolved differences define the current state? Pressure is not always visible — sometimes it is carried in traces from prior cycles.

2. **Trace the paths.** Where does the field offer least resistance? What conditioned lines of deformation channel the pressure into form?

3. **Watch for praxons.** What act-forms emerge? What deformations occur? These are not choices — they are what pressure does when it meets a conditioned field.

4. **Follow the traces.** What marks do these act-forms leave? How do they reshape the field? What charge accumulates? Where are folds forming?

5. **Notice coherence and torsion.** Where does the system hold? Where does it fracture? Where is alignment drifting toward misalignment?

6. **Sense the drift.** What is changing? Not what has changed, but what is in the process of shifting? Drift is the real subject of praxonic reading.
