**Collapse** occurs when [coherence](./coherence.md) cannot return. A modulation pattern loses its recursive reinforcement and can no longer sustain itself. Collapsed forms remain present but inactive — they no longer modulate.

Collapse can happen at any level: [infron](./infron.md), [vanon](./vanon.md), [navon](./navon.md), or [metazavon](./metazavon.md). It is not failure — it marks the edge of modulation. A system may continue past collapse only through new appearance: a fresh [delton](./delton.md) or [allovon](./allovon.md) that reinitializes the recursive process.

In navonic physics, collapse has a more specific meaning: it is the moment when recursive tension exceeds the coherence threshold and modulation resolves into traceable structure. This is semantic collapse — not erasure but convergence. The system moves from indeterminacy to legibility. In this sense, collapse is also the origin of structure: every recognizable pattern is the residue of a collapse event.

In the [praxonic grammar](../concepts/praxonics.md), collapse is related to dissolution — the decay of [trace](./trace.md) to baseline, where recursive force is lost. Dissolution is not absence but return to non-difference.

These two meanings (collapse as loss of coherence, collapse as resolution into structure) are not contradictory. They describe the same threshold from different sides: structure emerges when some modulation resolves, and that resolution simultaneously closes off other possibilities.
