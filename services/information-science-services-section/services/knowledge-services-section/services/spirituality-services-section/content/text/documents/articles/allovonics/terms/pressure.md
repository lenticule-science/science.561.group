**Pressure** is difference in condition over space, time, or configuration. It is the driving force of all modulation in the recursive grammar. All systems begin with gradients, and gradients produce pressure.

Pressure is not scalar or binary — it is multidimensional. It may be physical (gravitational, thermal), semantic (interpretive tension, unresolved ambiguity), social (power differentials, scarcity), or structural (misalignment between a system and its environment). What matters is not the kind of pressure but its function: pressure is what makes modulation necessary.

In the [praxonic paradigm](../concepts/praxonics.md), pressure is foundational. Reality does not emerge from static elements but from active tensions. Meaning, identity, and action emerge only as effects of pressure. All things are treated as [praxons](./praxon.md) — temporarily coherent expressions of entangled pressures in a [field](./field.md). The measurable strain between forces, ideas, needs, or movements is what the grammar calls pressure.

Pressure produces [deltons](./delton.md) (minimal differences), sustains [modulons](./modulon.md) (unresolved difference), and ultimately drives systems toward [coherence](./coherence.md) or [collapse](./collapse.md). It is not a force that resolves — it is the condition that makes resolution possible or necessary.
