A **zavon** is a recurring modulation regime across [navons](./navon.md). It emerges when modulation reinforces not just structure but behavior — when the pattern of activity, not only the configuration, begins to repeat.

Zavons are not fixed. They are regimes of activity: they may adapt, fluctuate, or persist. They shape how navons relate and evolve. If a zavon alters how it maintains itself — if the regime begins to adjust its own rules of persistence — a [metazavon](./metazavon.md) may appear.

The zavonic regime sits between stable structure and self-adjusting intelligence in the [navonic ontology](../concepts/navonics.md). It describes repeating fields of behavior — the recursive habits of a system. In navonic physics, a zavon corresponds to a harmonic field: a periodic, self-reinforcing pattern of modulation.

Biologically, habits, seasonal rhythms, and developmental cycles can be read as zavons: modulation regimes that repeat with variation across cycles.
