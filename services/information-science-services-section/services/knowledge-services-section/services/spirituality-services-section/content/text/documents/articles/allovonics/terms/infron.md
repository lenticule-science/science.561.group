An **infron** is a shaped asymmetry within a [vanon](./vanon.md) — a localized pattern of modulation that forms from uneven interaction between [modulons](./modulon.md). Where modulons are undirected units of difference, infrons are what modulation looks like when it acquires structure: a deformation with recognizable shape.

Infrons do not act. They condition how modulation is received — biasing the field in particular directions, favoring certain outcomes over others. An infron may drift, stabilize, [collapse](./collapse.md), or dissolve. A coherent infron contributes to the structure of its vanon; a disrupted infron dissolves back into undirected modulons.

In [allovonic cosmology](../concepts/allovonic-cosmology.md), infrons are the primary mechanism by which recursive influence becomes legible. The low-entropy origins of the universe are read as "infronic priming" — asymmetry seeded by recursive modulation before internal complexity could emerge. CMB anomalies are read as "infronic residue" — the persistent imprint of early asymmetries preserved across cosmic time. Dark energy is read as long-wave infronic drift carried forward through expansion.

The infron is the bridge between raw difference and recognizable structure. It is where modulation begins to leave marks that can be traced.
