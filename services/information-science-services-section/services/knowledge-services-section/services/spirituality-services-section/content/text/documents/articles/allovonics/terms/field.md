A **field** is the structured set of all [pressures](./pressure.md) and active [traces](./trace.md). It is the context in which [praxons](./praxon.md) emerge, act, and leave marks. A field conditions praxons but is also conditioned by them — it is simultaneously the landscape and the product of recursive modulation.

In the [praxonic grammar](../concepts/praxonics.md), the field is defined formally as the sum of all pressures weighted by their traces: the totality of forces at play. It is not static or predefined but evolves in response to both internal pressures (resonance strength, decay rates) and external factors (cultural shifts, [allovonic](./allovon.md) intrusion). The field is where modulation happens — the medium of recursive process.

The field concept overlaps with but is broader than a [vanon](./vanon.md). A vanon is a specific shared modulation field defined by participating praxons. The field in the praxonic sense is the entire landscape — all vanons, all pressures, all traces taken together. Charge (the field's readiness to emit further praxons) accumulates as unresolved gradients and trace overlaps within the field.

A field does not flow. Modulation is redistributed, not transmitted. This is a foundational principle of the grammar: what appears as movement is redistribution of recursive tension through shared structure.
