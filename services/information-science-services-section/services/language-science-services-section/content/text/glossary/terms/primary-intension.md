# Primary intension — two-dimensional semantics

A **primary intension** is an expression's **epistemic reference-determining function** in a specified two-dimensional semantics. Its argument is a scenario *considered as actual*: what would the expression refer to **if that scenario were the actual one**, using the designated reference-fixing conditions? The scenario space, expression type, knowledge conditions and reference rule must be stated; it is not a generic synonym for “first impression,” “initial premise” or “primary glossary sense.”

Under a common illustration, \`water\` is introduced by an epistemic role such as the liquid in rivers and rain. Under a scenario considered actual in which XYZ plays that role, the primary intension selects XYZ under that stipulated role-fixing; under the actual-world scientific fixing relevant to ordinary English, it selects H₂O. That model-dependent choice is not evidence that an arbitrary source saying “water: XYZ” changed its reference.

Contrast [secondary intension](secondary-intension.md): after the actual-world referent has been fixed, evaluation of *counterfactual* scenarios keeps that fixing constant. The two functions are not interchangeable and need not coincide at every scenario.

The March 28 **Absurd → Possible → Necessary → Determined** progression is a philosophical inquiry under accepted givens, not a construction of this primary intension. Likewise an ambiguous YAML key or lexical spelling must first be assigned a [world-qualified sense](world-indexed-term-sense.md) and reference-fixing semantics; one cannot immediately apply two-dimensional analysis to an untyped token.
