# Secondary intension — two-dimensional semantics

A **secondary intension** evaluates a counterfactual world **after** the expression's reference has been fixed with respect to the *actual* world under a specified semantic theory. It is therefore a world-indexed function of possible extensions **given the actual-world reference-fixing**, not a label for every distinct sense that an English word acquires in different documents.

For example, after ordinary English \`water\` is fixed in the actual world as H₂O, the secondary intension picks out H₂O in a counterfactual world **where it occurs** (or an empty extension where there is no H₂O). It does **not** switch to an XYZ substance merely because XYZ fills the lakes there. In a *scenario considered actual*, the [primary intension](primary-intension.md) can instead track whichever substance is stipulated to play the role. The example illustrates semantic **reference-fixing**, not an empirical claim that every counterfactual world contains water.

**Crucial lexical rule:** \`Necessary\` in relational inquiry and \`Necessary\` as the modal \`□\` operator are different *lexical senses in different governed worlds*. Merely separating those senses into two glossary records is **not** proof that they are two secondary intensions of one term. To make that stronger claim, identify the same expression, its actual-world fixing, the counterfactual scenario domain, its admissible model and the extension function. See [world-indexed term sense](world-indexed-term-sense.md).

### Do not assert accidental equivalences

- Co-reference in one world is not \`owl:sameAs\` or guaranteed equality of intensions.
- A glossary synonym is justified only where **substitution preserves the intended concept within the relevant world**; shared spelling is never enough.
- A source's self-description is not a change in reference-fixing or an independently observed linguistic fact.
- A technical operator in a proof calculus does not inherit a personal philosophical intension by being called \`possible\`, \`absurd\`, \`determined\`, or \`necessary\`.
