# Intension — formal/lexical semantic sense

The **intension** of an expression is a way of specifying its meaning or reference condition. In an **intensional model** with an explicitly defined family of worlds \`W\` and extension domain \`D\`, one common representation is a function \`⟦e⟧ : W → D\` (or an appropriate domain of truth values, sets or partial references), assigning an extension to the expression at each world. This presentation requires the model, expression type and evaluation conditions; not every English expression denotes an individual or has a total reference function.

The **extension** at a world is the actual value the selected intension assigns there. A change in reference across worlds does not by itself mean the intension changed. Conversely, two expressions can co-refer at one world while differing in sense—Frege's morning-star/evening-star example illustrates why co-reference cannot define lexical equivalence.

## Two-dimensional distinction

[Primary intension](primary-intension.md) evaluates scenarios considered as possible actual worlds, tracking how reference would be fixed *if that scenario were actual*. [Secondary intension](secondary-intension.md) uses the actual-world reference-fixing held constant and evaluates counterfactual worlds. Both require a specified semantic theory and reference-fixing rule.

Neither “a term has two meanings in different branches” nor “two people use the same word” automatically implies two primary or secondary intensions. Those are first cases of **world-indexed lexical disambiguation** ([separate term sense](world-indexed-term-sense.md)); only a qualified two-dimensional model supports a two-dimensional semantic assertion.

**Corrective example:** \`Possible\` in the March 28 relational inquiry denotes an inquiry role. \`◇φ\` in Kripke semantics denotes existential accessible-world satisfaction. They are not equivalent intensions or lexical synonyms because their observable application/interpretation rules differ. Do not combine them into a single term record for convenience.
