# Language Science Services Section

**Parent:** [Information Science Services Section](../../README.md).  
**Child:** [English Science Services Section](services/english-science-services-section/README.md).

Language Science investigates how sign systems, utterances, context, speakers, readers, conventions, and communicative tasks support or frustrate interpretation. It studies language as information-bearing activity: the same string can yield different interpretations across audiences and contexts.

Research here separates descriptive linguistic evidence, empirically tested comprehension claims, formal definitions/models, and proposed normative writing rules. It does not substitute style preferences for scientific findings.

The existing [Lenticule linguistics repository](https://github.com/lenticule-science/linguistics-services-section) develops linguistic observation and parsing; coordinate experiments and typed evidence with it rather than duplicating that work. The existing [Communication Science section](../communication-science-services-section/README.md) remains responsible for adjacent communication/writing investigations and their historical documents.

This section is private within the Lenticule Science research program. The service hierarchy is logical; a future separate repository needs governed formation and explicit ownership, not an assumed nested GitHub namespace.

**Founding research carrier:** [PlainEng/PTGAE science](services/english-science-services-section/services/ptgae-science-services-section/README.md).

This research complements the marks-to-interpretation model below.

- [Language science from marks to interpretation](research/marks-utterances-and-contextual-semantics.md) separates utterance, grammatical analysis, possible meaning, evidence and authority, with precise limits for contextual gluing.
- [English science](services/english-science-services-section/research/english-language-research-program.md) studies real English grammar, history, pragmatics, variation and human–agent language requirements; PlainEng is only one restricted derivative.
