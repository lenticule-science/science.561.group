# PlainEng research program — founding agenda (2026-10-10)

**Status:** Proposed scientific agenda. This document is not a published standard, certified method, or statement that comparative superiority has been demonstrated.

## Research question

Under what observable conditions does a specific English expression help a specified reader understand and act on a technical, scientific or institutional message correctly, with tolerable effort, without altering the source's material claims and obligations?

A writing norm has two independent tests: whether it is justified for the defined context and whether it actually produces the claimed effects there. A common-word preference, a shorter sentence or an active-voice preference may be useful; none is a universal theorem of comprehension.

## Research domains

1. **Meaning:** syntax and semantics of actors, actions, quantification, modality, negation, scope, reference, temporal conditions, causation, numerical values and units.
2. **Use and context:** pragmatics, discourse, genre, registers, technical terminology and the reader's task and prior knowledge.
3. **Evidence and authority:** distinctions among what is observed, claimed, tested, intended, normatively required and institutionally adopted. Multiple dimensions can apply to one sentence simultaneously.
4. **Cognitive and accessibility effects:** reader workload, information-seeking, assistive technology, first-language differences and stress-dependent task performance; actual usability outcomes, not grade-level scores alone.
5. **Transformation:** which restricted text changes preserve observable invariants, which require domain review, and which cannot be certified by finite syntax checks.
6. **Reproducibility:** versioned source, corpus sampling, annotations, tests, measurement and the ability to reject a proposed “plain” revision.

## Hypotheses (not conclusions)

- **H1, task context:** A reader-task-specific revision will yield higher task-completion accuracy than a generic readability-only rewrite, conditional on comparable length/content/participants. Null: no meaningful improvement.
- **H2, precision:** Explicit agents, concrete verbs, unambiguous modal operators and referential consistency can reduce interpretive error in high-stakes instructions relative to comparable ambiguous originals; there will be counterexamples where added words or passive voice are better.
- **H3, uncertainty:** Separating epistemic evidence status from normative force will reduce false inferences that a proposed system is deployed or that an unknown value equals zero.
- **H4, protected structure:** A rewriting system restricted to reviewed local morphisms and typed protected spans will produce fewer material claim drifts than unrestricted paraphrasing on the same test set; it may offer fewer improvements.
- **H5, automation:** Conformance detectors combining rule context and typed evidence will attain higher precision than substring-only bans, tested on unseen annotated data.

Test outcomes may falsify these hypotheses. Record failures and inconvenient counterexamples.

## Scientific outputs

A versioned terminology and register inventory; a formal local interpretation/conformance model with explicit limitations; an annotated original/approved/rejected transformation corpus; source-standard crosswalk; preregistered reader-task experimental designs; and uncertainty/error reports. All require sources, dates, provenance and a consumer/acceptance condition.
