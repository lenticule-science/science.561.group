- List every non-common term introduced in the document. A term is "non-common" if the default [audience](../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/audience.md) (an adult reader with general literacy, no specialized background) wouldn't know it.
- For each term, write a one- to three-sentence definition in plain language. Follow the [style guide](./style-guide.md)'s term definition conventions: opening definition sentence, then elaboration.
- Add any symbols or notation used alongside the term. [Richard Mayer](../../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/richard-mayer.md)'s signaling principle: readers comprehend better when essential material is highlighted through structural cues [@mayer2009].
- Remove circular definitions; each term should be understandable on its own. If term A's definition requires term B, define B first or include it in the glossary.
- Use each term consistently throughout the document — don't rotate synonyms for style. Consistent terminology reduces cognitive load [@sweller1988].
- Place the glossary adjacent to the document and link to it. If term definitions already exist in the vault, link to those rather than duplicating definitions.
