- Read the plain-language specification at [plain language](../../topics/plain-language/index.md). It draws on [Janice Redish](../../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/janice-redish.md)'s work on web content usability [@redish2012] and [Rudolf Flesch](../../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/rudolf-flesch.md)'s readability research [@flesch1949].
- Use the default [audience](../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/audience.md) unless an "Assumed audience" section is required. The default audience is: an adult reader with general literacy, no specialized background, and a practical reason for reading.
- Put the main point in the first screen of the page. Structure by reader need, not by the writer's reasoning process — the shift [Linda Flower](../../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/linda-flower.md) called moving from writer-based to reader-based prose [@flowerhayes1981].
- Define technical terms on first use and use them consistently. Don't rotate synonyms for style — repetition helps comprehension [@redish2012].
- Avoid acronyms unless they are explicitly approved and defined on first use.
- Before publishing, check the document against the publication checklist in the plain-language spec. For high-impact content (legal, financial, medical, safety), test with real readers — writers systematically overestimate reader comprehension [@schriver1997].
