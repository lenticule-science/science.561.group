***Coherence*** is the quality that makes a piece of writing feel unified and logically connected — the reader's sense that ideas follow from one another and that the whole adds up to more than its parts. It is distinguished from [cohesion](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/cohesion.md), which is the local connection between sentences through linguistic devices (transitional words, repeated terms, pronouns). Cohesion is the glue; coherence is the architecture.

A text can be cohesive without being coherent: every sentence can flow grammatically into the next while the overall argument goes nowhere. And a text can be coherent without heavy cohesion: a reader who understands the governing idea can follow sections that don't use explicit [transitions](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transition.md), because the logic connecting them is clear.

Coherence is created by:

- **A governing idea** — a [thesis](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/thesis.md), question, or central purpose that every part of the text serves. The reader who knows what the text is trying to do can evaluate whether each section contributes.
- **Logical order** — ideas arranged so that each one follows from or builds on the previous. [Joseph Williams](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/joseph-williams.md) argued that coherent paragraphs move from old information (what the reader already knows) to new information (what the text is adding) [@williams2006]. Reversing this order — leading with the new — forces the reader to hold unfamiliar material without context.
- **Consistent focus** — the text stays on topic. Digressions, tangents, and unrelated examples break coherence because they ask the reader to track a second thread.
- **Proportional development** — important ideas get more space; minor points get less. When a text devotes a paragraph to a major claim and three paragraphs to a minor example, the proportions signal a coherence problem.

Coherence problems are felt more than diagnosed. A reader who says "I couldn't follow it" or "it felt disorganized" is reporting a coherence failure. The fix is usually structural: reorder sections, cut digressions, add a clearer thesis, or move the point from the end (where the writer discovered it) to the beginning (where the reader needs it).

## Related terms

- [cohesion](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/cohesion.md) — sentence-level connectedness, distinct from but supporting coherence
- [thesis](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/thesis.md) — the governing idea that creates coherence at the essay level
- [transition](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/transition.md) — explicit signals of connection between ideas
- [paragraph](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/paragraph.md) — the unit where coherence is most visible

### Retained formulation


- [[define]]:: [[coherence]] is the [[condition]] in which an [[act]], its corresponding [[condition|conditions]], and supporting [[structure|structures]] hold together in a stable, self-sufficient dynamic. It is what marks that a relational system is **complete enough** to persist as a real part of the [[domain of relating]].
    

## Derivation

- [[derive|Deriving]] [[exist|existing]] shows:
    
    - A [[triage/thing]] is brought about by the primitive [[act|acts]] of [[include|including]] and [[exclude|excluding]];
        
    - These generate [[condition|conditions]] of [[inclusion]] and [[exclusion]];
        
    - Held together by the [[condition]] of [[tension]], which induces [[difference]];
        
    - Sustained by [[relate|relating]], which maintains the dynamic;
        
    - Clarified and stabilized through [[relation]] and the formation of a [[relative]] as a minimal [[structure]].
        
- Each of these elements is necessary but not sufficient on its own:
    
    - [[act|Acts]] do the work;
        
    - [[condition|Conditions]] are held open;
        
    - [[structure|Structures]] stabilize and durably maintain the results.
        
- But for the system to be **self-sufficient**:
    
    - These elements must not only be present but also **held together as a unified whole**;
        
    - Their interplay must resolve without collapse, dissipation, or irresolvable contradiction.
        
- This unified holding-together is the [[condition]] of [[coherence]]:
    
    - It ensures that all necessary components of the relational system **mutually support** one another;
        
    - It marks the point at which the system achieves **closure**: nothing further is needed to sustain its being.
        

## Implications

- [[coherence]] is the minimal indicator that a relational architecture is **real** within the [[domain of relating]]:
    
    - Without [[coherence]], even if acts and conditions arise, they fail to persist meaningfully;
        
    - Coherence is what allows a [[triage/thing]] to maintain its integrity over time.
        
- Any system lacking [[coherence]]:
    
    - Breaks down into unintelligibility or dissolution;
        
    - Cannot serve as a foundation for further [[act|acts]] or [[structure|structures]].
        
- [[coherence]] is what makes further derivations possible:
    
    - Once coherence is established at one level, new layers of [[relational dynamics]] can be built upon it;
        
    - This recursive building is what allows complex relational systems to emerge from minimal foundations.
