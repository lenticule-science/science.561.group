***Rhetoric*** is the study and practice of effective communication — the art of using language to persuade, inform, or move an [audience](./audience.md). Aristotle defined it as "the faculty of observing in any given case the available means of persuasion," a definition that remains useful because it frames rhetoric as analysis, not manipulation: the rhetorician studies what works and why.

Aristotle identified three appeals available to any communicator:

- **[Ethos](./ethos.md)** — the speaker's credibility. The audience must believe the speaker is trustworthy, knowledgeable, and acting in good faith.
- **[Pathos](./pathos.md)** — emotional engagement. The audience must feel something — concern, curiosity, indignation, hope — that makes the message matter to them.
- **[Logos](./logos.md)** — logical argument. The audience must find the reasoning sound, the evidence sufficient, and the conclusions warranted.

These appeals operate in every piece of writing, not just persuasive writing. A [technical document](../disciplines/technical-writing/index.md) relies on ethos (the reader trusts the documentation) and logos (the instructions are logically ordered). A [poem](../../../../../poetry-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/poem.md) relies on pathos (the reader feels) and logos (the images cohere). A [landing page](../../../../../copywriting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/landing-page.md) uses all three explicitly.

The rhetorical situation — the relationship among speaker, audience, message, and context — shapes every writing decision. [Lloyd Bitzer](../../general/people/lloyd-bitzer.md) argued that rhetoric is always a response to a situation: something is happening, someone needs to be addressed, and constraints limit what can be said effectively. [Carolyn Miller](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/carolyn-miller.md) extended this by showing that [genres](./genre.md) are typified rhetorical responses — recurring situations produce recurring forms [@miller1984].

Rhetoric is not synonymous with deception. The popular equation of "rhetoric" with empty or manipulative language reverses the discipline's purpose. Rhetoric studies how communication works so that communicators can be more effective and audiences can be more critical. [Wayne Booth](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/wayne-booth.md) called this "the rhetoric of assent" — the study of how reasonable people come to share beliefs through language [@booth1961].
