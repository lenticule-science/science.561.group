A ***think-aloud protocol*** is a research method in which participants verbalize their thoughts while performing a task — reading a document, writing a text, or solving a problem — so researchers can observe cognitive processes that are normally invisible.

[Linda Flower](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/linda-flower.md) and [John Hayes](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/john-hayes.md) used think-aloud protocols to develop the cognitive process model of writing, revealing that composing is recursive rather than linear — writers plan, draft, and revise in interleaving cycles, not in the prewrite-write-revise sequence that writing pedagogy had assumed [@flowerhayes1981]. Their protocol data showed what actually happens when people write, not what they report afterward.

[Karen Schriver](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/karen-schriver.md) extended the method to document testing, developing [protocol-aided audience analysis](./protocol-aided-audience-analysis.md). By watching readers think aloud while using a document, writers can identify exactly where comprehension breaks down — something that self-report surveys miss, because readers often don't know what they misunderstood [@schriver1997].

Think-aloud protocols are central to the vault's approach to writing for two reasons:

1. **As evidence for writing principles.** The vault's rules about [revision](../../../../../essay-writing-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/revision.md), [writer-based prose](../../../../../rhetoric-and-composition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/writer-based-prose.md), and [cognitive load](./cognitive-load.md) rest on protocol research, not intuition.
2. **As a testing method.** The [plain language specification](../topics/plain-language/index.md)'s paraphrase test (section 12.2) and task test (section 12.3) are simplified versions of protocol-aided testing.
