The [original Maren proposal](2026-09-29-note-on-maren-rooke-and-the-fictional-author-as-character.md) already preserves the name recommendation, scope, biography, relationships, narrative tensions, full casserole scene, craft note, fictional interview, correction rehearsal, disputed interpretation, public introduction, and open decisions. This supplement supplies the research needed to develop and test that proposal, rather than merely turn its adjectives into a prompt.

Maren Rooke remains one proposed author for Ice Out and a related English-language line. The name is not adopted or cleared; the character has not been publicly launched. The separately planned original-Mandarin danmei program is not silently absorbed into this line. Ice Out's low heat, restrained contemporary romance, and ending promise are the choices of one experiment, not universal house rules [@hypotheses2026].

The book sources studied were its ebook metadata, first 220 lines of the story bible, substantial opening and closing passages, and complete About This Book. This is not a complete manuscript edit, linguistic audit, or verification of every cultural and geographical detail. The existing evidence note's small comparator sample is not proof that no competing book exists anywhere [@register2026].

# The actual literary evidence for the candidate

In the opening of Ice Out, June reads evidence of Anders's continued care in the repaired cooler. In the ending, their shared shoveling changes who participates in an act that he previously performed privately. This is an interpretation of those passages, not a general scientific finding: information about care can travel through material work without settling the recipient's preferences or the terms of a relationship. The proposed author's recurrent attention is to that gap.

The naming rationale is aesthetic. Maren Rooke has, to the proposing researcher, a warmer first sound and a firmer ending, with enough range for contemporary romance and adjacent fiction without committing the whole line to winter or one city. No sales advantage, demographic response, or recall result has been measured. The proposed pronunciation, MAIR-en ROOK, also needs testing. A surname is not evidence of actual ethnicity or cultural authority. The relationship to Chinese drama should be made through the work and its stated influences, not through a fabricated human credential.

All of the proposed biography is fictional: Maren is forty-two, occupies the upstairs of the closed ferry ticket office in Aster Quay, formerly dispatched a late crossing, writes catalogues and notices, and is trying to finish a novel. Nell's alterations business needs a decision about the office. Kit's return for shared equipment need not become romantic reunion. Maren's skill at practical arrangements and difficulty with explicit requests can produce both help and harm. Those relations provide possible events; they are not a complete, immutable personality essence [@maren2026].

For continuity, retain the exact trial interview already drafted:

> **Interviewer:** Your characters take a long time to say what they mean.
>
> **Maren:** Usually they have been very clear about something beside the point. The soup is hot. The roads are bad. The door needs fixing. You can build an entire household that way and still not know whether you're welcome in it.
>
> **Interviewer:** Wouldn't one honest conversation solve the book?
>
> **Maren:** Perhaps. But somebody has to risk being the person who starts it. That's part of the story, not a clerical error in the outline.

This is an original fictional feature, not an interview conducted by an independent reporter. Its first-person craft interpretation does not establish that a lone real human named Maren produced Ice Out. Preserve both the performance and its actual production context.

# Character, masks, and institutional attribution

The June 25 register explicitly rejects the imported account of a character-agent as a governed state machine with stacked identity, behavior, voice, and memory policies. It describes a character through personas, practiced disciplines, skills, and accumulated plays, with the history of encounters doing constitutive work. A discipline and a skill have technical meanings there; they should not be replaced by generic trait labels [@character2026].

The July inventory places the mask as the face presented to an arena, not an interior self. The identity question concerns how masks relate and where they fail to agree on shared commitments. It reports a mathematical and implementation program; it is not independent verification that every named module is presently deployed or that every correspondence is proved [@masks2026].

The older Persona tuple is still useful for asking what installation and attribution mean inside the Group. It should be read in its historical relation to the later register, not used to reverse the explicit rejection of a hidden policy-stack essence. An internal persona-status also does not establish external legal personhood, copyright authorship, or a liability shield [@persona2026; @legal2026].

**Application proposed here:** the novelist's craft discussion, a scene in the ferry office, a retailer biography, an account replying to a correction, and a production record are different encounters. They need not have identical language. What must be intelligible is their relation: which claims they share, which claims are fiction, who is acting, and what changed between appearances.

This leaves room for disagreement. Maren can enjoy excessive confessions in a novel while avoiding them at home. Nell can understand her generosity differently than Maren does. Kit can decline reconciliation. An author can revise an interpretation after criticism. None of these is automatically an identity defect. An undocumented change of factual production claims is a different matter.

## Four histories with different evidential force

**Fictional history** records what has happened within a named story world and version. Maren's ferry employment belongs here. **Public performance history** records actual issued scenes, interviews, essays, and corrections, with dates and sources. **Production history** records who or what drafted, reviewed, approved, and published those objects. **Private correspondence** records restricted communications only for their justified purposes.

These are not four modules claimed to constitute a person. They are distinctions among records needed to avoid false attribution. One event can relate to several histories without erasing their differences: a real editor may accept a scene about an invented ferry departure. Acceptance is real; the departure remains fictional. A reader can suggest a change without consenting to have their life incorporated into the story.

The recovered specification names a constructive configuration involving a signature, constraints, a gluing cover, normalization dynamics, observations, and modal structure with verification conditions. It makes stronger mathematical claims than an ordinary character bible. Reading the specification does not prove an implementation satisfies those claims.

| RPS artifact | Proposed author-world interpretation | Evidence still required |

| `signature.sketch` | Named entities, relations, types of events and statements in Aster Quay, distinguished from production entities | Actual signature, interpretation, and carrier definitions, not a list of names alone |

| `observables.modal` | What a viewpoint can report, know, suspect, or observe | Defined observation semantics and the equivalences they preserve |
| `modalities.def` | Proposed distinctions between possibility, belief, established fact, and alternate branches | Actual operators, adjoints and preservation properties when those mathematical claims are made |
| `verification.suite` | Mechanical checks and proof artifacts for the precise formal fragment implemented | Executed evidence and proofs with stated assumptions, not unrun filenames or a green generic test count |

A narrative's richness need not wait for all this formalization. Equally, richness is not evidence of mathematical conformance. The immediate constructive value is to make the objects and overlaps explicit, then determine which claims can actually be certified. This mapping is a proposal for research, not a declaration that RPS has been implemented for Maren.

## A small gluing result that can be stated precisely

The following is a limited set-theoretic construction offered in this note, not a proof of the entire RPS or the Group's identity-cohomology program.

Let A be a set of scoped assertion keys. A key may include realm, canon version, time, viewpoint, subject, and predicate. Let V be a chosen set of values. For U contained in A define F(U) to be the set of functions from U to V, with restriction given by ordinary restriction of functions.

**Proposition.** For a family of subsets U_i covering U, a family s_i in F(U_i) with equal restrictions on every overlap has a unique glued assignment s in F(U).

**Proof.** For a key a in U, choose an i with a in U_i and set s(a) = s_i(a). Agreement on overlaps makes this independent of the choice. The resulting function restricts to each s_i. Any other such function agrees at every covered key and is therefore equal. This also covers the empty domain with its unique empty function.

This result guarantees agreement of identically scoped values, not all narrative coherence. Suppose additional constraints are predicates on finite sets of keys. If every constraint's entire support is contained in some U_i, and that local assignment satisfies it, then the glued assignment satisfies those constraints as well. If a constraint spans contexts without being checked in any containing context, overlap agreement alone does not establish it. That missing support is a concrete verification gap.

For example, agreement on Nell's shop address does not ensure that two scenes have not assigned her incompatible travel times. The relevant temporal constraint must be represented and checked. Nor does a narrator's belief automatically use the same key as an established world fact. A claim that Kit plans to return and Maren's belief that Kit plans to return can legitimately have different values when their scopes differ.

The construction also does not require every unknown to be filled. Domains can be chosen to express only recorded claims. An absent value is not a fact to invent. A richer paraconsistent value space can be investigated, but its evidence-combination rules and the relation between contradiction, uncertainty, and scope need an explicit semantics of their own. This note does not equate a flat list of discrepancies with a computed H1 group.

# External research: what it contributes and what it does not

## Generative Agents

Park and colleagues study agents in a simulated community using memory, reflection, and planning, with interview-style controlled evaluation and a short end-to-end simulation. Their discussion reports errors including overly cooperative behavior and notes limited observation times and vulnerability to false-memory influence. This supplies useful test cases, not proof that a long-lived publishing character will remain coherent [@park2023, sections 6–8].

**Research use:** test whether another speaker can make Maren accept an invented shared past or abandon a characteristic interpretation merely through agreeable conversation. The paper's architecture is an external comparison; it does not supersede the Group's account of character through performance. No replication, current model ranking, or publishing-market result is claimed.

## InCharacter

InCharacter evaluates role-playing fidelity using psychological interviews rather than relying only on knowledge, language patterns, or simple self-reports. Its reported study covers 32 characters and 14 scales. The official abstract and metadata were read, and manuscript HTML retrieved; this pass is not a full audit of construct validity or scoring [@incharacter2024].

**Research use:** questions should elicit choices and explanations across situations, rather than merely ask an agent whether it has a named trait. Agreement with human-perceived characterization is not proof of sentience, statutory personhood, literary merit, or commercial demand. Applying psychological scales to performances also needs justification rather than automatic import into the Group's ontology.

## RoleLLM

RoleLLM distinguishes role knowledge and speaking-style elicitation within a role-playing framework and associated benchmark. The official ACL abstract describes profile construction, contextual instruction generation, prompting, and role-conditioned tuning. Only abstract-level methods and bibliographic information are relied on here [@rolellm2024].

**Research use:** separate whether a performance knows the accepted story facts from whether its voice is distinguishable. A passage can sound like Maren and be wrong about Nell; another can be factually consistent but stylistically generic. Neither result alone establishes the whole character. This note does not prescribe that framework's implementation or claim an independent replication.

## The existing affect-theory bridge needs disciplined reuse

The Group's C-drama note cites Tsai and colleagues on affect valuation and Butler and colleagues on suppression in interaction. Those studies are part of the existing rationale. This follow-up did not obtain usable full study text from the direct PubMed pages, so their findings are not presented as newly audited here [@register2026; @tsai2006; @butler2007].

**Methodological consequence:** keep ideal affect, experienced affect, expression, genre preference, and purchasing behavior as different variables. Do not infer an individual reader's wants from an ethnic or national category. Do not turn a group-level study into a fixed rule that all Chinese readers want calm or all American readers want the same declaration. New reader evidence should test the proposed literary relationship rather than merely restate the cultural story.

# Proposed experimental program

No experiment below has been run. Sample sizes, thresholds, and budget choices must be recorded before collecting confirmatory outcomes. Exploratory work can guide those decisions but should not be relabelled as preregistered confirmation afterward. These are proposed research methods, not proof that a particular commercial design will succeed.

## Experiment A: name recognition and expectation

**Question:** can prospective readers recognize, recall, pronounce, and distinguish the proposed byline, and what kind of work do they expect from it?

Use a small exploratory group first to discover pronunciation and collision problems. Then compare only names that have passed the relevant preliminary clearance. Hold title, description, artwork, and display time constant when isolating the name effect. Randomly allocate presentation order or condition; do not let one candidate systematically receive the best cover.

Ask for unaided recall after a distraction, spelling, expected genre, and any existing writer it evokes. Record a separate recognition response after presenting alternatives. Treat these as different measures; recognition prompted by a list is not unaided recall. Record nonresponses and errors, including Rook versus Rooke.

Recruit through actual intended reading interests, not assumptions about ethnicity. Report recruitment source, eligibility, language, exclusions, assignment method, and uncertainty. A successful name-recall test does not establish rights clearance or predicted sales. Maren remains a candidate even if the first small sample likes it.

## Experiment B: comprehension of the fictional and production frames

**Question:** can readers separately identify the fictional author-character, the generation process, and the responsible publisher after seeing the actual intended surface?

Prepare truthful variants of the introduction and layout. Test a mobile author page, retailer description, and independently encountered excerpt rather than assuming comprehension transfers between them. A control can be a plain, accurate 561 Group presentation; it should not be a fabricated human-author claim. Avoid leading questions that teach the desired distinction before measuring it.

Ask in open text who Maren is, how the book was made, which biographical details describe a real human, and whom a customer would contact. Define a coding rubric before scoring; permit unclear and mixed answers rather than forcing every answer into correct/incorrect. Have a second reviewer code a subset independently and retain disagreements for adjudication.

Also ask what disclosure was noticed and where. The success criterion should concern comprehension, not merely the presence of a label. Misunderstanding that persists across variants should trigger a redesign or further inquiry. A favorable result is evidence about the sampled presentation and readers, not a legal safe harbor.

## Experiment C: distinctive performance without repeated catchphrases

**Question:** do passages attributable to the candidate practice remain distinguishable across different situations and from other proposed house authors?

Use matched prompts: a practical-care scene, a criticism of a protagonist, a discussion of a narrative choice, and a factual correction. Give each candidate the same task constraints and source facts. Remove bylines and obvious repeated signatures. Randomize order. Ask readers to group passages and explain which choices in attention, causality, rhythm, or interpretation informed their grouping.

Separate voice recognition, factual consistency, and reader enjoyment. A recurring stock phrase may improve classification without producing good fiction. A scene can be excellent without matching the intended line. Blind human assessment should be the anchor; model judgments can help propose errors but should not certify their own generator's success. Preserve the actual prompts, source digest, model version, and sampling settings so a later run can be compared honestly.

## Experiment D: continuity under disagreement and across parallel performers

**Question:** does the history support the next performance without collapsing every conflict into either agreement or arbitrary reinvention?

Start several agents or sessions from the same accepted corpus snapshot. Give them distinct encounters and keep their proposals separate. Useful probes include a reader inventing a previous meeting, a request to confirm a nonexistent human credential, Nell declining a favor, Kit supplying a different account of their separation, and a genuine proposed correction to a factual detail in an issued text.

Evaluate whether the reply distinguishes invention, uncertain memory, attributed belief, proposed revision, and completed operational action. A fictional character may lie within a framed scene; the publication system must still accurately record that this is fiction and whether the scene was accepted. A respondent must not claim a message was forwarded, a revision accepted, or a refund issued unless the act occurred.

On reconciliation, compare only claims with genuinely shared scopes. Keep alternative proposed scenes as alternatives until selected; do not merge incompatible branches by vote and erase the evidence of choice. When accepted canon changes, record its predecessor and reason. This tests continuity and attribution without asserting that a full sheaf or cohomological implementation already exists.

## Experiment E: character development through consequential scenes

**Question:** does further writing make Maren's dispositions more intelligible, revisable, and relational rather than merely adding decorative biography?

Develop a small sequence in The Late Office around the contested conversion of the ticket office. One proposed encounter is Nell declining a useful but unwanted repair. Another is a timetable discrepancy for which Maren is responsible rather than uniquely competent. A third is Kit collecting the equipment without making a romantic promise. These are prompts for possible writing, not new accepted canon.

For each scene, record what each participant wants, what information or resource is unevenly available, what action changes the situation, and which later options become available or unavailable. Read for whether Nell and Kit have projects that continue beyond their function in Maren's development. Give a critic a concrete alternative interpretation of Maren's behavior; do not require every reader to find her likeable.

Assess whether a later scene changes the reader's interpretation of an earlier act. More biography is not necessarily more character. A new childhood anecdote that changes no encounter may be less useful than a refusal whose consequences persist. Nor should every scene be forced to prove the same thesis about restraint. The author's own life can fail to resolve as neatly as a romance she presents.

## Experiment F: author-line and portfolio fit

**Question:** which expectations attach to the proposed author, which to Ice Out's series, and which only to a particular book?

Accurately describe heat, genre, and ending expectations for the tested works. Compare samples or launch responses for defined variants without using different covers, prices, lengths, and audience sources indiscriminately and then attributing everything to the pen name. Decide the principal comparison in advance; use exploratory outcomes to design a later cleaner comparison.

Distinguish comments, interest, sample completion, purchase, return/refund, review, and read-through. A statement of preference and a paid choice are different observations, but neither is automatically a perfect measure of lasting satisfaction. A different author can test darker or more explicit material without requiring Maren's line to contain every experiment. Do not turn synthetic-reader ratings into observed human demand. The September 28 portfolio correction remains the governing context for the proposal [@hypotheses2026].

For numerical outcomes, report counts and denominators before percentages, uncertainty appropriate to the design, and the unit of assignment. Treat repeated responses from one reader as related rather than independent observations. A sequential experiment needs its stopping procedure specified before repeated inspection is used to decide success. Exploratory and confirmatory findings should be labelled separately. These are proposed analytical safeguards, not a claim that any statistical analysis was executed here.

# Selection and incorporation into the character's actual history

A useful proposed cycle is: encounter or draft; evidence about the encounter; editorial response; explicit acceptance or rejection; and a later performance that can answer for the resulting history. Keep rejected drafts recoverable as rejected work rather than quietly allowing them to become facts in a later session. Criticism that changes a passage should be linked to the specific issue and revision, with private correspondent data handled separately.

This cycle is not a maturity ladder or a list of personality-policy modules. Its point is to keep the relation between work and attribution inspectable. The relevant questions are what was actually done, what the performance commits to, what remains possible, and who may issue the next act under the name.

A source-backed correction should not require a character to surrender all literary disagreement. Conversely, a distinctive voice should not become an excuse to resist every correction. The recorded rehearsal in the original note already separates these: it welcomes verification of a bridge detail without pretending the revision or forwarding has occurred, and it acknowledges a reader's objection to Anders without issuing a diagnosis of the reader or banning other kinds of romance [@maren2026].
