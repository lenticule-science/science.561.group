# Note on the C-drama register under American feeling rules

Paths below are relative to `lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/`.

## Why this note exists

On 2026-09-27 the founder asked for fiction written to fill a market gap,
and named one direction: "English-language fiction that has C-drama tropes
but hits American feeling rules". This note is the evidence packet the
manuscript is written from, the research-to-write edge of the publishing
note. Each claim carries its source and a grade: primary (the thing itself or
its makers' and buyers' own words), secondary (reporting), aggregator (a
statistics compilation).

## The market reading

The study and its readings are
`science-services-section/.../fiction-services-section/data/records/market-gaps/2026-09-27-cdrama-register-romance{,-readings}.json`,
assayed by the method in the note on finding a market gap
(`science-services-section/.../business-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/27/2026-09-27-note-on-finding-a-market-gap.md`).
In a sample of nine comparable titles:

- **English-original fiction in the xianxia register is occupied.** Sue Lynn
  Tan's *Daughter of the Moon Goddess* and Ann Liang's 2026 *To Dream in
  Darkness* are witnessed there, so this control reads "no gap", as it should.
- **An English-original, adult, contemporary, low-heat slow burn in the C-drama
  register, set in the United States, reads as a gap with demand behind it.**
  No sampled work is witnessed to have that combination. The one work that
  could, *Dial A for Aunties*, is on the reading list for slow burn, heat
  and register. Dropping any single quality still leaves no witnessed work:
  the gap is the combination.
- **The same book built on a contract marriage reads "both" on demand.**
  Contract marriage is among the most listed modern C-drama tropes
  (MyDramaList keeps a list of 17 modern contract-marriage dramas; primary).
  International viewers also push back on the possessive, wrist-grabbing lead
  such stories often carry (secondary). An arrangement can be used only in a
  form that sheds that.

## What C-drama viewers come for

- **Restraint and slow burn.** International viewers describe contemporary
  C-drama romance as "restrained" and "subtle". "Where Hollywood normalizes
  explicit physicality as a primary expression of passion, Chinese narratives
  strategically withhold it to amplify emotional resonance." The male lead
  holds attention "without demanding it"
  ([PhilSTAR Life](https://philstarlife.com/geeky/272033-chinese-dramas-edefining-handsome);
  [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/entertainment/why-chinese-dramas-like-the-first-frost-and-hidden-love-are-crushing-it-globally-1.500112635);
  secondary).
- **The inconsequential moments are the romance.** Slow-burn C-dramas spend
  their time on streets, meals and conversations a Western romance would cut
  ([Tatler Asia](https://www.tatlerasia.com/lifestyle/entertainment/slow-burn-c-dramas-romance);
  secondary).
- **Hidden, years-long love.** *Hidden Love* (Zhu Yi; drama 2023, English
  translation from Seven Seas) and *The First Frost* are the contemporary
  titles international audiences name. They share one author's universe and
  the unspoken love of years, the *an lian* (暗恋)
  ([Wikipedia, "Hidden Love"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Love); primary
  for the facts of publication).
- **Warmth and healing.** Gulf News's list of shows "just as warm and
  comforting" as *The First Frost* shows the register readers seek: calm,
  steady, restorative
  ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/entertainment/if-first-frost-sparked-your-c-drama-journey-these-6-calming-shows-will-keep-you-hooked-hidden-love-and-more-1.500105797);
  secondary).

## What American romance readers require

- **The genre's own rule.** The romance novel has "a central love story and
  an emotionally satisfying and optimistic ending" (Romance Writers of
  America's definition, via
  [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/question/What-defines-a-romance-novel-according-to-the-Romance-Writers-of-America);
  secondary). A bittersweet or tragic ending, which some C-dramas choose,
  breaks the contract.
- **Consent and the rejected tropes.** The wrist grab, the forced kiss and the
  jealous, possessive lead are read by many international viewers as red
  flags: "My arguments couldn't convince you, so instead I will assert
  physical dominance over you"
  ([The Interlude](https://theinterlude.net/2022/06/09/red-flags-in-k-drama/);
  secondary; the reception is mixed, per
  [a study of Vietnamese audiences](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12119-023-10080-4)).
- **Where readers buy.** Romance is about 40% of US ebook sales, and indie
  publishers hold 66% of the best-selling romance ebooks
  ([WordsRated](https://wordsrated.com/romance-novel-sales-statistics/);
  aggregator).

## The feeling rules, as science

- **Feeling rules** (Hochschild, *The Managed Heart*, 1983; the house term at
  `science-services-section/.../sociology-science-services-section/services/critical-theory-science-services-section/services/affect-theory-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/feeling-rules.md`)
  prescribe which emotions count as appropriate, how intensely and toward
  whom. A genre carries feeling rules: it tells its readers what should move
  them. This book writes in two genres at once, so it has to satisfy both sets
  of rules.
- **Ideal affect differs.** European Americans value high-arousal positive
  states (excitement, enthusiasm) more than Hong Kong Chinese, who value
  low-arousal positive states (calm, peacefulness) more; Asian Americans
  value both (Tsai, Knutson and Fung, "Cultural Variation in Affect
  Valuation", 2006,
  [PubMed 16536652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16536652/); primary).
- **Suppression reads differently.** For people holding European-American
  values, expressive suppression in face-to-face interaction lowered
  responsiveness and drew negative partner perceptions and even hostility.
  For women holding more Asian values, suppression did not draw those
  responses (Butler, Lee and Gross, "Emotion Regulation and Culture", *Emotion*
  7:30–48, 2007, [PubMed 17352561](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17352561);
  primary).

## The design rules this packet gives the manuscript

1. **Restraint on the surface, legible to the reader.** A lead who hides his
   feelings reads as cold to a reader holding American values (Butler et
   al.). The reader must see what the characters hide: alternate the point
   of view between the two leads, so each one's restraint is visible from
   inside while the other misreads it.
2. **Calm through the book, a high-arousal payoff at the end.** The C-drama
   register is low-arousal: meals, walks, small attentions (Tsai's ideal
   affect for Chinese viewers). An American reader also needs the excitement
   peak (Tsai's ideal affect for European Americans): the declaration comes
   out loud, and the ending is unambiguously happy (RWA).
3. **The years-long hidden love, the an lian, as the spine.** It is the trope
   international audiences name in *Hidden Love* and *The First Frost*.
4. **Consent-forward restraint.** The lead's restraint is his respect: he
   never grabs, never kisses unasked, and asks before he touches. The
   rejected tropes are left out, not softened.
5. **An arrangement only in a form that sheds the baggage.** If the plot uses
   a pretend relationship for a family's sake, it is her proposal, its terms
   are spoken, and either can end it (the "both" reading on contract
   marriage).
6. **Misunderstanding ends in talk, not fate.** C-drama misunderstandings are
   often resolved by revelation; American feeling rules prefer resolution by
   honest conversation. The misunderstanding may be long, but it ends when
   someone says the true thing out loud.
7. **Family duty honored, self not erased.** Family is central to the plot
   (the C-drama register), but the heroine's choices about her own life are
   hers (American individual autonomy). The ending honors both.
8. **Low heat.** Kisses on the page, no on-page sex. This matches the C-drama
   register and the "low-heat" quality of the gap.
9. **Set in the United States, with a Chinese American family at its center.**
   This is what makes the book the gap's combination rather than another
   translation or another xianxia.
