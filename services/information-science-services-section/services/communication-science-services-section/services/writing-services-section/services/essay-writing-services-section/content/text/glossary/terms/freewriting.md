***Freewriting*** is a composing method in which the writer writes without stopping for a set period — without editing, pausing, rereading, or correcting. [Peter Elbow](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/peter-elbow.md) introduced freewriting in *Writing Without Teachers* (1973) as a way to bypass the internal editor that blocks fluency [@elbow1973].

The point of freewriting isn't to produce good prose. It's to separate generation from evaluation — what Elbow later called [first-order thinking](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/first-order-thinking.md) (generative, associative, uncritical) from [second-order thinking](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/first-order-thinking.md) (analytical, editorial, critical). Most writers try to do both at once, which produces neither fluency nor quality [@elbow1981].

Freewriting works because writing itself is a form of thinking, not just a way to transcribe pre-formed thoughts. [Linda Flower](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/linda-flower.md) and [John Hayes](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/john-hayes.md)'s research confirmed this: their cognitive process model showed that planning, drafting, and reviewing interact recursively throughout composing [@flowerhayes1981]. Freewriting exploits this by giving the planning process raw material to work with.

In practice, freewriting produces [writer-based prose](../../../../../rhetoric-and-composition-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/writer-based-prose.md) — text organized around the writer's discovery process. The [revision](./revision.md) from writer-based to reader-based prose is a separate step that comes later. Trying to produce reader-based prose on the first pass is often what causes writer's block in the first place.
