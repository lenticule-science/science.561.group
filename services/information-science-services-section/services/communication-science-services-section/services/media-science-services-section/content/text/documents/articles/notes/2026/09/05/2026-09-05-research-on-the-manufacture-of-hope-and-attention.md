## Term boundary: supply of messages versus canonical ATT

This source studies public messaging mechanisms: impressions, repeat exposures, media distribution, targeted content, self-reports and advertising practices. Those observable variables are **not automatically canonical ATT**. A measured exposure does not establish one FOUR WIT; a person reporting awareness does not establish an independent ACK; attention to a campaign as an ordinary psychological construct is not a witnessed ACK of a defined claim.

When evaluating “manufactured attention,” identify the measured media-delivery unit q (for example: unique impressions per platform/time window), its observation method and contract, followed by an independently specified causal effect. If the claim is instead about the mathematical ATT budget, state B and the relevant costed-obstruction allocation. If the claim is the nested WIT-of-ACK, identify its source WIT, actual receiver ACK and second-order WIT.

**Status:** literature and older personal/authorial quotations retain their original wording as source evidence. This paper does not define WIT, ACK, ATT, Landauer equivalence or a normalized map between them.

# Research on the manufacture of hope and attention

This note answers a general-science brief: what technologies manufacture hope and attention in a population, historically and now, and what the literature says separates that manufacture from media that cares rather than extracts. Researched primarily from the open web (2026-09-05), then read against the house's own carriers per a mid-task correction. Every figure is a row in the data record `2026-09-05-manufactured-hope-and-attention-measurement.json` (115 rows, 12 case groupings), cited here by source title. Where the house already held a heading's answer, that carrier is cited first; the web pass adds only what it lacked.

## What the house already held

Before this pass, the house's carriers already held a developed theory of hope, attention, care, and content-mill economics — denser in places than the secular literature this brief asked for.

**Hope's two failure modes.** The philosophy seat's *Note on Abandonment, Acedia, and the Whirl* reads Aquinas (*Summa Theologiae* II-II qq. 17-21) as naming **despair** and **presumption** as hope's two opposed vices: despair treats the open future as settled loss, presumption as settled possession, and "hope is not a third judgment but the virtue of withholding the closure." The same carrier names the industrial-scale media formats these vices take: "the economy of commentary runs on certainty ... its two staple products are the doom-take ... and the solution-take ... despair and presumption as content formats" — and recovers **lament**, "complaint within relation ... without closure in either direction," as the genre the take-economy has no room for. A companion computer-science carrier, *The Unbuilt Affects*, proposes hope as a position where a resolving continuation exists but is held open rather than collapsed to true or false, reading cruel optimism the same way: "hope in a genre that has none." Both carriers grade their own claims honestly, the second explicitly as proposal, never proven theorem.

**Attention as supplied, not self-generated, continuity.** The founder's own essay, *Why Doomscrolling Feels Easy and Reading Feels Hard*, distinguishes reactive mode (ventral attention network, fast reward-seeking) from reflective mode (dorsal attention network, slow integration), states "the brain grows in the direction of what you practice most," and lists counter-practices (scroll windows, ending sessions with longer reading, noticing bodily arousal, no feed before bed, talking about what was read). A companion triage paper states the mechanism in the house's own geometry: the feed supplies "externally supplied continuity" that lowers the reader's own cost of continuing, so the reader supplies none and cannot easily stop. (That second carrier uses an idiosyncratic invented formalism, "Existential Stratified Directed Infinity-Homotopy Geometry," not an established mathematical field; it is reported as house theoretical framing, not external science.)

**Care as a records discipline.** The house's medical program carrier states its paradigm plainly: "Care is relation-repair, never extraction," operationalized as "no upsell, no dark pattern, no data sale, no subscription trap, no diagnosis sold as certainty," with "every claim carries its evidence status in plain words" — the house's own answer to the brief's fourth question, stated as design value rather than derived from outside literature.

**Content mills, from the inside.** Two 2026-08-20 science-seat memos measure the industry in more dated detail than this session's web pass recovered independently (see Content Farms below, each such figure marked). The house also holds a first-party negative example: the founder's 2018 log records founding "NTMY News, a content mill" (2015-06) and promoting a conspiracy meme through it; its remains sit in legacy code as revival inventory. The cybernetic-postliberalism glossary supplies a parts list for what the take-economy ships — *californication*, *savior-slave subject*, *socially ontogenic media*, *coherent confusion*, *industrial intellectualism*, *neurotic platformal intellectual*, *zen fascism* — each naming a mechanism by which structural contradiction is reformatted as manageable personal affect. These are house-coined terms, reported as house theory attributed to carrier, not established fact.

The web pass below fills the named gaps: Snyder's scales, learned helplessness's revision, Tronto's phases, the care economy's size, Reuters Institute figures, wellness-app outcomes, platform internal research, community/Indigenous media outcomes, and self-help replication literature — plus historical cases (Marx, Bernays, wartime propaganda, tobacco/pharma) the house does not measure.

## Religion: Marx's "opium of the people"

Marx's line is usually quoted as dismissal; the full sentence is not. Written 1843, published 1844: "Religion is the sigh of the oppressed creature, the heart of a heartless world, and the soul of soulless conditions. It is the opium of the people" ("Opium of the people - Wikipedia"). Marx's own gloss: abolishing religion as illusory happiness is a demand for people's *real* happiness — give up a condition that requires illusions, not merely the illusion. A Roskilde University treatment argues the phrase's context has received far less attention than the isolated quote. Read against the house's Aquinas material, this is structurally the same move the philosophy carrier makes about despair: naming real suffering without licensing its premature narcotic resolution.

## Mass advertising and public relations

Edward Bernays, "the father of public relations," published *Propaganda* (1928), recasting mass-persuasion as "public relations," then *The Engineering of Consent* (1947). His 1929 "Torches of Freedom" campaign for American Tobacco staged women smoking publicly at the Easter Parade, framed as liberation on a hired psychoanalyst's advice that smoking sublimated "oral eroticism"; women's cigarette-consumption share rose from 5% (1923) to 12% (1929). The tobacco industry's internal documents, forced into disclosure by a 1998 settlement (30 million pages), showed decades of concealment: a 1962 R.J. Reynolds memo already called the harm evidence "overwhelming" while executives discussed burying nicotine-addictiveness research. Pharmaceutical DTC advertising followed a parallel arc after the FDA relaxed broadcast rules in 1997: spend rose from $791M (1996) to $4.2B (2005) while FDA warning letters fell 142 to 21, and DTC explains ~31% of the rise in US drug spending since 1997. A randomized trial found brand-specific drug requests roughly double or quintuple prescribing rates by condition (53%/31% major depression; 55%/10% adjustment disorder) — measured evidence manufactured demand changes clinical outcomes, not just attitudes. Global ad spend crossed ~$1.08-1.17 trillion in 2025; PR is smaller and less consistently measured, $68.7-109 billion by vendor.

## Wartime and state propaganda

The US Committee on Public Information (Creel Committee, 1917-1919), the first large-scale government propaganda agency, placed material in 20,000 newspaper columns weekly and fielded 75,000 "Four-Minute Men" who delivered over 7.5 million speeches; remembered for suppressing dissent and cultivating wartime fear. The US Office of War Information (1942) printed 4 million poster sets 1943-1945; its total budget was not recovered (see Not Found). Nazi Germany's Propaganda Ministry (Jan. 1933, Goebbels) commissioned the cheap "Volksempfänger" radio (76 Reichsmarks, roughly two weeks' wages, or 35 RM) to expand the propaganda audience; by 1941, 65% of German households owned one, called a "vital element of success" in spreading Nazi ideology by one historian. No peer-reviewed study isolating the radio's causal persuasive effect from adoption alone was found (see Not Found). The house's theological carrier reframes hope's failure modes similarly — despair and presumption as premature closures of an open future — a parallel to propaganda foreclosing political judgment; the application to propaganda is this note's own observation, not the carrier's.

## Self-help and positive psychology industry

The global self-improvement market is estimated at $45.7-64.5 billion (2024-2025, vendor-dependent), the US market $11-16.5 billion/year. Peale's *The Power of Positive Thinking* (1952) sold over 5 million copies, 186 weeks on the NYT bestseller list (48 at #1); Byrne's *The Secret* (2006) sold over 35 million copies claiming a pseudoscientific "law of attraction." Ehrenreich's *Bright-Sided* (2009) documents that studies linking positive attitude to cancer survival are "full of problems and discounted by most researchers," arguing mandatory positive thinking pressures the ill to suppress legitimate fear. The clearest documented replication failure is Amy Cuddy's "power posing" (2010 study, a TED talk with 75M+ views), followed by failed replications (Ranehill et al. 2015: no effect on behavior or hormones) and co-author Dana Carney's 2016 public disavowal — though some pre-registered replications still find a modest self-reported-power effect even where hormonal effects vanish, a genuine disagreement. Positive psychology as a field is conventionally dated to Seligman's 1998 APA presidency.

## Hope as studied in psychology (Snyder, learned helplessness, Berlant)

C.R. Snyder's hope theory (1991, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 60(4), 570-585) defines hope as combining **agency** (motivational "willpower") and **pathways** (perceived routes, "waypower") toward a goal. His Adult Hope Scale is 12 items (4 pathways, 4 agency, 4 filler), scored 8-64, alphas .79-.95 across four samples; companion scales are the Children's Hope Scale (6 items) and State Hope Scale (6 items). The scales predict academic, athletic, and recovery outcomes beyond IQ, optimism, and self-efficacy. Criticism targets individualist bias (developed on American samples, agency assuming personal motivation rather than collectivist trust) and notes meta-analyses find only small effect sizes across contexts (Frontiers in Psychology, 2022). The exact per-sample n from 1991 was not recovered (see Not Found), nor does the house supply it. Snyder's requirement of a *pathway*, not just motivation, formally matches the house philosophy carrier's insistence that hope requires a real, held-open continuation rather than mere positive affect — independent convergence.

Seligman and Maier's 1967 study ("Failure to escape traumatic shock") found dogs given inescapable shock later failed to learn escape behavior, unlike dogs given escapable or no shock — the origin of "learned helplessness." Fifty years later, "Learned Helplessness at Fifty" (*Psychological Review* 123(4), 2016) **reversed the causal model**: passivity after inescapable shock is the default, unlearned response (serotonergic, dorsal raphe nucleus), and learning *control* (medial prefrontal cortex) is what actively suppresses that default. The theory "got it backward." This bears directly on the brief's fourth question: manufactured hope that never supplies actual control-learning cannot function like real hope, no matter how much positive affect is layered on.

Berlant's "cruel optimism" (2006/2011) names attachment to an object whose promise of flourishing is itself what obstructs it — persisting because it "provides a sense of continuity," not deception. Applied empirically to frontline poverty governance and post-2008 affective political economy; a citation count was not recovered (see Not Found). The house's philosophy and computer-science carriers sharpen this: cruel optimism is "hope in a genre that has none" — holding an open cell where the structure warrants a settled negative outcome, formally the mirror of despair (self-graded "[conjecture]"). The house's anarcho-nihilist term goes further, treating any *demand* for hope as coercive — "compels a psychological state as the price of political participation" — distinguished from faith-without-optimism (commitment not dependent on expected success). Three independent traditions (Snyder's pathway, the revised helplessness model's learned control, the house's genuinely-open cell) converge on one answer: **what separates manufactured hope from a real next step is whether an actual, actionable continuation exists**, not how much positive affect is produced.

## Wellness and meditation apps

Calm's 2025 revenue was an estimated $210 million (down 24% from 2024's ~$300M), 3.5 million subscribers (down 500,000); Headspace's paid subscribers fell to 2 million, though valued at $3 billion. Calm prices at $14.99/month or $69.99/year. Market-size estimates vary 2-3x across vendors ($701M-$2.2B for meditation apps; $4.97B-$26B for wellness apps broadly, 2025), reflecting inconsistent category boundaries. On outcomes: mindfulness-app RCTs show ~38% attrition (vs. 25% baseline elsewhere), heaviest in the first two weeks; real-world adherence is as low as 24%, and only ~2% of health-app users sustain benefit-level engagement (ScienceDirect 2023; PubMed). Yet completers show real benefit: one three-armed RCT randomized 343, 186 (54.2%) completed a post-test, and completers showed larger gains in distress, connectedness, and mindfulness versus waitlist controls. This is a genuine, unresolved tension: the apps work for people who use them, and most do not use them enough — a live instance of the brief's distinction between hope requiring ongoing structural support (the pathway) and hope sold as if presence alone (downloading the app) sufficed.

## Short-video and feed platforms

TikTok's feed is compared by ex-Google ethicist Tristan Harris to a slot machine: variable-reward uncertainty reinforces compulsive scrolling via the same schedule that drives gambling — a framing Harris traces to his 2013 internal Google memo. A leaked 2024 internal TikTok document ("TikTok Algo 101") and litigation disclosures showed a 2021 change meant to reduce harmful-content overwhelm was withheld from a 10% (~15 million) US-user control group to preserve engagement-metric measurement. Facebook's own internal research, leaked by whistleblower Frances Haugen ("Facebook Files," 2021), found researchers concluding "we make body image issues worse for one in three teen girls," and that 13% of UK and 6% of US teens with suicidal ideation traced the cause to Instagram — research the company kept from academics and lawmakers; Zuckerberg called it "inconclusive." Haugen's Senate testimony alleged the company chose "to prioritize its profits over people." TikTok creator payouts rose ~10-25x when the 2024 Creator Rewards Program replaced the 2020-2023 Creator Fund ($0.02-0.04/1,000 views, fixed shared pool) with $0.40-1.00+/1,000 qualified views. The 2023 US Surgeon General advisory found >3 hrs/day of social media roughly doubles the risk of poor mental health outcomes, with 95% of teens on a platform and 64% reporting hate-content exposure. On "digital wellbeing" features, evidence disagrees: Mozilla's crowdsourced "YouTube Regrets" study documented algorithmic rabbit-holing while a separate 2020 study found the opposite — the algorithm discouraging movement toward extremism; similarly, one 2026 study found Apple's Downtime added 45 minutes of nightly sleep and Android's Bedtime Mode raised sleep-satisfaction 35%, while another found no significant screen-time effect from nudges at all. Both are reported as genuine, unresolved tensions.

## Care as studied in ethics and organization

Joan Tronto's ethics of care (*Moral Boundaries*, 1993) identifies four cyclical phases — **caring about** (attentiveness), **taking care of** (responsibility), **care-giving** (competence), **care-receiving** (responsiveness) — later adding a fifth, **caring with** (shared deciding). Responsiveness is decisive for the brief's fourth question: it requires actually checking whether care met the need, independent of how attentive or competent earlier phases were — a relation that never closes this loop fails Tronto's own test regardless of good intentions elsewhere. The global care economy is valued, at hourly minimum wage, at 9% of global GDP ($11 trillion) — 16.4 billion hours/day of unpaid work (2 billion people's worth of 8-hour days); women perform 76.2% of it (4h25m/day vs. men's 1h23m) (ILO 2018). A "high-road" scenario models 475 million jobs by 2030 requiring $18.4 trillion (18.3% of GDP); regional variation is large (~21.4% of GDP in Latin America/Caribbean vs. ~15% OECD). On care as a media design value: a review of 22 experiments/19 studies on constructive/solutions journalism found consistent positive emotional effects but *inconsistent* trust effects — one study found opposite-direction trust results in US vs. Australian samples, stated as unresolved in the source. The house's medical-program carrier states care as a records discipline ("no upsell, no dark pattern, no data sale, no subscription trap") rather than a tone — the house's own stated design value, not independently peer-reviewed.

## Doomscrolling and news avoidance

Multiple independent 2022-2024 studies find doomscrolling predicts higher depression, PTSD symptoms, anxiety, reduced life satisfaction, and existential pessimism — not relief (Price et al. 2022; Satici et al. 2023; ScienceDirect 2024, Iran/US replication). Reuters Institute finds overall news avoidance flat at 42% (2026), up from 29% (2017); *selective* avoidance (managing specific distressing topics, distinct from general disengagement) has grown sharply — 50% (UK), 45% (US) actively reduce news intake, 60%+ in Bulgaria, Croatia, Greece, Turkey. Separately, selective avoidance shows a small *positive* effect on perceived wellbeing — the same behavior read as protective in wellbeing research and as a civic-information deficit elsewhere, a tension stated rather than resolved. The founder's essay frames the mechanism identically: reactive-mode engagement produces "attentional fragmentation" that makes reflective engagement progressively harder — house and external literature converge without citing each other.

## Content farms and AI-generated media

NewsGuard's tracking center counted 3,749 "Unreliable AI-Generated News" sites as of the most recent count (2026-09-05), up from 217 (April 2023) — growing 300-500 new sites/month. The house's own content-farm memos (through June 2026) supply sharper figures this session's web pass did not independently recover: the "AutoBait" MFA network of 200+ domains producing pages for under $2.25 each; 278 fully AI-generated YouTube channels amassing 63 billion views, 221 million subscribers, ~$117M/year revenue, out of a sample where >20% of videos recommended to new accounts were AI slop; ~35% of newly published websites AI-generated/assisted by mid-2025; NELA-PS's "pink-slime" dataset of 7.9 million articles/1,093 sources; the 2026 Nota News collapse after Poynter found 70+ plagiarized examples across 29 outlets/53 journalists; and 358 AI content farms tied to the pro-Russian Storm-1516 operation. Historically, Demand Media (2009) paid ~$15/article, ~$20/video; Google's 2011 "Panda" update changed 12% of US search results; MFA sites took 21% of programmatic impressions and 15% of ad spend (ANA 2023). AI-detection accuracy varies by vendor design: Pangram reports ~99% accuracy, ~0.004% false positives but 2-4% false negatives; GPTZero <1% false positives but 10.02% false negatives — a trade-off, not one tool simply better. The house holds a negative example: the founder's own mill, NTMY News (2015-06), framed as "revival inventory" already "refused ... once."

## Community, public, and Indigenous media

US federal public-broadcasting funding (CPB) was $525-535 million/year (2024-25) before Congress rescinded ~$1.1 billion in slated funding via the 2025 Rescissions Act; CPB announced it would wind down after zero future funding was approved — the first time in its history. NPR received only ~1% of funding directly from government (~$11M/year), PBS ~16% — understating dependence for smaller local/rural/tribal stations reliant on CPB pass-through grants. In Australia, NITV reaches 3.3 million Australians monthly (growing against a declining linear-TV market), established with AUD 48.5 million (2005) and recommended ongoing funding of AUD 15.235 million/year (2010) — though a separate source states Indigenous media funding has seen "no real funding increase in a decade." A study of Indigenous radio station La Voz de la CONFENIAE (Ecuadorian Amazon) found Indigenous communities referenced emotional/social wellbeing more than comparison groups, tying language/cultural programming to measured wellbeing. Community broadcasting shows measured economic, social, and cultural benefits, including direct employment and wellbeing benefits for participants.

## Synthesis: what separates manufactured hope from a real next step

Four independent lines converge on the same answer, none citing the others:

1. **Snyder's hope theory** requires both agency *and* a pathway — a perceived, actionable route — or the state collapses into mere wishing, not hope.
2. **The revised learned-helplessness model** (Maier & Seligman, 2016) requires *learned control*, not positive affect, to suppress default passivity — hope without control-learning does nothing biologically.
3. **The house's own philosophy and computer-science carriers** require a resolving continuation that is genuinely held open, not falsely foreclosed in either direction — cruel optimism is diagnosed as holding an open cell where the true structure has none.
4. **Tronto's ethics of care** requires the responsiveness phase to close the loop — actually checking whether a need was met — regardless of how attentive or competent the earlier phases were.

No single source states this as one claim, but it is recoverable across all four: manufactured hope is distinguished from care not by tone or the amount of positive affect produced, but by whether an actual, actionable, verifiable continuation exists and is checked — a structural, falsifiable criterion, not a psychological or rhetorical one.
