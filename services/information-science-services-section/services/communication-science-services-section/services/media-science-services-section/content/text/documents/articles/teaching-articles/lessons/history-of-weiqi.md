Weiqi&rsquo;s history is a long record of transmission and institutional refinement: a game that began in China, spread across East Asia, and later became a global competitive and educational tradition.

## Worked example: one game, three names

The same game is commonly called *weiqi* in Chinese, *baduk* in Korean, and *go* in Japanese-derived English usage [@britannica2026go; @igf2026historyofgo]. Those names mark more than translation. They show the route by which the game moved across linguistic and institutional settings while keeping a recognizable rules core.

## Origins in China

The game originated in China more than two millennia ago, and some accounts place it much earlier [@britannica2026go; @igf2026historyofgo]. Early Chinese traditions treated it not only as recreation but as a cultivated art associated with scholarly accomplishment.

What matters historically is durability. Weiqi kept the same basic logic of placement, enclosure, and capture across centuries even as rule explanations, board conventions, and social settings changed.

## Transmission across East Asia

Weiqi spread from China to Korea and Japan, where it developed strong local traditions and institutions [@igf2026historyofgo]. Japanese professional houses later played a major role in codifying teaching lineages, recording famous games, and circulating the name *go* beyond Japan.

This transmission changed how the game was taught and organized, but not its basic identity. The core mechanics remained territorial placement, [capture](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/board-games-services-section/services/weiqi-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/capture.md), and shape judgment. The major historical shifts were institutional and cultural rather than mechanical.

## Modern international play

In the twentieth century, weiqi expanded far beyond East Asia through national associations, translated teaching materials, and international federations [@igf2026historyofgo; @britgo2026whatisgo]. That growth made rank systems, tournament rules, and teaching methods more legible to new players outside the game&rsquo;s older centers.

One important feature of that modern spread is accessibility across skill levels. Organizations outside East Asia often present [handicap](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/board-games-services-section/services/weiqi-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/handicap.md) play as a normal and valuable part of the game, not as a lesser version of it [@britgo2026whatisgo]. That emphasis helped weiqi travel as both a competitive discipline and a teachable social practice.

## Exercises

- What does the existence of the names *weiqi*, *baduk*, and *go* show about the game&rsquo;s history?

Answer

It shows that the game spread across different linguistic and institutional settings while preserving a recognizable core structure.

- What changed most as weiqi spread internationally: its basic mechanics or its institutions of teaching and competition?

Answer

Its institutions changed more than its basic mechanics. The game kept the same territorial placement logic while different regions developed their own teaching systems, organizations, and naming traditions.
