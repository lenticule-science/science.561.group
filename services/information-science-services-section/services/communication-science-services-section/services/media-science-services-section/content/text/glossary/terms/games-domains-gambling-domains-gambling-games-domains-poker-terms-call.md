To call in [poker](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/index.md) is to match the current wager so the player can remain in the [hand](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hand.md) [@britannica2026poker]. Calling neither ends the action nor increases the price for later players. It accepts the current price of continuation.

Calling matters because poker decisions are comparative. A player calls when the expected value of continuing at the current price is better than [folding](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/fold.md), but not strong enough to justify a [raise](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/raise.md). This relation between hand strength, draw potential, and price is one of poker&rsquo;s central analytical problems [@sklansky1999].
