A hand in [poker](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/index.md) is a set of cards held by or assigned to a player, used to determine the outcome of a round [@britannica2026poker]. The term has two related meanings: the specific cards a player holds and a complete round of play from deal to [showdown](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/showdown.md).

Poker hands are ranked by a hierarchy that reflects the probability of being dealt each combination from a standard [deck](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/deck.md). The rarest combinations rank highest: a royal flush is the strongest hand in most variants, while a high card is the weakest. Between these extremes, the standard ranking runs through pairs, two pair, three of a kind, straights, flushes, full houses, four of a kind, and straight flushes. Some poker variants modify this hierarchy or introduce additional categories.

The strength of a hand is always relative to what opponents hold, which is unknown until showdown. This incomplete information is what makes poker a game of skill rather than a pure lottery: players must infer the likely strength of opposing hands from betting patterns, position, and revealed community cards [@sklansky1999]. A hand that is statistically strong can still lose, and a weak hand can win the [pot](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/pot.md) if opponents [fold](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/fold.md) to a well-timed bet.
