The two-step flow model holds that mass-media messages do not reach a mass audience uniformly and directly;
instead, media influence reaches attentive, well-connected "opinion leaders" first, who then interpret and relay that influence to less-attentive people within their own interpersonal networks.
It is the founding formulation of the "limited effects" tradition,
which pushed back against earlier direct-effects ("hypodermic needle") assumptions about mass media power.

## Empirical origin: The People's Choice

Paul Lazarsfeld, Bernard Berelson & Hazel Gaudet, *The People's Choice: How the Voter Makes Up His Mind in a Presidential Campaign* (Duell, Sloan & Pearce, 1944; kept in print by Columbia University Press since 1948) ([Columbia University Press](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/the-peoples-choice/9780231197953/)),
reports a panel study tracking Erie County, Ohio, voters across the 1940 US presidential campaign.
Its central and, at the time, surprising finding was that face-to-face contact with other people shaped vote decisions more strongly than direct media exposure did —
the empirical seed from which the two-step flow hypothesis grew.

## Formalization: Personal Influence

Elihu Katz & Paul Lazarsfeld, *Personal Influence: The Part Played by People in the Flow of Mass Communications* (Free Press, 1955) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Personal-Influence)), reports the Decatur, Illinois study, examining decision-making across four domains —
marketing, fashion, public affairs, and movie selection —
and formally names and tests the two-step flow hypothesis:
information and influence flow from media to opinion leaders, and from opinion leaders to the wider population, rather than from media to everyone equally and directly.

Both *The People's Choice* and *Personal Influence* are Columbia Bureau of Applied Social Research studies rather than journal articles, but both function as this sub-literature's peer-reviewed-grade empirical foundation;
no separate textbook-canon source was needed to anchor this term.
