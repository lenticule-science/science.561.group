The pot in [poker](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/index.md) is the total amount [wagered](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/terms/wager.md) by all players during a [hand](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hand.md) [@sklansky1999]. It accumulates through antes, blinds, and bets made across the round&rsquo;s betting streets. The player who wins the hand - either by holding the strongest cards at [showdown](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/showdown.md) or by making all other players [fold](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/fold.md) - collects the pot.

Pot size influences strategy at every decision point. A large pot relative to the cost of [calling](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/call.md) a bet gives a player favorable pot odds, making it mathematically correct to continue with a wider range of hands. A small pot makes aggressive betting riskier because there is less to gain. Skilled players manipulate pot size deliberately: building it when they hold strong hands to maximize value, and keeping it small when their hand is marginal or when they want to see additional cards cheaply.

In some situations, a pot splits. A split pot occurs when two or more players hold hands of equal rank at showdown - for example, when the best possible hand is on the board in a community-card game. Side pots form when a player goes all in for less than a full bet: that player can win only the main pot, while remaining players contest a side pot with their additional bets.
