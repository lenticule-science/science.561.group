Atari in [weiqi](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/index.md) is the state in which a stone or connected group has only one [liberty](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/board-games-services-section/services/weiqi-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/liberty.md) remaining and is therefore under immediate threat of [capture](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/board-games-services-section/services/weiqi-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/capture.md) [@britgo2026howtoplaygo].

Atari matters because it turns a broad positional game into a forced tactical problem. When a group falls to one liberty, the next move often becomes urgent for both players: one side tries to capture, while the other tries to extend, connect, or create shape.
