Ludus is one pole of Roger Caillois&rsquo;s paidia-ludus spectrum: play that has been structured by [rules](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rules.md), constraints, and conventions — play that has become a game. Where [paidia](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/paidia.md) is spontaneous and free-form, ludus is disciplined and formalized. The movement from paidia to ludus is the movement from splashing in a pool to swimming laps, from noodling on a guitar to performing a sonata, from roughhousing to wrestling with weight classes and a referee. Ludus doesn&rsquo;t eliminate the energy of paidia; it channels it through structure.

The distinction matters because most of what the games module studies sits toward the ludus end of the spectrum. [Card games](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/topics/card-games/index.md), [role-playing games](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/topics/role-playing-games/index.md), [gambling](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/topics/gambling/index.md) — these activities have explicit rules, codified procedures, and stable structures that persist across individual sessions. Learning to play chess is a movement from paidia toward ludus: the beginner experiments freely, trying things to see what happens; the experienced [player](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/player.md) operates within internalized constraints, and the interest comes from the depth those constraints create.

Ludus is also where the four categories — [agon](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/agon.md), [alea](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/alea.md), [mimicry](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/mimicry.md), and [ilinx](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/ilinx.md) — become most visible as distinct structures. Unstructured play blends them freely, but as play formalizes, specific categories tend to dominate: a sport crystallizes agon, a lottery crystallizes alea, a theater production crystallizes mimicry. Ludus doesn&rsquo;t just add rules to play; it clarifies what kind of play is being organized and what kind of experience it aims to produce.
