A character is the fictional persona a player portrays in a [role-playing game](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/index.md). Characters are the primary interface between player and fiction: the player decides what the character attempts, and the game&rsquo;s rules and [game master](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/role-playing-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/game-master.md) determine what happens as a result.

Most systems represent characters through a combination of narrative elements (name, background, personality, goals) and mechanical attributes (statistics, skills, hit points, special abilities). The balance varies: some games emphasize detailed mechanical builds while others reduce mechanical representation to a few descriptors and let the fiction carry the weight.

[Character creation](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/role-playing-games-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/character-creation.md) is often the first creative act a player performs in a new [campaign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/archive/campaign.md). The choices made during creation — ancestry, class, backstory, motivations — shape what kinds of stories the game can tell. Characters created in isolation sometimes clash; [session zero](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/session-zero.md) exists partly to ensure that the group&rsquo;s characters form a coherent ensemble.
