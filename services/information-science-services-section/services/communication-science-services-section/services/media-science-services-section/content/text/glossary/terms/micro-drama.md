A micro-drama is a scripted serial made for a phone held upright: episodes of one to two minutes, fifty to a hundred of them to a story, with a cliffhanger at the end of each. The first few episodes are free and the rest are unlocked per episode with coins or a subscription. The plots come from Chinese web-novel formulas: the hidden billionaire, the contract marriage, revenge, reincarnation and second chances, the underestimated heir. The format grew up in China as 短剧 (duǎnjù) and is distinct from the long-form C-drama of forty-odd hour-long episodes.

Chinese-owned apps took the format to the United States by producing it in English with American casts rather than translating Chinese shows. ReelShort (Crazy Maple Studio) and DramaBox are the largest, with ReelShort reported past 370 million downloads ([Entertain Lens](https://entertainlens.com/chinese-short-drama-apps-us-market/)). Production is fast and cheap by television standards: a series shoots in about a week, and nonunion leads earn about $500–1,500 a day by 2026 search synthesis ([Stageblind](https://stageblind.com/micro-drama-actor-pay)). DramaBox opened a US writer incubator with Stage 32 in December 2025, saying 30–40 vertical series shoot in Los Angeles every month and each needs a script ([Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/dramabox-seeks-new-u-s-writers-stage32-1236436618/)).

The economics behind it are the Chinese firms' going-abroad pattern (see [the 2026-09-26 note on what Chinese firms going abroad buy from US residents](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-what-chinese-firms-going-abroad-buy-from-us-residents.md)): the platform, the formula and the capital are Chinese, and the writers, actors and crews are hired in the market.
