A house rule is a change to a game&rsquo;s published or traditional [rules](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rules.md) adopted by a particular group of [players](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/player.md). House rules are local: they apply at this table, in this group, for these sessions. They don&rsquo;t change the game in general; they change the game *as played here*.

House rules emerge for many reasons. A rule may feel unfair, slow, confusing, or poorly suited to the group&rsquo;s preferred style. A missing rule may need to be invented (what happens when two players tie?). A group may want to adjust difficulty, remove content that makes someone uncomfortable, or adapt a game to available materials. In [role-playing games](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/topics/role-playing-games/index.md), house rules are pervasive — most groups modify their [system](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/system.md) to some degree. In [card games](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/topics/card-games/index.md), regional variants often began as house rules that spread.

Structurally, house rules sit between the game-as-designed and the game-as-played. They reveal what players actually value — when someone changes a rule, they are making a claim about what the game should feel like. Studying house rules across groups can surface design problems that formal playtesting misses.
