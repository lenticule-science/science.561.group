A deck is the bounded set of cards used as the core material of play in a [card game](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/ludics/games/index.md). A deck can be standardized — the 52-card French-suited deck (four [suits](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/suit.md), thirteen [ranks](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/card-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rank.md) each) is the most widely used — or custom to a specific game, as in trading card games and many modern board-adjacent card games.

Deck composition is the most fundamental design parameter in any card game. It determines the probability landscape: the number of cards in each [suit](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/suit.md), the distribution of [ranks](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/card-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rank.md), and the total card count all shape what [hands](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hand.md) are likely, what information is hidden, and how many meaningful decisions fit within a single round. A standard 52-card deck supports an enormous variety of games because its structure — four suits, evenly distributed — provides a balanced foundation that different [mechanics](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/mechanic.md) can exploit in different ways.

Changing the deck is a major [rules](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/rules.md) change, not cosmetic flavor. Removing cards (as in stripped-deck games like piquet or German-suited games that use 32 or 36 cards) concentrates high-value cards and changes the odds of every combination. Adding cards (jokers as [wild cards](../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/services/being-services-section/services/doing-services-section/services/playing-services-section/services/gaming-services-section/services/card-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/wild-card.md), extra suits, promotional inserts) dilutes the deck and introduces new strategic possibilities. Custom decks designed for a single game (Uno, Set, Dominion) can encode asymmetry, special actions, and scoring directly into the card distribution rather than relying on external rules to interpret a standard deck.
