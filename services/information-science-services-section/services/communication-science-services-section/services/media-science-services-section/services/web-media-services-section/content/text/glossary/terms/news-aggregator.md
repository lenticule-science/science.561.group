A news aggregator collects articles from many publishers — typically by consuming [syndicated](./web-syndication.md) feeds or structured data rather than through original reporting — and re-presents them in one place: a dedicated feed reader, a search engine's dedicated News surface, or a general social-media platform's timeline algorithm.
Aggregators sit structurally between publishers and readers, which gives them power over referral traffic that publishers do not fully control.
Rasmus Kleis Nielsen and Sarah Anne Ganter's peer-reviewed case study, "Dealing with Digital Intermediaries" (*New Media & Society*, 2018), documents this relationship from the publisher side: editors and product staff describing short-term benefit from aggregator-driven traffic alongside long-term strategic fear of dependency on platforms they do not control (see [the article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1461444817701318)).
Their 2022 book, *The Power of Platforms* (Oxford University Press), extends the same analysis across the US, France, Germany, and the UK.

The dependency risk that literature warned about has since been observed directly.
Per the Reuters Institute's *Digital News Report 2024*, referral traffic from Facebook to news sites fell 48% year-on-year and from X fell 27%, attributed to those platforms deliberately deprioritizing news and out-links in favor of proprietary/video content (see the [2024 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/dnr-executive-summary), which states the 48%/27% figures directly).
A specific volume figure once attached to this claim here — Facebook referrals dropping from roughly 2.3 billion in December 2022 to roughly 1.2 billion in November 2023 — could not be located in that executive summary or in the [Press Gazette piece](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/media_metrics/facebooks-referral-traffic-for-publishers-down-50-in-12-months/) it was sourced to, and has been removed as **[UNVERIFIED]**; that Press Gazette piece instead reports a 792-site aggregate decline from 1.3 billion (March 2018) to 561 million (May 2024).
This is distinct from Google's AMP program and the June 2021 removal of AMP as a requirement for mobile Top Stories placement, which concerned page performance and search-result eligibility rather than social referral volume, but both are instances of an aggregator or intermediary changing the rules by which it surfaces publisher content, largely unilaterally.

For the fuller literature — including the specific edition-by-edition Digital News Report figures this term should be re-checked against before reuse — see the [study note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-web-news-media.md), §2.
