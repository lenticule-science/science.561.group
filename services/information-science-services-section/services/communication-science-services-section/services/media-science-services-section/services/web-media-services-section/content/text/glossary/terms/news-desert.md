A news desert is a community — rural or urban — with little or no access to credible, comprehensive local news and information.
The term originated more narrowly as "a town without a newspaper," since newspapers were historically the primary source of local news in small and mid-sized US communities; Penelope Muse Abernathy's research program, begun at UNC's Hussman School (Center for Innovation and Sustainability in Local Media) and continued at Northwestern's Medill School, broadened the definition to "a place where there is limited access to the type of critical news and information ... needed to make informed decisions about the quality of ... life" (see [UNC](https://www.unc.edu/discover/well-said-news-deserts/) and [CJR's Q&A with Abernathy](https://www.cjr.org/business_of_news/the-context-for-the-crisis-a-qa-with-penny-abernathy.php)).

Northwestern Medill's annual *State of Local News* report is the current authoritative count, an institutional-report-tier project rather than a peer-reviewed publication.
Its 2024 edition counted 208 news-desert counties (no locally based news source at all), up from 204 in 2023, plus 1,563 counties with only one remaining news source, covering nearly 55 million people with limited or no local-news access (see [Northwestern's 2024 coverage](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2024/10/medill-report-shows-local-news-deserts-expanding?fj=1)).
The 2025 edition put the count at 213 news-desert counties, noted that over 60 counties had lost local news entirely since 2005, and introduced a "Watch List" of 250 further counties judged to have a 40% likelihood of losing local news within a decade, with 136 outlets having disappeared in the preceding year alone (see [the 2025 report](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/projects/state-of-local-news/2025/)).
Because these county counts move year to year, this term and any work citing it should reference a specific dated edition rather than "the State of Local News report" generically.

News-desert decline is one of the structural conditions [pink-slime journalism](./pink-slime-journalism.md) networks have been documented exploiting: Bengani's Tow Center investigations describe pink-slime sites explicitly targeting communities that have already lost independent local coverage.
