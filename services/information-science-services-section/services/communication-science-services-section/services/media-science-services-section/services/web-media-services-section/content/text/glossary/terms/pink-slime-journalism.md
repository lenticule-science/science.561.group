Pink-slime journalism describes networks of websites that mimic the look and naming conventions of ordinary local newspapers — often using town or county names — while actually distributing large volumes of algorithmically generated stories, syndicated wire content, and partisan talking points from a centralized operation, with little to no original local reporting and typically undisclosed or obfuscated ownership.
The term was popularized by Priyanjana Bengani, then the Tow Center for Digital Journalism's computational journalism fellow, in "Hundreds of 'Pink Slime' Local News Outlets Are Distributing Algorithmic Stories and Conservative Talking Points" (Tow Center / *Columbia Journalism Review*, December 2019), which identified at least 450 such sites spanning 21 networks operated by five corporate entities — 189 of them tied to a single operation, Metric Media, launched across ten states within roughly a year — and found that a two-week RSS-feed sample of just the 189 Metric Media sites (starting November 26, 2019) had produced over 15,000 unique algorithmic stories in that window, not, as an earlier version of this term stated, "15,000 per week across four related networks" (see [the investigation](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center_reports/hundreds-of-pink-slime-local-news-outlets-are-distributing-algorithmic-stories-conservative-talking-points.php)).
A 2020 follow-up, "As Election Looms, a Network of Mysterious 'Pink Slime' Local News Outlets Nearly Triples in Size," documented growth to roughly 1,200 sites in under a year (see [the follow-up](https://www.cjr.org/analysis/as-election-looms-a-network-of-mysterious-pink-slime-local-news-outlets-nearly-triples-in-size.php)).

Both investigations are Tow Center (Columbia Journalism School) research-center work distributed through CJR — institutional-report tier, methodologically closer to research than ordinary trade reporting, but not independent peer-reviewed publication.
Pink-slime networks are commonly discussed alongside, but are analytically distinct from, AI-generated content-farm sites tracked more recently by services like NewsGuard (see the study note's §6 below): pink-slime sites were originally defined by centralized/partisan direction and algorithmic story generation, predating the more recent wave of generative-AI text specifically, though the two phenomena have converged in newer networks.

For the fuller literature, including the tier distinction between this institutional-report work and academic peer review, see the [study note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-web-news-media.md), §6.
