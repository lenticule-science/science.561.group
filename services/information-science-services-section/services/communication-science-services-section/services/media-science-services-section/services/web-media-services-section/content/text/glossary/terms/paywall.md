A paywall is a mechanism restricting access to news content to paying subscribers, ranging from a "hard" paywall (nothing accessible without payment) to a "metered" paywall (a fixed number of free articles per period before payment is required) to "freemium" models mixing free and subscriber-only content by section or content type.
Merja Myllylahti's peer-reviewed comparative study, "Newspaper Paywalls—the Hype and the Reality" (*Digital Journalism*, 2014), examined paywall models across the US, UK, Slovakia, Slovenia, Poland, Australia, New Zealand, and Finland and found paywalls contributed roughly 10% of the studied corporations' publishing/circulation revenues, with the metered model the most common structure among Western newspapers and premium/local-affiliation content typically reserved for subscribers while wire copy and breaking news stayed open (see [the article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/21670811.2013.813214)).

Consumer willingness to pay has been tracked since by the Reuters Institute's annual Digital News Report: per its 2023 edition, roughly 83% of respondents across 20 countries paid nothing for news, with at least a third of existing subscribers in that period cancelling or renegotiating price; per its 2024 edition, only about 17% were willing to pay anything and 57% were unwilling to pay at all — a pattern read by industry commentators as a plateau rather than continued growth (see the [INMA summary](https://www.inma.org/blogs/reader-revenue/post.cfm/new-data-how-many-consumers-are-willing-to-pay-for-online-news) and [the 2024 report itself](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/how-much-do-people-pay-online-news-what-might-encourage-others-pay)).
Paywalls sit in tension with the reach a publisher needs from [news aggregators](./news-aggregator.md) and [web syndication](./web-syndication.md): content locked behind a hard paywall is by definition unavailable to aggregation and structured-data surfaces that depend on openly crawlable article content.

For the fuller literature, including which figures come from peer-reviewed research versus secondary summaries of institutional survey data, see the [study note](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-web-news-media.md), §5.
