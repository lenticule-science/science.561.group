Computational journalism is not one undifferentiated practice.
Mark Coddington's ["Clarifying Journalism's Quantitative Turn"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21670811.2014.976400) (*Digital Journalism* 3(3), 2015, pp. 331–348) supplies the field's governing typology, distinguishing **computer-assisted reporting**, **data journalism**, and **computational journalism** proper by their underlying journalistic values and practices
rather than treating them as synonyms.

Nicholas Diakopoulos's *[Automating the News: How Algorithms Are Rewriting the Media](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674976986)* (Harvard University Press, 2019) is the anchor monograph treating algorithmic and automated journalism as a socio-technical practice,
documenting automation as supplementing rather than replacing newsroom labor and proposing accountability mechanisms for algorithmically produced content.

**Automated narrative generation** specifically has its own sub-literature.
Matt Carlson's ["The Robotic Reporter"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21670811.2014.976412) (*Digital Journalism* 3(3), 2015, pp. 416–431) analyzes the automated-journalism provider Narrative Science and journalists' published reactions to it as a contest over journalistic authority and labor,
not merely a technical-capability question.
Christer Clerwall's ["Enter the Robot Journalist"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17512786.2014.883116) (*Journalism Practice* 8(5), 2014, pp. 519–531) is the founding reader-perception experiment:
respondents rated software-generated news as more descriptive and "boring" than human-written news but comparably objective, and could not reliably distinguish the two by authorship —
the peer-reviewed basis for any claim that readers "can't tell" automated from human-written news.

The Associated Press's 2014 deployment of Automated Insights' Wordsmith platform to automate corporate-earnings stories —
expanding coverage from roughly 300 to roughly 4,400 companies per quarter without displacing reporters —
is the field's most-cited industry deployment, documented in trade press ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2015/robot-writing-increased-aps-earnings-stories-by-tenfold/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/07/01/the-ap-is-using-robots-to-write-earnings-reports)) rather than a peer-reviewed case study;
it should be cited to that trade coverage, not treated as academically studied in its own right.
