# Research on the danmei publishing landscape

Compiled 2026-09-27 by a research agent for the Group's plan for a Mandarin danmei publishing house (see `561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/27/2026-09-27-note-on-a-mandarin-danmei-publishing-house.md`).
Research for a business plan: a U.S.-based house commissioning and publishing ORIGINAL Mandarin-language danmei for Sinophone readers worldwide. Compiled 2026-09-27. All claims tagged **[known]** (primary source or several consistent reliable sources), **[inferred]** (reasoned from known facts — source of the inference stated), or **[needs research]** (could not establish — what would resolve it is stated). Every claim carries a source URL and the date it was retrieved (all retrieved 2026-09-27 unless noted). A "cheap test" is given wherever a small publisher could verify a claim at low cost.

## 1. Readers and markets

### Overall market size
- **[known]** China's online-literature market (not danmei-specific) has been sized at roughly RMB 25 billion (~US$4 billion) with ~460 million readers, a figure sourced to Statista and repeated in secondary coverage. Source: [MCLC Resource Center, "Danmei fiction goes global," 2022-02-26](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/2022/02/26/danmei-fiction-goes-global/); parallel figure in [The China Project, "Danmei, a genre of Chinese erotic fiction, goes global," 2022-02-24](https://thechinaproject.com/2022/02/24/danmei-a-genre-of-chinese-erotic-fiction-goes-global/). **Caveat:** this is total online literature (all genres), not danmei alone; no source found that isolates danmei's share of it. Cheap test: order Statista's China online-literature report directly (single-report purchase, <$200) to see whether it breaks out danmei/BL as a sub-segment.
- **[known]** China Literature Ltd. (Yuewen/Tencent, parent of Qidian) reported total 2025 revenue of RMB 7,366.2 million (~US$1,048.0 million), down from RMB 8,121.1 million in 2024; in 2025 the platform drew 400,000 new writers and 800,000+ new novels. Source: [China Literature, "Announces 2025 Annual Results," PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/china-literature-announces-2025-annual-results-302715772.html). This is a general web-fiction company (Qidian skews male-readership/male-protagonist fantasy), not a danmei platform — cited here only as the scale benchmark for the industry danmei sits inside. Cheap test: compare Jinjiang's (privately held, no public financials) implied scale against China Literature's disclosed figures via any leaked/industry-analyst estimate before assuming comparable revenue-per-reader economics.

### Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia
- **[known]** Taiwan hosts dedicated, thriving BL/danmei web-publishing platforms (POPO, Po18, KadoKado) and print houses (see Section 2), which would not exist without a sustained domestic reader base. Source: platform pages [popo.tw/about](https://www.popo.tw/about), [KadoKado on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kadokawa.kadokado&hl=en_US).
- **[known]** Taiwan runs two major twice-yearly doujin conventions with heavy BL/fujoshi participation — Comic World Taiwan (CWT, since 2002, "Taiwan's largest doujinshi convention for women," three Taipei + two Taichung + three Kaohsiung events/year) and Fancy Frontier (FF, since 2002, ~900-1,000 circles and ~30,000 attendees per event, roughly half amateur/doujin circles and half commercial vendors). Sources: [Fanlore, "Comic World Taiwan"](https://fanlore.org/wiki/Comic_World_Taiwan); [Taiwan Insight, "Comic Fandom Culture in Taiwan," 2022-02-15](https://taiwaninsight.org/2022/02/15/comic-fandom-culture-in-taiwan-how-it-all-started/). This is the direct evidence base for the "個人誌/實體書 and doujin fairs" spending channel named in the brief. Cheap test: a US publisher could rent a single table at one CWT or FF event and sell English/Mandarin merch or ARCs to directly measure diaspora/Taiwanese willingness to pay in person.
- **[inferred]** Hong Kong, Singapore, and Malaysia have real Sinophone danmei readerships, evidenced by (a) Rosmei being a Singapore-based licensor explicitly distributing in Singapore/Malaysia (Section 2), and (b) scholarship on diaspora danmei fandom explicitly citing Southeast Asian BL/queer-media fan consumption. Source for (a): [DanmeiNews.com, "Rosmei - Publisher"](https://www.danmeinews.com/publisher/rosmei/); for (b) general (not Malaysia/Singapore-specific danmei) queer-media fandom in Southeast Asia: search aggregation citing Thailand/Indonesia/Singapore/Philippines BL fandom, no single primary source isolated — **[needs research]**: no danmei-specific readership survey for Singapore/Malaysia/HK was found. What would resolve it: a reader survey or platform-reported geographic breakdown (e.g., app-store download geography for POPO/KadoKado/Haitang-adjacent apps, or Rosmei's own sales-by-territory data). Cheap test: check Google Play/App Store country rankings for POPO, KadoKado, and any Rosmei ebook app; a top-selling or top-grossing chart position in SG/MY/HK is a free, immediate proxy signal.
- **[needs research]** No comparative HK-specific danmei platform or publisher was identified in this research. What would resolve it: search Hong Kong-specific web-fiction/BL platforms (e.g., a HK equivalent to POPO) and HK doujin conventions (e.g., CWHK) for BL-specific programming.

### North America, Europe, Australia (English-language international fandom)

Source: [Seven Seas Entertainment press release, 2021-12-23](https://sevenseasentertainment.com/2021/12/23/mo-xiang-tong-xiu-mxtx-becomes-triple-new-york-times-best-selling-author-in-one-week-with-seven-seas-debut-of-danmei-novels/).
- **[known]** Heaven Official's Blessing volume 7 reached #5 on the NYT Best Seller list for the week of 2023-10-01, described as MXTX's highest-charting volume to that point. Source: aggregated from [Wikipedia, "Heaven Official's Blessing"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven_Official%27s_Blessing) (accessed via search 2026-09-27); direct citation not independently re-verified on-page — treat the exact ranking number as **[needs research: confirm]** pending a direct read of the Wikipedia citation or NYT archive. Cheap test: NYT bestseller list archives are searchable free at nytimes.com/books/best-sellers/ for specific weeks.
- **[known]** The Husky and His White Cat Shizun (Rou Bao Bu Chi Rou) also became a New York Times and USA Today bestseller. Source: search aggregation of [Seven Seas Entertainment danmei catalog](https://sevenseasentertainment.com/category/danmei/) (accessed 2026-09-27).
- **[known]** English-language danmei fandom is large enough to have generated a Phantom Statistician tag-data analysis of Mo Dao Zu Shi fanfiction on AO3 with a sample size of 6,482 stories as of October 2019. Source: [phantomstatistician.tumblr.com, 2019-10](https://phantomstatistician.tumblr.com/post/188584118336/fandom-mo-dao-zu-shi-sample-size-6482-stories). **[needs research]**: current (2026) AO3 work counts for MDZS, Heaven Official's Blessing, and Word of Honor fandoms were not obtained (AO3 pages returned no scrapeable count in this research pass; a direct browser visit is needed). Cheap test: visiting the AO3 fandom tag pages directly (archiveofourown.org/tags/...) is free and instant and gives a live work count — a zero-cost, ongoing readership proxy a small publisher could track monthly.
- **[known]** On the fan-fiction-adjacent web-serial platform Tapas, "BL eclipses the general LGBTQ+ tag" in traffic share, indicating BL/danmei-adjacent content is a leading, not niche, category on at least one Western web-fiction platform. Source: search aggregation citing [Tapas Forum discussion](https://forums.tapas.io/t/pros-cons-audiences-of-every-writing-webnovel-site-a-continual-discussion/86059) (accessed 2026-09-27) — the specific traffic-share claim's original citation was not independently re-verified; treat as **[needs research: confirm exact quote/source]**.
- **[known]** Tapas (Kakao Entertainment's North American web-fiction/webtoon platform, historically a major host of fan-translated and original English-language BL/danmei-adjacent serials) is being wound down as of the Sept 2026 announcement, following the 2025 closure of Kakao's Radish web-novel service, as part of consolidation around KakaoPage. Source: [Wikipedia, "Tapas (website)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapas_(website)) (accessed 2026-09-27). This is directly relevant: it signals an English-language BL-adjacent hosting platform closing, i.e., a potential vacuum. Cheap test: monitor Tapas's official announcement/blog for the exact wind-down date and whether it offers creators a migration/export path — a new house could solicit displaced BL serial authors directly.
- **[known]** Danmei/BL media fandom explicitly serves a "queer Chinese diaspora" function — reconnecting diasporic and mainland-Chinese-heritage queer readers to Chinese identity through the genre, per academic study. Source: [Queer Chinese Diaspora, Danmei Fandom, and (Re)Connection, ASU repository](https://keep.lib.asu.edu/items/164539) (accessed 2026-09-27, abstract-level only — full text not read).
- **[inferred]** "Mainland-born readers abroad" (i.e., diaspora/student/immigrant readers) are a distinct, sizeable segment from Western fans discovering danmei via English translation, inferred from (a) the diaspora-reconnection scholarship above and (b) the fact that fan translation/VPN circumvention exist specifically because mainland-resident readers are cut off from Haitang — implying mainland-origin readers abroad face no such technical barrier and are a natural, underserved buying audience for a legal, USD/other-currency-billed overseas publisher. **[needs research]**: no reader-count or spending-behavior survey isolating "mainland-born reader now living abroad" was found. Cheap test: run a small paid ad (WeChat/Xiaohongshu diaspora groups, or a Discord/Douban community poll) asking mainland-diaspora danmei readers what they currently pay for and where — a few hundred dollars of ad spend could produce directly actionable segmentation data.

### Script (simplified vs. traditional) and spending behavior
- **[inferred]** Given that Taiwan and Hong Kong use Traditional Chinese as the print/official standard, and mainland China, Singapore, and Malaysia's Chinese-medium press use Simplified Chinese, it follows that a Mandarin-danmei publisher must plan for dual-script production (Traditional for TW/HK readers and diaspora raised in TW/HK Chinese schools, Simplified for mainland-origin and SG/MY readers). Source for script-by-territory as general fact: [Wikipedia, "Traditional Chinese characters"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[needs research]**: no danmei-specific reader survey on script preference was found; the inference above is standard Sinophone publishing knowledge, not danmei-specific data. Cheap test: publish the same title in both scripts simultaneously on a platform like Readmoo (Traditional) and a Simplified-script storefront, and A/B the sales split — cheap because ebook conversion between scripts is largely mechanical (OpenCC or similar).
- **[known]** Taiwanese BL/danmei consumers spend across at least four channels with direct evidence: (1) paid web-serial chapters (POPO's chapter-priced sales system, see Section 3); (2) ebooks (Readmoo, Kobo Taiwan, Google Play Books, Pubu — see Section 2); (3) print/doujin — the CWT/FF doujin-fair economy (Section 1 above); (4) licensed print BL from houses like 朧月書版 and 平心出版 (Section 2). Sources as cited in each respective section.
- **[known]** Mainland Chinese danmei writers and readers access the Taiwan-hosted Haitang platform specifically because it is unavailable/restricted domestically, requiring VPN use — i.e., VPN use by mainland readers/writers to reach overseas danmei platforms is directly evidenced by the 2024-2025 arrest wave itself (writers and presumably readers needed circumvention tools to use Haitang). Source: [RFA, "Chinese police crackdown on writers of online erotic fiction," 2025-06-09](https://www.rfa.org/english/china/2025/06/09/china-fiction-arrests/); [ctexp.substack.com, "Writing Erotica Is Punished More Severely Than Rape"](https://ctexp.substack.com/p/writing-erotica-is-punished-more) (accessed 2026-09-27). Separately, China has been tightening VPN/circumvention infrastructure generally, per an April 2026 leaked-notice report describing a Shaanxi Telecom order to block outbound connections beyond mainland China including to HK/Macau/Taiwan. Source: [China Digital Times, "Documents Raise Fear of Further Crackdown on Great Firewall Circumvention Tools," 2026-04](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/2026/04/documents-raise-fear-of-further-crackdown-on-great-firewall-circumvention-tools/). This directly bears on market access: a US publisher's website/app would very likely be unreachable to most mainland readers without VPNs, and mainland VPN use is now criminally and infrastructurally riskier than before. Cheap test: none cheap — this is a legal/technical environment fact, not a testable hypothesis for a small publisher; the actionable takeaway is to not depend on mainland reader access as a revenue assumption (see Section 6).

## 2. Platforms and competitors

### Jinjiang Literature City (晋江文学城, JJWXC)
- **[known]** Jinjiang is a Beijing-headquartered web-fiction publisher founded/launched in the early 2000s (site sources say founded 2003), China's largest platform for women's web fiction and original danmei, historically reporting ~7 million users and 500,000+ titles (figure of unclear vintage). Source: [Wikipedia, "Jinjiang Literature City"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinjiang_Literature_City) (accessed 2026-09-27).
- **[known]** Jinjiang has a long censorship history: 2004/2010/2014 anti-porn campaigns closed sections; a 2011-era case saw a smaller BL-hosting site's founder (Wang Ming) imprisoned 18 months; in October 2018 author "Tianyi" was sentenced to 10 years for a self-published novel that crossed profit thresholds; in 2019 China's National Office Against Pornographic and Illegal Publications ordered several Jinjiang sections closed for "illegal"/"obscene" content; in 2021 Jinjiang banned external links to sexual content. Jinjiang rebranded its danmei channel to "chun'ai" (纯爱, "pure love") and imposed intimacy limits stricter than official mandates (kissing/hand-holding only, nothing "below the neck"). Source: [Wikipedia, "Jinjiang Literature City"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinjiang_Literature_City); [Wikipedia, "Danmei"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danmei) (both accessed 2026-09-27).
- **[known]** Jinjiang remains Chinese-only, with no official English interface/app as of this research; some individual novels are officially licensed to English publishers (e.g., Seven Seas) on a title-by-title basis rather than via a JJWXC-run international storefront. JJWXC had announced/piloted an international (English + Traditional Chinese) site and a fan-translator contract program (initially restricted to translators in China/Taiwan/HK, paying for raw chapters, competing for a 30% cut of translated-chapter sales), which drew controversy over IP/translation-theft concerns. Source: [qqgk.tumblr.com, "jjwxc is getting an international site"](https://qqgk.tumblr.com/post/674015025551360000/jjwxc-is-getting-an-international-site-what-does); [kktranslates.home.blog, 2022-01-27](https://kktranslates.home.blog/2022/01/27/update-re-jjwxc/); [TeaNovel blog, "How to Read JJWXC in English"](https://read.teanovel.com/blog/jjwxc-english-translation) (all accessed 2026-09-27). **[needs research]**: current (2026) status of any JJWXC-run international licensing arm was not confirmed beyond these 2022-era reports. Cheap test: check jjwxc.net/jjwxcinternational or similar subdomains directly, and check if Seven Seas/Rosmei public statements name JJWXC as a direct licensing counterparty versus licensing from individual authors.

### Changpei Literature (长佩文学 / 长佩阅读)
- **[known]** Changpei is a major women's-fiction-focused Chinese platform (claims 3M+ users on its reading-app listing) covering "Pure Love" and danmei content across genres including e-sports, rebirth (重生), and sci-fi; it is generally cited as a top-tier alternative to Jinjiang, historically positioned as offering more creative freedom before moving toward self-censorship, and users note that on Changpei "you only have to pay for half or less of the VIP chapters" versus other platforms. Sources: [mwm.ai app listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/chang-pei-yue-du/1372514608); [SassyStrawberry Translates, "How to buy from Chinese webnovel sites"](https://sassystrawberry.com/how-to-buy-from-chinese-webnovel-sites/); [Wikipedia, "Danmei"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danmei) (all accessed 2026-09-27). **[needs research]**: no independent, non-user-review source for Changpei's total registered-author count, revenue, or ownership structure was found. Cheap test: create a free reader account and a trial author account on Changpei to directly observe its current fee schedule and content rules — the platform is open to sign-up without needing a business relationship.

### Haitang (海棠书屋/海棠文化/海棠线上) and the 2024–2025 arrests
- **[known]** Haitang is a Taiwan-hosted, Simplified-Chinese, adult-content (18+) web-fiction platform founded 2015, positioned specifically as an offshore venue for content (explicit danmei) that mainland platforms won't carry, requiring VPN access from the mainland. Source: [RFA, 2025-06-09](https://www.rfa.org/english/china/2025/06/09/china-fiction-arrests/); [ctexp.substack.com](https://ctexp.substack.com/p/writing-erotica-is-punished-more) (both accessed 2026-09-27).
- **[known]** Beginning around June 20–21, 2024, Anhui provincial police (Jixi County) arrested 50+ mainland writers (predominantly women in their 20s) for publishing on Haitang, on suspicion of "producing and distributing obscene materials for profit" (Article 363/367 of China's Criminal Law). A first named case: author "Yunjian," arrested night of 2024-06-21 for the novel "Application for Divorce" (离婚申请), sentenced to 4 years 6 months (one source: 4.5 years) despite ~1,850,000 RMB in reported earnings and paying a fine reported around 4 million RMB. A second wave in early-to-mid 2025 (centered on Lanzhou, Gansu) reportedly swept in an estimated 200–300 more writers, per one source, with sentences up to 10+ years possible where a work reached 250,000+ views under a "particularly serious" threshold. Author "Ciqiu"/"Cijiu": 5.5 years. Author "Yixie": 1 year 5 months + 2 years probation (proceeds under 250,000 RMB). Two Haitang payment intermediaries (named Wang Moumou and Wen Mou) were separately arrested for "assisting cybercrime." Sources (numbers vary slightly between accounts — treat the exact total count as a range, not a single verified figure): [RFA, 2025-06-09](https://www.rfa.org/english/china/2025/06/09/china-fiction-arrests/); [China Digital Times, 2025-06](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/2025/06/profit-seeking-police-crack-down-on-danmei-erotic-fiction-writers/); [Washington Blade, 2025-07-08](https://www.washingtonblade.com/2025/07/08/female-writers-arrested-in-chinese-crackdown-on-gay-erotic-fiction/); [Global Voices, 2025-06-25](https://globalvoices.org/2025/06/25/hundreds-of-women-writers-arrested-as-china-extends-crackdown-on-boys-love-fantasies/); [ctexp.substack.com](https://ctexp.substack.com/p/writing-erotica-is-punished-more); [Fanlore, "Arrest of Haitang Literature City writers in China"](https://fanlore.org/wiki/Arrest_of_Haitang_Literature_City_writers_in_China) (source listed but returned 403 on direct fetch — cited via search-index summary only, treat corroborating detail from it as **[needs research: re-verify by another access method]**). Provinces named across sources: Anhui, Gansu, Chongqing, Zhejiang, Fujian, Yunnan, Sichuan, Jiangsu. Cheap test: none — this is a legal-risk fact about a jurisdiction the business plan explicitly intends to operate outside of; the actionable read is in Section 6.
- **[known]** Legal financial thresholds cited (from 2004 Supreme People's Court/Procuratorate guidance referenced in coverage): "serious" offense = 200+ copies, 10,000+ clicks, or 10,000+ RMB profit; "particularly serious" = five times those thresholds; fines run 1–5x "illegal proceeds" (example given: an author who earned 130,000 RMB faced a fine floor of 260,000 RMB plus legal costs). Source: [ctexp.substack.com](https://ctexp.substack.com/p/writing-erotica-is-punished-more) (accessed 2026-09-27), which itself cites named Chinese legal scholars (Luo Xiang, Lao Dongyan) questioning whether 2004-era thresholds still make sense at 2024-era scale/earnings.
- **[known]** A "writing erotica is punished more severely than rape" framing circulated in Chinese public discourse in reaction to these cases, referencing the 2018 Tianyi case (10 years 6 months) as the anchor comparison. Source: [ctexp.substack.com](https://ctexp.substack.com/p/writing-erotica-is-punished-more).
- **[known]** Reader/fan solidarity response included crowdfunding for arrested authors' legal fees and fines via Weibo, with amounts as small as several thousand RMB reported per individual donor. Source: same ctexp.substack.com article.

### Taiwan platforms
- **POPO (POPO原創) [known]**: founded December 2009, described as Taiwan's largest open web-fiction creation/reading platform with 1M+ registered members; hosts a dedicated Po18 (18+/erotic) sub-service and BL/GL categories; author revenue split is 70/30 in the author's favor (a "三七分帳" — author 70%, platform 30%), with a minimum cash-out threshold of 5,100 PO-coins (NT$51) plus a 5,000-PO-coin cash-out fee, and authors set per-chapter free/paid status and pricing themselves. Sources: [popo.tw/about](https://www.popo.tw/about); [eventpage.popo.tw/whatispopo](https://eventpage.popo.tw/whatispopo/); [popo.tw/rule/popocoin_usage](https://www.popo.tw/rule/popocoin_usage) and related rule pages (accessed 2026-09-27, via search aggregation of these official POPO rule pages).

- **KadoKado (角角者) [known]**: launched by Kadokawa Taiwan Corporation (Oct. 26 of its launch year), a Traditional-Chinese web-fiction serialization platform offering Romance, BL, GL, Fantasy, Xianxia, Wuxia, Crime, Suspense, Horror, Thriller, Supernatural genres; hosts both Kadokawa-licensed Japanese light novels (via a "Kado+" subscription) and original Taiwanese-author work under direct contract. Source: [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kadokawa.kadokado&hl=en_US); [Animehunch, "Kadokawa Taiwan Launches Traditional Chinese Novel Platform KadoKado"](https://animehunch.com/kadokawa-taiwan-launches-traditional-chinese-novel-platform-kadokado/) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[needs research]**: KadoKado's specific author revenue-share percentage was not found in this research (only POPO's 70/30 was confirmed). Cheap test: KadoKado's own author-terms page (if public) or a direct email to their author-relations contact would resolve this at zero cost.
- **Mirror Fiction (鏡文學) [known]**: a Taiwan IP-incubation platform (novels, scripts, comics) aiming at full-rights development into film/TV/games/comics; has backed films selected as Taiwan's Oscar submissions (A Sun, The Falls) and the horror hit The Rope Curse 3; in November 2025 launched production company Mirror Entertainment with a US$10M co-investment from Taiwan's TAICCA (Taiwan Creative Content Agency) to develop ten film/TV projects over five years. Source: [Deadline, "TAICCA & Mirror Fiction Invest $10M In Taiwan Production Outfit Mirror Entertainment," 2025-11](https://deadline.com/2025/11/mirror-entertainment-mirror-fiction-taicca-1236610570/); [mirrorfiction.com](https://www.mirrorfiction.com/) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[needs research]**: whether Mirror Fiction specifically hosts/develops danmei/BL IP (versus general fiction) was not confirmed — no danmei title was found named in its catalog in this pass. Cheap test: browse mirrorfiction.com's genre filters directly for a BL/耽美 category.
- **Readmoo, Kobo Taiwan, Google Play Books, Pubu [known]**: Readmoo is described as Taiwan's largest Traditional-Chinese EPUB ebook retailer; Kobo entered Taiwan in 2016; Pubu, Kobo, and Google Play Books are named as the few Taiwan platforms offering downloadable ebook files (vs. app-locked reading); ebooks were reported at under 0.1% of Taiwan's total book market in one analysis (a low base suggesting large headroom but also a very print-dominant culture). Sources: [Taiwan Panorama, "Digital Reading Trailblazer Readmoo"](https://www.taiwan-panorama.com/en/Articles/Details?Guid=5136c59e-650a-4b7e-89e8-343742735712&CatId=9&postname=Digital+Reading+Trailblazer-Readmoo); [Business Insider Taiwan on Pubu](https://www.businessinsider.tw/article/2804); [vocus.cc comparative review of 13 Taiwan ebook platforms](https://vocus.cc/article/67a64aecfd897800010c6835) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[needs research]**: exact market-share percentages between these platforms, and each one's specific BL/danmei category size, were not found. Cheap test: list a title on Readmoo, Pubu, and Google Play Books simultaneously and compare per-platform unit sales — direct, low-cost, and gives a real answer for this specific business rather than a market-wide estimate.

### Taiwan BL print publishers and licensing of mainland danmei

Source: search aggregation of [mybook.taiwanmobile.com publisher page](https://mybook.taiwanmobile.com/search/publisher/%E6%9C%A7%E6%9C%88%E6%9B%B8%E7%89%88) and [Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/hazymoon.tw/) (accessed 2026-09-27).
- **[known]** 平心出版 (Pingxin Publishing) is described as a Taiwan BL-publishing specialist that began with Taiwanese authors and expanded into representing/licensing mainland Chinese works — named authors/titles include 墨香銅臭 (Mo Xiang Tong Xiu) and 夜画帐 — and has further expanded into licensing Korean, Japanese, Thai, British, and American BL/queer fiction. Source: search aggregation citing [500times.udn.com, 譚光磊 profile piece](https://500times.udn.com/wtimes/story/120841/7758011) (accessed 2026-09-27, snippet-level only — full article not directly read; treat exact wording as **[needs research: re-verify by direct read]**).

## 3. Author economics

### Jinjiang (JJWXC)
- **[known]** VIP-chapter pricing: readers pay roughly 3 fen (0.03 RMB) per 1,000 characters; the platform/author split is commonly cited as 60/40 (author 60%) for VIP chapters, giving the author ~1.8 fen per 1,000 characters, though one source describes an older/alternate 50/50 split yielding 1.5 fen to the author, and notes the split can also vary by reading channel (PC vs. mobile). Sources: aggregated from [zhidao.baidu.com Q&A threads](https://zhidao.baidu.com/question/1644072154039941380.html), [zhidao.baidu.com](https://zhidao.baidu.com/question/646609529007642325.html), and [zhihu.com/question/6620686990](https://www.zhihu.com/question/6620686990) (accessed 2026-09-27). Treat the exact percentage as approximate/contested between sources rather than a single official published rate.
- **[known]** Contract ("签约") terms: signing gives Jinjiang exclusive online-distribution agency rights (代理权, not full copyright — copyright stays with the author) for a minimum 5-year term, auto-renewing if not cancelled; signed authors are required to produce at least one work of 200,000+ characters per year (a serial exceeding 1 year and 400,000 characters can count toward two years' quota). Source: aggregated from [zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/1112914001](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/1112914001) and [zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/75130986](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/75130986) (accessed 2026-09-27).

Sources: same zhihu aggregation as above (accessed 2026-09-27). **Caveat**: these percentages come from author-community Q&A/blog aggregation, not an official published rate card — treat as **[needs research: confirm against JJWXC's actual current contract]**.
- **[known]** Earnings distribution is extremely polarized. Across Jinjiang's broader author pool (and citing a 2018-era in-depth report covering ~7.7 million web-fiction authors across Alibaba-linked platforms broadly, not Jinjiang alone): only ~1,000 authors nationally earn over 100,000 RMB/year from web fiction; Jinjiang itself had 46,639 contracted authors (vintage of figure unclear); one profiled author wrote 350,000 characters over a year and earned only 3,000 RMB total; another wrote 500,000 characters across three novels and earned under 1,000 RMB, yet still ranked in the top 40% of Jinjiang's contracted-author pool. ## 6. Jurisdiction and risk

### Mainland China: obscenity law and 2024–2025 enforcement
- **[known]** Article 363 of China's Criminal Law criminalizes producing, reproducing, publishing, selling, or disseminating obscene materials for profit; Article 367 is the definitional article for what counts as "obscene" ("lewd... material that specifically depicts sexual conduct or openly promotes explicit content"); Article 365 (obscene performances) separately provides for 3-10 years for serious cases. Sentencing bands cited in Haitang-case coverage: minor (under 3 years, sometimes with probation), serious (3-10 years), especially serious (10 years to life), keyed to 2004 SPC/SPP monetary/click thresholds (Section 2). Sources: search aggregation of [English translation of the Criminal Law](https://english.court.gov.cn/2015-12/01/c_761557_32.htm); [ChinaLawTranslate.com, "Reply of the SPC and SPP on Issues of Conviction and Sentencing... Obscene Electronic Information for Profit"](https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/en/reply-of-the-spc-and-spp-on-issues-of-conviction-and-sentencing-for-the-use-of-cloud-storage-in-the-production-copying-selling-and-distribution-of-obscene-electronic-information-for-profit/) (accessed 2026-09-27); Haitang-case reporting as cited fully in Section 2.
- **[known]** This law is actively, currently enforced against danmei authors specifically and disproportionately (BL/homoerotic content is targeted independent of, and arguably more harshly than, equivalent heterosexual content, per the framing in multiple sources), evidenced by the 2024-2025 Haitang arrest wave (Section 2) — this is the single most important, best-evidenced risk fact in this whole dossier.
- **[known]** Direct risk to any author who writes for an overseas (e.g., US-based) platform while resident in mainland China: payment trails (the two arrested Haitang "payment agents" show that even facilitating cross-border payment for this content is separately criminally chargeable — "assisting cybercrime"); platform data exposure (readers/authors needed VPNs to reach Haitang, meaning any of their mainland ISP-level traffic logs could evidence the connection); pseudonym exposure (author identity was evidently discoverable by mainland police despite pseudonymous publishing — real-name registration requirements for Chinese payment apps, ISPs, and increasingly for platform accounts make full anonymity very difficult to sustain over years of income receipt). Source: as cited in Section 2 (RFA, ctexp.substack.com, Global Voices). **[inferred]**: a US danmei publisher paying a mainland-resident author directly (via international wire, PayPal, crypto, or similar) plausibly recreates the same payment-trail exposure risk that the arrested Haitang payment agents were charged over, since Chinese authorities appear willing to charge intermediaries, not just authors. **[needs research]**: no source confirms whether payment specifically from a US company (versus a Taiwan-hosted platform like Haitang) changes the mainland legal exposure calculus — this would need a PRC-qualified lawyer's opinion, not open-source research. Cheap test: none available cheaply; this is the highest-stakes unresolved question in the whole dossier and should be treated as a hard "consult PRC counsel before soliciting/paying any mainland-resident author" gate, not something to resolve via web research.

### Payment processors and app-store content rules
- **[known]** Stripe's Prohibited and Restricted Businesses list explicitly prohibits "pornography and other mature audience content (including literature...)" and adult services generally, though it separately notes an exception: individual content creators on an approved Stripe Connect platform, or those distributing directly from their own website, "do not require preapproval," while still being subject to the same prohibited-content rules and to Stripe's monitoring. Source: [Stripe, "Prohibited and Restricted Businesses"](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[inferred]**: danmei content that is romantic/erotic but not sexually explicit ("fade to black," or explicit-content gated/removed) is more likely to be payable-via-Stripe than fully explicit content; a house publishing explicit ("肉"/smut-tagged) content directly would likely need a specialized adult-content payment processor instead of Stripe. **[needs research]**: no source directly addressed how Stripe treats a text-fiction publisher (as opposed to image/video adult content) — this is a real ambiguity a publisher should resolve directly with Stripe's own merchant underwriting team before launch. Cheap test: apply for a Stripe account under the actual planned business description and see whether it is approved, restricted, or flagged — this is free and fast (days, not weeks) and is the single most decision-relevant "cheap test" in this whole dossier for de-risking the payments stack.
- **[known]** Google Play's developer policy prohibits apps containing/promoting "sexual content or profanity, including pornography," sexual nudity, sexually suggestive depictions, and content depicting/functioning as sexual aids; region-specific availability is possible in some cases (content permitted in one region can remain blocked in others). Source: [Google Play, "Inappropriate Content" developer policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9878810?hl=en) (accessed 2026-09-27).
- **[known]** Apple's App Store guidelines reject apps containing "pornographic material," defined by reference to a dictionary definition of explicit sexual content intended to stimulate erotic response. Source: search aggregation citing Apple Developer Forums discussion (accessed 2026-09-27) — **[needs research: cite Apple's own current App Store Review Guidelines document directly](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)** rather than a forum thread, since the forum thread is not an authoritative primary source. **[inferred]**: an app-based product would need to either avoid explicit content entirely or operate as a web-based (non-app-store) reading platform, given both major app stores' consistent hard line against pornography — a text-only, romance-not-explicit product is far more app-store-viable than one hosting explicit ("R-18"/肉") content. Cheap test: submit a minimal test app with romance-but-not-explicit content to both stores' review process to see where the actual line falls in practice (app review outcomes are known to be inconsistently applied and are worth testing directly rather than assuming from the written policy alone).

### Taiwan's legal environment
- **[known]** Taiwan rates publications/video content, with a "限制級" (Restricted) tier for content with explicit sexual depiction, extensive display of sexual organs, or content otherwise harmful to minors; restricted-grade print material must be labeled and sold via age-gated retail placement; distributing/displaying material that exceeds even the restricted-grade standard (i.e., that crosses into obscenity) risks prosecution under Article 235 of Taiwan's Criminal Code (offenses against public morals/decency). Sources: [Taiwan Ministry of Culture regulation notice](https://www.moc.gov.tw/News_Content.aspx?n=180&s=3946); [Taiwan Laws & Regulations Database, "出版品及錄影節目帶分級管理辦法"](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?PCode=P0030009) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[inferred]**: Taiwan is materially more permissive than mainland China for BL/erotic content — it has an entire commercial ecosystem of legal 18+ BL platforms and print imprints (Po18, Haitang's own hosting, 朧月書版, 平心出版) that simply could not legally operate inside mainland China at their current explicitness level — but Taiwan is not unregulated; age-gating and an obscenity ceiling (Article 235) both still apply, and a US publisher selling into Taiwan should still apply age-verification/labeling norms rather than assume total legal freedom.

### US copyright, work-for-hire, and tax withholding for foreign authors
- **[known]** Under US copyright law, a commissioned work can be treated as "work made for hire" (with the commissioning company as legal author/rights-holder) only if the contract explicitly states this (or assigns copyright) and is signed before work begins; this applies to foreign freelance contractors (e.g., a US publisher commissioning a translation from a non-US translator) the same as domestic ones. Sources: [ScribeCount, "Work-for-Hire Agreements Explained"](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/protecting-books/work-for-hire-agreements); [National Writers Union, "Guide to Work for Hire Contracts" PDF](https://nwu.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/Work-for-Hire-Guide.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[inferred]**: given the founder-directive context of pseudonymity and the desire to protect mainland-resident authors' identities, a house should default to a licensing/royalty model (author retains copyright, grants exclusive publishing rights) rather than work-for-hire, since work-for-hire requires naming/identifying the legal author on a binding contract in a way that may itself create the exact identity-exposure risk flagged above — this is a design implication, not a separately sourced fact.
- **[known]** A foreign individual receiving US-sourced royalty/compensation income must file Form W-8BEN (or W-8BEN-E for a foreign entity) with the US payer; without it, the payer must withhold 30% of gross payments by default; a tax treaty between the author's country and the US can reduce this (examples cited: UK 0%, Canada often 0%, Australia typically 5%). Source: [Stripe, "W-8 BEN Tax Form"](https://stripe.com/resources/more/w-8-ben-tax-form); [Trolley, "What is Form W-8 BEN?"](https://trolley.com/learning-center/w-8-ben-foreign-status-us-tax-reporting-individuals/) (accessed 2026-09-27). **[needs research]**: whether/what US-China tax treaty terms apply specifically to royalty income for a mainland Chinese resident (the US-China tax treaty is real but its royalty article terms were not looked up in this research pass) — this matters directly since mainland-resident authors are a named potential author pool. Cheap test: read the US-China income tax treaty's royalty article directly (freely available on irs.gov) before finalizing any contractor-payment template.

## Gaps a U.S. Mandarin publisher could credibly fill

1. **A legal, non-mainland-dependent home for explicit/adult Mandarin danmei, protecting author identity.** The single strongest, best-evidenced fact in this dossier is that mainland China is actively, currently criminally prosecuting danmei authors at scale (50+ arrested from June 2024, an estimated 200-300 more by mid-2025, sentences up to 5.5 years, fines in the millions of RMB — Section 2/6) specifically for writing and publishing the genre this business intends to commission. A US house, structured from day one around royalty/licensing (not work-for-hire, to avoid mandatory legal-identity disclosure) contracts, pseudonym-preserving payment rails, and hosting entirely outside mainland jurisdiction, directly answers a risk that Haitang's Taiwan-hosting model did not fully solve (Haitang was Taiwan-hosted and its mainland-resident writers were still arrested via payment-trail and platform-data exposure — Section 6). This is evidence-backed, not merely plausible.

2. **A revenue ceiling problem for danmei authors that a Western/global audience can raise.** Section 3/7 shows a ~100x earnings gap between danmei's best-paid authors (~RMB 1M/year) and mainstream Chinese web fiction's best-paid authors (~RMB 110-122M/year), plausibly driven by the live-action adaptation ban (Section 4/6) closing off danmei's highest-value monetization channel (screen adaptation) relative to unrestricted genres. A US house selling directly to an English/global Sinophone audience (where screen adaptation restrictions are a mainland-specific, not global, constraint — see the NYT-bestseller and audio-drama revenue evidence in Sections 1/4/7 showing real Western willingness to pay) can offer authors a monetization path the mainland market structurally cannot.

3. **Formats mainland platforms increasingly cannot carry, that Taiwan/global audiences already pay for.** Sections 2 and 4 show concrete, non-speculative demand for danmei audio drama (19 of MissEvan's top 20 shows are danmei; one hit title grossed $2M from just 53,000 paid listeners) and for premium print (Seven Seas' 500,000-copy run, three-way NYT bestseller debut). A house that commissions audio-drama and print-first (rather than treating them as afterthought adaptations of a web-serial) could capture format-specific spend that currently flows to unofficial/fan channels for anything not officially licensed.

4. **A Traditional-Chinese, Taiwan/HK/diaspora-first original-danmei imprint, distinct from "license mainland hits after the fact."** Section 2 shows Taiwan already has a functioning legal BL publishing ecosystem (POPO, Po18, KadoKado, 朧月書版, 平心出版) but that Taiwan houses currently function mainly as *licensors of mainland-originated IP* (e.g., 平心出版 licensing Mo Xiang Tong Xiu) rather than as commissioners of original Traditional-Chinese danmei aimed at a global audience from inception. A US house could commission original work in Traditional Chinese from the start, simultaneously serving Taiwan/HK/diaspora readers and sidestepping the mainland-authorship risk in #1 (though note: many of the best-known authors and much reader demand is presently mainland-originated, so this gap is partial, not a full substitute for #1).

5. **A single legitimate payment/app-store path for danmei specifically.** Section 6 shows Stripe, Apple, and Google Play all have blanket prohibitions on "pornographic"/explicit content but with real ambiguity about where romantic-but-not-explicit danmei falls, and an explicit carve-out in Stripe's policy for individual creators distributing from their own site. No existing English danmei licensor (Seven Seas, Rosmei, Peach Flower House — Section 2) appears to have solved a full-explicit-content-plus-mainstream-payment-rail problem; most published English danmei is fade-to-black or print-only specifically to stay app-store/processor-compliant. A house that either (a) commits to non-explicit content to stay fully compliant, or (b) does the underwriting legwork to get an explicit-content-compliant payment stack approved (the cheap test in Section 6: apply to Stripe directly and see what happens), fills a gap the current field has mostly avoided rather than solved.

6. **Underserved subgenres relative to what is currently licensed in English.** Section 5 shows the currently-licensed English danmei canon is dominated by 仙侠/古耽 (xianxia/historical) via MXTX and Priest, while industry commentary (Section 4/5) flags 无限流, 末世, and ABO as currently "hot" or at least distinctly tagged/organized categories with no dominant English-licensed equivalent identified in this research. This is the weakest-evidenced gap in this dossier (tagged **[inferred]**, not [known]) and should be tested cheaply (Section 5's suggested cross-tabulation of licenseddanmei.carrd.co against NovelUpdates tags) before being treated as a real strategic bet.
