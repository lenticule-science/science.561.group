A scale degree is a pitch's position within a scale — its index from the [tonic](./fundamental.md) — and is the unit in which melodic motion within a scale is described. The scale's tonic is scale-degree 1; the next-higher pitch in the scale is scale-degree 2; and so on up to the octave, which is variously labeled 8 (in pentatonic notation, where the previous scale-degree is 5) or 1' (treating the octave as the recurrence of the tonic at the higher register) depending on convention. Scale-degrees may be modified by accidentals when a scale departs from a diatonic norm — the [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md)'s third degree, three semitones above the tonic, is labelled b3 ("flat 3") to distinguish it from the major third (4 semitones) that would be labelled simply 3.

The [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md) has five distinct scale-degrees within the octave — 1, b3, 4, 5, b7 — with the octave 8 as the recurrence of the tonic at the upper boundary. On the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md), these five degrees map directly onto the six [finger-hole](./finger-hole.md) states: all holes covered → scale-degree 1 ([fundamental](./fundamental.md)); hole 1 (foot-most) uncovered → scale-degree b3; holes 1 and 2 uncovered → scale-degree 4; holes 1, 2, 3 uncovered → scale-degree 5; holes 1, 2, 3, 4 uncovered → scale-degree b7; holes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 uncovered → scale-degree 8 (octave). Hole 6 (closest to the head) and the second-octave [overblowing](./overblowing.md) register extend the range; see [cedar-flute-fingering-chart](../../code/structures/cedar-flute-fingering-chart.md).

Scale-degrees carry *function* in a music-theoretic sense, not just position. The tonic (scale-degree 1) is the *stable* pitch — the pitch to which phrases resolve, the pitch that bears extended sustained tones, the pitch that defines the scale's identity. The dominant (scale-degree 5) is the *tension* pitch — the pitch most strongly oriented toward eventual resolution back to the tonic, the strongest stable point of melodic ascent, often the high point of a phrase before its descent toward the [cadence](./cadence.md). The sub-mediant (scale-degree b3 in the minor pentatonic) is the *characteristic* pitch — the pitch whose identity gives the scale its minor character (as opposed to the major-third scale-degree that would give a major character), and the principal cadential approach-tone to the tonic. The sub-tonic (scale-degree b7) is the *peak* pitch — the highest scale-degree below the octave, frequently the starting point of descending phrases, often the upper boundary of a [phrase](./phrase.md)'s melodic range.

The other two scale-degrees of the pentatonic minor — 4 (the subdominant) and 5 (the dominant) — provide the structural skeleton of melodic motion: phrases ascend by stepwise motion through 4 and 5 to higher points, descend back through 5 and 4 to b3 and 1. The dominant 5's relationship to the tonic 1 is a *perfect fifth* — the most acoustically stable non-octave interval — and it provides the strongest possible cadential approach when used as a descending-fifth motion. The subdominant 4's relationship to the tonic is a *perfect fourth* — likewise stable, and producing a slightly less strong but still definite cadential approach when used as a descending-fourth.

The functional identity of a scale-degree depends on the [mode](./mode.md) — that is, on which pitch is treated as the tonal center. The same pitch class can function differently depending on the modal context. The Lakotaean cedar-flute repertoire's use of Mode I means that the functional identities given above (tonic at 1, dominant at 5, etc.) hold for the cedar-flute scale-degrees; in other modes of the same pitch collection, the functional identities would shift.
