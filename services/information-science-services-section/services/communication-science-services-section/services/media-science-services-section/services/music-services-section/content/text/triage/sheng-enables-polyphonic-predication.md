# Sheng Enables Polyphonic Predication

The sheng's multiple pipes with independent finger holes enable true polyphonic predication capabilities absent in most other Chinese aerophones, allowing for complex chordal structures and harmonic predication patterns.

## Formal Statement

Let S be the predication system of a sheng with n pipes, and M be the predication system of a monophonic aerophone (e.g., dizi, xiao). The sheng's predication system S can produce simultaneous independent predicates (chords) that M cannot, making S strictly more expressive in terms of predication complexity.

## Supporting Arguments

1. **Independent Pipe Control**: Each sheng pipe has its own finger hole, allowing independent control of each pipe's pitch state.

2. **Simultaneous Activation**: Multiple pipes can be activated simultaneously through normal blowing, producing true concurrent pitches.

3. **Chordal Capabilities**: The sheng can produce dyads, triads, and tetrachords depending on the number of pipes and finger coordination.

4. **Harmonic Predication**: Enables predication patterns where the predicate is not just a single tone but a harmonic relationship between multiple tones.

5. **Traditional Usage**: Traditional sheng playing frequently employs chordal accompaniment patterns, demonstrating cultural recognition of this capability.

## Implications

- The sheng represents a aerophone predication system with higher expressive capacity for harmonic relationships
- This explains the sheng's historical role as a harmony instrument in Chinese ensembles
- The predication fiber structure of sheng is inherently multidimensional due to pipe interactions
