# Lakotaean Aerophones Collection

**This is an index, not a copy.** The corpus lives at
`philosophy-services-section/services/lakota-services-section/.../lakotaean-aerophones`
(138 files as of 2026-08-21) and stays there. Two reasons, both binding:
it is live, actively-organized content maintained elsewhere in this tree
and copying it here would fork it out from under its own maintenance; and
Lakota-specific material is engaged by cross-link only per house protocol
-- the owner and the Lakota community hold authority over that content,
not this section. Follow the [source link](../../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/lakota-services-section/content/text/glossary/lakota/discharging/library/predicating/library/positing/library/settling/library/hacking/lakotaean-aerophones)
to read it in place.

## What's there

A formal music theory of four traditional Lakota wind instruments -- the
cedar courting flute, the eagle wing-bone whistle, the elk/crane whistle,
and the grass-dance whistle -- organized as a theory corpus:

- `philB/prose/learning/surveys/` -- research surveys
- `philB/prose/learning/claims/` -- formal music-theoretic claims (e.g. a
  single-tone aerophone's content lives in breath-pulse sequence and
  timbral envelope, not pitch, since the pitch coordinate is degenerate)
- `philB/prose/teaching/explanations/` -- a glossary of terms (bore,
  embouchure, fundamental, harmonic, overblowing, warble, heterophony,
  rubato, duet, voice, and the named cadences)
- `philB/code/structures/` -- the mathematical structures the claims prove
  (single-tone-object, breath-pulse-sequence, timbral-envelope,
  whistle-drum-heterophony, cadence-set, ...)
- `philB/theories/lakotaean-aerophonic-culture/` -- ethnographic,
  ceremonial, legal, and social context, kept as a **separate sub-theory**
  by explicit design: the corpus's own `_index.md` states that analyzing
  the music only through its ceremonial frame is a category error, and
  that the music has its own theoretical content derivable on musical
  terms, distinct from the culture's own content derivable on cultural
  terms
- `b4/<instrument>/` -- a second, per-instrument organization of the same
  material (cedar-flute, warble, drum, bore, cents, cut, tap, finger-hole,
  finger-pop, finger-slide, pentatonic-minor-scale, instrumentalization,
  breath-pulse, and each named song/whistle)

The corpus's own path carries its home taxonomy in the open: it sits under
`lakota/discharging/library/predicating/library/positing/library/settling/library/hacking/`
-- the Lakota glossary's own staged vocabulary of discharging, predicating,
positing, and settling. That this independently-arrived-at taxonomy already
uses "settling" as a structural stage is a real point of resonance with the
charge-dynamics note's own settling vocabulary (cadence as FCA closure,
resolution as a charge configuration reaching its fixed point) -- worth
reading as convergence from a different tradition's own terms, not as this
section's vocabulary having been imported into that corpus. Nothing in the
corpus itself is framed as charge dynamics; that reading is this section's,
applied from outside, and should stay legibly outside.

## Direct relevance to the charge-dynamics claim

The founding note's dictionary maps directly onto material already worked
out here, independently:

- **Cadence.** The corpus's own `cadence.md` documents a small, named
  cadential vocabulary for the cedar-flute idiom (descending-minor-third,
  descending-perfect-fourth, descending-perfect-fifth, held-tonic-with-warble)
  used to mark differing levels of phrase- and strophe-closure -- a
  concrete instance of "cadence as audible settling" with graded settling
  strength, worked out with instrument-specific acoustic detail (the
  warble's jet-instability mechanism) the charge-dynamics note does not
  itself supply.
- **Rhythm without pitch.** The single-tone-aerophone claim (content lives
  in breath-pulse sequence and timbral envelope when the pitch coordinate
  is degenerate) is a rigorous, pre-existing case for "rhythm is causal-set
  structure made audible" that does not depend on tonal material at all --
  arguably a cleaner test case than pitched material for the causal-set
  reading, since there is no gauge (tonal) layer to confound it.
- **The wind lineage.** The founding note names the cedar courting flute
  by name as a founding witness of "feelings played through settled
  structure." This corpus is where that instrument's structure is actually
  worked out.

## What this collection does not do

It does not restate, summarize-into-claims, or reframe any of the corpus's
content as charge dynamics. It points. Any future work that wants to
*apply* the charge-dynamics reading to specific claims here (e.g. formally
mapping the four named cadences onto degrees of settling) belongs in this
section, as new content that cites the corpus, not as an edit to the
corpus itself.
