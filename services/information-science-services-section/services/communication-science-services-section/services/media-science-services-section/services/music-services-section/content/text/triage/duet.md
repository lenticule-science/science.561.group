A duet is a two-voice musical texture — a performance involving exactly two simultaneous musical streams, with the streams sharing melodic material, phrasing, and overall musical character such that the two together constitute a coherent unit. The duet is not the same as [heterophony](./heterophony.md) (which can involve two or more voices and which centers on simultaneous statement of melodic variants) nor the same as a generic two-instrument ensemble (which may not share melodic material at all); the duet is specifically two voices acting on common melodic and phrasal content, in coordinated relation.

In the Lakotaean aerophone tradition, the principal duet relevant to the music theory is the *cedar-flute-and-voice duet* — a [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) player and a [voice](./voice.md) singer performing together, with the two streams operating in the same [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md) pitch space and the same [rubato](./rubato.md) [phrase](./phrase.md) organization. The duet may take several forms: *substitution* (the voice carries the melodic line for one phrase, the flute carries the next, alternating phrase-by-phrase or sub-phrase-by-sub-phrase); *antiphony* (the voice states a phrase, the flute responds with a related phrase, in question-and-answer style); or *overlap* (the voice and flute both sound, with the flute's line being either a unison or a related variant of the voice's line). The [flute-voice-duet](../../code/structures/flute-voice-duet.md) structure formalizes these configurations.

The flute-voice duet is musically interesting because it brings the two principal melodic streams of the Lakotaean tradition into direct relation. The [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) [instrumentalizes](./instrumentalization.md) [wiilowan](./wiilowan.md) vocal song in solo performance; in duet performance, the two are present simultaneously, and the [instrumentalization](./instrumentalization.md) operation becomes a relation between simultaneous voices rather than an asymmetric translation from source to derivative. The duet therefore provides a context in which the music-theoretic content of the [instrumentalization](./instrumentalization.md) — pitch quantization, syllable articulation, vowel-quality contrast — can be heard side-by-side with the vocal source, and the listener can compare the flute's rendering against the voice's original. This is rare in solo flute performance, where the vocal source is implicit but not present.

The duet's musical conventions in the Lakotaean tradition are less extensively documented in the music-theoretic literature than the solo cedar-flute conventions, in part because the duet has historically been a more intimate and less performed-publicly context than solo flute playing — a duet is more often a private musical exchange between two individuals than a public-ceremonial performance. The conventions that are documented include: shared pitch space (both voices operate in the same [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md) at compatible registers); shared phrase organization (both voices observe the same [rubato](./rubato.md) [phrase](./phrase.md)-bounded-by-single-breath structure); coordinated cadences (both voices arrive at the [cadence](./cadence.md) of a phrase together, even if they have followed separate sub-phrases within the phrase); and substitution rules (when voice and flute are alternating, the flute usually takes the more decorated or ornamented sub-phrases, and the voice takes the more text-bearing sub-phrases, since the voice can carry text and the flute cannot).

The duet is *not* a chordal-harmonic relation. The two voices do not stack into chords; they are heard either as alternations (substitution, antiphony) or as a quasi-unison (overlap). The Lakotaean tradition does not use chord-functional harmony in any of its idioms, and the duet is no exception.

A related but distinct two-voice configuration is the *whistle-drum pair* (a single whistle in ensemble with a single drum), which is a generalized [heterophony](./heterophony.md) rather than a duet in the cedar-flute-voice sense — the whistle does not carry melodic material, and the drum is not a melodic instrument. See [whistle-drum-heterophony](../../code/structures/whistle-drum-heterophony.md) for that case.
