The sheng is a traditional Chinese mouth-blown free reed instrument consisting of multiple vertical pipes. It is one of the oldest known musical instruments in the world and capable of producing chords.

## Physical Characteristics

- **Material**: Bamboo pipes (typically), wooden wind chamber, metal or bamboo reeds
- **Structure**: 17-36 pipes mounted in a wind chamber, each with a free reed and finger hole
- **Reeds**: Free brass or bronze reeds (similar to harmonica reeds)
- **Wind Chamber**: Traditionally made of gourd or wood, modern versions often metal
- **Pipes**: Varying lengths determine pitch; longer pipes = lower notes

## Musical Properties

- **Sound Production**: Free reed vibration (air flowing through reed causes it to vibrate)
- **Timbre**: Warm, rich, organ-like, capable of both single notes and chords
- **Range**: Typically 2-3 octaves depending on number of pipes
- **Techniques**: Single notes, chords (2-4 notes), overlapping tones, vibrato, glissando, flutter tonguing

## Musical Role

- **Ensemble**: Harmony and rhythm instrument in Chinese orchestra, often plays chordal accompaniment
- **Solo**: Increasing solo repertoire exploring polyphonic capabilities
- **Genres**: Classical, folk, opera, contemporary, world music fusion

## Predication Theory Perspective

From a predication theory viewpoint:
- **Subject**: The sheng instrument

- **Modulation**: Finger hole coverage on multiple pipes simultaneously creates multidimensional predication space
