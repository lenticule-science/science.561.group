The creator economy is the industry of platform-native content production and its supporting monetization infrastructure — advertising revenue share, brand sponsorships, platform creator funds, subscriptions/tips, and merchandise — built around [influencers](./influencer.md) and other creators on platforms such as YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok.
Stuart Cunningham & David Craig's academic term for this formation, "social media entertainment" (SME), in *Social Media Entertainment: The New Intersection of Hollywood and Silicon Valley* (NYU Press, 2019) ([NYU Press](https://nyupress.org/9781479846894/social-media-entertainment/)), frames it as a creative-industry formation structurally distinct from legacy Hollywood models, built on direct creator-audience interactivity as a feature of the content itself rather than an add-on.
This is the peer-reviewed-adjacent academic anchor for the term; the industry's own dollar-value sizing is a separate, trade-tier literature discussed below.

## Trade-tier sizing (unverified at primary-source level)

Creator-economy market-size figures circulate widely in industry and trade-analyst reporting but proved harder to pin to a stable primary source than the academic literature above.
A secondary trade aggregator ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-economy-stats/)) reports, citing Statista, a creator-economy market size of "$21 billion in 2023," up from "$16.4 billion in 2022," and separately reports a Goldman Sachs Research projection that the creator economy "can almost double in size from $250 billion to a potential $480 billion" by 2027.
**[UNVERIFIED]**: repeated attempts to load Goldman Sachs's own report page during this term's verification returned HTTP 403, so this figure is confirmed only at the secondary-aggregator level, not against Goldman Sachs's primary publication.
SignalFire's creator-economy market map, frequently cited alongside the Goldman Sachs figures in trade coverage, could not be located at a stable URL during verification (plausible URLs returned HTTP 404); any SignalFire-specific creator-count or market-size figures should be independently re-sourced before being cited from this term.

## Labour and platform-governance literature

The lived experience of working inside this economy is analyzed by [influencer](./influencer.md)'s labour literature — Duffy's aspirational labour, Baym's relational labor, Duffy & Meisner's algorithmic precarity, and Bishop's algorithmic gossip — rather than by the industry-sizing literature in this term, which is trade reporting on the economy's aggregate scale rather than scholarship on how the work inside it is actually organized and experienced.
