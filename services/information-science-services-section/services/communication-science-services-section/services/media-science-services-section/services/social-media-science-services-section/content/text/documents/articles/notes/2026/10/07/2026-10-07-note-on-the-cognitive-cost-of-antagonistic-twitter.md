The exposure of interest is a Twitter feed dominated by persistent antagonistic disputes, sometimes interrupted by cute cat videos. Following such a feed can require reconstructing arguments, interpreting motives, evaluating accusations, deciding whether to respond, and checking unresolved developments. The central working hypothesis is that repeated conflict combines cognitive demands with emotional involvement and opportunities to resume those demands throughout the day. Strong engagement can coexist with fatigue and reduced capacity for other activities.

This is a targeted synthesis. Twitter research establishes some emotional and behavioral effects; laboratory studies establish some effects of social threat, rumination, and switching between tasks. Applying those findings to the particular feed described above is a hypothesis. None of the sources below measures the complete sequence of antagonistic Twitter exposure, prefrontal glutamate change, and delayed ME/CFS symptom exacerbation.

The [companion on ME/CFS cognitive exertion and glutamate](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../natural-science-services-section/services/biological-science-services-section/services/medical-science-services-section/services/myalgic-encephalomyelitis-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/10/07/2026-10-07-note-on-mecfs-cognitive-exertion-and-glutamate-evidence.md) examines that clinical and biochemical connection. Neither momentary stimulation nor momentary comfort establishes recovery from exertion.

## The cognitive work of following conflict

The following decomposition describes plausible demands in this exposure. It is an interpretation of the activity, not a validated Twitter workload scale. Demands vary with participation, familiarity, personal stakes, and how thoroughly a person follows each dispute.

| Activity | Possible cognitive demand |
| --- | --- |
| Reconstructing a dispute | Holding claims, chronology, screenshots, corrections, and missing context in working memory. |
| Interpreting social intent | Distinguishing sincerity, irony, bait, hostility, and ambiguity; updating an interpretation as new replies appear. |
| Evaluating an accusation | Comparing evidence, noticing contradictions, assessing credibility, and deciding what remains uncertain. |
| Considering a response | Choosing words and timing, anticipating audiences and consequences, and suppressing or revising an initial reply. |
| Following several disputes | Repeatedly loading different people, histories, and stakes while earlier questions remain unresolved. |
| Checking for developments | Returning attention to unresolved interactions and deciding whether new information changes an earlier judgment. |

Reading without posting can involve any of these operations. “Passive” describes a platform action; it does not specify how much interpretation or emotional involvement occurs. Conversely, not every glance at hostile content requires intensive reasoning. The relevant exposure includes what a person does with the content, not only its presence or the number of minutes the app is open.

Third-party conflict, threats to one's community, and personally directed harassment also need to remain distinguishable. A laboratory finding about being judged cannot automatically be applied to watching strangers argue. Actual social stakes may include relationships, reputation, information access, work, or safety; identifying these stakes does not imply that the reaction is irrational or that disengagement has no cost.

## What Twitter research directly supports

**Emotional outcomes during ordinary use.** Oldemburgo de Mello, Cheung, and Inzlicht's 2024 *Communications Psychology* study surveyed 252 US Twitter users five times daily for seven days, yielding 6,218 observations. Use predicted lower well-being and greater outrage, polarization, and belonging over the next 30 minutes. Inferred information-seeking use was associated with outrage; social use with belonging; passive use with lower well-being. Most associations reflected changes within individuals. Data were collected in March–June 2021, before the platform became X. These are historical observational associations, not a randomized test of today's platform or a measure of cognitive exhaustion, PEM, or neurotransmission. [Study and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-024-00062-z)

**Reinforcement of outrage expression.** Brady and colleagues' 2021 *Science Advances* research combined two preregistered observational Twitter studies, covering 7,331 users and 12.7 million tweets, with two preregistered behavioral experiments involving 240 participants. Social feedback and visible network norms influenced subsequent outrage expression. This supports a mechanism for the persistence of conflict-oriented behavior. The outcome was expression, which need not equal felt anger; the study did not establish that current ranking systems, particular accounts, or individual users operate in the same way. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abe5641)

These findings allow belonging and antagonism to coexist. A community can remain meaningful while interactions within it are demanding. Whether reinforcement of expression also increases an individual's total exertion is a separate question.

## Social threat and executive performance

In van Ast and colleagues' *Cerebral Cortex* experiment, published online in 2014 and in the 2016 issue, 21 healthy adults were randomized to social evaluative threat or a control condition. Threat impaired N-back working-memory performance and altered activity and connectivity in frontoparietal regions, including lateral prefrontal cortex. Cortisol response did not mediate the impairment and was instead associated with protection. The small sample and laboratory exposure limit generalization. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhu206)

The relevant extrapolation is conditional: if Twitter conflict creates personally meaningful social threat, it may interfere with executive processes used to follow that conflict and perform other tasks. This does not require greater measured prefrontal activation. Difficulty, effort, regional BOLD activity, and successful performance are distinct quantities.

Nor does an effortful activity necessarily feel boring or difficult to initiate. Compelling social stakes can keep someone returning while interpretation, response selection, or subsequent work becomes harder. Whether this occurs through arousal, habit, ongoing obligations, or another mechanism needs to be established for the specific person and exposure.

## Continued attention between encounters

A possible source of additional demand is continued mental involvement: rehearsing a response, anticipating a reply, reconsidering an accusation, or monitoring an unresolved situation after leaving the feed. In research this overlaps with perseverative cognition, meaning recurrent mental representation of a stressor. Deliberate problem-solving and necessary monitoring should not automatically be classified as dysfunctional rumination.

Verkuil and colleagues' 2010 *International Journal of Psychophysiology* study, published online in 2009, tested 59 participants with an unsolvable synonym task and monitored cardiac recovery over six minutes. Trait worry and reported intrusive thoughts were associated with slower heart-rate recovery. Heart-rate-variability findings depended on brooding, and an automatic-vigilance result ran in an unexpected direction. This supports a qualified association between recurrent cognition and recovery, not a universal effect or proof of the Twitter mechanism. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2009.09.003)

An important counterfinding comes from Lievaart and colleagues' 2017 study, published online in 2016. Among 72 participants, instructed angry rumination increased anger relative to distraction but did not reduce performance on an affective Go/NoGo task. Increased anger therefore cannot be used as a substitute for demonstrated executive impairment. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbtep.2016.07.016)

For an all-day feed, the hypothesis concerns both repeated encounters and the interval between them. Reopening a dispute before disengagement or recovery may create a different exposure from one short encounter. These studies do not quantify the cost of that schedule.

## Cute videos and tonal switching

A pleasant interruption could briefly improve experience, shift attention away from conflict, or encourage continued viewing. Those possibilities can occur separately. A cat video could be welcome while the preceding dispute remains unresolved, or it could be a real break. Whether alternating content adds effort is an empirical question.

Eckart and colleagues' 2023 fMRI study of 57 participants found behavioral and neural costs when switching between judgments of facial emotion and gender. Its tasks required explicit changes of judgment rules. It supports demands associated with switching between affective and non-affective tasks, not a claim that every change from an angry tweet to a cute video produces the same cost. Changing content, changing emotional state, and changing task rules are different manipulations. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsac054)

Fredrickson and colleagues' 2000 experiment found that some amusing or comforting films accelerated cardiovascular recovery after induced anxiety compared with other films. [Original experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3128334/) However, Behnke and colleagues' 2023 meta-analysis of 16 studies, 1,220 participants, and 72 effect sizes found that positive emotion usually did not accelerate sympathetic recovery relative to neutral conditions; support appeared when composite cardiovascular measures were used. Positive feeling cannot therefore be presumed to cancel physiological effects of antagonism. [Meta-analysis and author repository](https://dare.uva.nl/id/d79f34cd-7c79-4e99-9d6c-0f18e269c72a)

Hong and colleagues' 2026 *NeuroImage* study measured resting dorsal anterior cingulate glutamate and brain activity during short-video viewing in 56 young adults. Preferred viewing involved deactivation of control regions and greater connectivity between them; higher resting glutamate was associated with less deactivation. It studied neither antagonistic exchanges nor recovery after conflict. Its findings concern immersive viewing and cannot establish that a mixed Twitter feed is cognitively restful. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.121722)

## The connection to ME/CFS

The clinical question is whether this pattern constitutes sustained cognitive and emotional exertion that contributes to symptom worsening in someone with ME/CFS. That question remains meaningful even if the person enjoys parts of the feed, feels temporarily energized, or performs well while engaged.

The companion distinguishes immediate fatigue, executive performance, physiological recovery, and delayed PEM. It also distinguishes regional tissue glutamate measured by spectroscopy from extracellular signaling and toxicity. A feed-related symptom association would not, by itself, identify glutamate, cortisol, inflammation, or vascular changes as the cause.

## Questions that would distinguish the mechanisms

These are research comparisons, not a recommendation to provoke symptoms.

| Proposed explanation | Discriminating comparison or observation |
| --- | --- |
| Reconstructing arguments creates much of the demand | Compare exposures with similar hostility but different requirements to track context, evidence, and multiple participants. |
| Personally relevant social threat contributes additional interference | Distinguish direct targeting, conflict affecting one's community, and remote disputes; measure performance as well as emotion. |
| Unresolved interactions extend the exposure | Examine continued checking, rehearsal, and anticipation after app use, alongside recovery and necessary social obligations. |
| Rapid changes in task or emotional context add cost | Compare mixed and grouped presentations while holding content and total duration constant. Pleasant-content effects need their own comparison. |
| Pleasant interruptions provide recovery | Measure subsequent recovery and function rather than treating a pleasant rating as sufficient evidence. |
| Pleasant interruptions mainly prolong a session | Examine session duration and returns to conflict separately from immediate mood. |
| Cognitive exertion contributes to ME/CFS symptom worsening | Relate the exposure to later symptoms and function while accounting for other exertion, posture, sleep, illness variability, and medications. |

The strongest supported account is that Twitter can alter emotional experience and reinforce outrage expression. The proposed explanation of cognitive cost adds the demands of interpretation, response control, switching, and continued involvement. Their interaction with ME/CFS, and their biochemical mechanism, remain open.
