Microcelebrity names a self-presentation *practice*, not a fame threshold: treating one's audience as a community to be cultivated and maintained through sustained, apparently personal disclosure, using the techniques of celebrity self-branding at a smaller and more intimate scale than broadcast fame.
The term was coined by Theresa M. Senft in *Camgirls: Celebrity and Community in the Age of Social Networks* (Peter Lang, 2008) ([publisher page](https://www.peterlang.com/document/1105668); [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/camgirlscelebrit0000senf)), an ethnography of early webcam performers ("camgirls") building audiences on personal homepages, LiveJournal, early YouTube, and MySpace.

## From camgirls to Instagram

Alice E. Marwick's subsequent work operationalizes microcelebrity for the platform-native [influencer](./influencer.md) economy.
*Status Update: Celebrity, Publicity, and Branding in the Social Media Age* (Yale University Press, 2013) ([Yale Books](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300209389/status-update/)) is an ethnography of San Francisco's Web 2.0-era tech and social-media scenes, arguing these technologies produced a status economy turning ordinary users into self-marketers.
Marwick, "Instafame: Luxury Selfies in the Attention Economy," *Public Culture* 27(1 [75]), pp. 137–160 (2015) ([Duke University Press](https://read.dukeupress.edu/public-culture/article-abstract/27/1%20(75)/137/31071/Instafame-Luxury-Selfies-in-the-Attention-Economy)), extends the concept to Instagram specifically, defining microcelebrity there as "a self-presentation strategy endemic to social media in which users strategically formulate a profile, reach out to followers, and reveal personal information to increase attention and improve their online status."
This definitional move — from a practice observed among camgirls to a general strategy any platform user can adopt — is what makes microcelebrity distinct from, and broader than, [influencer](./influencer.md) as Crystal Abidin later defines it: an influencer is a professionalized, often commercial, category of person; microcelebrity is the underlying self-presentation practice an influencer (among others) performs.
