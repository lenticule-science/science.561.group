A networked public, in danah boyd's formulation, is a public that has been restructured by networked technologies: it is simultaneously (1) the space constructed through networked technology and (2) the imagined collective that emerges as a result of the intersection of people, technology, and practice.
boyd develops this account in her chapter "Social Network Sites as Networked Publics: Affordances, Dynamics, and Implications," in [Zizi Papacharissi's edited volume *A Networked Self*](https://www.routledge.com/A-Networked-Self-Identity-Community-and-Culture-on-Social-Network-Sites/Papacharissi/p/book/9780415801812) (Routledge, 2010), pp. 47--66.

The chapter grounds networked publics in four affordances of social network sites --- persistence (content endures beyond the moment of utterance), replicability (content can be copied), scalability (content can spread to unexpectedly large audiences), and searchability (content can be located after the fact) --- and traces the social dynamics these affordances produce: an "invisible audience" that a speaker cannot fully see or anticipate at the moment of speaking, and "context collapse," in which audiences that would ordinarily be kept separate (family, coworkers, casual acquaintances) are flattened into a single addressable public.
These dynamics complicate norms of self-presentation and privacy that depend on being able to address one audience at a time.

Networked publics is the affordance-level, individual-experience companion to the more macro, institutional accounts of platform power covered by [platformization](./platformization.md) and the [platform society](./platform-society.md) thesis: where van Dijck, Poell, and de Waal describe platforms displacing institutions at a societal scale, boyd's chapter describes what platform architecture does to the structure of audience and self-presentation at the level of a single post or profile.
Both lines of work share a starting premise that the technical architecture of a platform is not neutral scaffolding but an active shaper of the social action that takes place on it, a premise also central to [Tarleton Gillespie's](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1461444809342738) account of platforms' curated ambiguity about what kind of space they claim to be.

For fuller sourcing and the gaps this literature still carries, see the [study of platformization and platform studies](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/22/2026-08-22-note-on-the-study-of-platformization-and-platform-studies.md).
