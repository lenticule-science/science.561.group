# Social Media Science Services Section

Social media science is the study of platform-mediated social media:
social network sites and their affordances, the platformization of media and cultural production, networked publics, the contested media-effects literature specific to social platforms, and the influencer/creator stratum of platform-native media production.
It is modeled here as a media science —
a sibling to `news-media-science-services-section`, `television-services-section`, `music-services-section`, and `zines-services-section` under `media-science-services-section` —
because its object is a media form and its production/circulation system, even though its methods draw heavily on sociology and computational social science.

## Seating derivation

Per `science-services-section`'s ownership rule, a note has one owner at its most specific defensible service section.
A library survey (2026-08-22) found no social-media, platform-studies, or influencer science anywhere under `science-services-section`.
What exists nearby:

- Engineering's `web2-social-platform-science-services-section` (under `web-science-services-section`) owns social platforms as engineered web systems — the system side, not the media-science literature.
- `web-media-services-section` (sibling here) owns web media broadly and now carries the web-news-media study; social media is a distinct production/circulation regime with its own literature (boyd & Ellison's SNS lineage, platformization, creator studies) and is not reducible to "web media" generally.
- The public-services forest's `public-social-services-section` and `public-media-services-section` are operational sections (doing social media), not science sections (studying it).

No existing section is more specific than the union of platform studies, social-media effects, and creator/influencer science as media literatures, so the section is founded here.

## Scope

This section owns:

- Platform studies and platformization as they bear on media: Gillespie's politics of platforms, van Dijck/Poell/de Waal's platform society, Helmond's platformization, Nieborg & Poell on platformized cultural production.
- Social network sites and networked publics: the boyd & Ellison definitional lineage, affordance theory for social media.
- Platform-specific media effects and their active controversies (adolescent wellbeing, echo chambers), held to the contested status the literature actually has.
- Influencer, creator, and microcelebrity science: Senft, Marwick, Abidin, Duffy, Cunningham & Craig, parasocial-interaction lineage.
- Computational social science methods where they study social media as media.

It does not own:
platform engineering (engineering's web-science forest),
news circulation on platforms (news-media-science and web-media own their aspects and are related to),
brand and influencer *marketing* effectiveness (owned by `market-science-services-section` under social science, which this section relates to for the influencer-marketing boundary),
or the operational running of the group's own social accounts (public-services forest).
