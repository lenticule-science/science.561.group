Canon, in media studies, is the set of facts, events, and relationships accepted as authoritative within a fictional world. It establishes what "actually happened" in the world and what can be relied upon as true across different works, tellings, or media set in that world.

The concept becomes significant wherever a fictional world extends beyond a single work. A novel series, a franchise, a shared-universe game [setting](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/setting.md), or a collaboratively built [paracosm](paracosm.md) must each negotiate what counts as established fact and what does not. Canon is the conventional answer to this negotiation: it designates some subset of the total material as binding.

Canon operates in tension with [continuity](continuity.md). Continuity is the internal consistency of a world across its realizations; canon is the social and editorial mechanism by which that consistency is maintained or enforced. A work may be internally consistent but non-canonical (fan fiction, alternate timelines), and canonical material may contain continuity errors.

In the historical [Reproducible Paracosm proposal](../../documents/articles/notes/2025/10/10/2025-10-10-note-on-reproducible-paracosmic-models.md), normalization $N$ was presented as the means by which a canon settles. **Correction (2026-10-17):** editorial canon authority and computational normalization are different types of work. A source representation may have a terminating, confluent normalizer, while the fictional world's event transitions can branch or remain unresolved. Neither a deterministic serializer nor a character's asserted authority establishes a fictional-world fact. The gluing condition $J$ asks whether specified local observations admit a compatible global interpretation; uniqueness and existence are separate proof obligations, not consequences of calling a text canonical. See [RPS scientific status](../../documents/articles/specifications/reproducible-paracosm-specification.md) and the [Alice stress test](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/10/17/2026-10-17-note-on-alice-as-a-paracosmic-stress-test.md).
