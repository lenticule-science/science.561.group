# Narnia as a multiworld paracosmic stress test

## 0. Question, method and scientific status

**Question.** Can our held Reproducible Paracosm Specification (RPS) preserve a world across changing political and ecological epochs, journeys between differently timed worlds, a fictional creator's hierarchically disclosed laws, and the narrative destruction of the created world—**without** (i) conflating the times of separate worlds, (ii) asserting that rival ontological claims mean identical objects, or (iii) elevating a narrator's theology into established external physics?

**Result.** An explicit *source-indexed and world-indexed relational model* accommodates these observations. The elementary two-order and fixed-rate lemmas below give **hand-proved finite counterexamples** to tempting stronger assumptions. No Lean proof, unique categorical model, full seven-book machine-readable canon, actual runtime test, or external physical theorem is claimed.

**Owner.** This research belongs to the *existing* Lenticule Science paracosm-science-services-section, inside information → communication → media science. It improves the existing [RPS v0.1](../../../../specifications/reproducible-paracosm-specification.md), [content schema v0.2](../../../../specifications/paracosm-content-schema.md), [relational worlding grammar](../../../../guides/relational-worlding-grammar.md), [epic-paracosm research](../../09/28/2026-09-28-note-on-the-science-of-epic-paracosms.md), and the contemporaneous [Alice observational stress test](2026-10-17-note-on-alice-as-a-paracosmic-stress-test.md). It is **not** a new world/canon, separate simulator, IP adaptation, or a parallel encyclopedia.

Chapter-level descriptions are cross-checked against the [C. S. Lewis scholarly site](https://www.cslewis.com/the-narnian-order-of-things/), [C. S. ## 1. Scholarly readings to preserve as *readings*

- **Chronology and Ovid.** Emma Ljung, "[Ovid at Cair Paravel: Periodization and the Ages of Man in C. S. Lewis's Chronicles of Narnia](https://doi.org/10.34679/thersites.vol19.283)," *thersites* 19 (2024), 285–311, argues that Lewis's art of dividing Narnian history owes important structural debts to the age-periodization of Ovid's *Metamorphoses*. This is an attributable scholarly *interpretation*, not proof of intention or a mathematical invariant.
- **Multiple worlds and Eastern sources.** Warwick Ball, [*East of the Wardrobe: The Unexpected Worlds of C. S. Lewis*](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197626252.001.0001) (Oxford, 2022), devotes a chapter specifically to "Portals and Alternative Time" and studies both Eastern influences and Calormen. Its source interpretation should remain distinguishable from the books' speaker claims.
- **Planetary structure (contested thesis).** Michael Ward, [*Planet Narnia: The Seven Heavens in the Imagination of C. S. Lewis*](https://academic.oup.com/book/26647) (Oxford, 2008), hypothesizes a seven-planet architecture of the Chronicles. This is a useful candidate *work-level thematic observation*, but must not be hard-coded as Lewis's proved design intent.
- **Ecology and stewardship.** Matthew Dickerson and David O'Hara, [*Narnia and the Fields of Arbol: The Environmental Vision of C. S. Lewis*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt2jchzg) (University Press of Kentucky, 2009), study environmental stewardship and the ecological consequences of Narnian regimes. This prevents reduction of a living world to a bare human adventure timeline.
- **Political/cultural representation (contested).** Andrzej Wicher, "[The Discourse of Orientalism in C. S. Lewis's Chronicles of Narnia](https://repozytorium.uni.lodz.pl/xmlui/handle/11089/28813)" (2013), reads Calormen through critical Orientalism. Debate about racism, gender and Lewis's intentions must retain source attribution and counterinterpretations; the fictional narrator's framing is not a morally neutral inventory of cultures. Compare also [Warwick Ball's Calormen chapter](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197626252.003.0004).

No stance in these studies automatically modifies the literary canon. **Source-reported event**, **character assertion**, **later commentator's claim** and **our proposed formal map** are different epistemic types.

**Negative controls:** do not state "all Narnian portals are stable", "the two worlds have a common conversion constant", "a character's age is globally monotone", "deep law = higher law", "the stable has a physically measured negative volume", "the earlier and real Narnias are equal sets", or "being described as real establishes a scientific ontology."

## 3. Model proposal: histories are local; crossings bind them

Let \(W=\{E,\mathrm{Wood},\mathrm{Charn},N,A\}\) denote **modeling labels** for Earth, Wood Between the Worlds, Charn, historical Narnia and Aslan's Country. Their equivalences and the exact scope of \(A\) are not assumed.

For each \(w\in W\), take a local event set \(E_w\) with a *partial* event-order \(\preceq_w\), optional clock observations \(\tau_w:E_w\rightharpoonup T_w\), and dated, edition-bound testimony. A global story index \(d\) records **presentation order**, and a separate publication index \(p\) orders books. Neither is, by default, a global event clock.

A **world-bridge event** is a record

\[
b=(e_{\rm departure},\,e_{\rm arrival},\,w_{\rm source},\,w_{\rm target},
       \,\mathrm{actor/object},\,\mathrm{medium},\,\mathrm{source},\,\mathrm{scope}),
\]

where the two events belong to the respective world histories. A bridge is **not a map on clock values**. The actor or object crossing a bridge carries a provenance or testimony trail: old body and new body states need not match, and an apple can yield an object that supports a later portal.

A novel's **work observation** describes a window on several local histories with its own point of view. Chronological overlap is permitted: an event sequence in *The Horse and His Boy* can coexist with the Pevensies' Golden Age rather than being forcibly placed after their return.

**Identity through change.** An actor \(a\) carries a set of annotated appearances and provenance paths. The typed queries "same named character?", "same embodied age?", "same polity?", and "same visible form?" require different observers. The Pevensies' royal maturity and resumed English childhood, and Kirke as a boy and later professor, put pressure on the same distinction introduced for Alice's bodily transformations. An identity declaration requires its original literary source and constraints rather than universal name matching.

### Law strata and incomplete knowledge

The Witch's understanding of Deep Magic is a **character-accessible law report** \(K_{\text{Witch}}(L_{\rm deep})\); Aslan's later account of Deeper Magic adds a source-reported conditional \(L_{\rm deeper}\) and modifies characters' beliefs. The valid inference is **not** \(\neg L_{\rm deep}\), nor that every lower-level rule is superseded. Model applicability and reported knowledge with separate predicates:

\[
\operatorname{applies}(L,w,e,c),\quad
\operatorname{knows}(a,L,e),\quad
\operatorname{reports}(a,L,\mathrm{source}).
\]

No total law hierarchy or typed modal endofunctor is proved merely by writing these predicates. "Before the dawn of Time" is a fictional description of law precedence or priority, and **not sufficient on its own** to posit a globally measurable pre-time interval.

### Historical world, terminal event and more-real world

Let \(N_{\rm history}\) be the created world's event domain ending at a terminal event described in *The Last Battle*. Let \(A\) be a distinct **reported** ontological context, with a story-world relation \(\rho\subseteq N_{\rm history}\times A\) expressing "image/corresponding real counterpart" for *selected* places, persons and values when the text gives warrant.

Neither \(N_{\rm history}=A\) nor \(\rho\) being a bijection, functor, isomorphism, equivalence, or continuous embedding is assumed. Historical destruction and continued narrative reference can coexist in this typed model. The assertion that something is "more real" remains indexed to its narrative/religious register; it does **not** establish a physical scale of existence, quantum regime, transfinite dimension, or an Obsmetrologia proof.

The *stable door* gives a particularly sharp example: treat "inside" as an observed portal destination in \(A\), not automatically a measurable subset of the stable's exterior region in \(N_{\rm history}\). A locality or metric may change across this boundary. The logical task is to state the domain and measurement before testing containment.

### Source-to-Mark and Obsmetrologia boundary

This research **starts at the source, not with primitive magical cosmology**. The textual scene/edition and source-span record is a **Mark-bearing input** for the existing Obsmetrologia research program. A narrator's or character's utterance is articulated with its speaker and frame; possible readings ("the wardrobe connects world E to N", "the witch claims a rule") are predicated under explicit types; affirmative or adverse evidence is posited; and settlement may occur **only** under the corresponding source, scope, authority and proof conditions. A reader's alternate interpretation is not automatically an opposite FOUR truth witness. A typed bridge event, an event-order relation or a source-backed world claim is therefore a proposed **downstream derivative**, not an independent replacement foundation.

This ordering is *conjectural as an end-to-end mathematical derivation*: no Lean declaration, unique Obsmetrologia normal form, Landauer/quot.sound energy-accounting correspondence, or installed SignCraft rule for a Narnian portal is evidenced here. Engineering must use source-derived finite vectors only when their Science status permits, and not assert a primitive portal ontological law before its type and sources are admitted.

## 4. Finite results and their proof status

### Lemma 1 — A single increasing re-indexing cannot preserve incompatible work orders

Let \(P\) be the strict publication order and \(D\) the fictional chronological order. From the publisher record, \( \mathrm{LWW}<_{P}\mathrm{MN}\), while \(\mathrm{MN}<_{D}\mathrm{LWW}\). A map \(f\) from publication positions into chronological positions that is **strictly order-preserving for both works** would imply \(f(\mathrm{LWW})< f(\mathrm{MN})\), contradicting their required \(D\)-ordering.

**Result:** the two indices cannot be replaced by one **single monotonically re-indexed** work order. This is an elementary finite proof relying only on the verified reversal. The correct implementation has independent publication and diegetic coordinates, with a nontrivial relation between them.

### Lemma 2 — No one constant converts both recorded visit intervals in a simplified chronology

A well-known narrative comparison is approximately one Earth year versus ~1,300 Narnian years between the first two relevant adventures, but approximately one Earth year versus **three** Narnian years before the next. The [NarniaWeb timeline review](https://www.narniaweb.com/books/timeline/) explicitly warns of no consistent ratio; compare [*Prince Caspian*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Caspian) and [*Dawn Treader*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voyage_of_the_Dawn_Treader) descriptions.

**Idealized finite fixture (not exact observed durations):** put \((\Delta_E,\Delta_N)_1=(1,1300)\) and \((\Delta_E,\Delta_N)_2=(1,3)\) in year-like abstract units. There is no fixed \(k\) with \(\Delta_N=k\,\Delta_E\) for both. The first demands \(k=1300\), the second \(k=3\). Thus **a fixed global conversion constant fails on the chosen rounded data**.

The narrative itself gives approximate or interval-dependent claims, so this lemma does **not** prove an exact Narnian rate schedule nor that clocks cannot have all sorts of piecewise transformations. It falsifies only the unjustified *constant-slope* model under the stated fixture.

### Lemma 3 — A reported "larger interior" rejects ordinary containment under one monotone measure

For any monotone measure \(\mu\) on a common measurable space, if \(I\subseteq S\) then \(\mu(I)\leq\mu(S)\). This follows from monotonicity (or additivity on \(S=I\sqcup(S\setminus I)\) for an ordinary nonnegative measure). Therefore a model that demands (1) the entire stable interior \(I\) is literally a measurable subset of the same-region exterior \(S\), and (2) \(\mu(I)>\mu(S)\), is inconsistent. A **portal into another world**, differing metric, viewpoint, or metaphysical literary description avoids equating incompatible spatial domains. This is a conditional elementary fact, not proof of the actual geometry of Narnia.

**Status:** all three are hand-derived finite results. They are not Lean-checked, nor evidence that natural existence is literally Narnian.

## 5. Pressure, ecology and cultural sources

Use the [relational worlding grammar](../../../../guides/relational-worlding-grammar.md) rather than defining Narnia only by its map:

- **World creation:** Aslan's song is narrated as a creation emission that changes the world's population, ecological relations and duties. The result depends on who can hear, speak and act; it is not just a new scenic backdrop.
- **The Witch's winter:** a political regime is coupled to seasonal suppression, fear, movement and resource access. Restore spring and you change more than the weather: new options for creatures and people become available.
- **Telmarine rule:** Cair Paravel's ruin, memories of talking beasts and claims about "Old Narnia" are political and ecological traces. An authority declaring an older world fictitious does not delete sources that attest its existence.
- **The Dawn Treader's eastern route:** islands, captivity, commerce, enchanted phenomena and moral changes couple travel to social consequences; not merely a list of places.
- **End-time ecological loss:** destruction of trees and talking-creature lifeways in *The Last Battle* has material and affective stakes. A declaration that a "real Narnia" is eternal does not authorize an environmental or cultural analyst to treat those lost histories as costless.

The critical point is **local consequences**: a regime or belief changes the permitted future of beings. Its portrayal as good/evil is a narrative valuation; science records who asserts it and how the story enacts consequences. Wicher, Ball and Dickerson/O'Hara provide distinct interpretive lenses; avoid treating a British author's fictional categories as neutral ethnic or ecological facts.

## 7. Inquiry raised by the study

1. Construct a typed small category from a directed multigraph of source-indexed local histories and admitted portal transitions; specify whether a functor to any shared temporal order is demanded, and give concrete countermodels where it fails.
2. Determine what a world-identity statement means when a diegetic world can change regime, end, and be related analogically to a separate "real" world. Establish the actual type of \(\rho\); do not assert a categorical embedding by metaphor.
3. Distinguish law applicability, character knowledge of laws, and narrative revelation order; test whether any modal operator preserves the intended source-indexed observations.
4. Derive the minimum corpus requirements for *work chronology* (including overlaps) separately from the *publication and audience encounter* orders, with versioned provenance and disagreements.
5. Ask whether the group's own ideas of ascension, reality strata, and energetic capacity yield testable structures or merely share words with Lewis's literary theology. No correspondence is admitted until it is stated, typed, and independently justified.
6. Compare the observational preservation obligations from [Alice](2026-10-17-note-on-alice-as-a-paracosmic-stress-test.md) with Narnia's world-bridge obligations using **one common source-indexed model** rather than two application-specific simulators.

**Answer:** A paracosm can be **coherent under source-governed observation** while having no universal clock, no order-preserving map between a writer's publication chronology and the story's history, no uniform portal type, and no requirement that the fictional world remains materially unchanged to remain narratively identifiable. This is a **research construction and bounded argument**, not a proof of RPS's still-open full categorical conjecture.
