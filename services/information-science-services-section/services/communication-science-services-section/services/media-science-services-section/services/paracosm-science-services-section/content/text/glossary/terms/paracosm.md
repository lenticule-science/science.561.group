A paracosm is a detailed, internally structured imaginary world, typically developed over an extended period through sustained imaginative engagement. The term belongs to developmental research on sustained imaginary-world play. Robert Silvey initiated a retrospective study in 1977; Silvey and Stephen A. MacKeith published *The Paracosm: A Special Form of Fantasy* in 1988. David Cohen and MacKeith analyzed the material in *The Development of Imagination: The Private Worlds of Childhood* (Routledge, 1991). The earlier title *Creating Imaginary Worlds* (1991), formerly stated here, is not the title of the Cohen–MacKeith book. These bibliographic dates and titles are corroborated by [Michele Root-Bernstein's scholarly resource list](https://inventingimaginaryworlds.com/resources/) and the later [Child Development review of paracosms](https://academic.oup.com/chidev/article/91/1/e164/8258225). The psychological construct concerns sustained imagined-world creation and participation, not merely a list of invented places.

In media studies and literary theory, the term has come to denote any sustained imaginative construction that exhibits internal coherence, developmental continuity, and sufficient structural depth to support multiple narrative realizations. Authors such as J.R.R. Tolkien, Ursula K. Le Guin, and Stephen King are frequently cited as paracosmic creators, though the concept extends beyond fiction to encompass speculative philosophical systems, game worlds, and collaborative imaginative projects.

A paracosm is distinct from a [secondary world](secondary-world.md) in emphasis: the paracosm foregrounds the phenomenology and psychology of world-creation (how the creator relates to and develops the imagined domain), while the secondary world foregrounds the literary and structural properties of the resulting artifact. A single creative project may be both. The [emsean paracosm](../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/emsean-paracosm.md) exemplifies this overlap.

The 561 Group's [Reproducible Paracosm research program](../../documents/articles/notes/2025/10/10/2025-10-10-note-on-reproducible-paracosmic-models.md) proposes categorical sketches and modal observations to model reproducibility across retellings and enactments. **Its broad categorical theorem remains unproved**; the [scientifically qualified specification](../../documents/articles/specifications/reproducible-paracosm-specification.md) identifies the open proof obligations. The [Alice in Wonderland and Looking-Glass study](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/10/17/2026-10-17-note-on-alice-as-a-paracosmic-stress-test.md) gives concrete source-indexed models and finite tests of its observational assumptions. An authored secondary world is not automatically proof of the creator's psychological participation in a paracosm.
