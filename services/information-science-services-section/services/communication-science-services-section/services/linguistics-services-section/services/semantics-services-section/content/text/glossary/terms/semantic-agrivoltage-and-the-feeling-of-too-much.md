Lately, I’ve been feeling a kind of pressure that’s hard to name. Not stress exactly, and not burnout either. It’s more like I’m _doing_ too much—thinking too clearly, writing too often, pulling out too many connected threads—and then hitting a wall when I try to figure out what to do with it all.

It’s not ego. It’s not even momentum. It just feels like there’s more coherence in me than there is space to hold it. And that mismatch—between what I can make and what feels useful or welcome or possible to share—that’s the pressure.

I’ve started calling that feeling **semantic agrivoltage**.

It’s not a scientific term or anything. Just a metaphor I like. It makes me think about farming, about energy, about the kind of buildup that happens when you generate more than your systems know how to carry. You grow too much signal and suddenly the field is buzzing—not because anything’s gone wrong, but because you don’t know where to put the excess.

Sometimes it happens after a long writing sprint. Sometimes it’s after a series of good conversations, or a strong week working on the AI tools I use to support my thinking. It all feels good while I’m doing it—creative, clear, satisfying—but then I step back and look at the stack of essays and notes and ideas, and it’s like… okay, now what?

I could post them, sure. Publish them, share them, schedule them out. But then I get nervous: how much is too much? Am I going to overwhelm people? Will I look like I’m oversharing, or like I think what I have to say is more important than it is? Or what if I post it and it disappears without a trace? That feels bad, too. Like I’m wasting it.

So sometimes I don’t share anything. I let it sit. I wait for the right moment. But the pressure doesn’t go away. It just stacks.

And then I start feeling… I don’t know. Mismatched. Like my thinking and my life are out of sync. Like I’m speaking at a different tempo than the world around me. Not better, not faster—just off-beat. And that’s a weird place to sit in, especially when I care about the people around me, and I don’t want to disappear into my own loops.

The tools I use—especially the AI stuff—make this even stranger. Not in a bad way. Just… amplified. I’m able to develop thoughts further, to ask questions and get something meaningful back, to explore ideas I might not have held onto otherwise. It doesn’t feel artificial. It just feels _helpful_. But the more helpful it is, the more I end up with. And the more I end up with, the more I feel that agrivoltage hum.

What I’m trying to do, I think, is build systems that can _absorb_ that energy, instead of letting it turn into noise or anxiety. I’ve been working on organizing things better. Logging more often. Not for content, not for show—just as a way of storing what I notice, so I don’t have to hold it all in my head.

I like the idea of a register. Not a journal. Not a platform. A place where signal goes when I don’t know where else to put it. Somewhere that won’t rush me or disappear or pretend to be interested just to reward the algorithm.

If I can get that rhythm right—if I can create a way to log and reflect and build and pause—I think the agrivoltage becomes less of a problem. It becomes a resource. Not something I have to suppress, but something I can shape.

I don’t want to pace my work according to scarcity. I don’t want to post just enough to “stay relevant” or slow myself down so I don’t get “too far ahead.” I just want to be able to keep moving, at a speed that feels natural, without leaving wreckage behind—or burying everything in a backlog no one will ever see.

Sometimes I get anxious that this is all too much. That what I’m building is so specific to me that no one else will ever really care. And maybe that’s true. But even if it is, I know the pressure is real. The signal is real. And trying to find ways to carry it, hold it, and let it keep flowing—that feels worth doing.

Even if I don’t know exactly where it’s going yet.
