Where [hanzi](./hanzi.md) fixes the [meme
gauge](./script-as-gauge.md#a-namespace-warning-stated-explicitly), an
alphabet fixes the **phoneme gauge**: each letter (or, more precisely, each
grapheme) coordinates a phoneme or small phoneme-cluster of the language it
writes, not a morpheme or a meaning. This is [script as
gauge](./script-as-gauge.md)'s other half, and the founding note treats the
alphabet/hanzi split as a difference in *which stratum a script chooses to
coordinate*, not a difference in sophistication — an alphabet is not a
simplified or earlier-stage hanzi, and hanzi is not an ornamented or
later-stage alphabet; they are answers to different questions.

## Consequences of the phoneme-gauge choice

- **Thin per-symbol information.** A letter alone tells a reader almost
  nothing about meaning — 'r' is not a witness to anything beyond a class of
  sounds. The chart's coordinates are cheap precisely because they carry so
  little.
- **Fast acquisition of the symbol set.** An alphabet's inventory is small
  (roughly 20–40 symbols for most languages), learnable in isolation before
  any vocabulary is acquired — the reverse of hanzi, where each new
  character is close to a new unit of study in itself.
- **Vulnerable to gauge drift.** Because an alphabet's coordinates track
  sound and sound changes over time, an alphabetic orthography can drift far
  from the pronunciation it once tracked (English spelling being the
  frequently-cited case) without anything in the writing system itself
  flagging the drift — there is no equivalent of a radical staying visibly
  "wrong" the way an obsolete phonetic component can in hanzi. The chart
  keeps being used long after it has stopped matching the territory it was
  gauge-fixed to.
- **Meaning arrives only through the word, never the letter.** An
  alphabet's meme-layer information is carried entirely by the sequence
  (the word), never by any individual coordinate — the opposite of a hanzi
  radical, which can carry meaning-layer information even in isolation or
  as part of an unfamiliar compound.

## Not a value ranking

Naming hanzi "jade-stage" (in the founding note's thickness-doctrine
vocabulary) is not a claim that phoneme-gauge scripts are worse scripts.
Thinness is what makes alphabets cheap, fast, and highly portable across
languages with very different phoneme inventories (the Latin alphabet's
spread onto languages it was never designed for is exactly this
portability); a script's value is a function of what a writing community
needs a chart for, not an intrinsic property of the gauge chosen.
