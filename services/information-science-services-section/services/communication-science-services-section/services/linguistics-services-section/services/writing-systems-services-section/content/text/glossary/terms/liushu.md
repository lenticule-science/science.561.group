Liùshū (六書/六书, "six writings" or "six categories") is the traditional
taxonomy of how Chinese characters are formed, first systematized by Xǔ
Shèn (許慎) in the *Shuōwén Jiězì* (說文解字, c. 100 CE), the earliest
comprehensive Chinese character dictionary. It classifies every character
by *how* it carries its meaning and sound — which, read through this
section's [script-as-gauge](./script-as-gauge.md) framing, makes it a
witness-structure classification: a taxonomy, produced by the tradition
itself roughly two thousand years ago, of the different ways a character's
formation history stays legible in its form.

## The six categories

### 象形 xiàngxíng — pictographs
Characters that originated as stylized pictures of the object they denote:
日 (rì, "sun"), 木 (mù, "tree"), 山 (shān, "mountain"). The smallest
category in modern use, but the most transparent — the witness is the
resemblance itself.

### 指事 zhǐshì — indicatives (simple ideographs)
Characters that indicate an abstract relation or position rather than
picturing a concrete object: 上 (shàng, "up," a mark above a line), 下
(xià, "down," a mark below a line), 一/二/三 (the numbers one through
three, as accumulated strokes). Also small in number.

### 會意 huìyì — compound ideographs
Characters that combine two or more meaning-bearing components whose
combined sense yields the character's meaning: 休 (xiū, "to rest") is 人
(person) beside 木 (tree) — a person resting against a tree. 明 (míng,
"bright") is 日 (sun) beside 月 (moon). The witness here is compositional:
two prior charges combined into a third.

### 形聲 xíngshēng — phono-semantic compounds
Characters combining a semantic radical (indicating meaning category) with
a phonetic component (indicating, approximately, sound). This is by far
the largest category — commonly cited at roughly 80–90% of characters in
the modern standard set (DeFrancis 1984). 河 (hé, "river") pairs the water
radical 氵with the phonetic 可 (kě); 恨 (hèn, "to hate/resent") pairs the
heart radical 忄with the phonetic 艮 (gèn). This is the category the
founding note singles out for its double witness: the radical marks *what
kind* of thing, the phonetic marks *fossilized sound*, and both remain
visible after the character has otherwise drifted from either.

### 轉注 zhuǎnzhù — transfer characters (mutually explanatory)
The most contested category, with no settled consensus among traditional or
modern scholars on its exact criteria — usually described as characters
that share a radical and a closely related meaning, and were historically
treated as mutually defining or substitutable (老 lǎo "old" and 考 kǎo,
an older sense also connected to age/testing, are the textbook pair). Its
inclusion is often defended on completeness grounds (the *Shuōwén*'s own
account requires it) more than on a crisp formation mechanism.

### 假借 jiǎjiè — phonetic loan characters
Characters borrowed for their sound alone, to write a different word that
had no character of its own — the sound-witness detached from its original
meaning-witness and reassigned. 來 (lái) originally pictured a wheat plant
and was borrowed for the phonetically similar word "to come," which is now
its only common sense. This category is the one place in the traditional
taxonomy where the witness is sound *without* the meaning that produced the
graphic form — the closest liùshū category has to [script as
gauge](./script-as-gauge.md)'s account of alphabetic (pure phoneme-gauge)
coordination happening inside a meme-gauge script.

## Status

The six-category taxonomy and its examples are standard, long-established
historical-linguistic content, not a claim original to this estate's
theory; only the *reading* of liùshū as "a witnessed taxonomy of
witness-structures" is the estate's own framing, inherited from the
founding note and not independently argued further here.
