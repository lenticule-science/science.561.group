A writing system is a system for visually representing a language with
symbols — the original 2021 personal-glossary definition, retained here
unchanged as the entry's core claim. See the paired stub, [written
language](./written-language.md), for language presented through a writing
system: the object a writing system produces.

## In this section's terms

Read through [script as gauge](./script-as-gauge.md), a writing system is a
gauge choice: a coordinate chart fixing which stratum of a language's
witnessed field its visible symbols track. The major typological families
are different answers to that choice, not points on one scale of
sophistication:

- **Alphabets and abjads/abugidas** — [phoneme-gauge](./alphabet-as-phoneme-gauge.md)
  systems, coordinating the sound layer (Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic,
  Devanagari)
- **Logographic/morphosyllabic systems** — [meme-gauge](./hanzi.md) systems,
  coordinating the meaning layer directly ([hanzi](./hanzi.md) and its
  historical derivatives: kanji, hanja, chữ Nôm)
- **Syllabaries** — an intermediate gauge, coordinating the syllable as a
  unit without hanzi's compositional meaning-layer structure (hiragana,
  katakana, Cherokee syllabary)
