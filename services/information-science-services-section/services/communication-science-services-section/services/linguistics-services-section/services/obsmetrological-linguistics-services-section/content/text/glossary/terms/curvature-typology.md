Curvature typology is the fifth instrument: computing Lambek bi-residuation
curvature (Axiom A6's account of where the field curves — see [language
light cone](./language-light-cone.md)) empirically, per language, directly
from that language's corpus. Where composition is most non-commutative
(where word-order and binding constraints are tightest and most asymmetric),
the construction space curves; the instrument's proposal is to compute this
directly rather than relying on manually assembled feature checklists of the
kind typological databases currently use (word-order type, case-marking
pattern, and so on, catalogued by hand).

The claimed payoff is a replacement of discrete feature checklists with
continuous curvature signatures per language — and, more specifically, a
point where the theory's two research programs meet: predicted learner
difficulty becomes the transport cost between a learner's L1 curvature field
and the target L2's curvature field. The source note states this
explicitly: the physics of the language (curvature typology, computed
per-language) is offered as the terrain map for the kinematics of the
learner (transport cost as predicted difficulty).

## Status

Instrument design; no per-language curvature signature has been computed
from a real corpus, and no transport-cost difficulty prediction has been
tested against real learner error data. This is named directly as
falsification obligation #3 in the theory note: curvature-derived difficulty
should predict observed L2 error distributions, and that prediction has not
yet been checked.
