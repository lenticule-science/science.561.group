Persistence of constructions is the second of the five instruments. It
treats time-snapshots of a living corpus as forming a filtration — a nested
sequence of structures indexed by time, the object persistent homology is
built to analyze — using filtration machinery the source note states
pymetrologic already carries. Persistence over that filtration would
distinguish which constructions are stable cycles that recur across many
snapshots, which are recent births, and which are deaths, in the technical
sense persistence diagrams give those words in topological data analysis.

The contrast drawn in the source note is with historical semantics as
currently practiced: it has narration — a scholar's account of how a
construction arose, spread, and fell out of use, argued from selected
examples — where a persistence diagram over an actual corpus filtration
would be measurement: a quantity computed from the full witnessed record,
with the birth and death of a construction as datable, provenance-carrying
events in the diagram rather than as points in a narrative.

## Status

This is an instrument design carried over directly from the flywheel note;
no corpus filtration or persistence diagram for any real construction has
been computed. Whether persistence diagrams over witnessed-utterance
filtrations actually correspond to what historical linguists recognize as
construction birth/stability/death is an untested empirical question.
