The **perlocutionary act** is Austin's (1962) third component of a
[speech act](speech-act.md): what is brought about *by* saying
something — its effect on the feelings, thoughts, or actions of an
audience (persuading, alarming, convincing, deterring), as distinct
from the force with which it was said (illocution). Austin treats
perlocutionary effects as characteristically unintended and
audience-dependent: the same illocutionary act can persuade one hearer
and alarm another.

## In Obsmetrologia

The factorization theorem
([`predication-locution-illocution-perlocution-factorization.md`](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/predication-locution-illocution-perlocution-factorization.md))
identifies perlocution with the **closure step**: "The perlocution
factor applies the closure operator $\mathrm{cl}$, deriving all
consequences of the injected evidence," formally the closure 2-monad's
unit $\eta$ at the post-positing settling. The theorem states its own
idealization directly, under the name "perlocutionary determinism": "In
PredCat, the closure operator is deterministic: it derives exactly what
the rules say, no more, no less. The perlocutionary step
$\mathrm{cl}$ is therefore rule-governed and predictable — no
unintended effects... This is a deliberate idealization of the
speech-act model, applicable to formal systems (databases, knowledge
bases, institutional rule systems) where the inference rules are fixed
and mechanistic. In natural language, unintended perlocutionary effects
would require a more general (non-deterministic or probabilistic)
closure operator." This named idealization is the gap
[uptake](uptake.md) is read, as inference, to fill.

Under $K_4$, perlocution's equivariance is conditional rather than
fixed: the stratification theorem
([`predication-klein-four-stratifies-locution-illocution-perlocution.md`](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/predication-klein-four-stratifies-locution-illocution-perlocution.md))
proves "Perlocution is $K_4$-equivariant iff $\mathcal{S}$ is
$K_4$-closed" — whether the symmetry commutes with closure depends on
whether the closure-rule system itself respects the bilattice symmetry,
not on any structural fact about closure in general.

As a channel-theoretic object (status proven,
[`from-predicate-category-channel-theoretic-speech-acts.md`](../../../../../../../../../../../philosophy-services-section/services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/from-predicate-category-channel-theoretic-speech-acts.md)),
perlocution is **information flow** through a Barwise–Seligman channel:
"The closure operator $\mathrm{cl}$ defines a ... channel from
$\mathrm{Cls}(v)$ to $\mathrm{Cls}(\mathrm{cl}(v))$," and "the
perlocutionary effect of a speech act ... [is] the set of new incidences
introduced by closing $v'$" — new truth-designations propagating
downstream from the posited cell through the closure rules that make up
the channel's theory.
