Grammar as gauge is Axiom A5's claim: grammars, dictionaries, and
orthographies are coordinate choices on the field of witnessed use — gauge
fixings that make local computation possible and that carry no ontological
priority over the field itself. The field (the totality of witnessed
[utterance-events](./witnessed-utterance-event.md) and their [arrow
bundles](./arrow-bundle-meaning.md)) is the territory; a grammar is one chart
on it, chosen for convenience, not discovered as the underlying reality.

Rival orthographies for the same language are, on this account, different
charts on one manifold, and the work of reconciling them — building
transition maps between them — is what linguistics does when it compares
grammars. Gauge confusion is the name given to the error of mistaking a
chart for the territory: treating one grammar's coordinates as if they were
the field itself, so that whatever the chart does not represent is treated
as not existing. The theory diagnoses this as the colonial error in
linguistics — the historical practice of imposing one language's grammatical
categories as the measure of another's, or of a standard dialect's grammar as
the measure of a living speech community, is gauge confusion enacted as
policy. The forced-projection reading of standardization in [cohomological
texture](./cohomological-texture.md) is this same error described in its
cohomological effect rather than its epistemic one.

A further consequence: the traditional competence/performance distinction is
said to dissolve under this axiom. Competence becomes the gauge-fixed
compression of the witnessed field — a chart, not a separate underlying
faculty — rather than an idealized system that performance imperfectly
realizes.

## Status

This is a framing axiom rather than a numerically falsifiable claim on its
own; the source note grades the whole axiom set Research-grade and points to
its Obligations for what empirical work would confirm the larger theory. No
claim is made here that "gauge confusion" is an established diagnosis beyond
this theory's own framing.
