Computed dialect is the first of the five instruments obsmetrological
linguistics derives from the Witnessed Field theory. It takes [cohomological
texture](./cohomological-texture.md) (Axiom A4) and turns it into a
procedure: treat the speech community as a base space, treat witnessed
utterances as sections over speakers, places, times, and registers, and
define a dialect as a maximal region over which those sections glue
consistently.

This gives "a dialect" a mathematical definition for the first time, in
place of the traditional practice of drawing isoglosses by hand from a
linguist's judgment calls. Dialect boundaries become computed level-sets of
gluing failure — places where sections stop gluing, marked with provenance
(which witnessed forms produced the boundary) and error bars (how confident
the boundary computation is), rather than a line drawn on a map by
inspection. Nonzero `H¹` in a given region measures obstructions that no
single grammar can flatten into one description — the cohomological texture
of a living language read out as an actual computed quantity over that
region.

The source note frames this as, specifically, the anti-standardization
instrument for the Lakota continuum and its multiple orthographies: rather
than a single grammar declaring one orthography correct, the instrument
would compute where sections genuinely fail to glue and represent that
failure directly, with its own provenance, instead of resolving it by fiat.

## Status

This is an instrument design, not a built or run system. No corpus has yet
been processed this way; the claim that gluing-failure level-sets would
track intuitively recognized dialect boundaries is untested. It inherits the
Research-grade status, and the unmet obligations, of the [cohomological
texture](./cohomological-texture.md) axiom it computes.
