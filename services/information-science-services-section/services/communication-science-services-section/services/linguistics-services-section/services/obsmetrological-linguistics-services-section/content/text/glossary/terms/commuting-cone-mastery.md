Commuting-cone mastery is Axiom A8's account of what it means to have
learned an item of language, stated in category-theoretic terms. A language
is the apex of a cone over its column-leg diagram — legs that are
type-theoretic, proof-theoretic, categorical, domain-theoretic,
path-theoretic, and transfer, with the classical Curry-Howard-Lambek
three-column pattern (proofs = programs = morphisms) as a specialization of
this larger cone. This is the repo coinage "Language Cone," from the 2026-05
SPOTCOMP CHL Language Cone profile and the 2026-07 PPA Language Cone.

At the scale of the language as a whole, the coincidence theorem — that all
of the cone's legs commute (PPA-FR-040) — is a stated *open* obligation, not
a proven result. At the scale of an individual speaker, the same structure
gives a definition: a learner holds a partial cone over the diagram (a
reading leg, a listening leg, a speaking leg, a kin/protocol leg, per the
design dialogue's phrasing), and a given item is mastered exactly when its
legs commute — production agrees with comprehension agrees with hearing.
Obsmetrologia's consistency-across-contexts gluing check is offered as the
commutativity test. On this account, fluency is the coincidence theorem
worked out item by item, rather than a single global property a learner
either has or lacks.

The design dialogue frames the contrast with incumbent language-learning
tools explicitly: most test one leg only (typically recognition), where this
definition tests the full commuting square.

## Distinct from the language light cone (A6)

This term shares the word "cone" with [language light
cone](./language-light-cone.md) (Axiom A6) but names a different
mathematical object — a category-theoretic limit cone with legs and an
apex, not a causal-set light cone defined by temporal influence. See that
entry's disambiguation section; the two must not be conflated even though
both are literally called cones in the source material.

## Status

The coincidence theorem at language scale is explicitly an open obligation
in the source theory, not a proven result; the speaker-scale reading
(mastery as leg-commutation) is a Research-grade definitional proposal, and
the source theory's Obligations section does not separately list a test for
it beyond the general falsification program.
