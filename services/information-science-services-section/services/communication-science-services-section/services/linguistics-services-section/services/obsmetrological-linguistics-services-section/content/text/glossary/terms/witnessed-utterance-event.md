A witnessed utterance-event is the Witnessed Field theory's primitive: not a
sentence, not a proposition, not a token, but an observed event comprising
participants (each with a kin/social position), place-time, medium, a form
trace, uptake, and consent status.

The move this makes is ontological rather than merely notational. Prior
theories of language start from an idealized unit — the sentence in
generative grammar, the proposition in formal semantics, the token in
distributional models — and treat actual instances of speech as noisy
evidence for that idealization. The Witnessed Field theory inverts this: it
holds that there are no unobserved linguistic facts, only witnessed
observations, fused with provenance. What a grammar or a lexicon states is
downstream compression (see [grammar as gauge](./grammar-as-gauge.md)), not
the ground floor.

Consent is built into the primitive itself, not added as an ethical
afterthought. Regions of the field may be legitimately kin-gated or
season-gated, and the theory represents that gating natively — a witnessed
utterance-event that was never meant to circulate outside its kin group is
not incomplete data to be extracted; its consent status is part of what the
event *is*. The source note observes that no predecessor theory of language
carries consent in its foundations this way.

## Status

This is the foundational primitive of a Research-grade theory (see the
source note's Obligations section for what would confirm or falsify the
larger axiom set). No claim is made here that any existing corpus already
records events at this granularity — building an apparatus that does is
future work.
