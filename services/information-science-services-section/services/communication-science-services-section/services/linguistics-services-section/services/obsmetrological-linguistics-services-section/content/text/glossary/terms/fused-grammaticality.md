Fused grammaticality is the third instrument, and the one that most directly
retires a standard tool of formal linguistics: the starred-sentence oracle,
where a linguist marks a sentence `*ungrammatical` from their own judgment
and treats that judgment as ground truth. In its place, a construction's
grammaticality status is the fused, provenance-carrying belief computed from
witnessed judgments and witnessed productions across the community — every
speaker's acceptability judgment and every attested use is a witnessed
observation contributing to a fused distribution, not a single expert's
star.

Speaker variation is treated as structure to preserve rather than noise
around a "true" grammar that variation obscures. The instrument's slogan, in
its source: the fused distribution *is* the grammar. This is presented as
dissolving the competence/performance dodge — the move where deviations from
a stated grammar are explained away as performance error — into something
measurable: if the fused distribution shows a construction is contested,
that contestation is the grammatical fact, not evidence of noise to be
filtered before finding the real grammar underneath.

The source note frames this as philosophically native to a language whose
authority structure is kinship rather than an academy: settlement (see
[settlement](./settlement.md)) already fuses witnessed uptake under
kin-weighted authority, and grammaticality-as-fused-belief is that same
mechanism read out specifically for acceptability judgments.

## Status

Instrument design, not yet run. No fused grammaticality distribution has
been computed for any real construction; the claim that such a distribution
would track what linguists currently capture with starred-sentence judgments
(while additionally capturing contestation those judgments discard) is
untested.
