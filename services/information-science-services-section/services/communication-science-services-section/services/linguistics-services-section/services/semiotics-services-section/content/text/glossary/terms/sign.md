A sign is something that stands for something else to an interpreter.

This is [Peirce's](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) formulation, and it is more careful than it sounds. The sign relation is triadic: it involves the sign itself (the [representamen](./representamen.md)), the object the sign stands for, and the [interpretant](./interpretant.md) — the meaning produced in the interpreter by the sign. Without all three, there is no sign: a mark without an interpreter produces no meaning, and a meaning without a mark is not yet communicable [@peirce_CollectedPapers_1931].

The [interpretant](./interpretant.md) is the crucial third term. It is the functional effect the sign produces — the sign's "meaning" in the sense of what it does, what it allows the interpreter to do or infer or feel. The [interpretant](./interpretant.md) is itself a sign (of the same object), which is why [Peirce](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md)'s [semiotics](../index.md) is intrinsically dynamic: signs generate [interpretants](./interpretant.md) that are themselves signs that generate further [interpretants](./interpretant.md), producing an open-ended chain of [semiosis](./semiosis.md) [@short_PeirceTheorySigns_2007].

## Types of signs

[Peirce](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) classified signs in multiple intersecting ways. Three basic types by the relation between sign and object:

- **[icon](./icon.md)**: resembles its object (a portrait, a diagram)
- **[Index](./index-sign.md)**: is causally or spatially connected to its object (smoke indexing fire, a finger pointing)
- **[symbol](./symbol.md)**: is connected to its object by convention (most words)
