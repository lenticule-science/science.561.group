Denotation is the first-order meaning of a [sign](./sign.md) — the direct, conventional relation between [signifier](./signifier.md) and [signified](./signified.md).

The term was formalized by [Louis Hjelmslev](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/louis-hjelmslev.md) in *Prolegomena to a Theory of Language* (1943) and made central to cultural analysis by [Roland Barthes](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/roland-barthes.md) in *Elements of Semiology* (1964). In Hjelmslev's framework, denotation is the semiotic system in which the expression plane (signifier) and the content plane (signified) are directly linked. A photograph of a street scene denotes that street — that is its first-order meaning.

Denotation is not "natural" or "innocent." [Barthes](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/roland-barthes.md) argued that what appears to be pure denotation is always already shaped by [connotation](./connotation.md) — the second-order meanings that attach to the sign through cultural convention. The appearance of denotation as a transparent, unmediated relation to reality is itself an ideological effect: it naturalizes what is in fact culturally produced [@barthes_ElementsSemiology_1964].

## Relation to connotation

Denotation and [connotation](./connotation.md) form a hierarchy of signification. The denotative [sign](./sign.md) (the union of [signifier](./signifier.md) and [signified](./signified.md)) becomes the [signifier](./signifier.md) of a second-order system — the connotative system — whose [signified](./signified.md) is an ideological or cultural meaning. A photograph of a soldier saluting a flag denotes that scene; it connotes patriotism, national unity, military honor. Myth, in [Barthes](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/roland-barthes.md)'s analysis, operates at this connotative level.

## Related terms

- [sign](./sign.md) — the union of signifier and signified (in Saussurean terms)
