The representamen is the form a [sign](./sign.md) takes — the mark, sound, gesture, image, or token that enters into a [sign](./sign.md) relation. It is the thing that does the representing, as distinct from the object it represents and the [interpretant](./interpretant.md) it produces.

In [Peirce's](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) triadic model, the representamen is sometimes called the "[sign](./sign.md) vehicle" to distinguish it from "[sign](./sign.md)" in the broader sense (the whole triadic relation). A written word on a page is a representamen; the triadic relation among that word, its object, and the meaning it produces in a reader is the [sign](./sign.md) [@peirce_CollectedPapers_1931].

The representamen is roughly analogous to Saussure's [signifier](./signifier.md), but the correspondence is imperfect. Saussure's [signifier](./signifier.md) exists in a two-place relation with the [signified](./signified.md); [Peirce](../../../../../../../../../knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md)'s representamen exists in a three-place relation with object and [interpretant](./interpretant.md). The triadic structure means the representamen cannot be understood apart from its role in producing [interpretants](./interpretant.md) — it is not just a form awaiting meaning but a form that actively participates in meaning-making.

## Related terms

- [sign](./sign.md) — the whole triadic relation
- [interpretant](./interpretant.md) — the meaning the representamen helps produce
