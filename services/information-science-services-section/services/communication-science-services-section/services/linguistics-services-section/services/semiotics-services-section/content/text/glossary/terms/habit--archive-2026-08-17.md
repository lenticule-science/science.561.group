A habit is a tendency to act, think, or interpret in a particular way under particular circumstances. In [Peirce's](../../../../../../../references/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) philosophy, habit is not merely psychological but cosmological: it is the general form of regularity, the way the universe acquires its laws and the way [signs](./sign.md) acquire their meanings.

Peirce identified the **ultimate logical [interpretant](./interpretant.md)** — the final product of [semiosis](./semiosis.md) — as a habit-change: "the deliberately formed, self-analyzing habit — self-analyzing because formed by the aid of analysis of the exercises that nourished it — is the living definition, the veritable and final logical interpretant" (CP 5.491). An emotional [interpretant](./interpretant.md) is a feeling; an energetic [interpretant](./interpretant.md) is an action; a logical [interpretant](./interpretant.md) is a habit or a modification of a habit.

Habit-change is therefore the mechanism by which [semiosis](./semiosis.md) stabilizes. The open-ended chain of [sign](./sign.md) producing [interpretant](./interpretant.md) producing [sign](./sign.md) does not run forever in practice — it settles into habits of interpretation. These habits are not fixed: they are themselves subject to modification by further [semiosis](./semiosis.md). Peirce's "law of habit" extends this beyond psychology to nature itself: all regularities are habits, and habits change by the habit of habit-change.

Habit belongs to [Thirdness](./peirces-categories.md) in Peirce's categories — it is a general rule or tendency, not a particular feeling (Firstness) or brute reaction (Secondness).

## Formal correspondence

In the [semiotic universe](../../../../mathematics/objects/universes/semiotic-universe/semiotic-universe.md) construction, the modal closure operator $j$ formalizes habit-formation. An element $a$ of the meaning domain $H$ where $j(a) = a$ is a meaning that has become habitual — its interpretation no longer changes under further examination. The gap $j(a) > a$ marks meanings still undergoing semiosis, not yet settled into habit.
