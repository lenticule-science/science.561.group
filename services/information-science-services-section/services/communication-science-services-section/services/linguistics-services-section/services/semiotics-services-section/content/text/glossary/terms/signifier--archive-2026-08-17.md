The signifier (*signifiant*) is one half of [Saussure](../../../../../../../references/people/ferdinand-de-saussure.md)'s dyadic model of the [sign](./sign.md). It is the form of the [sign](./sign.md) — the sound-image, the written mark, the gesture — as distinct from the concept it evokes (the [signified](./signified.md)).

Saussure insisted that the signifier is not the physical sound itself but the mental impression of it — what he called the "sound-image." The relationship between signifier and [signified](./signified.md) is arbitrary: there is no natural connection between the sound /tri:/ and the concept of a tree. The pairing is conventional and differs across languages [@saussure_CourseGeneralLinguistics_1916].

In [Peirce's](../../../../../../../references/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) triadic model, the roughly corresponding concept is the [representamen](./representamen.md) — the [sign](./sign.md) vehicle. But the correspondence is imperfect: [Peirce](../../../../../../../references/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)'s [representamen](./representamen.md) participates in a three-place relation (with object and [interpretant](./interpretant.md)), while Saussure's signifier participates in a two-place relation (with [signified](./signified.md) only).
