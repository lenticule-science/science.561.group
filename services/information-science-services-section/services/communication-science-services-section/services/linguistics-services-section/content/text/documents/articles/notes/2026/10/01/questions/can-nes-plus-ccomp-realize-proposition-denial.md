# Can nes + ccomp realize proposition denial?

RelConLang already has two pieces that can be composed experimentally without inventing a new negative particle:

- `nes`, the lexical verb glossed "negate";
- `ve`, the complement-clause marker accepted after ordinary verbs.

Both the shared grammar strategy and current SignCraft parser permit a matrix verb to take an embedded `ve` clause. No existing corpus example was found using `nes` this way, so this remains a new assay rather than established grammar.

Candidate:

[
mi ga nes do ve P
]

reads at the syntax level as a matrix `nes` event whose complement is proposition (P).

This is attractive because current SignCraft already has a real negative-evidence substrate: formal contexts carry positive incidence and denial incidence separately, and the denial lane can derive `active` without erasing positive support.

The semantic bridge should still be two-stage.

1. The utterance creates a **denial report** about (P).
2. An explicit evidential policy may promote that authenticated report into an against-side witness for (P).

This avoids giving grammar magical truth authority while providing a lawful route from linguistic denial to the existing negative-evidence algebra.

A control construction using `nul … ve P` should remain refusal, not proposition falsity.
