# What makes a RelConLang register a register?

RelConLang currently labels four fixture families: everyday, technical, proof, and ritual. The tests verify properties of those labeled examples; they do not infer a register from an utterance or generate utterances from a register policy.

The shared communicating algebra already has a generic register fiber with separate axes for:

`register`, `style`, `evidentiality`, `politeness`, and `genre`.

That separation is useful. It means a register should constrain **how** an intended semantic object is realized, not manufacture semantic content.

In particular, the current proof fixture's `sha` should not become a rule that proof speech is contradiction-valued. `sha` remains an independently chosen stance. Likewise ritual parallelism should not automatically create acknowledgement force.

The candidate reconstruction treats:

- everyday as brevity / recoverable omission;
- technical as explicitness when the corresponding content is genuinely present;
- proof as proposition/warrant/repair visibility, with stance orthogonal;
- ritual as lawful parallelism and mnemonic form, with dialogue moves orthogonal.

A future RelConLang realization codec can then accept a register profile as one coordinate of the realization plan rather than hard-code four text templates.
