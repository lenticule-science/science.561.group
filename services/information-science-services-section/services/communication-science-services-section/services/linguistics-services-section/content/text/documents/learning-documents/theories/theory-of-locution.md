# Theory of Locution

Locution is the first layer of speaking: what is said. It fixes what an utterance is about before any force is attached to it. In the Group's mathematics it has an exact place. Every operationally realizable change to a settled record factors into three morphisms at three distinct positions:

1. **Locution:** select the cell φ that will be addressed. Nothing changes yet.
2. **Illocution:** posit a datum q at φ. This is the force of the act.
3. **Perlocution:** close under the rule system. This derives every consequence of what was posited.

This is Austin's speech-act analysis, and it is a categorical factorization, not an analogy.

## Primitives

1. **Cell-indexed settling category** PS_Φ: the Grothendieck construction whose objects are pairs (settling v, target cell φ) and whose morphisms pair a knowledge-increase of the settling with a cell translation in the predicate category.
2. **Locution projection** π: PS_Φ → Φ, the forgetful functor that keeps only the targeted cell.
3. **Positing** posit_(φ,q): the morphism that joins datum q into cell φ by ∨k.
4. **Closure** cl^S: the closure operator generated by a rule system S.

## Laws

- **C1. Factorization.** Every single-cell realizable step v₀ → cl(v₀ ∨k δ_(φ,q)) decomposes as (select φ) ; (posit q at φ) ; (close). For a batch of cells W the same holds with W as a multi-cell locution. The decomposition is unique up to the choice of a minimal positing set and the commuting of independent postings at distinct cells.
- **C2. Locution changes nothing.** Selecting the cell is prior to any evidence. In PS alone it is metalinguistic; PS_Φ internalizes it as the fiber selection of a Grothendieck construction.
- **C3. The four forces are FOUR.** The datum q of an illocution is one of four forces: asserting (t), denying (f), abstaining (⊥), contesting (⊤). Each is a distinct act, including abstention.
- **C4. Forces combine by the knowledge join.** Two assertions agree. Two denials agree. An assertion and a denial of the same content produce a contested content: t ∨k f = ⊤. The conflict is held, not collapsed. Contesting absorbs; abstaining adds nothing.
- **C5. Stratification by symmetry.** The Klein four-group K₄ = ⟨negation, conflation⟩ acts on the three layers differently. It acts trivially on locution (it never touches the cell index), non-trivially and equivariantly on illocution (negation swaps asserting and denying; conflation swaps abstaining and contesting), and on perlocution only if the rule system is K₄-closed. The K₄-closure defect of a rule system measures how it treats asserting and denying, or abstaining and contesting, asymmetrically.
- **C6. Denial at the boundary.** At the admission boundary, Obsmetrologia's no-denial law holds: a denial enters as the articulation of the codec's contrary predicate and is measured as negative evidence. The force algebra in C3 and C4 is what that measurement reads.
- **C7. Perlocution is rule-governed.** In a formal system, closure derives exactly what the rules say. Unintended effects in natural language need a non-deterministic closure; a machine's perlocution is deterministic and replayable.

## The engineering reading: locution is the codec

In a running system, locution is environmental decoding. A handler receives an environment shape (an argv vector, a hook payload, an HTTP body, a message from another node), parses and validates it, and yields typed input addressed to cells. That is the locutionary morphism. It is distinct from illocution (the pure positing of evidence) and from perlocution (closure and settlement).

A wrapper that parses input, writes state and fires side effects in one function body conflates three morphisms at three positions. It is malformed by C1, whatever its tests say. The split was first applied to creating a work item on 2026-05-22.

This is the second of Obsmetrologia's five moves, "write the codec to the boundary", made into a component: the codec is the only domain-specific part of any system that runs the engine.

## Consequences for the Group's systems

Required:

1. **Three positions per handler.** Every handler of work, governance or world events has a locution (codec), an illocution (append the utterance with its force) and a perlocution (run the owning section's closure). They are separate functions with separate inputs and outputs.
2. **Codecs are total and pure.** A codec decodes or refuses. It writes nothing. A refusal is a precondition failure, reported as such.
3. **Disagreement is a force, not an error.** When two parties post assert and deny to one cell, the record holds ⊤ and the system shows it. Adjudication holds contestation as a real state of the record.
4. **Rule systems are audited for symmetry.** A governance rule system that is not K₄-closed treats one side of a dispute differently from the other. The defect is computable and is reported with the rules.
