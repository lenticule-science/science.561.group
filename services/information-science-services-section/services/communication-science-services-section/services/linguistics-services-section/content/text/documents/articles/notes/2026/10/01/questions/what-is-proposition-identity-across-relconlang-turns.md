# What is proposition identity across RelConLang turns?

Dialogue compatibility requires more than shared words and less than exact surface identity.

The existing semantic stack can already derive simple role frames and logical forms such as a surface predicate with subject/object arguments. That is enough to distinguish the first blind-dialogue toy contrast after deixis is grounded.

It is not yet enough for a general proposition identity relation:

- first/second-person arguments must be resolved against speech-event context;
- aspect-suffixed verbs are not lemmatized to a declared RelConLang predicate root;
- `obl + case` provenance/locus/kind relations are not represented by the generic semantic-role frame;
- `ccomp` relations are flattened by the current logical-form reader into independent clauses;
- dialogue force and FOUR/evidence must remain outside the proposition nucleus rather than being baked into identity.

The experiment should therefore distinguish at least three relations:

1. **same nucleus** — same canonical predicate and context-resolved participants/embedded content;
2. **refinement/update** — one turn supplies polarity, aspect, evidence, focus, or another value over the same nucleus;
3. **related but distinct** — shared topic/entity without proposition identity.

A question and its positive or negative answer will usually be an update over the same nucleus, not two identical full utterance readings.
