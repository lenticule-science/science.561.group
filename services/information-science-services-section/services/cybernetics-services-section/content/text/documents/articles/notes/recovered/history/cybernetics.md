**Cybernetics** is the discipline-label for the body of work on regulation, control, feedback, and self-organization initiated by Norbert Wiener's *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* (1948).

## Why admitted, not derived

Disciplines are not substrate-categorical objects: the substrate organizes by morphism → generated-category → objects-in-that-category, not by discipline → sub-domain → term. The label "cybernetics" does not have a generator in the substrate's structural sense; rather, the discipline is generated by its constituent concepts, each of which has its own substrate-derived placement.

This $thing is therefore placed under `hacking/library/` at closure-rule-system as an admitted reference label — closure-rule-system is the substrate-position closest to the discipline's center (cybernetics studies regulation, and closure-rule-systems are the substrate's account of regulators). The placement records the discipline-label without claiming it is substrate-categorical.

## Historical attribution

- **Wiener** (1948): coined the modern term from Greek *kybernētēs* (steersman); the founding text.
- **Macy Conferences** (1946-1953): Wiener, von Neumann, Bateson, Mead, McCulloch — interdisciplinary formation.
- **Bateson**: carried cybernetic thinking into anthropology, psychology, ecology.
- **von Foerster**: second-order cybernetics — the observer in the system.
- **Maturana-Varela**: autopoiesis — self-producing living systems.
- **Ashby**: requisite-variety, ultrastability — formal regulation theory.
- **Beer**: viable-system-model — organizational cybernetics.
- **Argyris-Schön**: single-loop / double-loop learning.
