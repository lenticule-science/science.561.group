Faceted classification is a method of knowledge organization developed by S.R. Ranganathan, first implemented in his Colon Classification (1933). Instead of enumerating every possible subject in a fixed schedule (as Dewey and the Library of Congress systems do), faceted classification analyzes any subject into a small set of fundamental **facets** that combine freely to express compound subjects.

Ranganathan identified five fundamental facets, known by the mnemonic **PMEST**:

- **Personality** --- the distinguishing characteristic, the focal entity of the subject (e.g., rice in agriculture, the liver in medicine)
- **Matter** --- the material, substance, or property involved (e.g., nitrogen in soil science, glass in architecture)
- **Energy** --- the action, process, or operation (e.g., cultivation, surgery, combustion)
- **Space** --- the geographic location or spatial context
- **Time** --- the temporal period or chronological aspect

A subject like "the nitrogen cycle in tropical soils during the monsoon season" decomposes into Personality (soil), Matter (nitrogen), Energy (cycling), Space (tropics), and Time (monsoon season). The colon (`:`) in the Colon Classification serves as the notation device joining facet values into a composite class number.

Facets are orthogonal to domains. The same facet structure applies whether classifying agriculture, medicine, or engineering. This is the key theoretical contribution: subjects are not atomic units to be listed but composite structures to be analyzed. Any new subject can be classified by analysis into its facet components without revising the classification schedule.

The British Classification Research Group extended Ranganathan's work, refining facet analysis into a general method for constructing special classifications and thesauri. Faceted classification provides the internal structure within each [integrative level](integrative-levels.md) --- levels supply the vertical ordering of domains, facets supply the horizontal decomposition within domains.

## Earlier material

*Faceted-classification* is the method of knowledge organization developed by S. R. Ranganathan in his Colon Classification (1933) and elaborated by Ranganathan, the British Classification Research Group, and B. C. Vickery through the mid-20th century. Instead of enumerating every possible subject in a fixed schedule (as Dewey and the Library of Congress systems do), faceted-classification analyzes any subject into a small set of fundamental **facets** that combine freely to express compound subjects.

The load-bearing move is **compositional analysis**: subjects are not atomic units to be listed but composite structures to be decomposed into orthogonal dimensions. Any new subject can be classified by analysis into its facet components without revising the classification schedule.

Facets are **orthogonal to domains**: the same facet structure applies whether classifying agriculture, medicine, or engineering. This is the central theoretical contribution. The facet vocabulary is universal; the facet *fillers* are domain-specific.
