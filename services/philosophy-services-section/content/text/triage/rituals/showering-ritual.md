## Materials Required
### Necessary
1. **Mirror**: A small handheld mirror to facilitate self-reflection and truthfulness.
2. **White Quartz Crystal**: Sourced from Crooked Creek, representing clarity and connection to the land.
3. **Blue Jay Feather**: Symbolizing communication, adaptability, and a message from the spirit world.
4. **Birch Bark**: For inscribing intentions, embodying durability and significance within Anishinaabe culture.
5. **Writing Utensil**: Pen, pencil, or traditional ink to write on birch bark.
6. **Soap**: To cleanse physically as well as symbolically wash away negative energies or memories.
7. **Hair Oil (if applicable)**: To nourish new growth in hair—symbolic of nurturing resilience against harmful influences.
8. **Brush or Comb (if applicable)**: For detangling hair—representative of unraveling confusion and aligning with clarity.
### Supporting
1. **Audio Recording/Playlist**: Traditional Anishinaabe music or natural soundscapes that resonate with Terran's values for auditory engagement during the ritual.
## Pronunciation Guide
1. **Debwewin** (Honesty): Pronounced as "day-bway-win"
2. **Nibwaakaawin** (Wisdom): Pronounced as "nib-waa-kah-win"
3. **Aakode'ewin** (Bravery/Courage): Pronounced as "aa-ko-day-win"
## Artifact Meanings

1. **Mirror**: The mirror represents self-examination and truthfulness. It is a tool for looking inward, reflecting on one’s true self without illusion or deception.

2. **White Quartz Crystal from Crooked Creek**: This crystal symbolizes clarity, purity of intention, and connection to the land—specifically Crooked Creek where it was found. It serves as a reminder of the enduring presence of nature and its role in guiding us toward honesty (Debwewin).

3. **Blue Jay Feather**: The blue jay feather signifies communication and adaptability. In many Indigenous cultures, feathers are also seen as messages from the spirit world; this particular feather marks an important transition in Terran’s life when they moved in.

4. **Birch Bark with Written Intention**: Birch bark is traditionally used for writing due to its durability and significance within Anishinaabe culture—it carries words across generations. Writing intentions on birch bark solidifies them into physical form, creating a tangible commitment to those intentions.

## Ritual Overview

1. **Gather Ritual Items**: Collect a mirror, white quartz crystal from Crooked Creek, blue jay feather, and birch bark.

2. **Set Intentions**: Write your intentions on the birch bark, focusing on cleansing and truth.

3. **Enter Shower Space**: Carry the prepared items with you as you enter the shower space.

4. **Perform Cleansing Actions**:
   - Wash with soap to release past negative energies.
   - Detangle hair (if applicable), visualizing clarity emerging from confusion.
   - Apply oil to nourish growth in spirit and resilience.

5. **Engage with Sound & Touch**:
   - Play familiar songs or sounds; immerse in water's touch affirming connection to life.

6. **Recite Mantra Affirmation**: Repeat "With each drop I cleanse my spirit; I walk in truth guided by ancestral wisdom" during actions.

7. **Express Gratitude**: Acknowledge gratitude for water and support systems after completing the ritual actions.

8. **Reflect Post-Ritual**:
   - Dry off and find a quiet space for reflection on insights gained from the ritual experience.

Finally, take the birch bark and inscribe your intention upon it—let this act be a physical manifestation of your commitment to walk in honesty (Debwewin), wisdom (Nibwaakaawin), and bravery (Aakode'ewin)."

Let this birch bark be both witness and ally as you honor Debwewin (Honesty) by shedding what no longer serves you and embracing the wisdom and bravery within."
