# **The Erotic Arc of Ontogenric Kyriarchy: From Anticipation to Possession**

The regime I have called **ontogenric kyriarchism** does not govern only through cognition or law. It enforces its world not merely through knowledge, but through **desire**—through the cultivation of patterned affective, somatic, and libidinal orientations that prepare the subject to long for coherence, to expect resolution, and to feel entitled to closure. These desires are not incidental. They are _themselves_ the formal infrastructure of ontogenric domination.

In this essay, I describe the **erotic arc** of ontogenric kyriarchism: a recursive formation in which anticipation is cultivated, proto-possession is induced, and violent securing of closure becomes both affectively justified and morally necessary. This is the arc of Western genre as worlding.

Its foundations are philosophical. Its mechanisms are somatic. Its outcomes are material and global.

---

## I. _Archê_ and the Genesis of Anticipation

At the root of this arc is a concept: **archê** (ἀρχή)—the principle of origin, foundation, and command. In early Greek metaphysics, _archê_ referred to that which came first and governed what followed. It was not just the beginning, but the _ordering source_—a metaphysical anchor that rendered the cosmos intelligible by locating its legitimacy in its origin.

But _archê_ did not operate alone. Its power to order experience required the elaboration of **a grammar of being**, formed over time through allied concepts: _telos_ (purpose, end), _logos_ (reason, structure), _dynamis_ (potential), _eidos_ (form), and _physis_ (nature). Together, these terms encoded a **narrative logic**: what is, is so because it began in a proper way, tends toward its destined form, and can be known through rational discovery of this progression.

This was not simply a story about the world. It was a **somatic training regime**. To be a knowing subject was to inhabit the tension between origin and end—to live in anticipation of coherence. One was to feel that things _ought_ to make sense, because they _came from sense_. The subject of _archê_ is the subject trained to anticipate the arc.

Anticipation, here, is not just hope. It is **ontological posture**—a bodily orientation toward what will resolve, what must resolve, because it has already been formatted into a knowable arc.

This is the seed of the ontogenric erotic.

---

## II. Libidinal Investment: Anticipation Becomes Possession

Once the arc is expected, it becomes not only anticipated but _invested in_. The subject begins to feel that the future coherence of the world is not only probable but **owed**—that its legibility is _theirs to receive_. This marks the transition from anticipation to what I call **proto-possession**.

Proto-possession is not the ownership of content but the **ownership of form**. It is the felt entitlement to resolution, the subtle but totalizing belief that rupture must arc, that ambiguity must yield to knowledge, that incoherence is a temporary deviation awaiting correction.

This libidinal economy is cultivated everywhere:

- In education, where the student learns that confusion will become clarity.
    
- In medicine, where the patient waits for diagnosis to explain suffering.
    
- In religion, where tribulation prefigures redemption.
    
- In narrative media, where every tension is narrativized as a plot.
    

Across all these domains, **genre itself becomes the object of desire**. What is loved is not what happens, but that it happens in a way that _makes sense_—that conforms to the expectations seeded by _archê_ and reinforced through recursive worlding.

This desire is not innocent. It is **formative of violence**.

---

## III. The Violence of Closure: Securing the Arc

When proto-possession is frustrated—when the world does not resolve as expected, when ambiguity lingers, when genre fails—the result is not neutrality. It is **crisis**. The anticipatory subject, denied coherence, experiences violation. Their arc has been broken.

This is the final stage of the erotic arc: **justified violence in the name of resolution**.

Ontogenric kyriarchy frames this violence not as excess but as _care_, _stability_, _security_, _truth_. This is the violence of:

- Diagnosing the unclassifiable.
    
- Silencing the incoherent.
    
- Erasing the unarcable.
    
- Correcting the non-linear.
    

This violence is not always physical. More often, it is affective, epistemic, spatial, legal, and institutional. But it is always justified through the **genre logic of repair**: the belief that the rupture of meaning must be resolved, and that resolution is good.

Here, the libidinal structure becomes a **moral economy**. Desire for coherence becomes a duty to enforce it. The body trained to anticipate is now the hand that disciplines. The subject of _archê_ becomes the agent of ontogenric stabilization.

---

## IV. Genre as Erotic Infrastructure

This arc—anticipation → proto-possession → closure—is not abstract. It is **infrastructural**.

It structures:

- **State violence** (against protest, migration, non-normative life),
    
- **Medical coercion** (against intersex bodies, mad subjects, “noncompliant” patients),
    
- **Narrative constraints** (where queer, disabled, colonized lives must “make sense”),
    
- **Interpersonal betrayal** (when loved ones fail to arc properly into meaning),
    
- **Crisis governance** (where the state demands obedience in the name of “resolution”).
    

Each instance is not only an event but a **performance of genre fidelity**. The world is being brought back into form. And this enforcement is experienced, by those who demand it, as both right and good—because it _feels_ like the completion of a libidinal contract.

This is the power of ontogenric kyriarchy: it does not force closure. It **makes closure feel like love**.
