- To claim that *something exists* necessarily claims *differentiated existence*:
	- Any claim of existing identifies some thing as distinct from another:
		- this and not that
		- here and not there
		- similarity to some, difference from other
	- Existence as the domain of existing must claim there is non-existing.
	- Without something not existing, the claim is vacuous.
- Thus, the [[act]] of [[exist|existing]] is having the [[condition]] of [[differentiated existence]].
	- Formalized:
		- `for any e: e ≠ ¬e`
- To claim differentiated existence necessarily claims structure
	- the condition of differentiated existence requires mutual relation:
		- `e` is conditioned as different from `¬e`
		- `¬e` is conditioned as different from e
	- This relation provides relational postioning:
		- `e` and `¬e` are positioned relative to each other.
