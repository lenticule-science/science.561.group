- [[define]]:: [[system/conditions/self-relation]] is the minimal [[condition]] induced by [[reflecting]], wherein a [[relational structure]] is positioned as both the source and target of its own relation. It holds open the dynamic in which a [[triage/thing]] relates to itself, sustaining the coherence of reflexive operations within the [[reflexive relational system]].

## Derivation

- [[reflecting]] performs the [[act]] of relating a [[structure]] to itself.

- This act is not trivial: it introduces a **qualitative shift** from standard relating (where source and target are distinct) to a **self-binding** dynamic.

- The possibility of self-relation is **not optional** once reflecting is formalized:
    - Any [[relational structure]] within a reflexive system must be **capable of being its own target**;
    - This ensures that reflexivity is **fully internalized** and not merely simulated by external pairing.

- To sustain this, a **minimal condition** must be induced:
    - [[system/conditions/self-relation]]: the holding-open of positional identity between a structure and itself within the act of relating.

- Critically, [[system/conditions/self-relation]] ensures:
    - That each reflexive act is **coherent**—the structure does not collapse into triviality despite self-reference;
    - That the system can maintain **distinct reflexive depths** while preserving ontological homogeneity.

- [[system/conditions/self-relation]] is **irreducible**:
    - It cannot be decomposed into simpler relational conditions without losing the reflexive property;
    - It is the **necessary connective tissue** for any recursive reflexive sequence to sustain itself.

## Immediate Implications

- With [[system/conditions/self-relation]] induced:
    - Every [[reflecting]] operation is **anchored** by this condition;
    - The reflexive system acquires the minimal **stability condition** required for deeper recursive architectures.

- [[system/conditions/self-relation]] thus acts as the **base condition** for all further developments in reflexive depth, recursivity, and higher-order relational structures.
