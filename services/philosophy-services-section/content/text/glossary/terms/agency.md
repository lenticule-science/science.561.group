**Agency** in the [relationality](../_index.md) framework is the capacity to enact [relating](relating.md) — to perform [witnessings](../../../../services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/witnessing.md) that contribute to [settlement](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/settlement.md). An agent is whatever is positioned to enact relating: a person, an institution, a sensor, a computational process, a community, a legal entity. What makes something an agent is not what it is *made of* but what it is *positioned to do* within the system of relations.

The relationality reading of agency is *deflationary*: it does not posit a substantive self behind the acts. The agent is the stable configuration that performs the relating, not a substrate that pre-exists it. Agency, like the rest of the framework's categories, is constituted by its role in the relational structure — not added to that structure as a separate metaphysical fact.

## Agency without substantive self

Substance-metaphysical readings of agency posit an *agent-self* that exists prior to and independently of any actions: a pre-relational thing that then enters into relations by acting. The framework refuses this substrate. The agent IS the configuration that acts; remove the acting (or the configuration that acts) and there is no residual agent.

This is not a denial of agency. The framework recognizes agents as fully real — agents do things, are accountable for what they do, can be addressed and can address others. What the framework refuses is the substance-behind-acts story. The agent's reality is the reality of the acting configuration, not the reality of an actor-substrate that the acting expresses.

The same move that the framework makes for entities and relations applies here: agency is constituted by relating, not added to it.

## Agency is structural

What makes a particular configuration an agent depends on the structure it occupies, not on what it is made of. A computational system that performs witnessings is, in the framework's sense, an agent; a chemical reaction that does not perform witnessings is not. The criterion is functional and structural: agency is the role of *enacting relating*, regardless of the substrate that fills the role.

This structural reading dissolves the boundary between human agents, institutional agents, computational agents, and any other kinds of configurations that perform relating. The framework treats all of them as agents in the same structural sense; differences between them are differences in the kinds of witnessings they can perform, the kinds of recognitions they participate in, and the kinds of accountability structures they sit within — not differences in whether they are agents at all.

## Agency is distributed

A single relating may be enacted by configurations that span multiple individuals, institutions, and instruments. A scientific discovery may be performed by a team plus a research apparatus plus a community of peer-reviewers; a court ruling may be performed by a judge plus a legal tradition plus an enforcement mechanism. The framework does not require agency to be localized to a single point.

This is consistent with the deflationary reading: if the agent is not a substrate but a configuration, then any configuration that performs the relating is the agent of that relating. Distributed configurations can be agents jointly without requiring a unified agent-self that all the constituent parts somehow contain.

## Agency and accountability

Agency is structurally connected to accountability — the capacity to be held responsible for one's acts within a normative system. An agent's accountability is the structural fact that the system can address its acts back to it: it is positioned to be the bearer of the consequences of its relating. The framework's [duty](../../../../sociology/theories/work/theories/governance/terms/duty.md) and [discharge](../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/governance-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/discharge.md) terms develop this connection in detail.

The deflationary reading of agency does not weaken accountability. Even without a substantive self behind the acts, the configuration that acts is still the configuration that the consequences attach to. Holding an agent accountable does not require positing a deeper self that the holding addresses; it requires only that the configuration can be addressed and the addressing can have effect.

*The role concept for the capacity of actors to act otherwise — to produce effects not fully determined by structural position, normative pressure, or causal antecedent. Agency is what is at stake whenever sociology asks whether the actor "could have done otherwise."*

Agency is not the same as [action](../../../../services/relationality-services-section/content/text/triage/action.md). Action is what is done; agency is the capacity for that doing to be other than it is. The concept matters because every account of social life that takes structure seriously must answer: where is the room for change? An account in which actors are fully determined by structure cannot explain how structures change.

Schools differ on how much agency to credit. Strong-structuralist accounts (orthodox structural-functionalism, some structural-Marxism) credit very little: agency is the local enactment of structurally given roles. Voluntarist accounts (rational-choice theory, methodological individualism) credit a great deal: agents choose under constraint, but the choosing is theirs. Most contemporary accounts sit in between. Bourdieu's *regulated improvisation* — the [habitus](../../../../services/relationality-services-section/content/text/triage/habitus.md) generates appropriate action without conscious calculation, but is not deterministic — is one influential formulation. Sewell's "agents are empowered to act with and against others by structures" treats agency as itself structurally distributed: not a uniform human capacity but unequally available depending on position.

Black-feminist and decolonial sociology have insisted that the agency question is not abstract: who is recognized as having agency, and whose acts are read as expressions of structure ("they could not have done otherwise, given who they are") is itself a political assignment. The denial of agency is one of the operations of [power](../../../../services/relationality-services-section/content/text/triage/power.md).
