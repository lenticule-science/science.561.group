In formal logic, contradiction typically means that two claims cannot be
true together. In dialectical analysis, contradiction also names a
structured tension internal to a form, where the conditions of that
form's reproduction generate forces that destabilize it.

Dialectical contradiction is not a permission to ignore rigor. It is a
methodological signal: identify how incompatibility is produced,
mediated, and historically transformed.
