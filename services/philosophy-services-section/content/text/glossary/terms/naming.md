***Naming*** is the [act](../../../../services/relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/tekodynamics-concepts-act-act.md) of [designating](designating.md) a [name](../../../../services/logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/name.md) for a [thing](thing.md).

- "Names are not things, but signs of things. The name is not the object itself, but a mark by which we are enabled to talk or think about the object."

## Naming by what it is

A name says what its bearer is or what it produces. It does not say how the work felt or which pass made it.

- A thing with structure is named by the thing, as a noun: `nucleus`, `name`.
- Work that produces an artifact or a changed state is named by that output, or by a verb and its object: `reformed-unit`, `write-spec-file`.
- Work that yields a judgment is named by the kind of judgment: `critique`, `claim-classification`.
- A name that is ambiguous in its namespace takes the smallest qualifier that makes it unique.

Some names are refused: names of aspiration (`improve-`, `enhance-`, `sharpen-`), names of a step in some process (`run-`, `apply-`, `check-`), and numbered names (`step-3`, `pass-2`). A refused name is reframed from what the work produces.

## Naming a mathematical term

First classify what is being named, then choose a head word for its class, then add modifiers.

| Class | Test | Head word |
|---|---|---|
| Object | It has parts that can be pointed at and examined. | a noun: the most specific standard term that fits |
| Quality | It is a condition that holds or fails of something else. | an adjective, or its noun in `-ness` or `-ity`: `completeness`, `stability`, `idempotence` |
| Relation | It says how two or more things stand to one another. | a bare verb: `commute`, `restrict`, `correspond` |
| Act | It is defined entirely by what it does to its input. | a gerund: `settling`, `saturating`, `normalizing` |

When Object and another class both apply, name it as an object and carry the other character in a modifier: `saturation nucleus`, not `saturating`.

An object's head word is used only when its defining condition holds:

- [nucleus](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/nucleus.md): an inflationary, idempotent, meet-preserving endomorphism of a Heyting algebra or frame ($a \leq j(a)$, $j(j(a)) = j(a)$, $j(a \wedge b) = j(a) \wedge j(b)$), and no other map;
- [morphism](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md): a map between objects of one kind, defined by the structure it preserves; [functor](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/functor.md) when that structure is a category's; **map** when the map is given by a formula and whatever it preserves is proved separately; **retraction** only for $r$ with $r \circ i = \mathrm{id}$;
- [presheaf](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/presheaf.md) until gluing is established, [sheaf](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sheaf.md) only once the descent condition is stated or proved; [site](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/site.md) for a category with a Grothendieck topology, the input to sheafification; [topos](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/topos.md) only for a Grothendieck or elementary topos, never for a site or a presheaf category;
- [category](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/category.md) with no topology in play; **groupoid** when every morphism is invertible; **doctrine** for a Lawvere doctrine such as a [hyperdoctrine](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/hyperdoctrine.md); **comonad** only with its counit and comultiplication;
- **algebra** when operations and equations are the structure; [monoid](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/monoid.md) when it is exactly an associative operation with a unit; **language** when the object is given by a syntax, not by operations;
- **space** for points with a topology or metric; [locale](../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/locale.md) for a frame of opens without assumed points; **hull** for the least object containing a given one and closed under an operation; **moduli space** for a space of structures up to isomorphism;
- **tower** for a diagram $X_0 \to X_1 \to \cdots$ indexed by $\mathbb{N}$ or an ordinal; **sequence** for an indexed family whose limit or colimit is not the point.

Modifiers come before the head, scope before property: the domain the term lives in, then what makes this instance specific. A modifier made from a verb takes its attributive noun form (`saturation`, `transfer`); only an act's head takes `-ing`. A modifier is left out when the head alone is unambiguous across the corpus.

A theorem or conjecture is named for its subject, never with `theorem`, `lemma` or `conjecture` as its head.
