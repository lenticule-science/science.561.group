In [Alfred North Whitehead](../../../../general/people/alfred-north-whitehead.md)'s [process philosophy](../index.md), a society is a [nexus](./nexus.md) of [actual occasions](./actual-occasion.md) that share a common form — a "defining characteristic" — which each member inherits from its predecessors through [prehension](./prehension.md) and reproduces in its own [concrescence](./concrescence.md). Societies are how [process philosophy](../index.md) accounts for the enduring objects of ordinary experience without positing persistent substances.

A chair, a stone, an organism, an electron — none of these is a single [actual occasion](./actual-occasion.md). Each is a society: a structured sequence of occasions in which a common pattern is transmitted from one generation to the next. The chair does not persist as a substance through time; rather, a society of occasions inherits a chair-pattern, and what we call the chair's persistence is the stability of that inheritance. When the pattern ceases to be reproduced — when the chair breaks — the society dissolves, and the constituent occasions contribute their data to new patterns.

Societies vary in complexity. A "corpuscular society" is a linear sequence of occasions inheriting a simple form (an electron trail). A "structured society" involves multiple subordinate societies coordinated by an overarching pattern (an organism). The most complex structured societies — living organisms with a dominant occasion of experience (what Whitehead calls the "presiding occasion") — exhibit novelty, responsiveness, and what at high levels of integration becomes consciousness.

The concept of society is critical for [relational ontology](relational-ontology.md) because it shows that endurance is not a primitive fact but an achievement of relational process. Identity over time requires ongoing relational work — the continuous [prehension](./prehension.md) and reproduction of a defining form. When that work stops, the identity dissolves. This has implications beyond [metaphysics](../../../terms/metaphysics.md): social institutions, cultural traditions, and ecological systems are all societies in Whitehead's sense, sustained by the ongoing reproduction of their defining patterns.
