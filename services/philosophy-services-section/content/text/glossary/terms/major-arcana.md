The **major arcana** are the 22 trump cards in a tarot deck, numbered 0 (The Fool) through 21 (The World). They represent archetypal themes, life passages, and psychological forces that operate at a larger scale than the everyday situations depicted in the [minor arcana](minor-arcana.md).

The major arcana cards include The Magician, The High Priestess, The Empress, The Emperor, The Hierophant, The Lovers, The Chariot, Strength, The Hermit, Wheel of Fortune, Justice, The Hanged Man, Death, Temperance, The Devil, The Tower, The Star, The Moon, The Sun, Judgement, and The World. Each carries a cluster of meanings that readers interpret in the context of the question asked and the card's position in the [spread](../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/services/computer-engineering-services-section/services/computer-systems-engineering-services-section/services/semiotic-computer-system-services-section/services/divination-computer-system-service/services/tarot-services-section/content/glossary/terms/spread.md).

In readings, a major arcana card is typically understood as pointing to a significant force, transition, or theme — something beyond the reader's immediate control or daily routine. A spread dominated by major arcana cards suggests that the situation involves deep currents rather than surface-level choices.

The sequence from The Fool through The World is sometimes read as a journey (the "Fool's journey"), in which each card represents a stage of development or encounter with a fundamental aspect of experience. This narrative reading is one interpretive framework among many; not all readers or traditions use it.
