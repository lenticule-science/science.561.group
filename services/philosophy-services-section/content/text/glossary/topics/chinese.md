# Chinese philosophy: an entry into its distinct traditions

Chinese philosophy includes Confucian, Daoist, Mohist, Legalist, Buddhist and later traditions. They disagree about action, obligation, knowledge, naturalness and political order. **No English pun, literal translation, Chinese graph or modern process metaphor establishes that different traditions or concepts are identical.** A source may supply a philosophical interpretation, whereas an engineering tactic is an operational *analogy* with a separately tested outcome.

## Wu wei: sources, competing readings and non-coercive action

**無為 / 无为 (wúwéi)** joins 無/无 (wú, absence/not-having) and 為/为 (wéi, acting/doing in this usage). In the *Laozi*, the relevant cluster is **dao** (道, way), **de** (德), **ziran** (自然, self-so/naturalness) and **wuwei**. Do not interpret 無為 as an unconditional command for zero labor, political indifference, refusing care, or indiscriminate optimization of a scalar action cost. The Stanford Encyclopedia's *Laozi* entry explains variants including nonaction, non-coercive action and effortless action, while warning that wuwei concerns the actor's orientation and not a mechanically applicable list of things never to do.

One interpretive family stresses naturalness and simplicity; another emphasizes cosmological qi. Wang Bi's reading of *Laozi* 37 connects wuwei with following ziran; the history of commentaries matters. *Zhuangzi*'s Cook Ding story describes fitting conduct to seams and openings instead of forced cutting, but the philosophical point cannot be reduced to a claim that craft training or path planning is Daoism. In particular, the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy cautions against treating Zhuangzian wuwei as a merely acquired technique. **Working operational analogy:** investigate whether respecting existing causal affordances, changing conditions instead of pushing agents, or withdrawing an obstructive intervention produces better warranted outcomes. This is a testable hypothesis of an office, not a translation of an ancient doctrine into an algorithm.

Sources: [Stanford Encyclopedia: Laozi](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/laozi/) and [archived detailed discussion](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2017/entries/laozi/); [Stanford Encyclopedia: Neo-Daoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/neo-daoism/); [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Daoist Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/daoismdaoist-philosophy/).

## Distinguish homophonic signs before making claims

- **圍棋 / 围棋 (wéiqí)**, the Go board game, uses 圍/围 (wéi, surround/enclose) and 棋 (qí, board game/chess piece). The syllable wéi in this word has **a different graph and sense** from 為/为 in wúwéi. Japanese *igo* and Korean *baduk* are names for the same game tradition, without establishing a single universal competition ruleset.
- **武俠 / 武侠 (wǔxiá)** consists of 武 (wǔ, martial) and 俠/侠 (xiá, knight-errant/chivalric hero). This **wǔ** is not 無/无 (wú) in wúwéi. Genre history, martial traditions and the justice/obligation of the wandering hero matter beyond combat visuals.
- **仙俠 / 仙侠 (xiānxiá)** is a related but non-identical fantasy tradition in which immortality/cultivation motifs may be central; **玄幻 (xuánhuàn)** is another broader fantasy label. Boundaries vary among authors and contemporary readers. A proposed word like **wuxian** must retain an unresolved-language status until the exact Chinese characters, context and speaker sense are supplied. Do not manufacture an etymology for a brand pun.
- **Wusday, Weisday, Wuweisday, Weiqiday, Wuxday, Wuweiqi** are 561 Group **English house names**, not attested Chinese lexical compounds or historical Daoist schools. **Weiqisday** is an older Group label variant; the Leader's chosen canonical spelling is **Weiqiday**. Distinguish a name, its etymological inspiration, the research warrant and the action method.

The modern wuxia literature is diverse and transnational, not a single undifferentiated martial system. Yilin Wang's [history of wuxia and its xia/jianghu figures](https://sfwa.org/2021/12/28/what-speculative-fiction-writers-can-learn-from-the-origins-and-evolution-of-the-wuxia-genre/) is a primary contemporary writer/translator perspective that preserves the moral and literary richness lost by translating wuxia merely as 'kung fu fantasy'. Chinese-language primary texts and Sinophone readers remain important independent checks before claims of authenticity.

## What may be borrowed by the 561 Group's Method of Actions?

An operational tactic is a **guarded, revisable inference and action selection**, not a conversion of philosophy into guaranteed economic success. A legitimate mapping requires (1) a defined real-world state and receiver; (2) permitted office authority; (3) competing actions, including delay or nonintervention when lawful; (4) an explicit causal or game-theoretic hypothesis; (5) observable, prospective acceptance and refusal conditions; and (6) a record of what did and did not transfer between domains. Falsification is preferable to decoration.
