# Liberal as Eidic Field

## Abstract

This paper defines liberalism as a recursive ontological structuration system: a field governed by the projection of eidolons—idealized image-forms—through which coherence, legibility, and material formation are enforced. The paper introduces the concept of the **eidic field**, within which all entities, systems, and relations are subjected to a directional ontological force: the **eidogenic vector**. This vector operates not symbolically, but structurally—conditioning what is allowed to appear as real, actionable, or intelligible. Drawing from Plato’s theory of Forms, Aristotle’s teleology, Kant’s transcendental subject, and contemporary critical theorists including Foucault, Butler, and Povinelli, this paper delineates liberalism as an infrastructure of alignment toward eidic coherence across political, affective, spatial, and epistemic domains.

---

## 1. Eidic Structuration and Ontological Fields

In the context of this analysis, an **eidolon** is defined as a projected form or template through which reality is rendered intelligible. Ontoeidics is the study of how these image-forms are projected, stabilized, and recursively enforced across ontological, perceptual, and infrastructural dimensions. An **eidic field** is the condition under which these projections become the governing grammar of coherence. In such a field, beings and systems are only intelligible if they appear as aligned—or alignable—with one or more of the projected eidolons.

The concept of the **eidy** refers to the cohered result of successful alignment: a person, structure, behavior, or condition that stabilizes as a legible instance of being within the field.

---

## 2. The Eidogenic Vector: Force of Alignment

The **eidogenic vector** is defined as the structural force by which beings are drawn into ontological alignment with their projected eidolon. It operates via relational perception: to be seen in relation to a form is to be subjected to the pressure of becoming that form. This process is not mediated through belief or intention but through the activation of ontological directionality. It underlies classification systems, institutional norms, identity formations, and aesthetic regularities.

The vector functions across multiple registers—social, spatial, biological—and is operative wherever coherence is conditioned by alignment. For example, a legal subject becomes legible as such through alignment with normative projections of autonomy and rationality. These norms are not simply social values but eidic constructs enforced through the field.

---

## 3. Theoretical Lineage: Plato, Aristotle, Kant

The historical lineage of the eidic field begins with **Plato’s theory of Forms**, which posits that the material world is real only to the extent that it approximates ideal Forms. This introduced a principle of ontological hierarchy: the real must derive its coherence from alignment with immaterial projection. This model establishes eidolons as ontological regulators.

**Aristotle** modified this ontology by proposing that form inheres within matter as telos. In this model, entities are real not in proportion to their resemblance to an external Form, but in relation to their internal orientation toward completion. This embeds eidic alignment into material development—governing natural, ethical, and civic systems through assumptions of proper function and endpoint.

**Immanuel Kant** reframed the issue as a matter of transcendental preconditions: beings are intelligible only through their conformity to the forms of intuition (space and time) and the categories of the understanding. The subject is not simply governed by alignment with external forms, but by internal structuration that constrains what can appear. The eidogenic vector, in this context, is operative through the structuring conditions of cognition and the epistemic preformation of the real.

These three systems, though distinct, share the premise that **form precedes or governs being**—a premise liberalism inherits and distributes through its institutional and infrastructural systems.

---

## 4. Liberalism as an Eidic System

Liberalism institutionalizes eidic structuration by projecting idealized forms of subjectivity—autonomy, rationality, possessive individualism—and distributing them through law, policy, pedagogy, medicine, and media. The **liberal subject** is not an empirical entity but a projection against which individuals are formatted and assessed. The coherence of any person, claim, or institution is conditioned by its proximity to these eidic standards.

The field formed by liberalism is recursive: its operations are not localized in any single institution but generalized across multiple domains. Systems such as public health, immigration law, standardized education, and narrative representation function to induce and reward alignment. Deviation does not remain external to the field but is often reformatted as recognizable opposition (see Butler’s _Gender Trouble_ for how gender deviance is incorporated into binary schema through performative reiteration). This process is consistent with what Elizabeth Povinelli describes as the endurance of non-dominant social projects through conditions that both tolerate and constrain them (_Economies of Abandonment_).

Michel Foucault’s account of power as productive rather than repressive (_Discipline and Punish_) provides an early vocabulary for this mechanism. The eidogenic vector, however, operates not only at the level of the body or the population but at the level of **ontological qualification**: what may or may not count as a being, a relation, or an event is conditioned by its capacity to align with the eidic grammar.

---

## 5. Eidic Capture and Recursive Field Maintenance

Entities or practices that diverge from projected forms are not always excluded. In many cases, the eidic field captures deviation by reformatting it into a recognizable, and thus governable, eidy. For instance, racial or sexual deviance is often rendered intelligible through preconstructed identity categories that reproduce the field’s governing logic of classification. This is not resolution but containment.

The recursive structure of the field ensures that its own grammar is continuously reinforced through recognition, misrecognition, and correction. Fields become self-producing: institutions format subjects; subjects reproduce institutions; both are aligned toward eidic projections.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Liberalism operates not solely as a political or moral order but as an **eidic field**: a recursive system that structures being, intelligibility, and governance through the projection and enforcement of eidic alignment. The **eidogenic vector** is the underlying force that conditions the movement of bodies, identities, and systems toward those projections.

This framework accounts for liberalism’s consistency across heterogeneous domains—its ability to govern not only through ideology or policy but through the ontological formatting of life itself. Understanding liberalism as an eidic field allows for a precise description of its world-producing capacities and constraints.
