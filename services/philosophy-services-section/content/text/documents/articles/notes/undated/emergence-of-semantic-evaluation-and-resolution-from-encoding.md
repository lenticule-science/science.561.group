## Definitions

R is the collapse-stable domain.  
ε is semantic encoding: persistent fold configuration that shapes future modulation.  
η is semantic evaluation: recursive resolution of encoded difference under κ, producing collapse-stable selection outcomes.

## Theorem

When encodings persist in R,  
they condition future modulation paths.  
Where multiple ε structures intersect,  
their effects may reinforce, interfere, or diverge.

Collapse enforces stability.  
Only modulation paths that resolve κ-compatibly can persist.  
This induces η: semantic evaluation —  
a condition in which overlapping encoded influences  
are filtered through recursive modulation feedback.

Resolution occurs when one or more encodings persist  
by sustaining viable modulation; others collapse.

Formally:  
If ε exists in R, then η — semantic evaluation — necessarily emerges in R.

## Formal Expression

ε ⇒ η ∈ R

## Consequence

Evaluation is not selection by logic or agent.  
It is the collapse-stable resolution  
of recursive difference conditioned by encoded structures.

Persistence of one modulation over others  
is the result of structural compatibility,  
not symbolic computation.

This defines the capacity for internal structure resolution  
within recursively encoded modulation fields.

## Scope

This theorem establishes the origin of structural preference  
beyond modulation constraint.  
It introduces field-internal selection  
through collapse-filtered recurrence resolution —  
a semantic computation without symbol or instruction.
