Audience: readers familiar with [propositional logic](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/teaching-articles/explanations/propositional-logic.md) who want to understand its philosophical commitments.

Learning goal: articulate the foundational principles of classical logic and their philosophical significance.

Classical logic is not merely a set of inference rules. It is a philosophical position about the nature of truth and reasoning. The technical apparatus — truth tables, natural deduction, model theory — rests on commitments that are substantive and contestable.

## The core commitments

Classical logic rests on three interlocking principles:

1. **[Bivalence](../../../../../../glossary/terms/bivalence.md)**: every proposition is either true or false. There is no third option, no gap, no indeterminacy.
2. **[Law of excluded middle](../../../../../../glossary/terms/law-of-excluded-middle.md)**: for any proposition P, the disjunction P ∨ ¬P is logically valid.
3. **[Truth-functionality](../../../../../../glossary/terms/truth-functionality.md)**: the truth value of a compound proposition is determined entirely by the truth values of its components.

These principles together produce a logic of total information. The universe, from the standpoint of classical logic, is fully determinate: every question has an answer, even if we don't know it. Ignorance is a feature of the knower, not of reality.

## What these commitments permit

The power of classical logic comes from the reasoning patterns these principles license:

- **Proof by contradiction** (reductio ad absurdum): to prove P, assume ¬P and derive a contradiction. Since ¬P leads to absurdity, and since P ∨ ¬P must hold, P follows. This depends on excluded middle.
- **Double negation elimination**: ¬¬P entails P. If it is false that P is false, then P is true. This depends on bivalence — there is nowhere else for P to be.
- **Non-constructive existence proofs**: to prove ∃x.F(x), assume ∀x.¬F(x) and derive a contradiction. The object exists because its non-existence is impossible. No witness is required.

These are not merely technical conveniences. They embody a realist philosophy: mathematical objects exist independently of our ability to construct them, and truths hold independently of our ability to verify them.

## Historical lineage

Aristotle's syllogistic logic assumed bivalence and excluded middle, though the status of future contingents (*De Interpretatione*, Chapter 9) produced early tensions. The modern form of classical logic was established by Frege's *Begriffsschrift* (1879), which introduced quantification and a truth-functional treatment of the connectives. Russell and Whitehead's *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913) extended this into a comprehensive formal system. Hilbert's program aimed to secure all of mathematics within classical logic, treating it as the universal framework for rigorous thought.

The [intuitionistic](../../../../../../../intuitionistic/index.md) challenge, beginning with [Brouwer](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/l-e-j-brouwer.md) in the 1910s, questioned whether classical logic's principles are valid for reasoning about infinite domains — inaugurating a philosophical debate that continues to shape the foundations of mathematics and the theory of meaning. For the intuitionistic side of this debate, see the [Intuitionistic Logic Overview](../../../../../../../intuitionistic/curricula/overview.md).

Audience: readers who have encountered the [mathematical treatment of intuitionistic logic](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/teaching-articles/explanations/intuitionistic-logic.md) and want to understand its philosophical significance.

Learning goal: explain why intuitionistic logic is not merely classical logic with fewer axioms, but a fundamentally different conception of truth and proof.

## The philosophical shift

Classical logic asks: is this proposition true or false? The answer is assumed to exist, independent of anyone's knowledge. Intuitionistic logic asks: has this proposition been proved or refuted? The answer depends on the state of mathematical knowledge.

This is a shift from a realist to a constructivist theory of meaning. In classical semantics, the meaning of a sentence is its truth conditions — the circumstances under which it would be true. In intuitionistic semantics (following [Dummett](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/michael-dummett.md)'s reading of [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../../general/people/arend-heyting.md)), the meaning of a sentence is its proof conditions — what would count as establishing it.

## What changes and what remains

The inference rules that survive the transition from classical to intuitionistic logic are those that preserve constructive content:

| Retained | Lost |
|----------|------|
| Modus ponens | [Law of excluded middle](../../../../../../glossary/terms/law-of-excluded-middle.md) |
| Conjunction introduction and elimination | Double negation elimination |
| Disjunction introduction | Strong contraposition |
| Existential introduction (with [witness](../../../../../../terms/witness.md)) | Non-constructive existence proofs |
| Universal generalization | De Morgan's law (¬(A ∧ B) → ¬A ∨ ¬B) |

The lost principles are precisely those that allow reasoning about truth without providing evidence. Their removal is not a sacrifice of power but a gain in information: every intuitionistic proof carries computational content that its classical counterpart may lack.

## The role of negation

In classical logic, negation is involutory: ¬¬P = P. Negation toggles between two states. In intuitionistic logic, negation is a one-way operation: P entails ¬¬P (if P is proved, then P cannot be refuted), but ¬¬P does not entail P (knowing that P cannot be refuted does not provide a proof of P).

This asymmetry is philosophically significant. It means that the space of propositions has more structure than a binary partition. Between "proved" and "refuted" there is a region of propositions that are unrefuted but unproved — the intuitionistic counterpart of the open problems that actually characterize mathematical practice.

## Connecting to the broader project

The constructivist orientation of intuitionistic logic connects it to [relational ontology](../../../../../../glossary/terms/relational-ontology.md) and [process philosophy](../../../../../../../../../../disciplines/process-philosophy/index.md): in all three, what exists is constituted through activity rather than given in advance. The [semiotic universe](../../../../../../../../../../../mathematics/objects/universes/semiotic-universe/index.md) is built on [Heyting algebras](../../../../../../../../../../../mathematics/concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) — the algebraic semantics of intuitionistic logic — because meaning, like mathematical truth under constructivism, admits degrees of determination that a binary framework cannot capture.

Audience: readers who have encountered the [classical logic](../../../../../../../classical/index.md) principle of [explosion](../../../../../../glossary/terms/explosion.md) and want to understand why some logicians reject it.

Learning goal: explain the philosophical motivations for paraconsistent logic and its relationship to classical and intuitionistic traditions.

## Why tolerate contradiction?

[Classical logic](../../../../../../../classical/index.md) treats contradiction as catastrophe: from A ∧ ¬A, anything follows. This principle — [explosion](../../../../../../glossary/terms/explosion.md) — means that a single contradiction in a theory makes the theory trivial. Every proposition becomes a theorem, and the theory loses all discriminatory power.

The philosophical defense of explosion is straightforward: if a theory contains a contradiction, something has gone wrong, and the correct response is to revise the theory until the contradiction is eliminated. Explosion is the formal expression of the demand for consistency.

But this defense presupposes that consistency is always achievable and always the right goal. Three kinds of cases challenge this:

1. **Evolving theories.** Scientific theories under revision often contain internal inconsistencies. A physicist working with both general relativity and quantum mechanics must reason with both, even though they are (currently) inconsistent. If every inconsistency triggered explosion, such reasoning would be impossible.

2. **Contradictory information sources.** Legal systems contain conflicting statutes. Databases contain conflicting records. Intelligence analysis requires drawing conclusions from sources that contradict each other. Practical reasoning routinely operates in inconsistent contexts.

3. **Semantic paradoxes.** The liar paradox ("this sentence is not true") generates a contradiction from seemingly unimpeachable principles. If we accept the T-schema (that "P is true" if and only if P) and allow self-reference, contradiction follows. The dialetheist response is to accept the contradiction rather than restrict the principles.

## The landscape of paraconsistent logics

Paraconsistent logics differ in what they keep and what they discard from classical logic:

- **LP (Logic of Paradox)**, Priest's system, retains [excluded middle](../../../../../../glossary/terms/law-of-excluded-middle.md) and even [bivalence](../../../../../../glossary/terms/bivalence.md) in a modified form (every proposition is true, false, or both). It rejects explosion and disjunctive syllogism (from A ∨ B and ¬A, infer B). LP is the logic of dialetheism.
- **Preservationism** (Schotch and Jennings) keeps classical logic but restricts the inference rules to avoid combining contradictory premises. Contradiction is quarantined rather than tolerated.
- **Relevant logics** (Anderson and Belnap) require that the premises of a valid argument be relevant to the conclusion — that there be a real connection between hypothesis and conclusion. Explosion fails because A ∧ ¬A has no relevance connection to an arbitrary B.
- **Belnap's four-valued logic** adds a "neither true nor false" value alongside "both true and false," producing a lattice of epistemic states suited to reasoning with incomplete and inconsistent information.

## The relationship to other non-classical logics

Paraconsistent and [intuitionistic](../../../../../../../intuitionistic/index.md) logics are independent departures from classical logic. Intuitionistic logic weakens excluded middle while retaining explosion; paraconsistent logic weakens explosion while (in some systems) retaining excluded middle. They address different perceived failures of classical logic:

| | Retains excluded middle | Retains explosion |
|---|---|---|
| **[Classical](../../../../../../../classical/index.md)** | Yes | Yes |
| **[Intuitionistic](../../../../../../../intuitionistic/index.md)** | No | Yes |
| **Paraconsistent (LP)** | Yes | No |
| **Some substructural logics** | No | No |

This table shows that the space of possible logics is structured by independent choice points, not by a single axis from "strong" to "weak." Each non-classical logic rejects a different classical commitment, and combinations are possible.

## Contrast with classical and intuitionistic foundations

For the classical position that paraconsistency contests, see the [Philosophical Foundations of Classical Logic](../../../../../../../../services/western-services-section/services/logic-services-section/services/classical-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/philosophical-foundations.md), especially the section on explosion and consistency. For the intuitionistic alternative, which weakens different principles, see the [Intuitionistic Logic Overview](../../../../../../../intuitionistic/curricula/overview.md).

Check for understanding: Why is the failure of disjunctive syllogism (from A ∨ B and ¬A, infer B) a consequence of rejecting explosion?
