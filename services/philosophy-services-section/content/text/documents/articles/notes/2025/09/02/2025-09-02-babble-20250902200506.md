Thinking about the [influence](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/influence.md) that [Terry Pratchett](../../../../../../glossary/terms/terry-pratchett.md) has had on [my writing](../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/my-writing.md), specifically my writing style, and how every sentence can probably be written to be a little fun.

And it made me think about how, in [linguistics](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/linguistics.md), there's the idea of [pragmatics](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/pragmatic.md), the parts of speech that make it sound human, or whatever. And it makes me wonder about the idea of "ludimatics" or whatever the proper formation would be: the parts of speech that make it feel fun.
