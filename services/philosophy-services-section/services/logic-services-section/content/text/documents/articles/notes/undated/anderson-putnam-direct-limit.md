# Anderson–Putnam direct limit

For a substitution dynamical system with substitution self-map `ω` on a finite cell complex `Γ_ω` (the **Anderson–Putnam complex**), the dynamical space is the inverse limit

$$\Omega_\omega \;\cong\; \lim_{\leftarrow}\,\Bigl(\, \Gamma_\omega \xleftarrow{\omega} \Gamma_\omega \xleftarrow{\omega} \cdots \,\Bigr),$$

and its **Čech cohomology** is the **direct limit** of the induced cohomology maps:

$$\check{H}^n(\Omega_\omega) \;\cong\; \varinjlim\,\Bigl(\, \check{H}^n(\Gamma_\omega) \xrightarrow{\omega^*} \check{H}^n(\Gamma_\omega) \to \cdots \,\Bigr).$$

(Anderson–Putnam 1998.)

## Original setting: substitution tilings

The construction was developed for **substitution tilings** — non-periodic tilings (like the Penrose tiling) generated by repeated substitution rules. The dynamical space `Ω_ω` parameterizes all the tiling-equivalence classes under local-isomorphism equivalence (mutual local derivability, MLD).

Cohomology classes of `Ω_ω` are the **classifying invariants** of aperiodic tilings up to MLD. Two tilings are MLD-equivalent iff their cohomology classes agree.

## Adoption in predication categories

The substrate **adopts** the Anderson–Putnam construction as one of its **four cohomological frameworks** (framework #1; see `cohomological-framework`).

**Setup**: A substitution-shaped settlement is a settlement whose closure-rule-system has a self-similar substitution structure (e.g., each closure-rule application replaces a cell-pattern with a substituted pattern). Such settlements arise naturally in the aperiodic regime.

**Identification**: The substrate's `aperiodic-regime-cohomology-via-direct-limit` theorem (archive) identifies:

- The bitopological-settling-site cohomology `H^n(T*, F_U)` of an aperiodic substitution settlement,
- with the Anderson–Putnam Čech cohomology `Ȟ^n(Ω_ω)` of the corresponding substitution complex.

So the substrate's cohomological invariants for substitution-shaped aperiodic settlements can be **computed** using Anderson–Putnam machinery — Čech cohomology of the inverse-limit space.

## Computational role

Framework #1 (Anderson–Putnam) is the **computationally working** framework. The substrate's benchmark suite (7 specific substitution-shaped settlements) is computed using Anderson–Putnam tooling — Python implementations in `tools/predication_categories/examples/benchmark_1_fibonacci.py` etc.

This makes framework #1 the **operational anchor** for aperiodic-regime computations. The other three frameworks (Tarski-Laplacian, cellular-sheaf-on-clopen, Lawvere-Laplacian) are theoretically richer but less computationally developed.

## What the direct limit accomplishes

The direct limit `Ȟ^n(Ω_ω) ≅ colim(Ȟ^n(Γ_ω) → ...)` computes the cohomology of the limiting aperiodic structure by **iterating cohomology of the finite approximations** `Γ_ω`.

Each `Γ_ω` is a finite cell complex — its Čech cohomology is finite-dimensional and computable by hand. The maps `ω*: Ȟ^n(Γ_ω) → Ȟ^n(Γ_ω)` are matrices induced by the substitution rule.

So `Ȟ^n(Ω_ω)` is the colimit of a sequence of matrices acting on finite vector spaces — concrete, finitely-presentable, computable.

## Worked example: Fibonacci substitution

The Fibonacci substitution `ω(a) = ab`, `ω(b) = a` produces a Penrose-style aperiodic tiling. Its Anderson–Putnam complex `Γ_ω` has cohomology `Ȟ^0 = Z^2` (rank 2). The substitution matrix on `Ȟ^0` is

$$\omega^* \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The direct limit `Ȟ^0(Ω_ω)` is the colimit `Z^2 → Z^2 → ...` under this matrix — formally `Z[1/φ]^2` where `φ` is the golden ratio (largest eigenvalue of `ω*`).

This is the cohomological data of the Fibonacci substitution settlement. The benchmark suite computes analogous data for 6 other substitution settlements.
