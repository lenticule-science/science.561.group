## Annotation

Introduces the tripartite taxonomy of any utterance act into **locution** (the production of a meaningful utterance), **illocution** (the communicative force — asserting, questioning, commanding, promising, warning, declaring), and **perlocution** (the downstream effect on the listener). Establishes that a natural language utterance is never merely a description of the world — it is also an *act* whose force and effect are analytically separable.

The illocutionary taxonomy distinguishes at minimum: assertives (stating facts or hypotheses), directives (ordering, requesting), commissives (promising, vowing), expressives (thanking), and declaratives (acts that constitute facts by being performed — pronouncing someone married, declaring a session open). Declaratives are structurally distinctive: their truth condition and their performance condition coincide.
