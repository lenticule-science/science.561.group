## What this node is

This node holds the 561 Group's own first-class research: the theory of **predication categories** and the **predicated computer** built on them. It is the deepest layer of the library — the research from which the whole generative organization (relation → predicate → posit → settle) is *derived*, rather than a downstream application of it. Where the [predication](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/predication.md) node names the morphism, this node carries the mathematics of what that morphism is and does.

Its central claim is a reformulation of computation: the substrate is not the addressed memory cell but the **relation**, and computing is not instruction-execution but **settling** — the iteration of closure operators to a fixed point. A Turing tape becomes a relation-indexed store; the head becomes a *wavefront*; the transition function becomes the *settle* operator; halting becomes *quiescence*. From this single move the research derives a substantial body of structure: a four-regime classification of every settling by its closure ordinal (strict, Scott-continuous, transfinite-bounded, aperiodic), the closure machinery as a 2-monad, contradictions organized as an *inconsistency sheaf* whose cohomology is the audit record, two independent orders (of truth and of knowledge) crossing into a four-valued bilattice, and *work* itself read as iterated settling.

## Why it matters

This research is the ground the rest of the library stands on, and it is the corpus's, not borrowed:

- It is **native mathematics**, not a metaphor laid over computing. The four regimes, the closure 2-monad, and the inconsistency sheaf are mathematical objects the substrate's content *is*, not models it resembles.
- It supplies the **derivations** the spine asserts: that order is generated by settling, that the value logics are positing, that a speech act and a four-valued judgement share one bilattice, that work and speech are the same positing read at different bases. The atomic explanations here make those derivations legible from the corpus's own surveys and claims.
- It situates the whole program against **established theory** — Turing, von Neumann, Shannon, Eilenberg, Wiener, Landauer, Searle, Codd, Frege — as structural equivalences, so a reader from any of those traditions can find the substrate in vocabulary they already hold.
