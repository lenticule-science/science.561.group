# The Theory of Predication Categories

**Theorem.** Predication categories are the categorical semantics of bilattice-valued $(s, p, o)$ predication structures. The canonical value domain $\mathbf{FOUR}$ (the Belnap-Dunn bilattice) is the twist product $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$, derivable from the subobject classifier $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ of the computability theory via the double-negation nucleus $j = \neg\neg$. A predication hyperdoctrine is a Lawvere hyperdoctrine $P: \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{MALL}_{\mathbf{FOUR}}$ with fibers valued in the MALL algebra over $\mathbf{FOUR}$. Predication settling is the least fixpoint of a Medvedev-filter closure operator on the hyperdoctrine fibers. Priestley duality identifies the bitopological structure of $\mathbf{FOUR}$ with its dual compact ordered space.

**Proof.**

*The bilattice $\mathbf{FOUR}$ and its categorical origin.* The four-element set $\mathbf{FOUR} = \{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \bot, \top\}$ carries two lattice orders simultaneously: the truth order ($\mathbf{f} \leq_t \bot \leq_t \mathbf{t}$; $\top$ is above all in truth) and the knowledge order ($\bot \leq_k \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f} \leq_k \top$; $\bot$ = no information, $\top$ = contradiction = maximal information). The twist product $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$ (Fitting 1991) produces $\mathbf{FOUR}$ from the two-element Boolean algebra $\mathbf{2}$: elements are pairs $(a, b) \in \mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$ with $a$ = classical truth value and $b = \neg a$, under componentwise order. This is the internal construction: the double-negation map $\rho: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$ defined by $\rho(P) = (\neg\neg P, \neg\neg\neg P)$ sends the subobject classifier of $\mathbf{Eff}$ to $\mathbf{FOUR}$ (proved in theory-of-predicated-computing). The bilattice operations — truth-order join $\sqcup_t$, knowledge-order join $\sqcup_k$, negation $\neg$, conflation $-$ — are all definable from the twist product structure.

*Predication hyperdoctrines.* A predication hyperdoctrine over a base category $\mathcal{C}$ (the category of entities) is a Lawvere hyperdoctrine $P: \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{MALL}_{\mathbf{FOUR}}$, where $\mathbf{MALL}_{\mathbf{FOUR}}$ is the category of MALL algebras valued in $\mathbf{FOUR}$. For each entity $e \in \mathcal{C}$, the fiber $P(e)$ is a MALL algebra of predications about $e$: the multiplicative tensor $\otimes$ composes two predications into a joint witness; the additive conjunction $\&$ represents knowledge of both; the additive disjunction $\oplus$ represents commitment to the more strongly supported; the linear negation $(-)^\perp$ is the conflation $-$ (Fitting: truth-order complement, negating both the assertion and its negation). Reindexing along $f: e \to e'$ in $\mathcal{C}$ gives substitution $f^*: P(e') \to P(e)$ — pulling predications about $e'$ back to predications about $e$. Left adjoints $\exists_f \dashv f^*$ give existential quantification (the witness-producing operation). The Beck-Chevalley condition holds: $\exists_{f \circ g} = \exists_f \circ \exists_g$ (witnessing is compositional). This makes $P$ a bona fide hyperdoctrine in Lawvere's sense (1969), instantiated at the MALL algebra of $\mathbf{FOUR}$.

*Predication settling as least fixpoint.* A closure-rule system on a predicated computer defines a Medvedev-filter nucleus $j_F: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ (from theory-of-predicated-computing). Lifting to the hyperdoctrine: $j_F$ acts fiber-wise on $P(e)$ for each entity $e$, sending each predication to its $j_F$-closure (the weakest predication implied by the rule system given the current state). Predication settling is the iteration of this fiber-wise closure to its least fixpoint. Existence of the fixpoint follows from the Kleene fixed-point theorem, internal to $\mathbf{Eff}$: the chain $P(e) \leq j_F(P(e)) \leq j_F^2(P(e)) \leq \cdots$ stabilizes at the least fixed point $\bigvee_{n} j_F^n(P(e))$, which exists because $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ has all small joins (as the subobject classifier of an elementary topos) and $j_F$ is continuous (preserves directed joins, since it is a nucleus). The settled state is the unique fixpoint of the closure: the least element of $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ closed under all rule applications.

*Priestley duality.* The bilattice $\mathbf{FOUR}$, ordered by the knowledge order $\leq_k$, is a finite distributive lattice. By Priestley duality (1970, 1972), every finite distributive lattice $L$ is isomorphic to the lattice of clopen upsets of a finite ordered topological space $X(L)$ (the Priestley dual). The Priestley dual of $(\mathbf{FOUR}, \leq_k)$ is a four-point compact ordered space with the two truth values $\{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}\}$ as maximal elements (above $\bot$, below $\top$), giving the bitopological structure of $\mathbf{FOUR}$: the Scott topology (open = upward-closed under $\leq_k$) tracks positive information (what is known), and the Lawson topology (compact = Scott-closed ∩ Scott-open) tracks complete observability. This connects predication categories to domain theory (Scott-open = observable predications) and to computable analysis (the Weihrauch-reducibility structure on $\mathcal{K}_2$-computable functions, which extends to the predication-hyperdoctrine fibers via the $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizability of the fiber algebra operations). $\square$
