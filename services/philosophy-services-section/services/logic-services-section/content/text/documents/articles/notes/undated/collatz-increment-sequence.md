# Collatz increment-sequence

A particular [increment-sequence](increment-sequence.md) `U_collatz = (u_τ)_{τ<λ}`. It
is **not** a new kind of object and **not** a new morphism; it is one inhabitant of the
increment-sequence type, fixing the base and the per-step evidence so that the resulting
settling traces a Collatz orbit.

## The base it fixes

- `L = 𝟐` — the two-element Boolean residuated lattice (`⊙ = ⊓`, `a ⇒ b = ¬a ⊔ b`).
- `C` — a single-predicate category with one predicate `reached`.
- `E = ℕ₊` — the positive integers as entities. A cell is a single integer `n` (the
  diagonal use of `reached`; we write `reached(n)` for the entity-pair `(n,n)`).
- `λ` — a settling-bound; for a seed `m` it is the Collatz total stopping time of `m` (finite
  for every seed ever tested — the conjecture is exactly that `λ < ω` always).

## The per-step evidence

Fix a seed `m ∈ ℕ₊`. Define the integer trajectory `n_0 = m`, `n_{τ+1} = T(n_τ)` where

$$T(n) \;=\; \begin{cases} 3n+1 & n \text{ odd} \\ n/2 & n \text{ even.} \end{cases}$$

The increment at step `τ+1` posits exactly the newly-reached cell:

$$u_{τ+1}(\mathrm{reached})(n_{τ+1}) \;=\; t, \qquad u_{τ+1}(\mathrm{reached})(k) = \bot \ (k \neq n_{τ+1}).$$

Each `u_τ` is trivially `C`-natural (one predicate, no nontrivial reindexing). Joined into the
settling state and closed under the identity closure, this produces the
[[collatz-settlement]] — the orbit `(v_τ)` where `v_τ(reached)(k) = t` iff the integer `k` has
been reached by stage `τ`.

## The nucleus-shadow tag on each step

The branch taken at step `τ+1` is recorded as the finite **shadow** of an aperiodic nucleus
(the nuclei themselves act on Heyting-algebra elements, not integers — here they survive only
as the direction of the step):

- `n_τ` odd → `3n+1` is an **expansion** → `transferring`-shadow (Δ, forward-facing).
- `n_τ` even → `n/2` is a **contraction** → `saturating`-shadow (σ, backward-facing).

and the residue `n_τ mod 6` is carried, because mod 6 is where the `commutating` square's
closure survives (see the settlement's theorems).
