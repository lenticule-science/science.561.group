# Saturation respects asserted-set monotonicity

**Claim.** Let $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}$ be the σ-nucleus on the [predication fiber algebra](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md) $A_p$ generated by a [closure-rule system](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/from-predicate-category/teaching/explanations/closure-rule-system.md) $\mathcal{S}$, and let $X \subseteq Y$ be two finite asserted-atom sets in $A^{(0)}_p$ — the stratum-$0$ layer of the [stratified predication fiber](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/objects/stratified-predication-fiber.md) $A^{\flat}_p$. Then:

1. **Monotonicity.** $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y)$.
2. **Dependent-invalidity identity.** Writing $Y \setminus X = \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ for the retracted asserted atoms, the difference of the saturated sets factors through the witness chain:
$$
\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y) \;\setminus\; \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X) \;=\; \bigl\{\,c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y) \;:\; (Y \setminus X) \cap \mathrm{deps}^*(c) \neq \emptyset\,\bigr\},
$$
where $\mathrm{deps}^*(c)$ is the transitive closure of the witness chain — the set of stratum-$0$ atoms reachable from $c$ by following $\mathrm{deps}$ edges.
3. **Witness-determined retraction.** The dependent-invalidity set in (2) is computable from the witness chain alone in time linear in $|\mathrm{deps}^*(c)|$ summed over the candidate cells, *without* re-running the σ-pass.

The conjunction is the math content of "removing an asserted atom retracts exactly the saturated atoms whose witness chain transits the removed atom" — the structural correctness of the substrate's σ-pass dependent-invalidity discipline.

## Why this holds

**Part 1 — monotonicity.** $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}$ is a [closure operator](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/morphisms/closure-operator.md) on the powerset $\mathrm{Pow}(\mathrm{Atoms}(A_p))$ ordered by inclusion: it is extensive ($Z \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z)$), idempotent ($\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z)) = \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z)$), and **monotone** ($Z \subseteq Z' \Rightarrow \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z) \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z')$). Monotonicity is structural: a closure operator in the category-theoretic sense is necessarily monotone, since $Z \subseteq Z'$ implies $Z \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z')$ (extensivity on $Z'$ plus inclusion), so $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z) \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z')) = \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Z')$ (closure of the inclusion plus idempotence). Hence (1).

**Part 2 — dependent-invalidity identity, $\supseteq$ direction.** Suppose $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y)$ and $(Y \setminus X) \cap \mathrm{deps}^*(c) = \emptyset$. Walk the witness chain backwards from $c$: every atom in $\mathrm{deps}^*(c)$ is either an atom of stratum $> 0$ derived from earlier atoms, or a stratum-$0$ atom in $Y$. By the assumption, every stratum-$0$ atom encountered is in $Y \setminus (Y \setminus X) = X$. Each derivation step in the chain is a σ-firing whose premise atoms are all in $\mathrm{deps}^*(c) \cap \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$ (by induction on the trajectory ordinal of the firing, base case at stratum-$0$ atoms in $X$). Hence the firing of the rule producing $c$ is also enabled when starting from $X$, so $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$, contradicting $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y) \setminus \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$. So if $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y) \setminus \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$ then $(Y \setminus X) \cap \mathrm{deps}^*(c) \neq \emptyset$.

**Part 2 — dependent-invalidity identity, $\subseteq$ direction.** Suppose $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y)$ and $(Y \setminus X) \cap \mathrm{deps}^*(c) \neq \emptyset$ — there is a stratum-$0$ atom $x \in Y \setminus X$ on the witness chain of $c$. Suppose for contradiction that $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$. Then $c$ is derivable from atoms in $X$ alone, so there is *some* witness chain for $c$ that bottoms out entirely in $X$. But the lex-canonical [firing trajectory](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/from-predicate-category/canonical-firing-trajectory-via-lex-order.md) on $A^{\flat}_p$ assigns $c$ a *unique* witness chain — the one fixed by the lex-canonical firing that produced $c$ — and that chain transits $x$. The two chains disagree, so $c$ would have to admit two distinct emissions in $A^{\flat}_p$, one with $\mathrm{deps}$ in $X$ and one with $\mathrm{deps}$ touching $x$. But $A^{\flat}_p$ identifies atoms by the canonical pair (cell-coordinate, lex-canonical witness chain) — atoms with different witness chains are *different* atoms in $\mathrm{Atoms}(A^{\flat}_p)$. So if $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y) \setminus \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$ in the witness-chain-respecting sense, the two derivations are two different atoms; the atom $c \in \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y)$ touched by $x$ is *not* in $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$, even though a different atom at the same cell coordinate may be.

The two directions establish the identity at the witness-chain-respecting level. The *cell-coordinate-collapsed* identity (read $c$ as just the coordinate, ignoring witness chain) is a corollary: a coordinate is in $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y) \setminus \mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(X)$ at the coordinate level iff *every* atom at that coordinate has $(Y \setminus X) \cap \mathrm{deps}^* \neq \emptyset$.

**Part 3 — witness-determined retraction.** Given the saturated set $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y)$ already materialised in $A^{\flat}_p$ — that is, every saturated atom is on disk with its $\mathrm{deps}$ chain — the dependent-invalidity set is computed by:

```
mark = {}
for x in (Y \ X):
    push x onto worklist
while worklist non-empty:
    pop atom a from worklist
    for each c with a in deps(c):
        if c not in mark:
            mark.add(c)
            push c onto worklist
return mark
```

The traversal visits each atom at most once and each $\mathrm{deps}$-edge at most once, giving $O(|\mathrm{deps}^*|)$ time. No σ-pass is invoked.

## What this rules out

- **Premise-monotone but witness-amnesiac saturation.** A substrate that materialises $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}}(Y)$ but does not record per-cell $\mathrm{deps}$ has no efficient way to compute the dependent-invalidity set on retraction; it must re-run the full σ-pass against $X$ and diff. Part 3 fails. The witness chain is structurally required for retraction efficiency.
- **Witness-chain pruning that forgets pre-asserted leaves.** A substrate that prunes $\mathrm{deps}$ to the stratum-$1$-only atoms (forgetting which stratum-$0$ atoms ultimately justified each saturated atom) loses the stratum-$0$ leaves needed for the dependent-invalidity identity. Pruning above the stratum-$0$ floor is incompatible with Part 2's $\supseteq$ direction; Part 3's traversal would terminate too early.
- **Multiple-derivations-collapsed identity.** A substrate that records only a *single* $\mathrm{deps}$ chain per cell coordinate (rather than one per atom) loses the disambiguation of "this coordinate's value is also derivable without the retracted atom". The cell-coordinate-collapsed identity above is what justifies emitting a *retraction* atom at the coordinate level when *every* atom at the coordinate has been invalidated; a substrate that records only a single chain may emit a spurious retraction or miss a needed retraction.

## What this requires

- The σ-nucleus is a closure operator (extensive, monotone, idempotent) on $\mathrm{Pow}(\mathrm{Atoms}(A_p))$. This holds for any [closure-rule system](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/from-predicate-category/teaching/explanations/closure-rule-system.md); see [closure-rule-system-realizes-closure-2-monad](../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/closure-rule-system-realizes-closure-2-monad.md) for the 2-monad articulation.
- The stratified fiber $A^{\flat}_p$ identifies atoms by (coordinate, witness chain) pairs, not by coordinate alone. Per [stratified-predication-fiber](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/objects/stratified-predication-fiber.md), this is a structural commitment.
- The lex-canonical [firing trajectory](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/from-predicate-category/canonical-firing-trajectory-via-lex-order.md) is in force, so each atom's witness chain is uniquely determined by $(\mathcal{S}, X)$.
- Witness chains are persisted on disk on every saturated atom (the engineering-side commitment is `derived_from`/`derived_via` per stratified-cell-atom).

## Position

This theorem is the structural correctness statement that the substrate's σ-pass dependent-invalidity discipline rests on. It is what makes "retracting an asserted cell" a well-defined and efficiently-computable operation rather than a black-box re-saturation. The engineering surface is [dependent-invalidity-via-saturated-retraction](../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theorems/dependent-invalidity-via-saturated-retraction.md), which lifts Parts 2 and 3 to the substrate's wire-form retraction-cell emission contract.

The theorem also positions the stratified predication fiber's witness chain as *necessary structure*, not a bookkeeping decoration: without per-atom $\mathrm{deps}$, the substrate cannot perform retraction in linear time, cannot distinguish coordinate-cleared from coordinate-still-derivable, and cannot present a verifiable audit chain. The witness chain is what makes the closure-degree filtration *useful* rather than just *recordable*.
