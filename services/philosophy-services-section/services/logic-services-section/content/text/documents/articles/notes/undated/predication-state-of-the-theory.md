# State of the theory

Predication categories has been growing in distinct directions long enough that the chapter no longer describes itself accurately by listing what it contains. This narrative is a survey: what the theory is centrally about, what its structural skeleton looks like at the present moment, what is genuinely established versus what is load-bearing-sketch, where the unresolved tensions are, and what the recent work has added.

The corpus currently has 178 theorems, 95 examples, 58 questions, 69 narratives, 96 reference annotations, 74 objects, 36 concepts, and a substantial Python toolkit. By raw count the chapter is large; by the count of *load-bearing* mathematical claims and of cross-framework verifications the chapter is much smaller, and this gap is itself a piece of the present state worth naming.

## What the theory is centrally about

The corpus is the categorical study of **bilattice- and quantale-valued predication structures** — predicate-categorical relation cells fibered over a small predicate category, refined into predication hyperdoctrines, dualized through Priestley correspondence onto bitopological sites, and stratified by the cohomology and moduli of the resulting settling dynamics. The chief application that drove the chapter's development is *spatiotemporal knowledge*: modeling how relations between entities settle over space and time, with paraconsistent disagreement as first-class content rather than an error condition. Other applications — CRDTs, distributed-systems causal histories, ludics — sit inside the same categorical apparatus.

The chapter's distinctive content, which differentiates it from the surrounding bilattice / Priestley / sheaf-cohomology / distributed-systems literatures, is the *combination*: bilattice values (paraconsistency-native), settling dynamics (transfinite-temporal), bitopological / d-frame duals (pointfree spatial structure), and cohomological invariants of the resulting site. No one of these is novel individually; the combination — and the resulting four-regime classification of settling behavior — is what the corpus is for.

For a complementary reading that sorts the chapter into its *role*, *realization*, and *invariant* layers — and explains where each kind of new development slots in without disturbing the others — see [the stack of roles](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/from-predicate-category/the-stack-of-roles.md).

## The four cohomological frameworks

The corpus computes "cohomology of a settling" via four parallel frameworks (per the [cohomological-framework concept](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/content/cohomological-framework/cohomological-framework.md) and the [three-cohomological-frameworks narrative](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/from-predicate-category/three-cohomological-frameworks.md)):

- **Framework 1 — substitution-matrix Anderson–Putnam.** Computational, working. The benchmark suite runs on this. Does not prove abstract theorems.
- **Framework 2 — Tarski Laplacian on discrete-points fiber.** Proves strict / Scott-continuous regime d-frame cohomology theorems. Treats fibers as discrete topological spaces.
- **Framework 3 — cellular sheaf on order complex of clopen lattice (Q-enriched, parallel-pair convention).** The per-fiber Curry equivalence chain lives here. The downstream Verdier / d-frame Verdier / strong-MLD chain threads through this.
- **Framework 4 — Lawvere-Laplacian Q-enriched (weighted-meet convention).** Theoretical scaffolding for higher-degree-vanishing theorems. Not concretely computed against any specific settling.

These frameworks should agree where they overlap, but the corpus has only **one verified concrete agreement** at this writing: Framework 2 ↔ Framework 3 on the standard Belnap fiber, $H^0$ component, via the [per-fiber d-frame Curry on the Belnap fiber example](../../research/2026/07/31/examples/per-fiber-d-frame-curry-on-belnap-fiber.md) (5 passes complete). Frameworks 1 ↔ 2, Frameworks 1 ↔ 3, Frameworks 3 ↔ 4 — none of these has a single verified concrete cross-framework agreement on a specific settling. Frameworks 2, 3, 4 *give the same headline results* on the spaces where multiple are computable, but the agreement is by structural argument, not computational verification.

This is the chapter's most significant unresolved tension: the cohomology spine is multi-pathed and the paths have not been concretely shown to land at the same place except in one case.

## The four-regime stratification

The [four-regime dichotomy](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/references/four-regime-dichotomy.md) is the chapter's central organizing structural fact. Every settling falls into exactly one of four regimes by its closure ordinal $\kappa$:

| regime | $\kappa$ | $H^1$ | classification | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strict | $< \omega$ | $0$ | Designated-cell counts | proved end-to-end |
| Scott-continuous | $= \omega$ | $0$ | Polynomial in settling length | proved end-to-end |
| Transfinite-bounded | $\omega < \kappa < \omega_1$ | non-zero, $H^1$-generators per NSC firing | Tarski–Lawvere fixed-point at countable cofinality | proved |
| Aperiodic | $\geq \omega_1$ | direct-limit of countable truncations | Anderson–Putnam classifying invariant | proved (via direct-limit definition) |

The strict and Scott-continuous regimes are clean. The transfinite-bounded and aperiodic regimes now also have proved cohomology theorems at corpus rigor, no longer resting on the cohomology-spine sketch chain (the chain has closed; see §"Status reality check" above). The aperiodic-regime classification — *aperiodic settlings up to MLD-equivalence are classified by their $H^1$-class in a direct-limit cohomology* — remains the chapter's deepest result; its current risk profile is concentrated in the cross-framework-agreement question (§"The four cohomological frameworks"), not in the cohomology-spine chain.

The benchmark suite (7 substitutions: Fibonacci, Tribonacci, Tetranacci, Padovan, Thue–Morse, plus computational variants) verifies the orbit-type classification *on each specific case* via Framework 1. This is empirical evidence that the abstract classification is correct on the cases checked; it is not a proof.

## What the recent work has added

The agentic-swarm-theory strand (developed across the last few sessions) has substantially extended the chapter into the multi-agent / distributed-systems literature, with the following concrete additions:

**Network-sheaf-on-agent-graph reformulation** ([network-sheaf consensus on agent graphs](../../research/2026/07/31/theorems/network-sheaf-consensus-on-agent-graphs.md), proved). The corpus's CRDT / Mayer–Vietoris / cohomological-CALM trio specialized to the standard distributed-systems setting: $O = \hat G$ is the incidence poset of a finite agent graph $G$. Five claims (consensus, disagreement-paraconsistency, coordination-freeness, CALM-compatibility, higher-cohomology vanishing), each citing an existing proved corpus theorem. Reformulation work: the chapter is now legible to readers familiar with Hansen–Ghrist / Lopez / Felber.

**Bilattice extension** ([bilattice network-sheaf consensus on agent graphs](../../research/2026/07/31/theorems/bilattice-network-sheaf-consensus.md), proved). The d-frame-valued analog of the previous theorem. Six claims, each reducing componentwise to the abelian shadow + relational inheritance via the d-frame cohomology comparison. The corpus's distinctive content: paraconsistency-from-disagreement is captured natively at the d-frame level via $\mathrm{con}$-failure at $\top$-witness cells.

**Per-observer rule extension** ([CRDT convergence with per-observer rules](crdt-convergence-with-per-observer-rules.md), proved; [multi-observer system with rules](../../research/2026/07/31/objects/multi-observer-system-with-rules.md), object). Distinguishes evidence-layer disagreement from inferential-layer disagreement; observer-specific rule systems are first-class. The structural extension of the multi-observer-system needed for Direction 2 (Brewka–Eiter multi-context-systems engagement) of the swarm-theory plan.

**Hansen–Ghrist spectral engagement** ([hansen-ghrist-spectral-engagement question](../../learning-articles/questions/hansen-ghrist-spectral-engagement.md), passes 1+2 done, 3–4 open). The corpus and Hansen–Ghrist 2019 *agree on the consensus space* ($\Gamma = \lim V_G = H^0 = \ker L_F$); they *characterize it via complementary machinery* (corpus: Mayer–Vietoris obstruction-vanishing across all replica covers + bilattice extensibility; Hansen–Ghrist: spectral gap + convergence rate + Hodge orthogonal decomposition). Hansen–Ghrist's spectral apparatus *structurally cannot extend* to bilattice / d-frame values — no canonical inner product on $\mathbf B$, no canonical adjoint of d-frame morphisms, no $\Delta_F$-style operator on heterogeneous d-frame components. This is a substantive structural finding, not just a methodological choice on the corpus's part.

**Felber task-sheaf d-frame translation** ([felber-task-sheaf-d-frame-translation question](../../research/2026/07/31/learning/questions/felber-task-sheaf-d-frame-translation.md), pass 1 done, 2 partial, 3–4 open). Felber et al's task-sheaf stalks are *frames* (powersets of output sets), with restrictions $\vee$-preserving frame morphisms; the lift to $\mathbf{Ab}$ via $\mathbb Z[-]$ is computational, not structural. **Both Felber's task sheaves and the corpus's settling-associated sheaves share $\mathbf{Frm}_\vee$ as a common value category** — partially settling Sub-question 1 of the [unified distributed-systems cohomology question](../../research/2026/07/31/learning/questions/unified-distributed-systems-cohomology.md) affirmatively. The d-frame extension of Felber's task sheaf is structurally available, refining Felber's binary valid/invalid into Belnap's $\mathbf t$/$\mathbf f$/$\top$/$\bot$.

**Three corpus-wide sub-questions opened (not yet pursued in depth):**

- *Pass 3 / 4 of Hansen–Ghrist engagement.* Lopez 2024 fuzzy-adjoint as the bilattice-flavored quantale-graded analog; Lawvere Laplacian convergence rate as the corpus-side fixed-point analog of spectral gap. Both substantial research-program continuations.
- *Pass 2 (proof) and Pass 3 of Felber engagement.* The d-frame analog of Felber's Theorem 20 is constructed-but-not-proved; the cohomological-CALM analog for task sheaves is statable on this footing but not yet stated formally.
- *The remaining sub-questions of the unified-distributed-systems-cohomology question.* Sub-question 2 (does cohomological-CALM generalize?) and Sub-question 3 (mixed-system frameworks) — both are now downstream of the partially-settled Sub-question 1.

**Four reference annotations** added (Brewka–Eiter 2007, Sznitman 1991, Dung 1995, Fagin–Halpern–Moses–Vardi 1995), establishing the chapter's literature engagement for Directions 2, 4, 5 of the swarm-theory plan.

## Active tensions

The chapter's main load-bearing tensions, in roughly ranked order of how much rests on them:

1. **Cross-framework agreement.** Only one concrete instance verified (Frameworks 2 ↔ 3 on the standard Belnap fiber, $H^0$). The strong-MLD theorem and the higher-cohomology vanishing theorem assume framework agreement implicitly; the [per-fiber Curry working question](../../learning-articles/questions/per-fiber-curry-on-clopen-lattice-fiber.md) is the potential site for verifying further cases.

2. **Non-Boolean cohomology extensions.** The d-frame setting is closed at corpus rigor in the Boolean d-frame scope (per [Boolean d-frame derived cohomology agreement](../../research/2026/07/31/theorems/q-enriched-derived-cohomology-boolean-d-frame.md), proved). Heyting-frame and Lawvere-quantale extensions are sketch with documented structural obstructions (Heyting pseudo-complement; three Boolean facts failing in the Lawvere quantale) — see [q-enriched-hodge-theorem-non-boolean-frames](../../research/2026/07/31/theorems/q-enriched-hodge-theorem-non-boolean-frames.md), [q-enriched-hodge-theorem-lawvere-quantale](../../../../glossary/concepts/q-enriched-hodge-theorem-lawvere-quantale.md), [q-enriched-grothendieck-spectral-sequence](../../research/2026/07/31/theorems/q-enriched-grothendieck-spectral-sequence.md). Whether the corpus's distinctive content survives outside the Boolean scope is the natural next frontier for the cohomology side.

3. **Tarski–Lawvere convergence at $\omega_1$-cofinality — fully closed, both positively and negatively.** The unrestricted conjecture is `refuted`, and the resolution is structural rather than ad hoc. The [openness characterization theorem](../../research/2026/07/31/theorems/frame-morphism-meet-preservation-openness.md) (proved) shows that meet preservation by a frame morphism is equivalent to having a left adjoint, equivalent (spatially) to being induced by an open map. This single structural fact unifies four pieces: (i) explicit $\omega_1$-cofinal counterexample via the topology-refining identity $f : (\omega_1+1)_{\text{refined}} \to (\omega_1+1)_{\text{standard}}$, (ii) recovery of the cellular stalk-product theorem as the categorical-openness corollary (product projections always have inclusion left adjoints), (iii) explanation of the conditional theorem's hypothesis as exactly the openness condition transposed to sheaves, and (iv) explicit verification that the direct-limit invariant is strictly finer than the fixed-point invariant on the counterexample, with the gap measured by the meet-preservation defect. The corpus's d-frame-valued cellular sheaves on the bitopological settling site sit on the categorical-openness side; their fixed-point and direct-limit cohomologies coincide via the [equivalence theorem](../../research/2026/07/31/theorems/tarski-lawvere-direct-limit-equivalence.md). The unrestricted conjecture's failure is now understood as the same phenomenon as "not every continuous map is open."

4. **The benchmark-empirical / abstract-theory gap.** The benchmark suite verifies orbit-type classification via Framework 1 on 7 specific substitutions. The abstract strong-MLD theorem (Framework 3/4 chain) is structurally argued. These are *independent* supports for the same conclusion, not joint — a benchmark verification on a substitution does not constitute evidence that the abstract proof is sound, and vice versa.

5. **D-frame value category vs Hilbert-analytic frameworks.** The Hansen–Ghrist spectral engagement question's Pass 2 establishes that the corpus's distinctive content (bilattice / d-frame values) *structurally cannot import* the Hilbert-analytic spectral apparatus. The Lopez 2024 fuzzy-adjoint and Ghrist et al 2025 Lawvere-Laplacian frameworks are the corpus's substitutes; whether they actually compute spectral-analog content (convergence rate, eigenvalue gap) remains open in Pass 4 of that engagement question.

6. **Per-observer rules and inferential-layer disagreement.** The per-observer-rule extension is recent and not yet integrated with the broader closure-2-monad framework. The natural follow-up — when do per-observer rules give a CALM-compatible system? — is the cohomological-CALM-with-per-observer-rules question, not yet stated as a theorem.
