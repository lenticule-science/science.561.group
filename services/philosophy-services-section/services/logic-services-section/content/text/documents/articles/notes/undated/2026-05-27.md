## Analysis

During a discussion about Lakota philosophy and mathematical structure, a colleague noted Lakota's postpositional syntax:
- mas'óphiye él = "at the store" (literally 'store at')
- thípi=kiŋ ókšaŋ = "around the house" (literally 'house=the around')

This observation connects directly to our work on predication cells and SPOTCOMP's trace-based computing model.

### Predication-Cell Interpretation

In Lakota, the adposition follows the head noun, which can be read as:
`[entity] [relation]` rather than the prefix-style `[relation](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-4/articulating/library/aperiodic-articulation-category/hacking/library/lakotiyapi/hacking/library/adposition/docs/dev/journal/[entity])` common in many languages and formal notations.

This aligns precisely with how we structure predication events in SPOTCOMP:
1. First record the **entity involved** (the nominal head)
2. Then specify the **mode of relation** (spatial, temporal, causal, etc.)

For example:
- `thípi=kiŋ ókšaŋ` → record house, then apply "around" relation
- In predication-cell terms: `house` (subject) → `around` (generator) → `situated-house`

### Generator Morphism View

This postfix order suggests viewing adpositions as **converse generator morphisms**:
- Preposition (prefix): `relation → entity → situated-entity` (standard generator)
- Postposition (suffix): `entity → relation → situated-entity` (converse generator)

In categorical terms, if we have a generator `G : Entity → SituatedEntity` representing a prepositional relation, the Lakota postposition corresponds to the converse operation that flips the argument's position, making the entity the initial input.

### Connection to SPOTCOMP Trace Logic

This syntactic pattern relates to our trace-based approach:
- Each Lakota utterance builds a **trace of predication events** [@spotted-spec]
- `thípi=kiŋ ókšaŋ` creates a trace: [house-entity] followed by [around-relation applied to that entity]
- The final meaning emerges from settling this trace to its least fixed point in the bilattice
- Contradictions (if any) are recorded as values rather than failures—consistent with Lakota's embrace of paradoxical teachings [@wolakota-concept]

### Implications for Analysis

1. **Evaluation Strategy**: Lakota's word order might suggest an **eager evaluation** of entities before applying relations—first establish what we're talking about, then how it's situated.

2. **Compositionality**: Multiple adpositions (e.g., hypothetical "house store around at") would compose as nested generators:
   ```
   house 
     → apply "store" relation (if that made sense) 
     → apply "around" relation 
     → apply "at" relation
   ```
   Each step enriches the situated entity, matching our trace-accumulation model.

3. **Closure Discipline**: The Wolakota concept (balance, harmony) might relate to how these traces settle—seeking a coherent fixed point where all predicative constraints are simultaneously satisfied [@wolakota-concept]. This connects to the idea of wicozani as a settling-quiescence condition [@wicozani-concept], where well-being corresponds to relational cells settled to consistent values under the wolakota closure discipline.

### Initial Investigation

I searched the repository for Lakota linguistic resources, computational models, and discussions of parsing preferences or fixpoint computation strategies. While the repository contains rich formalizations of Lakota philosophy as closure-rule subsystems (Wolakota), settling-quiescence conditions (Wicozani), and explicit mappings to the FOUR bilattice, I did not find explicit discussion of:

- Parsing preferences in Lakota sentence processing
- Left-biased vs. right-biased evaluation strategies in Lakota language
- Computational models of Lakota trace settlement
- Discussion of fixpoint computation preferences in Lakota cognitive processing

The search revealed strong connections between Lakota cosmogony and the bilattice (with the four superior Wakȟáŋ mapping to ⊥, t, f, ⊤) and formalizations of closure and settling as discipline (Wolakota-as-closure-discipline), but no explicit traces of computational parsing strategies or fixpoint preferences in the available Lakota materials.

This suggests that while the philosophical and algebraic structures are well-developed in the repository, the linguistic/computational processing level remains an open area for investigation—precisely where the open question about trace-settling strategies resides.
