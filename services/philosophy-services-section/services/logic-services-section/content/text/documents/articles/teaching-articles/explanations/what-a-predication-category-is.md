This is an explanation. It tells you what a predication category is, the problem it was built to solve, the mechanism it uses, and why that mechanism is shaped the way it is. You can read it without any prior exposure to the framework, though some acquaintance with categories and lattices will help.

## The problem

Ordinary first-order logic treats a predicate as a function from individuals to truth values: either a relation holds between two things or it does not. That binary verdict throws away two distinctions that matter whenever knowledge is assembled from many partial reports. First, *absence of information* is not *falsehood* — being told nothing about whether two parties have a contract is different from being told they have none. Second, *conflict* is real — when one source says yes and another says no, a faithful record must hold both at once rather than silently collapsing to a verdict. Nuel Belnap's analysis of "how a computer should think" made this precise with four values rather than two, distinguishing *told true*, *told false*, *told nothing*, and *told both*. Predication categories take Belnap's four-valued epistemic stance and build a full categorical home around it, so that predicates, the entities they relate, the ways predicates transform into one another, and the way knowledge accumulates over time all live in one coherent structure.

## Predicates, entities, and cells

The unit of content is a *predication cell*, written $(s, p, o)$. Here $p$ is a **predicate** drawn from a fixed predicate set, $s$ is the **subject** — the entity *of which* $p$ is asserted — and $o$ is the **object** — the entity *to which* the subject is related under $p$. Both $s$ and $o$ are **entities**: the individuals or nodes the predicates discuss, elements of a fixed entity set $E$. A cell reads as the proposition "$p$ holds of $s$ in relation to $o$." (The word *object* here names the third coordinate of a cell; it is deliberately not the category-theoretic sense of "object of a category," which the framework reserves for predicates.)

A **predicate category** $\mathcal{C}$ is a small category whose objects are the predicates. What makes it a *category* rather than a bare set is that predicates come with morphisms — structured ways one predicate gives rise to another — generated by four canonical classes:

- *specialization*, refining one predicate to a more specific one;
- *converse*, swapping the subject and object roles;
- *subject-projection*, collapsing the entity pair along its diagonal to extract a unary predicate that depends only on the subject;
- *object-projection*, the same collapse extracting a predicate that depends only on the object.

Each generator is induced by a manipulation of the entity-pair index $E \times E$, and under a natural symmetry hypothesis (the relabelling of entities being respected, with at least three entities) these four classes exhaust the structure-respecting transformations between predicates. That exhaustiveness is why there are exactly four and not some open-ended list: they are not a chosen vocabulary but the complete set of ways the entity-pair index can be manipulated.

## The four-valued fiber

Every predicate carries its own epistemic algebra, its **fiber**. Fix a residuated lattice $\mathbf{L}$ of degrees — in the simplest case the two-element Boolean lattice $\mathbf{2}$. The fiber at a predicate assigns, to each entity pair $(s, o)$, a value built by a *twist product*: a pair $\langle a, b \rangle$ recording positive evidence $a$ and negative evidence $b$ separately. Keeping the two kinds of evidence on independent coordinates is the whole point, because it lets the algebra carry two distinct orderings at once.

The **truth order** $\leq_t$ raises positive evidence and lowers negative evidence — moving "more true" means the case for rises and the case against falls. The **knowledge order** $\leq_k$ raises *both* coordinates — moving "more known" means more evidence of either polarity. A negation simply swaps the two coordinates. An algebra carrying these two interleaved orders together with a coordinate-swapping negation is a *bilattice*, in the sense studied by Ginsberg and Fitting.

Specialising the degree lattice to $\mathbf{2}$ recovers the smallest non-trivial bilattice, $\mathbf{FOUR}$, whose four values are exactly Belnap's:

- $\mathbf{f} = \langle 0,1\rangle$ — *told false*;
- $\bot = \langle 0,0\rangle$ — *told nothing*;
- $\top = \langle 1,1\rangle$ — *told both*;
- $\mathbf{t} = \langle 1,0\rangle$ — *told true*.

In the knowledge order $\bot$ sits at the bottom (nothing known) and $\top$ at the top (a conflict, the most that can be "told"); $\mathbf{t}$ and $\mathbf{f}$ sit between, incomparable to each other. In the truth order $\mathbf{f}$ and $\mathbf{t}$ are the extremes and $\bot, \top$ sit between. The two orders run transverse to each other, and that transversality — not a single linear scale from false to true — is the structural heart of the whole framework.

A whole fiber is then a function from entity pairs to bilattice values, with all four operations and the negation applied cellwise. So the fiber is itself a bilattice over the entity-pair index, one per predicate.

## Stitching the fibers together

The predicates and their fibers are bound into one object by a **fibration** — a Grothendieck fibration $p : \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{C}$ that sits a fiber over each predicate and specifies how the four predicate-generators *reindex* one fiber into another. Three demands govern this stitching. Each fiber must genuinely be the four-valued algebra just described; each predicate-morphism must act on fibers by the corresponding manipulation of the entity-pair index; and that action must preserve all the bilattice structure — both meets and joins, the negation, and the residuated operations. This last demand is what guarantees the framework is coherent: transforming a predicate and then reading its epistemic state gives the same answer as reading the state and then transforming it.

When the fibration additionally supports quantifiers — left and right adjoints to reindexing, in *both* the knowledge order and the truth order, satisfying the standard Frobenius and Beck–Chevalley coherences — it becomes a **hyperdoctrine**, in Lawvere's sense, now doubled along the two orders. The two parallel quantifier systems are exactly what one expects from two transverse orders: one can quantify "for all, as far as truth goes" and "for all, as far as knowledge goes," and these are genuinely different operations. Notably these quantifier and comprehension properties are *properties* the fibration may or may not have, not extra data bolted on — a structural fact (the Kelly–Lack property-like theorem) forbids any independent choice in how they are realised.

## Settling: knowledge over time

A single snapshot — a **valuation** assigning each predicate an element of its fiber — is static. The framework's central indexed object is the **settling**: an ordinal-indexed family of valuations $(v_\tau)_{\tau < \lambda}$ subject to one law, *settling monotonicity*. For earlier stage $\sigma$ and later stage $\tau$, and every predicate, $v_\sigma(p) \leq_k v_\tau(p)$ cellwise. Knowledge only ever *accumulates* in the knowledge order; it never retracts. A new report can push a cell from *told nothing* to *told true*, or from *told true* to *told both* when it conflicts, but it can never erase what was already told.

That the discipline is stated in $\leq_k$ and not $\leq_t$ is deliberate and is exactly why the two orders are kept separate. The passage of time is monotone in *knowledge* — you learn more — but emphatically not monotone in *truth*, since learning more can flip a cell's truth-verdict or drive it into conflict. Were there only one order, settling could not be stated without lying about how evidence behaves.

Settlings themselves form a category, $\mathbf{PS}$, over a fixed base of degree lattice, predicate category, entity set, and ordinal bound. Its objects are settlings; there is a morphism $v \to v'$ exactly when $v'$ is everywhere-and-always at least as knowledge-settled as $v$. The hom-sets are *thin* — at most one arrow between any two settlings — so the category is really a partial order dressed as a category. Antisymmetry of the knowledge order makes mutual morphisms force outright equality, so $\mathbf{PS}$ is a poset-category. Its value is that operations on settlings — placing a single new datum at a single cell, or joining in a new report — become morphism-level facts that compose and inherit categorical coherence for free, rather than relations re-derived by hand in each context. The same class of settlings, re-indexed by the truth order, carries a *transverse* category whose morphisms are a *modeling* relation; the two are not interchangeable, and keeping them apart is once more the two-orders discipline showing through.

## What it buys

The payoff of the categorical packaging is that disparate concerns sit in one structure with one set of laws. A predicate is an object; the four ways predicates relate are morphisms; each predicate's epistemic state is a four-valued algebra that distinguishes ignorance and conflict from plain truth and falsehood; quantification is doubled to match the two orders; and the growth of knowledge over time is a monotone trajectory living in a poset-category of settlings. Because every layer respects the bilattice structure, you can move freely between transforming predicates, evaluating their states, quantifying over entities, and advancing the settling, confident that the answers agree. The framework does not merely record what is true; it records *what has been told*, honestly carrying ignorance and contradiction, and it lets that record only ever grow.
