## Full citation

G. M. Kelly. *Basic Concepts of Enriched Category Theory*. London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series, vol. 64. Cambridge University Press, 1982. Reprinted in Reprints in Theory and Applications of Categories, No. 10, 2005.

## Status

Standard reference. Reprint freely available at TAC.

## Annotation

The canonical reference for enriched category theory. Used in the corpus's [bilattice-valued cohomology chapter](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-3/narratives/bilattice-cohomology-opener.md) for two structural facts that ground the [Lawvere Laplacian extension theorem](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-3/theorems/lawvere-laplacian-grothendieck-sites.md):

- **§1.2, §3.1**: Composition of enriched functors is an enriched functor; weighted limits and colimits in enriched categories.
- **§3.3**: Enriched functors preserve weighted limits — the technical fact used to show the Lawvere Laplacian (an enriched functor by construction) preserves the sheaf-condition equalizer (a weighted limit).

Specialized to the case $\mathbf{V} = \mathsf{Q}$ for $\mathsf{Q}$ a commutative unital quantale, Kelly's results give the framework for $\mathsf{Q}$-enriched sheaf cohomology that the corpus's Layer-1 cellular extension work uses.

## Position in the program

Foundational reference for the Q-enriched-sheaves-on-Grothendieck-sites work. Cited where the proof argument relies on standard enriched category theory facts (Q-functor preserving weighted limits, etc.).
