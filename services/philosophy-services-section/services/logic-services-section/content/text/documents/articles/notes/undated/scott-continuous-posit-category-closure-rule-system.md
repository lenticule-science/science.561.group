# Closure-rule system

A **closure-rule system** `S` is a finite or countable set of closure-rules. The collection of rules that one posits when committing to a particular settling — the syntactic specification of the closure operator that will drive the orbit.

## The closure-operator generated by `S`

The closure-operator generated by `S` is the **least** operator `cl^S = (cl^S_p)_{p ∈ C}` satisfying three conditions for every fiber `p`:

**Inflationary.** `v ≤_k cl^S_p(v)`. The closure pushes values up; never pulls them down.

**Idempotent.** `cl^S_p ∘ cl^S_p = cl^S_p`. Applying the closure twice gives the same result as applying it once.

**Rule-firing.** For every rule `r ∈ S` and every instantiation `\vec{y}` of `r`'s free variables, if the antecedent of `r[\vec{y}]` holds in `cl^S(v)`, then the consequent of `r[\vec{y}]` holds in `cl^S(v)`.

## Existence and uniqueness

The family of operators satisfying the three conditions is **closed under pointwise meet**: if `cl_α` and `cl_β` both satisfy the conditions, so does their pointwise infimum `(cl_α ∧ cl_β)(v) := cl_α(v) ∧_k cl_β(v)`. The infimum of all such operators is therefore well-defined and is the least one.

This gives the standard **fixpoint construction**: `cl^S(v) = ⋃_τ F^τ(v)` where `F(w) := w ∨_k (consequents of all rules whose antecedents hold in w)`, iterated transfinitely until the orbit reaches a fixed point. The fixed point exists by Knaster–Tarski (`F` is monotone in `≤_k`, the bilattice is bounded-complete), and the closure-ordinal `κ` of `F` from input `v` lives in one of the four regime bands.

## What `S` chooses; what it determines

Choosing `S` choses three things:

1. **The rule-language fragment** — which atoms the rules' antecedents can use.
2. **The specific rules** — which conditional cell-updates `S` commits to.
3. **The regime band** — fixed by the language fragment, before any orbit is computed.

Choosing `S` determines:

1. The closure-operator `cl^S` — uniquely.
2. The orbit's behavior on any initial valuation `v_0` — uniquely (orbit is determined by `cl^S` and increment-sequence).
3. The closure-ordinal `κ(cl^S, v_0)` — fixed; lives in the band determined by (1).
4. The orbit's settled value (if it settles within `λ`) — `cl^S` applied transfinitely.

So `S` is the **complete specification** of the settling's deterministic dynamics. Two `S` agreeing on (1)–(3) of the chosen specifications produce literally identical orbits on the same initial conditions.

## Regime stratification by language fragment

The closure-rule-language fragment used in `S` determines the **regime class** of the generated operator. The four-regime tower IS the stratification by closure-ordinal:

| Language fragment in `S` | Closure-ordinal `κ(cl^S)` | Regime |
|---|---|---|
| Finite-only atoms; no `∀`-block | `κ < ω` | strict-finite |
| Finite `∀`-block over finite content | `κ = ω` | Scott-continuous |
| `∀` over infinite `E` | `ω < κ < ω₁` | transfinite-bounded |
| `∀` + infinitary connectives | `κ ≥ ω₁` | aperiodic |

This is the **four-regime dichotomy** (archive theorem, not yet pulled). The four-regime grammar takes this stratification as load-bearing.

## The realizability invariant: `L_ns(S)`

A subtle invariant of `S`: the set

$$L_{ns}(S) \;:=\; \{\, \mu \,:\, \mu \text{ a limit ordinal at which } cl^S \text{ adds content not derivable from any single earlier stage} \,\}.$$

These are the **non-Scott-continuous firing ordinals** of `S` — the limits at which closure does something genuinely transfinite that no individual step contains.

`L_ns(S)` is the **realizability-determining invariant**: the realizability program asks which subsets of countable limit ordinals arise as `L_ns(S)` for some `S` in a given language fragment.

| `L_ns(S)` | Regime conclusion |
|---|---|
| `∅` | Scott-continuous: closure commutes with all directed `∨_k`-joins; `κ ≤ ω`. |
| Non-empty, all `< ω₁` | transfinite-bounded |
| Containing `ω₁` or larger | aperiodic |

So `L_ns(S)` reads off the regime: empty means Scott-continuous; non-empty bounded means transfinite-bounded; unbounded means aperiodic.

## Operations on closure-rule-systems

`S` admits two natural operations that compose systems:

**Union `S₁ ∪ S₂`**: forms a larger system. The generated operator is `cl^{S₁ ∪ S₂}`, which is `≥_k` both `cl^{S₁}` and `cl^{S₂}` (more rules ⟹ stronger closure). Closure-ordinals of `S₁ ∪ S₂` are `≥` those of `S₁` and `S₂` separately.

**Intersection `S₁ ∩ S₂`**: keeps only common rules. The generated operator is generally NOT the meet of the two operators (operator-meet doesn't reduce to rule-meet in general). Intersection is well-defined as a syntactic operation but not always as a semantic one.

The union/closure-strengthening direction is what makes positing additive: positing more rules monotonically increases the orbit's content.

## Worked example: a small system

Take `L = 2`, `E = {a, b}`, `C` with two predicates `p` and `q`. System `S` with two rules:

**Rule `r_1`**: `∀ x.\; (v(p)(x, x) ≥_k t) ⟹ v'(q)(x, x) ≥_k t.`

Reading: whenever `p` holds reflexively at an entity, `q` also holds reflexively at that entity.

**Rule `r_2`**: `(v(p)(a, b) ≥_k t) ⟹ v'(p)(b, a) ≥_k t.`

Reading: if `p(a, b)`, then `p(b, a)` (a specific symmetry rule on the cell `(a, b)`).

Orbit starting from `v_0` = constant-`⊥`:

- `cl^S(v_0)`: no antecedents hold (all cells `⊥`), so the operator does nothing. `cl^S(v_0) = v_0`.
- Inject `v_0 ∨_k δ_{(a, p, a), t}` (set `v(p)(a, a) = t`).
- Apply `cl^S`: `r_1` fires with `x = a`, setting `v(q)(a, a) ≥_k t`. `r_2`'s antecedent is `v(p)(a, b) ≥_k t` — not yet held. After one application: `v(p)(a, a) = t, v(q)(a, a) = t`, others `⊥`.
- Continue applying `cl^S`: no new antecedents hold. Quiescence.

Closure-ordinal `κ = 1`. The system is strict-finite on this orbit.
