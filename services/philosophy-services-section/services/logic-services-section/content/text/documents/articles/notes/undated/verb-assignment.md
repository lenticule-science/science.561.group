  # Verb assignment

  A visual language uses *verb assignment* when each member of a small set of marks (colors, weights, devices) is bound to a verb — what it does — rather than to a feeling or a mood. A reader who learns the assignment can predict what the mark means in any context.

  ## Pattern

  The kit lists each load-bearing mark and assigns it a verb: *this mark warns; that mark cites; the third mark emphasizes*. The assignment is total inside the kit's domain: a mark assigned a verb is never used decoratively, and a verb that needs a mark is never expressed by an unassigned one. A mark with no verb is the kit's structural ground.

  ## Parameters

  - **`marks`** — the enumerated marks under verb assignment. Typically the kit's jewel colors, but may also include weights, rules, or devices.
  - **`verb-binding`** — the table mapping each mark to its verb. Each verb appears at most once.
  - **`default-mark`** — the mark that carries no verb; the kit's structural ground (typically the ink and the paper).

  ## Conformance shape

  For each verb-bearing mark in the artifact, *check that the span it occupies performs the verb the mark is bound to*. A red span that does not warn is a defect; a blue span that does not cite is a defect. The check is by reading: read each marked span and ask "does this span do the verb?"

  ## Lineage

  Verb assignment is the visual analogue of giving a function a name and a single responsibility. It supports [glance direction](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-4/articulating/library/aperiodic-articulation-category/library/aperiodic-articulation-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-articulation-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/hacking/library/predication-cell-set/docs/dev/scraps/from-stable-cell-stale-chain/library/predicate-set/library/predicate-category/hacking/library/object/library/thing/library/category/hacking/library/information/information-architecture/visual-engineering/terms/glance-direction.md): a reader who has learned the assignment can navigate the artifact without reading prose — they can see, at a glance, where the warnings are, where the citations are, where the argument is being emphasized. It supports [orientation before interpretation](../../../../../../../../../engineering-science-services-section/services/design-engineering-science-services-section/services/visual-engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/orientation-before-interpretation.md) by making the artifact's structure visible before its content is read.
