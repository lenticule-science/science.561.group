# Why the bitopological settling site uses fiber covers only

Three structural layers live on the dualized object $\mathbf{T}^*$ of an [predication hyperdoctrine](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-2/objects/predication-hyperdoctrine.md):

1. each fiber $X_q^\tau = \mathrm{Spec}(A_q)$ at settling stage $\tau$ carries a [paired Priestley space](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/pre-bilattice/content/bilattice/content/predication-fiber-algebra/content/priestley-space/content/paired-priestley-space/paired-priestley-space.md) structure;
2. the fibers vary contravariantly along [predicate-category](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-2/objects/predicate-category.md) morphisms;
3. they vary covariantly (in the dual direction) along settling-ordinal advancement.

A site reflecting all three could in principle be built three ways, none quite right on its own.

**Fiberwise.** A separate site at each $(q, \tau)$ — sheaves on a single paired Priestley space at a time — gives a cohomology per fiber but loses the cross-ordinal gluing the [aperiodic-regime classification](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/aperiodic-settling/references/aperiodic-regime-classification.md) and [replica Mayer–Vietoris](../../../../../../../relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/replica-mayer-vietoris.md) need. Rejected.

**Alexandrov product only.** Treating $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$ as a poset with its [Alexandrov topology](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-2/objects/alexandrov-topology.md) captures cross-ordinal and cross-predicate gluing as cellular sheaf data, but throws away the paired Priestley structure *within* each fiber. Cohomology computations lose access to the bitopological Stone duality that makes individual fibers tractable. Rejected.

**Full 2-site.** Treating $\mathbf{T}^*$ as a 2-category with a genuine 2-site (in Street's sense, [@street1974]) carries the most information but the additional machinery is needed only for the 2-cell content of [aperiodic regime](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/references/four-regime-dichotomy.md); for the strict and Scott-continuous regimes, and most of C, 2-cells are trivial and the 2-site collapses. Carrying the 2-site machinery throughout is overkill.

## The hybrid the [bitopological settling site](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/bitopological-settling-site/bitopological-settling-site.md) takes

A 1-site whose **objects** assemble fiber-level paired-Priestley clopens with the $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$ base, whose **morphisms** encode predicate-reindexing and settling-temporal advance, and whose **topology** consists of fiber-level pairwise-Stone covers only. The 2-cell content of the aperiodic regime is handled separately as a descent condition on sheaves rather than as extra structure on the site.

## Why no temporal covers

Including "temporal" cover families $\{(q, \tau_i, U) \to (q, \tau, U)\}_{\tau_i \geq \tau}$ would conflict with the cohomological role they need to play. At fixed $(q, U)$, varying-$\tau_i$ to $\tau$ is a *filtered* diagram, not a jointly-surjective family of clopen inclusions. Treating it as a cover would either violate Grothendieck axioms or force a different and less tractable cohomology.

The chosen design instead matches the [Anderson–Putnam direct limit](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-2/objects/anderson-putnam-direct-limit.md) template for [aperiodic dynamics](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/aperiodic-settling/aperiodic-settling.md): the site is indexed by finite stages, transfinite cohomological content is recovered as direct limits over bounded sub-sites $\mathbf{T}^*_{\leq \tau}$. Cross-ordinal settling structure is carried in the site's morphisms; cross-ordinal *cohomological* content is extracted at the level of cohomology, not the site. This separation is what keeps aperiodic-regime classification and CRDT replica Mayer–Vietoris using *disjoint* parts of the cohomology.
