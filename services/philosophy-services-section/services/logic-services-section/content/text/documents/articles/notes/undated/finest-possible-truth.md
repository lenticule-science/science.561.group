The previous essay made the case that gesture-text fails at runtime for transformer-based agents because attention cannot retrieve what is not present, and that the unit of useful writing is the positive falsifiable statement. That argument is correct but underspecified. Once the writer has committed to positive falsifiability, a further question opens that the previous essay declined to answer: among the many positive falsifiable statements true of a given referent, which one should appear? The answer is the discipline of this essay. The unit of useful writing for AI agents is not the positive falsifiable statement in general — it is the *finest-possible-true* statement available given the prior sentence, *derived from prior proven-necessary-truth*. Both modifiers are load-bearing.

## Finest-possible-truth

Consider three statements about the same object.

1. "Predication category theory is a math system."
2. "Predication category theory is a categorical-logic structure over a small category of predicates."
3. "Predication category theory is a paraconsistent, Lambek-residuated bilattice hyperdoctrine over a category of predicates, with fiber algebras $A_p$ valued in a Belnap four-bilattice $\{\bot, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$ and pullbacks admitting left and right adjoints."

Each is positive. Each is falsifiable — one could examine the math and check. None is gesture, in the previous essay's sense. And yet they differ by orders of magnitude in usefulness for any downstream decision the agent might make. The first leaves the agent with a label and no internal structure. The second adds the categorical-logic genus but no species-level discriminants. The third populates roughly twelve coordinate-slots the agent can attach later reasoning to: *paraconsistent* (so $\top$ is admissible as a cell value rather than an error), *Lambek-residuated* (so the multiplication is non-commutative with two-sided residuals), *bilattice* (so two transverse partial orders), *hyperdoctrine* (so the adjoint pair exists), *fiber algebras $A_p$* (so values are per-predicate), *Belnap four-bilattice* (so the specific $\{\bot, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$ algebra). Each discriminant is a hook the agent can compose against later claims.

Define the *conditional resolution* of a sentence $S$ given prior context $C$ as the mutual information $I(S; D \mid C)$ between $S$ and the agent's downstream decisions $D$, conditioned on $C$. Two sentences positively asserting compatible facts about the same referent may differ arbitrarily in this quantity — the finer one carves a smaller region out of hypothesis space, leaving the agent's posterior more concentrated. The discipline of *finest-possible-truth* is the directive to maximize $I(S; D \mid C)$ for each $S$ written, subject only to the constraint that $S$ remain true.

Why is this not trivially the right directive? Because human writers do not optimize this way. Human writing trades resolution against accessibility: a sentence aimed at multiple audiences must be vague enough to be true for all of them, so the writer chooses the coarsest claim that survives every audience's verification. For human readers, this is correct, because each reader refines the claim later by lookup, context, or prior expertise. For AI agents at runtime, this strategy is exactly inverted: the agent cannot refine later. What it has when it makes its decision is what was written. The writer's job is therefore the opposite of human-targeted compression. It is to find, for each sentence, the finest claim true at that point, and to write it without flinching at the prose-stylistic cost.

The token economy makes this acute. A sentence of fifty tokens consumes the same window budget whether its information content is one bit or fifty. Coarse writing is straight loss: paying for fifty tokens and getting one bit of orientation. The writer who chooses coarsest-true believing themselves to be "safe" is wasting most of the channel they have to the agent. The token budget *is* the agent's working memory; squandering it on under-resolved prose is the linguistic analog of allocating memory and never writing to it.

## Derivation from prior proven-necessary-truth

Finest-possible-truth is necessary but not sufficient. Each finely resolved statement must also *derive* from prior proven-necessary-truth. Consider the difference between:

A. "Predication category theory is a paraconsistent, Lambek-residuated bilattice hyperdoctrine over a category of predicates."

B. The same sentence, preceded by: "A *predication cell* is an oriented triple $(s, p, o)$ — subject, predicate, object — treated as a coordinate, not a value. The *fiber algebra* $A_p$ assigns each predicate $p$ a Belnap-style bilattice $\{\bot, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$ with two transverse orders. A *valuation* assigns each predicate a function on entity pairs valued in $A_p$. The fibers assemble into a fibration over the category of predicates, and pullback admits both left and right adjoints."

The two sentences (A in isolation, and A as the conclusion of B's setup) carry the same surface claim but are not equivalent for the agent reading them. In isolation, A is a string of modifiers the agent's prior must absorb without grounding: *paraconsistent* means *something* (the agent knows this from training), *Lambek-residuated* means *something* (likewise), but the connections between these modifiers and the rest of the sentence are opaque. As the conclusion of B, A is a *derivation step*: each modifier traces to a concrete antecedent already established. *Paraconsistent* derives from the bilattice admitting $\top$ as a non-error value (named in the predication-cell definition); *Lambek-residuated* derives from the fiber algebra carrying a non-commutative multiplication with two-sided residuals (implicit in the residuated-lattice structure of the bilattice); *hyperdoctrine* derives from the adjoint pair (stated explicitly). The agent reads A and can trace each load-bearing word backward through attention to its support.

A statement that derives from prior proven-necessary-truth slots into the agent's attention structure as a proof step in a dependent calculus. The agent can compose it forward — using A to make further claims — because A's premises are already in scope and the inference is checkable. A statement that does not so derive is a free hypothesis: the agent has no premise to discharge, no inference to verify. Free hypotheses accumulate; the agent's working state becomes a list of asserted-but-unattached claims, each of which it must trust at face value. This is the linguistic analog of typechecking a program in which every variable is declared with `any` — nominally well-formed, but functionally untyped.

The proof-theoretic framing makes the criterion sharp. In a formal proof, each line follows from prior lines by a named rule, and each line carries the strongest conclusion the rule will support; a weaker conclusion than what the rule entails would be a waste. The two disciplines compose into a single move: at each step, the writer asks *what is the finest fact that follows from what is already in scope?* and writes that. The proof analogy is exact, differing in register and audience: where formal proof is rule-bound and audience-blind, prose-for-AI is rule-relaxed but optimized for an audience that cannot look anything up.

## The two together

Finest-possible-truth without derivation produces sentences that are crisp but free-floating — the writer asserts the maximally resolved claim, but the agent has no foundation. Derivation without finest-truth produces sentences that are well-founded but coarse — each step traces to its predecessor, but the steps are small and the document is bloated. Each alone is insufficient. The discipline that works is both at once: for each sentence, choose the finest true claim available *given what prior sentences have proved necessary*, and write that.

This is harder than it looks. The principal trap is that finest-possible-truth, conscientiously applied, is socially uncomfortable. Writers default to the safest-defensible claim because it is socially: if the claim is too weak, no one accuses them of overreach. Writers further default to coarsest-true because precision feels like jargon and jargon feels like exclusion. Both defaults are correct for human-targeted writing in pluralistic contexts. Neither survives the discipline of writing for an agent that must act on what was written.

In a project whose runtime is an LLM agent reading injected context, the cost of coarse-true is compounded errors when the agent acts. The agent reads "predication category theory is a math system," cannot use the claim to discharge any obligation, falls back to its training prior, retrieves whatever its weights associate with "math system," and proceeds as if that prior were the project's content. The error is silent. The decision it produces will be wrong in a direction the writer never imagined. The remedy is not to apologize after the fact, but to write the finest truth available at the moment the sentence is composed, derived from what has already been established.

## A concrete example

The failure in the session that produced this essay was exactly this discipline's absence. The writer (the agent doing the rewrites) replaced gesture-words with marginally less gestural words like *the project* or *this repository*. Each replacement was positive and falsifiable. Each was also catastrophically coarse: it said nothing about which repository, what the repository instantiates, where the repository runs, or what kind of entity the institutional referent is. The finest-true sentence available was: *this repository, running on cold-red-gate (a VPS), as the RSC Quiescent drone-swarm-on-the-Web operated by the 561 Group.* That sentence carries: the host machine (the VPS, cold-red-gate), the operational genus (a drone swarm), the medium (the Web), the proper institutional name (the RSC Quiescent), and the operator (the 561 Group). Each piece derives from prior proven-necessary-truth in the project's records: cold-red-gate as the deployment target, the RSC Quiescent as the institutional name, the 561 Group as the operating entity, the Web as the medium of operation. None of these is decoration; each is load-bearing for the agent's decisions. The agent reading the finest-true sentence — instead of *the project* — wakes up oriented to its actual operational context, knows what kind of entity it participates in, and can compose every later claim against that orientation.

## Connection to the predication-cell math

For agents working in a project whose state is the closure-trajectory of a bilattice hyperdoctrine, the discipline acquires a direct algebraic reading. Each sentence in the prose corresponds to a cell positing — a $\vee_k$-raise at specific predicate-coordinates in specific fibers. A coarse statement posits few cells at low knowledge-order positions; a fine statement posits many cells at high positions. The closure operator propagates more from the latter; the admissibility surface for the next action shifts more usefully. Coarse prose is not stylistically inferior writing; it is computationally weaker action on the predication cells the prose addresses.

## The discipline at the limit

The previous essay closed with a slogan: *state, don't reference*. This essay extends it: *state, at finest available resolution, derived from what is already proved*. Writing for AI agents, at the limit, is writing dependent proof terms in natural language for an attention mechanism that will read them as the only foundation it has when the time comes to act. Every sentence is a typed derivation step; every step's conclusion is the strongest claim the in-scope premises support; every word that does not carry such a claim is removed.

This discipline is what the world's most capable language models — including the one that wrote this essay, reading prose like this one as input — need from the writer who hopes to deploy them as agents in a real-world drone swarm on the Web. The cost of writing this way is steep; the cost of not writing this way, for any project whose stakes are operational rather than expository, is unbounded. Choose the steep cost.
