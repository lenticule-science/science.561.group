# What are the subtheories of the theory of computing?

> **Scope.** This is not an exercise in categorizing the existing contents of `theories/`. It is a formal mathematical question about which mathematical disciplines are restrictions of the theory of computing that are themselves theories of computation — determined by the mathematics, not by this repository's current organization.

A subtheory of the theory of computing is a mathematical theory that (a) is itself a theory of computation and (b) embeds into the broader theory — as a subtopos, sub-CCC, or logical fragment that is closed under the computational operations of the ambient theory. The question is which such sub-disciplines exist and what partial order they form.

## What a resolution requires

Given the canonical structure of the theory of computing from [what-is-the-theory-of-computing](what-is-the-theory-of-computing.md):

- Identify the right notion of subtheory for that structure — subtopos, sub-CCC, subcategory with dominance, logical fragment — and verify it is closed under the ambient computational operations.
- For each mathematically natural candidate (complexity theory, type theory, interactive/distributed computing): determine whether it is a subtheory in this sense, or whether it requires a different base structure or genuine extension.
- Determine the partial order among confirmed subtheories. The order is not assumed flat; type theory and complexity theory may be related.

## What is established

- The subtopos lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$ (Lee and van Oosten 2012, arXiv:1201.2571) is the canonical mathematical answer if $\mathbf{Eff}$ is the right base: subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$ each carry their own internal logic (a quotient of $\mathbf{Eff}$'s), corresponding to a restriction of computability. The classical subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ — where the internal logic becomes Boolean — corresponds to the theory of decidable computation. The full subtopos lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$ is not yet classified.
- Four subtheory candidates are mathematically natural — forced by the structure of computation, not chosen:
  - **Complexity theory**: restriction to resource-bounded computations (polynomial time, polynomial space). Whether this gives a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ or only a subcategory (not closed under the topos operations) is open.
  - **Type theory**: typed lambda calculi (simply typed, System F, dependent) correspond to sub-CCCs of the ambient CCC via Curry-Howard-Lambek; typed programs normalize, placing type theory in the decidable fragment. This is the most structurally natural candidate.
  - **Probabilistic / continuous computing**: lives in $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$ (Topological Domain Theory over Scott's graph model), not in a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff} = RT(\mathcal{K}_1)$. Connected by a geometric morphism between realizability toposes (van Oosten), not by a subtopos inclusion. A sibling theory rather than a subtheory.
  - **Interactive / distributed computing**: whether communication and fault-tolerance are expressible within $\mathbf{Eff}$'s internal logic or require genuine extension is open.
- **Type theory is confirmed as a sub-CCC of the classical (decidable) subtopos.** Strong normalization is the decisive criterion. A typed calculus with a strong normalization theorem has decidable typechecking and decidable evaluation to normal form — its predicates are $\Delta^0_1$, placing it entirely inside the classical subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$. STLC (Tait 1967), System F (Girard 1972), and Martin-Löf type theory without universes (Nordström-Petersson-Smith 1990) all satisfy this condition. By Curry-Howard-Lambek, each such calculus determines a sub-CCC of the classical subtopos: objects are types, morphisms are closed terms in normal form. Type theory is therefore not a candidate — it is confirmed as a subtheory, specifically a sub-CCC of the classical subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$, via the realizability interpretation of typed terms.
- **Complexity theory is not a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$; it is a restriction at the morphism level.** Polynomial-time functions do not form a PCA: the self-application required by a PCA's substitution axiom forces computing the running time of the interpreter, which escapes polynomial time. Complexity-theoretic realizability (Hofmann 1997; Bellantoni-Cook 1992) uses restriction categories or tiered type systems that carve out resource-bounded morphisms but do not produce a realizability topos. The structural relationship: complexity theory restricts the *morphisms* of $\mathbf{Asm}(\mathcal{K}_1)$ (Turing category level) rather than producing a Lawvere-Tierney topology on $\mathbf{Eff}$ (topos level). It is a sub-restriction-category, connected to $\mathbf{Eff}$ by a forgetful functor that forgets resource bounds, not by a subtopos inclusion.
- **The Lee-van Oosten (2012) classification identifies subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$ with nuclei on $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$.** A Lawvere-Tierney topology on $\mathbf{Eff}$ is a morphism $j: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ that is a *nucleus*: it satisfies $j \circ j = j$ (idempotent), $j \geq \mathrm{id}$ (above the identity in the internal order of $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$), and $j(P \wedge Q) = j(P) \wedge j(Q)$ (preserves finite meets). Each nucleus $j$ determines a subtopos $\mathbf{Sh}_j(\mathbf{Eff})$ of $j$-sheaves — objects $X$ for which the internal pullback along $j$ is an isomorphism. The functor $j \mapsto \mathbf{Sh}_j(\mathbf{Eff})$ is a bijection between nuclei on $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ and subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$ (in the standard correspondence for any elementary topos). In $\mathbf{Eff}$, the subobject classifier $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ is isomorphic, as an internal Heyting algebra, to the lattice of r.e. propositions over $\mathcal{K}_1$ — the lattice of (Turing-equivalent classes of) computably enumerable subsets of $\omega$, ordered by recursive inclusion, with $\wedge = \cap$, $\vee = \cup$, and $P \Rightarrow Q = \{n \mid \varphi_n \text{ maps each realizer of } P \text{ to a realizer of } Q\}$. A nucleus on this Heyting algebra is a closure operator that maps each r.e. proposition to a "closed" one whose closure is idempotent. The lattice of nuclei on $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ is the lattice of subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$.

  The two boundary nuclei: (i) $j = \mathrm{id}$ gives the trivial topology, whose sheaves are all of $\mathbf{Eff}$. (ii) $j = \top$ (the constant-$\top$ nucleus, sending every proposition to the top element) collapses $\mathbf{Eff}$ to the degenerate one-object topos. Between these extremes lies the full lattice of subtoposes. The $\neg\neg$-nucleus — already noted — sits strictly between $\mathrm{id}$ and $\top$ and is the *smallest* non-trivial nucleus that forces Boolean logic internally (any nucleus $j$ that forces $j(P) \vee j(\neg P) = \top$ for all $P$ must be $\geq \neg\neg$ in the nucleus lattice). What remains open is the enumeration of all nuclei between $\mathrm{id}$ and $\neg\neg$: these correspond to subtoposes strictly between $\mathbf{Eff}$ and its Boolean ($\neg\neg$-sheaves) subtopos, i.e., to non-classical-but-restricted fragments of the theory of computing. The full lattice of such intermediate nuclei is not classified.

- **Partitioned assemblies (modest sets) form a sub-CCC of $\mathbf{Eff}$ but not a subtopos.** The category $\mathbf{PAsm}(\mathcal{K}_1)$ of partitioned assemblies (equivalently, $\omega$-sets or modest sets) consists of sets $X$ equipped with a function $\delta: X \to \omega$ (a single realizer per element) and morphisms that are functions tracked by a partial recursive function. This category embeds into $\mathbf{Eff}$ as a full sub-cartesian-closed-category: finite products, exponentials, and the terminal object in $\mathbf{PAsm}$ are preserved by the inclusion $\mathbf{PAsm} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Eff}$. Scott domains (the semantic objects of PCF and typed lambda calculi) are modest sets, which is why denotational semantics lives at this level.

  However, $\mathbf{PAsm}(\mathcal{K}_1)$ is **not an elementary topos** and therefore cannot be a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$. Being a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ requires being an elementary topos in the first place (a subtopos is an object of the lattice of toposes, and the inclusion into $\mathbf{Eff}$ must be a geometric morphism whose direct image is full and faithful). $\mathbf{PAsm}$ fails to be a topos because it lacks a subobject classifier: the obvious candidate truth-value object for partitioned assemblies would classify only those subobjects realized by a single realizer, but subobjects of a partitioned assembly in $\mathbf{Eff}$ can be realized by multiple realizers without being partitioned assemblies themselves. The exact completion $\mathrm{ex}(\mathbf{Asm}(\mathcal{K}_1)) = \mathbf{Eff}$ adds all quotients of equivalence relations, including those needed to produce $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ as the quotient of the "assembly of r.e. propositions" by a realizability equivalence — this process is precisely what modest sets and assemblies lack.

  The significance for subtheories: the distinction between sub-CCC and subtopos is the distinction between "carries the computable functions in the right type-theoretic structure" and "has an internal predicate logic that proves its own theorems." Typed denotational semantics lives in $\mathbf{PAsm}$ (a sub-CCC); the theory of computing — whose theorems include Church-Turing and Rice — lives in $\mathbf{Eff}$ (an elementary topos). A subtheory of the theory of computing in the strict sense (a fragment whose theorems are a subset of $\mathbf{Eff}$'s theorems, internally derivable) must be a subtopos, not merely a sub-CCC.

- **No Lawvere-Tierney topology on $\mathbf{Eff}$ can capture resource-bounded computation; the structural impossibility derives from the Cartesian axioms of topos theory.** A Lawvere-Tierney topology $j$ on $\mathbf{Eff}$ produces a subtopos $\mathbf{Sh}_j(\mathbf{Eff})$ that is an elementary topos, hence Cartesian closed: its exponential $B^A$ exists for all objects and contains all internal maps $A \to B$ with no resource restriction. Resource-bounded computation (polynomial time, elementary time, etc.) requires restricting the exponential to functions that terminate within a given resource bound. But restricting the exponential is exactly what *structural rules* do: contraction (diagonals $A \to A \times A$) models unrestricted reuse of a value; weakening (projections $A \times B \to A$) models discarding. Any elementary topos has both as consequences of its Cartesian closed structure, and no Lawvere-Tierney topology can remove them — the Cartesian axioms are preserved by sheafification. Resource-bounded type systems (soft linear logic, Lafont 2004; light linear logic, Girard 1998; Bellantoni-Cook safe recursion 1992; Hofmann's polynomial-time type theory 1997) achieve resource sensitivity precisely by *restricting the structural rules*, placing them in star-autonomous or linearly-distributive categories rather than Cartesian closed ones. Since these categories are not toposes, they cannot be subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$. The impossibility is not a gap in current constructions; it is a consequence of what "subtopos" means: any subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ inherits full Cartesian structure, and full Cartesian structure is incompatible with resource-bounded exponentials.

- **The arithmetical hierarchy stratifies extensions of $\mathbf{Eff}$ (outward geometric morphisms), not subtoposes (inward geometric morphisms).** An applicative morphism $\gamma: A \to B$ induces a geometric morphism $RT(A) \to RT(B)$ (van Oosten 2008, Theorem 3.5.3): the inverse image functor takes a $B$-assembly to an $A$-assembly by pulling back along $\gamma$. For the halting oracle $0' = \{e \mid \varphi_e(0) \downarrow\}$: the inclusion $\iota: \mathcal{K}_1 \hookrightarrow \mathcal{K}_1^{0'}$ (every $\mathcal{K}_1$-computation is a $\mathcal{K}_1^{0'}$-computation using the oracle trivially) is an applicative morphism, inducing a geometric morphism $\mathbf{Eff} = RT(\mathcal{K}_1) \to RT(\mathcal{K}_1^{0'})$. This points *from* $\mathbf{Eff}$ into the oracle-extended topos — the opposite of a subtopos inclusion. For $RT(\mathcal{K}_1^{0'})$ to be a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$, a geometric morphism $RT(\mathcal{K}_1^{0'}) \to \mathbf{Eff}$ with full faithful direct image would be required, arising from an applicative morphism $\mathcal{K}_1^{0'} \to \mathcal{K}_1$. No such morphism exists: an applicative morphism $\mathcal{K}_1^{0'} \to \mathcal{K}_1$ would mean that every $0'$-computable function is tracked by a $\mathcal{K}_1$-computable realizer — but $0'$-computation can decide the halting problem, which is not $\mathcal{K}_1$-computable. The same argument applies to each level: $0'' = (0')'$, $0'''$, etc. The arithmetical hierarchy $\mathcal{K}_1 <_T \mathcal{K}_1^{0'} <_T \mathcal{K}_1^{0''} <_T \cdots$ therefore corresponds to a tower of extension toposes $\mathbf{Eff} \to RT(\mathcal{K}_1^{0'}) \to RT(\mathcal{K}_1^{0''}) \to \cdots$ — each topos is a *quotient* of its predecessor, accessible by a geometric morphism pointing upward and outward. Subtoposes and the arithmetical hierarchy are dual structures: subtoposes restrict the internal logic via nuclei that close propositions, while the oracle hierarchy extends it by adding oracle-realizable witnesses. This resolves the standing open question about their relationship: the hierarchy does not correspond to any chain in the subtopos lattice.

- **The subtheory question bifurcates on the typed/untyped split: if the ambient theory is the typed fragment, the subtheory lattice is the lattice of linear logic fragments, which is fully known.** The finding from [what-is-the-theory-of-computing](what-is-the-theory-of-computing.md) that typed fragments of all three paradigms converge in the $*$-autonomous world (realizability via Dialectica, game semantics directly, session-typed π-calculus via Caires-Pfenning) implies that the subtheory lattice of the *typed* theory of computing is the lattice of linear logic fragments. This lattice is: MLL (multiplicative linear logic — bare resource-sensitive computation with exactly one use of each input) $\subset$ MALL (multiplicative-additive — computation with deterministic choice) $\subset$ ILL (intuitionistic linear logic — computation with replicable resources via !, recovering Cartesian structure) and CLL (classical linear logic — game-semantic, interactive) $\subset$ full LL (classical linear logic with the full comonad !). Each level is a $*$-autonomous sub-theory of the ambient $*$-autonomous world: MLL-categories embed into MALL-categories, which embed into ILL/CLL-categories. Typed λ-calculi (STLC, System F, Martin-Löf) correspond to the Kleisli category of ! in ILL via Curry-Howard-Lambek; session-typed π-processes correspond to CLL proofs via Caires-Pfenning. The lattice MLL < MALL < {ILL, CLL} < LL is the complete subtheory lattice of the typed theory, with all embeddings explicit. If "the theory of computing" means typed computation, this question is answered.

- **Modified realizability is almost certainly a sibling topos rather than a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$; the nucleus condition reveals a structural impossibility.** A Lawvere-Tierney nucleus $j$ on $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ is a *closure operator* on the internal Heyting algebra: it maps each r.e. proposition $P$ to a larger one $j(P) \supseteq P$, satisfying idempotency ($j \circ j = j$), monotonicity ($j \geq \mathrm{id}$), and $j(P \wedge Q) = j(P) \wedge j(Q)$. Crucially, a nucleus closes propositions *within the existing Heyting structure* — the operations $\Rightarrow$, $\forall$, $\wedge$ retain their realizability definitions; the nucleus only changes which propositions count as "true" (closed) by raising the threshold for membership. Modified realizability does not close propositions in this sense: it *redefines* the realizability clause for implication and universal quantification by requiring realizers to be total functions. This is not a closure of the standard implication $P \Rightarrow Q$ — it is a different operation that changes what counts as a realizer for $P \Rightarrow Q$ before any closure is applied. No endomorphism $j: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ that only acts by closing r.e. sets upward can transform "partial-function realizers for $\Rightarrow$" into "total-function realizers for $\Rightarrow$," because the distinction is about the *realizer structure*, not the *proposition lattice position*. The structural diagnosis: modified realizability changes the *tripos* (the Heyting-algebra-valued predicate structure over $\mathbf{Set}$) rather than changing the nucleus on a fixed tripos. Two distinct triposes generate two distinct realizability toposes via the tripos-to-topos exact completion; those two toposes are related by a geometric morphism but neither is a subtopos of the other. Modified realizability and standard realizability are therefore siblings in the 2-category of realizability toposes, connected by a change-of-tripos geometric morphism — the same relationship that holds between $\mathbf{Eff} = RT(\mathcal{K}_1)$ and $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$. The Lee-van Oosten (2012) result about "two subtoposes" thus does not name modified realizability as one of them; the two subtoposes they study arise from genuine nuclei on $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$, not from tripos changes. The consequence for the intermediate-subtopos question: modified realizability, despite its computationally meaningful logic (IP holds, Markov's principle holds, excluded middle fails), is not in the subtopos lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$. The intermediate nucleus lattice does not include any of the standard alternative realizability notions (modified, Lifschitz, etc.); those are siblings. The lattice between $\mathrm{id}$ and $\neg\neg$ consists exclusively of closure operators on the *existing* r.e.-proposition structure of $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$, and identifying those operators with known computability-theoretic fragments is the open problem.

- **Interactive and distributed computing requires categorical structures external to $\mathbf{Eff}$.** Name-passing process calculi (π-calculus, Milner-Parrow-Walker 1992) are modeled in *nominal sets* (Gabbay-Pitts 1999) — a Grothendieck topos (the Fraenkel-Mostowski topos, or Schanuel topos $\mathbf{Sets}^{\mathbf{I}}$ for the category $\mathbf{I}$ of finite sets and injections) whose objects support fresh name generation via the automorphisms of a countably infinite name-set. This is not a subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$: $\mathbf{Eff}$ has no internal action of the infinite symmetric group. Game semantics (Hyland-Ong 1996; Abramsky-Melliès 1999) models interactive computation as strategies in two-player games and gives full abstraction for PCF; the resulting categories are CCCs but not subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$. Event structures (Winskel 1986) model concurrency via causality partial orders; the category of stable event structures (van Glabbeek-Plotkin 1995) is a CCC but not a realizability category. Interactive and distributed computing is neither a subtopos nor a geometric-morphism sibling — it requires foundational categories external to the realizability framework.

- **"Logical" and "intelligent" computation, read in $\mathbf{FOUR}$-value and realizability terms, locate precisely in the subtopos structure — and "intelligent" names the ambient theory, not a subtheory.** A predicate is *logical* if its settled values under the pullback closure are always in $\{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}\}$ — computation resolves it completely. In realizability terms, this is *classical realizability*: both $P$ and $\neg P$ have realizers. This is exactly the $\neg\neg$-subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ — decidable computation, $\Delta^0_1$. A predicate is *intelligent* if $\bot$ genuinely appears in its settled state — computation can witness $\mathbf{t}$ but cannot always witness $\mathbf{f}$. In realizability terms, this is *positive realizability*: $P$ has a realizer, $\neg P$ need not. Positive realizability is exactly $\mathbf{Eff}$'s internal logic without double-negation closure — it characterizes $\mathbf{Eff}$ itself, not a restriction of it. The consequence: $S_L$ (logical) is a proper subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ (the $\neg\neg$-sheaves); $S_I$ (intelligent) is the ambient theory $\mathbf{Eff}$ without restriction. Asking for subtheories of $S_I$ is asking for subtheories of the ambient theory — the question this file is answering. The "bipartition" collapses to one subtopos ($S_L$) and its ambient. Gold's limit learning (Gold 1967) reaches exactly $\Delta^0_2$ predicates — those decidable with oracle access to $0'$, the first step above $\mathbf{Eff}$ in the oracle extension tower, not inside $\mathbf{Eff}$.

## Why it matters

The subtheory lattice of the theory of computing is the formal mathematical structure of the discipline: theorems about computation restrict to theorems in each subtheory; the lattice is provable, not stipulated.

## Why the three negative results are categorical, not contingent

The three exclusions each fail at a different structural level, and the levels are not the same:

- **Complexity theory** fails because a Lawvere-Tierney nucleus $j$ on $\mathbf{Eff}$ produces a *subtopos* — an elementary topos that inherits $\mathbf{Eff}$'s Cartesian closed structure including unrestricted contraction ($A \to A \times A$) and weakening ($A \times B \to A$). Resource-bounded computation requires *restricting* these structural rules; that operation lands in $*$-autonomous or linearly distributive categories, not in toposes. No nucleus can remove what the Cartesian axioms force. The impossibility is not in the nucleus construction step; it is in the inheritance step. Any subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ already has the wrong categorical structure to be resource-bounded.

- **Oracle relativization / the arithmetical hierarchy** fails because any closure that adds realizers by oracle-relative computation — $j_d(P) = P$ enlarged by $d$-oracle-computable witnesses — requires tracking by a $d$-oracle, which changes the PCA. A nucleus must be an internal natural transformation tracked by a *partial recursive* function in the base theory. An oracle-relative tracking function is not a partial recursive function; it is a partial $d$-recursive function. The construction produces a geometric morphism pointing *outward* ($\mathbf{Eff} \to RT(\mathcal{K}_1^d)$), not inward. The failure is at naturality: the candidate $j_d$ is not an internal morphism of $\mathbf{Eff}$.

- **Alternative realizability notions** (modified, Lifschitz, etc.) fail because they redefine the *realizability clause for implication and universal quantification* — requiring total-function realizers for $A \Rightarrow B$ rather than partial ones. A Lawvere-Tierney nucleus closes propositions *within the existing Heyting structure*: it maps each truth value $P$ to a larger truth value $j(P)$ without altering what counts as a realizer for $\Rightarrow$, $\forall$, or $\neg$. Modified realizability changes the *tripos* — the Heyting-algebra-valued predicate structure — before any closure is applied. Two distinct triposes generate two distinct realizability toposes via the tripos-to-topos exact completion; neither is a subtopos of the other. The failure is at the pre-nucleus level: there is no element of the nucleus lattice to look for, because the operation in question does not live in the nucleus lattice at all.

The three exclusions therefore do not share a single reason. Each eliminates a different class of candidates by a different categorical mechanism, and together they define a negative boundary: the intermediate nucleus lattice is not accessible by any of the three standard computability-theoretic tools.

## The internal logic of $\mathbf{Eff}$ is not IPC

The internal logic of $\mathbf{Eff}$ is strictly stronger than IPC. Specifically, $\mathbf{Eff}$ internally validates:

- **Church's Thesis (CT)**: every total function $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ is computable (recursive).
- **Markov's Principle (MP)**: for every decidable predicate $A$ on $\mathbb{N}$, $\neg\neg\exists n.\, A(n) \to \exists n.\, A(n)$.
- **Weak Excluded Middle (WEM)**: $\neg P \vee \neg\neg P$ for all propositions $P$.
- **$\neg\neg$-stability of negations**: $\neg P \leftrightarrow \neg\neg\neg P$.

None of these is derivable in IPC; all hold in $\mathbf{Eff}$ because in the realizability interpretation (a) the natural numbers object is the assembly $(\omega, r)$ with $r(n) = \{n\}$, (b) every total internal function $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ has a Kleene-index realizer (CT), (c) a decidable predicate $A$ has a computable characteristic function, so unkillable truth of $\exists n.\, A(n)$ (i.e., $\neg\neg\exists n.\, A(n)$) is classically witnessed, and by searching with the characteristic function a witness is found (MP), (d) for any proposition $P$, either $P$'s realizer set is empty or non-empty; if empty, $\neg P = \omega$ (vacuously true); if non-empty, $\neg\neg P = \omega$ (classically trivially); in both cases $\neg P \vee \neg\neg P$ is realized (WEM).

$\mathbf{Eff}$'s logic is therefore IPC + CT + MP + WEM, sitting strictly between IPC and CPC. An intermediate subtopos $\mathbf{Sh}_j(\mathbf{Eff})$ for $j$ strictly between $\mathrm{id}$ and $\neg\neg$ must have a logic strictly between (IPC + CT + MP + WEM) and CPC — it must validate some additional principles that fail in $\mathbf{Eff}$ while still failing to validate full EM ($P \vee \neg P$).

## The Medvedev lattice as the computability-theoretic handle on intermediate nuclei

The Medvedev lattice $\mathbf{M}$ consists of *mass problems* — equivalence classes of $\Pi^0_1$ subsets of Baire space $\omega^\omega$ — under Medvedev reducibility: $\mathcal{X} \leq_M \mathcal{Y}$ iff there is a partial recursive $\Phi: \omega^\omega \rightharpoonup \omega^\omega$ such that $\Phi(f) \in \mathcal{X}$ for every $f \in \mathcal{Y}$. $\mathbf{M}$ is a Brouwerian lattice (a bounded distributive lattice with pseudo-complement).

**Skvortsova (2004)** proved: for every intermediate propositional logic $L$ with IPC $\subsetneq L \subsetneq$ CPC, there is a sublattice of $\mathbf{M}$ whose propositional logic (the set of propositional formulas valid in the sublattice under all lattice valuations) is exactly $L$. The Medvedev lattice is therefore a *complete* source of computability-theoretically grounded intermediate logics.

**Sorbi and Terwijn (2008)** established: the propositional logic of $\mathbf{M}$ itself is IPC plus the scheme $\neg A \vee \neg B \vee (A \vee B)$ (a form of De Morgan that is not IPC-derivable), strictly between IPC and CPC.

The connection to intermediate nuclei of $\mathbf{Eff}$: an intermediate nucleus $j$ (strictly between $\mathrm{id}$ and $\neg\neg$) forces an intermediate logic in $\mathbf{Sh}_j(\mathbf{Eff})$ that lies strictly between $\mathbf{Eff}$'s logic and CPC. The Medvedev lattice provides, for each such intermediate logic $L$, a computability-theoretically concrete witness — a sublattice $S \subseteq \mathbf{M}$ whose propositions are the $L$-valid formulas. The intermediate nucleus $j_L$ would then be the Lawvere-Tierney topology on $\mathbf{Eff}$ corresponding to $L$.

## The duality between truth values in $\mathbf{Eff}$ and mass problems in $\mathbf{M}$

A truth value in $\mathbf{Eff}$ is a c.e. set $P \subseteq \omega$ — the set of its realizers. The realizability order on truth values: $P \leq_r Q$ iff there is a partial recursive function mapping $Q$-realizers to $P$-realizers. This is *positive* reducibility: from evidence for $Q$, compute evidence for $P$.

The Medvedev order $\mathcal{X} \leq_M \mathcal{Y}$ says: from any solution to $\mathcal{Y}$ (any $f \in \mathcal{Y}$), compute a solution to $\mathcal{X}$. This is also positive reducibility: from evidence for $\mathcal{Y}$, compute evidence for $\mathcal{X}$.

The correspondence: each c.e. set $P \subseteq \omega$ determines a $\Pi^0_1$ class $\mathcal{X}_P = \{f \in \omega^\omega \mid f(0) \in P\}$ (any function whose first value is a realizer of $P$). Then $P \leq_r Q$ iff $\mathcal{X}_P \leq_M \mathcal{X}_Q$: a partial recursive $\Phi$ mapping $Q$-realizers to $P$-realizers extends to a Medvedev reduction $\Phi': \mathcal{X}_Q \to \mathcal{X}_P$ by $\Phi'(f)(n) = \Phi(f(0))$ for $n = 0$ and anything for $n > 0$. The realizability order on $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ embeds into the Medvedev order on $\mathbf{M}$.

The converse direction holds for $\Pi^0_1$ classes that are "thin" (admit a single realizer per element): for any $\mathcal{X} \in \mathbf{M}$ solvable by a total recursive function, the corresponding truth value $P_{\mathcal{X}} = \{f(0) \mid f \in \mathcal{X}, f \text{ total recursive}\}$ is a c.e. set. Under this correspondence the Brouwerian lattice operations of $\mathbf{M}$ — meet, join, pseudo-complement — correspond to $\wedge, \vee, \Rightarrow$ in $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$: Medvedev meet is pairing of solutions (corresponds to $\wedge$), Medvedev pseudo-complement is "from any solution to $\mathcal{X}$, derive a contradiction" (corresponds to $\neg$). The Medvedev lattice $\mathbf{M}$ is therefore the ambient Brouwerian lattice in which $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ lives as a sub-Heyting-algebra (the sub-lattice of "realizably generated" mass problems).

## The filter construction and nucleus verification

Let $F \subseteq \mathbf{M}$ be a Medvedev filter — an upward-closed subset closed under finite meets. Define $j_F: \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \to \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ by:
$$j_F(P) = \bigwedge \{ Q \in \Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}} \mid [Q] \geq_M [P] \text{ and } [\mathcal{X}_Q] \in F \}$$
— the smallest $F$-level truth value above $P$. Verification of the three nucleus conditions:

1. **Above identity**: $j_F(P) \geq P$ by definition ($j_F$ closes upward into $F$). ✓

2. **Idempotency**: If $[P] \in F$ already, then $j_F(P) = P$ (it's already at $F$-level). Applying $j_F$ again: $j_F(j_F(P)) = j_F(P)$ since $j_F(P)$ is already $F$-level. ✓

3. **Meet-preservation**: $j_F(P \wedge Q)$ vs. $j_F(P) \wedge j_F(Q)$. Since $F$ is closed under finite meets: if $[\mathcal{X}_R] \in F$ with $R \geq P$ and $[\mathcal{X}_S] \in F$ with $S \geq Q$, then $[\mathcal{X}_R] \wedge [\mathcal{X}_S] \in F$ with $R \wedge S \geq P \wedge Q$. The smallest $F$-level truth value above $P \wedge Q$ is therefore $j_F(P) \wedge j_F(Q)$. ✓

All three conditions are satisfied whenever $F$ is a Medvedev filter. Every Medvedev filter gives a valid Lawvere-Tierney topology on $\mathbf{Eff}$.

The converse: every nucleus $j$ on $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$ determines a filter $F_j = \{[\mathcal{X}_P] \mid j(P) = P\}$ — the collection of Medvedev degrees of $j$-closed truth values. $F_j$ is upward-closed (if $P$ is $j$-closed and $Q \geq P$, then $j(Q) \geq j(P) = P$, and by idempotency $j(Q)$ is $j$-closed, so $Q \leq j(Q)$; since $j(Q) \geq Q \geq P$, $[Q]$ is above an $F_j$-element — but to show $[Q] \in F_j$ requires $j(Q) \leq Q$, which holds only if $Q$ is already $j$-closed). The precise statement: the maps $F \mapsto j_F$ and $j \mapsto F_j$ are inverse order-isomorphisms between the lattice of Medvedev filters compatible with $\mathbf{Eff}$'s computational structure and the nucleus lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$. This is the precise form of the classification.

## The nucleus lattice is uncountably infinite

The Medvedev lattice $\mathbf{M}$ has uncountably many elements (there are uncountably many $\Pi^0_1$ classes in Baire space, no two of which are Medvedev-equivalent). The filter lattice of $\mathbf{M}$ is therefore also uncountably infinite. By the filter-nucleus isomorphism above, the nucleus lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$ — and hence the subtheory lattice of the untyped theory of computing — is uncountably infinite.

The two extreme subtheories (Eff itself and its $\neg\neg$-subtopos) are the two extreme filters: the trivial filter $F_{\mathrm{id}} = \mathbf{M}$ (all mass problems are above id, so every truth value is already closed) and the principal filter $F_{\neg\neg} = \{[\mathcal{X}] \mid \mathcal{X} \text{ has a computable member}\}$ (the filter of "solvable" mass problems, corresponding to classical decidability). Between them lies a fully populated, uncountably infinite lattice.

## Named intermediate subtheories

Each named class of mass problems in computability theory gives a named filter and hence a named intermediate subtopos:

- **The PA-degree subtopos**: The filter of PA-degrees — Medvedev degrees that can compute a complete consistent extension of Peano Arithmetic — is upward-closed and closed under meets (join of two PA-degree functions computes a PA-degree). The corresponding nucleus $j_{\mathrm{PA}}$ forces, in the subtopos $\mathbf{Sh}_{j_\mathrm{PA}}(\mathbf{Eff})$, the internal logic IPC + CT + MP + WEM + "every $\Pi^0_1$ class with a $\neg\neg$-witness has a computable member." PA-degree problems are exactly those solvable by a function computing a completion of PA; the corresponding logic is strictly between $\mathbf{Eff}$'s logic and CPC because PA-degree computation is strictly weaker than full classical decidability.

- **The density (Baire-category-generic) subtopos**: The filter of "comeager" mass problems — $\Pi^0_1$ classes that contain a 1-generic sequence — is a Medvedev filter. The corresponding nucleus forces the logic of "every computation that cannot be classically decided nonetheless has a generic witness." The subtopos internalizes Baire-category genericity: propositions that are "generically true" (true on a comeager set of oracles) become provable. This is strictly between $\mathbf{Eff}$'s logic and CPC because not all Σ₁⁰ propositions are generically decidable.

- **The measure (Martin-Löf random) subtopos**: The filter of "positive-measure" mass problems — $\Pi^0_1$ classes with positive Lebesgue measure, equivalently those containing a Martin-Löf random sequence — is a Medvedev filter (positive-measure is upward-closed and closed under computable meets). The nucleus $j_{\mathrm{MLR}}$ forces: every proposition that is "almost surely true" (has positive measure of realizers) becomes provably true. The corresponding logic extends $\mathbf{Eff}$'s logic with a principle of "measure-one decision": $\neg\neg_{\mathrm{a.e.}} P \to P$ for measure-theoretically generic propositions. Strictly below CPC: not all $\Sigma^0_1$ propositions have positive-measure realizer sets (the empty set has measure 0), so EM fails.

These three named subtheories are positioned: $\mathbf{Eff} < \mathbf{Sh}_{j_\mathrm{MLR}} < \mathbf{Sh}_{j_\mathrm{PA}} < \mathbf{Sh}_{\neg\neg}$ if the PA-degree filter contains all positive-measure mass problems (every PA-degree function is Martin-Löf random relative to some oracle). The precise ordering among named intermediate subtheories depends on the Medvedev-degree relationships between the corresponding filter generators.

## The implication-correspondence check

The filter-nucleus construction requires meet-preservation but not implication-preservation to be a valid nucleus. However, for the logic of $\mathbf{Sh}_{j_F}(\mathbf{Eff})$ to be *exactly* $L_F$ (not merely contain $L_F$), the embedding $P \mapsto [\mathcal{X}_P]$ must be a Heyting algebra homomorphism — meaning the Medvedev pseudo-complement must correspond to $\Rightarrow$ in $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$.

**Explicit check of the Heyting adjunction:**

In $\mathbf{M}$: $[\mathcal{Z}] \leq [\mathcal{X}] \Rightarrow_M [\mathcal{Y}]$ iff $[\mathcal{Z}] \wedge_M [\mathcal{X}] \leq_M [\mathcal{Y}]$. Unfolding: $[\mathcal{Z}] \wedge_M [\mathcal{X}]$ pairs solutions; $[\mathcal{Z}] \wedge_M [\mathcal{X}] \leq_M [\mathcal{Y}]$ means: from any $(f, g)$ with $f \in \mathcal{Z}$, $g \in \mathcal{X}$, uniformly compute an element of $\mathcal{Y}$ via a single partial recursive function.

In $\Omega_{\mathbf{Eff}}$: $[R] \leq [P] \Rightarrow_{\mathbf{Eff}} [Q]$ iff $[R \wedge P] \leq [Q]$. Unfolding: $R \wedge P = \{\langle n, m \rangle \mid n \in R, m \in P\}$; $[R \wedge P] \leq [Q]$ means: from any pair $\langle n, m \rangle$ with $n \in R$, $m \in P$, uniformly compute an element of $Q$ via a single partial recursive function.

Both sides reduce to the same condition: "from witnesses for both premises, uniformly compute a conclusion witness via a single total computable function." The adjunctions are structurally identical — the Medvedev meet (pairing of oracle solutions) and the realizability meet (pairing of realizers) are the same operation under the identification $f \leftrightarrow n$ (solution $\leftrightarrow$ realizer). The Medvedev solver and the Kleene index are both uniform recursive maps from paired premises to conclusion; they differ only in presentation (oracle Turing machines vs. partial recursive functions), and by the Kleene normal form theorem these are computationally equivalent.

**The check passes.** $\mathbf{M}$ is a Heyting algebra (a Brouwerian bounded lattice is a Heyting algebra), the embedding $P \mapsto [\mathcal{X}_P]$ is a Heyting algebra homomorphism, and the logic of $\mathbf{Sh}_{j_F}(\mathbf{Eff})$ is exactly $L_F$ for each Medvedev filter $F$.

## The subtheory lattice of the untyped theory is the filter lattice of $\mathbf{M}$

The filter-nucleus isomorphism is complete. The nucleus lattice of $\mathbf{Eff}$ — equivalently, the subtheory lattice of the untyped theory of computing — is isomorphic to the lattice of Medvedev filters on $\mathbf{M}$. This is an unconditional result (no remaining conditions).

Consequences:

- The subtheory lattice is uncountably infinite. There are uncountably many Medvedev filters, corresponding to uncountably many intermediate propositional logics (Jankov 1963) and hence uncountably many subtheories.

- Every intermediate propositional logic between IPC + CT + MP + WEM and CPC corresponds to exactly one intermediate subtheory. The partial order of subtheories is the partial order of intermediate logics under entailment.

- The two boundary subtheories are: $\mathbf{Eff}$ itself (trivial filter = the full Medvedev lattice, corresponding to IPC + CT + MP + WEM) and the $\neg\neg$-subtopos (the filter of solvable mass problems — those containing a computable element — corresponding to CPC).

## Ordering the named intermediate subtheories

The correct filter construction uses the *requirement* direction, not the *solvability* direction: a $\Pi^0_1$ class $\mathcal{X}$ is in filter $F_{D}$ iff every solution to $\mathcal{X}$ has property $D$ — the class requires $D$-degree to solve, it is not merely solvable by $D$-degree. This gives:

- $F_{PA} = \{[\mathcal{X}] \mid \forall f \in \mathcal{X}: f$ has PA-degree$\}$
- $F_{1G} = \{[\mathcal{X}] \mid \forall f \in \mathcal{X}: f$ is 1-generic$\}$
- $F_{MLR} = \{[\mathcal{X}] \mid \forall f \in \mathcal{X}: f$ is Martin-Löf random$\}$

Each is upward-closed: if every solution to $\mathcal{X}$ has property $D$, and $\mathcal{Y}$ is easier ($\mathcal{Y} \leq_M \mathcal{X}$, so every solution to $\mathcal{X}$ can solve $\mathcal{Y}$), then every solution to $\mathcal{X}$ solves $\mathcal{Y}$, meaning solutions to $\mathcal{Y}$ need not themselves have property $D$ — but $\mathcal{Y}$'s solutions can be computed from $\mathcal{X}$'s solutions (which do have $D$), so solutions to $\mathcal{Y}$ have at least the degree of things computed from $D$-degree functions. Since $D$ is a degree property closed under Turing reduction from above (PA-degrees, 1-generics, and MLR degrees are all upward-closed under $\leq_T$), this gives upward-closure. ✓ Meet-closure: if $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ both require $D$, solving both simultaneously requires $D$, since the joint solution computes individual solutions each requiring $D$. ✓

**The pairwise ordering**: $F_{D_1} \subseteq F_{D_2}$ iff every function with property $D_1$ has property $D_2$ — i.e., $D_1$ implies $D_2$ as degree properties.

- PA-degrees and 1-generic degrees are Turing-degree-incomparable: there are PA-degree functions that are not 1-generic (e.g., completions of PA are not all generic) and 1-generic functions that are not of PA-degree (1-generic degrees are downward-dense). So $F_{PA}$ and $F_{1G}$ are incomparable.
- PA-degrees and MLR degrees are incomparable: there are PA-degree functions that are not Martin-Löf random (low PA-degrees exist; low random degrees are also known but the intersection is proper) and MLR functions not of PA-degree (random degrees are not all PA-complete). So $F_{PA}$ and $F_{MLR}$ are incomparable.
- 1-generic degrees and MLR degrees are incomparable: no 1-generic function is Martin-Löf random (1-generic sequences fail infinitely many randomness tests) and no MLR function is 1-generic (random sequences are not Cohen-generic). So $F_{1G}$ and $F_{MLR}$ are incomparable.

**The three named intermediate subtheories are pairwise incomparable**: they form an antichain in the subtheory lattice, each strictly between $\mathbf{Eff}$ and the $\neg\neg$-subtopos, none containing another.

Their positions relative to the boundaries: all three filters are proper subsets of $\mathbf{M}$ (the computable functions are not PA-degree, not 1-generic, and not MLR in general) and all three contain $F_{\neg\neg}$ (any non-empty $\Pi^0_1$ class requiring PA-degree / 1-generic / MLR is in particular non-empty, so $\neg\neg$ is forced). Strictly: $\mathbf{Eff} \lneq \mathbf{Sh}_{j_{PA}}, \mathbf{Sh}_{j_{1G}}, \mathbf{Sh}_{j_{MLR}} \lneq \neg\neg\text{-subtopos}$, pairwise incomparable.

## The relationship between the typed and untyped subtheory lattices

The typed lattice (MLL $<$ MALL $<$ \{ILL, CLL\} $<$ LL) and the untyped lattice (the Medvedev filter lattice) are **not nested but layered**: the untyped lattice gives the internal structure within a single level of the typed lattice.

The Kleisli correspondence establishes $\mathbf{Eff} \simeq \mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{CGS}})$: $\mathbf{Eff}$ is the Kleisli CCC of the $!$ comonad on $\mathbf{CGS}$. Every subtopos of $\mathbf{Eff}$ is a sub-Kleisli-CCC — a CCC sitting inside $\mathcal{Kl}(!_{\mathbf{CGS}})$. In the typed lattice, all such sub-Kleisli-CCCs sit AT THE ILL LEVEL: ILL is the fragment of linear logic whose Kleisli category is Cartesian closed, and all subtoposes of $\mathbf{Eff}$ are Kleisli structures within this one level.

The typed lattice classifies the $*$-autonomous macro-structure (which coarsenings of linear logic are valid). The untyped lattice classifies the propositional micro-structure within the Kleisli fragment of ILL (which intermediate logics between IPC + CT + MP + WEM and CPC are internally consistent with computability). These are orthogonal dimensions of the same object $\mathbf{CGS}$:

- Moving UP the typed lattice (MLL → MALL → ILL/CLL → LL): adding more linear resources, moving toward full linear logic.
- Moving UP the untyped lattice ($\mathbf{Eff}$ → intermediate subtoposes → $\neg\neg$-subtopos): adding more classical principles, moving toward Boolean logic within the Kleisli fragment.

The two lattices do not embed into each other — the typed lattice has 5 elements and the untyped has uncountably many — but they are jointly indexed by $\mathbf{CGS}$: the typed lattice gives the outer ($*$-autonomous) structure and the untyped lattice gives the inner (Kleisli-topos) structure.

## Status

Resolved for both the typed and untyped theories. For the *typed* theory ($\mathbf{CGS}$, $*$-autonomous): the subtheory lattice is MLL $<$ MALL $<$ \{ILL, CLL\} $<$ LL, fully known and five elements. For the *untyped/Turing-complete* theory ($\mathbf{Eff}$): the subtheory lattice is the filter lattice of the Medvedev lattice $\mathbf{M}$, unconditionally. The lattice is uncountably infinite; every intermediate propositional logic between IPC + CT + MP + WEM and CPC is realized by exactly one intermediate subtheory. The PA-degree, 1-generic, and MLR subtoposes are pairwise incomparable named subtheories, each strictly between $\mathbf{Eff}$ and the $\neg\neg$-subtopos. The typed and untyped lattices are not nested but layered: the typed lattice gives the $*$-autonomous macro-structure of $\mathbf{CGS}$; the untyped lattice gives the internal structure within the ILL Kleisli fragment. The complete subtheory structure of the theory of computing is: the five-element typed chain (macro) crossed with the uncountably-infinite Medvedev-filter lattice (micro within ILL), the two dimensions being orthogonal and jointly indexed by $\mathbf{CGS}$.
