## Pure form

An endeavor is striving with commitment. The defining predication: `(endeavor, has-aim, aim-description)` together with `(endeavor, committed-by, agent)`. The committing agent is the locus of the commitment — without a determinate committer, there is activity but not endeavor.

An endeavor has five constitutive parts: a committing agent, a directed aim, self-commitment, sustained exertion, and failure-openness. The aim need not be fully specified in advance — endeavors can be aim-open, discovering what they strive toward through the striving.

## Characteristic predication-shape

- `has-aim(endeavor, aim-description)` — the orientation; the individuating predication.
- `committed-by(endeavor, agent | ship | polity)` — the unified locus of commitment.
- `specified-by(endeavor, charter)` — the charter that constitutes the commitment (when chartered).
- `has-temporal-kind(endeavor, {aim-terminated | indefinite | aim-open})` — whether the endeavor has an endpoint, persists indefinitely, or self-modifies as it advances.
- `pursued-by(endeavor, captain | crew)` — the office or persons carrying the striving.
- `has-status(endeavor, {active | succeeded | failed | abandoned})` — current lifecycle state.

## Self-commissioning

No external sender is required. The commitment is self-generated. This distinguishes endeavor from [mission](mission.md) (which requires being commissioned) and from duty (which derives from a position in a normative system).

## Normative weight on the striving

The commitment creates accountability for *trying*, not just for *achieving*. An endeavor can be failed by insufficient striving even when the world would have cooperated. This is what distinguishes endeavor from mere effort.

## Failure-openness

The aim is not guaranteed by the endeavoring. Without failure-openness, the activity is a procedure, not an endeavor — a procedure's output is structurally guaranteed when its preconditions hold, while an endeavor's outcome remains contingent on circumstance.
