# Relation between the two "bigraded" structures: cohomology bigrading vs common-knowledge bigrading

The corpus contains **two distinct bigraded structures**, both natural and both called "bigraded," exploiting different two-axis features of the predication hyperdoctrine. They are *not* the same construction, and the relation between them is open structural content.

The naming clash is real but the underlying mathematics is genuinely two different things, both surfaced from the predication hyperdoctrine's two parallel adjoint systems.

## The two structures

**(I) Bigraded settling cohomology** — the corpus's existing bigrading, indexed by *evidential degree*.

For a predication settling $v$ with [paraconsistent settling-associated sheaf](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/paraconsistent-settling-associated-sheaf/paraconsistent-settling-associated-sheaf.md) admitting the splitting $\mathcal{F}_U^{(k)} \cong \mathcal{F}_U^{(t)} \oplus \mathcal{F}_U^{(\top)}$, the [bigraded settling cohomology](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/content/bigraded-settling-cohomology/bigraded-settling-cohomology.md) is

$$H^{m, n}_{k, t}(\mathbf{T}^*, \mathcal{F}_U) := H^n\bigl(H^m\bigl(C^{\bullet, \bullet}_{k, t}(\mathbf{T}^*, \mathcal{F}_U)\bigr)\bigr)$$

with $m \in \{0, 1\}$ tracking consistent ($\mathcal{F}_U^{(t)}$) vs paraconsistent ($\mathcal{F}_U^{(\top)}$) components and $n$ the cohomological degree. The [truth-knowledge cohomology long exact sequence](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/references/truth-knowledge-cohomology-long-exact-sequence.md) (proved) characterizes this bigrading via the standard homological algebra of the sheaf splitting.

This bigrading lives at the **sheaf cohomology** layer.

**(II) Bigraded common knowledge** — the May 2026 bigrading from Pursuit 1, indexed by *bilattice order*.

For a [bilattice-valued multi-agent Kripke frame](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/settling-bound/content/world-set/content/bilattice-accessibility-relation/bilattice-accessibility-relation.md), the [bigraded common knowledge theorem](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/theorems/bigraded-common-knowledge.md) (sketch) defines two independent greatest-fixed-point operators

$$C^t \phi := \mathrm{gfp}_{\leq_t}\, \psi.\, \phi \wedge_t E^t \psi, \qquad C^k \phi := \mathrm{gfp}_{\leq_k}\, \psi.\, \phi \wedge_k E^k \psi$$

— *truth-axis* common knowledge (gfp in $\leq_t$) and *knowledge-axis* common knowledge (gfp in $\leq_k$). On benchmark-5: $C^t p$ and $C^k p$ have *different bottoms*, yielding $(\mathbf{f}, \mathbf{f}, \mathbf{f})$ vs $(\bot, \bot, \bot)$ uniformly.

This bigrading lives at the **modal operator** layer.

## The shared structural ingredient

Both bigradings exploit the [predication hyperdoctrine](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/objects/predication-hyperdoctrine.md)'s **two parallel adjoint systems**: $\exists_f^k \dashv f^* \dashv \forall_f^k$ in the knowledge order AND $\exists_f^t \dashv f^* \dashv \forall_f^t$ in the truth order, with both being properties (not added structure) per [Kelly-Lack 1997 property-like-ness](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/predication-kelly-lack1997.md).

The cohomology bigrading uses this two-axis structure to split the *coefficient sheaf* into $\leq_t$-only and $\top$-witness components.

The common-knowledge bigrading uses the two-axis structure to define *modal operators* with two independent fixed points.

## What's known about each

### About the cohomology bigrading

- [Bigraded cohomology via mapping cone](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/content/bigraded-settling-cohomology/content/assets/legacy/bigraded-cohomology-via-mapping-cone.md): the bicomplex with only two non-zero rows ($m = 0, 1$) is a mapping cone.
- [Bigraded aperiodic-regime trichotomy](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/references/bigraded-aperiodic-regime-trichotomy.md): aperiodic settlings stratify into three patterns by the bigrading.
- Truth-knowledge LES gives an explicit long exact sequence relating $H^n_t, H^n_k$, and $H^n(\mathcal{F}^{(\top)})$.
- For *consistent* settlings ($\mathcal{F}_U^{(\top)} = 0$): $H^n_t \cong H^n_k$ (cohomologies coincide).
- For *inconsistent* settlings: the two cohomologies genuinely differ.

### About the common-knowledge bigrading

- [Bigraded common knowledge theorem](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/theorems/bigraded-common-knowledge.md) (sketch): existence via Knaster-Tarski on each order.
- [KZ-diamond strict commutation theorem](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/theorems/kz-diamond-strict-commutation.md) (sketch): the modal-operator iteration commutes strictly with the closure operator due to axis-orthogonality.
- Axis-collapse criterion: $C^t \phi = C^k \phi$ iff the frame is structurally Boolean (no $\top$ or $\bot$ cells anywhere). Equivalently, the two bigraded common-knowledge fixed points coincide *exactly when the cohomology bigrading collapses*.

## The question

What is the precise relationship between the two bigradings? Specifically:

1. *(Structural correspondence.)* Does the modal-operator-level common-knowledge bigrading $(C^t \phi, C^k \phi)$ factor through the sheaf-level cohomology bigrading $(\mathcal{F}^{(t)}, \mathcal{F}^{(k)}, \mathcal{F}^{(\top)})$ in a precise way? Specifically: is there a canonical map identifying $C^t \phi$ with the cohomology of $\mathcal{F}^{(t)}$-component, and $C^k \phi$ with the cohomology of $\mathcal{F}^{(k)}$ as a whole?

2. *(Axis-collapse compatibility.)* Both bigradings collapse to single-grading on consistent / Boolean frames. Is this collapse synchronous — i.e., does the cohomology bigrading collapse iff the common-knowledge bigrading collapses?

3. *(Long exact sequence analog.)* The cohomology bigrading has the [truth-knowledge cohomology LES](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/references/truth-knowledge-cohomology-long-exact-sequence.md). Is there an analogous "long exact sequence" for the common-knowledge bigrading, relating $C^t \phi$, $C^k \phi$, and a third "$\top$-common-knowledge" object?

4. *(Mapping cone analog.)* The cohomology bigrading is presented as a [mapping cone](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/settling-cohomology/content/bigraded-settling-cohomology/content/assets/legacy/bigraded-cohomology-via-mapping-cone.md). Is there an analogous mapping-cone presentation of the common-knowledge bigrading?

## Sub-questions

### (a) The "$\top$-common-knowledge" object

For the cohomology side, the third object is the [inconsistency sheaf](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/sheaf/content/inconsistency-sheaf/inconsistency-sheaf.md) $\mathcal{F}_U^{(\top)}$. For the common-knowledge side, no analogous object has been explicitly defined. Sub-question (a) asks: define the "paraconsistent common knowledge" $C^\top \phi$ as the cofiber of the natural map $C^t \phi \to C^k \phi$, and verify it has a sensible epistemic interpretation (e.g., "the agents commonly know that they disagree about $\phi$"). On benchmark-5, $C^\top p$ should capture the $\top$-witness world's content.

### (b) Long exact sequence of common-knowledge fixed points

Sub-question (b): state and prove a long exact sequence

$$\cdots \to C^{\top}_{n-1} \phi \to C^t_n \phi \to C^k_n \phi \to C^\top_n \phi \to \cdots$$

(if such exists) where $C^t_n, C^k_n, C^\top_n$ are some natural cohomology-graded versions of the per-axis common-knowledge operators. Conjecture: this LES is induced by a short exact sequence of fixed-point objects in a derived category of $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie$-valued sheaves on the temporal 2-category.

### (c) Cohomology of the common-knowledge fixed points

Sub-question (c): compute the cohomology $H^n(C^t \phi, C^k \phi)$ — the cohomology of the bigraded common-knowledge fixed points themselves, treated as $\mathbf{L}^\bowtie$-valued sheaves on the multi-agent Kripke frame. This connects the modal-operator-level bigrading to the sheaf-cohomology-level bigrading by *applying $R\Gamma$ to the common-knowledge objects*.

The candidate connecting theorem: $H^0(C^\bullet \phi)$ recovers the cohomology bigrading of the underlying coefficient sheaf, with $C^t \phi$'s degree-0 cohomology equal to $H^0(\mathcal{F}^{(t)})$ on the consistent component and $C^k \phi$'s degree-0 cohomology equal to $H^0(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})$ on the full sheaf.

### (d) Connection to bigraded aperiodic-regime trichotomy

The [bigraded aperiodic-regime trichotomy theorem](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/references/bigraded-aperiodic-regime-trichotomy.md) classifies aperiodic settlings into three patterns by their cohomology bigrading. Sub-question (d): does the common-knowledge bigrading also stratify aperiodic *epistemic Kripke frames* into corresponding patterns? Concrete deliverable: a table mapping the three cohomology-bigrading patterns to corresponding common-knowledge-bigrading patterns, with worked examples.

### (e) The two-bigraded combined invariant

Sub-question (e): define a *combined* bigraded invariant $H^{m, n}_{k, t}(C^{\bullet} \phi)$ — the cohomology bigrading applied to the common-knowledge bigrading. This is a *four-graded* invariant — two axes from the cohomology side (consistent/paraconsistent × cohomological degree) and two axes from the common-knowledge side (truth-axis/knowledge-axis × iteration count). The full classification of paraconsistent multi-agent epistemic frames may live at this combined-invariant level.

This is the most ambitious sub-question; settling it would unify the modal-operator-level and sheaf-cohomology-level bigraded structures into a single four-axis classification.

## Why this question

The two bigraded structures look superficially similar (both have "bigraded" in the name, both come from the two-axis structure of the predication hyperdoctrine) but operate at different layers of the corpus's framework. Without a precise connecting theorem, downstream readers cannot tell whether the corpus has *one* bigraded framework or *two distinct* bigraded frameworks.

Settling the relationship clarifies the corpus's structural shape:

- If the two bigradings are *equivalent* (one-to-one correspondence at every level), they should be identified — saving the corpus from having two parallel notations.
- If the two bigradings are *complementary* (each captures content the other doesn't), they should both be retained and the connecting theorem should make the relationship precise.
- If the two bigradings are *connected via a four-graded combined invariant*, that combined invariant becomes the corpus's natural classifying object — finer than either bigrading alone.

The empirical evidence so far (axis-collapse synchrony on benchmark-5, both bigradings exploiting the same hyperdoctrine adjoint systems) suggests the third option: complementary bigradings combining into a four-graded invariant.
