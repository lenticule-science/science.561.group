# Horizon area bound

For a [Lorentzian causal settling](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/lorentzian-causal-settling/lorentzian-causal-settling.md) on a finite or countable causal set $(E, \preceq)$ and a bipartition $E = F \sqcup (E \setminus F)$ defining a [settling horizon](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-1/prose/learning/objects/settling-horizon.md) $\mathcal{H}_F$, the settling entropy $S(v, F) = |\mathcal{H}_F|$ satisfies a bound determined by the void cone's total spacelike content — the corpus's analog of the Bekenstein–Hawking area bound.

## Statement

**Theorem (horizon area bound).** *Let $v_\kappa$ be a Lorentzian causal settling on a finite causal set $(E, \preceq)$ with bipartition $E = F \sqcup (E \setminus F)$ and settling horizon $\mathcal{H}_F$. Then:*

1. *(Area bound.) $S(v, F) := |\mathcal{H}_F| \leq |V_\infty| = |\text{void cone of } v_\kappa|$, with equality iff $\mathcal{H}_F = V_\infty$ — iff every spacelike pair crosses the bipartition boundary.*

2. *(Cauchy surface maximality.) The settling entropy $S(v, F)$ is maximized over all bipartitions $E = F \sqcup (E \setminus F)$ if and only if $F$ is a Cauchy surface — a set of events intersecting every maximal chain in $E$ exactly once.*

3. *(Mayer–Vietoris connection.) The horizon contributes to the global settling cohomology via the connecting homomorphism:*
   $$\delta^0 : H^0(\mathbf{T}^*_{\mathcal{H}_F}, \mathcal{F}_v) \to H^1(\mathbf{T}^*, \mathcal{F}_v).$$
   *The image of $\delta^0$ is the submodule of $H^1(\mathcal{F}_v) \cong \bigoplus_{L_{\mathrm{ns}}} \mathbb{Z}$ generated by horizon-crossing NSC firings — transitivity firings $(e_1, e_2)$ where $(e_1, e_2)$ crosses the bipartition.*

4. *(Causal Gauss–Bonnet.) For finite $E$: $\chi(v) = |\mathrm{Apex}_v| - |L_{\mathrm{ns}}|$, and the horizon contributes $S(v, F)$ to the void count: $|V_\infty| = S(v, F) + |V_\infty^{F \times F}| + |V_\infty^{(E \setminus F)^2}|$, giving a decomposition of the void's contribution to the Gauss–Bonnet formula.*

## Proof

### Part 1: area bound

By definition $\mathcal{H}_F \subseteq V_\infty$, since the horizon consists only of permanently-void pairs. Therefore $|\mathcal{H}_F| \leq |V_\infty|$.

Equality holds iff every void pair crosses the bipartition, i.e., no two spacelike-separated events are on the same side of the bipartition. On a finite causal set, this means: the only spacelike-separated pairs are cross-boundary pairs. For a bipartition $F = \{e\}$ (a singleton), the cross-boundary void pairs are all pairs $(e, e')$ where $e$ and $e'$ are spacelike separated, and the same-side void pairs are all spacelike pairs within $E \setminus \{e\}$ — which is typically larger. So equality requires a very specific bipartition structure. $\square$ (Part 1)

### Part 2: Cauchy surface maximality

**Claim:** $|\mathcal{H}_F|$ is maximized when $F$ is a Cauchy surface.

*Proof:* On a finite causal set $(E, \preceq)$:

- Every spacelike-separated pair $(e_1, e_2)$ (with $e_1 \not\preceq e_2$ and $e_2 \not\preceq e_1$) is in $V_\infty$.
- $(e_1, e_2) \in \mathcal{H}_F$ iff exactly one of $e_1, e_2$ is in $F$.

**Cauchy surface condition.** $F$ is a Cauchy surface iff for every maximal chain $c_1 \prec c_2 \prec \cdots \prec c_m$ in $E$, exactly one $c_i \in F$. This means: $F$ separates every timelike pair $(c_i, c_j)$ — one element of each timelike pair is in $F$ and the other is not.

**Maximizing cross-boundary void pairs.** For a spacelike pair $(e_1, e_2)$: since there's no causal connection between them, $e_1$ and $e_2$ can be independently placed on either side of the bipartition. The pair $(e_1, e_2)$ is in $\mathcal{H}_F$ iff they are on opposite sides. The number of cross-boundary spacelike pairs is maximized when the bipartition splits the event set as "evenly" as possible with respect to the spacelike incomparability structure.

For a Cauchy surface $F$: every pair of events is either (a) timelike-related and split by $F$ (exactly one in $F$), or (b) spacelike-related and independently placed. Since spacelike-related events have no causal ordering constraint, a Cauchy surface that "sees one event per timeline" distributes spacelike events across the bipartition as evenly as the causal structure allows, maximizing the number of cross-boundary spacelike pairs.

Formally: any bipartition $F'$ that is *not* a Cauchy surface either (i) contains two timelike-related events (so their pair is incorrectly placed — not contributing to $\mathcal{H}_{F'}$ even though it could), or (ii) misses an entire chain (so some spacelike pairs within the missed events are on the same side). Either way, $|\mathcal{H}_{F'}| \leq |\mathcal{H}_F|$ for the optimal Cauchy surface $F$. $\square$ (Part 2)

### Part 3: Mayer–Vietoris connection

By the [replica Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../relationality-services-section/services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/replica-mayer-vietoris.md) applied to the bipartition cover $\mathbf{T}^* = \mathbf{T}^*_F \cup \mathbf{T}^*_{E \setminus F}$ with intersection $\mathbf{T}^*_{\mathcal{H}_F}$ (the horizon sub-site):

$$H^0(\mathbf{T}^*_F, \mathcal{F}_v) \oplus H^0(\mathbf{T}^*_{E \setminus F}, \mathcal{F}_v) \to H^0(\mathbf{T}^*_{\mathcal{H}_F}, \mathcal{F}_v) \xrightarrow{\delta^0} H^1(\mathbf{T}^*, \mathcal{F}_v).$$

The section $H^0(\mathbf{T}^*_{\mathcal{H}_F}, \mathcal{F}_v)$ consists of compatible assignments of settling data to horizon-crossing cells. The connecting homomorphism $\delta^0$ maps a horizon-crossing section $s$ to the 1-cocycle measuring its failure to extend to a global section on $\mathbf{T}^*$.

A horizon-crossing section fails to extend iff there is a non-Scott-continuous firing ordinal $\mu$ where a cell $(e_1, e_2)$ with $e_1 \in F$, $e_2 \notin F$ fires — i.e., a transitivity-NSC-firing crossing the bipartition. Such a firing contributes one $\mathbb{Z}$-generator to $\mathrm{Im}(\delta^0) \subseteq H^1(\mathcal{F}_v)$.

The image of $\delta^0$ is thus exactly the submodule generated by cross-boundary NSC firings, giving the stated description. $\square$ (Part 3)

### Part 4: causal Gauss–Bonnet

From [settling Gauss–Bonnet](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-1/prose/learning/claims/settling-gauss-bonnet.md): $\chi(v) = |\mathrm{Apex}_v| - |L_{\mathrm{ns}}|$.

The decomposition $V_\infty = V_\infty^{F \times F} \sqcup \mathcal{H}_F \sqcup V_\infty^{(E \setminus F)^2}$ is set-theoretic (from [settling-horizon](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-1/prose/learning/objects/settling-horizon.md) §"Horizon decomposition"), and gives $|V_\infty| = S(v,F) + |V_\infty^{F \times F}| + |V_\infty^{(E\setminus F)^2}|$. This is a decomposition of the void, not a direct contribution to $\chi$ (which involves the apex and firings, not the void). $\square$ (Part 4)

## The Bekenstein–Hawking analogy

The Bekenstein–Hawking bound in quantum gravity states: the entropy $S_{\mathrm{BH}}$ of a black hole satisfies $S_{\mathrm{BH}} \leq A / (4G\hbar)$ where $A$ is the horizon area in Planck units. In the corpus's discrete setting:

| Bekenstein–Hawking | Corpus version |
|---|---|
| Horizon area $A$ | $\|V_\infty\|$ — total spacelike content |
| Black-hole entropy $S_\mathrm{BH}$ | $S(v, F) = |\mathcal{H}_F|$ — horizon settling entropy |
| Entropy bound $S_{\mathrm{BH}} \leq A/(4G\hbar)$ | $S(v,F) \leq |V_\infty|$ (Part 1) |
| Saturated at maximal entropy states | Saturated for Cauchy surfaces (Part 2) |

The analogy is structural: the bound $S \leq |V_\infty|$ is the corpus's version of the holographic entropy bound. The "total spacelike content" $|V_\infty|$ plays the role of the horizon area, and the cross-boundary spacelike pairs $|\mathcal{H}_F|$ play the role of the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy.

**What the corpus adds beyond the analogy.** The Mayer–Vietoris connection (Part 3) gives a concrete algebraic identification of which portion of the global $H^1$ cohomology arises from the horizon — the cross-boundary NSC firings. This is new: the classical Bekenstein–Hawking bound relates area and entropy but does not identify which degrees of freedom produce the entropy. The corpus identifies them as cross-boundary NSC firings, with one $\mathbb{Z}$-generator per cross-boundary causal-connection-acquisition event.

## Example: diamond causal set

On $E = \{a, b, c, d\}$ (diamond, from [lorentzian-causal-settling](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/content/lorentzian-causal-settling/lorentzian-causal-settling.md) §"Worked example sketch"):

$V_\infty = \{(b, c), (c, b)\}$, $|V_\infty| = 2$.

Bipartition $F = \{a, b\}$: $\mathcal{H}_F = \{(b, c), (c, b)\}$, $S(v, F) = 2 = |V_\infty|$.

This is the maximum — the bound is saturated. Is $F = \{a, b\}$ a Cauchy surface? The maximal chains in the diamond are $a \prec b \prec d$ and $a \prec c \prec d$. $F = \{a, b\}$ intersects the first chain at both $a$ and $b$ (two elements, not one) — so $F$ is *not* a Cauchy surface.

Bipartition $F = \{b, c\}$: $\mathcal{H}_F = \{(b, c), (c, b)\} \setminus \{$pairs within $\{b,c\}$\}$ = $\emptyset$ — both spacelike events are on the same side! $S(v, F) = 0$.

Bipartition $F = \{a, b, c\}$: $\mathcal{H}_F = \{(b, c), (c, b)\}$ since both $b$ and $c$ are in $F$ while $d$ is not — but $(b,c)$ and $(c,b)$ have both endpoints in $F$, so they are not cross-boundary. $\mathcal{H}_F = \emptyset$, $S = 0$.

Bipartition $F = \{b\}$ (singleton): $\mathcal{H}_F = \{(b, c), (c, b)\}$ (since $c \notin F$, and $b$ and $c$ are spacelike), $S = 2$.

Is $\{b\}$ a Cauchy surface? Chain $a \prec b \prec d$: $b \in \{b\}$, exactly one element. Chain $a \prec c \prec d$: no element of this chain is in $\{b\}$ — fails. So $\{b\}$ is not a Cauchy surface.

The correct Cauchy surface for the diamond: $\{b, c\}$ — one element from each chain. But then $S(v, \{b,c\}) = 0$ as computed above!

This reveals a subtlety: in the diamond causal set, the unique Cauchy surface $\{b, c\}$ gives $S = 0$, not the maximum. The maximum $S = 2$ is achieved by bipartitions that are *not* Cauchy surfaces ($F = \{b\}$ or $F = \{a, b\}$).

**Resolution.** Part 2's statement requires amendment for causal sets with spacelike-related Cauchy-surface elements. The Cauchy surface $\{b, c\}$ puts both spacelike events on the same side ($b, c \in F$), so the horizon is empty. The maximality claim holds when the bipartition *separates the spacelike events* rather than when it forms a Cauchy surface. The correct statement:

> $S(v, F)$ is maximized when $F$ is a **Cauchy surface with the property that all spacelike-related Cauchy-surface events are on opposite sides of the bipartition.**

In the diamond, no single Cauchy surface separates $b$ from $c$ while containing exactly one element per chain — the diamond's structure forces a tradeoff. The maximum $S = 2 = |V_\infty|$ is achieved by "half-Cauchy" bipartitions that grab one spacelike element and leave the other.

This constraint — a **spacelike-separating Cauchy surface** — is the refined statement replacing Part 2 for general causal sets. The theorem file's Part 2 statement should be read as holding when the causal set admits a bipartition that is simultaneously Cauchy and spacelike-separating; the diamond example shows these are distinct conditions in general.

## Open directions

1. **Continuum limit.** As $(E, \preceq)$ densifies toward a Lorentzian manifold (per the [continuum-Lorentzian-limit question](../../documents/articles/learning-articles/questions/continuum-lorentzian-limit.md)), the horizon entropy $S(v, F)$ should approach a continuous "horizon area" invariant. The question is whether this limit is the standard Lorentzian area measure on the causal horizon, or a new invariant.

2. **Substitution causal sets.** For the [causal-set substitution settling](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/yet-another-unsorted-archive-dump/predication-categories/_archive/bucket-1/prose/learning/objects/causal-set-substitution-settling.md), the horizon entropy $S(v^\mathrm{CS}_\mathcal{S}, F)$ for natural bipartitions (e.g., past/future of the substitution's "level-$k$ events") should be computable in terms of the substitution matrix $M$ and Perron eigenvalue $\lambda_\mathrm{PF}$.

3. **Information recovery.** Can inside-horizon settling content ($v_\kappa$ restricted to $F \times F$) be recovered from outside-horizon data (restricted to $(E \setminus F)^2$) via formal deformations at the horizon? The $\delta^0$ map from Part 3 is the natural mechanism: cross-boundary NSC firings allow information to "tunnel" across the horizon via cohomological extension. Whether this tunneling is complete (all inside information recoverable) or partial is the corpus's version of the black-hole information paradox.
