# Theology Services Section

The Theology Services Section maintains knowledge and practices for
philosophical theology as a register in its own right: the vocabulary
developed across the scholastic, Orthodox, Jewish, and Islamic/Sufi
traditions for hope and its collapses, grace and its distribution,
acedia and rest, lament and dereliction, and the disciplines of chosen
self-abandonment. It holds these terms with the same care given to any
technical vocabulary elsewhere in the estate — exact citations, formal
distinctions, and developmental history — in service of the
critical-theory research line's attention/affect analysis, for which
the theological register supplies precision and priority that the
secular literatures often lack.

Its holdings are deliberately plural across traditions rather than
organized around a single confession. Latin scholasticism (Aquinas,
Pieper) sits alongside the Jewish tradition of lament and Sabbath
(Brueggemann, Heschel), the Islamic/Sufi vocabulary of surrender and
annihilation (tawakkul, fanā/baqā, the Mevlevi sema), and the
patristic and Orthodox material on kenosis and the apophatic way. This
section does not duplicate the estate's other religious-philosophical
holdings: Buddhist analyses of the settled and unsettled continue in
the Buddhist Services Section, Daoist material in the Dao Services
Section, and Lakota ceremonial and cosmological material in the Lakota
Services Section. Comparative work that would draw those traditions
into direct dialogue with the vocabulary held here is a standing
direction, not yet undertaken.

Its founding document is "Abandonment, Acedia, and the Whirl" (a
research note in philosophical theology, dated 2026-08-23), which
first worked this register at length — Aquinas's two vices against
hope, acedia from Evagrius through Pieper's inversion, Byung-Chul
Han's burnout society read as political Pelagianism, lament against
the take-economy, Moltmann on dereliction, de Caussade and the Sufi
frame on chosen self-abandonment, and Aristotle's quintessence as an
old figure for the circulating rather than the settling. That note
continues to live in the parent Philosophy Services Section's notes
and is referenced here by title rather than moved or edited; this
section exists to hold the vocabulary the note discovered it needed,
at the depth a glossary allows and a note does not.

Its material is organized by artifact role under `content/`: glossary
terms and topic statements under `content/text/glossary/`, and
reference material — including an annotated bibliography of the
sources behind its terms — under
`content/text/documents/articles/references/`.
