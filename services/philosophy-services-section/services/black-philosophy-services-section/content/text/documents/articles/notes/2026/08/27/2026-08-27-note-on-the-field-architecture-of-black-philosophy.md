## Decision

The Black Philosophy Services Section is the home for a general field of philosophical inquiry. It is not a parent label invented merely to contain the Black Radical Tradition and abolition, and neither of those lineages is a synecdoche for the field. The section should make room for questions about existence, knowledge, value, beauty, religion, embodiment, nature, and technical life whether a source answers them through radical politics, liberal reform, nationalism, feminism, religious commitment, conservatism, pluralism, or a position that does not fit those names.

This decision does not make Black philosophy politically neutral. Racial slavery, colonialism, segregation, racial capitalism, gendered violence, and struggles for freedom have decisively shaped its questions and institutions. The decision instead preserves disagreement. A field cannot be reconstructed if only the positions favored by its current curators are allowed to count as philosophy.

## A field needs crossing dimensions

A single directory tree cannot represent every meaningful relation in a field. Organizing only by topic obscures historical formation. Organizing only by geography separates arguments that travelled across oceans. Organizing only by political tradition treats metaphysics, aesthetics, or philosophy of religion as secondary. This section therefore uses five crossing dimensions:

1. **Problem-space** identifies the philosophical question: what exists, who is a person, how knowledge is authorized, what is valuable, what freedom requires, or what beauty does.
2. **Historical and geographic formation** identifies the concrete world in which the question arose: a particular African society, the antebellum United States, the Caribbean plantation complex, colonial Europe, or a later diasporic formation.
3. **Tradition or conversation** identifies inherited positions, alliances, and disputes: Pan-Africanism, Black feminism, womanism, existential philosophy, liberalism, nationalism, Marxism, abolition, or the Black Radical Tradition, among others.
4. **Form and site** identifies where philosophical activity occurs: a treatise, slave narrative, sermon, court argument, organization, school, novel, song, visual practice, oral tradition, household practice, or movement.
5. **Transmission condition** identifies how the material reached the present: self-publication, editorial mediation, translation, hostile recordkeeping, retrospective interview, institutional canonization, silencing, or archival recovery.

Each artifact can occupy several positions on every dimension. Anna Julia Cooper's work, for example, belongs to Black feminist philosophy, political philosophy, ethics, philosophy of education, and religious criticism; it arose within specific African American institutions and circulated in essays, speeches, teaching, and organizing. No single folder name should erase those relations.

## Field names are tools, not settled borders

Black philosophy overlaps with Africana philosophy, African philosophy, African American philosophy, Caribbean philosophy, Afro-Latin American philosophy, Black European philosophy, and other diasporic formations. The terms are not interchangeable. Africana philosophy is often used as the widest transnational umbrella for philosophy by African and African-descended peoples. African philosophy can designate continental traditions whose peoples and problems cannot be reduced to the racial categories of the Americas. Black philosophy foregrounds Blackness as a contested historical, political, cultural, and sometimes ontological formation.

No definition eliminates every disputed case. Black authorship alone does not make every utterance an intervention in Black philosophy, but limiting the field to explicit discussion of Blackness would reproduce a canon in which Black thinkers are permitted to speak only about race. The practical test is relational: the work belongs when its authorship, intellectual formation, problem, reception, or use gives it a substantive place in Black philosophical conversation. That placement must be argued rather than presumed.

The same restraint applies to diaspora. “Diasporic” must not turn Africa into a single origin or Black movement into a story with only Atlantic routes. It must not erase Indigenous peoples whose lands Black settlements, maroon communities, states, and institutions have occupied. Research should name particular peoples, polities, languages, lands, routes, and periods whenever the sources permit.

The “initial anchors” are openings, not canonical closure. They identify people and conversations from which source-bound work can begin. The disability, ecology, and technology rows are intentionally less settled in this corpus; their presence marks questions that must be researched, not positions the section can already claim to possess.

## Traditions should be represented as arguments

A tradition is not a bucket of authors who agree. The section should preserve disputes over integration, emigration, nationalism, Pan-Africanism, socialism, liberal rights, armed self-defense, nonviolence, abolition, and state power. It should preserve disputes over art as propaganda or individual expression; religion as liberation, accommodation, or both; solidarity grounded in identity, shared domination, practice, or political commitment; and Blackness understood as culture, political position, imposed racial category, historical formation, or mode of being.

The current Black Radical Tradition topic is too compressed when it treats racial capitalism as the thesis uniting every figure named under that lineage. Cedric Robinson's formulation is indispensable to one powerful reconstruction of the tradition, but historical revolt, Du Bois's political economy, C. L. R. James's account of Haiti, Angela Davis's abolitionism, and Ruth Wilson Gilmore's geography are not interchangeable instances of one proposition. A later revision should give each thinker an argued relation to the lineage and record disputes over its membership and coherence.

The current abolition topic is similarly narrow. It strongly names contemporary prison-industrial-complex abolition, but the field also requires the relation between slavery abolition and prison abolition; arguments over legal emancipation, colonization, immediatism, gradualism, fugitivity, and self-emancipation; and abolition's constructive claims concerning social provision and freedom. Expanding the topic must not imply an uninterrupted doctrine running unchanged from the antebellum period to the present.

## Forms and sites need evidence controls

Black philosophical activity has often taken forms excluded from a philosophy department's canon. That fact justifies wider inquiry, not interpretive license. A narrative makes philosophical claims when a speaker states or sustains judgments about the world; an institution or practice can supply evidence of a shared problem, norm, or practical distinction; an artwork can think through form as well as through propositions. None is transparent.

For every source, notes should distinguish at least three levels:

- an **explicit proposition** stated by the historical actor;
- an **interpretation of reported practice** warranted by the available context; and
- a **later analytic reconstruction** introduced by the researcher or another theorist.

The enslaved-labor inquiry already uses this distinction. “Rightful earnings” closely follows Douglass's language. “Labor jurisdiction” is a present synthesis across several witnesses. The difference is part of the claim and must remain visible. Similar care is required for oral traditions recorded after the event, texts mediated by editors or translators, and archives built by hostile institutions.

## Development sequence

The next investment should deepen connected fronts without immediately converting each one into an empty service hierarchy.

1. **Black feminist philosophy and archive method.** This front most directly corrects the current labor corpus, supplies a method for silencing and mediation, and crosses ethics, epistemology, embodiment, religion, and political economy.
2. **Being, personhood, and Black existential philosophy.** This front extends the labor inquiry's account of bodily, temporal, and practical authorship beyond an economic vocabulary.
3. **Black epistemology.** This front should formalize the difference among testimony, credibility, standpoint, ignorance, archive, and reconstruction that the present research already needs.
4. **Black aesthetics.** This front should connect canonical debates over art and propaganda with ordinary expressive forms while refusing compulsory political usefulness as a condition of Black art.
5. **Black religious philosophy and womanist ethics.** This front should examine moral authority, evil, suffering, survival, and liberation across religious and secular traditions rather than treating theology as decorative politics.
6. **Disability, ecology, and technology crossings.** These fronts should begin as bounded research questions attached to cases. They should become services only after the corpus can define their distinctions from adjacent fields.

Black Feminist Philosophy is the strongest candidate for the next specialized service because it is already necessary to correct the labor inquiry and has a substantial, internally differentiated literature. Even there, the directory should follow a source dossier, a thick research note, and a working term set. The architecture should register scholarship that exists, not promise scholarship through folder names.

## Current limits and next repairs

This batch establishes a field map and six root topics: Black philosophy, Black feminist philosophy, Black existential philosophy, Black epistemology, Black aesthetics, and Black religious philosophy. It does not yet create equivalent depth across the matrix.

The inherited Black Radical Tradition and abolition trees also contain substantial broken-link debt and mixed old path conventions. Those links require source-by-source repair; a mechanical rewrite would conceal differences among moved, renamed, duplicated, and absent targets. The two standing topics need separate conceptual revisions for the compressions identified above. Those are future manual batches, not reasons to defer the field map.
