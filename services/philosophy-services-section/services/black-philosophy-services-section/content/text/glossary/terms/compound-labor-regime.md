# Compound Labor Regime

A **compound labor regime** is an order that governs several connected but nonidentical appropriations of human capacity. In antebellum slavery, agricultural and skilled production, domestic service, reproduction, sexual coercion, care, kin-making, and medical experimentation were organized through the same underlying claim of property in persons, even though those activities did not have identical forms or meanings.

The term is a synthesis introduced by the [note on Black feminist labor, reproduction, and epistemic authority](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-black-feminist-labor-reproduction-and-epistemic-authority.md). It is not recovered antebellum vocabulary. Its purpose is to prevent a theory formed around wages and commodity output from treating other appropriations as secondary, while also preventing every form of captivity from being flattened into one undifferentiated activity called labor.
