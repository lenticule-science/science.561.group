**Reproductive expropriation** is the seizure of gestational, birthing, and life-sustaining capacities and their results through an order that denies the person's authority over body, kin, and future. Under racial slavery, the rule that a child's status followed the mother allowed enslavers to convert birth into an enlargement of property while retaining powers of sale, separation, labor command, and sexual coercion.

The term is later analytic vocabulary, not a phrase attributed to enslaved people. It names a structural appropriation without reducing enslaved women to reproductive capacity or assuming that pregnancy, birth, nursing, child care, and sexual domination are one activity. Its use requires sources that establish the relevant legal, economic, or coercive relation.
