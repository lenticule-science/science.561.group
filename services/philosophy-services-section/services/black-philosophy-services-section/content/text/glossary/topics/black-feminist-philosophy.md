# Black Feminist Philosophy

Black feminist philosophy studies how race, gender, class, sexuality, nationality, disability, and other relations of power constitute one another, while developing accounts of knowledge, embodiment, ethical agency, labor, violence, care, freedom, and collective survival from Black women's thought and practice. It is not philosophy that adds Black women to a prior universal framework. It asks how frameworks built by excluding or misdescribing Black women must themselves change.

The field has a long history before its current academic name. Anna Julia Cooper analyzed education, voice, Christian hypocrisy, sexual violence, labor exploitation, and the political position of Black women. Later Black feminist and womanist thinkers developed distinct approaches rather than a single school. Patricia Hill Collins theorized Black feminist thought, standpoint, self-definition, and interlocking systems of oppression. Kimberlé Crenshaw developed intersectionality through the failure of antidiscrimination law to recognize Black women's injuries. Kristie Dotson analyzes silencing, epistemic violence, and Black feminist metaphilosophy. Katie Cannon develops womanist ethics from Black women's survival practices and moral wisdom.

Black feminist philosophy crosses the usual disciplinary partitions. A claim about who is heard is epistemology and political philosophy. A claim about reproductive labor is ethics, embodiment, political economy, and metaphysics of the person. A claim about controlling images is aesthetics and social ontology. A claim about survival can be theological, existential, and practical at once.

The topic is indispensable to the current labor inquiry. An account centered on men's wage hiring cannot explain reproductive appropriation, domestic and care labor, sexual coercion, family separation, compulsory self-provisioning, or the way Black women's testimony is mediated and discounted. Adding women as examples after forming the theory would preserve the distortion. Their problem formulations must participate in determining the theory's categories.

The [research note on Black feminist labor, reproduction, and epistemic authority](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/08/27/2026-08-27-note-on-black-feminist-labor-reproduction-and-epistemic-authority.md) begins that reconstruction. Its glossary grows from distinctions the argument requires, including compound labor regime, reproductive expropriation, sexual political economy, presence of force, bodymind, testimonial conditions, reading along the bias grain, and interpretive ceiling.

## Boundary

Black feminist philosophy is not synonymous with all thought by Black women, and Black women do not share a single politics or standpoint automatically. A standpoint is achieved through interpretation and struggle, not biologically given. The field also includes internal critique concerning sexuality, trans experience, disability, class, nation, religion, and whose Black womanhood becomes representative.
