The captive maternal is Joy James's term, developed in "The Womb of Western Theory: Trauma, Time Theft, and the Captive Maternal" (*Carceral Notebooks* 12, 2016), for the caretaker — not necessarily female, and not defined by biological motherhood — whose generative, stabilizing labor is captured to reproduce the very carceral and racial-capitalist order that consumes them. The captive maternal absorbs shocks, holds families and communities together, and reproduces the social relations that a hostile order depends on, while that order continues to extract from and endanger the captive maternal and those in their care.

James traces stages through which the captive maternal can move: from caretaker, absorbing harm to stabilize others; to protester, naming the harm; to movement maker, organizing collectively against it; to maroon, one who breaks from the captor order rather than continuing to stabilize it from within. Each stage is a different relation to the same captured position, not a replacement of care by refusal but a deepening of what the caretaking confronts.

The captive maternal is distinguished from [care work](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/care-work.md) in general: care work names the labor of tending bodies, relationships, and communities as such, performed under many different social relations, some coerced and some not. The captive maternal names specifically the position in which that caretaking labor is captured — extracted under conditions of racial and carceral domination to reproduce the order that captures it — so that the caretaking itself becomes, without the caretaker's consent, part of what stabilizes their own subjection.
