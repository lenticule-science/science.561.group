Prehension is [Alfred North Whitehead](../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/alfred-north-whitehead.md)'s term for the way one [actual occasion](./actual-occasion.md) grasps, receives, or takes account of another. The word is chosen to avoid the intellectualist connotations of "perception" or "apprehension" — prehension is not necessarily conscious, not necessarily cognitive, and not limited to organisms. Every [actual occasion](./actual-occasion.md) prehends the occasions in its past, and this prehension is the basic relational act from which all else is built.

[Whitehead](../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/alfred-north-whitehead.md) distinguishes two primary kinds: physical prehensions, in which an occasion receives the actualized data of a prior occasion (its achieved definiteness), and conceptual prehensions, in which an occasion grasps a possibility (an [eternal object](./eternal-object.md)) without that possibility being derived from any particular past occasion. Complex prehensions combine both. The process of integrating prehensions into a unified experience is [concrescence](./concrescence.md).

Prehension has a structure with three components: the prehending subject (the occasion in the process of becoming), the datum (what is prehended), and the subjective form (how the datum is received — with what valuation, intensity, or exclusion). [Negative prehension](./negative-prehension.md) is also possible: an occasion can exclude a datum from its integration, and this exclusion is itself constitutive.

For [relational ontology](../../ontology/terms/relational-ontology.md), prehension is the key concept. It is the mechanism by which relations constitute their terms: an [actual occasion](./actual-occasion.md) has no character apart from its prehensions. The occasion is not a thing that then prehends; it is a prehending that achieves thingness. This makes prehension the process-philosophical analog of what the [semiotic universe](../../../../mathematics/objects/universes/semiotic-universe/index.md) formalizes algebraically: the constitution of structure through relational acts.
