In [Alfred Whitehead](../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/alfred-north-whitehead.md)'s [process philosophy](../_index.md), ***eternal objects*** are *pure potentials*: qualities, patterns, and forms that are available for [ingression](./ingression.md) into [actual occasions](./actual-occasion.md), but are not themselves "actual."

Examples of eternal objects are a shade of blue, a mathematical ratio, or a spatial pattern. They don't exist in the way [actual occasions](./actual-occasion.md) do, through the process of [concrescence](./concrescence.md); they subsist as possibilities that may or may not be realized in any particular occasion.

Eternal objects serve a similar function in [Whitehead](../../../../../../../../../../../references/people/alfred-north-whitehead.md)'s system that universals play in Aristotelian and Platonic metaphysics, but with a crucial difference: eternal objects are not substances or forms imposed on matter. They are potentials that acquire definiteness only through their [ingression](./ingression.md) into [actual occasions](./actual-occasion.md) — through the way an occasion selects and integrates them via conceptual [prehension](./prehension.md). An eternal object apart from any ingression is a bare potential, not an actuality.

Eternal objects have a dual aspect. Each has an individual essence (what it is in itself, apart from any realization) and a relational essence (the way its ingression into one occasion conditions its possible ingression into others). This relational essence connects eternal objects to the formal structures of this research program: the relational essence of an eternal object is a function from contexts of ingression to modes of definiteness, which is structurally analogous to the typed operators of the [semiotic universe](../../../../mathematics/objects/universes/semiotic-universe/index.md).
