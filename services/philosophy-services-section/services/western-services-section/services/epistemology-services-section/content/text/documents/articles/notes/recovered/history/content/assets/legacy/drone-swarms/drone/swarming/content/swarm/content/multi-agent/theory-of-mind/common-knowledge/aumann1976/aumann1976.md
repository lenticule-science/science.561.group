# Aumann 1976 — Agreeing to disagree

Aumann, R.J. (1976).
Agreeing to disagree. *The Annals of Statistics*, 4(6), 1236-1239.

The four-page paper proving the *agreement theorem*: two Bayesian agents with a common prior who have common knowledge of each other's posteriors about an event must have *equal* posteriors.
Bayesian agents cannot agree to disagree under common-knowledge conditions.

The result is short — the proof is a few paragraphs — but the implications are foundational:

- It established *common knowledge* as a central technical concept in epistemic game theory.
- It implied that real-world disagreement requires *some* assumption violation: non-common priors, non-Bayesian updating, asymmetric information not yet made common knowledge, or bounded rationality.
- It seeded the *no-trade theorems* and *speculation impossibility* results in financial economics.
- It is the foundational result of *interactive epistemology* — the study of agents with knowledge about other agents' knowledge.

Aumann received the Nobel Prize in Economics (2005), in substantial part for this work and the subsequent program of interactive epistemology it founded.

The paper's significance for multi-agent AI is that it characterizes the conditions under which Bayesian multi-agent systems must reach agreement — and correspondingly identifies persistent disagreement among Bayesian-style AI agents as evidence of specific assumption violations worth diagnosing.
