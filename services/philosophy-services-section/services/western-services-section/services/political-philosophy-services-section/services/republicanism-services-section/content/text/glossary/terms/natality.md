Natality, as theorized by [Hannah Arendt](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/general/people/hannah-arendt.md) in *The Human Condition* (1958), names the capacity to begin something new — the fact that each human action introduces novelty into the world. For [Arendt](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/general/people/hannah-arendt.md), natality is not a biological fact about birth but a political and [ontological](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/ontology.md) condition: because humans are born, because each person arrives as someone who has never existed before, every action carries the potential to initiate what could not have been predicted or determined in advance.

[Arendt](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/general/people/hannah-arendt.md) treats natality as the political condition par excellence. Politics arises not from necessity or violence but from the human capacity to act — to bring into being what did not exist before. This sets her apart from political theorists who ground politics in sovereignty, conflict, or contract. For [Arendt](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/general/people/hannah-arendt.md), the defining feature of political life is that people act together in ways that generate new beginnings, and these beginnings cannot be fully controlled or anticipated by any actor, institution, or system.

emsenn invokes natality as the [anarchist](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md) alternative to fascist inevitability. Where fascism insists the storm is coming and positions its adherents as those who endure it, [anarchist](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/sociology/schools/anarchism/index.md) grammar insists on immanence and beginning — the possibility that action in the present can open futures not already contained by existing structures. Natality grounds the claim that political life is not a matter of surviving what has been determined but of initiating what has not yet been imagined. It stands in direct opposition to [reproductive futurism](../../../../../queer-theory-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/reproductive-futurism.md), which orients politics toward continuation rather than beginning.
