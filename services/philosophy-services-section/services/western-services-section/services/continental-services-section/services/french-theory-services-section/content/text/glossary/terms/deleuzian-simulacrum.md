In Gilles Deleuze's sense, a **simulacrum** is an image that lacks the resemblance a copy has to a model and is built on a difference it holds inside itself. The sense comes from his reading of Plato in "Plato and the Simulacrum" (the first appendix of *The Logic of Sense*, 1969, from an essay of 1967) and from the account of difference and repetition in *Difference and Repetition* (1968).

## The Platonic division
The method of division in Plato's dialogues sorts claimants to a standing. It keeps the **well-founded claimant**, the **copy** (*eikon*): an image that resembles a model, the Idea, because it carries the model's identity in its own relation to it. It rejects the **false claimant**, the **simulacrum** (*phantasma*): an image that only looks like the model from a viewpoint, while the relation underneath is one of dissimilarity and difference. The copy has its rights granted by the model. The simulacrum claims the standing without that grant.

## The reversal
Deleuze defines the task of his philosophy as reversing Platonism: denying the primacy of the original over the copy and of the model over the image. The reversal does not make the simulacrum a degraded copy. A copy is measured against the model by resemblance. A simulacrum is measured by the difference it internalizes, and that difference makes it a thing of its own kind. In *Difference and Repetition* the Same and the Similar are produced as effects of differential repetition. Resemblance is an effect of the simulacrum's internal difference, never the ground of it.

## What the Group takes from it
The Group uses the structural half of the sense, the **false claimant**: an artifact that holds a standing (a requirement met, a design followed, a rule derived) by appearance, with no relation to the model that would give it the standing. Deleuze affirms the simulacrum as a power. The Group does not take the affirmation: the Group's work method requires every artifact's standing to come from the chain that determines it, so an artifact in the simulacrum's position is a defect to be repaired, and the repair is to give it its original or to withdraw its claim.

## Contrast with Baudrillard
[Baudrillard's simulacra](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/simulacra.md) are signs that circulate with no relation to any reality, the fourth stage of the image. Deleuze's simulacrum is an image with a positive differential constitution, set against the copy in a division of claimants. The two share the word and differ in their structure; a sentence must say which one it uses.
