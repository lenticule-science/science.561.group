This is a **tutorial** text. It teaches one beginner workflow through a
small, guided exercise.

## Prerequisites

- [Dialectics](../../../glossary/terms/dialectics.md)
- [Determinate Negation](../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/determinate-negation.md)
- [Mediation](../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/mediation.md)
- [Historicization](../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/historicization.md)

## Learning objective

By the end, you should be able to produce a five-line dialectical map of
a short argument.

## Step 1: Start with a tiny claim

Use this toy claim:

> "A labor market that treats labor as a commodity depends on stable
> workers, yet competitive pressure destabilizes those workers' lives."

Do not debate whether the claim is true yet. First map its structure.

## Step 2: Mark contradiction

Write two commitments that cannot be jointly stabilized:

- Commitment A:
- Commitment B:

In the toy claim:

- A: the market needs reliable labor capacity.
- B: market competition undermines that reliability.

## Step 3: Add mediations

Now list mediating mechanisms (minimum two):

- wage pressure,
- housing precarity,
- health stress,
- turnover,
- productivity decline.

Your list should connect contradiction to outcomes without logical leaps.

## Step 4: State the transition

Write one sentence beginning with:

"Given these mediations, the prior form tends to..."

Example:

"Given these mediations, the prior market form tends to generate policy,
organizational, or collective counter-measures that partially re-stabilize labor while reproducing new tensions."

## Step 5: Historicize

Add time/place bounds in one sentence:

"This map describes [period], [region], under [institutional conditions]."

Without this sentence, your map is too abstract.

## Final output format

Produce exactly five lines:

1. Object
2. Contradiction
3. Mediations
4. Transition
5. Historical bounds

If you can do this clearly, you have performed a basic dialectical
analysis workflow.
