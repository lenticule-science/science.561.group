Dialectical writing often names contradiction but skips mediation. That
skip is the main reason many analyses sound dialectical while remaining
methodologically thin.

A contradiction, by itself, is an incompatibility claim. In strong
analysis, the contradiction must be shown as internal to the object's own
conditions of reproduction. Then mediation must be traced: institutions,
practices, temporal phases, and relation chains that carry the tension
into transformation.

This two-part requirement appears in different traditions with different
vocabulary. In Hegelian framing, contradiction and mediation are internal
moments of conceptual development [@hegel-dialectics-sep-2019]. In
Maoist strategic analysis, contradiction must be differentiated and
located concretely (principal/secondary, aspect shifts) rather than
handled abstractly [@mao-on-contradiction-1937].

## Minimal method

A usable procedure is:

1. Name the object and its boundary conditions.
2. State the internal contradiction precisely.
3. List mediating mechanisms.
4. Identify what changes and what is preserved.
5. Bound the claim historically.

Without steps 3 and 5, contradiction talk becomes rhetorical shorthand.

## Common failure pattern

The most common failure is immediate transition language: "X contradiction
therefore Y outcome." This treats contradiction as magical causation.
Dialectical method instead requires process articulation.

## Why this matters

If contradiction and mediation are handled together, dialectical analysis
can explain nonlinear transitions without collapsing into determinism. If
they are separated, dialectics degrades into style.
