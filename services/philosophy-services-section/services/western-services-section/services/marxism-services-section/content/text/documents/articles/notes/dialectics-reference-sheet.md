This is a **reference** text in the Diataxis sense. Use it as a compact
lookup when reading or writing dialectical analysis.

## Core terms

- **[Dialectics](../terms/dialectics.md)**: analysis of development
  through internal contradiction.
- **[Determinate Negation](../terms/determinate-negation.md)**:
  transformation specific to a form's own limits.
- **[Immanence](../../metaphysics/terms/immanence.md)**: explanation from internal
  relations rather than external transcendence.
- **[Mediation](../terms/mediation.md)**: intermediate mechanisms that
  connect one determination to another.
- **[Historicization](../terms/historicization.md)**: treatment of forms
  as historically produced and therefore transformable.
- **[Endoxa](../../../../../terms/endoxa.md)**: reputable starting opinions in
  dialectical testing.

## Minimal diagnostic checklist

A strong dialectical claim should answer all six:

1. What contradiction is internal to the object?
2. Why is that contradiction structurally necessary?
3. Through what mediations does transition occur?
4. Which determinations are preserved in transition?
5. Which determinations are negated?
6. What historical conditions bound the analysis?

## Frequent errors

- **Narrative triad substitution**: using thesis-antithesis-synthesis as
  a script instead of demonstrating determinate transition.
- **Immediate causality**: skipping mediation and asserting direct
  transformation.
- **Ahistorical universalization**: treating situated contradictions as
  timeless laws.
- **Category drift**: moving between conceptual and empirical
  contradiction without argument.

## Use cases

- Checking whether a text is genuinely dialectical.
- Building a curriculum sequence for dialectical method.
- Revising drafts that over-rely on rhetorical contradiction.
