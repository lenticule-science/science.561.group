# Caribbean Services Section

The Caribbean Services Section holds philosophical traditions emerging
from the Caribbean experience — the plantation system's aftermath and
the encounter between African, European, and Indigenous thought under
colonial and postcolonial conditions. Its direct glossary content is one
topic entry, `content/text/glossary/topics/caribbean.md`, naming Glissant's
creolization and opacity, Fanon's phenomenology of race, and Césaire's
négritude as the traditions it covers; only the first of these is
developed further so far.

That development lives under `services/creolization-services-section/`,
which holds Édouard Glissant's framework in two entries: a topic file,
`creolization.md`, describing creolization as the production of
irreducible novelty through encounter (neither the melting pot's
synthesis nor colonialism's assimilation), and a fully worked term,
`terms/opacity.md`, on the right of a person, culture, or knowledge-form
to refuse translation into the terms of dominant understanding — cited to
Glissant's *Poétique de la Relation* (1990) and its English translation
(1997). Fanon's and Césaire's traditions, named in the topic file as part
of the section's scope, have no entries yet.
