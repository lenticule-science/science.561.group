# Dao Services Section

The Dao Services Section holds the estate's Daoist-philosophy material and is currently the active repository home for questions provisionally framed as Chinese philosophy.
Its developed works live at `content/text/documents/articles/books/`, as two books rather than a glossary or a triage backlog: a 2024 personal essay and a 2026 formal treatise, both original writing rather than commentary on the *Daodejing* or *Yijing* as external objects.
First-class questions live at `content/text/documents/articles/research-articles/questions/`; their placement here does not itself establish that a question or its eventual answer is Daoist.
The section's ruled vocabulary — terms defined once, independently, and admitted through the house term service — lives at `content/text/glossary/terms/`; machine-checkable structure for those terms lives at `schemas/glossary/terms/` and `data/records/glossary-records/terms/`; the sources behind every cited translation live at `content/text/documents/articles/bibliographies/`.

`the-way-of-collapse.md` (dated 2024-02-28) is the earlier and shorter of the two: an anarcho-nihilist reading of the *Daodejing*'s Way and Power against ecological collapse, written after the author's first reading of a translation and stated plainly as connective, not scholarly — "there's nothing true in this text; it is not meant to teach, but connect ideas in my head to ideas in your head."

`2026-book-of-changes.md` is a nineteen-chapter work reading the *Yijing* (rather than a third pass at the *Daodejing*, which the estate had already rewritten twice in 2025) through obsmetrologia's mathematics — Belnap four-valued logic, bilattices, closure operators — keyed to the *Yijing*'s own line/trigram/hexagram structure.
Each chapter pairs a verse with a commentary and closes with an explicit grading line in the estate's own vocabulary (**[B]** background fact, **PROVED** mechanically checked, **FRAMING** an offered interpretive move, **VALVE** deliberately ungraded speculation), and a back-matter appendix restates every formal claim with file citations.
The book states its own limits directly: it sets a seven-week-old formal apparatus beside a three-thousand-year commentarial tradition (the Ten Wings, Wang Bi, Cheng Yi, Zhu Xi) and offers itself as *a* reading, not an account of what the *Yijing* "really" means.

## Glossary: Daodejing terms across published translations

`content/text/glossary/terms/` holds six admitted terms — [dao](content/text/glossary/terms/dao.md), [de](content/text/glossary/terms/de.md), [wu-wei](content/text/glossary/terms/wu-wei.md), [ziran](content/text/glossary/terms/ziran.md), [pu](content/text/glossary/terms/pu.md), and [daodejing](content/text/glossary/terms/daodejing.md) (the title itself, which declares `dao` and `de` as its constituent keywords rather than re-explaining either) — each written through the house term service (`term-engineering-services-section`) and each admitting cleanly with zero findings. A sixth term is not sixty-four: this is a sample of the *Daodejing*'s own most-retranslated vocabulary, not an enumeration of it, on the same theory Book Three of the *Yijing* treatise already states for itself.

Each term states, for its locus, how named published translations render it (Legge 1891, Waley 1934, Lau 1963, Mitchell 1988, Ames & Hall 2003, and Henricks 1989 for the Mawangdui witness — six bibliography carriers at `content/text/documents/articles/bibliographies/`), and sets the section's own reading beside them: quoted from an existing carrier where one already existed (dao and wu-wei, both already present in the 2026 treatise before this glossary), and honestly minted new and marked as such where none did (de, ziran, pu) — without inventing a mathematical anchor those three do not yet have, since a forced formalization would be exactly the overclaiming this estate has been warned against.

The same structure is typed twice: in prose, for a reader, in each term's own `references:` map and body; and machine-checkably, in `schemas/glossary/terms/` and `data/records/glossary-records/terms/`. One shared JSON Schema (`translation-variance-record.schema.json`, with building blocks `translation-rendering.schema.json` and `house-translation.schema.json`) types the shape every term's comparison shares — a `term`, `hanzi`, `pinyin`, `locus`, an array of grounded `translations`, and one graded `houseTranslation` — rather than six near-identical schemas repeating that shape by name; each term still gets its own thin, discoverable `<term>.schema.json` that fixes `term` and `$ref`s the shared shape, matching the math section's own `functor.schema.json`/`category.schema.json` precedent. All six `data/records/glossary-records/terms/<term>.v1.json` records validate against their schemas (checked with ajv, 2026-09-10). Every translator rendering carries a `grounding` field naming how it was checked (this pass checked against a translation-comparison aggregator, not against a physical copy of each cited edition — recorded as such rather than as more certain than it is).

## Generated ontology: `data/ontology/dao/`

`ontology.ttl` and `shapes.ttl` are generated, not hand-authored: `npm run generate-ontology` (or directly, `json-schema-to-owl-shacl-service`'s `project-json-schema-as-ontology` CLI) reads every `*.schema.json` under `schemas/glossary/terms/` and mechanically derives an `owl:Class` per schema that declares its own `properties` — `translation-variance-record`, `translation-rendering`, `house-translation` — with the six thin per-term schemas (`dao.schema.json` etc.) correctly producing no class, since each only fixes one field over an already-classed schema rather than defining its own shape. SHACL shapes follow the same required/optional split already stated in the JSON Schema: `required` becomes `sh:minCount 1` + `sh:Violation`, everything else `sh:Warning`.

This closes a real gap the estate had: no package anywhere derived RDF/OWL/SHACL from a JSON Schema (checked cold-red-gate and every GitHub repository first — the closest prior art was `transform-json-schema-to-typescript-service`, targeting TypeScript, and a dormant, RDF-*native* `2025-10-04-rdf-cms` prototype with its ontology and shapes hand-written directly, the reverse flow). The `json-schema-to-owl-shacl-service` lives under `computer-serialized-content-engineering-services-section/services/computer-triplet-serialized-content-engineering-services-section/services/`, built on that section's own RDF primitives (term construction, named-graph assembly, Turtle writing) rather than template strings. Editing this section's schema and rerunning the one command regenerates both files; nothing here duplicates what the schema already states.

Verified this session with an isolated rdflib/pyshacl venv (nothing installed into the repo): both generated files parse as valid Turtle; a hand-built test instance graph conforming to the schema produces zero SHACL findings, and a deliberately broken one (missing `hanzi`/`pinyin`/`locus`/`houseTranslation`, and an object property pointed at a literal instead of a `translation-rendering` individual) raises exactly the five expected violations, including a real `sh:class` mismatch pyshacl catches on its own.
