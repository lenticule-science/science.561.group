Dao (道) names, in the *Daodejing*'s own first line, both the way that generates and orders the world and the thing that line immediately says cannot be fixed by being named: 道可道，非常道, "the way that can be way-ed is not the constant way."

Dao is distinguished from de (德): dao is the generative, ordering way itself, where de is what a particular thing has when dao's operation is present and active in that thing specifically; dao is also distinguished from the loose vernacular English "the Tao," used as a synonym for a life philosophy in general, which drops chapter 1's own claim that the sayable dao is not the constant one.

## Renderings across published translations

Chapter 1's opening line is the single most retranslated sentence in the corpus of English Daodejing scholarship, and the variance is not merely stylistic:

- Legge (1891): "The Tao that can be trodden is not the enduring and unchanging Tao." [@legge1891]
- Waley (1934): "The Way that may truly be regarded as the Way is other than a permanent way." [@waley1934]
- Lau (1963): "The way that can be spoken of is not the constant way." [@lau1963]
- Mitchell (1988): "The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao." [@mitchell1988]

Lau's "constant" and Mitchell's "eternal" are not stylistic synonyms: "constant" points toward something reliable or invariable under description, where "eternal" implies a timeless absolute existing independently of description. The two readings carry different metaphysical commitments from the same four characters, 道可道，非常道.

## The section's own translation

This section's 2026 treatise on the *Yijing* opens with its own chapter-1 rewrite, in the estate's own settling-closure vocabulary, offered at FRAMING grade (a checked-as-far-as-it-goes interpretive move, not an assertion of final meaning):

> The Way that can be settled is not the constant Way.
> Yet what settles, settles somewhere—

This section's earlier (2024) essay gives an ungraded, pre-formal gloss on the same term, written before any of the estate's own formal apparatus existed: "The Way is beyond what can be Known or Described: it is the Source of the Dimensionality that makes things Knowable and Describable." Both are house readings of dao; neither is offered as settling what dao "really" means, and this glossary entry does not adjudicate between a settling-closure reading and a pre-formal cosmological one.
