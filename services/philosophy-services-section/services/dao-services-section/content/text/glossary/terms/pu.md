Pu (朴), "the uncarved block," names in chapters 19, 28, and 32 the condition of unworked wholeness a piece of wood, or a person, is in before being cut into distinct, named, functional shapes — chapter 28's 朴散則為器, "the uncarved block, dispersed, becomes vessels."

Pu's valuing of undividedness is distinguished from mere passivity or a refusal to use carved things: the chapter pairs pu with the sage's continued use of what has been carved from it, and pu is distinguished from wu wei — wu wei is a claim about how one acts, where pu is a claim about a prior undivided condition a thing or person can return to or be cut from.

## Renderings across published translations

Chapter 28's 朴散則為器 and its closing 復歸於朴 ("return to pu") are rendered:

- Legge (1891): "The unwrought material, when divided and distributed, forms vessels." [@legge1891]
- Lau (1963): "When the uncarved block shatters it becomes vessels. The sage makes use of these and becomes the lord over the officials." [@lau1963]
- Mitchell (1988): "The world is formed from the void, like utensils from a block of wood. The Master knows the utensils, yet keeps to the block: thus she can use all things." [@mitchell1988]
- Victor H. Mair (1990): "When the unhewn log is sawn apart, it is made into tools; When the sage is put to use, he becomes the chief of officials."
- Lin Yutang (1948): "Break up this uncarved wood And it is shaped into vessel In the hands of the Sage They become the officials and magistrates."

All five keep the same two images (wood cut into vessels; the sage governing officials) in the same order, which is why this glossary records pu's variance as chiefly stylistic: Legge's passive "is divided and distributed," Lau's more violent "shatters," and Mitchell's gender-marked "she" recast the same underlying claim rather than disputing it.

## The section's own translation

No existing carrier in this section formalizes pu against the estate's own mathematical apparatus, and this entry does not manufacture one. Offered at FRAMING grade, plainly: this section's own reading is that pu is not raw material waiting to be improved by carving, but the fact that carving is a real cost as well as a real gain — something is made into one particular useful thing, and thereby stops being everything else it could have been.
