Daodejing (道德經), "the classic (jing) of the way (dao) and its power (de)," is the received title of the work whose two named halves — the Dao chapters and the De chapters — are, per the Mawangdui silk-manuscript witness translated by Henricks, ordered in reverse from the received text, so the title's own word order is a fact about textual transmission rather than a fixed property of the work.

The plain transliterated title (Daodejing, Tao Te Ching) is distinguished from titles that substitute an interpretive translation for it before the first chapter is even reached — Waley's *The Way and Its Power*, or Ames & Hall's subtitle "Making This Life Significant" — each already committing to a reading of what the title itself claims.

## Renderings of the title across published editions

- Legge (1891): *The Tao Teh King* [@legge1891]
- Waley (1934): *The Way and Its Power: A Study of the Tao Te Ching and Its Place in Chinese Thought* [@waley1934]
- Lau (1963): *Tao Te Ching* [@lau1963]
- Mitchell (1988): *Tao Te Ching: A New English Version* [@mitchell1988]
- Ames & Hall (2003): *Dao De Jing: "Making This Life Significant": A Philosophical Translation* [@ames2003]
- Henricks (1989): *Lao-Tzu: Te-Tao Ching: A New Translation Based on the Recently Discovered Ma-wang-tui Texts* [@henricks1989]

Henricks's title reorders the two halves — "Te-Tao" rather than "Tao-Te" — because the 168 BCE Mawangdui silk manuscripts he translates carry the De chapters before the Dao chapters; this is the clearest available evidence that the title's word order is a manuscript-history fact, not a fixed one. This glossary does not hold a Guodian (1993 bamboo-slip) based citation, and states that gap rather than treating Mawangdui as the earliest available witness.

## The section's own translation

This section's own two books do not retranslate the title: the 2026 treatise takes its title and its structural cue from the *Yijing* instead, stated explicitly in its own preface as a decision not to attempt a third pass at the *Daodejing*, which the estate had "already spent itself on" via two prior 2025 works. This entry records that the *Daodejing*'s title itself remains untranslated by this section's own house voice, by that preface's own explicit choice, rather than by oversight.
