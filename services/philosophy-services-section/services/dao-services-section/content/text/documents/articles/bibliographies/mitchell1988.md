# Mitchell 1988 — Tao Te Ching: A New English Version

New York: Harper & Row.

## Why this is central to the glossary

The most widely read English-language version, and a deliberate outlier in this glossary's set: Mitchell does not read Chinese, and his text is a poetic adaptation built by consulting existing scholarly translations rather than a translation from source. Citing it as itself, not as a scholarly translation, is the discipline this glossary tries to hold across every entry that includes it.

## What it does not settle

Because Mitchell is not translating the Chinese directly, a rendering unique to him (present in no scholarly translation this glossary also cites) is evidence about Mitchell's own reading, not independent evidence about 道德經. This glossary marks such renderings as his alone rather than as a second attestation.

## Bridges

- To [pu](../../../glossary/terms/pu.md) — his chapter 28 "block of wood" / "the Master" rendering is this glossary's most widely recognized popular English phrasing of 朴.
