# Legge 1891 — The Tao Teh King

In The Texts of Taoism, Sacred Books of the East vol. 39 (Oxford: Clarendon Press). Public domain.

## Why this is central to the glossary

The earliest complete scholarly English translation still in wide circulation, and the source almost every later translation answers to, agrees with, or breaks from by name. Wade-Giles romanized ("Tao", "Tao"), missionary-sinological register, heavily footnoted against the classical commentarial tradition rather than offered as free-standing literature.

## What it does not settle

Legge reads Dao and Heaven through categories borrowed from his own comparative-religion project (he was simultaneously translating the Confucian classics for the same series), and his own footnotes register discomfort with rendering 自然 (ziran) and 無為 (wuwei) in English at all. He does not resolve, and says so, whether "Tao" names a personal or an impersonal principle.

## Bridges

- To [dao](../../../glossary/terms/dao.md) — chapter 1's "enduring and unchanging Tao" is this glossary's oldest still-cited English rendering of 常道.
- To [de](../../../glossary/terms/de.md) and [wu-wei](../../../glossary/terms/wu-wei.md) — Legge's own footnoted hesitation over both terms is recorded rather than smoothed over in this glossary.
