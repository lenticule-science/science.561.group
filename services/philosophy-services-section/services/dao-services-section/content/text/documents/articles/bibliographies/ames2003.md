# Ames–Hall 2003 — Dao De Jing: "Making This Life Significant": A Philosophical Translation

New York: Ballantine Books.

## Why this is central to the glossary

A process-philosophy reading built to resist importing a transcendent, noun-like "the Way" into English: 道 is glossed and frequently rendered as "way-making", a gerund naming an ongoing process rather than a thing or a place a process happens in. It is this glossary's sharpest attested contrast to the noun-centered readings (Legge's, Lau's, Mitchell's "the Tao").

## What it does not settle

The gerund choice is itself an interpretive commitment against rival readings (Daoism as cosmology naming a source, versus Daoism as process philosophy naming an activity) that classical commentary does not settle; this glossary records the choice as one attested reading among several, not as the resolution of that dispute.

## Bridges

- To [dao](../../../glossary/terms/dao.md) — "way-making" is this glossary's clearest process-reading contrast to every noun-centered rendering it also cites.
