# Henricks 1989 — Lao-Tzu: Te-Tao Ching: A New Translation Based on the Recently Discovered Ma-wang-tui Texts

New York: Ballantine Books.

## Why this is central to the glossary

Translates the 168 BCE Mawangdui silk-manuscript text (discovered 1973), which orders the two halves of the received work in reverse: the De (德) chapters before the Dao (道) chapters, hence "Te-Tao" rather than "Tao-Te" in the title. It is this glossary's evidence that the text's own two-part structure, and which half is read first, is a manuscript-history question, not a fixed fact.

## What it does not settle

Henricks's edition predates the 1993 Guodian bamboo-slip discovery (an even earlier, partial, and differently-ordered witness); this glossary does not carry a Guodian-based citation and states that gap rather than implying Mawangdui is the earliest available witness.

## Bridges

- To [daodejing](../../../glossary/terms/daodejing.md) — the Mawangdui chapter-order reversal is this glossary's primary evidence that the title's own two halves are not a fixed textual fact.
