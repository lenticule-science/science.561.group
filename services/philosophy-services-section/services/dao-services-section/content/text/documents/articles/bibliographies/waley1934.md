# Waley 1934 — The Way and Its Power: A Study of the Tao Te Ching and Its Place in Chinese Thought

London: George Allen & Unwin.

## Why this is central to the glossary

Waley refused the Wade-Giles loanword "Tao" on principle and translated 道 as "the Way" throughout, the choice most later English titles that avoid transliteration ("The Way and Its Power", "the Way") descend from. His long introduction is a standard short essay on classical Daoist thought independent of the translation itself.

## What it does not settle

Waley reads the text as a composite anthology assembled by later editors from older sayings rather than as a single author's unified work, a historical-critical stance this glossary neither adopts nor argues against; the glossary cites his renderings without adopting his compilation theory.

## Bridges

- To [dao](../../../glossary/terms/dao.md) — "the Way" is the rendering choice this glossary's own house translation is written in deliberate relation to.
- To [ziran](../../../glossary/terms/ziran.md) — Waley's "the Self-so" for 自然 is this glossary's clearest attested non-agentive rendering.
