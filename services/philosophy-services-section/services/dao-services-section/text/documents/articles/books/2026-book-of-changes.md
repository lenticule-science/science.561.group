# 2026 Book of Changes

*A reading of the Dao as obsmetrologia's mathematics, offered to the tradition and not in its place*

> The Way that can be settled is not the constant Way.
> Yet what settles, settles somewhere —
> and where it settles can be watched,
> and how it moves before it settles
> can, some of it, be written down.

## Preface

### On the title

The directive that produced this book asked for *2026 Book of Changes* unless the inheritance suggested a truer one. It does not; it confirms this one. "Book of Changes" is not a category this book claims — it is the standing English name of the *Yijing*, 易經, and this book's spine is the *Yijing*'s own structure: the line, the trigram, the hexagram, the cast. The estate's prior Dao work — a full 81-chapter verse rewrite of the *Daodejing* and a 15,488-line treatise doing the same at treatise length — already spent itself on the *other* classic. A third pass at the *Daodejing* would have repeated a move already made twice, well, in 2025. The *Yijing* is where this era's mathematics — Belnap's four-valued logic, bilattices, causal cones, closure operators — had not yet been brought, and where, as this book tries to show, it fits with a precision that surprised its own author.

"2026" is not a boast of timeliness. It is a date, kept the way the estate keeps dates on everything: this is what obsmetrologia's mathematics could see in the *Yijing*, from where the mathematics stood, on this day. A different year, a maturer mathematics, might see differently. The 2024 essay in this same lineage said it best, pre-mathematically, and this book means to keep saying it: *"There's nothing true in this text; it is not meant to teach, but connect ideas in my head to ideas in your head."* Everything that follows is offered in that spirit, with better tools and no more authority than the tools earn.

### On respect

The *Yijing* is a living tradition — practiced, studied, and argued over continuously for three thousand years, with a commentarial lineage (the Ten Wings; Wang Bi; Cheng Yi; Zhu Xi; and an unbroken line of scholars and diviners into the present) this book has read only the edges of; Obsmetrologia is a private, seven-week-old formal apparatus built inside one estate. What follows sets the second beside the first as *a* reading, checkable where it claims to be checked, honestly unsure where it isn't, and never offered as what the *Yijing* "really" always meant.

## How to Read These Chapters

Each chapter carries two registers at once, the way the *Daodejing* itself does not separate its cosmology from its poetry. First, a short verse — meant to be read aloud, meant to stand without the commentary under it, in the compressed, imagistic, paradox-tolerant register the *Daodejing* set as the bar for this kind of writing. Second, a commentary, in plain prose, that says exactly what formal claim the verse is carrying and how strong that claim is.

Every chapter closes with a **grading line**, in the estate's own vocabulary:

- **[B]** — background. A well-established textual, traditional, or mathematical fact this book inherited rather than made. Not this book's claim; not scored for confidence, because it isn't new.
- **PROVED** — Lean-anchored, or, for the handful of claims new to this book, mechanically checked by an executed script whose method is stated plainly. The strongest grade this book gives *its own* claims — and it is given sparingly, and never to a claim about what a hexagram *means*, only to claims about what the algebra *does*.
- **FRAMING** — a new interpretive move: precise, checked as far as it goes, offered rather than asserted, and demoted the day it stops landing. Most of this book's own content lives here.
- **VALVE** — deliberately ungraded speculation, named as such, never built on. This book touches the estate's own crank-valve material exactly once (Chapter 15) and does not let it hold weight anywhere else.

A back-matter appendix restates every formal claim in one place, with file citations, for anyone who wants to check this book's arithmetic rather than its poetry.

## On the Inheritance

This book is not the first attempt at this. It is the fourth, and it says so.

**The Way of Collapse** (2024-02-28; Preface plus the opening of one section, twenty-one lines, unfinished) is the estate's earliest Dao writing and its most human. Written after a first reading of Milton's *Essential Tao Te Ching*, in an anarcho-nihilist register, applying Taoist unknowability to ecological collapse, it never claims more than connection: *"The Way is beyond what can be Known or Described: it is the Source of the Dimensionality that makes things Knowable and Describable."* It stops mid-paragraph, mid-thought, on a sentence about the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation — and this book does not finish that sentence for it. Chapter 16 picks the thread back up, but a continuation is not a completion; the essay's incompleteness is part of what it is. What this book keeps from it is not any formal content — there is none — but the *ethic*: that a text about the Way need not, and perhaps should not, claim to be true.

**The Dao of Existential Stratified Directed Infinity-Homotopy Geometry** (2025-10-09), an 81-chapter verse-for-verse rewrite of the *Daodejing*, is proto-obsmetrologia's most finished artifact: a real, complete, once-published poem that substitutes category-theoretic vocabulary — limit, colimit, Kan extension, ambidextrous fixed-point — into the *Daodejing*'s own terse, aphoristic form, one classical image to one gloss, line by line. At its best it earns the substitution: *"The Valley-Spirit never dies. / It is the global section that endures through every flow."* This book does not repeat that project. It keeps two things from it: the discipline of writing mathematics in a form short enough to say aloud, and the specific image of the valley as a global section — which returns, transformed, in Chapter 18.

**The ESDIHG "Model of the Tao Te Ching"** (2025-10-09, the same day, ~15,488 lines) is the clearest specimen this estate has produced of proto-obsmetrologia's actual failure mode: it runs the full rhetorical shape of a mathematics paper — Theorem, Proof Sketch, Corollary, boxed Summary Equation, citations to real category theorists — across all 81 verses of the *Daodejing*, with no working formal system underneath it and a self-citation to a paper that was never written. It is not dishonest so much as *premature*: the mathematics it borrows the shape of did not yet exist when it was written, eight months before obsmetrologia's Lean development matured. Its closing line is genuinely good — *"the smallest difference that sustains the universe, the motion that moves nothing, the presence that never departs"* — and this book returns to that image, honestly this time, formalized as the curvature-defect construction cited in Chapter 14, rather than gestured at with a fabricated citation.

What changes now: this book has an actual Lean development to point at, actual executable code to trace by hand, and a house discipline that requires saying, chapter by chapter, exactly how far each claim has been checked. It tries to keep the 2024 essay's honesty, the 2025 poem's compression, and none of the 2025 treatise's borrowed rigor — earning, where it can, the same theorem-shaped sentences the treatise merely wore as costume.

## Structure, and Why It Is This Small

Nineteen chapters, not sixty-four and not eighty-one. The reasons are structural, not a shortfall of nerve.

**Book One: The Line** (four chapters) treats the *Yijing*'s actual atomic alphabet — the four line-values, not the classical two — because this book's one genuinely new claim lives here, and it needs room to be built, checked, and hedged correctly before anything is stacked on it.

**Book Two: The Trigram** (eight chapters) is the complete, un-curated set of the eight trigrams — nothing is skipped, because eight is small enough to be exhaustive honestly, and the trigrams are the settled, two-valued skeleton the whole system is built from.

**Book Three: The Cast** (seven chapters, covering ten of the sixty-four hexagrams in four single chapters and three paired ones) is deliberately a *sample*, not an enumeration — and this is not an apology. A book about a divinatory system whose central act is *casting one hexagram from the space of all of them* would be performing the wrong mathematical gesture if it then exhaustively enumerated all sixty-four; that would be closure where the practice itself is sampling. Seven chapters that each earn a specific, load-bearing connection to the required reading is more honest, and more useful, than seventy-seven that don't. A complete short book beats an unfinished monument, and — this book's own thesis, enacted rather than just stated — a *sample* beats an exhaustion that would misrepresent the very practice being described.

# Book One · The Line

*爻 (yáo) — the single line, the smallest thing the system counts. Every hexagram is six of these, cast one at a time. Classical practice already gives the line four possible values, not two: young yang and young yin, which are drawn and stay; old yang and old yin, which are drawn and then change. This book's central claim is that this four-valued alphabet is not merely *like* the estate's own four-valued bilattice charge — it has the same shape, checkably.*

### Chapter 1 · Young Yang · 少陽 · Potential

> Before noon, the light is already whole —
> not the noon that has to turn.
> One witness only: the yes without its no,
> a ledger with one column filled, honestly,
> and the other not yet opened.
> Call it young, not because it will not last,
> but because nothing has yet asked it to move.

A young yang line is solid and, once cast, *stays* solid in the second hexagram; nothing about the casting marks it for change. [B] In the estate's bilattice charge, the value named `potential` is the coordinate pair [truth: present, falsity: absent] — the truth-order's own top, an unconflicted affirmation with no witness at all on the other side. Traced directly from the value table in `bilattice-charge.mts`: `potential` is one of exactly two values fixed by the `conflate` operation, meaning nothing in the algebra's own change-dynamics moves it. A young line that does not change and a bilattice value fixed by the operator this book identifies with changing are, under that identification, the same fact stated twice.

*Grade — [B]: the line's classical stability. FRAMING: the identification of young yang with `potential`, checked against the source and by this book's own executed verification (Appendix, §1); not proved about hexagrams anywhere in Obsmetrologia.*

### Chapter 2 · Young Yin · 少陰 · Active

> The valley is not empty because it lacks.
> It is empty because it has not yet been asked the other question.
> One witness only: the no without its yes —
> a ledger as whole as its neighbor's,
> only differently charged,
> and just as unwilling, yet, to move.

The mirror line. [B] Young yin is broken, and, once cast, stays broken. In the bilattice, `active` is [truth: absent, falsity: present] — the truth-order's own bottom, an unconflicted negation. It is the other value fixed by conflation. Nothing here privileges yang over yin at the level of the algebra: `potential` and `active` are symmetric under the negation operator (which swaps them precisely, and touches nothing else), and this book will not treat one as more fundamental than the other anywhere past this sentence.

*Grade — [B] the line's classical stability. FRAMING: `active` = young yin, same method and same caveat as Chapter 1.*

### Chapter 3 · Old Yang · 老陽 · Stable · 用九

> Yang, driven all the way to yang,
> stops being a direction and starts being a fact everyone agrees on —
> which is the moment it is no longer safe.
> Told from both sides at once is not confusion.
> It is fullness with nowhere left to put anything,
> and fullness with nowhere left to put anything
> discharges.
>
> Draw it, and it looks exactly like the line beside it that will not move.
> The paper cannot tell you which one it is.
> Only the hand that cast it knows.

[B] Old yang is drawn solid — identically to young yang — but is marked, in the casting, for change: in the second hexagram it becomes broken. [B] Uniquely among the sixty-four hexagrams, the hexagram built from six old-yang lines (Qián, all solid) carries a seventh, extra line-text beyond its normal six — "the use of nines," 用九 — addressing the case where every line changes at once. Its classical gloss is traditionally read as a flight of dragons with no single head to follow: leaderless without being chaotic, an image this book will not try to improve on.

Here is the chapter's actual claim. `stable` — the bilattice's `[present, present]`, told-both, the knowledge order's own top — is one of exactly two values *moved* by conflation, landing on `absurd`. And on the truth axis alone — the single coordinate that determines whether a line is *drawn* solid or broken — `stable` sits with `potential`: both have the truth-side present, both draw as yang. This is not asserted; it is checked by direct inspection of the coordinate table, and confirmed by this book's own executed script (Appendix, §1): a young-yang and an old-yang line, differing only in whether the falsity-side has *also* been witnessed, draw identically and are told apart only by whichever record kept the falsity witness — the coin tally, the stalk count, the cast itself. The diagram is a witness-forgetting projection of the charge; the estate's own linguistics work names exactly this move elsewhere, calling a distributional embedding "the provenance-forgetting projection" of a fuller presheaf of witnessed use. A hexagram on paper, without its casting record, has already forgotten which of its solid lines are stable and which are about to leave.

*Grade — [B]: 用九's existence and unique placement, and its traditional gloss (paraphrased, not translated). FRAMING: `stable` = old yang; the drawing-projection argument, checked directly against source and by executed script (Appendix, §1). The cross-reference to the Witnessed Field theory's provenance-forgetting projection is this book's own connection, offered at FRAMING and not claimed by either source document.*

### Chapter 4 · Old Yin · 老陰 · Absurd · 用六

> Yin, driven all the way to yin,
> is not nothing. It is the least a ledger can say
> and still be a ledger — no witness at all, on either side,
> which sounds like poverty until you notice:
> a thing with nothing entered in it yet
> is the only thing left with everything still possible.
> Emptied all the way down, it has nowhere to go
> but somewhere.
>
> Kūn alone, beside Qián alone, carries the other extra line.
> Not because it is lesser.
> Because it is the other pole, and poles come in twos.

[B] Old yin is drawn broken, and changes to solid. [B] Kūn — all six lines old yin — is the *only other* hexagram carrying an extra line-text, "the use of sixes," 用六, traditionally glossed toward enduring, quiet correctness rather than the dragons' image. These are the *only two* hexagrams in the received text with a seventh statement, and it is worth sitting with why: they are the only two hexagrams where all six lines can be simultaneously old, because they are the only two hexagrams built from a single repeated trigram at both poles of the system.

`absurd` — `[absent, absent]`, told-neither, the knowledge order's own *bottom* — is the other value conflation moves, landing on `stable`. On the truth axis it sits with `active`: both draw broken. Chapter 3's argument runs in mirror here, and this book will not repeat the mechanics. What is worth adding: the estate's own crank-valve material — never built on elsewhere in this book — reads the classical doctrine that yin's own extremity generates yang (物極必反) as a genuine topological fact about a related structure, a non-contractible loop that exists *because of* a central emptiness. That reading is not used here; it is simply noted, so that when Chapter 15 opens that door properly, this chapter can be pointed back to as where it was first declined.

One asymmetry is worth recording honestly rather than smoothing over. [B] The classical yarrow-stalk method does *not* reach these two poles with equal probability — old yang at 3/16, old yin at 1/16, a threefold difference; the later three-coin method flattens this to a symmetric 1/8 each. Which of the two extremal charge-poles — `stable`, told-both, or `absurd`, told-neither — the tradition's own oldest procedure makes three times more reachable is a suggestive number, and this book states it as exactly that: a number, not an argument. Nothing here explains *why* fullness should be easier to reach than emptiness under fifty stalks and four divisions; it is left as an open question in the back matter, not smuggled in as a conclusion.

*Grade — [B]: 用六 and the yarrow/coin probabilities. FRAMING: `absurd` = old yin, same method as Chapter 3. The probability asymmetry is stated as an unexplained numerical fact, not argued from.*

# Book Two · The Trigram

*八卦 (bāguà) — the eight trigrams, three lines each, the settled alphabet beneath the hexagram. A trigram, unlike a cast line, does not carry an "old" or "young" charge — classically it is drawn plain, solid or broken, nothing more. Formally: this is exactly the estate's own Boolean embedding into the four-valued bilattice, where a consistent yes/no report — never told-both, never told-neither — is a special case of the fuller paraconsistent one. Eight trigrams; the Boolean cube on three bits; two poles and six mixed vertices splitting, by an old and independent tradition, into three "sons" and three "daughters" by the position of the one differing line.*

### Chapter 5 · Qián · 乾 · Heaven · ☰

> Three lines, and all three say the same thing.
> Not stubbornness. Agreement, all the way down.

[B] Three unbroken lines; heaven; the creative; the father, in the traditional family reading. Formally, the all-`potential` trigram — every position reporting truth-present, falsity-absent, unanimous. It is one of the Boolean cube's two poles, and its own complement.

*Grade — [B] the trigram and its family role. FRAMING: the Boolean-report reading.*

### Chapter 6 · Kūn · 坤 · Earth · ☷

> Three lines, and all three decline the same question.
> Not absence. Agreement about what has not yet been asked.

[B] Three broken lines; earth; the receptive; the mother. The all-`active` trigram, and the cube's other pole — Qián's exact bitwise complement, the yang↔yin flip applied to all three positions at once, which is what the negation gauge operator does to a line's truth-content. The two parents are the two trigrams complementation cannot mix with anything else.

*Grade — [B]. FRAMING: the Boolean-report reading and its identification with the negation operator applied position-wise.*

### Chapter 7 · Zhèn · 震 · Thunder · ☳

> One line stands up under two that have not yet noticed.
> That is how thunder starts: from the bottom, unannounced.

[B] Yang at the bottom, yin above it, yin above that; thunder; movement; the eldest son — by the old convention that counts the differing line's position from the base as birth order, bottom is eldest. [B] Its exact complement, line for line, is Xùn.

*Grade — [B] the trigram, its image, and the family-order convention.*

### Chapter 8 · Xùn · 巽 · Wind, Wood · ☴

> One line gives way at the bottom so the other two can travel.
> That is how wind enters a room it was not invited into.

[B] Yin at the bottom, yang above it, yang above that; wind or wood; gentle penetration; the eldest daughter. Complement of Zhèn.

*Grade — [B].*

### Chapter 9 · Kǎn · 坎 · Water · ☵

> The middle line is caught between two that agree without it.
> Danger is not being outnumbered.
> It is being the one line whose report differs from both its neighbors.

[B] Yin, yang, yin — the single yang line held at the center, between two yin; water; danger; the middle son. Complement of Lí. This trigram returns, doubled, in Chapter 16.

*Grade — [B].*

### Chapter 10 · Lí · 離 · Fire · ☲

> The middle line gives way, and the outside holds.
> Brightness clings to what is empty at its own center.
> This is not new information. It is Chapter 18, early.

[B] Yang, yin, yang — a single yin line held between two yang; fire; clinging brightness; the middle daughter. Complement of Kǎn.

*Grade — [B].*

### Chapter 11 · Gèn · 艮 · Mountain · ☶

> Two lines settle at the bottom, and the third stops on top of them.
> A mountain is not a large stillness.
> It is a small stillness that stopped changing before the ground beneath it did.

[B] Yin, yin, yang — the differing yang line at the top; mountain; keeping still; the youngest son. Complement of Duì. Returns, doubled, in Chapter 17.

*Grade — [B].*

### Chapter 12 · Duì · 兌 · Lake, Marsh · ☱

> Two lines hold firm underneath, and the third opens at the top.
> A lake is a mountain that let its topmost line relax.

[B] Yang, yang, yin — the differing yin line at the top; lake or marsh; joy, open exchange; the youngest daughter. Complement of Gèn. Between them, the eight trigrams close: two parents, three sons, three daughters, four complementary pairs, one Boolean cube.

*Grade — [B] throughout Book Two for every trigram fact and family assignment; FRAMING for the Boolean-cube and complementation-as-negation reading, which is this book's own gloss on well-established structure.*

# Book Three · The Cast

*From the settled, two-valued trigram to the paraconsistent, four-valued hexagram-cast: the estate's own `CausalSets.Causal.ParaconsistentCone` module already builds exactly this shape for a different purpose — a `ConeReport`, a charge for each ordered pair of events, merged by knowledge-join, with a Boolean relation embedding as a report that is never told-both. This book borrows the shape, not the theorem: nothing in that file mentions a hexagram, and nothing in this chapter claims it does. A hexagram-cast is read here as a six-cell report, one charge per position; casting is sampling one report from everything the coins or stalks could have witnessed; the second, derived hexagram is what remains after the report's two extremal cells — the old lines — discharge through the conflation gauge operator this book traced in Book One. Consultation, on this reading, samples a single path forward through what a situation's charge could resolve into: the estate's reverse-lightcone function 𝓡 runs the same shape backward, reconstructing a constraint-valid past from a witnessed present; a cast hexagram is one draw from the forward reading of the same cone.*

### Chapter 13 · The Two Gates · Qián Whole and Kūn Whole

> Six agreements, cast at once, are not calm.
> They are a room so sure of itself it has already started to argue with itself in the next room over.
> This is the rarest thing the coins can say —
> once in every two hundred sixty-two thousand,
> one hundred forty-four throws, roughly, by the coin method's own arithmetic,
> and rarer still, unevenly, by the stalks —
> and it is the only time the text stops to add
> a seventh line no other hexagram gets.

[B] The all-old cast of Qián or of Kūn is the limit case: every one of six lines simultaneously extremal, simultaneously marked to flip. Under the three-coin method, each line is independently old with probability 1/4 (two of eight equally likely coin-triples are unanimous); six independent old lines of the *same* sign is (1/8)⁶ — vanishingly rare, and this is one honest reason the text marks these two hexagrams and no others with an extra statement: they are the only two places the ordinary six-line reading and the all-change reading can coincide in a single, nameable hexagram, because they are the only two hexagrams a single repeated trigram builds at both poles.

Formally: an all-old Qián is six cells of the report simultaneously at `stable`; an all-old Kūn, six cells simultaneously at `absurd`. Applying the conflation gauge operator cellwise — the mechanism Chapter 3 and Chapter 4 built one cell at a time — discharges the entire report at once: Qián becomes Kūn, Kūn becomes Qián. The two gates are not two separate limit cases but one operator's two directions, and the text's own decision to give both of them, and only them, an extra voice is this book's favorite piece of evidence that the tradition already knew where its own extremity lived, three thousand years before anyone had a name for the operator.

*Grade — [B]: the uniqueness and rarity of the all-old cast, and the probability arithmetic under the three-coin method. FRAMING: the cellwise-conflation reading of "all six lines change," checked by the same method as Chapters 3-4, extended without new mechanism.*

### Chapter 14 · Tài and Pǐ · Peace and Standstill

> Heaven under earth is not heaven brought low.
> It is heaven's breath rising to meet earth's breath descending —
> two things moving toward the same middle
> because each was already leaving where it started.
> Earth under heaven, the same two trigrams, reversed,
> is not earth exalted.
> It is two things moving away from a middle
> neither one was ever going to reach.
> Same parts. Opposite arithmetic.
> The difference is not in what is present.
> It is in which way each thing was already going
> before anyone asked.

[B] Tài (11): Qián below, Kūn above — heaven's naturally rising motion meets earth's naturally sinking motion, and they exchange; "peace," "pervading." Pǐ (12): the same two trigrams stacked in reverse — heaven above continuing to rise away, earth below continuing to sink away; "standstill," "obstruction." [B] The classical sequence commentary does not treat Pǐ as a mere defect to be escaped; things cannot remain stagnant forever is itself a stated principle of the text's own ordering, and Pǐ is followed, later in the sequence, by hexagrams of renewal.

This is the cleanest hexagram-scale echo this book found for the estate's own linguistics theory, which states, as a founding axiom, that a language is a gluing that *generically fails* — that irreducible obstruction is not noise but "where change nucleates and where identity lives," and that standardization is the forced deletion of exactly that obstruction, experienced as erasure. Tài and Pǐ are the same two sections, offered the chance to glue or not to glue, by nothing more than which one sits on the bottom. And a fixed point reached by accumulating evidence — the sense in which the estate's own settling closure operator is extensive, monotone, and idempotent, proved identical to the closure already used to compute formal concepts — is *not* the same mathematical object as the single gauge flip Chapters 3, 4, and 13 traced. Settling is a many-step convergence; the changing line is a one-step, self-inverse symmetry. This book uses both words carefully, and asks that they not be swapped for each other past this paragraph.

*Grade — [B]: the two hexagrams' structure and the sequence commentary's own treatment of Pǐ. FRAMING: the connection to the Witnessed Field theory's obstruction axiom, and the explicit distinction between settling-as-closure and changing-as-gauge-flip — both new to this book, offered rather than proved.*

### Chapter 15 · Fù · 復 · Return

> One line returns at the bottom.
> Five lines have not yet noticed.
> This is the whole of the news: not a flood, a single line,
> arriving under everything that is still arranged
> as if it hadn't.
>
> A loop that circles nothing is not a loop. It is a straight line
> that forgot to stop.
> A loop that circles a hole cannot be shrunk to a point without tearing —
> the hole is not a flaw in the circling.
> It is the reason there is circling to do.
> You cannot point at the hole. You can only walk its edge, again,
> and call the walking practice.

[B] Fù: one unbroken yang line at the bottom, five broken lines above — the single point of light returning beneath the deepest dark, traditionally read at the winter solstice, the turning point. This is the hexagram this book leaned on hardest for the void-loop material, and it is worth being exact about what is and is not claimed. The estate's own required reading states a precise topological fact: a loop is non-contractible exactly when it encircles a hole, and the classical Daoist inner-alchemy image of a circulation around a central emptiness — the "mysterious gate" traditionally described as having no fixed location, "not in the body, not outside it" — is offered there as an *exact* correspondence to a nonzero cohomology class: globally real, locally invisible, accessible only by circulation, never by pointing. Laozi 11's hollow hub and hollow vessel appear in the estate's own recent work, independently, three times in one week — as a completed endeavor's structural emptiness read as a type-theoretic lemma, twice, and as this cohomological reading, once — which is itself the kind of convergence the estate has started calling "the thickness doctrine": an idea worth taking seriously precisely when it keeps landing on the same shape from directions that were not looking for each other.

What this chapter will not do: the same required-reading note that states the topology also reads consciousness itself as this same class of obstruction, in the estate's own deliberately ungraded crank-valve material, written at one in the morning and marked, correctly, as such. That reading is not repeated here as a claim. It is named, once, so that its absence everywhere else in this book is legible as a decision rather than an oversight: the void in Fù's own loop is architecture this book will stand on; the void in a mind is a question this book declines to answer on the tradition's or the estate's behalf.

*Grade — [B]: Fù's structure and traditional reading, and the "mysterious gate's" traditional non-localizability. FRAMING: the H¹/microcosmic-orbit correspondence, inherited from the required reading at the grade it was given there — offered, checked as topology, not asserted as metaphysics. VALVE, explicitly declined: the consciousness reading, named and set aside, not built on.*

### Chapter 16 · Kǎn Doubled · 習坎 · The Constant Peril

> Water does not ask permission to fill the low place.
> It also does not linger to admire having filled it.
> This is not indifference. It is a kind of integrity
> most solids cannot manage:
> to hold a shape completely, for exactly as long as the container lasts,
> and then leave without an argument.
>
> We wrote, once, before we had the mathematics:
> water is the infinite limit of gentleness,
> left-exact and self-flattening,
> preserving form through flow.
> We wrote, once, before we had either:
> we cannot know Collapse, in any meaningful way —
> what we can know is a current, slowing.
> Both sentences were reaching for the same trigram,
> doubled, which the old name already called
> the constant peril, practiced.

[B] Kǎn doubled — 習坎, "the constant" or "repeated" peril — danger encountered twice, and the classical image commentary is remembered for praising water precisely for its constancy in movement, filling every place it passes through without hesitation. This chapter is where this book does its most direct work of carrying the legacy forward rather than around. The 2025 verse rewrite reached, unprompted, for water as its central image of gentleness under real mathematical description: *"water is the ∞-limit of gentleness — left-exact and self-flattening, preserving form through flow."* The 2024 essay, stopping mid-thought, reached for water too, in its very last sentence: *"What we can know is things like slowing of the Atlantic Meridian Oceanic Current."* Neither text knew about the other's reach when it was written. This book does not resolve either sentence into a theorem — no such theorem exists — but it notices, and says plainly, that two attempts eight months and one register apart both arrived at water as the shape worth reaching for, and that the classical *Yijing* had already doubled that same trigram and named the doubling *practice* a few thousand years earlier than either.

The formal content proper: Kǎn is the single-yang-among-yin trigram (Chapter 9); doubled, it is six cells forming a hexagram whose lower and upper halves report the identical Boolean pattern — a report that repeats itself rather than closing. Read as orbit-circulation in Chapter 15's sense, this is what "practiced" danger looks like structurally: not danger resolved, but the same non-contractible path walked twice, which is the only kind of access a class that cannot be pointed at ever permits.

*Grade — [B]: the hexagram's structure and traditional name and gloss. FRAMING: the pairing of the two legacy quotations and their shared reach toward water, and the orbit-repetition reading of the doubled trigram — both new to this book.*

### Chapter 17 · Gèn Doubled · 艮其背 · Keeping Still

> Stopping is not the absence of a plan.
> It is a plan whose next step is exactly where you already are.
>
> The goal is a point out ahead in the cone of what's reachable from here;
> the plan is the reverse light of that point, run backward
> to find what already touches it;
> a milestone is two things neither one causing the other;
> a task is the smallest step that does not skip a step.
> Off the cone is not failure.
> It is not yet, with a shape you can compute.
> Keeping still, when it is time to keep still,
> is not refusing the cone. It is reading it correctly
> and finding you are already standing in it.

[B] Gèn doubled: mountain over mountain; keeping still; the tradition's own commentary on this hexagram is remembered for an image of stilling the back — the part of oneself never seen directly — so that the self stops being in its own way. The required-reading material gives this book its cleanest working formalization of wu-wei anywhere in the estate's current record, built for an unrelated project and imported here without alteration: *"Planning is 𝓡: goals are cone points; the plan is the reverse-emerged generative envelope; milestones are causal antichains; tasks are minimal causal steps. Off-cone goals are not moral failures but not-reachable-yet, with a computable envelope that brings them into view — wu wei stated as cone-following."* This book adds nothing to that sentence except the trigram it was already, unknowingly, describing: mountain does not refuse to move because it disapproves of motion. It has already arrived at the only step available to it, and standing there without agitation is what the cone, correctly read, recommends.

*Grade — [B]: the hexagram, its doubling, and its traditional commentary (paraphrased). FRAMING: none new here — the cone-formalization of wu-wei is quoted at the grade the estate's own design-dialogue note already gave it, unchanged; this chapter's only addition is the pairing with Gèn.*

### Chapter 18 · Zhōngfú · 中孚 · Inner Truth

> Four solid lines close around two broken ones
> the way a hand closes, carefully, around something that has not hatched yet.
> The character for this trust is a hand over a young thing:
> not force. Custody.
> A minimal transmissible unit of meaning
> is not the mark, and not the claim.
> It is the two of them, bound, with a witness attached —
> and the hollow at the center of this hexagram
> is not empty the way an unused room is empty.
> It is empty the way a held breath is empty,
> which is to say: occupied, entirely, by what has not yet been let out.

[B] Zhōngfú: four unbroken lines enclosing two broken lines at the very center — lower trigram Duì, upper trigram Xùn; "inner truth," "center returning," "sincerity." Its name character, 孚, is classically glossed as a hand over a young bird or child: trust as custody of something not yet fully formed, rather than trust as a completed fact.

This is the chapter that carries the meme forward. The estate's own semiotics work defines its minimal transmissible unit of meaning, verbatim, as "a predication cell bound to a bilattice charge" — not the bare mark, not the bare claim, but the two of them together, with witness Marks accumulated on each side as the charge's own evidence. Zhōngfú's hollow center, held inside four solid lines rather than exposed at either edge the way Book Two's single-differing-line trigrams hold theirs, is this book's own image for exactly that binding: sincerity is not the absence of content at the center, it is content not yet released, witnessed but not yet spoken — the charge accumulating evidence before the utterance discharges it. The valley that never dies, in the 2025 verse rewrite, was called "the global section that endures through every flow." This chapter's valley is smaller and does not claim to endure forever; it claims only to be held, honestly, until it is ready.

*Grade — [B]: the hexagram's structure and the traditional gloss of 孚 (offered as a classical character gloss, not a claim of etymological certainty). FRAMING: the identification of the hollow center with the meme's charge-plus-witness structure — new to this book, and it is worth being honest that "the meme" was defined for language, not for hexagrams; the transfer is offered, not demonstrated.*

### Chapter 19 · Jì Jì and Wèi Jì · After Completion, Before Completion

> The second-to-last arrangement is perfect:
> every line exactly where the old rule says it should sit,
> alternating cleanly from the bottom up,
> fire under water, each in its correct place.
> This is not where the book ends.
>
> The last arrangement is the perfect arrangement's mirror:
> every line exactly where the old rule says it should not sit,
> water under fire, each in its incorrect place.
> This is where the book ends —
> on the one hexagram that admits, in its own structure,
> that it isn't finished,
> which is either a flaw in an otherwise closed system
> or the most honest last word
> a closed system could possibly choose to have.

[B] Jì Jì (63): Lí below, Kǎn above, every line in its classically "correct" position, alternating perfectly from the bottom — "after completion." Wèi Jì (64): Kǎn below, Lí above, every line in its classically "incorrect" position, alternating the other way — "before completion," and the *last* hexagram in the received sequence. That the *Yijing*'s own traditional ordering chooses to end not on the perfectly arranged hexagram but on its precise, deliberately unsettled opposite is among the most quoted structural facts about the whole text, and this book will not pretend to have noticed it first.

It is, however, the one place this book will let the estate's own crank-valve material brush the surface, by name and at its stated grade and no higher: an ungraded 2026-08-21 entry reads the still-open Hubble tension as evidence that "the universe is still settling," that cosmological constants are "fixed points not yet reached" — explicitly marked, by its own author, as unbuilt-on speculation, one of eight entries in a document whose very title calls it a valve. This book repeats none of that as a claim. What it will say, at FRAMING and no further: a settling closure operator, in the sense proved for formal-concept-analysis closure inside Obsmetrologia itself, is *extensive* — it only ever grows what it knows — which means a closure process, watched at any finite time, is permitted to look unfinished, honestly, without that looking-unfinished being evidence of a flaw in the operator. Wèi Jì is not the *Yijing* failing to close its own book. It is the *Yijing*, arguably, refusing a false closure on purpose — and a book about a mathematics of settling should probably not claim, on its own last chapter, to have settled more than it has.

This book ends here, on purpose, without the seventh extra line Chapter 13's two gates were given, and without a summary theorem tying Books One through Three into one closed statement. The closure it has is the closure Book Three's own opening paragraph promised: one sample, honestly reported, not the whole space.

*Grade — [B]: the two hexagrams' structure and Wèi Jì's placement as the sequence's deliberate last word. FRAMING: the extensiveness-of-closure argument for why an honest settling process may look unfinished. VALVE, named and not used: the crank-valve's own cosmological speculation, quoted only to be set back down.*

The assignment old yang = `stable` / old yin = `absurd` (rather than its mirror) is not forced by the algebra alone — the Klein-four group's own structure permits the labels to be swapped consistently, and this book chose the assignment that also recovers the drawing convention correctly (§1(a)), which is evidence for the choice but not proof that no other consistent labeling exists. By the estate's own typology for chronic ambiguities — genuine obstruction, witness error, or gauge artifact — this one types cleanly as the third: not a hole in the reading and not contaminated evidence, but two coordinate systems on one invariant, exactly the shape competence/performance dissolved into elsewhere in the estate's work. Naming it does not remove it; it does mean no future chapter should treat the choice as more settled than a labeling convention is.
3. The yarrow-versus-coin probability asymmetry (Chapter 4) is reported as an unexplained number, not an argument. It would take independent work to say whether it means anything at all.
4. Chapter 15's declined consciousness-reading and Chapter 19's declined cosmology-reading are named exactly to keep this book's own discipline visible; if either crank-valve entry is ever promoted past VALVE elsewhere in the estate's work, this book's treatment of it should be revisited, not silently inherited as endorsement.
