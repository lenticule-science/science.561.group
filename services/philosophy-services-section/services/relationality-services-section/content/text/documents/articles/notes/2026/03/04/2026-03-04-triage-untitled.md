## **Title:** _Mutualism as Phenomenon, Mutual Aid as Platform_

### **Thesis:**

Mutualism should be understood as a phenomenon that emerges from shared constraint and relational necessity—not as a fixed form or moral code. In contrast, what is now often called “mutual aid” operates as a platformed format: a set of performative signals that stabilize theory or narrative, often retroactively. This paper contrasts these two logics, not to critique acts of care, but to surface how explanation is increasingly routed through performances of need that are legible to institutions but detached from the conditions that once produced mutualism.

---

## **I. Introduction: Mutualism Isn’t Moral. It’s Emergent.**

- Define mutualism as **phenomenon**:
    
    - Emerges under shared constraint, where neither market nor state can carry social reproduction
        
    - Relational, embedded, durational
        
    - **Citing:** Ostrom (commons as practice), Moten & Harney (logistics of care), Graeber (debt as social relation)
        
- Contrast with **mutual aid as platform**:
    
    - Aesthetically similar signals operating in a different media and institutional context
        
    - Extractable, referential, narratively stabilized
        
    - **Citing:** Povinelli (quasi-events, endurance), Berlant (affective labor and cruel optimism), Chun (synchronization and system maintenance)
        
- Core claim:
    
    - The shift from phenomenon to platform is not about misuse—it’s about **the redirection of care into meaning production infrastructure**.
        

---

## **II. Mutualism as Phenomenon: Entanglement Under Constraint**

- Historical and contemporary examples:
    
    - Rotating credit associations, unregulated housing defense, community eldercare
        
    - Examples from **Citing for Containment**: pre-Cop City forest practices that refused legibility​Citing for Containment
        
- Theoretical grounding:
    
    - **Ostrom**: Commons emerge from **situated relations**, not imposed design
        
    - **Graeber**: Obligations precede institutionalized debt; care circulates where coercion isn’t possible
        
    - **Moten & Harney**: Mutualism as logistics of study and survival, not planning
        
- Mutualism is **not** visible by default—it’s often suppressed by the systems it evades
    
    - **Berlant**: Intimate publics form under pressure, not policy
        
    - **Scott**: Once legible to the state, resistant practices lose their friction
        

---

## **III. Mutual Aid as Platform: Legibility as Performance**

- Post-pandemic mutual aid networks as case study:
    
    - Initial emergence as mutualism (2020): embedded, immediate, materially constrained
        
    - Later stabilization as **aesthetic template**: memes, funding links, moral signaling
        
- Platform logic:
    
    - Help becomes **content**
        
    - Crisis becomes **currency**
        
    - Care becomes a **proof-of-alignment** mechanism
        
- Theory of recursive explanation:
    
    - **Chun**: Interpretation functions as a cybernetic closure tool—aligns subjects with system rhythm
        
    - **Povinelli**: Endurance under quasi-events becomes a way to **keep systems appearing coherent**
        

---

## **IV. The Drift: From Embedded Relation to Explanatory Infrastructure**

- Concept of **latency**: relational forms that linger after their enabling conditions have passed
    
    - Today’s mutual aid often functions as a **residual simulation** of past mutualism
        
- Critique is not about the sincerity of ask/give
    
    - It’s about the **use of those signals to justify theories** that don’t emerge from the behavior itself
        
- **Citing for Containment** on how narrative coherence replaces lived contradiction​Citing for Containment
    
- **Mutualism becomes flattened into a legible “event”**, retrofitted to match abolitionist theory or organizational priorities
    

---

## **V. Refusing the Simulation Without Refusing Care**

- Diagnostic distinction:
    
    - “Am I embedded with this person?”
        
    - “Is this care or is it coherence production?”
        
    - **Is the system metabolizing this gesture into stability?**
        
- You can’t re-enter mutualism by mimicking its signals
    
    - You have to re-enter the **conditions** that made it necessary
        
- **Berlant & Chun**: Delay and interpretation do not produce clarity—they absorb volatility for the system
    
    - Need ≠ Narrative
        
    - Care ≠ Confirmation
        

---

## **VI. Strategic Implications: Rerouting Without Shame**

- Stop gatekeeping mutual aid on aesthetics
    
- Refuse to let platforms treat care as symbolic labor
    
- Let care persist without forcing it to validate frameworks
    
- Reframe for capacity:
    
    - Mutualism isn’t the goal
        
    - It’s the **symptom** of shared vulnerability
        

---

## **VII. Conclusion: Let Mutualism Die So It Can Return**

- Mutualism will come back when systems fail again
    
- When it does, it won’t be branded
    
- Don’t try to save the image of mutual aid—save the **relational field** that allowed it to emerge
    
- Final citation loop:
    
    - **Povinelli**: Endurance without legibility
        
    - **Bifo Berardi**: Burnout isn’t failure—it’s proof-of-work
        
    - **Governing by Confusion**: Explanation isn’t outside governance—it **completes the loop**
