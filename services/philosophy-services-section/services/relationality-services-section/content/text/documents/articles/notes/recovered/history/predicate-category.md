A **predicate category** is a small category, denoted $\mathcal{C}$, whose objects are [predicates](../../prose/teaching/explanations/predicate.md) and whose morphisms are generated by four canonical classes:

- [predicate specialization](../transformers/predicate-specialization.md)
- [predicate converse](../transformers/predicate-converse.md)
- [predicate subject-projection](../transformers/predicate-subject-projection.md)
- [predicate object-projection](../transformers/predicate-object-projection.md)

Each class is induced by a specific manipulation of the entity-pair index $E \times E$ over the [entity set](entity-set.md) $E$, and each induces a bilattice homomorphism between the predication fiber algebras at the predicates it relates. Under a $\mathrm{Sym}(E)$-equivariance hypothesis with $|E| \geq 3$, the four classes exhaust the natural bottom-preserving bilattice homomorphisms between fiber algebras — see [predicate-generators-are-exhaustive](../../prose/learning/claims/predicate-generators-are-exhaustive.md).

The object set $\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ is the [predicate set](predicate-set.md) $\Pi$.

Specific theorems may require additional structure on $\mathcal{C}$ — finite limits / pullbacks for Beck–Chevalley commutation on fiber algebras; cohomological dimension $\leq 1$ as a poset / Alexandrov-topology category for settling-cohomology-vanishes-above-degree-1 and downstream theorems using $H^{\geq 2} = 0$. These conditions are automatic for finite predicate categories and for directed posets; higher-cd predicate categories require re-verification of downstream vanishing.
