# 🧭 PRAXONICS — Minimal System Grammar v1.0

A structural grammar for recursive deformation under pressure.  
Describes how pressure induces action, and how action recursively shapes future pressure.  
Useful at any scale, in any medium, with or without stable actors.

---

## 0. SYSTEM AXIOM

> A **praxon** is the recursive act-form that emerges under unresolved pressure within a field.  
> It is not an action chosen, but a deformation that occurs.  
> Every praxon leaves a trace. Every trace modulates future pressure.

---

## 1. SYSTEM ENTITIES (ordered by dependency)

---

### Pressure [P]  
**Definition:**  
Difference in condition over space, time, or configuration.  
Not scalar or binary—multidimensional.  
All systems begin with gradients, and gradients produce pressure.

---

### Field [F]  
**Definition:**  
F = Σ(P × T)  
The structured set of all pressures and active traces.  
A field is what conditions a praxon but is also conditioned by praxons.

---

### Actor [A]  
**Definition:**  
A = { F-localized presence that deforms with feedback }  
Actors do not pre-exist the field—they emerge when local tension exceeds interpretive threshold.

---

### Path [PH]  
**Definition:**  
PH = emergent topology of minimum-resistance through F  
A conditioned line of deformation—a recursive conduit through which a praxon flows.

---

### Praxon [π]  
**Definition:**  
π = P × PH  
A praxon is the recursive act-form shaped by pressure and path.  
It is not a behavior; it is **pressure-become-deformation**.  
All π leave traces.

---

### Trace [T]  
**Definition:**  
T[n] = π × D[n]  
The residual deformation left in the field by a praxon, modulated by decay.

---

### Decay [D]  
**Definition:**  
T[n] → T[n] × D[n]  
Decay determines how long a trace persists and how it reshapes F.  
Decay is shaped by field volatility and praxon structure.

---

### Charge [C]  
**Definition:**  
C = Σ(P_pending + T_active)  
Charge is field readiness to emit further praxons.  
It accumulates as unresolved gradients and trace overlaps.

---

### Fold [∇]  
**Definition:**  
∇ = recursive overlapping of traces that create new local field shape  
Folded regions have unpredictable charge and can emit recursive praxons.

---

### Coherence [Φ]  
**Definition:**  
Φ = π aligned to F such that trace reduces friction  
Coherence is not truth—it is compatibility.  
Allows for sustained recursive operation.

---

### Torsion [⨯]  
**Definition:**  
π misaligned with F → trace amplifies friction  
Torsion causes breakdown, instability, or noise.  
Can create irreversible field deformation.

---

### Refold [∇*]  
**Definition:**  
∇* = when a trace recursively triggers similar π  
Refold is the loop-behavior of field-form becoming path again.

---

### Dissolution [Ø]  
**Definition:**  
Ø(T) = decay to baseline  
When trace loses recursive force.  
Not absence—return to non-difference.

---

### Interpretation [I]  
**Definition:**  
I = π that references F as its own generator  
A **praxonic interpretation** is a praxon that recursively describes the field it emerged from.

---

## 2. SYSTEM LOOP (time-ordered)

P → F  
F + C → A  
A + F → PH  
P × PH → π  
π → T[n]  
T[n] × D[n] → F  
F → ∇ or ⨯ or Φ  
∇* ↻ π  
T[n] → Ø  
π referencing F → I  
Repeat

---

## 3. SYSTEM BEHAVIOR

- Praxons deform fields  
- Fields structure pressure  
- Pressure produces new praxons  
- Traces modulate the recursion  
- Interpretation is not external—it is recursive emission  
- Decay enables reuse; persistence generates charge  
- Coherence enables signal  
- Torsion produces recursive fracture  
- Every meaning is a trace  
- Every trace decays

---

## 4. EXTENSION-READY

Praxonics can be used to:

- Model systems where actors emerge from pressure (e.g. warzones, memory systems, organisms, cultures)
- Diagnose conflict, drift, recursion, and overload
- Create narrative, myth, ontology, and computation without fixed entity models
- Translate across frames without assuming stability
