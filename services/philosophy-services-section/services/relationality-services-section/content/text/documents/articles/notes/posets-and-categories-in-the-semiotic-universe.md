The semiotic universe's history object (H, ≤) is a thin category — a [partial order](../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/partial-order.md) viewed as a [category](../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/categorical-services-section/services/ordinary-categories-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/category.md). A [morphism](../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md) from a to b is the unique witness that a ≤ b. There is at most one morphism between any two objects, and its existence expresses the ordering relation.

The Heyting algebra H is a [complete lattice](../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/order-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/lattice.md) with the Heyting implication — the algebraic structure underlying the semiotic universe. Its elements are semantic values and its ordering expresses entailment: a ≤ b means "a entails b."
