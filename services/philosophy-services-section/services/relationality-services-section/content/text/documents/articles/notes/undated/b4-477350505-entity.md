An **entity** is an element $e \in E$ of an entity-set $E$ — the base set of a predication fibration $p : \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{C}$ over a predicate category $\mathcal{C}$ and a residuated lattice $\mathbf{L}$ [@benabou1985; @grothendieck1971]. As an element of a bare set, an entity carries no internal structure of its own: $E$ is set-typed, with finiteness or sort-stratification imposed by specific theories rather than by the entity-form itself.

What an entity acquires through the fibration is its **bundle of incident cells**: for each predicate $p$ and each other entity $o$, the fiber algebra $A_p = \mathbf{L}^{\bowtie\, E \times E}$ assigns a bilattice value to the pair $(e, o)$ and to the pair $(o, e)$ — the values of the predication cells with $e$ in the subject and object positions respectively. The collection of all such cells, viewed categorically, is the slice category $\mathcal{P}/e$ in the predication category $\mathcal{P}$.

The bundle around $e$ is not a property of $e$ alone; it is determined by the valuation that supplies values to every fiber $A_p$. Two valuations over the same entity-set produce different bundles at the same entity. The entity itself is invariant under valuation choice — it is just an element of $E$ — and its bundle is what some valuation makes of its incident cells.

## Sort stratification

When $E$ is sort-stratified, an entity belongs to one or more sorts — subsets of $E$ used by predication-shape declarations to constrain which cells the entity can participate in. Sort membership is an additional structural fact about the entity, not a derived consequence of its incident-cell bundle.
