# Generative Sheaf Universe Specification (HTT-SPEC v1)

This document specifies the **Generative Sheaf Universe** structure as a mathematical object. In earlier drafts this structure was called the **Hypertensor Topos**; the terminology has been updated to match the Math Paper.
Normative key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD**, **MAY** are used in the sense of RFC 2119.

The specification is written as Markdown + TeX. It is intended for mathematically literate readers
and for direct translation into LaTeX or a proof assistant.

Non-normative note: Axioms HT-A0–HT-A6 and the derived constructions in Sections 2–6 restate, in specification form, the theorems and constructions proved in *Generative Sheaf Universe and Acts of Relationality: Mathematical Specification* (“the Math Paper”). All mathematical references flow from that document into this one, and this specification is written relational-first: categorical phrasing is only referenced by citation to the Math Paper, not by new exposition here.

# 0. Global Conventions

- A *category* is always locally small unless stated otherwise.
- A *universe* means an (elementary) Grothendieck topos.
- For a category $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)$ denotes the category of set-valued sheaves on the site $(\mathcal{C},J)$.
- For a set $X$, $\mathcal{P}(X)$ denotes its powerset.
- For a universe $R$, $\mathrm{Obj}(R)$ denotes its class of objects.

**Notation alignment.** The universe is denoted $R$, the distinguished recognition object is $H \in R$ with fixed subsheaf $H^\ast$, and fibers are $H_t$. This matches the Math Paper’s usage; in the machine specification a Cell is a finite presentation of some fiber $H_t$. Modalities are $\sigma$ (stabilizer) and $\Delta$ (drift) with components $\sigma_t,\Delta_t$.

We fix the following **categories**:

- $\mathbf{RecAlg}$: objects are bounded recognition algebras; morphisms are recognition homomorphisms.
- $\mathbf{RecModal}$: objects are tuples
  $$
  (H,\land,\lor,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top,\sigma,\Delta)
  $$
  where $(H,\land,\lor,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top)$ is a bounded recognition algebra and
  $\sigma,\Delta : H \to H$ are unary operations satisfying HT-A1 (below).
  Morphisms are recognition homomorphisms preserving $\sigma$ and $\Delta$.

We also use the following **notation**:

- For a small category $\mathbf{T}$, $\mathbf{T}^{op}$ denotes its opposite.
- For an object $X$ in a universe, $\mathrm{Sub}(X)$ denotes the poset of subobjects of $X$.
- For a functor $F : \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{Set}$ and $c \in \mathcal{C}$, we write $F(c)$ for the set of sections at $c$.

# 1. Primitive Structures

This section specifies the **primitive data** that define a Generative Sheaf Universe instance.

## 1.1 Hypertensor Trace Site (HTS)

**Definition HTS-1 (Hypertensor Trace Site).**
A **Hypertensor Trace Site** is a pair $\mathrm{HTS} = (\mathbf{T},J)$ where:

- $\mathbf{T}$ is a small category (the **trace category**).
- $J$ is a Grothendieck topology on $\mathbf{T}$.

The pair $(\mathbf{T},J)$ MUST satisfy Axiom HT-A0 below.

---

### Axiom HT-A0 (Trace Category Constraints)

Let $\mathbf{T}$ be as above. Relationally, objects of $\mathbf{T}$ are canonical witness traces (canonicalized sequences of primitive relational steps), and morphisms are normalization sequences that reorder or delete independent steps.

1. **Objects as traces.**
   There is a chosen set $\Sigma$ (the **step alphabet**) and a surjection
   $$
   \mathrm{word} : \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{T}) \twoheadrightarrow \Sigma^\star
   $$
   such that each object of $\mathbf{T}$ is identified with an equivalence class of finite words on $\Sigma$ modulo a congruence generated by *admissible commutations* and *deletions*.

2. **Morphisms as normalizations.**
   For objects $t,t' \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{T})$, a morphism $f : t \to t'$ exists if and only if $t'$ is obtained from $t$ via a finite sequence of normalization moves:
   - deletion of a neutral or redundant step,
   - commutation of adjacent independent steps,
   - rebracketing or reassociation moves compatible with independence.

   The set $\mathbf{T}(t,t')$ of morphisms MUST be finite for all $t,t'$.

3. **Free partially commutative monoid structure.**
   The isomorphism classes of objects of $\mathbf{T}$ MUST form the free partially commutative monoid over $\Sigma$, modulo the given independence relations.

4. **Composition.**
   Composition in $\mathbf{T}$ MUST coincide with concatenation of normalization sequences followed by re-normalization, and MUST be associative by confluence of normalization.

5. **Identities.**
   For each object $t$, the identity $\mathrm{id}_t : t \to t$ MUST be represented by the empty normalization sequence.

---

### Axiom HT-A0.1 (Trace Topology)

The Grothendieck topology $J$ on $\mathbf{T}$ captures which families of trace refinements count as covers; it MUST satisfy:

- For each $t \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{T})$, there exists at least one covering family $\{u_i : t_i \to t\}_{i \in I} \in J(t)$ (non-degenerate topology).
- $J$ MUST be stable under pullback along morphisms of $\mathbf{T}$, contain isomorphisms as covers, and be closed under composition of covers (standard Grothendieck axioms).

## 1.2 Fiber recognition–modal algebras

**Definition HT-H (Fiber recognition–modal family).**
Given a trace category $\mathbf{T}$, a **fiber recognition–modal family** over $\mathbf{T}$ is an assignment:
$$
t \ \mapsto\ H_t
$$
with $H_t \in \mathbf{RecModal}$ for each $t \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{T})$.

Each fiber $H_t$ MUST satisfy HT-A1.

---

### Axiom HT-A1 (Fiber Constraints)

For each trace $t \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{T})$: relationally, $H_t$ is the recognition algebra of what is valid at $t$, equipped with the operations listed below.

1. **Finite bounded recognition algebra.**
   $H_t$ MUST be a finite bounded recognition algebra
   $$
   (H_t,\land_t,\lor_t,\Rightarrow_t,\bot_t,\top_t).
   $$

2. **Modal endomorphisms (stabilizer and drift).**
   There MUST exist two unary operations
   $$
   \sigma_t, \Delta_t : H_t \to H_t
   $$
   such that:
   - (Monotonicity) $a \le_t b \Rightarrow \sigma_t(a) \le_t \sigma_t(b)$ and $\Delta_t(a) \le_t \Delta_t(b)$.
   - (Stabilizer) $a \le_t \sigma_t(a)$ and $\sigma_t(\sigma_t(a)) = \sigma_t(a)$.
   - (Drift) $a \le_t \Delta_t(a)$ and there exists $n \ge 1$ such that
     $\Delta_t^{n+1}(a) = \Delta_t^n(a)$ for all $a \in H_t$.
   - (Commutation) $\sigma_t(\Delta_t(a)) = \Delta_t(\sigma_t(a))$ for all $a$.
   - (recognition compatibility) $\sigma_t$ preserves finite meets and $\top_t$; $\Delta_t$ is meet-subdistributive and preserves finite joins.

3. **Fixed fiber.**
   Define
   $$
   H_t^\ast := \{ a \in H_t \mid \sigma_t(a) = a = \Delta_t(a) \}.
   $$
   This MUST be nonempty and MUST form a bounded recognition *subalgebra* of $H_t$.

---

## 1.3 Reindexing Functor

**Definition HT-F (Universe Reindexing Functor).**
Reindexing is the relational rule that adapts recognitions when a witness trace is refined or normalized. A **Universe Reindexing Functor** is a functor
$$
H : \mathbf{T}^{op} \to \mathbf{RecModal}
$$
such that:

- $H(t) = H_t$ for each $t$,
- each $H(f) : H_{t'} \to H_t$ is a morphism in $\mathbf{RecModal}$.

This MUST satisfy HT-A2.

---

### Axiom HT-A2 (Reindexing Constraints)

For every morphism $f : t \to t'$ in $\mathbf{T}$:

1. $H(f)$ MUST be a recognition homomorphism:
   $$
   H(f)(a \land_{t'} b) = H(f)(a) \land_t H(f)(b),
   $$
   $$
   H(f)(a \lor_{t'} b) = H(f)(a) \lor_t H(f)(b),
   $$
   $$
   H(f)(a \Rightarrow_{t'} b) = H(f)(a) \Rightarrow_t H(f)(b),
   $$
   $$
   H(f)(\bot_{t'}) = \bot_t,\quad H(f)(\top_{t'}) = \top_t.
   $$

2. $H(f)$ MUST preserve modalities:
   $$
   H(f)(\sigma_{t'}(a)) = \sigma_t(H(f)(a)),\quad
   H(f)(\Delta_{t'}(a)) = \Delta_t(H(f)(a)).
   $$

3. Functoriality MUST hold:
   $$
   H(\mathrm{id}_t) = \mathrm{id}_{H_t},\quad
   H(g \circ f) = H(f) \circ H(g).
   $$

4. Fixed elements MUST be preserved:
   $$
   H(f)(H_{t'}^\ast) \subseteq H_t^\ast.
   $$

## 1.4 Sheaf Condition

**Definition HT-SH (Generative Sheaf).**
The underlying set-valued functor of $H$,
$$
|H| : \mathbf{T}^{op} \to \mathbf{Set},
\quad |H|(t) := H_t,
$$
is called the **generative presheaf**.

---

### Axiom HT-A3 (Sheaf Condition)

Recognitions consistent on refinements MUST glue uniquely on the coarse trace; this requirement is enforced by the sheaf condition below.

The presheaf $|H|$ MUST be a **sheaf** for the topology $J$:

For each $t \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{T})$ and each covering family
$\{u_i : t_i \to t\}_{i \in I} \in J(t)$:

1. (**Locality**) If $a,b \in H_t$ satisfy
   $H(u_i)(a) = H(u_i)(b)$ for all $i$,
   then $a = b$.

2. (**Gluing**) If $a_i \in H_{t_i}$ satisfy
   $H(v)(a_i) = H(w)(a_j)$ for all morphisms
   $v : s \to t_i, w : s \to t_j$ with $u_i \circ v = u_j \circ w$,
   then there exists a unique $a \in H_t$ such that
   $H(u_i)(a) = a_i$ for all $i$.

3. (**Modal-compatibility**) The gluing MUST commute with $\sigma,\Delta$: if $a$ is the glued global section of $(a_i)$, then $\sigma_t(a)$ and $\Delta_t(a)$ MUST be the glued global sections of $(\sigma_{t_i}(a_i))$ and $(\Delta_{t_i}(a_i))$, respectively.

---

### Axiom HT-A4 (Invariant Subsheaf)

Fixed recognitions (stable under stabilizer and drift) MUST assemble coherently across trace refinements. The assignment $t \mapsto H_t^\ast$ and $f \mapsto H(f)|_{H_{t'}^\ast}$ MUST define a subsheaf
$$
H^\ast : \mathbf{T}^{op} \to \mathbf{RecAlg},
$$
i.e.:

- $H^\ast$ MUST satisfy the sheaf locality and gluing conditions (as above), and
- any glued section of invariant locals MUST be invariant globally.

## 1.5 Internal Recognition Term Language (RTL)

**Definition HT-L (Recognition Term Language, RTL).**
A **Recognition Term Language** over $(\mathbf{T},J,H)$ consists of:

1. A set of **types** generated by:
   - a base type $\mathrm{Rec}$,
   - $(A,B) \mapsto A \times B$,
   - $(A,B) \mapsto A \Rightarrow B$,
   - (optionally) sums and fixed-point types.

2. For each trace $t$, a set of **terms** with **typing judgments** of the form:
   $$
   \Gamma \vdash_t M : A
   $$
   where $\Gamma$ is a finite context $x_1 : A_1, \dots, x_n : A_n$.

3. A **typing system** implementing at least:
   - variable, weakening, exchange rules,
   - introduction/elimination rules for $\times$ and $\Rightarrow$,
   - (optionally) rules for sums and fixed-point types,
   - term formers $\mathbf{Stabilizer}, \mathbf{Drift} : \mathrm{Rec} \to \mathrm{Rec}$.

4. A **semantics** mapping:
   - $\llbracket A \rrbracket \in \mathrm{Obj}(R)$ for each type $A$,
   - $\llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket \in \mathrm{Obj}(R)$ for each context $\Gamma$,
   - $\llbracket \Gamma \vdash M : A \rrbracket : \llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket \to \llbracket A \rrbracket$ for each judgement.

RTL is presented as a simply-typed $\lambda$-calculus; $\lambda$-syntax is a convenient notation, but the normative object is RTL itself.

The semantics MUST satisfy Axiom HT-A5.

---

### Axiom HT-A5 (Lambda Semantics Constraints)

Let $R := \mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{T},J)$ (to be defined in Section 2).

The RTL semantics MUST satisfy:

1. $\llbracket \mathrm{Rec} \rrbracket = H$ (the distinguished recognition object).
2. Products and exponentials MUST interpret as categorical products and exponentials in $R$.
3. Term constructors for logical operations MUST interpret as the internal recognition operations on $H$.
4. $\mathbf{Stabilizer}$ and $\mathbf{Drift}$ MUST interpret as the natural transformations $\sigma,\Delta : H \to H$.
5. Reindexing along morphisms in $\mathbf{T}$ MUST commute with interpretation:
   for each $f : t \to t'$, the semantics of reindexed judgments MUST coincide with pulling back the internal morphism along the corresponding geometric morphism induced by $f$.
6. The semantics MUST be **sound and complete** for the internal logic of $R$ restricted to the object $H$, in the following sense:
   - Any derivable judgment or equation of RTL MUST hold in $R$.
   - Any morphism $f : \llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket \to \llbracket A \rrbracket$ generated by products, exponentials, subobjects, and the operations interpreting RTL constructors MUST be denotable by some term $M$ with $\Gamma \vdash M : A$.

## 1.6 Structural Operations (Constructors)

**Definition HT-FS (Generative Structural Operations).**
A **Generative Structural Signature** is a finite set
$$
\mathcal{F} = \{F_i\}_{i \in I}
$$
of primitive operations on objects of $R$, including at least:

- trace-level concatenation $\star$ on objects of $\mathbf{T}$,
- the internal recognition operations on $H$: $\land,\lor,\Rightarrow,\top,\bot$,
- the internal modal operations $\sigma,\Delta : H \to H$,
- their corresponding term constructors in RTL at type $\mathrm{Rec}$.

---

### Axiom HT-A6 (Structural Closure)

The structural operations $\mathcal{F}$ MUST satisfy:

1. **Naturality.**
   For any $F_i$ acting on $H$ or its powers, the action MUST be natural with respect to $H$-reindexing induced by $\mathbf{T}$.

2. **Sufficiency.**
   Every object of $R$ that arises in this specification (Sections 2–7) MUST be constructible from:
   - representables $y(t) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{T}}(-,t)$,
   - the hypertensor object $H$,
   - application of finite limits, finite colimits, exponentials, subobjects,
   - the operations in $\mathcal{F}$,
   - sheaf gluing and restriction.

3. **No extra primitives.**
   No additional primitive object-forming operations beyond those above are permitted in the core generative universe.

# 2. Generative Sheaf Universe Construction

Given primitive data satisfying HT-A0–HT-A6, this section defines the **Generative Sheaf Universe** (formerly “Hypertensor Topos”).

## 2.1 Sheaf Universe

**Definition HT-R (Generative Sheaf Universe).**
Let $\mathrm{HTS} = (\mathbf{T},J)$ be a Hypertensor Trace Site satisfying HT-A0–HT-A3, and let $H : \mathbf{T}^{op} \to \mathbf{RecModal}$ be a Universe Reindexing Functor as in HT-F.

Define:

- $R := \mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{T},J)$ (the **sheaf universe**),
- $H \in R$ as the sheaf corresponding to the presheaf $|H|$,
- $H^\ast \hookrightarrow H$ as the subsheaf of fixed points.

The tuple
$$
\mathrm{HTT} := (R,H,\sigma,\Delta,H^\ast)
$$
is called a **Generative Sheaf Universe instance**.

---

## 2.2 Existence and Uniqueness (Specification Form)

**Requirement HT-R1 (Existence).**
Given any primitive data satisfying HT-A0–HT-A6, a corresponding Generative Sheaf Universe instance $\mathrm{HTT}$ **MUST** exist as in Definition HT-R.

**Requirement HT-R2 (Uniqueness up to equivalence).**
If two sets of primitive data $(\mathbf{T},J,H,\sigma,\Delta)$ and $(\mathbf{T}',J',H',\sigma',\Delta')$ satisfy HT-A0–HT-A6 and induce universes $R$ and $R'$, then there **MUST** exist a universe (topos) equivalence
$$
F : R \simeq R'
$$
preserving the distinguished structure (up to isomorphism):
$$
F(H) \cong H', \quad
F(\sigma) = \sigma', \quad
F(\Delta) = \Delta', \quad
F(H^\ast) \cong H'^\ast.
$$

# 3. Generative Universe Closure Mechanism

This section specifies the **generative universe closure operator** and the self-generation property of $R$.

## 3.1 Atomic Objects

**Definition HT-A (Atomic Object Set).**
Let $R = \mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{T},J)$.
Define the **atomic object set**:
$$
\mathcal{A} := \{ y(t) \mid t \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{T}) \} \cup \{ H \},
$$
where $y : \mathbf{T} \to R$ is the Yoneda embedding.

---

## 3.2 Generative Closure Operator

**Definition HT-G (Generative Closure Operator).**
Define a function
$$
\mathcal{G} : \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Obj}(R)) \to \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Obj}(R))
$$
as follows:

For any $X \subseteq \mathrm{Obj}(R)$, $\mathcal{G}(X)$ is the **smallest** subclass $Y \subseteq \mathrm{Obj}(R)$ satisfying:

1. (**Atoms and base set included**)
   $\mathcal{A} \subseteq Y$ and $X \subseteq Y$.

2. (**Isomorphism-closure**)
   If $A \in Y$ and $B \cong A$ in $R$, then $B \in Y$.

3. (**Finite limits/colimits**)
   If $A,B \in Y$, any object obtained as a finite limit or finite colimit of diagrams built from $A,B$ MUST be in $Y$.

4. (**Exponentials**)
   If $A,B \in Y$, then the exponential object $B^A$ MUST belong to $Y$.

5. (**Subobjects**)
   If $A \in Y$ and $S \hookrightarrow A$ is a subobject, then $S \in Y$.

6. (**Structural operations**)
   If $F_i \in \mathcal{F}$ is an $n$-ary structural operation and $A_1,\dots,A_n \in Y$ are in its domain, then $F_i(A_1,\dots,A_n) \in Y$.

7. (**Sheaf gluing/restriction**)
   If $A \in Y$, and $A'$ is obtained from $A$ by:
   - restriction along a geometric morphism induced by a subsite of $(\mathbf{T},J)$, or
   - sheaf gluing along a covering family in $J$,

   then $A' \in Y$.

---

## 3.3 Generative Properties

**Requirement HT-G1 (Monotonicity & Inflationarity).**
For all $X,Y \subseteq \mathrm{Obj}(R)$:

- If $X \subseteq Y$, then $\mathcal{G}(X) \subseteq \mathcal{G}(Y)$.
- $X \subseteq \mathcal{G}(X)$.

**Definition HT-GC (G-closed subclass).**
A subclass $X \subseteq \mathrm{Obj}(R)$ is **$\mathcal{G}$-closed** if $\mathcal{G}(X) \subseteq X$.

**Requirement HT-G2 (Idempotence on closed subclasses).**
If $X$ is $\mathcal{G}$-closed, then $\mathcal{G}(X) = X$.

---

## 3.4 Generated Subuniverse

**Definition HT-Rgen (Generated Object Class).**
Define:
$$
\mathrm{Obj}(R_{\mathrm{gen}}) :=
\bigcap \{ X \subseteq \mathrm{Obj}(R)
          \mid \mathcal{A} \subseteq X \text{ and } \mathcal{G}(X) \subseteq X \}.
$$
We call $R_{\mathrm{gen}}$ the **Generative Subuniverse**.

**Requirement HT-G3 (Generated Subuniverse is G-closed).**
$\mathrm{Obj}(R_{\mathrm{gen}})$ MUST be $\mathcal{G}$-closed and MUST contain $\mathcal{A}$.

## 3.5 Self-Generation and Initiality

**Requirement HT-G4 (Self-Generation).**
The Generative Sheaf Universe $R$ MUST satisfy:
$$
\mathrm{Obj}(R) = \mathrm{Obj}(R_{\mathrm{gen}}).
$$
Equivalently, $\mathrm{Obj}(R)$ is the least $\mathcal{G}$-closed subclass containing $\mathcal{A}$.

---

**Definition HT-HM (Generative Model).**
A **Generative Model** is a tuple
$$
\mathrm{HM} = (S,K,\sigma^{S},\Delta^{S},K^\ast,\mathcal{A}_{S})
$$
such that:

- $S$ is a universe (elementary topos),
- $K \in S$ is a recognition–modal internal object with fixed subobject $K^\ast$,
- there exists a trace site $(\mathbf{T},J)$ whose representables and $K$ form an atomic set $\mathcal{A}_{S}$,
- the analogues of HT-A0–HT-A6 hold internally in $S$.

A morphism of Generative Models MUST be a geometric morphism of universes preserving all distinguished structure up to isomorphism.

---

**Requirement HT-G5 (Initiality).**
Let $\mathrm{HTT} = (R,H,\sigma,\Delta,H^\ast)$ be the Generative Sheaf Universe instance associated to a given trace site $(\mathbf{T},J)$.

Then, in the category of Generative Models over $(\mathbf{T},J)$, $\mathrm{HTT}$ MUST be **initial**:
for any other Generative Model $\mathrm{HM}$ over the same trace site, there MUST exist a unique (up to isomorphism) structure-preserving morphism
$$
F : \mathrm{HTT} \to \mathrm{HM}.
$$

# 4. Internal Recognition Term Language and Normalization

This section specifies additional requirements on RTL and its normalization properties.

## 4.1 RTL Soundness and Completeness

**Requirement HT-L1 (Soundness).**
For any derivable judgment $\Gamma \vdash M : A$ in RTL:

- The semantic morphism $\llbracket \Gamma \vdash M : A \rrbracket$ MUST exist in $R$.
- If $\Gamma \vdash M \equiv N : A$ is derivable, then
  $$
  \llbracket \Gamma \vdash M : A \rrbracket
  =
  \llbracket \Gamma \vdash N : A \rrbracket
  \text{ in } R.
  $$

**Requirement HT-L2 (Completeness for Generative Fragment).**
Let $R$ be the Generative Sheaf Universe and RTL be its lambda calculus.

If a morphism $f : \llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket \to \llbracket A \rrbracket$ is generated from:

- finite limits, finite colimits,
- exponentials,
- subobjects,
- and the operations interpreting RTL term constructors,

then there MUST exist a term $M$ with $\Gamma \vdash M : A$ such that
$$
\llbracket \Gamma \vdash M : A \rrbracket = f.
$$

---

## 4.2 Normalization and Canonical Forms at Type $\mathrm{Rec}$

**Definition HT-NF-Alg (Normalization Algorithm NF\_Term).**
There MUST exist an algorithm
$$
\mathrm{NF\_Term} : \{\text{RTL terms}\} \to \{\text{RTL terms}\}
$$
satisfying:

- **Termination**: For every term $M$, $\mathrm{NF\_Term}(M)$ is defined and in normal form.
- **Confluence**: If $M$ reduces (by β, η, modal reductions) to both $N_1$ and $N_2$, then $\mathrm{NF\_Term}(N_1) = \mathrm{NF\_Term}(N_2)$.
- **Equivalence preservation**: If $M$ and $N$ are provably equivalent, $\mathrm{NF\_Term}(M) = \mathrm{NF\_Term}(N)$.

**Definition HT-NF-Rec (Canonical Rec-Terms).**
For closed terms $M : \mathrm{Rec}$, define the **canonical representative**:
$$
\mathrm{NF\_Rec}(M) := \mathrm{NF\_Term}(M),
$$
restricted to closed terms of type $\mathrm{Rec}$.

A closed $\mathrm{Rec}$-term $N$ is **canonical** iff $N = \mathrm{NF\_Rec}(N)$.

---

**Requirement HT-L3 (Canonical Forms for $\mathrm{Rec}$).**
For closed terms $M : \mathrm{Rec}$:

1. There MUST exist a unique canonical term $N : \mathrm{Rec}$ such that
   $$
   \vdash M \equiv N : \mathrm{Rec}.
   $$
2. The interpretations MUST satisfy
   $$
   \llbracket \vdash M : \mathrm{Rec} \rrbracket
   =
   \llbracket \vdash N : \mathrm{Rec} \rrbracket \in H.
   $$
3. If $N_1$ and $N_2$ are canonical and
   $$
   \llbracket \vdash N_1 : \mathrm{Rec} \rrbracket
   =
   \llbracket \vdash N_2 : \mathrm{Rec} \rrbracket,
   $$
   then $N_1$ and $N_2$ MUST be syntactically identical (modulo α-renaming).

# 5. Fixed Locale and Defect-Mode Structure

This section specifies the fixed locale and a defect operator at a specification level.

## 5.1 Fixed Locale (Stability Locale)

**Definition HT-Phase (Fixed Locale).**
The fixed subsheaf $H^\ast$ is regarded as an internal frame in $R$.
The **Stability Locale** is a locale object $\mathsf{Stability} \in R$ with
$$
\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{Stability}) \cong H^\ast.
$$

**Requirement HT-P1 (Frame Laws).**
$H^\ast$ MUST satisfy the internal frame (complete recognition algebra) axioms in $R$, and the identification
$\mathcal{O}(mathsf{Phase}) \cong H^\ast$ MUST be an isomorphism of internal frames.

### Derived Requirement HT-STAB (Stabilization map)

There MUST exist an internal map
$$
\mathsf{Stab} : H \longrightarrow H^\ast
$$
such that:

   - it is **monotone**,
   - it is **idempotent on fixed elements** (restricts to the identity on $H^\ast$),
   - it is **compatible with reindexing** along $\mathbf{T}$,
   - it is **definable from $\sigma,\Delta$** (no new primitive operator is introduced).

Intuitively, $\mathsf{Stab}(a)$ returns the fixed limit of $a$ under the iterates of $\sigma$ and $\Delta$; equivalently, the least fixed element above the $\sigma/\Delta$-orbit of $a$.

---

## 5.2 Sheaf of Fixed Modes

**Definition HT-V (Sheaf of Fixed Modes).**
Fix a commutative ring object $K$ in $R$ (e.g. a constant sheaf on a commutative ring).

Define $V : \mathbf{T}^{op} \to \mathbf{Mod}_K$ by:

- $V(t)$ is a finite free $K$-module induced from $H_t^\ast$ (e.g. $K^{H_t^\ast}$),
- $V(f)$ is induced by reindexing of fixed elements along $H^\ast(f)$.

$V$ MUST satisfy the sheaf condition and thus defines a $K$-module object in $R$, called the **Sheaf of Fixed Modes**.

---

## 5.3 Defect Operator and Defect Mode Decomposition

**Definition HT-D (Defect Operator).**
There MUST exist a natural $K$-linear endomorphism
$$
\delta : V \to V
$$
such that:

1. For each trace $t$, the component
   $$
   \delta_t : V(t) \to V(t)
   $$
   is a $K$-linear map.

2. The collection $(D_t)$ is natural with respect to reindexing:
   for each $f : t \to t'$,
   $$
   V(f) \circ \delta_{t'} = \delta_t \circ V(f).
   $$

3. $\delta$ vanishes on “fully fixed” directions: any vector corresponding to a globally fixed mode MUST be in the kernel of $\delta$.

**Requirement HT-S1 (Fiberwise Defect Mode Decomposition).**
Under a chosen algebraic condition (e.g. each $\delta_t$ is diagonalizable over $K$):

- Each $\delta_t$ MUST admit a decomposition
  $$
  V(t) \cong \bigoplus_{\lambda \in \Lambda_t} E_{t,\lambda}
  $$
  into eigenspaces.

**Requirement HT-S2 (Defect-Mode Sheaves).**

# 6. Meta Properties (Normative Summary)

This section restates key global properties that MUST hold for any Generative Sheaf Universe implementation.

## 6.1 Presentation Independence

**Requirement HT-M1 (Presentation Independence).**
If two primitive specifications $(\mathbf{T},J,H,\sigma,\Delta)$ and $(\mathbf{T}',J',H',\sigma',\Delta')$ satisfy HT-A0–HT-A6 and define universes $R$ and $R'$, then:

- There MUST exist a universe (topos) equivalence $F : R \simeq R'$ preserving all distinguished structures up to isomorphism as in HT-R2.
- All derived constructions (stability locale, defect operator, defect‑mode sheaves, RTL semantics) MUST be transported along this equivalence.

## 6.2 Refinement Robustness

**Requirement HT-M2 (Refinement Robustness).**
If $(\mathbf{T}',J')$ is a refinement of $(\mathbf{T},J)$ (e.g. finer trace discretization or stronger topology) and the fiber and modal data are refined compatibly, then the resulting universe $R'$ MUST be equivalent to $R$ as a Generative Sheaf Universe model.

## 6.3 Structural Maximality

**Requirement HT-M3 (Maximality Under A0–A6).**
Any additional primitive object-forming operation that:

- is not definable via the closure operator $\mathcal{G}$ built from $\mathcal{F}$, limits, colimits, exponentials, subobjects, and sheaf gluing/restriction, and
- is intended to be *primitive*,

MUST be understood as changing the underlying axiom set (i.e. resulting in a structure that is no longer a pure Generative Sheaf Universe instance under HT-A0–HT-A6).

## 6.4 Universality

**Requirement HT-M4 (Universal Generative Model).**
A Generative Sheaf Universe instance $\mathrm{HTT} = (R,H,\sigma,\Delta,H^\ast)$ associated to a trace site $(\mathbf{T},J)$ MUST be:

- **Self-generated** under $\mathcal{G}$ (HT-G4),
- **Initial** among Generative Sheaf Universe models over $(\mathbf{T},J)$ (HT-G5).

This universality property MUST be preserved under any equivalence of such models.
