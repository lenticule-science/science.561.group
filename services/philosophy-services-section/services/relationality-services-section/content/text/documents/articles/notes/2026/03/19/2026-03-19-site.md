## What this is

A **Site** is a pair (C, J) where C is a small category and J is a **Grothendieck topology** on C: an assignment to each object c ∈ C of a collection J(c) of **covering sieves** satisfying three axioms.

A **sieve** on c ∈ C is a downward-closed set of morphisms with codomain c: if f: d → c is in the sieve and g: e → d is any morphism, then f ∘ g: e → c is also in the sieve. A covering sieve is a sieve J judges to be "enough" evidence for c.

## Formal definition

A Grothendieck topology J on C assigns to each c ∈ C a collection J(c) of sieves on c satisfying:

1. **Maximality**: the maximal sieve on c (containing all morphisms into c) is in J(c).
2. **Stability**: if S ∈ J(c) and f: d → c is any morphism, then the pullback sieve f*(S) = {g: e → d | f ∘ g ∈ S} is in J(d).
3. **Transitivity**: if S ∈ J(c) and R is any sieve on c such that f*(R) ∈ J(d) for every f: d → c in S, then R ∈ J(c).

The pair (C, J) is the site. The **sheaves** on (C, J) are the presheaves F: C^op → Set that satisfy the gluing condition for every covering sieve: if sections {x_f ∈ F(d) | f: d → c ∈ S} are compatible (agree on all further restrictions), there exists a unique x ∈ F(c) restricting to each x_f. The category Sh(C, J) of all sheaves on (C, J) is a Grothendieck topos.

The site is not itself the topos. It is the generative skeleton from which the topos is built by sheafification — the left adjoint to the inclusion Sh(C,J) ↪ PSh(C) that forces the gluing condition.

## What the topology encodes

The topology J encodes an epistemology: what constitutes sufficient evidence for a claim at c. A covering sieve on c is a family of "probes" into c that together witness everything worth knowing about c. Different topologies on the same category C generate different toposes with different internal logics — the topology determines which statements can be "seen" locally and which require global evidence.

The three axioms have the following readings:
- Maximality: the full collection of all morphisms into c always counts as sufficient evidence.
- Stability: if a family witnesses c, then restricting to any subcone d → c still witnesses d.
- Transitivity: if a family witnesses c, and each member of the family is itself witnessed, then the composition witnesses c.

## The relational history site

A **proposed**, not yet fully formalized, site for the relational-universe theory is the **relational history site** (T, J), where:

- T is the history category: objects are histories (finite commutation-equivalence classes of step sequences), morphisms are prefix extensions.
- J is the commutation topology: a family of morphisms into a history t covers t iff they form a commutation partition — they account for all ways to decompose t into a preceding context plus one step, up to commutation equivalence.

**Research condition:** these descriptions define a genuine Grothendieck site only after the history category and proposed commutation coverings satisfy the identity, pullback-stability and transitivity axioms. Once this is established, Sh(T, J) is a mathematically well-formed sheaf topos. Identifying that topos with the proposed relational universe is another map and theorem, not a consequence of naming it R. Commute-equivalence classes and purported covers must be given exact morphism and sieve types.

## Relation to this system

**Conditional interpretation.** If (T, J) is established as proposed, its sheaves form Sh(T, J). A *proposition in the internal topos logic* is represented by a subterminal object, equivalently a global truth value 1 → Ω; an arbitrary sheaf is not automatically a truth-valued proposition, nor is every section a truth value. The [subobject classifier](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/subobject+classifier) formalizes this distinction. The proposed [Vessel](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/vessel.md) correspondence requires a separately proved equivalence of the mathematical carrier and the organizational interpretation.

The proposed saturation/transfer operators σ and Δ would have to be specified and proved as Lawvere–Tierney topologies (closure operators on the subobject classifier) on **this particular** Sh(T,J) to justify a nuclear interpretation. The existing abstract commuting-nuclei and FOUR results in Obsmetrologia do not themselves construct those two topologies on the proposed history site.

The FARS locale is a site in the informal sense: its `math/` objects are the objects, its `AGENTS.md` and `SOUL.md` coverings define what constitutes sufficient institutional recognition. Making this informal structure precise — identifying the explicit category T and topology J for each locale — is an open research question.

## The formalized finite site and a protected-cover counterexample

The current [Obsmetrologia Site.Basic](https://github.com/lenticule-science/obsmetrologia/blob/main/Obsmetrologia/Site/Basic.lean) is a **different, explicit finite carrier**: at a fixed predicate q and stratum τ, its clopen opens use the finite Stone carrier and a cover relation satisfying `covers_self`, `covers_pullback`, and `covers_trans`. The bundled `bitopological_site_grothendieck` establishes its basis laws. In this model **covers refine support at fixed (q, τ)**; the theorem `no_temporal_covers` distinguishes advances in τ from the covering families. Time-indexing may be carried by morphisms while a particular topology regulates what counts as local evidence. This is a real categorical distinction, not a derivation of physical 3+1 dimensions from a database tuple.

The separate [Site.ProtectedCover](https://github.com/lenticule-science/obsmetrologia/blob/main/Obsmetrologia/Site/ProtectedCover.lean) defines `ProtectedCovers B U fam`: a geometric cover is only admissible when an indivisible protected region B is contained whole in at least one patch. The module proves identity, pullback, transitivity and an explicit negative case: two coordinate patches geometrically cover the full clopen but **fail** to preserve the designated whole protected region. This is a particularly useful mathematical constraint on would-be institutional or semantic *gluing*: locally sufficient coverage does not by itself confer authority to split protected content. Which institutional territory is protected is an external legal or governance fact, **not** determined by the mathematics.

The separate [Site.Sheaf](https://github.com/lenticule-science/obsmetrologia/blob/main/Obsmetrologia/Site/Sheaf.lean) establishes pointwise knowledge-join laws and a separation theorem, not an unrestricted equivalence of all sheaves with all institutional work records. An admissible L-Space translation must specify the domain context, allowed covering/refinement, independent testimony and externally accepted effect.

These **proved-source interfaces** are useful inputs to [LS-SAS-001](https://github.com/lenticule-science/linguistics-services-section/blob/main/specifications/l-space-semantic-architecture.md): a proposed claim-context cover that divides a protected authority or erases a temporal distinction must be rejected until the receiving office and type-specific preservation law admit it. An HLD/LLD may use a bounded finite instance as a test fixture without declaring the relational-history site or physical spacetime theory complete.

## Directed stratified site: self-similarity of the hyperverse tower

**Source:** [Directed Stratified Site](../../../directed-stratified-site.md).

The **proposed** relational history site (T, J) would carry two additional structures if its category and covering assignment are constructed as claimed:

- **Stratification**: the depth filtration T^(0) ⊆ T^(1) ⊆ ... on the history category, with each stratum indexed by a natural number (the history's depth in the prefix order)
- **Directedness**: the prefix order on histories is irreversible — histories only grow; there are no morphisms that shorten a history

The directed-stratified-site conjecture asks whether a hyperverse tower can carry corresponding structures at another level:

| Level | Site | Stratification | Directedness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground level | History site (T, J) | Depth filtration T^(n) by depth | Prefix order: histories only grow |
| Meta-level | Tower site (category of relational universes, density covers) | Tower level n = stratum | Tower inclusions are inflationary and non-invertible at every finite level |

**The proposed self-similarity (conjecture).** The claim is that an appropriately defined construction could carry strata, morphisms and covering families from an admitted directed-stratified site to a compatible construction at the next level. This requires an explicit category and topology at each level, a specified comparison functor, and proofs that the required strata and directionality survive the construction. It has not been established for arbitrary sites. In particular, Sh(T, J) is a sheaf **category**; to treat it as a *site* for a next iteration requires a fresh covering topology and a proof of that choice's axioms. The proposed hyperverse tower remains conditional on this missing data.

If such a comparison is constructed and its relevant diagrams commute, the base history site and the putative tower site **could be compared** as directed-stratified sites. Without those maps, calling the tower self-similar is the proposed mathematical statement, not its proof.

**Institutional research interpretation.** One may investigate an institutional site whose objects are typed records or histories, whose morphisms are lawful restrictions or extensions, and whose covering sieves encode **explicitly authorized** conditions of local adequacy. Roles and office authority would be external data and admission laws, not automatic consequences of a Grothendieck topology. Legal revocations, corrections, competing testimony, and organizational change prevent treating all institutional facts as a simple irreversible prefix chain. Modeling a sequence of institutions as another directed-stratified site remains a separate, falsifiable proposal.

**Open question on institutional locales.** A future representation of a locale as a directed stratified site would need an explicit category of records/morphisms, a separately defined covering assignment, proof of the topology axioms, and a distinction between authorized action and represented evidence. An AGENTS.md or SOUL.md file alone is not a covering sieve. The conjecture suggests this programme but does not answer whether all locales admit such a representation, or prove self-similarity of the institutional tower.

**Status.** Structural observation (conjecture). The self-similarity is visible and motivates the conjecture; formal proof depends on: (1) a precise definition of directed stratified site with correct axioms, (2) (∞,1)-categorical infrastructure for the meta-level case, and (3) verification that the subtopos-join formulation of the meta-covering satisfies the sheaf condition.

*Source.* [Directed Stratified Site §Conjecture](../../../directed-stratified-site.md). Status: open structural conjecture; no proof-closing symbol is warranted.
