An **entity** is an element $e$ of an entity-set $E$ — the base set of a predication fibration $p : \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{C}$ over a predicate category $\mathcal{C}$ and a residuated lattice $\mathbf{L}$ [@benabou1985; @grothendieck1971]. As an element of a bare set, an entity carries no internal structure on its own: $E$ is set-typed, with finiteness or sort-stratification imposed by specific theories rather than by the entity-form itself.

What an entity acquires through the fibration is its **bundle of incident cells**: for each predicate $p$ and each other entity $o$, the fiber algebra $A_p = \mathbf{L}^{\bowtie E \times E}$ assigns a bilattice value to the pair $(e, o)$ and to the pair $(o, e)$ — the values of the predication cells with $e$ in the subject and object positions respectively. The collection of all such cells, viewed categorically, is the slice category $\mathcal{P}/e$ in the predication category $\mathcal{P}$ — the standard bundle-around-a-basepoint construction [@maclane1971, ch. I §3].

The cell `(entity, slicing, slice-category)` records this: every entity has a slice category attached by the slicing morphism. Colloquially, an entity is a *fiber bundle around its basepoint in $\mathcal{P}$*; rigorously, the bundle is the slice category $\mathcal{P}/e$.

## Distinction from structure

An entity is not a structure-in-the-Bourbaki-sense (a set with characterizations and axioms). An entity is a *point* in a base; a structure is a *categorical object* in $\mathcal{P}$ equipped with a signature. An entity may *inhabit* one or more structure-forms — the cell-graph attached to $e$ in $\mathcal{P}$ may satisfy a structure-form $S$'s signature — but the inhabiting morphism is a relation between an element and a type, not an identity of the two notions.
