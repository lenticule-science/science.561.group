The library holds strong philosophical coverage of time (McTaggart A/B-series, Whiteheadian concrescence, Buddhist momentariness, arrow-of-time) and thin operational scheduling vocabulary (deadline, schedule, cadence, milestone), with nothing in between. The system has no concepts for duration, horizon, lifecycle, recurrence, or urgency — the bridging terms that would let it reason about how long something takes, how far ahead to plan, how entities move through temporal phases, how events return, or how time creates pressure. Governance terms (norm, duty, mandate, accountability, charter) carry no temporal dimension; norms have validity periods and mandates have terms but those relationships aren't encoded. This plan fills the bridge: one survey pass, five bridging-term definitions, a critical-theory-grounded research pass into temporality-of-normative-systems, two texts (temporality-of-normative-systems, temporality-of-endeavors), and a final task that connects the library concepts back to the operational tools that need temporal grounding. Full gap diagnosis, what-exists inventory, step DAG, and the non-canonical frontmatter keys (also-serves, advances, ordering) live in `analyses/temporality-coverage-enrichment-context.md`.
