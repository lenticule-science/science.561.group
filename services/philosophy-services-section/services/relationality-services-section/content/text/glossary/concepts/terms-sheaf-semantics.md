Relational sheaf semantics describes how [Profile](../../documents/articles/notes/undated/profile.md)s — the nested relational universes produced at step 17 — function as sheaves: local data that glues together consistently into global structure.

In standard mathematics, a sheaf on a topological space (or on a site, in the categorical generalization) assigns data to open sets such that the data are compatible on overlaps and can be uniquely glued. Sheaf semantics provides the internal logic of a topos — every topos is equivalent to the category of sheaves on some site. Presheaf models ([functors](../../triage/relationality-2/mathematics/objects/categories/ordinary-categories/terms/functor.md) from a small category to sets) are the simplest case. Earlier formulations of relationality used the [presheaf](../../documents/articles/notes/presheaf.md) category PSh({0→1}) — [functors](../../triage/relationality-2/mathematics/objects/categories/ordinary-categories/terms/functor.md) on the two-element cell — as a concrete model for the relational structure.

The derivation produces sheaf-like structure through the following sequence:

1. [Geometry](../../documents/articles/notes/undated/geometry.md) (step 16) is the space where [Flow](../../triage/relationality-2/ai-generated-relationality/terms/flow.md) and [Nucleus](../../../../services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/predicated-agentic-orchestration-platform/library/relational-hyperverse-repository/math/nucleus.md) cohere. Within Geometry, [Discipline](../../documents/articles/notes/discipline.md)s carve stable patterns, and [Regime](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/regime.md)s record what each Discipline stabilizes.
2. A [Filter](../../documents/articles/notes/filter.md) is a Discipline compatible with all Flows and all Nuclei — maximally compatible with the entire Geometry. What a Filter carves out inherits the full structure of the field.
3. A Profile is what a Filter carves out: a complete relational universe containing its own [Term](../../triage/relationality-2/ai-generated-relationality/terms/term.md)s, [Judgement](../../triage/relationality-2/ai-generated-relationality/terms/judgement.md)s, [Order](../../triage/relationality-2/ai-generated-relationality/terms/order.md), Flow, Nucleus, and Geometry. The entire derivation reconstructs itself within each Profile.
4. Profiles nest: applying a Filter within a Profile yields a sub-Profile, which also contains the full tower. This nesting is indefinite.

The [sheaf condition](../../documents/articles/notes/sheaf-condition.md) holds because Profiles are carved by Filters that commute with everything: local data (the contents of a Profile) are compatible with the global structure (the full Geometry) by construction. There is no gluing problem — compatibility is guaranteed by the algebraic properties of Filters.

This is what makes the relational structure a [topos](infinity-topos.md): the internal logic (the [Heyting algebra](../../triage/relationality-2/ai-generated-relationality/relationality-slop-2026-03-03/mathematics/terms/heyting-algebra/index.md)) and the sheaf semantics (Profiles as sections over Filters) are two aspects of the same derived structure. The [Heyting algebra](../../triage/relationality-2/ai-generated-relationality/relationality-slop-2026-03-03/mathematics/terms/heyting-algebra/index.md) provides the logic; the sheaf semantics provides the models.
