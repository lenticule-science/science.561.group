The minimal act required for a thing to act, be conditioned, or have relations. To claim that something exists is to claim that it is not what it is not — this claim draws a distinction, and distinction is relational in form.

## What it is

Draft 7 provides the extended derivation. To claim something exists specifies two things: that the thing is not some other thing, and that the thing is not nothing. These specifications compel two primitive, mutually irreducible acts: including (holding a thing within) and excluding (holding a thing out). Including and excluding must be held together in ongoing opposition, producing the condition of tension. Tension produces difference — the condition of being set apart. Sustaining tension compels the act of relating, which produces the condition of relation and the structure of the relative (the first durable entity).

The derivation follows draft 7's sequence:

1. **Including and Excluding** — To be a thing and not another, a thing must exclude what it is not and include what it is. These are primitive acts producing inclusion and exclusion as conditions.
2. **Tension** — Including and excluding must be held open together, producing the condition of mutual opposition.
3. **Irreducible asymmetry** — Every inclusion demands exclusion of its own negation, which itself is a condition requiring inclusion. This recursion generates an asymmetry that ensures the derivation is never final.
4. **Difference** — Tension induces the condition of coherent separation: the thing is set apart from what it is not.
5. **Relating** — To sustain tension over time, an overarching act must maintain it. Relating is this act.
6. **Relation** — Relating produces the condition that binds the thing to its ongoing existing.
7. **Relative** — Relating and relation together produce the first structure: a relative holds relation open durably. This is where the architecture of existing achieves closure.

## Why it matters

Existing is what the phenomenological layer IS. All other acts (including, excluding, relating, reflexing, differentiating) are derived as requirements of existing. The formal operators (Together, Either, Induces, Negate) are the algebraic encoding of the acts that existing compels.

Draft 7: existing is a perpetual process, not a state. The irreducible asymmetry ensures it is never complete — every inclusion generates new exclusions, sustaining the derivation indefinitely.

## Relationships

- Compels: including, excluding (as primitive acts)
- Produces: tension (condition), difference (condition), relation (condition), relative (structure)
- Requires: relating (as the sustaining act)
- Grounded by: the Articulation Theorem (to describe "without relation" is to effect an exclusion, which is relational in form)
- Formalized by: the Heyting algebra operators (Together, Either, Induces, Negate) which encode the requirements of coherent existing
