**Existing** is the minimal act required for a thing to act, be conditioned, or stand in relation. Where being has to do with conditions, existing has to do with acts.

To claim that something exists is not trivial: it specifies that the thing is not some other thing and that it is not nothing. Existing therefore has to be derived, and deriving it produces the first architecture of relationality: two primitive acts, the conditions they bring about, the [tension](tension.md) that holds them together, the [difference](difference.md) that tension induces, the act of [relating](relating.md) that sustains it, the [relation](relation.md) it produces, and the [relative](relative.md), the first structure. With the relative the architecture of existing closes into coherence: an act that has the condition and structure necessary to act.

Existing is a perpetual process, not a state. To include, one must also include the inclusion of inclusion as a condition, which is meaningful only if its exclusion is also a condition; that is itself a condition, and the recursion does not end. Every inclusion generates new exclusions, so the derivation is never final.

## The derivation

In emsenn's words:

- To claim _something exists_ is not trivial. It specifies:
    - A thing is not some other thing;
    - A thing is not nothing.
- Therefore, existing must be derived.
- To be a thing and not another, a thing must exclude what isn't itself, and therefore must include what it is.
    - This establishes two acts: including and excluding.
    - Including: the act by which a thing is brought into what is.
    - Excluding: the act by which a thing is held out of what is not.
    - These are primitive acts: each brings about a corresponding condition, inclusion and exclusion.
    - Neither reduces to the other, and both are required for anything to be meaningfully said to exist.
- The acts of including and excluding must be held open together. This introduces the condition of tension.
    - Tension is the dynamic holding of inclusion and exclusion in active opposition.
    - To exist is to be sustained not just in tension but in a **perpetual process of derivation**: an ongoing act that holds open what cannot be fully closed.
- The condition of difference is induced by tension.
    - Difference is the minimal condition that marks a thing as set apart from what is not.
    - It is **held open by the ongoing tension** between including and excluding.
    - Without tension, there can be no difference; without difference, existing fails to achieve coherence.
- To sustain tension over time, there must be an overarching act that maintains it.
    - Otherwise, the opposing acts would lose their mutual relation and the condition of existing would collapse.
    - We name this act relating: the minimal act by which a thing sustains its own tension of including and excluding.
- The act of relating introduces a further requirement: the condition of relation.
    - Relating engages the thing in sustaining its own existing, but this engagement must itself have a condition to ensure that the thing is not merely involved abstractly but is bound into it.
    - Without the condition of relation, the act of relating remains transient and fails to stabilize the dynamic.
- The act of relating together with the condition of relation necessarily produces a relative.
    - A relative is the minimal structure that holds a relation open durably.
    - While relating is an act and relation is a condition, acts and conditions alone are transient.
    - The relative is the **first structure**: the stable outcome in which the thing is maintained as existing within the relational field.
    - Only with the relative does the architecture of existing achieve **closure**: the coherent stabilization of the entire dynamic.

All the other acts of relationality (including, excluding, relating, reflexing, differentiating) are derived as requirements of existing.
