In the [relationality](../_index.md) framework, **settlement** names the joint four-cornered structure within which every relational claim has its position. It is not a single value attached to a claim but a *position in a two-dimensional space* — the space whose two axes are the truth axis ([true](true.md) ↔ [false](false.md)) and the knowledge axis ([absurd](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/absurd.md) ↔ [determination](determination.md)). The four corners of this space are the extremal positions a claim can occupy; the interior is the mass of partial settlements that constitute most of what inquiry actually deals with.

Settlement replaces the classical commitment to bivalent truth-value (every proposition is true or false) without replacing it with a higher-arity truth-value. A claim's position is not one of two, three, or four discrete values but a position in the joint structure, with the four corners as poles. Most claims live in the interior, with truth-axis and knowledge-axis values that are neither maximal nor minimal.

## The two axes

The truth axis runs from [false](false.md) (denial) to [true](true.md) (affirmation). It records the *direction* in which evidence accumulates — whether what has been witnessed affirms or denies the relation.

The knowledge axis runs from [absurd](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/absurd.md) (no evidence) to [determination](determination.md) (full evidence). It records the *amount* of witnessing structure that has accumulated — how much relational machinery the framework has applied to the claim.

The two axes are independent. A claim can be:

- High on the knowledge axis (determined) and high on the truth axis (true) — this is the *true-only* corner: fully settled toward affirmation.
- High on the knowledge axis (determined) and low on the truth axis (false) — this is the *false-only* corner: fully settled toward denial.
- High on the knowledge axis (determined) and *neither* on the truth axis — this is the *both* corner: fully settled with evidence in both directions, paraconsistently. This is the [determination](determination.md) extreme without truth-axis tilt.
- Low on the knowledge axis (absurd) — this is the *neither* corner: no settlement, the truth axis is undefined because no evidence has been accumulated. This is the [absurd](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/absurd.md) extreme.

The four-cornered shape is therefore not a flat enumeration of four values; it is the four extremal positions of a two-dimensional joint structure.

## The four corners

| Corner | Truth axis | Knowledge axis | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| true-only | true | determined | "Affirmed and structurally witnessed" |
| false-only | false | determined | "Denied and structurally witnessed" |
| both | mid | determined | "Witnessed in both directions; paraconsistent" |
| neither | mid | absurd | "Un-witnessed; held bare or not held at all" |

Classical bivalent logic recognizes only the first two and refuses the second two. Three-valued logics (like Kleene's) recognize the first three but treat *neither* as a special non-classical value. Relationality treats all four as positive structural data — corners of the same joint space, none of them defective relative to the others.

## Settlement as the work of inquiry

A claim begins its life in the *neither* corner — absurd, no relations yet apply. As [relating](relating.md) acts on it, witnessing accumulates and the claim moves through the joint space. Some of that motion is along the truth axis (evidence tilts the claim toward true or false); some is along the knowledge axis (the determination becomes more or less fully witnessed). The trajectory is the work of inquiry; the position at any given moment is the claim's settlement.

Settlement is local and revisable. A claim that has settled at one corner under one configuration of relations may settle differently under another, or may become unsettled when new relations are introduced. The framework treats this as the honest description of what inquiry actually produces, rather than as a defect to be patched out by additional axioms.

## Settlement and the math layer

The mathematical articulation of settlement is the bilattice-valued [predication fiber algebra](../../../../math/domains/voidmath/terms/predication-fiber-algebra.md). The two axes — truth and knowledge — are the bilattice's two parallel orders ($\leq_t$ and $\leq_k$). The four corners are the bilattice's extremal elements: $\mathbf{t}$ (true-only), $\mathbf{f}$ (false-only), $\top$ (both/determined), $\bot$ (neither/absurd). Every value in the fiber algebra is a settlement position; the bilattice operations describe how settlements combine, how negation flips the truth axis, how knowledge-meet and knowledge-join compose evidence.

The bilattice synthesis is not a free choice. The four-corner structure is forced by taking [predication](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/predication.md) — the directed triadic primitive — seriously and asking what truth-value structure is consistent with the framework's commitments. Bilattice logics arrived at the four-corner structure independently in the 1980s (Belnap, Ginsberg, Fitting); the relationality framework recovers the same structure from its own derivation, and identifies its philosophical articulation with the bilattice-mathematical one.
