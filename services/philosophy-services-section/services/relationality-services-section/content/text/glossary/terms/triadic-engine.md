The pattern by which each phase of the derivation unfolds. Each phase shows that a given structure's coherence requires three dynamics: determination (act), relation (condition), and structure (form). The new structure leaves something undetermined, inciting the next phase.

## What it is

Draft 1 (most explicit): Each of the 9 phases follows the pattern:
- "Given that X is determined by Y, it cannot not derive there is a dynamic of determination" → names the ACT
- "Given that the act is determined by how it engages Z, it cannot not derive there is a dynamic of relation" → names the CONDITION
- "Given that the condition is determined by how it structures W, it cannot not derive there is a dynamic of structure" → names the STRUCTURE
- "Determining [act], [condition], and [structure] provides the minimal dynamic determination for cohering [the unit], but leaves unresolved dynamics which must be derived" → INCITES next phase

PRE: "Act–Condition–Structure Triad" (Lemma 4 in §I.4). "Act: formation of a Differentiate. Condition: application of Induces(a, −). Structure: composition T(a) = Together(a, Induces(a, Top))."

## The incitement pattern

Each phase's structure is incomplete — its coherence requires something not yet given. This incompleteness is not a deficiency but the generative engine of the derivation. Draft 1: "but leaves unresolved dynamics which must be derived."

This is also the link to emsenn's vocabulary of "incitement" and "induction" as relational methods: incitement is what an incomplete structure compels, induction is what a completed structure produces.

## Relationships

- Organizes: all 9 phases of draft 1, all 5 movements of draft 12, all 8 phases of Foundations
- Each phase's triad: one act, one condition, one structure
- Exceptions: phases 7 and 9 in draft 1 produce TWO acts, TWO conditions, TWO structures (the field splits into local and global)
