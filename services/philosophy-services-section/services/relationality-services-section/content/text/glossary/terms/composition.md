**Composition** places relational structures side by side into an ordered whole. A composite of $p$ structures at a shared [relational depth](relational-depth.md) $n$ is written

$$\mathrm{composite}(p, [S_1, \dots, S_p], n).$$

It introduces no act beyond [relating](relating.md). It is how relating holds several structures at once, and it gives the relational field breadth as [reflexive sequencing](reflexive-sequencing.md) gives it depth.

## Order is identity

A composite is determined by its components together with their positions. Swapping two positions gives a different composite, even when the two components are the same structure:

$$\mathrm{composite}(2, [A, B], n) \neq \mathrm{composite}(2, [B, A], n).$$

Positional difference holds differentiation open inside a unit, and so the unit does not collapse into sameness.

## Kinds of composite

- A [tension](tension.md) is a composite of two.
- A [chain](chain.md) is an ordered sequence of composites, typically tensions, at one depth.
- A composite is itself a relational structure, so it can be deepened, placed in further composites, and nested.
