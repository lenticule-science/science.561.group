**Tension** is the condition that holds inclusion and exclusion in mutual, active opposition. It is not a third act alongside including and excluding but the dynamic holding that binds two opposed conditions together, the minimal dynamic by which a thing is coherently sustained as both what it is and not what it is not.

Tension is the first non-trivial condition in the derivation of [existing](existing.md), and it is why the derivation must go on. Once including and excluding are established, their opposition has to be held coherently, and holding it induces [difference](difference.md). Without tension relationality collapses either into triviality (total inclusion) or into incoherence (total exclusion). Tension is sustained by [relating](relating.md) and precedes coherence.

In a Heyting algebra this openness shows where excluded middle fails: $a \vee \neg a$ need not be the top, so a claim and its complement need not exhaust what is.

## Formally

A tension is the ordered [composite](composition.md) of two relational structures at one shared [relational depth](relational-depth.md):

$$\mathrm{Tension}(A, B, n) := \mathrm{composite}(2, [A, B], n).$$

It introduces no act beyond [relating](relating.md). Its order carries its identity: $\mathrm{Tension}(A, B, n) \neq \mathrm{Tension}(B, A, n)$ even when $A = B$, which is how a tension holds differentiation within a unit. Both structures keep their own content and depth. A tension is itself a relational structure: it can be reflexively deepened, keeping $A$ before $B$ ([reflexive sequencing](reflexive-sequencing.md)), and assembled into [chains](chain.md).
