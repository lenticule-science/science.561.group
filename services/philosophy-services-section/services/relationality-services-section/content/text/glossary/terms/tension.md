**Tension** is the condition that holds inclusion and exclusion in mutual, active opposition. It is not a third act alongside including and excluding but the dynamic holding that binds two opposed conditions together, the minimal dynamic by which a thing is coherently sustained as both what it is and not what it is not. It is "a productive dynamic where relating binds and unrelating resists."

Tension is the first non-trivial condition in the derivation of [existing](existing.md), and it is why the derivation must go on. Once including and excluding are established, their opposition has to be held coherently, and holding it induces [difference](difference.md). Without tension relationality collapses either into triviality (total inclusion) or into incoherence (total exclusion). Tension is sustained by [relating](relating.md) and precedes coherence.

In the math, tension is what a two-sided value allows: evidence for a claim and evidence against it can both be held without either cancelling the other, which is the told-both corner of the four-valued reading ([evidence](../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/evidence.md)). In a Heyting algebra the same openness shows where excluded middle fails: $a \vee \neg a$ need not be the top, so a claim and its complement need not exhaust what is.
