In the [relationality](../_index.md) framework, **possible** names the mode in which a claim has begun to be witnessed but is not yet structurally determined. Where [absurd](absurd.md) is the bare-assertion mode (no relational structure constrains the claim), possible is the *opening* mode: relations have begun to apply to the claim, but the relations are not yet tight enough to force the claim's shape. The claim is a candidate for determination but has not yet been determined.

Possibility is the second mode in the four-mode trajectory of [inquiry](inquiry.md): absurd → possible → [necessary](necessary.md) → [determination](determination.md). It is not a separate truth-value alongside [true](true.md) and [false](false.md); it is a *position* in the inquiry's progress, distinguishable by how much witnessing has accumulated and how tight the resulting constraints are.

## Possibility is what absurdity excites

The framework's characteristic move at the bottom is that [absurdity](absurd.md) — taken seriously rather than dismissed — produces relations. The first relations an absurdity produces are *possibilities*: claims that could be true given the absurd starting point, not yet forced but no longer arbitrary. To take an absurd claim seriously is to begin opening the space of what becomes possible *because* of it.

This is why possibility is a *structurally consequent* mode rather than an arbitrary one. Different absurdities excite different possibilities; the absurdities the inquiry has accepted determine what the inquiry can recognize as possible at all. There is no possibility outside the structure of accepted absurdities.

## Possibility versus mere consistency

Possibility in the framework is not the same as logical consistency. A claim is consistent if it does not contradict already-determined claims; a claim is possible if some [witnessing](../../../../services/agency-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/witnessing.md) has begun to relate it to the rest of the system. Consistency is a property a claim has *given* a system; possibility is what an inquiry has *produced* with respect to a claim. A consistent claim that no one has examined is not yet possible in the framework's sense — it is absurd and consistent.

This distinction matters because much of inquiry's work is moving claims from *absurd-but-consistent* to *possible*: opening up the relational structure within which a claim could be witnessed, not merely verifying that nothing forbids it.

## Possibility moves toward necessity

As inquiry continues, possibilities tighten into [necessities](necessary.md). The relations that opened a claim's possibility become more constraining; the claim's shape narrows; eventually the claim is *forced* by the structure rather than merely accommodated by it. The trajectory does not end at possibility; possibility is a stage en route to determination.

A claim that remains possible without ever being either ruled out or determined is one whose inquiry has stalled — the witnessings that would tighten it into necessity have not been performed, or the absurdities the inquiry rests on do not constrain the claim further. The framework treats this as honest description rather than a defect.

## Possibility versus modal possibility

Possibility in the framework is not modal possibility (true in some possible world). The framework does not commit to a space of possible worlds; it works inside one configuration of accepted [absurdities](absurd.md) and tracks how claims move from un-witnessed to witnessed within that configuration. A claim is possible *here, now, given these absurdities*; the framework offers no inter-frame possibility relation that would let one claim be possible in one inquiry and impossible in another while both inquiries shared the same logical resources. The claim's mode depends on the inquiry's specific structure.
