A map j : A → A on a Heyting algebra A is **extensive** if a ≤ j(a) for every a ∈ A. Extensiveness means j can only move elements upward — it closes or strengthens, never weakens or deletes. A closure operator that moved elements downward would reduce information; extensiveness ensures that applying j accumulates rather than discards.

Extensiveness is one of the three conditions that together define a nucleus on a Heyting algebra: extensive, idempotent, and meet-preserving. In the [generative fibered recognition trace universe](../../documents/articles/notes/generative-fibered-recognition-trace-universe.md), both the [stabilizer](../../documents/articles/notes/generative-fibered-recognition-trace-universe.md) and the fiber-transfer operator are asserted extensive and idempotent on each fiber, which is what makes them nuclei rather than arbitrary endomorphisms — this term names, generally, the property those operators are each claimed to satisfy.
