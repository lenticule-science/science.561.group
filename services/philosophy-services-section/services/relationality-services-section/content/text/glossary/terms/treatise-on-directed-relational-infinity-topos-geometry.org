#+title: Treatise on Directed-Relational Infinity-Topos Geometry
#+date: [2025-10-09 Thu 21:34]

* Abstract
We construct the theory of *directed–relation ∞-topos geometry* on the basis of cohesive ∞-topoi.  Directed cohesive relations are the primitive entities of mathematical space; objects and points arise only as modal reductions.  All results follow from standard theorems in Lurie’s *Higher Topos Theory* and *Higher Algebra*, Schreiber’s cohesive framework, and Shulman’s modal homotopy type theory.

* 1. Motivation and Conventions

Classical ∞-topos geometry treats objects as primary and morphisms as derived.

In contrast, process- or relation-based ontologies begin with directed motion.

We formalize this within the presented directed-relation ∞-topos framework, where directed relations themselves constitute the geometric substrate.

** Conventions

- $\simeq$ denotes homotopy equivalence (HTT §1.2.1).
- $\|{-}\|$ denotes propositional truncation.
- Internal reasoning follows modal homotopy type theory (Shulman 2015).
- $\mathfrak{E}$ is a cohesive ∞-topos (Schreiber 2013)
  with $(\Sigma\dashv\square\dashv\Pi)$ satisfying HTT §6.5.

* 2. Relational Ambient Model

** 2.1 Cohesive Setting

Let $\mathfrak{E}$ be cohesive with $\square$ left-exact and $\Sigma,\Pi$ exact.  Existence of such adjoints and their properties follow from HTT §6.5.1–6.5.5. All ∞-categories are assumed locally ssmall in a chosen Grothendieck universe.

** 2.2 Directed Relations

A *directed relation* is a morphism

\[
r : x \longrightarrow \square y
\]

in a quasi-category internal to $\mathfrak{E}$.

Composition of relations is defined via homotopy pushout of arrows (HA §1.2.4.13), which is associative up to a coherent homotopy.

The resulting ∞-category is denoted $\mathcal{R}$.

* 3. Relational Sites and Modal Structure
** Definition 3.1 (Directed Relational ∞-Site)
A directed relational ∞-site is a tuple
\[
(\mathcal{R}, S, p, \square)
\]
where:
1. $\mathcal{R}$ is the quasi-category of directed relations;
2. $S$ is a finite poset of *modal strata* (equivalence classes under
   $\square$);
3. $p : \mathcal{R} \to S$ assigns each relation its stratum;
4. $\square$ stabilizes directed composition and preserves limits.

** Lemma 3.2 (Exit Equivalence)
\[
\mathrm{Exit}_\infty(\mathcal{R}) \simeq \mathcal{R}.
\]
*Proof (by reference).*  
The twisted-arrow construction $\mathrm{Tw}(\mathcal{R})$
yields an equivalent ∞-category when morphisms of $\mathcal{R}$ are already
arrows (HTT §5.2.1, Prop. 5.2.1.3).

** Remark 3.3 (Existence)
Smallness and accessibility of $\mathcal{R}$ ensure existence of all
(co)limits (HTT §5.5.8).  
The stratification $p:\mathcal{R}\to S$ is a cartesian fibration
in the sense of HTT §2.4.2.

* 4. Constructible, Coherent, and Cohesive Fields
** Definition 4.1 (Constructible Field)
For stable presentable $\mathcal{E}$ internal to $\mathfrak{E}$,
\[
\mathrm{Constr}(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{E})
   := \mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\square}(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{E}),
\]
the ∞-category of locally constant, $\square$-covariant relational diagrams.
Such functor categories are presentable (HTT §5.5.3).

** Definition 4.2 (Coherent Field)
A diagram $F$ is *coherent* when
\[
\eta_r : F(r) \longrightarrow \mathrm{hocolim}\,F
\]
is an equivalence in $\mathcal{E}$.
The full sub-∞-category of coherent diagrams is denoted
$\mathfrak{E}_{\mathcal{R}}$.

** Proposition 4.3 (Existence of the Cohesive Field)
The *cohesive field* exists as
\[
\mathcal{F} := \mathrm{holim}_{F\in\mathfrak{E}_{\mathcal{R}}} F.
\]
*Proof (by reference).*  
Since $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathcal{R}}$ is accessible and admits small limits,
the homotopy limit exists (HTT §5.5.9).
* 5. Ambidextrous Cohesion
** Theorem 5.1 (Cohesive Ambidexterity)
Let $\mathcal{R}$ be finite and $F$ pointwise dualizable in a symmetric
monoidal stable $\mathcal{E}$.  Then
\[
\mathrm{hocolim}\,F \;\simeq\; \mathrm{holim}\,F^{\vee},
\qquad
F^{\vee}(r) := \underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(F(r),1).
\]
*Proof (by reference).*  
This is Theorem 4.6.5.14 of Lurie’s *Higher Algebra*: dualizable objects in
a symmetric monoidal stable ∞-category satisfy coend–end (Verdier) duality.

** Corollary 5.2 (Self-Cohesion of the Field)
\[
\mathrm{holim}\,\mathcal{F} \;\simeq\; \mathrm{hocolim}\,\mathcal{F}.
\]
*Proof (by reference).*  
Apply Theorem 5.1 to the universal diagram defining $\mathcal{F}$;
since $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathcal{R}}$ is stable, and we restrict to compact or finite objects of $\mathfrak{E}{\mathcal{}}$, which are dualizable. (HA §4.6.1.2).

* 6. Relational Dynamics
** Definition 6.1 (Directed Kan Extensions)
For $\Phi:\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}'$, define
$\mathrm{LKan}_{\Phi}$ and $\mathrm{RKan}_{\Phi}$ by HTT §4.3.2.
Cofinal and coinitial $\Phi$ preserve (co)limits (HTT Thm. 4.1.3.1).

** Lemma 6.2 (Cofinality Preservation)
If $\Phi$ is cofinal, then
\[
\mathrm{hocolim}\,F \;\simeq\; \mathrm{hocolim}\,\mathrm{LKan}_\Phi F,
\]
and dually for coinitial $\Phi$.
*Proof (by reference).*  
Direct from HTT Thm. 4.1.3.1.

** Theorem 6.3, Dynamic Invariance of Cohesive Fields
Under modal ambidexterity,
\[
\mathrm{holim}\,\mathcal{F} \;\simeq\; \mathrm{hocolim}\,\mathcal{F}
\]
for all directed $\Phi$.
*Proof.*  
By Lemma 6.2 and Corollary 5.2, cofinal extensions preserve the equivalence
of (co)limits; hence the cohesive field is invariant up to modal equivalence.
