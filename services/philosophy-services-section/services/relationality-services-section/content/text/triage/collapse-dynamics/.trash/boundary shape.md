A ***boundary shape*** is a [[field geometry]] induced by the [[recursive stabilization]] of [[internal modulation under constraint]].

When a constraint system encounters internal modulation that cannot be extended without collapse or interference,  
the resulting [[curvature]], [[collapse]], or [[interference resolution]] stabilizes as a boundary.

> A boundary shape marks the outer limit of coherent modulation propagation.

It constrains:
- the extension of identity through propagation  
- the directionality of recursive modulation  
- the location of interference nodes and edge attractors



A ***boundary shape*** is a [[field structure]] that [[emerge|emerges]] from recursive [[modulation]], [[collapse]], or [[curvature]] within a [[constrained domain]]. It marks the ***[[limit]]*** at which [[internal consistency]], [[coherence]], or [[identity]] can be [[maintain|maintained]] under [[ongoing propagation]].

Boundary shapes may result from:
- [[recursive internal constraint self-modulation]]
- [[repeat frustration]] of [[unresolved desire]]
- [[interference]] from [[overlapping constraint surface|overlapping constraint surfaces]]

They are induced by [[modulation history]], and ***[[sustain|sustained]]*** by [[curvature]] and [[identity coherence]].

> That is, a boundary shape is what remains  
> when modulation cannot continue without contradiction,  
> and instead turns back into the field that produced it.

Boundary shapes encode:
- limits of transformation  
- edges of semantic identity  
- sites of potential collapse or resonance

They function as ***[[soft event horizons]]*** for symbolic systems:  
visible in modulation, but ***not resolvable through desire alone.***
