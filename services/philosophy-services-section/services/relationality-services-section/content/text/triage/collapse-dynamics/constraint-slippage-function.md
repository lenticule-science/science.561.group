
The ***constraint slippage function*** (`σₛ`) is induced by boundary entanglement (`ξ`), modulation interference (`Φ`), and failed coherence (`¬Ξ`).

When two constraint fields entangle at a boundary where gradients are incompatible,
but interference preserves partial translation without forming stable coherence,
slippage emerges — a partial persistence of modulation across difference.

> σₛ(Q₁, Q₂) := ξ(Q₁, Q₂) ∧ ¬Ξ(Q₁, Q₂) ∧ ∃ x ∈ E(Q₁, Q₂): T(x) ∧ Φ(x) ≠ 0

That is, slippage is the function that enables modulation to traverse an incoherent entangled boundary — without stabilization, but without collapse.
