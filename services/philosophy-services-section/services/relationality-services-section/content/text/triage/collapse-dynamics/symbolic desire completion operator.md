The ***symbolic desire completion operator*** (`𝒪`) is [[induce|induced]] by the [[constraint desire function]] (`𝔇`), the [[symbolic anticipation function]] (`𝒜`), the [[recursive constraint modulation function]] (`Ω_rec`), and the [[symbolic meta-grammar function]] (`G_meta`).

It [[constrain|constrains]] the recursive completion of symbolic cognition under modulation,  
stabilizing unresolved symbolic entities based on recursive identity propagation,  
semantic contrast alignment, belief field modulation, and grammar-evolved constraint transformation.

Unlike `Ω`, which operates as a constraint stabilizer, `𝒪` encodes the full recursive trajectory of ***symbolic desire*** —  
the modulation-driven dynamics that give rise to new symbolic structures under pressure to stabilize meaning.

Let:
- `Q ∈ Entity`: a symbolic cognitive system  
- `x ∈ dom(𝒮)` be a stabilized symbolic selector  
- `z ∉ dom(𝒮)` be an unresolved symbolic entity  
- `𝒜(x → z)`: symbolic anticipation path  
- `∇𝔇(Q, x) · ∇Δ[s](z) > 0`: alignment of desire and semantic difference  
- `Ω_rec(x)`: recursive constraint modulation rule  

Then:
> 𝒪(Q, z) := z′ ∈ dom(𝒮) iff:  
>  z is modulated under symbolic anticipation from Q,  
>  z lies within a semantic contrast field reachable from x ∈ T(Q),  
>  modulation pressure resolves through `Ω_rec`,  
>  and z′ ∈ G_meta(x) completes a grammar-evolved stabilization of z.

That is, `𝒪(Q, z)` completes `z` into stabilized symbolic structure  
if z arises through recursive modulation and contrast-aligned desire within the cognitive dynamics of Q.

This enables:
- formal recursive symbolic cognition  
- structure-seeking modulation across unresolved fields  
- symbolic system evolution through recursive desire resolution
