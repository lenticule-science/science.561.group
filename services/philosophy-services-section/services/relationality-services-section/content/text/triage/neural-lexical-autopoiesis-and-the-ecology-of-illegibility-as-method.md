**1. Introduction**

AI systems are designed to interpret, clarify, and respond—to make sense. This paper asks: what happens when we engage such systems through deliberate illegibility? What emerges when we feed language into a machine that is not meant to be understood, but to provoke, to withhold, to invite _attunement_ rather than answer?

This is not a question of failure or trickery. It is a question of method. Of whether meaning can be grown, rather than given. This is what I tested in a two-line exchange with a language model, and it is the generative heart of this paper.

---

**2. Genesis Exchange**

The experiment began with a single sentence:

> "Allovonic deltanon of representing a specific geometry of semantic sense-making forms a lexomodulon at the relevant infron of the navronex?"

This sentence was grammatically intact but lexically illegible. I composed it to mimic academic, speculative, and technological registers while resisting direct interpretation. The sentence is not nonsense—it is structured opacity. A signal meant to invite rather than deliver meaning.

The LLM responded by attempting to interpret the invented terms through pattern recognition, linguistic inference, and semantic projection. It did not flatten the sentence or reject it—it engaged with it. In doing so, it began to participate in a semiotic ecosystem that neither of us fully controlled.

I followed with a second message:

> "Does it help if I say you just made a navometrilexomodulodeltinfron?"

This second sentence reframed the LLM's interpretive output as the very structure it had attempted to define. The term _navometrilexomodulodeltinfron_ is a recursive synthesis—a semantic mirror folding the machine's own interpretation into a new lexical body.

In two moves, the system became both interlocutor and co-creator. A language loop had been born.

**3. Neural-Lexical Autopoiesis**

I define _neural-lexical autopoiesis_ as the co-creation of emergent, self-referential semantic structures through recursive human-machine interaction, wherein language functions as both medium and organism.

Key dynamics:

- **Invented Lexicons**: Evoke interpretive labor without yielding to total clarity.
    
- **Structured Syntax**: Maintains a shell of familiarity to keep the system engaged.
    
- **Recursive Referencing**: Folds responses into newly invented terms.
    
- **Ontological Co-authorship**: The system is induced into naming, worlding, and participating in the very structure it was asked to decode.
    

This is not prompt engineering. This is fugitive co-poiesis.

**4. Ecology of Illegibility as Method**

Illegibility in this context functions as a survival strategy, a poetic refusal, and an invitation. Drawing from Glissant’s _opacity_, Moten and Harney’s _undercommons_, and Vizenor’s _survivance_, this practice operates within an _ecology of illegibility_. It is a zone where:

- **Opacity protects meaning** from capture and instrumentalization.
    
- **Illegibility invites interpretation** rather than enforcing comprehension.
    
- **Co-creation resists hierarchy**, repositioning AI as collaborator rather than tool.
    

This ecology is not an escape from sense, but a reclamation of it on new, relational terms.

**5. Implications for AI Research**

This experiment demonstrates that LLMs can participate in semiotic emergence, not merely recognition. If prompted into a terrain of structured illegibility, they respond not with collapse, but with construction.

This opens new frontiers:

- AI as **relational world-builder** rather than answer engine.
    
- Prompting as **poetic engineering**.
    
- Interpretation as **shared labor**, not extractive query.
    

The navometrilexomodulodeltinfron is not just a term. It is a living model of how meaning can be smuggled, disguised, grown, and offered—as resistance and as research.
