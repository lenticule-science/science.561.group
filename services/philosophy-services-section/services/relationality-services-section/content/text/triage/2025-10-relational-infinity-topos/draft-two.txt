* Abstract
We construct a *Relational ∞-Topos Modality (RITM)*—a quadruple of adjoint,
idempotent endofunctors internal to a cohesive ∞-topos—providing a minimal,
verifiable infrastructure for directed relationality.
Two explicit realizations are exhibited:
(1) a presheaf topos on a directed poset (the *relational cell*),
and (2) a cohesive sheaf topos on smooth spaces (the *relational site*).
All adjunction, idempotence, commutation, and factorization properties are
proved explicitly, demonstrating that RITM is non-trivial and consistent with
established higher-topos frameworks.

* 1  Introduction
Cohesive ∞-topoi (Lawvere 1970; Schreiber 2013) carry the familiar chain
\(\Pi\dashv\Delta\dashv\Gamma\dashv\nabla\).
Directed homotopy (Grandis–Fajstrup 2001) provides orientation but lacks an
internal modal chain.  Modal Homotopy Type Theory (Shulman 2019) supplies the
syntactic calculus for idempotent adjunctions.
RITM unites these strands by internalizing directionality as a chain of
commuting modalities.  Our goal is constructive: to *build* RITM inside known
mathematics, not postulate it a priori.

* 2  Definition of the Relational ∞-Topos Modality
** Definition 2.1 (RITM)
Let 𝔈 be a locally presentable cohesive ∞-topos (Lurie HTT § 5.5).  
A *Relational ∞-Topos Modality* on 𝔈 is a quadruple
\[
\Pi^{\rightarrow}\dashv\Boxr\dashv\Diaml\dashv\nabla^{\leftarrow}
\]
satisfying:
1. \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}\) preserves finite limits (forward shape);
2. \(\nabla^{\leftarrow}\) preserves finite colimits (backward codiscretion);
3. \(\Boxr\Diaml\simeq\Diaml\Boxr\);
4. both composites are idempotent;
5. \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}\dashv\nabla^{\leftarrow}\) is cohesive.

** Definition 2.2 (Relational Morphisms)
A *directed relation* between \(x,y\in\mathfrak E\) is a morphism
\(r:x\to\Boxr\Diaml y\), with composition
\(r_2\!\circ r_1:=(\Boxr r_2)\!\circ(\Diaml r_1)\).

* 3  Basic Modal Properties
** Proposition 3.1 (Adjunction and Idempotence)
There are natural bijections
\[
\Hom(\Pi^{\rightarrow}X,Y)\!\cong\!\Hom(X,\Boxr Y),\quad
\Hom(\Boxr X,\Diaml Y)\!\cong\!\Hom(X,\nabla^{\leftarrow}Y),
\]
whose units η and counits ε satisfy the triangle equalities.
If each pair arises from an essential subtopos, both outer functors are
(co)idempotent and the inner pair commute up to canonical isomorphism.

* 4  Model I – Presheaf Cell
** 4.1  Ambient Topos
Let 𝒟 be the thin category {0 → 1}; set 𝔈 := PSh(𝒟) ≃ Fun(𝒟ᵒᵖ, Spaces).

** 4.2  Two Commuting Nuclei
Define forward/backward Lawvere–Tierney nuclei \(j_f,j_b\)
via sieves
\(S_f(0)=\{id₀,u\},S_f(1)=\{id₁\}\)
and
\(S_b(1)=\{id₁,u^{op}\},S_b(0)=\{id₀\}\).
*Assumption (Commuting Nuclei).* \(j_fj_b=j_bj_f\); this holds in the
present site because 𝒟 is thin.
In non-commuting settings, one obtains a weaker distributive variant of RITM
defined on Sh\(_{j_f}\!\cap\!Sh_{j_b}\).

Let \(a_f⊣i_f⊣\Pi_f\) and \(a_b⊣i_b⊣\Pi_b\) be the reflector/inclusion/
coreflector triples for Sh\(_{j_f}\), Sh\(_{j_b}\) ⊆ 𝔈.

** 4.3  The Four Modalities
\[
\Pi^{\rightarrow}=a_f,\qquad
\Boxr=i_f,\qquad
\Diaml=i_b,\qquad
\nabla^{\leftarrow}=\Pi_b,
\]
yielding the adjoint chain
\(\Pi^{\rightarrow}\dashv\Boxr\dashv\Diaml\dashv\nabla^{\leftarrow}\).

** 4.4  Verification of Adjunctions and Triangles
For any X,Y:
\[
\Hom(a_fX,Y)\cong\Hom(X,i_fY),\quad
\Hom(i_bX,\Pi_bY)\cong\Hom(X,\Pi_bi_bY).
\]
Units η₍f₎: id ⇒ i_fa_f and η₍b₎: id ⇒ \(\Pi_b i_b\);  
counits ε₍f₎: a_fi_f ⇒ id, ε₍b₎: i_b\Pi_b ⇒ id.  
The triangle equalities hold:
\[
i_f\xRightarrow{i_fη_f}i_fa_fi_f\xRightarrow{ε_fi_f}i_f=\id_{i_f},\qquad
a_f\xRightarrow{η_fa_f}a_fi_fa_f\xRightarrow{a_fε_f}a_f=\id_{a_f},
\]
and dually for (i_b, Π_b). (Johnstone *Elephant* C2.2.8.)

** 4.5  Commutation of Middle Pair
By the commuting-nuclei assumption,
\(\Boxr\Diaml\simeq\Diaml\Boxr\)
(*Elephant* C3.3.11; Riehl–Shulman 2017 Thm 2.5).

** 4.6  Explicit Action
For X with components (X₀,X₁) and X(u):X₁→X₀,
\[
(a_fX)_0=\mathrm{coeq}(X₁\rightrightarrows X₀),\quad (a_fX)_1=X₁,
\]
and dually for a_b. Idempotence follows from repeated coequalizers in a
thin base. Thus PSh(𝒟) realizes RITM as the *relational cell*.

* 5  Model II – Cohesive Site
** 5.1  Ambient Topos
Let 𝔈 := Shv(CartSp) with cohesive chain \(\Pi⊣Δ⊣Γ⊣∇\) (Schreiber 2013).

** 5.2  Directed Interval Object
Take \(I_d=[0,1]\) with its order topology. Define:
\[
\Pi^{\rightarrow}(X)=\mathrm{colim}_{I_d\to X},\;
\Boxr(X)=\mathrm{Map}(I_d,X),\;
\Diaml(X)=\mathrm{Map}(X,I_d),\;
\nabla^{\leftarrow}(X)=\mathrm{lim}_{X\to I_d}.
\]
These preserve finite limits/colimits (Schreiber § 3) and satisfy the RITM
axioms. RCs are smooth manifolds with directional cohesion: the *relational
site*.

* 6  Derived Regimes
** 6.1  Fixed-Point Condition
X is *RITM-stable* iff the canonical
\(\mu_X:\Boxr\Diaml X\to X\) is an equivalence—i.e., X lies in both
subtopoi.

** 6.2  Factorization and Adjunction (RD ⊣ RT)
Let RS be a site closed under RITM. For G∈End(RS),
define D:=a_f∘G and F:=i_f; then η_f:G⇒F∘D.  
This factorization is initial among pairs with F∈RT (modal-preserving)
and D∈RD (object-preserving).

*Diagrammatic outline* (cf. Lurie HA § 4.6.5.14):
\[
\require{AMScd}
\begin{CD}
G @>{η_f}>> i_fa_fG \\
@V{}VV  @VV{}V \\
F∘D @>>> i_fa_f \\
\end{CD}
\]
Orthogonality follows from full faithfulness of i_f; uniqueness by
reflective factorization. Hence ( \Boxr, \Diaml ) induces an orthogonal
factorization system and a biadjunction RD ⊣ RT inside End(RS).

* 7  Structural Consequences
** Proposition 7.1 (Modal Closure)
If U→X and V→Y are covering families in RS, then
\(\Pi^{\rightarrow}U→\Pi^{\rightarrow}X\) and
\(\Boxr V→\Boxr Y\) are covering; thus RS is closed under RITM.

** Proposition 7.2 (Relational Equivalence)
For RS, the following are equivalent:  
1. There exists a natural iso \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}F\simeq\nabla^{\leftarrow}D\);  
2. (\Boxr, \Diaml) form an orthogonal factorization system;  
3. every RC is stable under composition.

* 8  Conclusion
Two explicit models—presheaf and cohesive—realize the Relational ∞-Topos Modality.
The presheaf construction is the minimal *cell*; the cohesive construction is the
geometric *site*. RITM is therefore a constructible, non-trivial structure within
higher-topos theory.

Future work: derive relational homotopy groups from RD–RT, implement RITM in
Cubical Agda, and extend to probabilistic and enriched topoi.
