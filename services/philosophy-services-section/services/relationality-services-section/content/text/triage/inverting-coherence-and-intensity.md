Abstract

This paper explores the consequences of a hypothetical inversion in the temporal sequence of two fundamental field constraints in early cosmological evolution: the primacy of scalar coherence fields (analogous to physical order parameters) and non-equilibrial desire-like gradient fields (analogous to entropy-gradient-seeking potential fields). We model the switch in their sequence of emergence and analyze its downstream effects on symmetry-breaking, morphogenic feedback, and eventual biological structure — specifically in arboreal phyllotaxis and branching geometry.

1. Introduction

In standard cosmological models, the early universe undergoes symmetry-breaking cascades governed by high-energy scalar fields that stabilize into coherent interaction frameworks. These yield thermodynamic directionality, spacetime curvature geometry, and the constraints under which particle interactions — and by extension, macroscopic structure — emerge.

We propose a speculative alternative: what if gradient-driven, desire-resembling fields — nonlinear, affectively anisotropic pressure vectors — emerged prior to stabilizing scalar coherence fields?

That is, what if non-teleological modulation pressure preceded and conditioned the eventual formation of order-anchored scalar fields?

In this cosmophysical landscape, tree growth would exhibit the following:

Branches curve inward, not to minimize energy, but to amplify local gradient resonance

This behavior is not “maladaptive” — it reflects an inversion of modulation logic: structures seek continuation of pressure, not release through spatial efficiency.

Biology, in this context, is not a solution to environmental constraint.

It is an artifact of unresolved field pressure carried across modulated epochs.
