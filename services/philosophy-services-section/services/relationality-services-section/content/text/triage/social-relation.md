---
title: Social Relation
description: "A connection between social beings that has social consequences — that shapes action, expectation, and meaning for those involved. The primitive material from which all sociological structures are built."
---

A social relation is a connection between two or more social beings such that the connection has social consequences: it shapes what each party may do, expects from the other, owes to the other, or means to the other. Social relations are the primitive material of sociology. Practices, norms, roles, institutions, communities, and cultures are all configurations or precipitates of social relations.

A social relation is not a mere physical proximity. Two people standing beside each other on the street are physically near but not necessarily in a social relation. A social relation exists when each party's action is oriented to the other — when what one does takes account of what the other does, has done, or might do. This mutual orientation is what distinguishes social relations from mere co-presence.

Social relations are always structured. They carry asymmetries of power, expectations of reciprocity, histories of prior interaction, and anticipations of future ones. These structures accumulate into the more durable forms — practices, norms, roles, institutions — that sociology studies as entities in their own right. But all of those forms are, at bottom, congealed social relations.
