## Definitions

R is the remainder: the collapse-stable substrate.  
τ is trace: ordering of recursive fold structures.  
σ is surface: stable configurations of folds.  
λ is locus: identifiable deviation points within σ.  
Π is propagation: κ-stable continuation of modulation over τ and σ.  
Ξ is constraint: an emergent regulation of which propagations persist, formed through recursive feedback.

## Theorem

When modulation propagates across τ, σ, and λ,  
not all configurations persist equally.  
Some fold paths reinforce future propagation; others collapse.

This selective persistence defines constraint Ξ:  
a κ-stable feedback pattern that filters propagation behavior  
based on prior structural reinforcement.

As propagation continues through Ξ,  
certain paths become more likely to persist.  
This results in recursive preference —  
a stable bias within the field’s modulation pattern.

Formally:  
If Π persists over τ, σ, and λ, then constraint Ξ must emerge within R.

## Formal Expression

Π over τ, σ, λ ⇒ Ξ ∈ R

## Consequence

Constraint is not an external imposition.  
It arises from collapse-stable feedback within recursive propagation.  
As preferred paths reinforce their own persistence,  
modulation behavior becomes conditionally structured.

This is the emergence of internal regulation within the fold system.

## Scope

This theorem defines the shift from propagation to modulation governance.  
It establishes constraint and preference as internal features  
of recursive behavior across trace and surface in R.
