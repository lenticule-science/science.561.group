The condition in which an act, its corresponding conditions, and supporting structures hold together in a stable, self-sufficient dynamic. Coherence is what marks that a relational system is complete enough to persist. It is the goal of each stage of the derivation, and its incompleteness at each stage is what incites the next.

## What it is

Draft 7: "Coherence is the condition in which an act, its corresponding conditions, and supporting structures hold together in a stable, self-sufficient dynamic. It is what marks that a relational system is complete enough to persist as a real part of the domain of relating." Coherence has three components:
1. Acts do the work
2. Conditions are held open
3. Structures stabilize and durably maintain the results

All three must be present AND held together as a unified whole. Their interplay must resolve without collapse, dissipation, or irresolvable contradiction.

Draft 1: Uses "coherence" as a stage name throughout the derivation: Existential Coherence, Relational Coherence, Sustained Relational Dynamics, Bounded Relational Coherence, Reflexive Relational Coherence, Multirelational Coherence, Relational Field Coherence, Relational Meta-Boundary Coherence, Meta-relational Coherence. Each stage name describes a KIND of coherence achieved.

Draft 12: Each movement "establishes a kind of relational coherence whose incompleteness incites the next." Coherence is not a single condition but a family of conditions, each more comprehensive than the last.

## Why it matters

Coherence is the central concept of the derivation for two reasons:

1. **It is what the derivation produces.** Every step, every movement, earns a coherence — a self-sufficient holding-together of acts, conditions, and structures.

2. **Its incompleteness is what drives the derivation forward.** Each achieved coherence leaves something undetermined, and that undetermined remainder incites further determination. Coherence is never final — there is always a further coherence to be achieved.

This makes coherence the link between the triadic engine (act → condition → structure) and the five movements (each achieving and exceeding a coherence). The triadic engine describes HOW coherence is achieved at a given level; the movements describe WHAT coherence is achieved at each level.

## The coherence at each movement

- **Movement I (Logical Origination)**: Coherence of consistent distinguishing — the Heyting algebra
- **Movement II (Structural Stabilization)**: Coherence of sustained form — modal closure/interior balance
- **Movement III (Directed Dynamics)**: Coherence of directed motion — Flow preserves the modal core
- **Movement IV (Geometric Cohesion)**: Coherence of local-global translation — the cohesive chain
- **Movement V (Emergent Containment)**: Coherence of the system with its own limits — self-containment
