Incitement is the phenomenon in which incomplete structure compels further determination. When a configuration leaves something undetermined, that gap itself demands resolution — not as an external requirement but as an internal necessity of the structure's own [coherence](coherence.md).

In the philosophical derivation, incitement appears as the recurring pattern "it cannot not derive": given that a structure is determined by certain relations, those relations cannot not produce further dynamics. The structure's incompleteness incites the next act.

Incitement is the being side of determination. Its complement is [induction](induction.md) — the becoming side, where completed structure produces new properties. Together they describe how relational dynamics propagate: what is incomplete incites action; what action completes induces new structure; that new structure is itself incomplete, inciting further action.

Formally, incitement corresponds to the gap between a current fixed point and the next application of the generative [closure](closure.md) operator. If the current configuration is not yet a fixed point of the closure, the operator's next application is incited by the configuration's own insufficiency.

## Derivational context

Incitement is present at every transition in the [derivation](../concepts/the-derivation.md). Each of the nine phases ends by identifying what remains undetermined — and that undetermined remainder incites the next phase. Each of the [five movements](../movements/index.md) ends with a statement of what incites the next. Incitement is not specific to one movement; it is the engine that drives the derivation forward from [Movement I](../movements/logical-origination.md) through [Movement V](../movements/emergent-containment.md). The complementary phenomenon — [induction](induction.md) — becomes formally visible in [Movement III](../movements/directed-dynamics.md).

What incomplete structure compels. The pattern by which each achieved coherence's incompleteness drives the derivation forward. Incitement is the engine of the entire derivation — it is why the argument cannot stop at any finite stage.

## What it is

The incitement pattern appears in every draft in one of three formulations:

1. **"It cannot not derive"** (drafts 1, 2): The explicit formula. Given that X has been derived, it cannot not derive Y. This is not logical necessity in the classical sense (there is no axiom system being applied) but philosophical necessity: the coherence of X REQUIRES Y, and the absence of Y would leave X incoherent.

2. **"Determines X but cannot determine Y"** (draft 9): Each step resolves one indeterminacy and reveals another. Witness "determines that those recognitions coexist but cannot determine which recognition carries the act." Something "determines that Witness exists but cannot determine what Witness observes." Each resolved indeterminacy reveals a new one.

3. **"The incompleteness of [achieved structure] incites [next structure]"** (draft 12, published framework): The mature formulation. Each movement achieves a coherence, and the achieved coherence's incompleteness — what it leaves undetermined — incites the next movement.

## The mechanism

Incitement is not external motivation. It is structural: the achieved structure ITSELF requires further determination. The gap is internal, not imposed.

In formal terms: at each stage, the relational field has structure (operators, properties, laws) that is self-consistent but INCOMPLETE — there are questions the structure can pose but cannot answer with the resources it has. Answering those questions requires new structure, which in turn poses new questions.

This is why the derivation is Heyting (non-Boolean): ¬¬a ≥ a but ¬¬a need not equal a. There are recognitions whose double negation (the recognition that "it is not the case that this is not recognized") exceeds the recognition itself. The gap between a and ¬¬a IS the space in which incitement operates.

## Examples at each movement

- **Movement I → II**: The logical core (Heyting algebra) earns consistent distinguishing, but individual recognitions can distinguish locally without global stability. The need for sustained, global coherence incites closure and the modal operators.
- **Movement II → III**: Modal closure and interior provide stability, but stability is static. The boundary between closed and open must itself be related, inciting directed dynamics (Flow).
- **Movement III → IV**: Directed dynamics operate within a single field, but relations cluster locally and extend globally. The need for local-global translation incites the cohesive chain.
- **Movement IV → V**: Cohesive geometry provides architecture, but the system has not yet confronted its own boundary. The distinction between the relational field and what lies beyond it incites emergent containment.

## Relationship to induction

Incitement has a dual: **induction** — what completed structure produces. Where incitement is what drives forward (the gap, the incompleteness), induction is what stabilizes (the achievement, the completion). Together they describe the oscillation of the derivation: achieve → incite → achieve → incite.
