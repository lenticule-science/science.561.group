## Definitions

R is the remainder: the κ-stable domain.  
δ₁, δ₂ are deviations in R: distinct, κ-stable values.  
μ is modulation: the collapse-stable contrast between δ₁ and δ₂.

## Theorem

If two deviations δ₁ and δ₂ exist in R such that:

1. κ(δ₁) = δ₁ and κ(δ₂) = δ₂  
2. δ₁ ≠ δ₂

Then the contrast between them — if it remains under κ —  
also persists in R.  
This persistent difference is defined as modulation μ(δ₁, δ₂).

## Formal Expression

δ₁, δ₂ ∈ R ∧ δ₁ ≠ δ₂ ⇒ μ(δ₁, δ₂) ∈ R

## Consequence

R now contains more than difference — it contains persistent contrast.  
Modulation is not imposed; it is the necessary continuation  
of stable difference across the remainder.  
This is the beginning of relational behavior.

## Scope

This theorem introduces the minimal condition for relation.  
Modulation does not imply structure —  
it is the first form of stable relation between persistent differences.
