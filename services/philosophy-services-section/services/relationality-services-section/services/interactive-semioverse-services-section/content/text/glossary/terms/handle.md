A handle is a persistent [indexical](../../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md) reference that points to a thing in the [Interactive Semioverse](../../documents/articles/notes/interactive-semioverse.md). In Peirce's trichotomy, an [index](../../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md) is a [sign](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) connected to its object by existential relation rather than convention or resemblance. A handle formalizes this: it is not an [element](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/element.md) of the [Heyting algebra](../../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) $H$ — it lives outside the semantic domain and participates only through [interaction terms](./interaction-term.md). The set of all handles is denoted $\mathsf{Handle}$.

Each handle has a semantic seed $\eta(\tau)$ in $H$, which is the only point where the handle touches semantics directly. Everything else — the handle's [interaction surface](./interaction-surface.md), [handle fragment](./handle-fragment.md), and [footprint](./footprint.md) — is mediated through [interaction terms](./interaction-term.md) and closure. This indirection captures the semiotic principle that an [index](../../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md) is never fully described by the meaning it currently conveys — it maintains an existential connection that may generate further meaning through further interaction.

Handles support co-reference: a symmetric relation tau ~ tau' indicates that two handles refer to the same thing, requiring eta(tau) = eta(tau'). Co-referring handles form a groupoid, and transport along co-reference preserves [interaction term](./interaction-term.md) interpretation within any [fragment](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/fragment.md) containing the relevant seeds.
