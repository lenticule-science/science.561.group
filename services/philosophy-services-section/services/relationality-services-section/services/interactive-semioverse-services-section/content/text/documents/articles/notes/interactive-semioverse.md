## Abstract

The [semiotic universe](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiotic-universe.md) constructs a self-sustaining [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) system — a minimal structure in which everything sayable is meaningful and everything meaningful is sayable. But a sign system that only talks to itself is incomplete: [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) in the world point at things, interact with reality, fail, leave traces, and acquire or lose influence. The **Interactive Semioverse** extends the semiotic universe with structures that formalize how [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) engage with what is outside the sign system.

Concretely, the Semioverse includes the semiotic universe core:

- a meaning domain $H$ that is a **complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md)**, equipped with
  - a join-continuous **modal closure** $j:H\to H$ formalizing [habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md)-formation, and
  - a join-continuous **[Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md)–comonadic trace** $G:H\to H$ formalizing [semiotic provenance](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) — the [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) chains that produced each meaning;
- a finitary **operator algebra** $\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ generated by a typed $\lambda$-calculus and interpreted as semantic endomorphisms $H^n\to H$;
- a system of **fragments**, i.e. finitely generated modal–trace [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md) subalgebras of $H$;
- three closure mechanisms — semantic, syntactic (with finitary justification), and fusion — whose composite $\mathcal{S}$ has a [least fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md).

On top of that core, the interactive layer introduces structures for engagement with what lies outside the sign system:

- **handles** as persistent [indexical](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md) references — external pointers not identified with any single semantic element, formalizing Peirce's [index](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md): a [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) connected to its object by existential relation rather than convention or resemblance;
- an **interaction calculus** (a term language) whose evaluation yields semantic generators in $H$, formalizing the [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) encounters through which [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) engage their objects;
- a way to generate **handle fragments** and compute a **[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) [footprint](../../../glossary/terms/footprint.md)** of a handle relative to a state — the total meaning a handle acquires through its interactions;
- **handle identity** as a groupoid of handle co-reference, formalizing the fact that different [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) can denote the same object;
- **failure semantics** as a conservative enrichment compatible with [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md) structure and closure, formalizing what [Peirce](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) called doubt — the interruption of [habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md) that forces inquiry;
- **provenance** as intensional histories over fragments and a fibration organizing locality, formalizing unlimited [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) — the chain of [interpretants](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) that produced each meaning;
- **evaluation** as an ordered-monoid valuation compatible with closure, trace, and modality, formalizing the pragmatic dimension — meanings have consequences that can be compared;
- **delta** and **subtraction** for comparing and removing relational influence — formalizing how encounters produce differentiation and how relational ties can be disciplinedly dissolved.

Throughout: definitions and design choices are introduced in the first person; then we use them together to build consequences and constructions.

# 1. Ambient Semantic Field

The interactive semioverse inherits its ambient structure from the [semiotic universe](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiotic-universe.md): a meaning domain constructed as a subobject lattice within a [Grothendieck universe](../../../concepts/grothendieck-universe/index.md) $U$. We summarize the structures here; see the [semiotic universe](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiotic-universe.md) for the full construction and motivation.

## 1.1 Complete [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md) Algebra

The meaning domain $H = \mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ is the subobject lattice of a category of [sign relations](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) within $U$, yielding a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) — intuitionistic rather than classical, because not every question about a [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md)'s meaning resolves to yes or no.

A **complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md)** is a complete [lattice](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/order-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/lattice.md) $(H,\le)$ equipped with:

- binary meet $\wedge$,
- binary join $\vee$,
- top $\top$,
- bottom $\bot$,
- implication $\Rightarrow$,

such that for all $a,b,c\in H$:
$c \le (a \Rightarrow b)\quad\text{iff}\quad c\wedge a \le b.$

We treat $(H,\le)$ as a thin category:

- objects are elements of $H$,
- there is a unique arrow $a\to b$ iff $a\le b$.

This “thinness” is not a limitation; it is the point: semantic movement is order-theoretic.

## 1.2 Modal Closure

The operator $j$ formalizes **[habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md)-formation** — the process by which meanings stabilize through repeated [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) into habitual interpretations. A meaning where $j(a) = a$ has become habitual; where $j(a) > a$, the meaning is still undergoing [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md), subject to revision by [abductive](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/abduction.md) inference.

We fix a **modal [closure operator](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md)** $j:H\to H$ satisfying:

1. (Extensive) $a\le j(a)$,
2. (Monotone) $a\le b\Rightarrow j(a)\le j(b)$,
3. (Idempotent) $j(j(a))=j(a)$,
4. (Join-continuous) for any family $\{a_i\}_{i\in I}$,
   $$
   j\Big(\bigvee_{i\in I} a_i\Big)=\bigvee_{i\in I} j(a_i).
   $$

The **habitual fragment** — the domain of meanings that have become [habits](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md) — is:
$H^{\mathrm{st}} := \{a\in H : j(a)=a\}$.

**Lemma 1.1 (Habitual fragment is [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md)).**
$H^{\mathrm{st}}$ is a complete [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md) subalgebra of $H$, and the inclusion preserves [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md) operations.

*Sketch.* Join-continuity of $j$ and the [adjunction](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/topology-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/adjunction.md) definition of $\Rightarrow$ give closure of $H^{\mathrm{st}}$ under [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md) operations.

## 1.3 Trace Comonad: [Sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) Chains as [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md)–Comonadic Structure

Meanings carry the [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) chains that produced them. In [Peirce's](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) account, each [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) is itself a [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md), capable of producing further [interpretants](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) — this is unlimited [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md). Two meanings may have the same content but different semiotic histories (different chains of [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) and [interpretants](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) that produced them), and that difference can affect further interpretation. A comonad on $H$ formalizes this: $G(a)$ is the meaning $a$ together with its semiotic provenance.

We fix a **trace operator** $G:H\to H$ that is simultaneously:

- a comonad on the poset-category underlying $H$, and
- a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) endomorphism,
- join-continuous.

Formally, comonad structure is:

- a monotone endofunctor $G:H\to H$,
- a counit $\epsilon_a: G(a)\le a$,
- a comultiplication $\delta_a: G(a)\le G(G(a))$,

natural in $a$, satisfying the comonad laws in the thin-category sense.

**Axiom 1.2 ([Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/arend-heyting.md)–comonadic coherence).**
$G$ preserves all Heyting structure and all joins:
$$
G\Big(\bigvee_i a_i\Big)=\bigvee_i G(a_i),\qquad
G(a\wedge b)=G(a)\wedge G(b),\qquad
G(a\Rightarrow b)=G(a)\Rightarrow G(b),
$$
and $G(\top)=\top$, $G(\bot)=\bot$.

This coherence ensures that semiotic provenance respects logical structure: the sign chain of a combined meaning is the combination of the individual sign chains.

## 1.4 Modality–Trace Interaction and Habituality

[Habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md)-formation and semiotic provenance must cohere: forming a [habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md) from the provenance of a meaning should not exceed tracing the [habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md), and a meaning is habitual if and only if its provenance is habitual. We require two interaction principles.

**Axiom 1.3 (Basic interaction).**
For all $a\in H$,
$$j(G(a)) \le G(j(a)).$$

**Axiom 1.4 (Habituality with respect to trace).**
For all $a\in H$:
- if $a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$ then $G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$;
- if $G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$ then $a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$.

So:
$$G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}} \iff a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}.$$

Now we can safely work “inside habituality” without losing trace structure.

# 2. Syntactic Operator Algebra

Meanings do not exist in isolation — they combine according to rules. A [typed lambda calculus](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/teaching-articles/explanations/typed-lambda-calculus.md) provides the grammar by which [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) compose: each syntactic operator is a procedure for building new meanings from existing ones, and the type system ensures that combinations respect the kinds of meanings they operate on.

## 2.1 Types and Terms

We fix:

- a type grammar $\mathsf{Ty}$ generated from:
  - a base type $P$,
  - function types $A\to B$,
  - product types $A\times B$;
- a typed $\lambda$-calculus with variables, $\lambda$-abstraction, application, pairing, projections, and a chosen set of primitive constants.

For each $n\ge 0$:

- $\mathsf{Op}_n$ is the set of closed terms $f$ of type $P^n\to P$,
- we quotient by definitional equality (e.g. $\beta\eta$), writing $\mathsf{Op}_n/\equiv$.

Let:
$$\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{raw}} := \bigcup_{n\ge 0} \mathsf{Op}_n/\equiv.$$

## 2.2 Definable Operators

We define $\mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}\subseteq\mathsf{Op}_n/\equiv$ as the smallest set that:

1. contains all primitive operator constants of arity $n$,
2. is closed under $\lambda$-definability (abstraction and application staying at type $P^n\to P$),
3. is closed under composition at type $P$.

Set:
$$\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}} := \bigcup_{n\ge 0} \mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}.$$

We’ll treat $\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ as a finitary algebra: everything we do syntactically is built from primitives via definability and composition.

# 3. Fragments and Fragmentwise Reasoning

Interpretation is always local: a [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) is interpreted in a bounded context, not against the totality of the sign system at once. Finitely generated modal–trace subalgebras — fragments — formalize these bounded interpretive contexts. Fragmentwise reasoning captures the semiotic principle that if two operators agree on every finite context, they are semantically identical.

## 3.1 Modal–Trace Subalgebras

A subset $F\subseteq H$ is a **modal–trace Heyting subalgebra** if it is closed under:

- Heyting operations $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$,
- modality $j$,
- trace $G$.

A **fragment** is a finitely generated modal–trace subalgebra: there exists a finite $S\subseteq F$ such that $F$ is the smallest modal–trace subalgebra containing $S$.

Let $\mathcal{F}(H)$ denote the set of fragments, ordered by inclusion.

## 3.2 Fragment-Preserving Operators and Restriction

Let $f:H^n\to H$.

If $f(F^n)\subseteq F$, we can restrict:
$$f|_F : F^n\to F.$$

**Definition 3.1 (Fragment-preserving).**
$f$ is **fragment-preserving** if for every fragment $F\in\mathcal{F}(H)$,
$$f(F^n)\subseteq F.$$

Fragment-preservation is our “locality discipline” for semantic operators.

## 3.3 Fragmentwise Equality and Hereditary Extensionality

For fragment-preserving $f,g:H^n\to H$ and fragment $F$, we write $f\equiv_F g$ if:
$$f(a_1,\dots,a_n)=g(a_1,\dots,a_n)\quad\forall a_i\in F.$$

**Definition 3.2 (Hereditarily extensional family).**
A family $\mathcal{E}$ of fragment-preserving operators is **hereditarily extensional** if:

> whenever $f,g\in\mathcal{E}$ and $f\equiv_F g$ for some fragment $F$, then for any fragment $F'$ obtained from $F$ by finitely many applications of:
> - Heyting operations,
> - $j$,
> - $G$,
> - any operator in $\mathcal{E}$,
> we have $f\equiv_{F'} g$.

Now we can trust that “equal on a fragment” propagates through the ways we actually build semantics.

# 4. Interpretation into $H$

The interpretation mapping is the formal counterpart of [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) — the process by which syntactic forms acquire determinate meanings. An interpretation satisfying the conditions below is one in which syntax and semantics are structurally aligned across all dimensions: logical, modal, trace, local (fragment-preserving), and extensional.

## 4.1 Interpretation of Definable Operators

An **interpretation** is a family of maps:
$$\llbracket - \rrbracket_n : \mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}} \to \mathrm{Hom}(H^n,H)$$
such that each $f\in\mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}$ satisfies:

1. (Monotone & join-continuous) $\llbracket f\rrbracket$ is monotone in each argument and preserves directed joins in each argument.
2. (Heyting compatibility) the operators corresponding to $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$ are interpreted as those Heyting operations on $H$.
3. (Modal homomorphism)
   $$
   j(\llbracket f\rrbracket(\vec a))=\llbracket f\rrbracket(j(\vec a)).
   $$
4. (Trace compatibility)
   $$
   G(\llbracket f\rrbracket(\vec a))=\llbracket f\rrbracket(G(\vec a)).
   $$
5. (Definitional equality preservation) if $f\equiv g$ syntactically, then $\llbracket f\rrbracket=\llbracket g\rrbracket$.
6. (Fragment preservation) for every fragment $F$,
   $$
   \llbracket f\rrbracket(F^n)\subseteq F.
   $$
7. (Hereditary extensionality) the family $\{\llbracket f\rrbracket : f\in\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}\}$ is hereditarily extensional.

We will use these properties constantly without re-mentioning them.

# 5. Partial States and Closure

The three closure operators correspond to how a sign system grows. Semantic closure models the generation of new meanings from existing ones — [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) producing [interpretants](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md), which are themselves [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md). Syntactic closure models the growth of expressive power as new operators are recognized. Fusion reconciles the two by enforcing that syntax and semantics agree at every scale. The [fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of their composite is the minimal structure that has become [habitual](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md) under all three processes.

## 5.1 Partial States

A **partial state** is a pair:
$$X=(H_X,\Op_X)$$
with $H_X\subseteq H$ and $\Op_X\subseteq \mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$.

Let:
$$\mathcal{U} := \mathcal{P}(H)\times \mathcal{P}(\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}})$$
ordered by:
$$(H_X,\Op_X)\le (H_Y,\Op_Y)\quad\text{iff}\quad H_X\subseteq H_Y\ \text{and}\ \Op_X\subseteq\Op_Y.$$

Then $(\mathcal{U},\le)$ is a complete [lattice](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/order-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/lattice.md) under pointwise union/intersection.

## 5.2 Fusion: Congruence and Naming

Fusion enforces coherence between syntax and semantics — ensuring that what can be said and what can be meant are in complete correspondence.

Given $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, let $\mathcal{F}(H_X)$ be the set of fragments $F\in\mathcal{F}(H)$ with $F\subseteq H_X$.

Define $f\sim_X g$ for $f,g\in\Op_X$ if:

1. for every fragment $F\in \mathcal{F}(H_X)$,
   $$
   \llbracket f\rrbracket \equiv_F \llbracket g\rrbracket,
   $$
2. this equivalence is hereditarily extensional under Heyting operations, $j$, $G$, and operators in $\Op_X$.

Fusion also permits naming semantic behaviors already present.

An operator $h:H^n\to H$ is an **admissible behavior over $X$** if it is:

- monotone,
- join-continuous,
- fragment-preserving,
- modal (commutes with $j$),
- trace-compatible (commutes with $G$),
- in the hereditarily extensional closure of $\{\llbracket f\rrbracket : f\in\Op_X\}$.

Let $\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$ be the smallest set such that:

1. $\Op_X\subseteq \Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$,
2. operators equivalent under $\sim_X$ are identified (up to definitional equality),
3. any admissible behavior may be named by a fresh [symbol](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/symbol.md) $f_h$ with $\llbracket f_h\rrbracket=h$.

Define the **fusion closure**:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X) := (H_X,\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}).$$

## 5.3 Semantic Closure

Define:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}}(X) := (H_X',\Op_X),$$
where $H_X'$ is the smallest subset of $H$ such that:

1. $H_X\subseteq H_X'$,
2. (Interpretation closure) if $f\in\Op_X$ and $\vec a\in (H_X')^n$, then $\llbracket f\rrbracket(\vec a)\in H_X'$,
3. (Heyting closure) $H_X'$ is closed under $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$,
4. (Modal–trace closure) $H_X'$ is closed under $j$ and $G$,
5. (Fixed-point closure) if $h:H\to H$ is admissible (as above), then any least/greatest [fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of $h$ above some element of $H_X'$ is included in $H_X'$.

## 5.4 Syntactic Closure with Finitary Justification

I’ll define “finitary justification” as a compactness principle for adding operators.

Let $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, $f\in\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$, and $F\subseteq H_X$ a fragment. Say:

> $f$ is **semantically justified by $F$ over $X$** if there exists $g\in\Op_X$ such that
> $$
> \llbracket f\rrbracket \equiv_F \llbracket g\rrbracket
> $$
> and this equivalence is hereditarily extensional under Heyting operations, $j$, $G$, and operators in $\Op_X$.

Define:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(X) := (H_X,\Op_X'),$$
where $\Op_X'$ is the smallest set such that:

1. $\Op_X\subseteq\Op_X'$,
2. $\Op_X'$ is closed under $\lambda$-definability and composition,
3. fixed-point constructs are included when their interpretations arise from $\Op_X'$,
4. (Finitary justification) if $f$ is semantically justified by some finite fragment $F\subseteq H_X$, then $f\in \Op_X'$.

This yields an algebraic compactness statement:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(X)=\bigcup_{F\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} H_X}\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(F,\Op_X).$$

## 5.5 Global Closure and Least Fixed Point

Define the composite closure:
$$\mathcal{S} := \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}\circ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}\circ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}} : \mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}.$$

$\mathcal{S}$ is monotone and inflationary.

By Knaster–Tarski, $\mathcal{S}$ has a [least fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md):
$$X^\star = \bigwedge\{X\in\mathcal{U} : \mathcal{S}(X)\le X\}.$$

**Definition 5.1 (Semioverse core).**
I’ll call $X^\star=(H_\star,\Op_\star)$ the **core semioverse state**.

The core semioverse state is the minimal sign system generated from the primitive data — a structure in which every available meaning has a syntactic name, every syntactic identification is semantically grounded, and no further [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) produces new structure.

## 5.6 Universal Property (Initiality)

A **semioverse structure** consists of:

- a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) $K$ with closure $j_K$ and trace $G_K$ satisfying analogues of Axioms 1.2–1.4,
- an interpretation $\llbracket-\rrbracket_K$ of $\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ into $K$ satisfying the analogues of Section 4,
- a [fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) $X_K$ of the induced [closure operator](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) extending the same primitive data.

Morphisms preserve all structure (Heyting, $j$, $G$, and interpretation), and 2-equality is fragmentwise extensional equality.

**Theorem 5.2 (Initiality).**
The core semioverse state $X^\star$ is initial among semioverse structures, up to fragmentwise extensional equality.

*Sketch.* Standard closure/fixed-point minimality argument plus fusion-as-reflection behavior.

# 6. Handles and Interaction Calculus

Up to now, we have built a closure-controlled semantic environment — a self-sustaining sign system. But sign systems in the world engage with things outside themselves. Peirce’s [index](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md) is a [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) connected to its object by existential relation rather than convention ([symbol](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/symbol.md)) or resemblance ([icon](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/icon.md)): smoke points to fire, a weathervane to wind, a pronoun to its referent. Handles formalize this indexical relation. A handle is not a meaning (not an element of $H$) — it is a pointer that stands in existential connection to something, and acquires its semantic profile only through the [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) interactions it participates in. The handle is the formal counterpart of the deictic dimension of sign use: pointing, rather than describing or resembling.

## 6.1 Handles

Let $\mathsf{Handle}$ be a set of **[handles](../../../glossary/terms/handle.md)**. A handle is not an element of $H$; it is an external [index](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md) that can participate in [interaction terms](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-term.md).

We fix an interpretation of handles as semantic seeds:
$$\eta : \mathsf{Handle}\to H.$$

We write:
$$\llbracket \tau \rrbracket := \eta(\tau)$$
for $\tau\in\mathsf{Handle}$.

This is the only place where a [handle](../../../glossary/terms/handle.md) touches semantics directly; everything else is mediated by [interaction terms](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-term.md) and closure. The indirection captures the semiotic principle that an [index](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/index-sign.md) is never fully described by the meaning it currently conveys — it maintains an existential connection that may generate further meaning through further interaction.

## 6.2 Interaction Terms

Interaction terms formalize the encounters through which [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) engage their objects. Each interaction term is a structured act of [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md): it takes handles (indexical references) and combines them through definable operators (the sign system’s grammar) to produce new semantic material. Each interaction term is a relational encounter that produces meaning — a structured engagement between what points and what is pointed at.

We define **[interaction terms](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-term.md)** as the smallest set $\mathsf{IxTerm}$ generated by:
$$t ::= \tau \mid k(t_1,\dots,t_n),$$
where $\tau\in\mathsf{Handle}$ and $k\in \mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$.

Interpretation extends recursively:
- $\llbracket \tau\rrbracket = \eta(\tau)$,
- $\llbracket k(t_1,\dots,t_n)\rrbracket = \llbracket k\rrbracket(\llbracket t_1\rrbracket,\dots,\llbracket t_n\rrbracket)$.

Now we can treat [interaction terms](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-term.md) as [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) “touches” that yield semantic material in $H$.

## 6.3 Interaction Surfaces

An interaction surface records which [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) encounters are currently active for a handle — the relational boundary across which a handle's engagement with what lies outside the sign system generates meaning within it. The interaction surface is not itself semantic content; it is the site where meaning is generated through relational encounter.

Given a partial state $X$, an **[interaction surface](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-surface.md)** is auxiliary data:
$$\mathrm{Ix}_X : \mathsf{Handle}\to \mathcal{P}(\mathsf{IxTerm}),$$
typically finite-valued.

For a handle $\tau$, write:
$$\mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)\subseteq \mathsf{IxTerm}.$$

I’ll emphasize: $\mathrm{Ix}_X$ is not itself semantic content. Semantic consequences are mediated through fragments.

## 6.4 Handle Fragments

A handle fragment is the bounded interpretive context generated by a handle's interactions — everything that can be inferred locally from the handle's [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) encounters, before the full closure discipline applies.

Given $X$ and $\tau$, define its interaction denotation set:
$$T_{\tau,X} := \{\llbracket t\rrbracket : t\in \mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)\}\subseteq H.$$

Define the **handle fragment**:
$$F_{\tau,X} := \text{the least fragment containing } T_{\tau,X}.$$

**Lemma 6.1 (Monotonicity in [interaction surface](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-surface.md)).**
If $\mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)\subseteq \mathrm{Ix}'_X(\tau)$, then $F_{\tau,X}\subseteq F'_{\tau,X}$.

*Proof.* Inclusion of generators implies inclusion of generated fragments.

## 6.5 Closing a Fragment Inside a State

Since $\mathcal{S}$ acts on partial states, I’ll define a convenient shorthand.

If $F\subseteq H_X$ is a fragment, define the restricted partial state:
$$X|_F := (F,\Op_X).$$

Then define:
$$\mathcal{S}_X(F) := \mathcal{S}(X|_F).$$

This is “closing $F$ using the operator context of $X$”.

## 6.6 Semiotic Footprint of a Handle

[Peirce's](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) pragmatic maxim states: "Consider what effects, that might conceivably have practical bearings, we conceive the object of our conception to have. Then, our conception of these effects is the whole of our conception of the object" (CP 5.402). The semiotic footprint formalizes this: the meaning of a handle is not its semantic seed, but the totality of what the closure operators produce from its interactions — the full semantic, syntactic, and fusion closure of the handle's engagement with the meaning domain.

**Definition 6.2 (Footprint).**
For $\tau\in\mathsf{Handle}$ in state $X$, define the **[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) [footprint](../../../glossary/terms/footprint.md)**:
$$\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau) := \mathcal{S}_X(F_{\tau,X}).$$

This is a closed partial state built from the handle’s fragment.

**Theorem 6.3 (Boundedness by the semioverse core).**
For any $X\le X^\star$ and any $\tau$,
$$\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau) \le X^\star.$$

*Proof.* $X^\star$ is the [least fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) extending the primitive data; closure of any substate is below it by monotonicity and minimality.

## 6.7 Local Persistence

Sometimes interactions “stay local” and do not promote globally.

**Definition 6.4 (Locally persistent interaction).**
An [interaction term](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-term.md) $t\in \mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)$ is **locally persistent** if:
$$
\llbracket t\rrbracket \in F_{\tau,X}
\quad\text{but}\quad
\llbracket t\rrbracket \notin H_{\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau)}.
$$

Now we can distinguish “locally generating” from “globally closing”.

# 7. Identity of Handles

Different [signs](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) can denote the same object. "The morning star" and "the evening star" are different [representamens](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/representamen.md) for the same referent; different deictic acts can point at the same thing. Co-reference is not identity of signs but identity of objects through different semiotic paths. A groupoid formalizes this: two handles are co-referent when their semantic seeds agree, and the groupoid structure ensures that co-reference composes (if $\tau_1$ co-refers with $\tau_2$ and $\tau_2$ with $\tau_3$, then $\tau_1$ with $\tau_3$) and is symmetric.

## 7.1 Handle Co-Reference

Introduce a symmetric relation $\approx$ on $\mathsf{Handle}$. Informally, $\tau\approx\tau'$ means “these handles co-refer”.

I’ll require semantic seed coherence:
$$\tau\approx\tau' \Rightarrow \eta(\tau)=\eta(\tau').$$

## 7.2 Identity Groupoid

Define a groupoid $\mathcal{G}$ with:

- objects: $\tau\in\mathsf{Handle}$,
- [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md): $(\tau,\tau')$ whenever $\tau\approx\tau'$,
- identities $(\tau,\tau)$,
- inverses $(\tau',\tau)$,
- composition $(\tau,\tau')\circ(\tau',\tau'')=(\tau,\tau'')$.

**Theorem 7.1.**
$\mathcal{G}$ is a small groupoid.

## 7.3 Transport on Interaction Terms

If $\tau\approx\tau'$, define transport $t[\tau'/\tau]$ by replacing occurrences of $\tau$ with $\tau'$ inside $t$.

**Lemma 7.2 (Interpretation coherence under transport).**
If $\tau\approx\tau'$, then for any [interaction term](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-term.md) $t$ whose evaluation is defined,
$$\llbracket t\rrbracket = \llbracket t[\tau'/\tau]\rrbracket$$
in any fragment containing the relevant seeds.

*Proof.* Transport preserves leaf denotations ($\eta(\tau)=\eta(\tau')$) and definable operators are extensional on fragments.

# 8. Failure Semantics and Partiality

[Semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) can fail. [Peirce](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) identified doubt as the interruption of [habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md) — a genuine surprise that halts the flow of interpretation and forces inquiry (CP 5.417). Failure is not pathological; it is constitutive of how sign systems learn and change — relational patterns that break open space for new relational engagement. But failure must be tracked honestly: a failed interpretation that is silently healed produces false confidence. We formalize failure as a conservative enrichment where the guiding rule is: failure is never “silently healed.”

## 8.1 Failure Value and Failure Marks

We designate $\bot\in H$ as a semantic failure value:
$$\bot_{\mathrm{fail}} := \bot.$$

A **failure-annotated partial state** is:
$$X=(H_X,\Op_X,\mathsf{Fail}_X),$$
where $\mathsf{Fail}_X\subseteq H_X\cup \Op_X \cup \mathsf{IxTerm}$ is a set of marked elements.

If an [interaction term](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-term.md) is ill-formed or an application is undefined, we interpret it as $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$ and mark the corresponding element.

## 8.2 Failure Fragment

Define the failure fragment as the least fragment containing $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$:
$$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fail}} := \{\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}\}.$$

**Lemma 8.1 (Failure propagation inside a fragment).**
If any generator of a fragment is $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$, then the fragment collapses to $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fail}}$.

## 8.3 Closure Compatibility

We require that [closure operators](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) record failures monotonically:

- if a closure step introduces $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$, then $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$ remains present thereafter,
- failure marks only grow under monotone extension.

This is bookkeeping, not new semantics; the [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) already contains $\bot$.

# 9. Provenance Semantics

The trace comonad $G$ records that meanings carry semiotic provenance — but $G$ is extensional: it tracks that provenance exists, not what the provenance was. Provenance semantics makes this intensional. Each element of a fragment acquires a history: a finite sequence of construction steps recording how it was produced. This is the formal counterpart of unlimited [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) at the level of construction: each [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) is itself a [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md), and the chain of [interpretants](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) that produced a meaning is itself a structured object that can be examined, compared, and refined.

## 9.1 Provenance Histories

A **provenance history** for $a\in F$ is a finite sequence of construction steps, each step being one of:

- introduction as $\llbracket t\rrbracket$ for some $t\in\mathsf{IxTerm}$,
- formation by a Heyting operation, $j$, or $G$,
- application of a definable operator $\llbracket f\rrbracket$,
- inclusion under fragment extension.

Two histories are comparable if one refines the other by inserting/collapsing intermediate steps.

## 9.2 Provenance Category of a Fragment

For each fragment $F$, define a small category $P(F)$ where:

- objects are provenance histories of elements of $F$,
- [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md) are refinement relations (coarser $\to$ finer).

Composition is concatenation of refinements.

## 9.3 Fragment-Indexed Structures and Provenance Functor

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the poset-category of fragments under inclusion.

Define a total category $\mathcal{E}$:

- objects are pairs $(F,X)$ where $F$ is a fragment and $X$ is a partial state with $H_X\subseteq F$,
- [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md) $(F,X)\to(F',X')$ exist when $F\subseteq F'$ and $X\le X'$.

Projection:
$$\pi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F},\quad \pi(F,X)=F.$$

Define:
$$\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{E}\to \mathbf{Cat},\quad \mathcal{P}(F,X):=P(F),$$
and on [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md), extend histories along fragment inclusions.

**Lemma 9.1.**
$\mathcal{P}$ is functorial.

# 10. Evaluation Semantics

[Peirce’s](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/charles-sanders-peirce.md) pragmatic maxim holds that the meaning of a concept is the sum of its practical consequences: "Consider what effects, which might conceivably have practical bearings, we conceive the object of our conception to have. Then, our conception of those effects is the whole of our conception of the object" (CP 5.402). Evaluation formalizes this pragmatic dimension: meanings have consequences that can be measured and compared. The valuation $\mu$ assigns a quantity to each meaning without imposing a normative reading — it captures the fact that some meanings carry more practical weight than others, and that [semiosis](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiosis.md) (closure, [habit](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/habit.md)-formation, tracing) does not diminish that weight.

## 10.1 Evaluation Monoid and Valuation

Fix a commutative ordered monoid $(M,\le_M,+,0)$.

A **valuation** is a monotone map:
$$\mu:H\to M$$
satisfying:

1. $\mu(\bot_{\mathrm{fail}})=0$,
2. $\mu(a\vee b)\le_M \mu(a)+\mu(b)$,
3. $\mu(j(a))\ge_M \mu(a)$,
4. $\mu(G(a))\ge_M \mu(a)$.

## 10.2 Fragment and Footprint Valuations

For a finitely generated fragment $F$ with a chosen finite generator set $\mathrm{gens}(F)$, define:
$$\mu(F):=\sum_{a\in \mathrm{gens}(F)} \mu(a).$$

For a closed partial state $Y=(H_Y,\Op_Y)$, define:
$$\mu(Y):=\sup\{\mu(F) : F\subseteq H_Y \text{ is a fragment}\}.$$

For a handle $\tau$ in state $X$, define:
$$\mu(\tau;X) := \mu(\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau)).$$

Now we can compare [footprints](../../../glossary/terms/footprint.md) quantitatively.

# 11. Delta: Difference Across Interactions, Fragments, Footprints

Meaning emerges through relational encounter, and encounters produce differentiation — what one relation generates that another does not. Delta formalizes this relational differentiation at three levels: syntactic (which interactions differ), semantic-local (which fragment elements differ), and semantic-closed (what difference remains after closure). Each level captures a different grain of relational contrast.

## 11.1 Interaction-Surface Delta

For finite [interaction surfaces](../../../glossary/terms/interaction-surface.md) $A,B\subseteq \mathsf{IxTerm}$:
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{ix}}(A,B) := (A\setminus B)\cup (B\setminus A).$$

## 11.2 Fragment Delta

For fragments $F,G\subseteq H$:
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{frag}}(F,G) := \text{least fragment containing }(F\cup G)\setminus(F\cap G).$$

## 11.3 Footprint Delta for Handles

Given $\tau,\tau'\in\mathsf{Handle}$ in state $X$, define:
$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{foot}}(\tau,\tau';X)
:= \mathcal{S}_X\big(\Delta_{\mathrm{frag}}(F_{\tau,X},F_{\tau',X})\big).
$$

We can also measure magnitude:
$$\mu\big(\Delta_{\mathrm{foot}}(\tau,\tau';X)\big).$$

# 12. Subtraction: Semiotic Forgetting

Relations dissolve as well as form; sign systems shed content as well as acquire it. The Heyting pseudo-complement $(b \Rightarrow \bot)$ gives a constructive semantics for this relational withdrawal: to subtract $b$ from $a$ is to retain the largest part of $a$ that is incompatible with $b$. Subtraction is not negation (we remain in intuitionistic logic) but disciplined removal: it respects the lattice structure and stays within the closure discipline.

## 12.1 Heyting Subtraction on Elements

For $a,b\in H$, define:
$$a\ominus b := a \wedge (b\Rightarrow \bot).$$

This uses the Heyting pseudo-complement $(b\Rightarrow\bot)$.

**Lemma 12.1.**
For all $a,b\in H$:
1. $a\ominus b \le a$,
2. if $c\wedge b\le \bot$ then $c\le a\ominus b$.

## 12.2 Fragment Support and Fragment Subtraction

Define fragment support:
$$s(F):=\bigvee_{x\in F} x.$$

Define fragment subtraction:
$$F\ominus G := \text{least fragment containing }\{a\ominus s(G) : a\in F\}.$$

## 12.3 Subtracting a Handle From a State

Let $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$ and $\tau\in\mathsf{Handle}$.

Define the subtraction of $\tau$ from $X$ as:
$$X\ominus \tau := (H_X',\Op_X)$$
where
$$H_X' := \text{least fragment containing }\{a\ominus s(F_{\tau,X}) : a\in H_X\}.$$

Intuitively: we remove the semantic support induced by the handle’s fragment from every element of the current carrier, then re-fragment.

## 12.4 Relation to Syntactic Forgetting

Sometimes we want to “forget” a handle syntactically.

Define:
$$X\setminus \tau := \big(H_X\setminus\{\eta(\tau)\},\ \Op_X\setminus\{f\in\Op_X : f\ \text{mentions}\ \tau\}\big).$$

Then the **restricted closure** is $\mathcal{S}(X\setminus\tau)$.

**Theorem 12.2 (Subtraction vs forgetting).**
For all $X$ and $\tau$,
$$\mathcal{S}(X\ominus \tau)\le \mathcal{S}(X\setminus \tau).$$

*Sketch.* Forgetting deletes syntactic access routes as well as semantic seeds; subtraction removes semantic support but preserves operator vocabulary.

# 13. Interaction Operators (Capabilities)

An interaction operator is a structured action on the semioverse — a way of changing the state that respects all the discipline built up so far: fragments, closure, failure, provenance, evaluation. The operator class is defined by its compatibilities: not every state-transformation counts as semiotic action, only those that preserve the structural invariants that make the semioverse a coherent sign system.

## 13.1 Interaction Operators

An **interaction operator** is a monotone map:
$$E:\mathcal{U}\to \mathcal{U}$$
compatible with:

- fragments (preserves fragment structure of carriers),
- closure (respects $\mathcal{S}$ up to inequality),
- failure monotonicity (does not unmark failures),
- provenance (admits a lifting on provenance histories),
- evaluation monotonicity (does not decrease $\mu$).

I’m not asserting that all these compatibilities are free; I’m defining the operator class by them.

## 13.2 Monoidal Category of Interaction Operators

Let $\mathbf{Int}$ be the category:

- single object $\bullet$,
- [morphisms](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/morphism.md) $\bullet\to\bullet$ are interaction operators,
- composition is function composition.

Tensor is composition:
$$E\otimes F := E\circ F,$$
identity is $\mathrm{id}$.

Then $\mathbf{Int}$ is a strict monoidal category.

## 13.3 2-Category of Partial States

Define a strict 2-category $\mathbf{Part}$ where:

- 0-cells are partial states $X\in\mathcal{U}$,
- 1-cells are monotone maps $f:X\to Y$,
- 2-cells are refinements $f\Rightarrow g$ when $f(X)\le g(X)$ pointwise.

Then $\mathbf{Int}$ acts on $\mathbf{Part}$ by evaluation:
$$(E,X)\mapsto E(X).$$

Now we can talk about composing capabilities and comparing them by refinement.

## 13.4 Internal Agents as Controlled Interaction Operators

An agent in the semioverse is not a new primitive — it is stateful iteration of interaction operators under admissibility constraints. This captures the relational insight that agency is not a substance but a pattern: agency consists in the structured selection and sequencing of sign-mediated actions, constrained by an internal state and a policy that determines which actions are admissible given the current situation.

An **internal agent** is a triple
$$A=(\Sigma,\Pi,K)$$
where:

- $\Sigma$ is an internal agent-state (bounded or well-founded),
- $K\subseteq \mathbf{Int}$ is a finite skillset of interaction operators,
- $\Pi:(\Sigma\times\mathcal{U})\to\mathcal{P}(K)$ is a policy selecting which
  operators are admissible in a given state.

The induced agent step operator is:
$$E_A:(\mathcal{U}\times\Sigma)\to(\mathcal{U}\times\Sigma)$$
with
$$E_A(u,\sigma)=(E(u),\sigma')$$
for some $E\in\Pi(\sigma,u)$ and an updated $\sigma'$.

Agenthood introduces no new operator class: it is stateful iteration plus
admissibility constraints on operator selection. The induced $E_A$ remains
monotone in $\mathcal{U}$ and subject to the same fragment, closure, and
provenance discipline as other interaction operators.

# 14. Interface Algebra (External Actions)

The interface algebra formalizes how external actions — inscribing new interactions, comparing handles, subtracting influence, refining fragments — are compiled into interaction operators that respect the semioverse’s discipline. Each interface action describes what an interpreter does; the compilation map translates that relational act into its semantic effect within the semioverse.

## 14.1 Interface Actions

Let $\mathsf{Act}$ be the set of **interface actions** generated by:

- $\mathsf{Inscribe}(\tau,A)$ where $\tau\in\mathsf{Handle}$ and $A\subseteq \mathsf{IxTerm}$ finite,
- $\mathsf{Compare}(\tau,\tau')$,
- $\mathsf{Subtract}(\tau,\mathrm{Ctx})$ where $\mathrm{Ctx}\in\mathcal{U}$,
- $\mathsf{RefineFrag}(F,\varphi)$ where $F$ is a fragment and $\varphi$ is a refinement directive,
- $\mathsf{Evaluate}(F)$ (observational),
- finite compositions.

## 14.2 Compilation

Define a compilation map:
$$\llbracket - \rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} : \mathsf{Act}\to \mathbf{Int}$$
by recursion:

- $\llbracket \mathsf{Inscribe}(\tau,A)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\mathrm{ix}}_{\tau,A}$, which updates $\mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)$ to include $A$ (auxiliary) and then optionally re-closes induced fragments as artifacts;
- $\llbracket \mathsf{Compare}(\tau,\tau')\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\Delta}_{\tau,\tau'}$, which computes and may materialize $\Delta_{\mathrm{foot}}(\tau,\tau';X)$ as comparison artifacts;
- $\llbracket \mathsf{Subtract}(\tau,\mathrm{Ctx})\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\ominus}_{\tau,\mathrm{Ctx}}$;
- $\llbracket \mathsf{RefineFrag}(F,\varphi)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\mathrm{ref}}_{F,\varphi}$;
- $\llbracket a_2\circ a_1\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := \llbracket a_2\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}}\circ \llbracket a_1\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}}$.

**Lemma 14.1.**
Every compiled action is an interaction operator, i.e. $\llbracket a\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}}\in \mathbf{Int}$.

# 15. Fragment Fibration and Sheaf Semantics

Interpretation is local — a [sign](../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/sign.md) is always interpreted in a bounded context — but local interpretations must cohere when contexts overlap. This is the semiotic gluing problem: if the same meaning appears in two overlapping fragments and agrees on both, there should be a unique meaning in the combined fragment that restricts to each. A Grothendieck fibration organizes fragments categorically; the sheaf condition formalizes gluing; and the preservation theorem ensures that closure respects locality.

## 15.1 Grothendieck Fibration of Fragments

Recall:

- $\mathcal{F}$ is the poset-category of fragments under inclusion,
- $\mathcal{E}$ is the total category of fragment-indexed states,
- $\pi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}$ is projection.

**Theorem 15.1.**
$\pi$ is a Grothendieck fibration.

*Sketch.* Cartesian lifts are given by extending the ambient fragment while holding the state fixed; uniqueness follows from thinness/inclusion structure.

## 15.2 Presheaf of Semantic Carriers

Define a [presheaf](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/presheaf.md):
$$\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{F}^{op}\to \mathbf{Set}$$
by:
$$\mathcal{H}(F):=F,$$
and on inclusions $F\subseteq F'$ by restriction.

## 15.3 Sheaf Condition (Gluing)

Say $\{F_i\}_{i\in I}$ covers $F$ if:
$F = \bigvee_i F_i,$ i.e. $F$ is the least fragment containing $\bigcup_i F_i$.

**Theorem 15.2 (Sheaf condition).**
If $a_i\in F_i$ agree on all intersections $F_i\cap F_j$, then there exists a unique $a\in F$ restricting to each $a_i$.

*Sketch.* Functional closure under Heyting operations and fragment generation makes gluing unique.

**Theorem 15.3.**
If $\{F_i\}$ covers $F$, then $\mathcal{S}_X(F) = \bigvee_i \mathcal{S}_X(F_i)$ inside the [presheaf](../../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/presheaf.md), up to fragmentwise extensional equality.

# 1. Ambient Semantic Field

I'll fix the semantic ambient once and for all.

## 1.1 Complete Heyting Algebra

A **complete Heyting algebra** is a complete lattice $(H,\le)$ equipped with:

- binary meet $\wedge$,
- binary join $\vee$,
- top $\top$,
- bottom $\bot$,
- implication $\Rightarrow$,

such that for all $a,b,c\in H$:
$c \le (a \Rightarrow b)\quad\text{iff}\quad c\wedge a \le b.$

We will treat $(H,\le)$ as a thin category:

- objects are elements of $H$,
- there is a unique arrow $a\to b$ iff $a\le b$.

This “thinness” is not a limitation; it is the point: semantic movement is order-theoretic.

## 1.2 Modal Closure

I'll fix a **modal closure operator** $j:H\to H$ satisfying:

1. (Extensive) $a\le j(a)$,
2. (Monotone) $a\le b\Rightarrow j(a)\le j(b)$,
3. (Idempotent) $j(j(a))=j(a)$,
4. (Join-continuous) for any family $\{a_i\}_{i\in I}$,
   $$
   j\Big(\bigvee_{i\in I} a_i\Big)=\bigvee_{i\in I} j(a_i).
   $$

The **stable fragment** is:
$H^{\mathrm{st}} := \{a\in H : j(a)=a\}$.

**Lemma 1.1 (Stable fragment is Heyting).**
$H^{\mathrm{st}}$ is a complete Heyting subalgebra of $H$, and the inclusion preserves Heyting operations.

*Sketch.* Join-continuity of $j$ and the adjunction definition of $\Rightarrow$ give closure of $H^{\mathrm{st}}$ under Heyting operations.

## 1.3 Temporal Trace as a Heyting–Comonadic Endofunctor

I'll also fix a **temporal trace** $G:H\to H$ that is simultaneously:

- a comonad on the poset-category underlying $H$, and
- a complete Heyting algebra endomorphism,
- join-continuous.

Formally, comonad structure is:

- a monotone endofunctor $G:H\to H$,
- a counit $\epsilon_a: G(a)\le a$,
- a comultiplication $\delta_a: G(a)\le G(G(a))$,

natural in $a$, satisfying the comonad laws in the thin-category sense.

**Axiom 1.2 (Heyting–comonadic coherence).**
$G$ preserves all Heyting structure and all joins:
$$
G\Big(\bigvee_i a_i\Big)=\bigvee_i G(a_i),\qquad
G(a\wedge b)=G(a)\wedge G(b),\qquad
G(a\Rightarrow b)=G(a)\Rightarrow G(b),
$$
and $G(\top)=\top$, $G(\bot)=\bot$.

This is the coherence that makes temporal unfolding compatible with logic.

## 1.4 Modality–Trace Interaction and Stability

I'll require two interaction principles.

**Axiom 1.3 (Basic interaction).**
For all $a\in H$,
$$j(G(a)) \le G(j(a)).$$

**Axiom 1.4 (Stability w.r.t. trace).**
For all $a\in H$:
- if $a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$ then $G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$;
- if $G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$ then $a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$.

So:
$$G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}} \iff a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}.$$

Now we can safely work “inside stability” without losing trace structure.

# 2. Syntactic Operator Algebra

I'll now set up the syntactic side: a finitary source of operators we can interpret into $H$.

## 2.1 Types and Terms

We fix:

- a type grammar $\mathsf{Ty}$ generated from:
  - a base type $P$,
  - function types $A\to B$,
  - product types $A\times B$;
- a typed $\lambda$-calculus with variables, $\lambda$-abstraction, application, pairing, projections, and a chosen set of primitive constants.

For each $n\ge 0$:

- $\mathsf{Op}_n$ is the set of closed terms $f$ of type $P^n\to P$,
- we quotient by definitional equality (e.g. $\beta\eta$), writing $\mathsf{Op}_n/\equiv$.

Let:
$$\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{raw}} := \bigcup_{n\ge 0} \mathsf{Op}_n/\equiv.$$

## 2.2 Definable Operators

I'll define $\mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}\subseteq\mathsf{Op}_n/\equiv$ as the smallest set that:

1. contains all primitive operator constants of arity $n$,
2. is closed under $\lambda$-definability (abstraction and application staying at type $P^n\to P$),
3. is closed under composition at type $P$.

Set:
$$\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}} := \bigcup_{n\ge 0} \mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}.$$

We’ll treat $\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ as a finitary algebra: everything we do syntactically is built from primitives via definability and composition.

# 3. Fragments and Fragmentwise Reasoning

I'll formalize locality: how we reason in finitely generated regions of $H$.

## 3.1 Modal–Temporal Subalgebras

A subset $F\subseteq H$ is a **modal–temporal Heyting subalgebra** if it is closed under:

- Heyting operations $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$,
- modality $j$,
- trace $G$.

A **fragment** is a finitely generated modal–temporal subalgebra: there exists a finite $S\subseteq F$ such that $F$ is the smallest modal–temporal subalgebra containing $S$.

Let $\mathcal{F}(H)$ denote the set of fragments, ordered by inclusion.

## 3.2 Fragment-Preserving Operators and Restriction

Let $f:H^n\to H$.

If $f(F^n)\subseteq F$, we can restrict:
$$f|_F : F^n\to F.$$

**Definition 3.1 (Fragment-preserving).**
$f$ is **fragment-preserving** if for every fragment $F\in\mathcal{F}(H)$,
$$f(F^n)\subseteq F.$$

Fragment-preservation is our “locality discipline” for semantic operators.

## 3.3 Fragmentwise Equality and Hereditary Extensionality

For fragment-preserving $f,g:H^n\to H$ and fragment $F$, we write $f\equiv_F g$ if:
$$f(a_1,\dots,a_n)=g(a_1,\dots,a_n)\quad\forall a_i\in F.$$

**Definition 3.2 (Hereditarily extensional family).**
A family $\mathcal{E}$ of fragment-preserving operators is **hereditarily extensional** if:

> whenever $f,g\in\mathcal{E}$ and $f\equiv_F g$ for some fragment $F$, then for any fragment $F'$ obtained from $F$ by finitely many applications of:
> - Heyting operations,
> - $j$,
> - $G$,
> - any operator in $\mathcal{E}$,
> we have $f\equiv_{F'} g$.

Now we can trust that “equal on a fragment” propagates through the ways we actually build semantics.

# 4. Interpretation into $H$

I'll now connect syntax to semantics.

## 4.1 Interpretation of Definable Operators

An **interpretation** is a family of maps:
$$\llbracket - \rrbracket_n : \mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}} \to \mathrm{Hom}(H^n,H)$$
such that each $f\in\mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}$ satisfies:

1. (Monotone & join-continuous) $\llbracket f\rrbracket$ is monotone in each argument and preserves directed joins in each argument.
2. (Heyting compatibility) the operators corresponding to $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$ are interpreted as those Heyting operations on $H$.
3. (Modal homomorphism)
   $$
   j(\llbracket f\rrbracket(\vec a))=\llbracket f\rrbracket(j(\vec a)).
   $$
4. (Trace compatibility)
   $$
   G(\llbracket f\rrbracket(\vec a))=\llbracket f\rrbracket(G(\vec a)).
   $$
5. (Definitional equality preservation) if $f\equiv g$ syntactically, then $\llbracket f\rrbracket=\llbracket g\rrbracket$.
6. (Fragment preservation) for every fragment $F$,
   $$
   \llbracket f\rrbracket(F^n)\subseteq F.
   $$
7. (Hereditary extensionality) the family $\{\llbracket f\rrbracket : f\in\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}\}$ is hereditarily extensional.

We will use these properties constantly without re-mentioning them.

# 5. Partial States and Closure

Now I’ll define the state space in which the Semioverse closes.

## 5.1 Partial States

A **partial state** is a pair:
$$X=(H_X,\Op_X)$$
with $H_X\subseteq H$ and $\Op_X\subseteq \mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$.

Let:
$$\mathcal{U} := \mathcal{P}(H)\times \mathcal{P}(\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}})$$
ordered by:
$$(H_X,\Op_X)\le (H_Y,\Op_Y)\quad\text{iff}\quad H_X\subseteq H_Y\ \text{and}\ \Op_X\subseteq\Op_Y.$$

Then $(\mathcal{U},\le)$ is a complete lattice under pointwise union/intersection.

## 5.2 Fusion: Congruence and Naming

Fusion enforces coherence between syntax and semantics.

Given $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, let $\mathcal{F}(H_X)$ be the set of fragments $F\in\mathcal{F}(H)$ with $F\subseteq H_X$.

Define $f\sim_X g$ for $f,g\in\Op_X$ if:

1. for every fragment $F\in \mathcal{F}(H_X)$,
   $$
   \llbracket f\rrbracket \equiv_F \llbracket g\rrbracket,
   $$
2. this equivalence is hereditarily extensional under Heyting operations, $j$, $G$, and operators in $\Op_X$.

Fusion also permits naming semantic behaviors already present.

An operator $h:H^n\to H$ is an **admissible behavior over $X$** if it is:

- monotone,
- join-continuous,
- fragment-preserving,
- modal (commutes with $j$),
- trace-compatible (commutes with $G$),
- in the hereditarily extensional closure of $\{\llbracket f\rrbracket : f\in\Op_X\}$.

Let $\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$ be the smallest set such that:

1. $\Op_X\subseteq \Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$,
2. operators equivalent under $\sim_X$ are identified (up to definitional equality),
3. any admissible behavior may be named by a fresh symbol $f_h$ with $\llbracket f_h\rrbracket=h$.

Define the **fusion closure**:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X) := (H_X,\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}).$$

## 5.3 Semantic Closure

Define:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}}(X) := (H_X',\Op_X),$$
where $H_X'$ is the smallest subset of $H$ such that:

1. $H_X\subseteq H_X'$,
2. (Interpretation closure) if $f\in\Op_X$ and $\vec a\in (H_X')^n$, then $\llbracket f\rrbracket(\vec a)\in H_X'$,
3. (Heyting closure) $H_X'$ is closed under $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$,
4. (Modal–trace closure) $H_X'$ is closed under $j$ and $G$,
5. (Fixed-point closure) if $h:H\to H$ is admissible (as above), then any least/greatest fixed point of $h$ above some element of $H_X'$ is included in $H_X'$.

## 5.4 Syntactic Closure with Finitary Justification

I’ll define “finitary justification” as a compactness principle for adding operators.

Let $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, $f\in\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$, and $F\subseteq H_X$ a fragment. Say:

> $f$ is **semantically justified by $F$ over $X$** if there exists $g\in\Op_X$ such that
> $$
> \llbracket f\rrbracket \equiv_F \llbracket g\rrbracket
> $$
> and this equivalence is hereditarily extensional under Heyting operations, $j$, $G$, and operators in $\Op_X$.

Define:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(X) := (H_X,\Op_X'),$$
where $\Op_X'$ is the smallest set such that:

1. $\Op_X\subseteq\Op_X'$,
2. $\Op_X'$ is closed under $\lambda$-definability and composition,
3. fixed-point constructs are included when their interpretations arise from $\Op_X'$,
4. (Finitary justification) if $f$ is semantically justified by some finite fragment $F\subseteq H_X$, then $f\in \Op_X'$.

This yields an algebraic compactness statement:
$$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(X)=\bigcup_{F\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} H_X}\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(F,\Op_X).$$

## 5.5 Global Closure and Least Fixed Point

Define the composite closure:
$$\mathcal{S} := \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}\circ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}\circ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}} : \mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}.$$

$\mathcal{S}$ is monotone and inflationary.

By Knaster–Tarski, $\mathcal{S}$ has a least fixed point:
$$X^\star = \bigwedge\{X\in\mathcal{U} : \mathcal{S}(X)\le X\}.$$

**Definition 5.1 (Semioverse core).**
I’ll call $X^\star=(H_\star,\Op_\star)$ the **core semioverse state**.

Now we can treat “the Semioverse” as the closure discipline encoded by $\mathcal{S}$ together with its least fixed point $X^\star$.

## 5.6 Universal Property (Initiality)

A **semioverse structure** consists of:

- a complete Heyting algebra $K$ with closure $j_K$ and trace $G_K$ satisfying analogues of Axioms 1.2–1.4,
- an interpretation $\llbracket-\rrbracket_K$ of $\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ into $K$ satisfying the analogues of Section 4,
- a fixed point $X_K$ of the induced closure operator extending the same primitive data.

Morphisms preserve all structure (Heyting, $j$, $G$, and interpretation), and 2-equality is fragmentwise extensional equality.

**Theorem 5.2 (Initiality).**
The core semioverse state $X^\star$ is initial among semioverse structures, up to fragmentwise extensional equality.

*Sketch.* Standard closure/fixed-point minimality argument plus fusion-as-reflection behavior.

# 6. Things and Interaction Calculus

Up to now, we’ve built a closure-controlled semantic environment. Now I’ll introduce **things** as the entities we interact with inside it.

## 6.1 Thing Handles

Let $\mathsf{Thing}$ be a set of **thing handles**. A handle is not an element of $H$; it is an external index that can participate in interaction terms.

I’ll fix an interpretation of handles as semantic seeds:
$$\eta : \mathsf{Thing}\to H.$$

We will write:
$$\llbracket \tau \rrbracket := \eta(\tau)$$
for $\tau\in\mathsf{Thing}$.

This is the only place where a thing handle touches semantics directly; everything else is mediated by interaction terms and closure.

## 6.2 Interaction Terms

I’ll define **interaction terms** as the smallest set $\mathsf{IxTerm}$ generated by:
$$t ::= \tau \mid k(t_1,\dots,t_n),$$
where $\tau\in\mathsf{Thing}$ and $k\in \mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$.

Interpretation extends recursively:
- $\llbracket \tau\rrbracket = \eta(\tau)$,
- $\llbracket k(t_1,\dots,t_n)\rrbracket = \llbracket k\rrbracket(\llbracket t_1\rrbracket,\dots,\llbracket t_n\rrbracket)$.

Now we can treat interaction terms as semiotic “touches” that yield semantic material in $H$.

## 6.3 Interaction Surfaces

Given a partial state $X$, an **interaction surface** is auxiliary data:
$$\mathrm{Ix}_X : \mathsf{Thing}\to \mathcal{P}(\mathsf{IxTerm}),$$
typically finite-valued.

For a handle $\tau$, write:
$$\mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)\subseteq \mathsf{IxTerm}.$$

I’ll emphasize: $\mathrm{Ix}_X$ is not itself semantic content. Semantic consequences are mediated through fragments.

## 6.4 Thing Fragments

Given $X$ and $\tau$, define its interaction denotation set:
$$T_{\tau,X} := \{\llbracket t\rrbracket : t\in \mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)\}\subseteq H.$$

Define the **thing fragment**:
$$F_{\tau,X} := \text{the least fragment containing } T_{\tau,X}.$$

**Lemma 6.1 (Monotonicity in interaction surface).**
If $\mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)\subseteq \mathrm{Ix}'_X(\tau)$, then $F_{\tau,X}\subseteq F'_{\tau,X}$.

*Proof.* Inclusion of generators implies inclusion of generated fragments.

## 6.5 Closing a Fragment Inside a State

Since $\mathcal{S}$ acts on partial states, I’ll define a convenient shorthand.

If $F\subseteq H_X$ is a fragment, define the restricted partial state:
$$X|_F := (F,\Op_X).$$

Then define:
$$\mathcal{S}_X(F) := \mathcal{S}(X|_F).$$

This is “closing $F$ using the operator context of $X$”.

## 6.6 Semiotic Footprint of a Thing

Now we can define what we actually use.

**Definition 6.2 (Footprint).**
For $\tau\in\mathsf{Thing}$ in state $X$, define the **semiotic footprint**:
$$\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau) := \mathcal{S}_X(F_{\tau,X}).$$

This is a closed partial state built from the thing’s fragment.

**Theorem 6.3 (Boundedness by the semioverse core).**
For any $X\le X^\star$ and any $\tau$,
$$\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau) \le X^\star.$$

*Proof.* $X^\star$ is the least fixed point extending the primitive data; closure of any substate is below it by monotonicity and minimality.

## 6.7 Local Persistence

Sometimes interactions “stay local” and do not promote globally.

**Definition 6.4 (Locally persistent interaction).**
An interaction term $t\in \mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)$ is **locally persistent** if:
$$
\llbracket t\rrbracket \in F_{\tau,X}
\quad\text{but}\quad
\llbracket t\rrbracket \notin H_{\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau)}.
$$

Now we can distinguish “locally generating” from “globally closing”.

# 7. Identity of Things

Now I’ll formalize co-reference: the same thing can be addressed by different handles.

## 7.1 Handle Co-Reference

Introduce a symmetric relation $\approx$ on $\mathsf{Thing}$. Informally, $\tau\approx\tau'$ means “these handles co-refer”.

I’ll require semantic seed coherence:
$$\tau\approx\tau' \Rightarrow \eta(\tau)=\eta(\tau').$$

## 7.2 Identity Groupoid

Define a groupoid $\mathcal{G}$ with:

- objects: $\tau\in\mathsf{Thing}$,
- morphisms: $(\tau,\tau')$ whenever $\tau\approx\tau'$,
- identities $(\tau,\tau)$,
- inverses $(\tau',\tau)$,
- composition $(\tau,\tau')\circ(\tau',\tau'')=(\tau,\tau'')$.

**Theorem 7.1.**
$\mathcal{G}$ is a small groupoid.

## 7.3 Transport on Interaction Terms

If $\tau\approx\tau'$, define transport $t[\tau'/\tau]$ by replacing occurrences of $\tau$ with $\tau'$ inside $t$.

**Lemma 7.2 (Interpretation coherence under transport).**
If $\tau\approx\tau'$, then for any interaction term $t$ whose evaluation is defined,
$$\llbracket t\rrbracket = \llbracket t[\tau'/\tau]\rrbracket$$
in any fragment containing the relevant seeds.

*Proof.* Transport preserves leaf denotations ($\eta(\tau)=\eta(\tau')$) and definable operators are extensional on fragments.

# 8. Failure Semantics and Partiality

Now I’ll add failure as a conservative enrichment. The guiding rule is: failure is never “silently healed.”

## 8.1 Failure Value and Failure Marks

We designate $\bot\in H$ as a semantic failure value:
$$\bot_{\mathrm{fail}} := \bot.$$

A **failure-annotated partial state** is:
$$X=(H_X,\Op_X,\mathsf{Fail}_X),$$
where $\mathsf{Fail}_X\subseteq H_X\cup \Op_X \cup \mathsf{IxTerm}$ is a set of marked elements.

If an interaction term is ill-formed or an application is undefined, we interpret it as $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$ and mark the corresponding element.

## 8.2 Failure Fragment

Define the failure fragment as the least fragment containing $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$:
$$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fail}} := \{\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}\}.$$

**Lemma 8.1 (Failure propagation inside a fragment).**
If any generator of a fragment is $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$, then the fragment collapses to $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fail}}$.

## 8.3 Closure Compatibility

We require that closure operators record failures monotonically:

- if a closure step introduces $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$, then $\bot_{\mathrm{fail}}$ remains present thereafter,
- failure marks only grow under monotone extension.

This is bookkeeping, not new semantics; the Heyting algebra already contains $\bot$.

---

# 9. Provenance Semantics

Now I’ll attach intensional histories to semantic elements inside fragments.

## 9.1 Provenance Histories

A **provenance history** for $a\in F$ is a finite sequence of construction steps, each step being one of:

- introduction as $\llbracket t\rrbracket$ for some $t\in\mathsf{IxTerm}$,
- formation by a Heyting operation, $j$, or $G$,
- application of a definable operator $\llbracket f\rrbracket$,
- inclusion under fragment extension.

Two histories are comparable if one refines the other by inserting/collapsing intermediate steps.

## 9.2 Provenance Category of a Fragment

For each fragment $F$, define a small category $P(F)$ where:

- objects are provenance histories of elements of $F$,
- morphisms are refinement relations (coarser $\to$ finer).

Composition is concatenation of refinements.

## 9.3 Fragment-Indexed Structures and Provenance Functor

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the poset-category of fragments under inclusion.

Define a total category $\mathcal{E}$:

- objects are pairs $(F,X)$ where $F$ is a fragment and $X$ is a partial state with $H_X\subseteq F$,
- morphisms $(F,X)\to(F',X')$ exist when $F\subseteq F'$ and $X\le X'$.

Projection:
$$\pi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F},\quad \pi(F,X)=F.$$

Define:
$$\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{E}\to \mathbf{Cat},\quad \mathcal{P}(F,X):=P(F),$$
and on morphisms, extend histories along fragment inclusions.

**Lemma 9.1.**
$\mathcal{P}$ is functorial.

# 10. Evaluation Semantics

Now I’ll add a quantitative layer without baking in any normative reading.

## 10.1 Evaluation Monoid and Valuation

Fix a commutative ordered monoid $(M,\le_M,+,0)$.

A **valuation** is a monotone map:
$$\mu:H\to M$$
satisfying:

1. $\mu(\bot_{\mathrm{fail}})=0$,
2. $\mu(a\vee b)\le_M \mu(a)+\mu(b)$,
3. $\mu(j(a))\ge_M \mu(a)$,
4. $\mu(G(a))\ge_M \mu(a)$.

## 10.2 Fragment and Footprint Valuations

For a finitely generated fragment $F$ with a chosen finite generator set $\mathrm{gens}(F)$, define:
$$\mu(F):=\sum_{a\in \mathrm{gens}(F)} \mu(a).$$

For a closed partial state $Y=(H_Y,\Op_Y)$, define:
$$\mu(Y):=\sup\{\mu(F) : F\subseteq H_Y \text{ is a fragment}\}.$$

For a thing $\tau$ in state $X$, define:
$$\mu(\tau;X) := \mu(\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau)).$$

Now we can compare footprints quantitatively.

---

# 11. Delta: Difference Across Interactions, Fragments, Footprints

Now I’ll define the three deltas we use.

## 11.1 Interaction-Surface Delta

For finite interaction surfaces $A,B\subseteq \mathsf{IxTerm}$:
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{ix}}(A,B) := (A\setminus B)\cup (B\setminus A).$$

## 11.2 Fragment Delta

For fragments $F,G\subseteq H$:
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{frag}}(F,G) := \text{least fragment containing }(F\cup G)\setminus(F\cap G).$$

## 11.3 Footprint Delta for Things

Given $\tau,\tau'\in\mathsf{Thing}$ in state $X$, define:
$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{foot}}(\tau,\tau';X)
:= \mathcal{S}_X\big(\Delta_{\mathrm{frag}}(F_{\tau,X},F_{\tau',X})\big).
$$

We can also measure magnitude:
$$\mu\big(\Delta_{\mathrm{foot}}(\tau,\tau';X)\big).$$

---

# 12. Subtraction: Removing Semantic Influence

Now I’ll define a disciplined removal operation.

## 12.1 Heyting Subtraction on Elements

For $a,b\in H$, define:
$$a\ominus b := a \wedge (b\Rightarrow \bot).$$

This uses the Heyting pseudo-complement $(b\Rightarrow\bot)$.

**Lemma 12.1.**
For all $a,b\in H$:
1. $a\ominus b \le a$,
2. if $c\wedge b\le \bot$ then $c\le a\ominus b$.

## 12.2 Fragment Support and Fragment Subtraction

Define fragment support:
$$s(F):=\bigvee_{x\in F} x.$$

Define fragment subtraction:
$$F\ominus G := \text{least fragment containing }\{a\ominus s(G) : a\in F\}.$$

## 12.3 Subtracting a Thing From a State

Let $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$ and $\tau\in\mathsf{Thing}$.

Define the subtraction of $\tau$ from $X$ as:
$$X\ominus \tau := (H_X',\Op_X)$$
where
$$H_X' := \text{least fragment containing }\{a\ominus s(F_{\tau,X}) : a\in H_X\}.$$

Intuitively: we remove the semantic support induced by $\tau$’s fragment from every element of the current carrier, then re-fragment.

## 12.4 Relation to Syntactic Forgetting

Sometimes we want to “forget” a handle syntactically.

Define:
$$X\setminus \tau := \big(H_X\setminus\{\eta(\tau)\},\ \Op_X\setminus\{f\in\Op_X : f\ \text{mentions}\ \tau\}\big).$$

Then the **restricted closure** is $\mathcal{S}(X\setminus\tau)$.

**Theorem 12.2 (Subtraction vs forgetting).**
For all $X$ and $\tau$,
$$\mathcal{S}(X\ominus \tau)\le \mathcal{S}(X\setminus \tau).$$

*Sketch.* Forgetting deletes syntactic access routes as well as semantic seeds; subtraction removes semantic support but preserves operator vocabulary.

# 13. Interaction Operators (Capabilities)


## 13.1 Interaction Operators

An **interaction operator** is a monotone map:
$$E:\mathcal{U}\to \mathcal{U}$$
compatible with:

- fragments (preserves fragment structure of carriers),
- closure (respects $\mathcal{S}$ up to inequality),
- failure monotonicity (does not unmark failures),
- provenance (admits a lifting on provenance histories),
- evaluation monotonicity (does not decrease $\mu$).

I’m not asserting that all these compatibilities are free; I’m defining the operator class by them.

## 13.2 Monoidal Category of Interaction Operators

Let $\mathbf{Int}$ be the category:

- single object $\bullet$,
- morphisms $\bullet\to\bullet$ are interaction operators,
- composition is function composition.

Tensor is composition:
$$E\otimes F := E\circ F,$$
identity is $\mathrm{id}$.

Then $\mathbf{Int}$ is a strict monoidal category.

## 13.3 2-Category of Partial States

Define a strict 2-category $\mathbf{Part}$ where:

- 0-cells are partial states $X\in\mathcal{U}$,
- 1-cells are monotone maps $f:X\to Y$,
- 2-cells are refinements $f\Rightarrow g$ when $f(X)\le g(X)$ pointwise.

Then $\mathbf{Int}$ acts on $\mathbf{Part}$ by evaluation:
$$(E,X)\mapsto E(X).$$

Now we can talk about composing capabilities and comparing them by refinement.

---

# 14. Interface Algebra (External Actions)

Now I’ll define an external action syntax and compile it into interaction operators.

## 14.1 Interface Actions

Let $\mathsf{Act}$ be the set of **interface actions** generated by:

- $\mathsf{Inscribe}(\tau,A)$ where $\tau\in\mathsf{Thing}$ and $A\subseteq \mathsf{IxTerm}$ finite,
- $\mathsf{Compare}(\tau,\tau')$,
- $\mathsf{Subtract}(\tau,\mathrm{Ctx})$ where $\mathrm{Ctx}\in\mathcal{U}$,
- $\mathsf{RefineFrag}(F,\varphi)$ where $F$ is a fragment and $\varphi$ is a refinement directive,
- $\mathsf{Evaluate}(F)$ (observational),
- finite compositions.

## 14.2 Compilation

Define a compilation map:
$$\llbracket - \rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} : \mathsf{Act}\to \mathbf{Int}$$
by recursion:

- $\llbracket \mathsf{Inscribe}(\tau,A)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\mathrm{ix}}_{\tau,A}$, which updates $\mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)$ to include $A$ (auxiliary) and then optionally re-closes induced fragments as artifacts;
- $\llbracket \mathsf{Compare}(\tau,\tau')\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\Delta}_{\tau,\tau'}$, which computes and may materialize $\Delta_{\mathrm{foot}}(\tau,\tau';X)$ as comparison artifacts;
- $\llbracket \mathsf{Subtract}(\tau,\mathrm{Ctx})\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\ominus}_{\tau,\mathrm{Ctx}}$;
- $\llbracket \mathsf{RefineFrag}(F,\varphi)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := E^{\mathrm{ref}}_{F,\varphi}$;
- $\llbracket a_2\circ a_1\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}} := \llbracket a_2\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}}\circ \llbracket a_1\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}}$.

**Lemma 14.1.**
Every compiled action is an interaction operator, i.e. $\llbracket a\rrbracket_{\mathrm{iface}}\in \mathbf{Int}$.

# 15. Fragment Fibration and Sheaf Semantics

Now I’ll make locality categorical and support gluing of local views.

## 15.1 Grothendieck Fibration of Fragments

Recall:

- $\mathcal{F}$ is the poset-category of fragments under inclusion,
- $\mathcal{E}$ is the total category of fragment-indexed states,
- $\pi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}$ is projection.

**Theorem 15.1.**
$\pi$ is a Grothendieck fibration.

*Sketch.* Cartesian lifts are given by extending the ambient fragment while holding the state fixed; uniqueness follows from thinness/inclusion structure.

## 15.2 Presheaf of Semantic Carriers

Define a presheaf:
$$\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{F}^{op}\to \mathbf{Set}$$
by:
$$\mathcal{H}(F):=F,$$
and on inclusions $F\subseteq F'$ by restriction.

## 15.3 Sheaf Condition (Gluing)

Say $\{F_i\}_{i\in I}$ covers $F$ if:
$F = \bigvee_i F_i,$ i.e. $F$ is the least fragment containing $\bigcup_i F_i$.

**Theorem 15.2 (Sheaf condition).**
If $a_i\in F_i$ agree on all intersections $F_i\cap F_j$, then there exists a unique $a\in F$ restricting to each $a_i$.

*Sketch.* Functional closure under Heyting operations and fragment generation makes gluing unique.

## 15.4 Closure Preserves Sheaf Semantics

**Theorem 15.3.**
If $\{F_i\}$ covers $F$, then $\mathcal{S}_X(F) = \bigvee_i \mathcal{S}_X(F_i)$ inside the presheaf, up to fragmentwise extensional equality.

# 16. Coherence Theorems

Now I’ll state the coherence results that let us treat this as one object instead of a pile of parts.

## 16.1 Identity Stability

If $\tau\approx\tau'$, then interaction transport preserves footprints: $\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau)=\mathrm{Foot}_X(\tau')$ whenever the interaction surfaces are transported coherently.

## 16.2 Provenance Coherence

For any interaction operator $E$ that admits a provenance lifting, and any inclusion $F\subseteq F'$, the natural square on provenance categories commutes up to canonical refinement equivalence:
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
P(F) & \xrightarrow{E^\sharp_F} & P(E(F)) \\
\downarrow & & \downarrow \\
P(F') & \xrightarrow{E^\sharp_{F'}} & P(E(F'))
\end{array}
$$

## 16.3 Evaluation Coherence

For any interaction operator $E$ and any state $X$:
$$
\mu(H_X)\le_M \mu(H_{E(X)}),
\qquad
\mu\big(\mathcal{S}(E(X))\big)\ge_M \mu\big(\mathcal{S}(X)\big).
$$

## 16.4 Stability Under Closure

For any finite composition of interaction operators $E_n\circ\cdots\circ E_1$ and any $X\le X^\star$:
$\mathcal{S}\big(E_n(\cdots E_1(X)\cdots)\big)\le X^\star.$

This is the “nothing escapes the core semioverse” property.

# 18. Conclusion

I’ve defined an Interactive Semioverse in which things are accessed only via interaction, and where interaction yields semantic material that is controlled by fragments and closure.

Now we can:

- treat “thing work” as disciplined fragment generation and closure,
- compare things by delta of footprints,
- remove influence by subtraction or forgetting,
- reason locally via fragments and glue via sheaf semantics,
- implement capabilities as interaction operators compiled from external actions.

The Semioverse is not a theory of what things “really are”; it is a theory of what happens—mathematically—when we interact with things through semiotic structure.
