A pass in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is a legal turn in which a player declines to place a stone, leaving the board unchanged and giving the move to the opponent [@britgo2025default]. Passing is always available; it is never restricted by [ko](./ko.md), [suicide](./suicide.md), or any other placement rule, since it places nothing. Two consecutive passes, one by each player in immediate succession, end the main phase of play and open [dead-stones](./dead-stones.md) settlement and scoring; if the players cannot agree during that settlement, play conventionally resumes from the pass to resolve the disagreement by capture [@usgo2022agarules].

Passing is where the two major scoring conventions attach a small but real procedural difference. Under strict territory scoring, a pass costs nothing. Under some area-scoring implementations, and under the AGA's hybrid counting method specifically, each pass hands the opponent a stone (a "pass stone") to be placed in the passer's own territory before counting, which is precisely the device that keeps [area-scoring](./area-scoring.md) and [territory-scoring](./territory-scoring.md) results aligned [@usgo2022agarules].

## Related terms

- [dead-stones](./dead-stones.md) - The pair of passes that ends play is what starts dead-stone settlement.
- [area-scoring](./area-scoring.md) - Some area-scoring implementations use a pass stone to keep area and territory counts consistent.
- [territory-scoring](./territory-scoring.md) - Territory scoring is the convention the pass stone device was designed to stay consistent with.
- [seki](./seki.md) - A seki is left unresolved through passing; neither side plays its shared liberties before the game ends.
