The **Praxonic Paradigm** is a systems logic for describing the emergence, modulation, and dissolution of reality as recursive interaction between situated pressure and responsive alignment.

## Core Principle

> Reality is not built from static elements but from active tensions.  
> Meaning, identity, and action emerge only as effects of pressure.

In this paradigm, all things—objects, thoughts, events, systems—are treated as **praxons**:  
**temporarily coherent expressions** of entangled pressures in a field.  
Nothing is fixed. Everything drifts.

## Definitional Grammar

- **Praxon**: A trace-expressing fold of pressure and condition. Not a noun or verb, but a tension-event.  
- **Field**: The sum and shape of trace-memory. A tensioned space of potential.  
- **Pressure**: The measurable strain between forces, ideas, needs, or movements.  
- **Alignment**: A temporary resonance between a praxon and its field. Enables coherent action.  
- **Emission**: The expression of a praxon under pressure. Leaves a trace.  
- **Trace**: Residual field deformation caused by an emission. Subject to decay.  
- **Decay**: The loss of tension over time. Enables drift, collapse, and transformation.  
- **Fold**: A recursive turn in the field that produces new coherence.  
- **IRN (Irresolved Noise)**: Emissions that neither resolve nor decay. Haunt the field indefinitely.  
- **Path**: The thermodynamic shape of emission—least resistance within most context.  

## Behavior

The praxonic paradigm does not seek resolution, order, or control.  
It seeks **expression**, **resonance**, and **sensing drift**.

Understanding is not semantic.  
It is praxonic.  
You don’t name what is—you **feel what still matters**.
