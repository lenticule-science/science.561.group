# Learn Sign Formalization

Peircean semiotics has structural features — compositionality, iterative closure, constructive reasoning — that invite mathematical articulation. This skill is the bridge between informal sign theory and the formal structures used to talk about signs precisely.

## What you will be able to do

- Explain why Peirce's semiotic theory calls for mathematical formalization: signs combine compositionally, semiosis generates further signs (iterative closure), and not all sign-claims are decidable (constructive rather than classical reasoning).
- Identify the correspondence between semiotic concepts and mathematical structures: the space of signs as a Heyting algebra, modal stability as a closure operator, interpretive history as a comonad, syntactic combination as typed lambda calculus.
- Describe what formalization provides that informal semiotic theory cannot: a precise account of when semiosis stabilizes, what coherence between syntax and semantics means, and how different sign systems relate through structure-preserving morphisms.
- Explain the role of the least fixed point (via the Knaster–Tarski theorem) in grounding a semiotic universe as a minimal self-sustaining sign system.

## Prerequisites

- `learn-semiotics-basics` — the triadic sign (representamen, object, interpretant), icon/index/symbol, semiosis as iterative process. The lesson motivates each mathematical structure from a specific semiotic concept, so the prior vocabulary is required.

The mathematical structures referenced (Heyting algebras, closure operators, typed lambda calculus) are explained motivationally, not assumed. Completing the relevant math `learn-*` skills before or alongside this one will deepen understanding but is not strictly required — this skill shows *why* those structures appear, not how they're developed formally.

## Scope

This skill covers the *motivation* for formalizing semiotics — the bridge between Peircean theory and the mathematical structures used downstream. It does not cover:

- The full formal specification of any particular semiotic universe.
- Category-theoretic aspects (semiotic morphisms, 2-categorical structure).
- Non-Peircean semiotic traditions and their potential formalization (Saussurean, biosemiotic, Indigenous — acknowledged gaps).
- Typed lambda calculus in detail — covered by `learn-typed-lambda-calculus`.

## Verification

Pick one of the three motivations for formalization (compositionality, iterative closure, constructive reasoning). In your own words, explain the semiotic concept, the mathematical structure it corresponds to, and what the formalization adds that the informal account lacks.
