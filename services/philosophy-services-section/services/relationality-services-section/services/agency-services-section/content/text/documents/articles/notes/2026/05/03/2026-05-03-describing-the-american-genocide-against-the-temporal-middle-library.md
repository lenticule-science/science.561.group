The elimination of the [temporal middle](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/04/02/2026-04-02-temporal-middle.md) — the position of inheriting, continuing, and transforming a tradition without being frozen in the past or severed from it — is not only a conceptual problem or a representational problem.
It is, in the context of the settler-colonial history of the United States, an empirical historical description of a process that had specific material operations, specific perpetrators, and specific victims.

Describing this process requires precision.
The temporal middle is a sociological concept — it names the position every living person occupies — but the settler-colonial assault on the temporal middle had specific institutional forms, specific geographies, and specific populations.
What follows is a sociological-historical description of the assault, using the temporal-middle concept as the analytical frame.

## What the temporal middle is and why it is targeted

The temporal middle is the position of living tradition — the position of a person or community that is actively inheriting from its past while also actively transforming what it inherits, such that the tradition is neither static nor broken.
The temporal middle is an unremarkable position to occupy; every living community, every living person, occupies it.
The interesting question is not whether a community is in the temporal middle but what forces make the temporal middle illegible or impossible.

Settler colonialism targets the temporal middle because the temporal middle is what makes indigenous peoples' claims continuous.
A people frozen in the past can be managed as a historical artifact; a people severed from their past can be managed as assimilated citizens.
A people in the temporal middle — actively inheriting, developing, and claiming continuity — cannot be so managed.
They are contemporaneous political actors with historical claims that are still alive.

Patrick Wolfe (2006) identified the logic of elimination as settler colonialism's defining logic: not merely killing but eliminating indigenous *presence* as such — eliminating the political, cultural, and social forms through which indigenous peoples' claims are continuously reproduced.
The temporal middle is the site of this continuous reproduction; eliminating the temporal middle is eliminating the claim.

## The operations of elimination in the U.S. context

The elimination of the temporal middle operated through multiple simultaneous institutional means, each of which targeted the specific mechanisms through which the temporal middle was reproduced.

**Killing.** The direct killing of indigenous people — in campaigns like the California Gold Rush period's deliberate extermination of coastal California peoples, documented by Benjamin Madley (2016) with state-funded scalping bounties, formal military campaigns against specific communities, and the destruction of entire peoples — reduced population to levels below the threshold of cultural reproduction.
The temporal middle requires people; killing enough people eliminates the temporal middle by eliminating the people who would occupy it.

**Land seizure.** The temporal middle is reproduced in specific places — on the territory to which the community has specific relations of ceremony, kinship, resource-use, and ancestral obligation.
Removing a community from its territory does not sever the community from its tradition immediately, but it removes the spatial ground in which the tradition is reproduced.
The territorial seizures of the 19th century — the treaty violations, the Dawes Act allotment system, the removal campaigns — targeted the spatial substrate of the temporal middle.

**Disruption of kinship and cultural reproduction.** The boarding school system — in which indigenous children were removed from their families and placed in institutions where their language was forbidden, their cultural practices were punished, and their familial and communal bonds were systematically severed — targeted the temporal middle at the generational level.
The temporal middle is reproduced through kinship: elders transmit to the next generation, and the next generation inherits and transforms.
The boarding school's specific operation was to break this transmission, severing the generations from each other to eliminate the tradition's continuity.

**Legal-political disposal.** The elimination of indigenous peoples' political standing — through the Civilization Regulations that criminalized spiritual practices, through the federal recognition system that made some communities' continuity legible and others' not, through the allotment system that converted communal land tenure to individual title — targeted the institutional forms through which indigenous communities' collective political life was organized.
Without the institutional forms, the temporal middle cannot be sustained as a political claim; the community becomes a collection of individuals, each individually manageable, rather than a collective with historical standing.

## What is specifically American about the American form

The American form of temporal-middle elimination has several specific features that distinguish it from other colonial eliminations.

**The democratic frame.** The elimination was performed under a democratic political system that simultaneously proclaimed universal rights, equality before the law, and the moral basis of governance in the consent of the governed.
This democratic frame required that the elimination be narrated in ways compatible with the democratic principles — as civilizational progress, as legal process, as the inevitable consequence of natural superiority, as unfortunate but necessary development.
The narration required, in turn, the [genring](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/genring.md) that produced indigenous peoples as genre figures (noble savages frozen in the pre-historical) whose elimination was a natural process rather than a political choice.

**The laboratory structure.** California, as analyzed in [california-as-laboratory](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/04/27/2026-04-27-california-as-laboratory.md), was the most intensive site of temporal-middle elimination precisely because it was the laboratory for the apparatus's most rapid iteration.
The California genocide — in which the indigenous population was reduced from approximately 150,000 in 1846 to approximately 30,000 in 1870 — provided the cleared ground for the apparatus's subsequent iterations.

**The [crisis settlerism](../../04/27/2026-04-27-crisis-settlerism.md) pattern.** The elimination was not a one-time event followed by a active settler society.
It followed the crisis-settlerism pattern: each crisis within settler society produced a new frontier-projection, a new elimination operation, a new clearing of ground for the next iteration.
The Gold Rush was crisis-settlerism (the economic crisis of the 1840s producing frontier projection); the reservation system was crisis-settlerism (the post-Civil-War political crisis producing the bureaucratic apparatus that managed the cleared ground).

## The ongoing structure

The temporal-middle elimination is not a completed historical event.
It is an ongoing structure.
The mechanisms through which it operates have changed — the boarding schools have been formally closed, the explicit extermination campaigns are not currently state policy — but the structural operation continues through new institutional forms.

The federal recognition system continues to operate as a mechanism for determining which communities' temporal middles are legible.
A community that was disrupted by the boarding school system, that was scattered by the allotment system, that survived by adapting to urban or mixed-community conditions — that community's temporal middle may be illegible to the recognition system, which requires a specific form of documented continuity that the disruption was specifically designed to prevent.

The [californication](../../../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/content/relating/content/relational-entity/content/social-being/content/social-relation/content/power/subjectifying/content/subject/content/possessive-individual/ontologizing/content/liberalism/analyzing/content/cybernetic-postliberalism/teaching/content/describing-the-american-genocide-against-the-tempo/teaching/explanations/californication.md) apparatus continues to operate the genring that makes the temporal middle invisible: the wellness industry's appropriation of indigenous practices (yoga, plant medicine, smudging) reformatted as personal-developmental tools strips the practice of the relational obligations and contextual meanings through which the temporal middle is sustained.
The [savior-slave subject](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/savior-slave-subject.md)'s use of appropriated practices sustains the genre figure of the authentic-native whose practices are available for settler use, while making invisible the living indigenous people who hold those practices within continuing relations.

The ongoing structure is what predicated sociology's settler-colonial analysis is designed to make visible — not as historical critique of completed events but as structural analysis of operations currently running.
