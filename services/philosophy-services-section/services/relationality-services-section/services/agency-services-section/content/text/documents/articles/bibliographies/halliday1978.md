## Annotation

Halliday's *Language as Social Semiotic* is the founding document of systemic-functional linguistics' social-semiotic extension. For this theory's framing, two Halliday threads are load-bearing:

1. **Register / field / tenor / mode as four axes of social meaning.** Register is the *configuration* of meaning that a context calls for; field is the *what* (subject-matter), tenor is the *who-with-whom* (social relation), mode is the *how* (medium of transmission). The four axes are independent variables that jointly determine an utterance's social meaning. This grounds the [codeswitching](../../../glossary/terms/codeswitching.md) morphism's substrate-level reading as per-cell scope-restriction along the social-semiotic axes.
2. **Language as social semiotic.** The discipline's commitment that meaning is produced *in, for, and by* social groups, not in the heads of individual interpreters. This grounds the substrate-level absorption of social-semiotic content into the discourse-community-as-fiber reading.
