---
name: "intension"
date-created: 2025-12-31
---

An **intension** is the rule, concept, or mode of presentation that determines what a term refers to. Where [extension](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/extension.md) is the actual referent a term picks out, intension is the criterion that does the picking.

Consider the term &ldquo;the tallest building in the world.&rdquo; Its intension is the descriptive rule: find the building that exceeds all others in height. Its [extension](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/extension.md) — the building that satisfies that rule — changes over time as taller buildings are constructed. The intension stays fixed even as the extension shifts.

Gottlob Frege drew an early version of this distinction with his concept of &ldquo;Sinn&rdquo; (sense), which he contrasted with &ldquo;Bedeutung&rdquo; (reference). Two expressions can have the same reference but different senses: &ldquo;the morning star&rdquo; and &ldquo;the evening star&rdquo; both refer to Venus, but they present Venus differently. The sense of each expression is what a competent speaker grasps when understanding it.

In possible-worlds semantics, an intension is modeled as a function from possible worlds to extensions. The intension of &ldquo;water&rdquo; maps each possible world to whatever substance fills the water-role in that world. This formalization makes intension precise enough for compositional semantic theories, where the intension of a sentence is built from the intensions of its parts.

David Chalmers developed the distinction further by separating [primary intension](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semantics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/primary-intension.md) (the epistemic component, sensitive to how things turn out) from [secondary intension](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semantics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/secondary-intension.md) (the [metaphysical](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/philosophy/terms/metaphysics.md) component, fixed by how things actually are). This two-dimensional framework addresses puzzles that arise from terms like &ldquo;water,&rdquo; which refers to H2O in the actual world but might refer to different substances in counterfactual scenarios.
