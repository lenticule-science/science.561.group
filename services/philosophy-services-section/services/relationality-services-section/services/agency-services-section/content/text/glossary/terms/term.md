# Term — algebraic/typed-language sense

For a specified many-sorted or one-sorted algebraic signature \`Σ\` and permitted variables \`X\`, an **algebraic term** is built inductively from admitted variable and operation constructors. In a one-sorted first-order algebraic signature, the set of such terms is the **free term algebra** \`T_Σ(X)\`: generators are variables, nullary constructors are constants, and an \`n\`-ary constructor applied to \`n\` already-formed terms yields another term. No additional string counts as a well-formed term without an actual grammar and typing rule.

A variable assignment into a \`Σ\`-algebra \`A\` extends uniquely to an interpretation homomorphism \`T_Σ(X) → A\`. This is the universal property **under the named algebraic assumptions**, not a theorem that every concept ever called “term” in every discipline is a free-algebra element. Typed lambda terms, higher-order logics, dependent theories and the house SEN language impose additional binding, typing, normalization or equality rules; cite the actual chosen calculus.

## Nonidentities that matter

- **Glossary term / lexical item**: a named intension or sense in a declared interpretive world. A word entry is not itself a well-typed \`Σ\`-term. See [world-indexed term sense](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/information-science-services-section/services/language-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/world-indexed-term-sense.md).
- **Token occurrence**: one spelling/mark at a particular source revision and span. It is not the whole algebraic term, its denotation or its reference. See [token occurrence](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/information-science-services-section/services/language-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/token-occurrence.md).
- **Formula or proposition**: in ordinary first-order logic a formula is built by predicates and logical connectives from terms. Other calculi may represent propositions *as types* or have terms inhabiting proposition types, so this separation must be stated under the language selected, not as universal disjointness.
- **Term in a contract**: an authored binding condition under an agreement and authority, not a mathematical constructor and not automatically a law.
- **SEN typed term**: a particular Engineering carrier with its own constructors and admission. A syntactically named term does not yet possess a content address, a witness or settled identity.

**Use rule:** specify \`Σ\`, allowed variables, typing context and the intended interpretation whenever making a mathematical assertion about a Term. For prose vocabulary and meaning across worlds, use the lexical/world-qualified glossary sense rather than assuming this definition. The old glossary mixed these senses and asserted all fields shared one algebra; that overstatement is retired, while original prose remains recoverable from Git history.
