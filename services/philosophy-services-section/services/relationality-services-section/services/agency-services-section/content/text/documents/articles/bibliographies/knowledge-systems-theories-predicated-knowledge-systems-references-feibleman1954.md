## Annotation

Feibleman formalises [@needham1937] into twelve laws governing integrative levels. The most-cited are: (L1) each level organises the level below; (L2) the higher the level, the more complex the organisation; (L5) the laws of a higher level are not deducible from the laws of the level below; (L7) for an entity at any level, its mechanism lies at the level below and its purpose at the level above; (L9) higher levels are more dependent than lower levels; (L11) time is required for organisation; (L12) failure of an organism is by collapse to a lower level.

The framing reads Feibleman's laws as *closure-regime constraints on a stratified predicate-fibration*. (L5) — non-deducibility — is the formal claim that no closure rule has higher-level predicates in its head and only lower-level predicates in its body; equivalently, there is no fiber-algebra surjection from the lower level onto the higher level. (L7) — mechanism-below-purpose-above — is the bidirectional structure of [specialize](../../../glossary/terms/specialize.md) (downward, mechanism-disclosing) and federation (upward, purpose-disclosing) morphisms. (L9) — dependency-asymmetry — is the contravariance of the predication-fibration on the level-ordering.

Feibleman is one of the canonical sources for treating *level* as a structural rather than ontological category. The framing inherits this: levels are *fibration indices*, and the choice of stratification (Feibleman's twelve laws, BFO's continuant/occurrent split, DOLCE's endurant/perdurant split) is the choice of how the predicate-category-stratification cuts the fibration.
