# Accountable closure

*Accountable closure* is the meta-discipline. An artifact is legitimate not merely when its claims are true, but when its communicative form makes the required [closures](communicative-closure.md) inspectable, repairable, and transportable.

This is the parametric concept that anchors the entire communication theory: every other concept in this register names *some shape* in which closures can be made accountable. Accountable-closure is the property the others jointly produce.

## Pattern

For every closure the artifact demands of the reader, the artifact makes three things locatable: the closure's class (so the reader knows what kind of work is being asked), the form of accountability (how an external observer could check whether the closure was performed legitimately), and the repair protocol (what the reader does if they cannot perform the closure). An artifact whose closures are inspectable, repairable, and transportable across registers exhibits accountable closure.

## Parameters

- **`closure-class`** — the inventory of closure-classes the practice admits (typically: inference, recognition, manipulation, transport, plan-reconstruction, citation-resolution, register-translation).
- **`accountability-form`** — the form by which an observer audits a closure (re-derivation, formal proof check, peer review, executable test, citation verification).
- **`repair-protocol`** — the protocol the reader follows when the closure cannot be performed (consult cited lemma, run cited example, expand cited proof obligation, fetch cited definition, request a worked instance).

## Conformance shape

Walk every demanded closure in the artifact. For each: classify (closure-class), audit (accountability-form), verify a repair-protocol exists. Tally. An artifact whose every demanded closure is classifiable, auditable, and repairable exhibits accountable closure. Defects are demanded closures that are unmarked, un-auditable, or un-repairable.

## Lineage

The accountable-closure framing is the central thesis of the [good-mathematical-communication](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/communication/math/texts/good-mathematical-communication.md) essay. It synthesizes McCloud's gutter-and-closure vocabulary, Andersen's empirical work on regulated proof gaps (*Synthese* 197, 2020), Hamami & Morris on plan reconstruction (*Noûs* 58, 2024), and Ashton on audience-relative legitimacy (*Synthese* 198, 2021).
