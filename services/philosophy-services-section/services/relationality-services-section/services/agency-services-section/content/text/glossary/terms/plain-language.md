***Plain Language*** (capital P, capital L) is the proper-noun name for the U.S. federal practice of writing public-facing documents in language ordinary readers can understand. Codified by the Plain Writing Act of 2010 (Pub. L. 111-274), it required executive-branch agencies to write covered documents in plain language, train staff in plain-language writing, and report on compliance.

Plain Language is also the international movement that produced the federal practice. In its broader sense, the movement spans government, legal, medical, and technical communication, and treats clear public-facing communication not as a stylistic preference but as a matter of access and rights.

## The federal program

The Plain Writing Act of 2010 produced a substantial public-domain corpus:

- The **Federal Plain Language Guidelines** ([@fplg2011]) — the consolidated handbook that translated decades of plain-language research into rule form. Public domain.
- **plainlanguage.gov** — the federal coordinating site, hosting guidelines, training materials, before/after examples, and agency style guides.
- The **Federal Register Document Drafting Handbook** — the rulemaking-prose style reference.
- Agency-specific style guides written under the Act's mandate (HHS, SSA, USCIS, IRS, and others).

The program was led inside government by the **Plain Language Action and Information Network (PLAIN)**, an interagency working group of federal employees who had been doing this work informally since the 1990s. Outside government, the **Center for Plain Language** carried the same work into private-sector advocacy and certification.

## Why this object exists in PTGAE

PTGAE descends from Plain Language. Most of the rules PTGAE prescribes — substitute simple words for bureaucratic ones, prefer active voice, prefer short sentences, structure by reader task, test on real readers — come straight from this tradition. The Federal Plain Language Guidelines are the immediate ancestor of [PTGAE's style guide](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/writing/_archive/library/plain-technical-general-american-english/code/structures/prose/teaching/explanations/style-guide.md).

## Provenance and intellectual debts

The federal practice synthesized work from several sources:

- [Rudolf Flesch](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/references/people/rudolf-flesch.md) developed [readability](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/services/plain-language-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/readability.md) formulas in the 1940s and argued that government and legal writing could be made accessible without losing precision [@flesch1949].
- [Janice Redish](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/references/people/janice-redish.md) extended the work from readability metrics to [usability testing](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/services/plain-language-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/usability-testing.md), contributed to the Federal Plain Language Guidelines, and treated clear government communication as a civil-rights issue [@redish2012].
- [Karen Schriver](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/references/people/karen-schriver.md)'s [document design](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/services/plain-language-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/document-design.md) research added that visual presentation shapes comprehension as much as word choice and sentence structure [@schriver1997].

International parallels include the U.K. Government Digital Service's writing guide, Australia's plain-English style manual, and the Council of Europe's plain-language guidance.

## Status

Plain Language as an active U.S. federal program is being dismantled: the federal corpus is at risk of removal, the interagency coordination has been deprecated, and ongoing maintenance has stopped. The reasons are political; the practical effect is that the public-domain materials this object names — the Guidelines, plainlanguage.gov, agency style guides — are at imminent risk of disappearance.

PTGAE absorbs Plain Language as part of its preservation work.
