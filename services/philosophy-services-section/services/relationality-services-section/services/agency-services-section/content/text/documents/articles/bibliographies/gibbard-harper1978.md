# Gibbard & Harper 1978

Gibbard, A., & Harper, W.
(1978).
Counterfactuals and Two Kinds of Expected Utility.
In C.A. Hooker, J.J. Leach, & E.F. McClennen (Eds.), *Foundations and Applications of Decision Theory*.
D.
Reidel.

The founding statement of **Causal Decision Theory**.
Gibbard and Harper distinguish *evidential* expected utility (what Jeffrey computes) from *causal* expected utility (which holds the past fixed and varies the act, severing causal arrows into the act).
They argue, against Jeffrey, that causal expected utility is the right normative target on cases like the Smoking Lesion, and they accept the consequence that CDT two-boxes on Newcomb's problem.

The paper triggers the modern decision-theoretic debate between CDT and EDT.
The debate is unresolved within the classical framework; Functional Decision Theory is one proposed exit (see [fdt-newcomb-resolution](../notes/undated/fdt-newcomb-resolution.md)).
