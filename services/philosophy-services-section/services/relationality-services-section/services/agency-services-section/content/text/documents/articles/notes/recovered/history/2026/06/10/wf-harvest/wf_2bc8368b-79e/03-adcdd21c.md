FINDINGS — ATTACK ON THE 2026-06-11 PLAN (waves 0–5), ranked by severity.

---

**1. FATAL — No organ for identity, keys, or capability authority anywhere in waves 0–5.**
Element attacked: the entire wave decomposition; Wave 4 conduits; Wave 5 mill intake.
Argument: Way 4 declares provenance-gated admission to be "the security model, the federation protocol, and the epistemology — one mechanism." But provenance gating presupposes machinery the plan never names: who verifies that `signature.by` is genuine (key management, signature verification), who decides which sources are recognized (the recognizer registry as an authority surface), and how authority itself is granted/revoked/attenuated/delegated. The old stack has all of this — `lib/web-indieauth` (PKCE, auth-code validation), `lib/memetics-capabilities` (grant/revoke/attenuate/delegate with the four-valued `can()` fold, `CAPABILITY_CONVERSE`, accountable-through-delegation), `lib/web-sync-guard`, swarm's `authz`/`auth-surface` and the agent-loop capability gate — and the plan's six waves mention none of it. Meanwhile Wave 4 mounts webmention/micropub conduits (micropub *requires* IndieAuth tokens) and Wave 5 opens the mill to "anyone's conformant wire" — public intake with the immune system unbuilt. Without keys, "no witness, no world" degrades to "no *claimed* witness, no world," which is spam's front door.
Amendment: add a named organ to Wave 1 or early Wave 3 — `lib/provenance` (or fold into the daemon's admission gates): content-addressed identity, key/signature verification, recognizer registry, and the capability algebra recast as bedrock readings over witness traces (memetics-capabilities' `can()` is already a fold-to-charge; it belongs on meme-topology natively). Make it a hard precondition for Wave 4.2 and Wave 5.1.

**2. FATAL — "One settling daemon, many mounted topologies" conflates engine-unity with process-unity, and the plan's own mathematics argues against it.**
Element attacked: the frame decision "one settling daemon, many mounted domain topologies"; Wave 1.3.
Argument: Way 7 ("one descent, many worlds") is a claim about *code* — one settling engine, no per-domain settling logic. The plan silently upgrades it to a claim about *processes*: one daemon owning one address space. Three independent objections: (a) **Regime interference.** The program map itself states "a daemon in the transfinite regime needs different scheduling than one in the strict regime." Mounting an aperiodic-regime research-archive topology and the strict-regime news topology in one single-threaded Node process means one runaway settling loop starves the live wire — the production-proof property Wave 2 depends on. (b) **Isolation.** The MHC doctrine gates effector conduits with a four-condition meet precisely because actuation is dangerous; co-locating drone actuation, public web intake, and the news wire in one address space throws away process-level isolation that the admission discipline cannot replace (a bug in the daemon bypasses every gate at once). This recreates lib/swarm's god-shape as a god-process — the disease at the operational layer. (c) **The theory already prices federation.** The proved cohomological CALM theorem (coordination-free ⟺ H¹ = 0) is literally a computable criterion for when multiple daemons need no coordination, and codecs-as-geometric-morphisms is the sync mechanism. The plan possesses a federation theory and chooses a monolith anyway, without argument.
Amendment: restate the frame as "one settling *engine*, many daemon *instances*": the daemon is a deployable organ, one per site/risk-domain, inter-daemon sync as geometric morphism with the H¹ obstruction computed (dogfooding the CALM theorem). At minimum, Wave 1.3 must specify the multi-tenancy model — per-mount scheduling, regime-aware budgets, effector isolation — before anything mounts on it.

**3. SERIOUS — The `lib/<thing>-codecs-<standard>` grain puts the codec on the wrong end of the morphism and guarantees both duplication and micro-package relapse.**
Element attacked: the naming/packaging convention; Wave 4.1.
Argument: by the plan's own mathematics, a codec is a geometric morphism between *sites*, and the standard is the site. RSS, Atom, JSON Feed, JSON-LD, microformats are thing-agnostic: news pushes into RSS, but so do the digital garden, the microblog, and the research library (the old stack proves this — `web-feed` served everyone). Naming codecs under `<thing>` privileges one end of the morphism, so either `news-codecs-rss` and `garden-codecs-rss` each reimplement the RSS site (duplication, violating Way 6: the name misstates the derivation), or one of them becomes the de-facto shared one under a misleading name. And the combinatorics are bad: news × {iptc, rss, atom, jsonfeed, jsonld, microformats} alone is six zero-dep micro-packages; the full standards inventory in the capabilities map yields 30–40 — the exact count and shape of the old web-* stack the refactor is curing. Zero-dep pure modules gain nothing from package boundaries.
Amendment: package per *standard-site family*, modules within: `lib/codecs-syndication` (RSS/Atom/JSON Feed), `lib/codecs-iptc`, `lib/codecs-linked-data`, each exporting the site definition plus pullback/pushforward pairs parameterized by domain topology. Split only when a codec acquires a heavyweight dependency or an independent release consumer. Keep `news-codecs-iptc` as exemplar only if IPTC genuinely is news-specific (it is — that one survives; the convention generalized from it does not).

**4. SERIOUS — The wave order drains only the news closure; the old kernel survives indefinitely under everything else, so the refactor's stated end-state ("@561-group/meme becomes unnecessary and removed") is unreachable by this plan.**
Element attacked: waves 2–5 as a whole; the OS-from-first-principles arc.
Argument: waves 2–5 are the news vertical wearing four costumes (parity, choke points, codecs, mill). Terminal-interface, the language stack, ledger, fizzos/farstone, posix-holography, the editor — the organs of the stated OS aspiration — have no wave, and all of them stand on the old kernel chain. The kernel cannot be deleted until its *last* dependent moves; this plan moves ~54 of ~75 packages' worth of dependency surface and leaves two kernels coexisting indefinitely — precisely the two-stacks-in-parallel state the 06-10 correction ("packages do not have futures") was meant to end. Compounding it: Wave 3.2, the single highest-leverage seam in the entire plan (web-server → ~25 packages), gets one sentence, no design, and no parity oracle (the wire is the oracle for newsroom settling; nothing is named as the oracle for requests-as-traces semantics).
Amendment: either add an explicit Wave 6 ("drain the kernel: remaining dependents enumerated, ordered, each with its oracle, ending in deletion of meme/MPU/MMU/memetic-topology/memetics") or state in the plan that the OS arc is deferred and the two-kernel state is accepted for N months — but decide it, don't default into it. Promote Wave 3.2 to its own design document with a named oracle before Wave 3 starts.

**5. SERIOUS — Wave 3.1 bypasses the swarm god-package for one site; it does not sever it, and the unrouted ~60 swarm modules include the project's most important safety machinery.**
Element attacked: Wave 3.1 ("severs the 42-dependency god edge; collapses the site's closure to ~15").
Argument: extracting newsroom's small observer/trace/study slice fixes rete-agentium-filum's closure — and leaves lib/swarm intact for every other consumer, with its lib→service inversion, its 42 deps, and its module zoo (agent-loop, stigmergy, A2A, MCP client/server, cognition, plus ~60 named modules). "Severed" is the wrong verb; "routed around" is what Wave 3.1 does. Worse, the unplanned remainder contains the governed tool-call chain (`runGovernedToolCall`'s gate sequence, contested-capability halting via `AgentLoopStop="contested-tool"`) — the canonical admission-discipline implementation that Wave 4.2's MHC pattern and any future effector conduit will need. The capability-parity doctrine (06-10) is violated by a plan that inventories capabilities and then routes only the news-relevant fraction.
Amendment: Wave 3.1 should name the fate of the swarm remainder: which modules become bedrock organs (agent-loop governance → the daemon's admission layer; stigmergy → already theorized as the admit morphism), which are capability-parity debt for a later wave, and which die. Otherwise correct the claim: the god edge is bypassed, not severed.

**6. SERIOUS — The FOUR-as-shadow demand is doctrine in one document and absent from Wave 1's deliverable list, which is the only place it can be enforced.**
Element attacked: Wave 1.1's enumeration ("API boundary + docs, audit fixes, site-morphism carrier, incremental settling").
Argument: the cosmogony document states the bedrock demand plainly — general residuated-lattice charge as primary carrier, FOUR as named finite projection, "or at minimum the API shaped so that generalizing the value category later is a widening, not a rewrite" — and explicitly says to add it "to the bedrock demands alongside the site-morphism carrier and incremental settling." Wave 1.1 lists the other two and omits this one. Wave 1 is the freeze point: the developer-surface workflow is *right now* designing the API boundary that waves 2–5 consume. If that boundary hardens around `bilattice-charge.mts`'s FOUR, the q-enriched mathematics (Lawvere quantales, fuzzy adjunctions, the non-Boolean apex tower — the program's entire research frontier) becomes a rewrite of every downstream consumer, violating Way 5 in the one place it most matters.
Amendment: add to Wave 1.1's acceptance criteria: charge-generic API signatures (value category as parameter), FOUR as the named default instantiation, and an audit item verifying no public surface assumes four values.

**7. SERIOUS — The daemon has no persistence, recovery, or growth story, and Way 2's "forgetting has an explicit price" is never priced.**
Element attacked: Wave 1.3 ("at rest it is the topology on disk; running, it is the flow").
Argument: that sentence is the entire durability design. Incremental settling (Wave 1.1) presumes a resumable witness stream — resumable from what medium, with what crash-recovery semantics, what admission ordering under concurrent writers (settling ordinal vs. arrival), what checkpoint format? The old stack answers these badly but at least answers them (MMU, services/memetics trace loop, swarm flatfile logs, posix-holography); the plan vacates them without replacement. And append-only-forever has a cost curve: a daemon serving news + the 83k-file archive + swarm observers grows its witness stream without bound. The doctrine permits forgetting at an explicit Landauer price — but no wave designs the snapshot/compaction/cold-trace mechanism that would charge it. The serialization line ("in concert with meme-topology-addressing") covers identity, not durability.
Amendment: add to Wave 1.3's deliverables: the trace store carrier (append, checkpoint, resume), crash-recovery semantics stated as a theorem-shaped invariant (recovered settling ≡ unbroken settling), and a priced-forgetting design even if unimplemented.

**8. MINOR — "Verify the wire publishes identically" is the wrong parity criterion and will pressure the bedrock into bug-compatibility.**
Element attacked: Wave 2.1.
Argument: newsroom's four hand-rolled functions are, by the project's own diagnosis, an *approximation* of settling written before the upstream existed. If bedrock settling differs on some contested cell, the difference is at least as likely an old-code bug as a new-code bug — but an "identical output" gate mechanically rules in favor of the prototype, the one artifact the 06-10 correction said is "an oracle, then gone," not a normative spec.
Amendment: parity-with-adjudication: diffs are triaged against the mathematics (which fixed point is the correct one is decidable from the rules), with old-bug diffs recorded as expected deviations rather than failures.

**9. MINOR — The governance questions block Wave 1, not Wave 2.**
Element attacked: "Governance questions… needed before Wave 2 ships."
Argument: Wave 1 *creates* the workbench app and the settling-daemon service — both need a scope (@emsenn vs @561-group) and a directory the moment they exist, and the scope decision is in every import line of every later wave. Deferring to "before Wave 2" guarantees either a stall inside Wave 1 or a rename churn across the whole closure.
Amendment: resequence as "needed before Wave 1's app/service packages are created."

**10. MINOR — The workbench app violates the plan's own wave discipline: nothing in production proves it.**
Element attacked: Wave 1.2; the rule "each wave is provable in production."
Argument: Wave 1.3 is proved by the wire (via Wave 2.1); Wave 1.1 by the daemon and tests. The workbench has no named consumer, no oracle, and is assembled from "embryos" (program-processor morphism language, terminal-interface) — exactly the kind of speculative organ the wave discipline exists to forbid. It risks becoming the new stack's first drift artifact.
Amendment: give it an acceptance task that the plan already needs done: the Wave 2.2 archive status-resettling performed *through* the workbench interactively, making the archive dogfood the workbench's proof.
