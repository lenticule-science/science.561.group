# Learn Semiotics Basics

## What you will be able to do

- Given a concrete [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/sign.md) situation (a word, a smoke signal, a photograph), identify the [representamen](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/representamen.md) (the sign vehicle), the object (what it refers to), and the [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) (the meaning it produces).
- Classify a sign as [icon](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/icon.md) (resembles its object), index (causally connected to its object), or [symbol](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/symbol.md) (connected by convention).
- Describe [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/semiosis.md) — the process by which [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/sign.md) produce meaning through chains of interpretation — and explain why it does not terminate at a fixed endpoint.
- Explain the difference between [Charles Sanders Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/general/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)'s triadic model and Ferdinand de Saussure's dyadic model ([signifier](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signifier.md)/[signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md)).

## Prerequisites

None. This skill is a starting point. No mathematical background is required.

## Lessons

- [Signs and Interpretants](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/signs-and-interpretants.md) — the sign as a triadic relation, [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/general/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) vs Saussure, types of [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/sign.md)
- [Semiosis and Sign Processes](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiotic-relations.md) — how [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/sign.md) generate meaning through interpretation, unlimited [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/semiosis.md), the role of habit and context

These two lessons are sequential: start with Signs and Interpretants, then Semiosis and Sign Processes.

## Scope

This skill covers the foundations of Western [semiotic](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/index.md) theory as developed by [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/general/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) and Saussure. It does not cover:

- Indigenous or non-European sign theories (acknowledged gap — these traditions have their own systematic accounts of [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/sign.md) and meaning that are not represented here)
- Formal or mathematical aspects of [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/terms/sign.md) (covered by **learn-[semiotic](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/index.md)-universe**, which depends on this skill plus mathematical prerequisites)
- [Biosemiotics](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/schools/biosemiotics.md), computational [semiotics](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/index.md), or [semiotic](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/content/semiotics/content/index.md) applications in specific fields

## Verification

After completing both lessons, work through the self-check exercises in each. You should be able to take any everyday sign — a stop sign, a fever, the word "tree" — and identify its components and classify it without consulting the definitions.
