## Annotation

Dahlberg's referent-oriented concept theory: a *concept* is a triple (referent, characteristics, designation), where the referent is the object the concept is *about*, the characteristics are the attributes asserted of the referent that constitute the concept's intension, and the designation is the term used to name the concept. Crucially, designation is *not* identified with the concept; two designations can name the same concept and one designation can name two distinct concepts in two contexts.

The predicated framing reads Dahlberg's triple directly into the [predication-cell](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/predication-cell.md) substrate: the referent is the cell's *subject*, the characteristics are the cells whose subject is the referent (an object's *aboutness*-bundle, recovered by [aboutness](../../../glossary/concepts/aboutness.md)), and the designation is a name in the predicate vocabulary attached to the referent by a *naming predicate*. The naming-vs-concept separation Dahlberg insists on is exactly what the substrate provides by treating names as *just another predicate* applied to the referent — there is no privileged identity-naming relation.

Dahlberg is one of the canonical authorities for *terminology science* as distinct from lexicography or classification. The framing inherits her commitment that vocabulary management (which terms designate which concepts in which discourse community) is itself a knowledge-organisation activity — and is therefore handled algebraically by the framing's [federate](../../../glossary/terms/federate.md) morphism rather than relegated to an editorial layer.
