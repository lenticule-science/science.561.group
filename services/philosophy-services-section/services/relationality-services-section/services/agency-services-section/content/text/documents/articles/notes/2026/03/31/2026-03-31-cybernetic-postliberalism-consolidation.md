**Work type:** Derivation (fixed method — trace lineage — unpredictable output)

The `_index.md` and `overview.md` already narrate this synthesis.
But the genealogy is not yet mapped as a structured document showing: which concepts come from which traditions, what the original theorist's claim was, and what cybernetic postliberalism does with it (extends, synthesizes, reframes).
The triage files contain additional genealogical threads not yet in the library: the Christian inheritance of liberal subjectivity, the covenant-to-platform genealogy, the kyriarchist genring lineage.

Read: - All 10 existing term files to extract their cited lineages - Triage files: `on-the-inherited-christianity-of-liberal-subjectivity.md`, `covenant-platform-a-model-of-christian-charity-as-the-first-social-media.md`, `a-history-of-liberal-ontologizing-as-kyriarchist-genring.md`, `history-of-californication.md` - Babble: `2025-08-29-1912h-on-the-lineage-of-californication.md` - Letters: `2025-04-07-the-theory-that-survived-the-war.md`

**Tasks:** 1.

### Step 3: Articulate the methodology

**Work type:** Inquiry (the method is practiced but not yet explicitly stated)

**Context brief:** Cybernetic postliberalism makes claims.
But what *kind* of claims?
What counts as evidence?
What would falsification look like?
The framework is described as "analytical, not prescriptive" — but that's what it *isn't*.
The methodology — what it *is* and how it validates — is implicit in the deployed analyses but never stated as such.

From the existing material, the method appears to involve: - Identifying a feedback loop (cybernetic analysis) - Showing how structural contradiction is formatted as personal affect (the californication move) - Tracing the rhetorical grammar that sustains the formatting (fascist grammar analysis) - Demonstrating that the subject's response stabilizes rather than disrupts the system (governance analysis)

Read: - `measuring-ideologies-by-statistical-prerequisites.md` (library text) - `cybernetic-governance.md` (library text) - The deployed case studies: `describing-fascism.md`, `subjects-under-californication.md`, `postliberal-grammar.md`

**Tasks:** 1.
Read all source material listed above 2.
Produce `operations/science/research/cybernetic-postliberalism/methodology.md` — an explicit statement of: what kind of claims the framework makes, what evidence looks like, what the analytical procedure is, and what standards a claim must meet 3.
Note: the `describing-liberal-subjectivity-as-informational-malfunction.md` triage file belongs to neuroreflexivity (medicine), not CP — do not include it here

**Depends on:** Step 1 **Verify:** The methodology, when applied to an existing case study, would produce the analysis that was actually written.
If it wouldn't, the methodology statement is wrong. **If unclear:** This step is likely to produce questions for the user about what they consider the framework's epistemic commitments.
Ask rather than assume.

The letters deploy vocabulary that may or may not match the formalized terms.
This step produces a complete inventory: every concept the framework uses, whether it has a formal term definition, and where it's defined or used.

**Tasks:** 1.
List all 10 existing terms with one-line definitions 2.

Check whether any of these are already defined in upstream domains (affect theory, cybernetics, governance, etc.) 4.
Produce `operations/science/research/cybernetic-postliberalism/vocabulary-inventory.md` — complete list with status (formalized / used-but-unformalised / upstream-domain / needs-definition) 5.
For each gap, note which source file contains the richest treatment

**Depends on:** nothing (can run in parallel with Steps 2-3) **Verify:** Every technical term used in the existing texts and triage files appears in the inventory **If unclear:** When a word is used technically in one place but colloquially elsewhere, flag it — the user decides whether it's vocabulary

### Step 5: Extract and state the framework's claims

**Work type:** Derivation (apply the methodology from Step 3 to identify what the framework actually asserts)

**Context brief:** The framework makes claims — about how liberal governance works, about what platforms do, about the structure of subjectivity.
These claims are currently embedded in narrative texts and case studies.
This step extracts them and states them as propositions precise enough to evaluate.

Examples of claims visible in existing material: - Liberal governance reproduces itself through cybernetic feedback loops, not through ideology or coercion - Californication is the mechanism: structural contradiction → personal affect → self-regulation → system stability - Fascist grammar is a six-step rhetorical structure shared by fascism, liberalism, and postliberalism - Platforms industrialized affect governance at scale, closing the circuit described by Foucault/Berlant/Hochschild/cybernetics/Povinelli - The subject produced is the savior-slave: responsible for crises they cannot structurally transform

**Tasks:** 1.
Read all 16 library texts and the overview 2.
Extract every assertion that functions as a claim of the framework (not a definition, not an example — a claim about how something works) 3.
State each claim as a proposition 4.
Group claims by type: structural claims (how the system works), genealogical claims (where something comes from), subjective claims (what subjects experience/do), methodological claims (how to analyze) 5.
Produce `operations/science/research/cybernetic-postliberalism/claims.md`

**Depends on:** Step 3 (methodology needed to classify claims correctly) **Verify:** Each claim can be traced back to at least one source text.
No major argument in any text is left without a corresponding claim. **If unclear:** When a text makes an assertion that seems like a one-off observation rather than a framework claim, flag it for the user

### Step 6: Identify what must be proved and to what standard

**Work type:** Inquiry (this is the core intellectual work of the project)

**Context brief:** Given the claims (Step 5) and the methodology (Step 3), which claims are theorems — things the framework asserts but has not yet demonstrated to the standard its own method requires?
Which are axioms (taken as given)?
Which are already demonstrated (the case studies constitute evidence)?

This step is where the project becomes a research program rather than just a collection.

**Tasks:** 1.
For each claim from Step 5, classify: axiom (assumed), demonstrated (case study evidence exists), or theorem (asserted but not yet proved to standard) 2.
For theorems: state what would constitute proof within the methodology 3.
For demonstrated claims: assess whether the existing case studies actually meet the methodology's standard, or whether they're illustrations rather than demonstrations 4.
Produce `operations/science/research/cybernetic-postliberalism/research-agenda.md` — the theorems to prove, ordered by dependency (which must be established before others can be)

**Depends on:** Steps 3, 5 **Verify:** Every theorem has a stated proof standard.
The dependency ordering is consistent (no circular dependencies unless explicitly flagged as such). **If unclear:** The distinction between "illustrated" and "demonstrated" may require the user's judgment about epistemic standards
