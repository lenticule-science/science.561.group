This is the framework's central diagnostic procedure: given a candidate artifact, find the closures it is asking the reader to perform that the reader has no principled way to perform. The procedure is the [gutter audit](guides-how-to-classify-the-gutters-of-an-artifact.md) followed by a more demanding test on the gaps the audit flagged as suspect.

## The audit, recapped

The gutter audit produces five categories: self-evident, disciplinary, acceptable-but-unflagged, audience-drift, and unaccountable. The first three are not defects. The last two require attention. This procedure focuses on the unaccountable category and on the suspicious cases that landed in audience-drift but might really be unaccountable.

## The procedural test

For each candidate unaccountable closure:

**Step 1: Identify the closure.** Restate explicitly what the artifact is asking the reader to do at this gutter. "From the previous statement to this one, the reader must conclude X." If you cannot restate the closure in a single sentence, the closure is at minimum poorly framed; consider it suspect.

**Step 2: Identify the apparatus the closure would need.** What would have to be true of the reader for them to perform the closure correctly? Specific theorems they have absorbed. Specific patterns they have seen. Specific computational facility. Specific contextual knowledge of the artifact's domain.

**Step 3: Compare to the declared audience.** Does the declared audience have what Step 2 names? If yes, the closure is acceptable for that audience and the audit was wrong to flag it. If no, the closure is unaccountable for the declared audience.

**Step 4: Check whether the artifact licenses the apparatus.** Even if the audience does not have the apparatus, the artifact may have provided it: a citation that the reader can follow, an earlier section that established the apparatus, an explicit "see also" that points at the resource. If the license is present and reachable, the closure is disciplinary, not unaccountable. If the license is absent or unreachable, the closure is unaccountable.

**Step 5: Run the convergent-completion check, where possible.** For the highest-stakes closures, give the artifact to two or three qualified members of the declared audience and ask each of them, independently, to perform the closure. If they converge, the closure is in fact acceptable and the audit was overconservative. If they diverge, the closure is unaccountable in fact, even if the audit had judged it acceptable in principle.

## The repairs

Each unaccountable closure has four possible repairs:

- **Cite the apparatus.** If the closure relies on a named result, theorem, technique, or convention, cite it. The closure becomes disciplinary.
- **Expand the step.** Write out the closure work explicitly in the artifact. The closure becomes self-evident.
- **Narrow the audience claim.** Acknowledge that this artifact is not for the broader audience the framing claimed; redeclare it for the audience whose training closes the gap.
- **Drop the closure.** Restructure the artifact so the closure is not required. Sometimes the reader was being asked to fill in a gap that did not need to be there, and the artifact reads better without it.

The wrong repair is to add a disclaimer that some readers may find this section challenging. The disclaimer transfers the cost from the writer to the reader without paying it down.

## Why this is the central diagnostic

The framework's whole positive program is, ultimately, the elimination of unaccountable closures from the practices it studies. The terms the framework introduces ([prestige-icon](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/prestige-icon.md), [frame-cross-dressing](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/frame-cross-dressing.md), [medium-imitation](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/medium-imitation.md), [audience-drift](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/audience-drift.md)) name the principal *routes* by which unaccountable closures get into artifacts. The concepts ([accountable-closure](../../../../glossary/concepts/accountable-closure.md), [convergent-completion](../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/convergent-completion.md)) name the positive criteria. This procedure is the operational instrument that connects the diagnosis to the repair. If you do nothing else with the framework, do this.
