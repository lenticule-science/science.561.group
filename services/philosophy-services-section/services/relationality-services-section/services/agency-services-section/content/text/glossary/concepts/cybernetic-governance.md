**Cybernetic governance** models governing as a coupled system of observation, regulation and communication. It asks which actors can detect a disturbance, what responses they are authorized and equipped to make, how feedback changes subsequent behavior, and how the intended outcome is independently observed. It does not mean that every system self-stabilizes or that command and accountability disappear.

External sources are W. Ross Ashby's *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (1956) on requisite variety and Stafford Beer's *The Heart of Enterprise* (1979) on organizational viability. The 561 Group's [single source-owned analysis](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/03/06/2026-03-06-cybernetic-governance-research.md) treats the political analogy and observable tests as **research**, not proven rules for a live enterprise. A told achievement differs from an actual change and independent receiver acceptance.
