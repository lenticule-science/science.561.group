## Source

M.E. Bratman. *Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason*.
Harvard University Press, 1987.

## Form

Monograph.
The philosophical upstream of the *belief-desire-intention* agent architecture and the load-bearing analysis of *intention as a distinct mental state* with its own functional role in practical reasoning.

## Content summary

Argues that *intention* is not reducible to *belief plus desire* — an agent can desire to do $A$ and believe that doing $A$ is possible without intending to do $A$.
Intention plays a distinct functional role: it (i) settles what one is committed to doing, foreclosing further deliberation about alternatives; (ii) is partial — an intention to do $A$ leaves the means and the time of $A$ open for further planning; (iii) is *conduct-controlling* — it directs further reasoning and action toward $A$'s realization rather than merely rationalizing $A$ post hoc; (iv) is subject to *consistency* and *coherence* norms that beliefs and desires alone are not.
Bratman develops the *planning theory of intention*: agents form *plans* (partial structures of intentions) over time; the plans serve as inputs to further deliberation, providing background commitments that limit what new options are reconsidered.
The analysis is the philosophical foundation that the BDI architecture ([rao-georgeff1995](../rao-georgeff1995/rao-georgeff1995.md)) and the practical-reasoning agent literature build on.
