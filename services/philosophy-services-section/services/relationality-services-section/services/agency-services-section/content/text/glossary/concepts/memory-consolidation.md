# Memory consolidation

**Memory consolidation** is the process by which short-term
memories are transformed into stable long-term storage. The
classical neuroscience picture (McClelland-McNaughton-O'Reilly
1995) has the *hippocampus* rapidly encoding episodic traces
and the *neocortex* slowly integrating those traces into
semantic structure through repeated replay.

## Why consolidation matters

Without consolidation, all storage would be either short-term
(fast to encode, fast to forget) or slow-cortical (slow to
encode, durable). Consolidation provides the bridge: events
captured fast in episodic form are gradually re-presented to
slower-learning systems for integration into active
representation.

## The McClelland-McNaughton-O'Reilly account

Two complementary learning systems:

- **Hippocampus** — fast, episodic, sparse, context-rich,
  prone to interference. Suitable for one-shot encoding of
  individual events.
- **Neocortex** — slow, semantic, distributed, generalizing,
  catastrophic-interference-prone if learned too fast.
  Suitable for extracting regularities across many events.

Consolidation is the iterative replay of hippocampal traces to
the neocortex, allowing the neocortex to incorporate the new
experience without overwriting prior knowledge. This addresses
the *stability-plasticity dilemma*: how to learn new things
without forgetting old ones.

## Computational analogs

The McClelland-et-al. account has direct analogs in AI:

- **Experience replay** in deep RL: store experiences in a
  replay buffer (the "hippocampus"); periodically replay
  them to the value network (the "neocortex"). This was
  introduced precisely to manage the stability-plasticity
  dilemma in deep Q-learning.
- **Continual learning** algorithms (Elastic Weight
  Consolidation, Progressive Networks, generative replay)
  attempt to consolidate new tasks without forgetting old
  ones.
- **In LLM agents**: there is no in-place consolidation
  mechanism. New facts encountered in conversation do not
  enter the model's weights. Consolidation, if it happens,
  is by deliberate fine-tuning on accumulated logs — a slow,
  external process.

## Open questions

- Whether consolidation requires explicit replay, or whether
  generative replay (replaying *imagined* rather than stored
  experiences) suffices. Empirically, generative replay can
  work but with quality bounded by the generator.
- Whether consolidation is necessary at all in agents with
  effectively unlimited storage. The answer turns on whether
  the storage is *retrievable*: an unconsolidated store may
  contain everything but retrieve nothing useful.
- Whether the absence of consolidation in current LLM agents
  is a fundamental limitation or a missing component
  awaitable in future architectures.

## Connection to other memory phenomena

Consolidation is the substrate of the *spacing effect*
(distributed practice beats massed practice), of the *sleep
effect* (consolidation accelerates during sleep, possibly
through generative replay), and of the *generation effect*
(memories generated by the agent consolidate better than
memories merely received).
