The signifier (*signifiant*) is one half of [Saussure](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/ferdinand-de-saussure.md)&rsquo;s dyadic model of the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md). It is the form of the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) — the sound-image, the written mark, the gesture — as distinct from the concept it evokes (the [signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md)).

Saussure insisted that the signifier is not the physical sound itself but the mental impression of it — what he called the &ldquo;sound-image.&rdquo; The relationship between signifier and [signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md) is arbitrary: there is no natural connection between the sound /tri:/ and the concept of a tree. The pairing is conventional and differs across languages [@saussure_CourseGeneralLinguistics_1916].

In [Peirce&rsquo;s](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) triadic model, the roughly corresponding concept is the [representamen](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/representamen.md) — the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) vehicle. But the correspondence is imperfect: [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)&rsquo;s [representamen](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/representamen.md) participates in a three-place relation (with object and [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md)), while Saussure&rsquo;s signifier participates in a two-place relation (with [signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md) only).
