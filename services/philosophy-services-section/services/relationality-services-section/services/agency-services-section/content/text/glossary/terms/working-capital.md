Working capital is current assets (cash, [accounts receivable](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-receivable.md), inventory) minus current liabilities ([accounts payable](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-payable.md), short-term debt, accrued expenses).
It represents the money available to fund daily operations after covering immediate obligations.

Positive working capital means the business can pay its near-term bills.
Negative working capital means current liabilities exceed current assets — the business may struggle to cover payroll, rent, or supplier invoices without additional financing.

For a new business, working capital is funded by the initial investment and should be explicitly allocated in the [use of funds](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/financing/content/working-capital/use-of-funds.md).
The amount needed depends on the gap between when costs are incurred (before opening and during the ramp-up period) and when revenue covers those costs.
A common mistake in [financial projections](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/financing/content/working-capital/financial-projections.md) is underestimating the working capital required to survive the first months of operation.
