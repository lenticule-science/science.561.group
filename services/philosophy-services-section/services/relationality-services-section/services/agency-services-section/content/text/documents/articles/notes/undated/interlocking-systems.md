# interlocking-systems

Interlocking-systems is the framing the Combahee River Collective introduced in its 1977 statement: "the major systems of oppression are interlocking.
The synthesis of these oppressions creates the conditions of our lives."
Written by a group of Black feminist and lesbian socialists in Boston, the statement insisted that the position of Black women could not be understood by treating race, class, gender, and sexuality as separate problems to be addressed one at a time.
The single word "interlocking" carries the analytical weight that everything downstream — Patricia Hill Collins's matrix of domination, Kimberlé Crenshaw's intersectionality — would go on to develop.
Generated by: domination, in its interlocking mode — the recognition that its several axes condition one another in operation rather than running in parallel.

The framing was defined against two alternatives that were available in 1977 and remain available now.
The first is the additive view: race, class, gender, and sexuality are separate systems whose effects on a given person can be summed, so that a Black working-class lesbian supposedly experiences race oppression plus class oppression plus gender oppression plus sexual-orientation oppression, each independent of the others.
Combahee refused this — the systems do not operate independently, and their effects are not summable.
The second is the hierarchical view: one system is primary and the rest derivative.
Class-first Marxism, race-first nationalism, and gender-first radical feminism each take this form, picking one axis as fundamental and treating the others as secondary.
Combahee refused this too: no axis is primary, because the systems are interlocked.

The positive content of "interlocking" is that the systems condition one another in their very operation.
Capitalism in the United States works through racial differentiation; patriarchy works through racial and class structures that distribute its effects unevenly; heterosexual normativity works through racial, class, and gender categories that decide which families and which sexualities count as legitimate.
The systems are not separate analytical objects whose interactions can be modeled after the fact — they are mutually constitutive, each partly made of the others.
This is why a politics that addresses one in isolation tends to leave the rest intact, and often reinforces them.

The framing has had a long downstream life within this library and beyond it.
Collins's matrix of domination is its sociological systematization; Crenshaw's intersectionality is its translation into legal theory; bell hooks's compound phrase "imperialist white-supremacist capitalist patriarchy" is the same framing in essay register, the modifiers deliberately concatenated to block any move that would set one of them aside as secondary.
Interlocking-systems is, in this library, the hinge concept of the whole interlocking branch of domination — the upstream insight from which the more formal tools of intersectional and matrix analysis are derived, and the reason race, gender, class, age, and colony are read here as a single weave rather than a stack of independent variables.
