## Annotation

A comparative ethnography of two laboratory sciences — high-energy physics (CERN) and molecular biology (a Max Planck institute) — arguing that even within the natural sciences there are *radically different epistemic cultures*: how knowledge is made, what counts as evidence, who is authorised to assert, how disagreement is resolved, what relation the experimenter has to the object of study. Knorr Cetina shows the physics culture is *negative* (knowledge by elimination, large collective subject, austere relation to the object) while the molecular biology culture is *positive* (knowledge by accumulation, individual researcher subject, intimate relation to the bench).

For the predicated framing, Knorr Cetina is the strongest empirical evidence that *fibers cannot be unified by appeal to a common scientific method*. The two cultures are *both* legitimate, *both* producing genuine knowledge, and *neither* reducible to the other — yet they share many predicates (proton, gene, measurement, evidence) whose meaning differs across the cultures. The framing recognises this as: the predicate vocabulary is *partially shared* across fibers, but the closure-rule signature is *culture-specific*, and federation across cultures requires explicit translation rules rather than implicit lifting.

Knorr Cetina's work is the load-bearing reference for the [discipline-vs-school-as-fibers](../learning-articles/questions/discipline-vs-school-as-fibers.md) question: even within a single discipline (physics, biology) there are sub-cultures with sufficiently distinct epistemic protocols that they should be treated as separate fibers. The fibration index is therefore *finer than discipline* — it must accommodate the cultural fragmentation Knorr Cetina documents. This is the empirical impetus for the *three-fiber-index proposal* (discipline ⊗ school ⊗ project) the question proposes.
