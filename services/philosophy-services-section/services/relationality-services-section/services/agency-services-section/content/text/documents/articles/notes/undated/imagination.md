# Imagination

**Imagination** is the morphism

$$
\text{(world model, seed state, policy)} \;\longrightarrow\;
\text{(synthetic trajectory)}
$$

taking a world model, a starting state, and a policy, and producing
an [imagination-rollout](imagination-rollout.md) — a
synthetic trajectory generated by chaining the model's predictions.

## The procedure

```
imagine(model, s0, policy, H):
  trajectory = [s0]
  for t in 0 .. H-1:
    a_t = policy(s_t)
    s_{t+1} = model.transition(s_t, a_t)
    r_t    = model.reward(s_t, a_t)
    trajectory.append((a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}))
  return trajectory
```

Two variants:

- **Deterministic.** Take the mean prediction at each step.
  Cheap, but underestimates uncertainty and produces a single
  trajectory.
- **Stochastic.** Sample from the predictive distribution at each
  step. More expensive (need many samples for a good estimate)
  but represents uncertainty correctly and supports distributional
  RL.

## Where imagination is performed

In the **observation space.** Roll out by predicting next
observations. Used in pixel-prediction world models. Suffers from
expensive forward passes (rendering pixels at every step) and
accumulated visual artifact.

In the **latent space.** Roll out by predicting next latent
states; decode to observations only when needed (often never
during training). The Dreamer pattern. Cheap forward passes —
typically a single transformer layer or RSSM step per imagined
timestep.

## What imagination buys

The economy of model-based RL is built on imagination being
cheap relative to environment interaction:

| Operation | Cost |
|---|---|
| One real environment step | Real-world latency, possibly seconds, rarely parallelizable |
| One imagined latent step | One forward pass through a small network, microseconds, trivially batched |

A typical Dreamer training loop processes thousands of imagined
trajectories per real environment step. The policy improvement
that drives behavior comes overwhelmingly from imagination, not
real interaction.

## What it costs

Every imagined step is also an opportunity for model error to
accumulate. The
[model-error-compounds](model-error-compounds.md)
theorem bites: imagination of length $H$ has error roughly
$\epsilon \cdot H$ to $\epsilon \cdot e^{LH}$ depending on the
dynamics. Practical systems mitigate by:

- Capping $H$ at modest values (15–50 in Dreamer).
- Re-grounding from many real seed states rather than one.
- Penalizing the policy for entering high-uncertainty regions
  (trust regions, ensemble disagreement).
