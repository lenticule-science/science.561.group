# Composability with predicated knowledge systems

The structural framing this theory commits to is *composable* with the [predicated-knowledge-systems](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/communication/archive/library/linguistics/library/information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems) substrate at three load-bearing places. This narrative names the three bridges, argues for their faithfulness, and records the open work each bridge leaves for follow-up.

The bridges are the discipline's load-bearing self-application: predication is a special case of sign-relation; closure under inference is a special case of interpretant chain; discourse community is a special case of semiosphere. The substrate was named first in the corpus's actual order of construction; the semiotic framing now grounds the substrate's vocabulary in the older and broader discipline that supplies it.

## Bridge 1 — Sign-vehicle ↔ predication-cell predicate

The first bridge places the [sign-vehicle](../../../../glossary/concepts/sign-vehicle.md) (Peirce's representamen, Saussure's signifier) at the *predicate position* of the substrate's predication-cell $(s, p, o)$. The bridge's faithfulness rests on three structural facts:

1. *The predicate does the relating.* In a cell $(s, p, o)$, $p$ is what makes $s$ stand for $o$ (or stand to $o$). The sign-vehicle in a sign-relation $(V, O, I)$ is what makes $V$ stand for $O$ for $I$. The structural role is the same: doing-the-relating between the two non-relator positions.
2. *The predicate is from a structured vocabulary.* Substrate predicates live in a [predicate category](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/communication/archive/library/linguistics/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predicate-category.md) with morphisms (specialisation, inversion, composition, fusion). Sign-vehicles live in the [code](../../../../glossary/concepts/code.md)'s structured inventory with paradigmatic and syntagmatic relations. The structural role is the same: living in a relational structure that constrains substitution.
3. *The predicate is what the cell is "by".* A cell is *by* its predicate — the predicate is the cell's structural identity. A sign-relation is *by* its sign-vehicle — the sign-vehicle is the relation's perceptible identity. The structural role is the same: identifying the relation by what does the relating.

The bridge's two non-relator positions: the substrate's $s$ and $o$ play the role of the [sign-object](../../../../glossary/concepts/sign-object.md) jointly. Peirce's distinction between immediate and dynamic objects maps to the substrate's distinction between the cell's *as-asserted* content and its convergent settling under closure.

## Bridge 2 — Interpretant chain ↔ closure operator

The second bridge places the [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) chain (each interpretant becoming the sign-vehicle of a further sign-relation) at the substrate's [closure operator](../../../../glossary/concepts/closure-as-fitting-fixpoint.md) iterating over a starting cell. The bridge's faithfulness rests on two structural facts:

1. *Each iterate generates further structure from the same source.* The closure operator's $T^n(v)$ produces the cells derivable in $n$ steps from the asserted cell at $v$. The interpretant chain produces the further interpretants reachable in $n$ steps from the original sign-relation. The structural role is the same: bounded-step reachability under a structured derivation.
2. *The chain converges to a fixpoint determined by the operator's regime.* Per [unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint](../../../../glossary/concepts/unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint.md), the chain converges to the bilattice-$\leq_k$-least fixpoint, with regime determined by the closure-rule structure. The substrate-level [closure-as-fitting-fixpoint](../../../../glossary/concepts/closure-as-fitting-fixpoint.md) gives the same fixpoint construction.

The bridge places Eco's encyclopedia bound at the *Scott-continuous* regime and Peirce's open chain at the *transfinite-bounded* regime; the bridge does not collapse one into the other.

## Bridge 3 — Semiosphere ↔ discourse-community fiber

The third bridge places the [semiosphere](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/semiosphere.md) (Lotman's bounded cultural-historical region) at the substrate's [discourse-community-as-fiber](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/communication/archive/library/linguistics/library/information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/concepts/discourse-community-as-fiber.md). The bridge's faithfulness rests on three structural facts:

1. *Both have a boundary regulated by translation.* The semiosphere's boundary regulates what enters and leaves via [translation](../../../../glossary/terms/translation.md); the substrate-fiber's boundary regulates what crosses via [federate](../../../../glossary/terms/federate.md). The structural role is the same: bounded region with explicit cross-region operation.
2. *Both have core-periphery asymmetry.* Lotman's core (stable, prestigious) and periphery (productive, unstable) correspond to the substrate-fiber's *closure-density gradient*: cells near the fiber's core are settled under many closure rules; cells near the periphery are settled under few. The structural role is the same: graded internal structure governing how new content arises and migrates.
3. *Both accumulate memory.* The semiosphere's accumulated sign-relations are the substrate-fiber's accumulated cells. New interpretation draws on accumulated memory in both; the structural role is the same.

## Bridge 4 — Aboutness (parallel concept on both sides)

The discipline keeps [aboutness](../../../../glossary/concepts/aboutness.md) as a *parallel concept on both sides of the bridge*: the semiotic-side aboutness and the substrate-side [aboutness](../../../../glossary/concepts/aboutness.md) are the same structural fact under two presentations. Editing either should consult the other; they are not two concepts that happen to share a name, they are one structural fact captured at two registers.

The parallel-concept discipline is the bridge's *visibility* mechanism: a corpus-wide search for "aboutness" finds both notes, and either note's frontmatter `see-also:` points at the other. Future readers see the bridge before they see either side.

## What the bridges buy

The four bridges, taken together, give the discipline:

- **A substrate-level reading of every semiotic primitive.** Sign-relations, codes, interpretants, semiospheres become substrate operations rather than analytical posits.
- **A regime classification of historical semiotic systems.** Peircean (transfinite-bounded), Saussurean (Scott-continuous within langue), Hjelmslevian (Scott-continuous on each connotative layer), Lotmanian (transfinite-bounded by semiosphere memory).
- **An operational binding to working systems.** The corpus's own predication-cell substrate inherits the semiotic reading; the substrate's *aboutness* operation is the discipline's *what-an-artifact-is-about* under another name.
- **A composability discipline with [predicated-knowledge-systems peer-comp morphisms](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/communication/archive/library/linguistics/library/information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/morphisms/_index.md).** The discipline's [specialization](../../../../glossary/terms/specialization.md), [translation](../../../../glossary/terms/translation.md), and [codeswitching](../../../../glossary/terms/codeswitching.md) compose with the substrate's [specialize](../../../../glossary/terms/specialize.md) and [federate](../../../../glossary/terms/federate.md) operations.

## What the bridges leave open

- The full faithfulness of the sign-vehicle ↔ predicate identification (Bridge 1) — argued at the structural-role level, not yet at the categorical-isomorphism level.
- The regime classification of semiotic systems beyond the four worked examples (Bridge 2) — see the [semiosis-bound-and-grounding](../../learning-articles/questions/semiosis-bound-and-grounding.md) open question.
- The asymmetry between semiosphere translation and substrate federate (Bridge 3) — see the [translation-as-fiber-morphism](../../learning-articles/questions/translation-as-fiber-morphism.md) open question.
- The synchronisation discipline between the two `aboutness` notes (Bridge 4) — currently informal; the corpus does not yet have a parallel-concept discipline that would make it formal.
