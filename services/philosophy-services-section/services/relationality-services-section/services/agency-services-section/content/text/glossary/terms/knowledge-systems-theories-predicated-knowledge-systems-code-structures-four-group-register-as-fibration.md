The four-group directory layout — **governing**, **engaging**, **situating**, **defining** — that this corpus inherits from earlier prior-art work is the visible surface of a four-stratum decomposition of the predicate category $\mathcal{C}$ in any predicated knowledge system.

Concretely, $\mathcal{C}$ admits a partition into four sub-categories:

- $\mathcal{C}_G$ (governing-stratum): predicates about the system's normative discipline — methods, practices, standards. Subjects are typically activities, agents, or artifacts; objects are typically norms, criteria, or commitments. Examples: `follows-method`, `complies-with-standard`, `evaluated-by-criterion`.
- $\mathcal{C}_E$ (engaging-stratum): predicates about transactions with knowledge — learning, researching, applying. Subjects are typically agents or activities; objects are typically knowledge content, sources, or applications. Examples: `learns-from`, `cites-as-source`, `applies-to-situation`.
- $\mathcal{C}_S$ (situating-stratum): predicates about intellectual placement — disciplines, schools, lineages. Subjects are typically agents, communities, or works; objects are typically disciplinary or methodological labels. Examples: `belongs-to-discipline`, `aligns-with-school`, `inherits-from-lineage`.
- $\mathcal{C}_D$ (defining-stratum): predicates about vocabulary — topics, concepts, terms. Subjects are typically vocabulary entities; objects are typically other vocabulary entities or contextual qualifications. Examples: `denotes-concept`, `is-topic-of`, `is-term-for`.

The four sub-categories are not disjoint — they share entities, and predicates in one stratum often have morphisms to predicates in another. But each stratum carries its own characteristic closure-rule sub-language, its own typical evidence patterns, and its own admissible regime under the [four-regime dichotomy](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/references/four-regime-dichotomy.md). The [theorem on the four-group register as a regime](../concepts/four-group-register-as-regime.md) (status: sketch) is the substrate-level claim that this is what the four-group layout is.

## Why the four groups

The four-stratum partition is not arbitrary. The synthesis in [process-ontology-and-situated-learning](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/texts/process-ontology-and-situated-learning.md) §6 names what each stratum captures: every working knowledge community has to do four classes of activity — govern its work (set and follow norms), engage with knowledge (acquire, study, apply), situate itself intellectually (place its work relative to others), and define its vocabulary (settle terminology and concepts). The four-group register is the predicated-substrate naming of this division.

Within each group, the prior-art layout subdivides into three: governing into methods/practices/standards, engaging into learning/researching/applying, situating into disciplines/schools/lineages, defining into topics/concepts/terms. The 4×3=12 cells are the layout's directory structure. The predicated framing recognises the 12 cells as a *finer* stratification of $\mathcal{C}$ — twelve sub-sub-categories of predicates, each with characteristic shape — but does not require the finer level for the structural fibration claim. The four-group register suffices.

## How the fibration acts

The fibration over the four-group partition is the assignment $G \in \{\mathcal{C}_G, \mathcal{C}_E, \mathcal{C}_S, \mathcal{C}_D\} \mapsto \mathcal{K}|_G$ — the restriction of the predicated knowledge system to the predicates in stratum $G$. Each restriction $\mathcal{K}|_G$ is a sub-system in its own right; the four restrictions cover the system; the cross-stratum predicate morphisms are what make the four into one system rather than four parallel systems.

The cross-stratum morphisms are typically of three kinds:

- **Reification**: a predication in one stratum becomes an entity participating in predications in another. Asserting `cites-as-source(W, X)` (engaging-stratum) reifies the citation as an entity whose `belongs-to-discipline(citation, X)` lives in the situating-stratum.
- **Specialisation**: a defining-stratum predicate (`denotes-concept`) is specialised to engaging-stratum (`learns-concept`) or governing-stratum (`certifies-knowledge-of-concept`) by restricting subject-type.
- **Quotient**: a situating-stratum predicate (`belongs-to-discipline`) projects governing-stratum and engaging-stratum predications under a common discipline, giving a quotient view restricted to that discipline.

The fibration's cross-stratum morphisms are what make the four-group layout *one* knowledge system rather than four; the morphisms are how a community's governance (norms) constrain its engagement (work), its situation (placement) shapes its definition (vocabulary), and so on.
