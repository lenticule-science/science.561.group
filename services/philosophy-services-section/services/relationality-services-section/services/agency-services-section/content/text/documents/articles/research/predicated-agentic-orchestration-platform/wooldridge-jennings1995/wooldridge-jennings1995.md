M.

"Intelligent Agents: Theory and Practice." *Knowledge Engineering Review*, 10(2), 115–152, 1995.

## Form

Survey paper.
The canonical 1990s survey of agent theory and the load-bearing reference for the *weak* and *strong* notions of agency that organize the field.

## Content summary

Surveys the agent-oriented programming literature of the late 1980s and early 1990s and proposes two notions of agency.
The *weak notion*: an agent is a software entity exhibiting *autonomy* (acts without direct human intervention), *social ability* (interacts with other agents), *reactivity* (responds to environment changes), and *pro-activeness* (initiates goal-directed behavior).
The *strong notion* additionally requires *mentalistic* attributes — beliefs, desires, intentions, knowledge — that admit folk-psychological explanation.
The paper organizes the field into three layers: agent theories (formal frameworks like BDI logics), agent architectures (concrete designs like IRMA, PRS, dMARS), and agent languages (programming languages with agent-as-primitive constructs).
The survey is the load-bearing characterization for what counts as an agent in the academic multi-agent-systems tradition.
