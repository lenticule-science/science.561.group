# Sign-object

The *position in a sign-relation occupied by what the sign-vehicle stands for* — the entity, state of affairs, type, or further sign that the sign-relation makes available to the interpretant.

The Peircean *object* (per [Peirce CP](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/peirce-cp.md)) is the canonical name; the Saussurean *signified* (per the [signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md) term) is the same position viewed as the *concept-side* of the dyadic langue. Peirce further distinguishes the *immediate object* (the object as the sign represents it) from the *dynamic object* (the object as it is independent of the sign); semiotic systems differ in whether they admit the dynamic object as substrate-level content or treat it as an interpretive limit-concept.

The discipline's preferred name *sign-object* (with the hyphen) avoids name-collision with the [predicated-knowledge-systems](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems) corpus's own object-as-predication-cell-target ($o$ in $(s, p, o)$). The collision is not accidental: the substrate-level reading places the sign-object at the *subject* and *object* positions of the predication-cell, the two non-predicate positions whose joint identification is what the predicate is *about*.

A sign-object can itself be a sign — this is the substrate of unlimited semiosis (per [unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint](unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint.md)): an interpretant becomes the sign-vehicle of a further sign-relation, whose object may be the previous sign-relation as a whole. Eco (per [Eco 1976](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/eco1976.md)) bounds this regress with the *encyclopedia*; Peirce leaves it open as the substrate of inquiry itself.
