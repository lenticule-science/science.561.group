# Confederation

A *confederation* in FARS is a set of [sovereign nodes](../sovereign-node/sovereign-node.md) connected by [federation](../../teaching/explanations/federation.md): each node owns its own substrate, each node settles under its own rule-system version, each node's incoming and outgoing federation envelopes are signed by the node's identity-key and admitted at peers' inboxes per the receiving node's governance.
The confederation is the federated whole; federation is the relation; the sovereign nodes are the members.

The distinction between confederation and federation is structural, not nominal:

- **[Federation](../../teaching/explanations/federation.md)** is a *pattern* — the discipline (routing predications, signed-witness authentication, merge-on-receive) by which two predicated computers exchange settled content.
- **Confederation** is a *noun for the federated set* — the membership graph whose edges are admit-relations between sovereign nodes' inboxes and outposts.

A FARS realization can participate in zero confederations (sovereign and isolated), one confederation (it has peers; the federation graph is connected at this node), or several confederations (it admits envelopes from disjoint or partially-overlapping peer-sets).
The confederation has no substrate of its own — there is no event log, no rule-system, no dispatch table at the confederation level.
Each member's substrate is sovereign per the [sovereign-node](../sovereign-node/sovereign-node.md) discipline.

## What "confederation" pins down

- **No central authority.** A confederation has no node distinguished as "the coordinator."
  Each member admits its peers per its own governance; trust is bilattice-graded per [federation-trust-bilattice-graded](../../../../../theorems/federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md), evaluated locally at each receiving node.
- **Per-node sovereignty preserved.** A member's participation in the confederation does not delegate any aspect of its sovereignty: its event log remains its own, its rule-system version is settable independently, its [swarm](../swarm/swarm.md) is generated by its own agential-self-generation.
  Federation is *additive*, not replacing.
- **Convergence is per-pair, not global.** Per [federation-convergence](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/federation-convergence.md), two peers exchanging the same set of envelopes converge to the same merged content.
  The confederation's *global* state is the union of pairwise convergences, not a single agreed substrate.
- **The confederation has no record of itself.** Each node's [`PeerRecord`](../../code/structures/peer-record.md) population names its peers; the confederation's reach is computed by traversing peers-of-peers.
  There is no entity "the confederation" — the confederation *is* the peer-graph.

## What confederation is not

- *Not a [cluster](../../../../theories/distributed-coordination-platform/concepts/cluster-membership.md).* A cluster-membership discipline is for *consensus within a coordinated subset* — Raft-like agreement on a shared resource, with quorum and leader semantics.
  Confederation is *eventual convergence across sovereign peers* — no consensus, no quorum, no leader.
  The two compose: a sub-set of confederation members may form a coordination cluster for some shared resource, but the disciplines are distinct.
- *Not a [swarm](../swarm/swarm.md).* A swarm is the agential population at one sovereign node.
  A confederation has no agential population of its own; its activity is the federated activity of its members' swarms, mediated by envelopes.
- *Not a deployment topology.* Multiple sovereign nodes may run at the same physical site; one sovereign node may span multiple machines via shared filesystem.
  Confederation is independent of physical topology — it is the substrate-level pattern of inter-node admission.

## Position

Confederation is FARS's *unit of multi-node deployment*.
It composes with [swarm](../swarm/swarm.md) (each confederation member has its own), with [governance](../../teaching/explanations/governance.md) (each member retains its own; confederation does not unify governance), and with [boundary](../../../../teaching/explanations/boundary.md) (a peer is reached at this node via a federation-class boundary, distinct from human-actor boundaries).

The concept names what was previously left implicit: when prior FARS material says "a federation," it sometimes means the *pattern* (the discipline) and sometimes means the *set* (the federated whole).
This concept fixes the latter sense as `confederation`, leaving `federation` for the pattern.
