## Annotation

Needham's foundational lecture introducing *integrative levels* as a non-reductive ordering of the sciences: physical → chemical → biological → psychological → social, where each level is structurally novel, depends on but is not exhausted by the level below, and demands its own predicate vocabulary. The framing rejects both flat materialism (everything is physics) and dualism (mind is separate from matter); each level is a stratum of organisation that the lower levels make possible without determining.

The predicated framing reads integrative levels as a *stratified predicate-category fibration*: each level contributes a sub-vocabulary of predicates, level-specific assertions live in the corresponding fibers, and *cross-level reasoning* requires the [specialize](../../../glossary/terms/specialize.md) and [federate](../../../glossary/terms/federate.md) morphisms to mediate between levels. The non-reductive commitment translates into a structural commitment: there is no closure rule that *eliminates* a higher-level predicate in favour of lower-level predicates — the closure-rule system is *level-respecting*. This is what [predicate-category-stratification](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/archive/teaching/explanations/predicate-category-stratification.md) names mathematically.

Needham's lecture is the philosophical anchor for the *upper-ontology-as-predicate-vocabulary* reading: an upper ontology is a level-stratification of the predicate category, and the choice of upper ontology is the choice of which integrative levels to recognise as primary.
