# Agential Orchestration Platform

A subtheory inheriting from two parents:

- The engineering parent: [predicated computing](../../_index.md) — the engineering theory of computers whose primary substrate is the predication.
  AgOP is the predicated-computing setting in which agents (human and AI) act on the substrate, with the additional commitment that the operations agents use are themselves substrate entities.
- The math parent: [predication categories](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/_index.md) — the categorical study of bilattice-valued $(s, p, o)$ predication structures.
  The platform's substrate has [predication fiber algebras](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md) at each predicate, the platform's runtime trajectory is a [predication settling](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) under [closure operators](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-operator.md) generated by the substrate's [closure-rule system](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/closure-rule-system.md).

The platform inherits both: it commits to all of the parent engineering theory's disciplines (driver/substrate split, four-component anatomy, settling discipline, witness chain, federation as substrate join) *and* it operates under the math parent's algebraic apparatus (twist-product fusion, knowledge/truth orders, closure-operator naturality across the predicate category, bilattice homomorphisms in restriction maps).

The platform's defining structural commitment, beyond what either parent supplies, is **agential self-generation**: every operative element of the platform — every [agent](concepts/agent.md), every [skill](concepts/skill.md), every [runbook](concepts/runbook.md), every [shape](scope/shape/shape.md), every [office](concepts/office.md), every [governance](concepts/governance.md) rule — is itself a substrate entity.
The platform is therefore self-referential at the agential level: the system is its own subject of operation.
This is a strictly stronger commitment than the engineering parent's substrate-level [self-representation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/self-representation.md), which guarantees only that the substrate's *substrate-level* operative elements are entities; AgOP extends the commitment to the *agential* operative elements.

## Position as a subtheory

The engineering parent characterizes the predication substrate without committing to *who* is operating on it.
A predicated computer can run with no agents at all (a CRDT-replicated store with no agential layer is a valid predicated computer), with agents that are external processes (a graph store driven by an external orchestrator), or with agents that are themselves entities in the substrate.
AgOP commits to that last case.

The math parent characterizes the algebraic apparatus the substrate operates under without committing to *what* engineering shape the runtime takes.
A predication fiber algebra can be wired into a single-process program, a distributed service cluster, or a flatfile agential resource system.
AgOP commits to a runtime in which agents are first-class substrate citizens, and the math parent's apparatus — closure operators, the [knowledge order](../../../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md), [predication valuations](../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/predication-valuation.md), settling-monotonicity — is what governs every agential operation.

The dividing line between the three theories is sharp.
Anything about the substrate itself — the predicated computer, its primitive operators, the closure machinery, persistence, application sublocale as a *kind* of view — belongs in the engineering parent.
Anything about the substrate's algebraic structure — bilattices, twist products, predication fibrations, closure-rule semantics — belongs in the math parent.
Anything specifically about agents-acting-on-the-substrate — dispatch, scheduling via heartbeat, federation, governance, session memory, agent-as-entity, scope-bounded skill invocation — belongs here.
