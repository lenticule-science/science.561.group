- To claim that something exists is not trivial.
    
    - It is to claim:
        
        - Something is different than another thing,
            
        - Something is not nothing, or at a minimum,
            
        - Something is not what it is not.
            
    - That is: `thing ≠ not-thing`
        
- This claim has dynamics which must be derived:
    
    - What is a **thing?**  
        ➔ At minimum, it is **what is claimed to exist.**
        
    - What is **not?**  
        ➔ At minimum, it is **a denial or negation of what the thing is.**
        
    - What is **equivalence/inequivalence?**  
        ➔ It is the basis for asserting that a thing **is or is not** something else.
        
    - These cannot be **assumed primitives;**  
        their roles and interplay **must be derived to explain how existence becomes intelligible.**
        
- Given a thing is determined to be inequivalent to anything that isn't itself, it cannot not derive there is an **act of determination:**
    
    - The assertion `thing ≠ not-thing` is not a static fact;  
        it requires **an act that sets the inequivalence.**
        
    - This act is what **relates the thing to what is not the thing,**  
        producing a distinction between them.
        
    - Without this act, the inequivalence between thing and not-thing is **not established.**
        
- This act relates the thing to not-thing. How?
    
    - The act must bring **the thing and not-thing into relation** to distinguish them.
        
    - But in doing so, the act makes **what is “not the thing” present within the relation,**  
        even as it sets it apart.
        
    - Thus, the act of determination is **not merely separating;**  
        it is also **holding both in relation as distinct.**
        
- Given **not** is vacuous without relation (`thing` or `equivalence`), it cannot not derive that **not conditions relation:**
    
    - “Not” has no force or meaning unless it **negates something.**
        
    - Therefore, “not” **depends on the existence of a thing or relation to negate.**
        
    - But by negating, “not” also **shapes relation:**  
        ➔ it defines a boundary within relation between what is and what is not.
        
    - Thus, while “not” depends on relation, it also **conditions the appearance of relation,**  
        giving structure to how relation is experienced as differentiated.
