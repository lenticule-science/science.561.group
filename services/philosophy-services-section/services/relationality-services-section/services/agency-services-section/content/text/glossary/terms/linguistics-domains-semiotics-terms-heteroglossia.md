**Heteroglossia**, in Mikhail Bakhtin's account, is the coexistence of distinct social languages, registers, worldviews, and evaluative positions within a community. These are **situated voices**, not merely dialect variations or opposing truth values. A monologic representation erases some of their positions when it speaks as though one narrative stands for everyone.

For an L-Space or FOUR-valued claim system, heteroglossia motivates preserving **source attribution, speech context, and disagreement**; it is not itself an algebraic FOUR theorem or a warrant that every utterance is true. The [source-owned exposition](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/04/02/2026-04-02-heteroglossia.md) retains the original account, examples, distinctions from dialogism and genring, and bibliography.

Reference: Mikhail Bakhtin, *The Dialogic Imagination: Four Essays* (1981).
