Biosemiotics: the study of sign processes in living systems, extending Peircean semiotics to biology. Founded on Thomas Sebeok's zoosemiotics and Jakob von Uexküll's Umwelt theory.

Biosemiotics is the study of [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) processes in and among living systems. It extends [Peircean semiotics](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/peircean-semiotics.md) beyond human communication to encompass all life, treating [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/semiosis.md) — the production and interpretation of [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) — as a defining characteristic of living organisms. Its founding figures are Thomas Sebeok (1920–2001) and, retrospectively, the biologist Jakob von Uexküll (1864–1944).

## Methods and approach

Biosemiotics draws on [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)&rsquo;s triadic sign model to argue that sign processes are not confined to language or culture but are coextensive with life. Wherever an organism interprets its environment — a bacterium detecting a chemical gradient, a bee performing a waggle dance, a cell responding to a hormone — [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/semiosis.md) is occurring. The biosemiotic claim is ontological: life and [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/semiosis.md) are coextensive.

### Key contributions

- **Zoosemiotics** — Sebeok&rsquo;s term for the study of [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) processes in animal communication and behavior. Sebeok argued that all animals communicate through signs, and that the study of animal sign systems is a branch of [semiotics](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/index.md) rather than a separate discipline. He extended the Peircean classification ([icon](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/icon.md), [index](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/index-sign.md), [symbol](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/symbol.md)) to non-human organisms, showing that animal communication uses all three sign types.

- **Umwelt** — von Uexküll&rsquo;s concept of the organism&rsquo;s subjective perceptual world. Each species inhabits its own Umwelt — a world constituted by the [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) it can perceive and the actions it can perform. A tick&rsquo;s Umwelt consists of butyric acid, warmth, and skin texture; a bat&rsquo;s Umwelt is constituted by echolocation. The Umwelt is the organism&rsquo;s sign-world: the totality of [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) that are meaningful for it.

- **Functional cycle** — von Uexküll&rsquo;s model of the organism-environment relationship as a loop of perception signs (*Merkzeichen*) and action signs (*Wirkzeichen*). The organism perceives features of its environment as signs, interprets them, and acts; the action changes the environment, producing new signs. This is a biological instance of [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/semiosis.md): the iterative process of sign-interpretation-sign.

- **Endosemiotics** — the study of [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) processes within organisms: cell signaling, immune recognition, genetic expression. The cell&rsquo;s response to a hormone is a [semiotic](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/index.md) process: the hormone is a [representamen](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/representamen.md), the cellular receptor mediates the relation, and the cellular response is an [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md). The genetic code itself — the mapping from nucleotide triplets to amino acids — is a [semiotic](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/index.md) system.

- **The semiotic threshold** — biosemiotics challenges the assumption that [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/semiosis.md) requires consciousness, intention, or language. The threshold of sign use is not the human mind but life itself. This expands the scope of [semiotics](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/index.md) to include domains traditionally claimed by biochemistry and molecular biology.

## Relation to Peircean semiotics

Biosemiotics is the most direct extension of [Peircean semiotics](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/peircean-semiotics.md). [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) himself did not restrict [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/semiosis.md) to human beings — his definition of the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) as &ldquo;something that stands for something else to some interpreter&rdquo; places no species restriction on the interpreter. Biosemiotics takes this generality seriously and investigates its consequences for biology and the philosophy of life.

## Key texts

- Sebeok, Thomas A. *Signs: An Introduction to Semiotics*. 2nd ed. Toronto: University of Toronto Press, 2001.

- Hoffmeyer, Jesper. *Biosemiotics: An Examination into the Signs of Life and the Life of Signs*. Scranton: University of Scranton Press, 2008.

- von Uexküll, Jakob. *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans*. Translated by Joseph D. O&rsquo;Neil. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 2010 [1934].

- Favareau, Donald, ed. *Essential Readings in Biosemiotics*. Dordrecht: Springer, 2010.

- Chandler, Daniel. *Semiotics: The Basics*. 2nd ed. London: Routledge, 2007.

## Active research

Since Hoffmeyer&rsquo;s death in 2019, biosemiotics has continued to develop at the University of Tartu (Kalevi Kull, Timo Maran), in the journal *Biosemiotics* (Springer), and at *Sign Systems Studies*. Kull&rsquo;s recent work explores the role of freedom and choice in organismic behavior and evolution (&ldquo;Choices by Organisms,&rdquo; *Biological Journal of the Linnean Society*, 2023). The field has also engaged with AI through the topos-theoretic framework of Tohme, Gangle, and Caterina (2024), which shows that machine learning processes share the formal structure of semiosis.

- [Social Semiotics](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/social-semiotics.md) — shares the concern with sign-making beyond language but restricts its scope to human social practice
