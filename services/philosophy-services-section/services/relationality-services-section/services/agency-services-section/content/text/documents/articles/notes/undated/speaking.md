**Speaking** is the paradigm act of [doing](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/docs/doing.md).
J.L. Austin's discovery (*How to Do Things with Words*) was that to say something is, characteristically, to *do* something: uttering "I promise," "I name this ship," "I warn you" is not reporting an act but performing one.
Austin began with a special class of these — *performatives* — and ended by concluding that *every* utterance does something; saying is a species of acting.
That is why speaking sits here as the exemplary deed: it is the act in which a someone, in Arendt's phrase, discloses who they are, and it is the act whose anatomy is clearest.

## What generates it

Speaking is generated by doing: it is the act-form proper.
Doing is the capacity to act and the bounded episode of acting; speaking is that episode carried out in words — the selecting of something to say and the saying of it with a force.

## What it generates — Austin's three layers

Austin found that a single speech act is really three acts at once, and the distinction is his, not an overlay:

| layer | Austin's term | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| [locution](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/content/library/locution/docs/locution.md) | the locutionary act | *what is said* — the meaningful content, what the utterance is about, before any force |
| [illocution](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/content/library/illocution/docs/illocution.md) | the illocutionary act | *what is done in saying it* — the force: asserting, asking, promising, ordering |
| [perlocution](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/content/library/perlocution/docs/perlocution.md) | the perlocutionary act | *what is brought about by saying it* — the downstream effect on the hearer |

So speaking runs the whole shape of an act in miniature: it *selects* something to address (locution), *charges* it with a force (illocution), and *brings about* consequences (perlocution).
The force belongs to illocution, not locution — locution only fixes what is spoken of.
The kinds of force an illocution can carry are the four illocutionary [forces](forces.md).
