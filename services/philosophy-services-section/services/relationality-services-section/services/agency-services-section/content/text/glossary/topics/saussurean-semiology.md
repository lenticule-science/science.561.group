Saussurean semiology is the tradition of [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) theory founded by [Ferdinand de Saussure](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/ferdinand-de-saussure.md) (1857–1913). It defines the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) as a dyadic relation between [signifier](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signifier.md) (the sound-image or written form) and [signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md) (the concept it evokes), and treats meaning as arising from differences within a system rather than from reference to the world [@saussure_CourseGeneralLinguistics_1916].

## Methods and approach

Saussure&rsquo;s semiology emerged from structural linguistics and was published posthumously in the *Course in General Linguistics* (1916), reconstructed from students&rsquo; lecture notes. His project was to establish a general &ldquo;science of signs&rdquo; — *sémiologie* — of which linguistics would be one part.

### Key contributions

- **The dyadic sign** — the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) is the union of [signifier](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signifier.md) and [signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md). These two are inseparable, like two sides of a sheet of paper. The sign is not a name attached to a thing but a concept linked to a sound-image.

- **Arbitrariness of the sign** — the relationship between [signifier](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signifier.md) and [signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md) is conventional, not natural. There is no inherent reason why the sound-image &ldquo;tree&rdquo; should evoke the concept of a tree; the connection is established by social convention.

- **Differential meaning** — signs do not carry positive content. Each sign gets its value from what it is *not* relative to other signs in the system. The concept &ldquo;dog&rdquo; is defined not by what it refers to but by its difference from &ldquo;cat,&rdquo; &ldquo;wolf,&rdquo; &ldquo;pet,&rdquo; and every other term in the system.

- **Langue and parole** — Saussure distinguished between *langue* (the abstract system of conventions shared by a speech community) and *parole* (the individual acts of speech that instantiate the system). Linguistics, in his view, should study *langue* — the system — rather than *parole* — the performance.

- **Synchronic and diachronic analysis** — Saussure distinguished between studying a language system at a given moment (synchronic) and studying its historical development (diachronic), privileging the former as the proper object of structural linguistics.

## Contrast with [Peircean semiotics](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/peircean-semiotics.md)

The two founding traditions differ on fundamental points. Saussure&rsquo;s model is dyadic ([signifier](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signifier.md)/[signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md)); [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)&rsquo;s is triadic ([representamen](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/representamen.md)/object/[interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md)). Saussure treats the sign as a static element in a system of differences; [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) treats the sign as a dynamic event in an ongoing process of [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/semiosis.md). Saussure&rsquo;s model accounts for the pairing of form and concept but says little about interpretation as a process; [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)&rsquo;s makes interpretation constitutive of the sign itself [@chandler_Semiotics_2007].

## Influence

Saussure&rsquo;s semiology became the foundation of European structuralism. His concepts of differential meaning, the arbitrariness of the sign, and the distinction between system and use were taken up by Roman Jakobson, Claude Lévi-Strauss, [Roland Barthes](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/roland-barthes.md), [Louis Hjelmslev](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/louis-hjelmslev.md), and the broader tradition of [French semiology](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/french-semiology.md). The concept of the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/terms/sign.md) as [signifier](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signifier.md)/[signified](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/signified.md) remains foundational in cultural studies, literary theory, and media studies.
