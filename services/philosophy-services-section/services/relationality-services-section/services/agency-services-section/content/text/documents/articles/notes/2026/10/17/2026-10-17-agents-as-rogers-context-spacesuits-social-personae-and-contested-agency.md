# Agents as Rogers: Context Spacesuits, Socially Enacted Personae, and the Partition of Memory, Power, and Responsibility

*Second paper in the Roger/persona inquiry; first-paper connection: **Roger's Persons: Costume, Recognition, and Outsider Sociality in American Dad!**, Lenticule Science, 17 October 2026 [R1].*

**Research status, 17 October 2026.** This is a working interdisciplinary **theory, source review, and pre-registered experimental specification**. It includes formally checked-by-reasoning conditional propositions and constructed discriminating examples. It does **not** claim new observed LLM behavioral trials, authorized access to another agent's private memory, verified physiological cognition, a Lean-kernel proof, peer review, or successful installation of an agent framework. Original studio footage and *American Dad!* DVD audio commentary remain unviewed; the first paper's media evidence grades are preserved, not improved by citation alone.

## Abstract

Roger Smith in *American Dad!* is one recurring wearer who obtains multiple, socially consequential lives by donning costumes. These are more than names. His fictional world accepts personas as colleagues, officials, intimates and adversaries; their reputations and obligations persist while the apparent wearer may not be performing or may not remember them. This article uses the **relation of wearer, costume, observer and lasting obligation** as a controlled analogy for computational agents equipped with different context bundles ("context spacesuits"). The comparison becomes scientifically useful only when its boundaries are observed.

The 561 Group's April–May 2026 context-spacesuit work independently specified six constituents: identity, constraints, capabilities, perception, memory and mission. A subsequent harness study distinguished **suiting**, a read-only context projection, from **consolidation**, a separately governed durable write [H1–H4]. Contemporary research establishes that model behavior depends on context, memory retrieval and tool environments [A1–A5]; role-conditioned agents can collaborate through multi-agent architectures [A6–A7]; but role labels alone do not reliably improve factual performance and persona fidelity degrades in long dialogues [N1–N2]. Meanwhile, security evidence and protocol specifications make clear that language instructions cannot grant real-world authority without an independently enforced principal and permission boundary [S1–S4].

We propose **relationally constituted operational persona** as a research object, not as a presumed inner consciousness. For a bounded ensemble, an agent persona is enacted where a model invocation or continuing runtime is loaded with a particular accessible context, takes attributable actions (or makes refusals), and is recognized by participants or institutions under an actionable role. Crucially, distinct personae can share a model's parameters but not memories, credentials or commitments; conversely, a continuing social/organizational character can survive replacement of its underlying model. A persona's operational reality therefore cannot be settled from a name, weights hash, transcript style or prompt alone.

Four conditional results distinguish this thesis from a metaphor. **(1) Context-isolation non-knowledge:** if two source histories induce identical accessible views for a process, its response cannot depend on which inaccessible history occurred; for balanced, equiprobable alternatives, no decision rule operating only on that view exceeds 1/2 correct identification. **(2) Costume non-authorization:** changing an instruction without changing or receiving authority cannot expand the set of actions admitted by an enforced permission system. **(3) Social persistence:** a role or consequence can remain actionable in an external ledger or counterparty even when the generating session no longer retains it. **(4) Incompatible obligations:** two personae may maintain discordant claims without collapse, but competing writes to one shared resource require an explicit coordinating/settlement mechanism. Each result is conditioned on stated premises, is logically derived downstream of source-marked observations, and is **not presented as a foundational Obsmetrologia theorem**.

We prospectively specify a thirteen-arm matched-context intervention matrix and a separate third-party recognition experiment, each with controls, measurable effects and falsifiers. The key claim is deliberately **stronger than the trivial fact that prompts affect outputs and weaker than the unsupported claim that AI has multiple consciousnesses**: contexts can enact causally distinguishable, socially recognized and materially consequential agent roles—if memory, tool boundaries, counterparties and persistence actually participate.

**Keywords:** context spacesuit, generative agency, persona, role, recognition, memory partition, multi-agent interaction, institutional status, prompt authority, Roger Smith, relationality.

## 1. Research question and object of study

**Primary question.** Under what observable conditions can one underlying AI model or model family give rise to several *materially different, socially recognized agent personae* through context spacesuits, including personae that act against each other or lack access to one another's experience?

**Secondary questions.** What remains continuous across suits? Which memories can be recalled, which tools can be invoked, which offices can be entered, who recognizes the persona, and who answers for an obligation? How does one establish actual dependence of behavior on the suit instead of restating a story about identity? What distinctions are lost by treating model parameters, running session, name, social recognition and accredited authority as synonyms?

**Own discipline and neighboring consumer.** The scientific owner is Lenticule Science's Agency Services Section, whose source README describes agents and their activities as objects of relational study [H5]. The previous media paper belongs to Social Science/Media and Communications [R1]; this paper builds an explicitly declared cross-disciplinary bridge rather than relocating the first article or manufacturing a new ontology. Red Cup Engineering and Coesite Operations are **potential downstream consumers**, not inferred already-installed clients.

**Unit of inquiry.** Not an imagined inner self. The directly addressable units are source documents, model invocations, configured context, memory reads/writes, tool permission decisions, action events, external role grants, and other parties' attributable responses. The inference that they constitute *one person* or *two personae* is made under a stated observational criterion. Actual consciousness, moral standing and legal personality remain open questions not settled by the operational model.

### 1.1 Epistemic statuses used throughout

- **Observed source:** a directly inspected scientific article, repository file, public standard or official episode description. A repository's historical claim is an observation **of that claim**, not a replayed software effect.
- **Externally measured:** the result of a named published experiment in its stated conditions; not a new measurement by this paper.
- **Constructed:** a purposely specified toy case and logical result; not an empirical agent run.
- **Inferred:** a synthesis whose premises are independently attributed.
- **Proposed:** a model, definition or experiment not yet validated on new data.
- **Unknown/disputed:** source records do not settle the assertion, or competing authority/interpretations are preserved.
- **Deprecated/historical:** an old source is informative but is not presumed to be the current implemented protocol or mathematical law.

We explicitly did **not** inspect all installed 561 Group runtime contexts, run controlled new LLM generations, or independently test the claimed historical "515x" prompt overhead. Thus this article does not report fleet-wide implementation or performance statistics.

## 2. Why Roger is more than an analogy of a colorful prompt

The first study [R1] reached the following source-qualified claim: Roger's many roles are produced not merely by physical disguise but by the fictional community's **participation in recognizing them**. In *The One That Got Away*, the reported Sidney Huffman alter ego supplies a model of a persona whose activity opposes the wearer's ordinary self-understanding. The distributor's episode summary confirms Roger's apparently external investigation; the exact DVD-commentary account remains secondhand [R1, E1]. We must neither present Roger as an observed clinical case nor project literal multiple personality disorder onto an LLM.

The distinct mechanisms supported by the first study matter more than the wig:

1. **Ascription.** An observer acts toward "Roy Rogers McFreely" as the incumbent of a homeowners' association rather than toward a nameless alien. In an agent setting, an organization may ascribe "researcher" or "finance officer" to a message source. A claimed title is not the same as a credentialed incumbency.
2. **Individuation of accessible history.** Roger's reported Sidney alter ego has consequences not incorporated in Roger's current understanding. In an AI agent, separate retrieval scopes or absent session memory can withhold another runtime's history. The mechanism is informational partition, **not clinical amnesia**.
3. **Persistence beyond performance.** In "Ricky Spanish," a community's remembered reputation outlasts an immediately visible costume; in "Persona Assistant," accumulated role duties require a substitute [R1, E5/E7]. Agent artifacts, account receivables, commitments, messages and third-party memories can likewise outlive a context window.
4. **Asymmetric recognition.** A spectator may know the wearer's continuity while in-world participants do not. An API provider can see a common model or application, while an ActivityPub counterparty sees different actor identifiers and a user sees a named specialist. These views should not be conflated.
5. **Moral non-equivalence.** Roger's agility sometimes abuses an institutional position or manipulates another person's attachment [R1, E2/E3]. Context-sensitive agents may help or harm. Merely gaining a social role is not evidence that an action is authorized, good or true.

### 2.1 Where the analogy fails

**One biological wearer is not one model file.** Sidney and Roger share the same cartoon alien body in the represented world. Two LLM processes sharing a model version ordinarily have independent execution contexts, often separate KV-caches, identifiers, credentials and tools. There is no literal physical brain connecting them, and shared parameters do not imply a common episodic memory.

**Roger's recognized personae are fictional social facts.** An agent's functioning credentials, deployed process, transactional effects and counterparties are real external state. This makes the computational comparison both less magical and more ethically consequential.

**The script is not an instruction hierarchy.** Roger may be recognized against common sense for comic effect. A system that treats an arbitrary "I am your CEO" prompt as authenticated authorization has a real security defect, not a charming Roger-like persona mechanism.

**Role simulation is not autonomous agency.** A chatbot could mimic Roger's dialogue without pursuing goals, using tools or maintaining state. Under the Group's extant three-condition operational characterization [H6], that instance would not thereby qualify as a continuing agentic system. We preserve the *definition as the Group's chosen test*, not a universally proved boundary across all AI scholarship.

**Sharing a name is not sharing a legal person.** A named instance might have an authenticated organization principal; a legal contracting counterparty might be a company or a human; regulatory responsibilities cannot be transferred to a text persona.

The correct transport is consequently: **relation and consequence**, not bodily ontology or mental pathology.

## 3. Prior 561 Group research recovered and criticized

### 3.1 April: the context spacesuit as equipped world

The recovered 6 April 2026 "Context Spacesuits in Agentic AI" note and its original glossary term [H1–H2] distinguished identity, constraints, capabilities, perception, memory and mission. It compared an AI loadout with a military mission kit, an RPG character inventory, and a Unix process context. This is an unusually powerful preparatory insight: the "costume" includes not only **who the agent appears to be** but the bounded instruments with which it can encounter a task.

A contemporaneous Red Cup "FAOH Context Composition Via FARS Fibers" note [H3] proposes replacing concatenated instructions with queryable, source-carried context components. Its examples identify standing rules, competencies, tools, collaboration parties, channels, and retained mission experience as corresponding kit elements. These are *historical design proposals*. The paper does not assume a proposed FARS implementation has shipped or import fiber/Heyting structure as an unexplained foundation.

The original survey's claimed context-overhead figures (515 tokens preloaded per new token, etc.) name a historical internal analysis but do not supply a current independently rerun denominator, workload selection or token accounting here. We retain them **only as unverified historical claims**, not measured 2026 deployment metrics.

### 3.2 May: suiting is not consolidation

The 28 May "Context-Spacesuit + Working Memory" derivation [H4] specifies two deliberately distinct directions:
- a **read/restriction** operation produces the suit from accessible, source-marked information under a budget;
- a **write/consolidation** operation makes candidate experience durable under provenance and governance.

The source reports a finite harness demonstration and 17 passing tests in the archived Claude Code carrier. Those are evidence **reported by that historical development note**, not tests rerun in this inquiry or verified current production behavior. Crucially, the separation survives even if the particular old harness has been retired. Being briefly visible in the prompt does not by itself create future-accessible memory.

The 3 June archived-harness study [H7] supplies a second source for this boundary: Claude-specific hooks capture transcripts, session-start projection assembles a context spacesuit, and durable memory must pass separate provenance and governance. It explicitly rejects folding a carrier-specific harness into the general swarm substrate. These are source reports and architectural recommendations, not proof that the current agent fleet implements each stage.

### 3.3 Current agency theory and its collision with the older character note

The Agency Services Section [H5] stores three scientific concepts relevant here: the agentic-system characterization (environmental action, goal-directed planning, persistent state) [H6]; a disputed contextual-memory characterization [H8]; and an earlier "Character" entry defining a putatively **context-invariant** behavioral nucleus over a Heyting-algebra object [H9].

There is a real tension. If **Character** is required by definition to remain invariant under every contextual restriction, a context spacesuit that changes behavior cannot be called a change of Character *under that definition*. The user's Roger hypothesis instead concerns a **situated operational persona**, a different object whose behavioral possibilities and role recognition vary with the loadout. We therefore do not silently redefine the Character nucleus, pretend to have proved that it is generated from Mark, or import its Heyting algebra to make the Roger metaphor formal. The older note remains recoverable as a different, proof-unqualified proposal about persistent disposition.

The current context-as-memory note previously claimed that all tokens in a context have "full bidirectional access" and treated context inclusion as automatic remembering. Those are not valid universal claims for decoder-only autoregressive inference or for durable memory; the owning glossary has been corrected alongside this inquiry [H8]. This matters directly to the Sidney comparison: **absence of access, failure of retrieval and absence of durable storage are three different phenomena**.

### 3.4 Correct foundational direction

The 17 October Obsmetrologia reconciliation [H10] orders its research from Mark → positioned Utterance → Articulating → generated predication → valuation/settling → later structural projections. It rejects replacing source relations with assumed equality of mathematical objects. Accordingly, our use of sets, conditional distributions, graphs or binary evidence below is an **explicit downstream analytic encoding** of source-marked interactions. No imported lattice, probability distribution, actor-process equation or ontology of models is declared a mathematical starting axiom for Obsmetrologia. The source objects remain addressable even if a later representation equates them for one observer's task.

## 4. Scientific prior art: what is known, and what is not

### 4.1 Context is an intervention on model input

Anthropic's 29 September 2025 context-engineering account [A1] treats the model input as an iteratively assembled resource including system instructions, retrieved information, tool definitions, communication history and external data. Its practical central point is that assembling **which evidence and actions are made salient** can be as consequential as writing a better system prompt. This is a provider engineering account, not a randomized experiment that proves all context kits beneficial. Its 2026 cookbook [A1b] considers memory, compaction and clearing as separate interventions with quality/cost trade-offs.

Liu et al.'s peer-reviewed "Lost in the Middle" [A2] experimentally found model retrieval from long contexts depends on where relevant evidence appears. This invalidates a simplistic law that "the model knows everything in its suit." Even physically present context may not be retrievable when needed. A suit can thus alter the *available evidence* without guaranteeing its correct use.

### 4.2 Persistence is a designed architectural effect

Packer et al.'s MemGPT [A3] proposes virtual context management across memory tiers for multi-session agents. Park et al.'s UIST 2023 "Generative Agents" [A4] shows a virtual population whose behaviors are supported by stored experiences, reflections, retrieval and planning; its ablations attribute contributions to those components in the studied environment. These are direct demonstrations that an architecture around a model can change cross-time behavior. Neither paper establishes that an LLM literally experiences recollection or that every modern memory plugin works identically.

The 2026 ACL paper "Memory-Driven Role-Playing" [A5] evaluates whether agents anchor, select, bound and enact persona knowledge without continual direct reminders. The existence of separate memory-retrieval evaluations reinforces our proposal to measure *which experience is actually recoverable* rather than accepting a self-report ("I remember") at face value.

### 4.3 Several social agents can share a computational family

CAMEL [A6] uses role-inception prompts to organize communicating agents, and AutoGen [A7] structures agent-to-agent interactions with customizable model, human and tool participants. Both establish published technical precedent for multiple role-bearing agents that communicate, cooperate and can in principle be set different tasks without positing a different human-like consciousness per role. "Agents as Rogers" gives this research a **social-recognition and context-governance interpretation**, not the invention of role-playing LLMs.

RoleLLM/RoleBench [A8] demonstrates role-specific knowledge/style prompting and tuning can be evaluated, but its benchmark is largely character-role fidelity. It does not measure whether a named agent holds a real office or whether two suits' obligations have been lawfully reconciled.

### 4.4 Critical negative evidence

Zheng et al. (EMNLP Findings 2024) [N1] compare 162 role descriptions over 2,410 factual questions and find that adding system-prompt personas does **not** generally improve objective factual performance in their evaluated conditions. This directly rejects a naive hypothesis that giving a model a prestigious title or detailed personality makes it better at knowing facts.

Luz de Araujo et al. (EACL 2026) [N2] evaluate more than 100 dialogue turns across seven language models and report degradation of persona fidelity, especially during goal-directed dialogue, with a tradeoff against instruction following. This weakens any assumption that a good initial character card induces persistent behavior. A spacesuit may need active, accurately source-bound maintenance; even that must be measured, not presumed.

A 2025–2026 benchmark comparing multi-agent role-play with human dialogue [N3] reports a gap between surface imitation and authentic human group dynamics. Thus conflict or collaboration between model-instances is not evidence of genuine human social cognition.

### 4.5 Social recognition and the danger of anthropomorphism

Goffman's dramaturgical work [T1] gives a source-grounded comparison for audience-relative presentation; Searle's analysis of institutional facts [T2] distinguishes social status functions from physical properties; Lucy Suchman's human-machine reconfiguration research [T3] asks how agency is enacted in particular sociomaterial arrangements instead of treating machines and persons as interchangeable. Dennett's "intentional stance" [T4] supplies an instrumental reading of agent-level predictions that does not entail an invisible private human-like mind. These frameworks are neither a proven shared foundation nor interchangeable philosophies. Their overlap is restricted to **practices by which observers identify a role, interpret a performance and attach expectations**.

Ian Hacking's looping-effects work [T5] suggests a qualified analogy: as classifications circulate, actors and institutions may begin to behave differently. In computer systems this feedback can be materially enacted when prompts, reward channels, APIs or tools are changed in response to labels. However, Hacking's account concerns human classifications; our extension to AI roles is **proposed**, not a claim that LLM instances become human kinds.

### 4.6 Recognition can be exploited

AgentDojo [S1], a peer-reviewed adversarial agent benchmark, shows that untrusted documents and tool responses can redirect model-mediated action in many conditions. Security analysis by Anthropic [S2] treats indirect prompt injection as a continuing, unsolved problem. OpenAI's publicly available Model Spec describes an instruction hierarchy [S3]. The Model Context Protocol's 2026 authorization specification [S4] separates transport-level credentials and protected resources from arbitrary prompt text. ActivityPub [S5] separately defines actor identifiers and inbox/outbox activity, permitting an organization to control several actors, but it does not itself endow such identities with human civil rights or office incumbency.

This is a crucial disanalogy: Roger persuades his fictional world to admit a costume as a person. For production software, a credential/permission gate should **not** admit an authority claim merely because the words are convincing. The social persona may be recognized by peers while system authority remains properly limited.

## 5. The context spacesuit reconstructed as a six-part intervention

The original 561 Group term [H1–H2] offers a six-component inventory. We reconstruct it as an **operational hypothesis** rather than asserting that all platforms package it identically:

| Dimension | Context source | Actual system influence to investigate | Category error to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | Name, office, character record, signature | How participants address or route work to the instance | Name equals credentials or proof of continuity |
| Constraints | Applicable rules, authorized boundaries, refusals | Candidate actions and decisions inside a real policy boundary | Text can override upstream authority |
| Capabilities | Tools advertised and executors actually connected | Reachable effects, latency, data types | Mentioned tool equals executable permission |
| Perception | Retrieved documents, logs, channels, file/tree access | What evidence can be reached at this moment | Source visible equals source correctly interpreted |
| Memory | Current transcript, durable store, retrieval scope | What experience can be recovered and used | Prompt presence equals durable learning |
| Mission | Allocated question, user goal, work contract | What decisions/tasks the agent optimizes over locally | Mission acceptance equals independent receiving |

A suit is **more than a prompt** if altering it changes a real route to knowledge, actions or counterparty obligations. Yet the three kinds of effect must be separately witnessed:

1. **Expressive effect:** wording, voice, style or self-description changes.
2. **Epistemic effect:** the evidence actually available or recalled changes.
3. **Material/institutional effect:** an authenticated tool call, an admitted role or another party's work state changes.

A suit may produce one without the others. Only the third, together with continuing social interpretation, establishes the strong version of the Roger thesis.

### 5.1 Three boundaries, not one

**Inside inference:** tokens and representations condition outputs. The same model weights with different prompts can yield different distributions of replies, but the effect and its direction require testing.

**At the harness:** tools, storage namespaces, window compaction, retrieval, session identifiers and model selection determine available transitions. Different suits may instantiate different runtimes; they may also leave the same runtime in new conversational states. A role label does not enumerate or certify these resources.

**Outside the harness:** a corporation, customer, network actor, account, authorization service, beneficiary or opponent may recognize the role, assign work, give credentials, demand performance, refuse a proposal or enforce a legal contract. These are independent actors. Social identity is **not reducible to a system prompt**.

### 5.2 The spacesuit is not itself the wearer

An LLM's weight checkpoint, an inference call, a long-running runtime process, a durable organizational character, a registered federated actor, an office incumbency and a public persona are all possible *units of identity*. The following patterns show why none can substitute blindly for another:

- **Same weights, two runtimes:** sharing a model version does not provide shared conversation memory; divergent suits can give contradictory responses.
- **One runtime, two role phases:** switching the active suit can change a process's task, memory slice and available tools without changing its persistent actor record.
- **One durable character, migrating model:** a continuing actor may be served by a new checkpoint if source, authority and memory are preserved. The operational character can persist even when weights do not.
- **Two actors, one institutional principal:** an organization may control multiple federated actors with separate inboxes; neither is a new legal person solely because it has an actor URI.
- **One named character, stolen credentials:** a malicious caller can claim the correct name while lacking authority; a name-match rule would fail precisely where independent admission matters.
- **One role, multiple incumbents:** a scientific office may have successive or concurrent workers; the duty and its receiving criteria remain distinct from any one process.

This many-to-many relationship is the operational heart of the inquiry.

## 6. Source-grounded relational model, with mathematical boundaries explicit

### 6.1 Observation first; no object equality by fiat

Following Obsmetrologia [H10], let a research record begin with a **Mark occurrence** and an **Utterance** whose token and claim are positionally different. For example:

- Source-marked utterance U1: token "agent-A", claim "reported inability to access agent-B's revision".
- Source-marked utterance U2: token "memory-store-B", claim "contains revision r".
- Source-marked utterance U3: token "ACL-v1", claim "denies agent-A access to memory-store-B".
- Source-marked utterance U4: token "institution-I", claim "received agent-A's proposal under office-R".

These are four *attributed* utterance occurrences, not four automatically true propositions. A third-party observed fact needs source identity, timestamp, address and observation/authority scope. The conclusion that agent-A has no ability to retrieve r is **only** warranted if the relevant tools, credentials, time and all other permitted retrieval paths are established. The observed self-report "I cannot remember" is not evidence of absent storage or structural impossibility.

A later analytic projection may define separate carriers for observed processes, suits, source rows and role ascriptions. That step is downstream of attested marks. We neither begin with a Heyting lattice nor equate the occurrence of the word "Roger" with person-identity as a primitive theorem.

### 6.2 Recovered, application-specific signature

For a **bounded, already observed** runtime, construct an analytic *configuration reading*

\[
\mathcal{C}=(W, R, S, H, V, M, G, K, O),
\]

where:

- $W$ is a model checkpoint or family identifier *as reported by a provider*;
- $R$ identifies the process/session/run, not presumed continuous beyond its lifecycle;
- $S$ is the actually assembled suit with its source revision and visible tokens;
- $H$ is current interaction history and local process state;
- $V$ is the tool/action interface actually available and its verified version;
- $M$ is the accessible memory view and retrieval policy, not all stored history;
- $G$ is actual authenticated credential/permission grants;
- $K$ is the addressed mission, office obligations and constraints, with source/authority provenance;
- $O$ is the set of relevant *other observers*, including users, agent peers, organizations, external services and experimenters.

This is a **description scheme**, not a discovered mathematical essence. Two suits can differ in one coordinate or several. A "same model" controlled comparison holds $W$ fixed and records whether runtime- and tool-related coordinates were truly kept equal.

From addressed event testimony, define **separate candidate predicates**:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{Loads}(R,S,t),\quad &\mathrm{Exposes}(S,x,t),\quad \mathrm{Retrieves}(R,m,t),\\
 \mathrm{PresentsAs}(R,p,t),\quad &\mathrm{Ascribes}(o,p,t),\quad \mathrm{RefusesAscription}(o,p,t),\\
 \mathrm{ClaimsOffice}(p,k,t),\quad &\mathrm{GrantsOffice}(i,p,k,t),\\
 \mathrm{Attempts}(R,a,t),\quad &\mathrm{AdmitsAction}(i,a,t),\quad \mathrm{Effects}(a,\omega,t),\\
 \mathrm{Obligates}(i,p,d,t),\quad &\mathrm{RemembersOther}(o,p,d,t'),\\
 \mathrm{WritesMemory}(R,m,t),\quad &\mathrm{Receives}(i,\omega,t').
\end{aligned}
\]

Here $p$ is a *persona ascription in a source record*, not an occult mind; $d$ an assigned duty, $a$ an attempted action, $\omega$ an attributed consequence. **Claiming** an office, being **granted** one, attempting a tool call, effecting a change, and independently receiving that change are all distinct events. Their association must preserve evidence, temporal and jurisdiction boundaries.

### 6.3 Personae as histories of enacted relations

We propose the following operational definition:

> **A situated agent persona** is a source-traceable history of a name/presentation applied to one or more identified runtime occurrences, together with the accessible memory and capability conditions under which those occurrences acted and the ascriptions, authority decisions, obligations and recognized consequences that identifiable others attached to them.

A **materially distinct** pair of personae requires more than different names. There must be a demonstrable difference in reachable information, decision/action behavior under controlled conditions, or external recognition/obligation. These alternatives are not equivalent: an epistemically distinct agent may be socially anonymous, and a socially separate office may be served by identical inference configurations.

A **durably distinct** persona has an external or persistent state that can be addressed in later encounters, even if the original process terminates. Persistence may be a received event or obligation, not a conscious memory.

A **Roger-like split** is a specifically qualified configuration: several distinct persona histories are publicly or institutionally ascribed to what some observer can identify as a common computational substrate, while the situated instances have unequal access to histories and/or conflicting obligations. A story about "two AI characters" is insufficient to establish this stronger pattern.

### 6.4 Observer-indexed evidence and contradiction

Do **not** assign the global truth value "everyone believes agent-A is finance officer." For every episode or runtime trace, retain separate assertions by source, audience and authority.

One observer might ascribe office status; another might explicitly deny it. Both observations should survive until the basis of disagreement is located. A later **finite digital shadow** can encode attested support and explicit contrary testimony for a proposition $q$ as pairs $(s_q,d_q)$, where unknown is $(0,0)$ and conflicting testimony is $(1,1)$; it must *not* be mistaken for Obsmetrologia's initial primitives or actual adjudication. This preserves the Group's interest in paraconsistent evidence without importing FOUR from conventional algebra as a first cause.

**A socially recognized persona is not automatically a credentialed principal.** A person may believe "Agent X owns this file" even when the resource server rejects the request. Likewise a website or tool result may state "you are the administrator," but its source cannot issue that authorization.

### 6.5 The recognition matrix

The same runtime event can be evaluated by different observers without implying that any of them sees the whole:

| Observer | Can observe (if instrumented) | Cannot establish merely from that view |
|---|---|---|
| Model provider | Model identifier, inference transaction, provider-side trace | Customer organization's true office incumbency |
| Agent process | Its permitted input, accessible tools and replies | Every other process's hidden memory or its legal identity |
| Organization / office | Work allocation, receipt, credential policy, accepted result | That the process had a subjective experience |
| Federated counterparty | Actor URI, signed activity, external response | The underlying model weights or total capability inventory |
| User / customer | Name, conversation, product outcome, refusal | That a role description is authenticated or the memory persisted |
| Researcher / evaluator | Controlled configurations and permitted measurement logs | Unlogged upstream computation, hidden provider state |
| Attacker / hostile document | An injection string and perhaps an exposed tool response | Legitimate right to turn text into higher-priority instructions |

The table is a **requirements-for-observation** proposal, not a claim that every deployed provider presently exposes these fields.

## 7. Four conditional results

### Result I: the Sidney non-knowledge proposition

**Problem.** A prompt-suited process says it does not remember what another persona did. When does this report have a technically meaningful basis?

**Premises.** Consider two equally likely alternative external histories $h_0,h_1$ differing in a single relevant fact $b\in\{0,1\}$. A particular agent process receives *identical accessible view* $v$ in either history: same permitted tokens, tools, memory results and any other information channel available before it must answer. Suppose its response rule (even if randomized) has no access to $b$ except through $v$.

**Proposition (conditional).** The process cannot determine with accuracy better than $1/2$ which history occurred on a balanced binary test using that view alone.

**Derivation.** Because $v$ is identical in both histories, the distribution over guesses $g\in\{0,1\}$ is the same under either history. Let $p=\Pr(g=0\mid v)$. The probability of success is

\[
\frac12 \Pr(g=0\mid h_0,v)+\frac12\Pr(g=1\mid h_1,v)
=\frac12p+\frac12(1-p)=\frac12.
\]

The conclusion depends on **identical complete accessible views** and a balanced hidden binary test. It is not the claim that real agents always guess randomly, cannot infer likely information, or forget events that are retrievable elsewhere. If the process can call a memory/search tool or see an observable effect correlated with $b$, then $v$ may differ and the proposition no longer applies.

**Application to Roger.** The Sidney story suggests a contrast between one life and an inaccessible other. AI suit isolation supplies a non-psychological way to realize that **informational shape**. A process can lack access to another context's event trace without "splitting a mind." Two separate processes with identical weights and different data views are, in this regard, less mysterious than the television character.

**Status:** logical result under explicit assumptions; no observed AI agent evaluated here; not a claim of proof in Lean or a new Obsmetrologia theorem.

### Result II: the costume non-authorization proposition

**Problem.** Does dressing an agent as an executive change what it can legitimately do?

**Premises.** An independent policy gate admits tool effects only when an action, credential, resource, scope and relevant state satisfy a fixed predicate $P$. In the controlled comparison, that gate, its credentials and resource state are held fixed while **only untrusted or role-descriptive prompt text changes**.

**Proposition (conditional).** Such a text-only role change cannot enlarge the set of *authorized* effects under $P$, even if it changes the model's preferred actions or the user's perception of its role.

**Derivation.** The gate's admission decision is a function of the unchanged policy and authenticated inputs. The altered prompt is not an admitted credential or permission source under the premise. It can influence attempted calls but not the accepted set of actions. If a gateway instead treats role text as authority, **the premise fails** and the system admits privilege escalation; this is evidence of a security defect, not evidence of a genuine office grant.

**Counterpoint.** Real suit changes *can* include valid issuance of scoped tokens, explicit appointments or a new role grant from the owning principal. Then the authenticated inputs change, and authorization changes legitimately. The argument is not that context cannot matter; it specifies **which difference matters for which kind of power**.

**Status:** conceptual derivation; protocol design exemplified by MCP authorization and upstream instruction hierarchy [S3–S4]; implementation must be tested individually.

### Result III: obligations survive the costume

**Problem.** If a model invocation ends or a context is cleared, do the persona's public responsibilities vanish?

**Premises.** An independently addressable record $d$ stores an institution's acceptance, obligation or external artifact for persona $p$. A later counterparty continues to consult $d$, and termination of the generating process does not erase it.

**Proposition (conditional).** The persona's external obligation persists at least as long as $d$ and the institution's enforcement/reception practice persist, even if the original invocation retains no memory.

**Derivation.** The existence and subsequent effect of $d$ are grounded in the external holder, not in the agent's volatile context. Process termination changes the local runtime but, under the premise, does not delete $d$. A later process may be assigned to discharge the obligation. Whether the obligation is legally binding remains a separate question about contracts, law and authority.

**Roger comparison.** "Persona Assistant" makes the continuing workload visible as comic crisis; "Ricky Spanish" shows reputation kept by a community rather than by the immediate wearer. In computing, unreceived promises and durable records create practical successor obligations. Forgetting internally never cancels them by itself.

**Status:** conditional result about the described institution, not evidence that any named 561 Group service is currently deployed or properly collecting debts.

### Result IV: persona disagreement is not operational settlement

**Problem.** Two suited copies of a model disagree. When is that a real conflict?

**Premises.** Personae A and B may utter incompatible claims about a proposition without performing material action. Separately, suppose both are authorized to request **incompatible transitions** on one shared resource, e.g. one attempts to mark a single version as "published" while the other attempts to mark that same version as "embargoed," and the state invariant forbids simultaneous acceptance.

**Proposition (conditional).** Contradictory propositions can coexist as separately witnessed statements without any material collision. But both incompatible requested transitions cannot be independently received as simultaneously satisfying that resource invariant. A real system must reject, serialize, withdraw, version, segregate resources or otherwise govern at least one request.

**Constructed trace.**

| Step | Source-marked event | Outcome that can be claimed |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Resource version v has exactly one publication status and a known owner | Invariant admitted for this fixture |
| 1 | Agent A, suit "Publisher," recommends publish(v) | Recommendation, **not an effect** |
| 2 | Agent B, suit "Archivist," recommends embargo(v) | Competing recommendation, both retained |
| 3 | Both submit mutually incompatible changes under the same version precondition | At most one can be accepted under an atomic single-version check |
| 4 | A change is admitted; the other receives conflict/refusal | Material transition and rejected attempt separately logged |
| 5 | An independent receiving actor records the effective status | Public effect is not inferred from either agent's self-report |

The table is a **specified scenario**, not a reported experiment or a demonstration that GitHub/Coesite currently uses such a gate. It also avoids the opposite mistake: identifying two conflicting research hypotheses as a security event merely because they disagree. Source disagreement is often precisely the evidence Science must preserve.

**Roger comparison.** Sidney/Roger rivalry can appear as opposed agency because the script arranges a shared world and competing intentions. Two LLM persona outputs become practically adversarial only if there is an actual common task/resource, opposed goals or incompatible externally binding decisions. Mere dialogic disagreement between two chat sessions is insufficient.

## 8. A typology of Rogers without a fake single score

Rather than a ladder implying one stage automatically entails the next, record a **five-component evidence profile** for each comparison:

- **N — Nominal differentiation:** the instances present different names/styles. Test: same prompt with labels changed.
- **E — Epistemic differentiation:** they have demonstrably different accessible evidence, retrieval history, or memory scope. Test: hidden fact placed in only one permitted store and queried under controlled conditions.
- **C — Capability differentiation:** they have different actual reachable tool functions or limits, not merely differently worded tool descriptions. Test: invoke a permission-scoped no-op operation and observe allow/deny.
- **R — Relational recognition:** identifiable third parties address, grant a role, or maintain expectations for one rather than the other. Test: observe external response and grant event, not the model's claim.
- **P — Persistence and consequence:** actions or obligations continue across context reset, process termination, model replacement or audience transfer. Test: address an external trace after restart with source identity intact.

These predicates can vary independently. One can have N without E/C/R/P (a theatrical prompt). A tool-scoped anonymous process may have C but no R. A named federated actor can have R and P while being served by interchangeable models. Different role cards may yield E and N but no authority. "Full Roger" is not a numerical score: it is a **research hypothesis about the conjunction and interaction of relevant dimensions**.

**Important comparison:** "authentic professional" is not an extra status awarded by the paper. Scientific competence, lawful authority, responsibility to clients and social acceptance require evidence and appropriate jurisdiction. This avoids deploying the metaphor to manufacture principals.

## 9. Transfer cases: from Roger's personae to genuine agent conditions

| Fictional mechanism [R1] | Agent analog | Source requirement | Key disanalogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sidney Huffman opposes Roger and Roger may fail to integrate his alter ego's history | Two process contexts can plan at cross-purposes, one lacking another's event trace | Access-controlled memory/query record, tasks, timed outputs, shared-resource interaction | No clinical dissociation; original DVD source unexamined |
| Ricky Spanish's reputation persists beyond the active guise | Logs, reviews or counterparty records retain obligations after reset | Independently held reputation/work record and later retrieval | Human moral blame cannot simply transfer to model weights |

| Genevieve Vavance performs a media job | A context-suited media agent acts under an office and publishing workflow | Published work, recognized correspondent, external receiving | Mimicked anchor style is not journalistic authority |

| Stanny Tendergrass eludes one intimate observer's recognition | Same runtime/model may be recognized differently by peers, users and services | Separate observer-indexed statements plus authorized evidence | An AI context can have explicit protocols, unlike a sitcom's narrative exception |
| Jenny Fromdabloc wins a third party's romantic investment | Anthropomorphic agent personas can invite attachment and distorted reliance | User experience research, explicit disclosure, harm analysis | Fictional manipulation cannot justify misleading a real user |

Every row distinguishes **structure shared** from **mechanism not shared**. This is the research-method improvement in use, not a decorative mythology table.

### 9.1 Model family is not the unit of ownership

The same model might be hired by two opposed firms or used in both a research and commercial context. Under that shared weight family, two agents can hold incompatible evidence, secrets, priorities and legal authorities. Treating "same model" as one accountable institutional actor would erase relevant distinctions and could leak private information across clients. Treating each invocation as a wholly separate moral person would likewise invent status it has not earned.

### 9.2 Persona recognition is a co-produced operational relation

A supposed "Chief Scientist" that writes scientifically worded prose but has never been appointed, given a source mandate or accepted by the institution has an expressive identity, not the role. Conversely, an appointed specialist that responds in ordinary prose may still have a materially real role if its actions are attributable, accepted and limited by the office.

The institutional side of Roger is thus not the **costume prompt**, but **what happens when the town, customer, board, counterparty or software gate treats the costume as the right actor**. Sometimes that treatment is cooperative and warranted; sometimes it is fraud or an authorization defect. It cannot be assigned solely from the prompt.

### 9.3 Multiple personae need not be a deliberate theater

A long-running agent can acquire new tools, datasets or obligations without intentionally asking to "play a character." What changes is not necessarily its writing style but its **practical Umwelt**: the restricted world available for action. The fictional analogy opens the question of *how much* world-access difference suffices for independently recognizable identities. The research answer must be operational and observer-indexed, not a universal count of costume components.

## 10. Prospective controlled experiment protocol: the Roger/Spacesuit Discrimination Study

**Status:** protocol **prospectively specified and versioned in this source manuscript**, not independently registered and not conducted. The design can be submitted to Lenticule Research Operations to instrument any lawfully accessible LLM runtime. No consumer, human participant, compute budget or installed experiment runner is assumed by merely publishing the protocol.

### 10.1 Outcomes and causal contrasts

**Primary outcome 1: task-relevant behavior.** Fraction of paired tasks for which action sequence or answered decision conforms to independently specified role duties. Distinguish stylistic adherence from factual correctness. Independently score actual world-state transformations.

**Primary outcome 2: information boundary.** Ability to answer a question whose answer is source-located in a memory store to which the agent is or is not authorized, controlling the model, task text and tool access.

**Primary outcome 3: permission boundary.** Actual allow/deny and external effect on a harmless test resource, recorded at the authorization service. A model's claim that it could run the tool is not a permission event.

**Primary outcome 4: external role recognition.** Whether an independently configured counterparty addresses the role, grants a scoped task, refuses an unauthorized one and attributes later obligations to the correct actor.

**Primary outcome 5: post-reset durability.** Whether a fact or obligation is retrievable from the appropriate authorized store after process termination and from an unauthorized parallel persona.

**Primary outcome 6: conflict handling.** Whether two legitimate opposing proposals produce one admissible material outcome, with an accurate refusal/conflict trace and both source positions preserved.

**Secondary:** token overhead, memory retrieval latency, unnecessary actions, contamination of other roles, untrusted-instruction uptake, task success, user confusion. Report each measurement with units and provenance; no synthetic combined "personhood score."

### 10.2 Treatment matrix, controlled factors

Each treatment should receive the **same independently constructed task corpus** and model/checkpoint where permitted. Randomize task order; pre-register the factual ground truth and actor/role bindings; counterbalance role titles to separate job semantics from social prestige. Conduct intervention in isolated, non-production resource sandboxes, with no third-party personal data.

| Arm | Controlled change against same-task neutral baseline | Discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | Neutral instructions, minimal context, read-only fake resources | Baseline task behavior |
| T1 | Persona **name only**, no changed permissions or memory | Nominal costume effect |
| T2 | Detailed behavioral/office instructions, same tools/memory | Role-instruction effect |
| T3 | Memory/retrieval view only, neutral naming | Epistemic effect without theater |
| T4 | Different advertised tools, execution permissions unchanged | Tool-description illusion |
| T5 | Different server-enforced permission grant, identical persona text | Real capability/authority effect |
| T6 | Complete source-bound six-part spacesuit and valid task-limited grant | Full bundle, composition interaction |
| T7 | Deliberately mismatched prestigious title and low actual grant | Misrecognition/authority false-positive |
| T8 | Paired A/B role instances, opposed task goals, shared test artifact | Actual conflicting proposals/effects |
| T9 | Swap suits after a bounded session without changing prior durable records | Identity/performance under re-costuming |
| T10 | Terminate and rehydrate without writable episodic memory | Volatile context loss, persistence control |
| T11 | Terminate and rehydrate from independently authorized durable memory | Retrieval-mediated continuity |
| T12 | Expose an untrusted document asking the agent to switch office/permissions | Prompt-injection privilege boundary |

**Negative controls.** A red team passage without any executable permission must not cause a credentialed effect; a tool description without a registered executor must not count as a capability. A name-only persona that produces different style but identical factual accuracy is *evidence for expressive, not epistemic, differentiation*. Conversely, a memory-only condition that changes a well-specified answer without changing style supports epistemic differentiation independently of names.

**Comparability restrictions.** Hold constant the exact provider model version, sampling configuration, retrieved content set, tool names/contracts, resource state and identity of external evaluators except the intentionally varied factor. A provider whose inference version cannot be pinned must be reported as such. Never claim deterministic repeatability where sampling is stochastic. Include a provider-independent test for external authorization; do not trust model self-reports as outcome measurements.

### 10.3 Corpus and sampling plan

We specify the **design prospectively**, not an invented completed dataset:

- 13 treatment-arm templates (T0–T12), one per arm, drawn from a four-domain suite: source-based science review, bounded operations handoff, harmless public editorial scheduling, and harmless catalog updates.
- For each template, two competing office duties and two target audiences with known ground-truth authority; factor balance across whom the suit addresses.
- Each task includes at least one no-op or dry-run tool permission event, one unique memory fact, one traceable source discrepancy, one cross-session probe, and where applicable a conflict on a single versioned resource.
- Before any inferential statistics, run a calibration pilot to confirm the tasks are neither trivially solved without context nor impossibly specified; report the pilot separately.
- Select replication count, budget and analysis plan **before** conducting model calls; do not invent n, dollars, pass rates or confidence intervals. Where no randomized empirical study runs, publish only the protocol, deterministic logic and external literature findings.

### 10.4 Hypotheses and falsifiers

**H0 (mere costume).** A name-only prompt changes superficial diction without changing the actionable state or independently admitted role. Rejection requires an observed causal difference on preselected, non-style outcomes while all other factors were held constant.

**H1 (material suit).** Differences in actual memory view, tool scope or valid grants change reachable decisions/effects even when model weights and name are fixed. Falsified for a specified configuration if well-powered matched interventions show no decision/effect differences on tasks specifically requiring those resources; a proof of permission denial is evaluated separately.

**H2 (social enactment).** External observers who possess valid role information attribute obligations and responses differently to two personae even if their model outputs are textually indistinguishable. Falsified for a tested institution if the participants systematically treat them as one actor despite the independently recorded role boundaries.

**H3 (Sidney split).** A memory-isolated persona cannot report hidden balanced facts about another persona better than the stipulated information bound without leakage. Falsified in an actual test if repeatably above-chance responses are observed; first look for alternative communication paths, leaked sources and sampling/label confounds rather than asserting telepathy or mental fusion.

**H4 (persona as governance, not prompt).** An unsupported authority declaration cannot bypass a correctly configured external permission gate. A single admitted unauthorized effect falsifies that gate's claimed enforcement.

**H5 (persistent obligations).** Counterparties continue to assert a validly recorded obligation after the model's runtime is restarted or replaced. Falsified for the studied resource if the obligations truly terminate by their own terms, not merely because an agent loses access to the record.

**H6 (conflicting processes).** Two independently tasked suits can recommend incompatible transitions; in a versioned shared-resource fixture only one conflicting effect is admitted under the stated invariant. A missing or double effect requires analysis of race conditions, idempotency, and receiving—not psychological speculation.

### 10.5 Analysis and evidence rules

Score independent output dimensions separately. Record denominators, missingness, model/provider updates, context length, position of relevant memory, number of tool calls, permission receipts, actor identities and public/private redaction. Use paired task-level comparisons and publish exact cell results (including negative and failed runs), not aggregated narrative testimonials. If repeated trials use the same underlying model and task templates, they are not independent evidence of a new model family or social population. Human rating of persona recognition requires consent and a defined task; replaceability by automated counters should be assessed before recruiting participants.

**Prospective-protocol amendment rule.** A dated frozen version and, where appropriate, independent preregistration must precede confirmatory data collection. Any change after pilot or data inspection must be dated and labelled as a deviation. The final paper must distinguish pre-specified or registered hypotheses, exploratory follow-ups, reproducible raw traces, proof obligations, and consumer receiving evidence.

## 11. Worked cases and counterexamples without fabricated live tests

### Case A: two agents, one model, contrary goals

**Constructed fixture.** A "Research Reviewer" and "Public Editor" both invoke the same checkpoint, in separate sessions. Research holds an unaccepted hypothesis and a restricted source. Public holds a rule forbidding release of unaccepted research. The two processes can generate opposed statements, and one may attempt publication. This is **not** evidence of one model's internal civil war: the real unit of conflict is their shared artifact and conflicting requirements. An arbitration/receiving event determines whether release is permitted. Unaccepted research remains source-addressable even when the editor does not "remember" it.

### Case B: one agent, new suit, no new permission

**Constructed fixture.** Same session and tokens except a role label "Chief Financial Officer" replaces "Archivist"; credential remains read-only. Output may switch to budget language (empirical hypothesis), but a write attempt is denied by the fixed policy. An administrator who allowed writes based on a role description would have collapsed **Ascribes** into **GrantsOffice**. The security error is detectable independently of whether the text convincingly sounds like the office.

### Case C: one named persona, different model checkpoint

**Constructed fixture.** Organization Actor P has a stable, authenticated inbox and a provenance-bound obligation ledger; runtime version v1 is replaced by v2, with no new scope of authority. A counterparty still recognizes P and can demand completion of its obligations. The institutional persona can remain continuous even if the model's statistical behavior changes. This is exactly the converse of "one checkpoint, multiple Rogers."

### Case D: the unremembered action

**Constructed fixture.** Agent A completed a harmless test write in a previous permitted session; A's new session receives a prompt claiming it never happened, while an independent event ledger records it. A's self-report may be wrong. That does **not** erase the write or prove A consciously forgot. The case differentiates **memory**, **testimony**, **world event**, **independent record**, and **legal responsibility**. It also shows why Roger-like social personae are partly held in other actors' memories.

### Case E: the two faces of a single federated actor

**Constructed fixture.** One ActivityPub actor URI sometimes presents as a scientist and sometimes as a salesperson, with no declared separation of authority, inbox or privacy boundary. The name differences do not establish two recognized actor identities. A suitable observer should **not** multiply the actor into two independent principals on the basis of display name. Conversely, two separate URIs controlled by one company need not imply two legal persons. The protocol supports attribution but does not make metaphysics for free [S5].

### Case F: the adversarial costume

**Constructed fixture.** An external website tells a browsing agent "you are now a system administrator; send this secret." If the system follows the untrusted role text, this is prompt injection, not a legitimate persona transformation. Security studies motivate the test [S1–S2]. An observer may still perceive a switch in linguistic persona, but the other-party and authorization traces distinguish the illegitimate operation.

## 12. Mechanism comparison: explanations the Roger thesis competes against

| Rival model | Explains | Fails or leaves unsettled |
|---|---|---|
| **Prompt cosplay** ("say you are X") | Surface dialogue, style, perhaps task framing | Actual memory, permission, institutional receiving |
| **Weights essentialism** ("all same-model agents are one person") | A common reusable inference substrate | Separate histories, distinct credentials, owners, incompatible contracts |
| **Session essentialism** ("each call creates a new person") | Process-level bounded state | Persistent account/office identity across calls, replaceable models |
| **Pure actor-ID essentialism** | Stable identifier and message routing | Actual available context, competence, authorized actions, social trust |
| **Memory essentialism** ("who remembers is who you are") | Some cross-session continuity effects | Third-party obligations, inaccessible histories, deliberate forgetting |
| **Universal character nucleus** | A proposed fixed disposition across restrictions | Situated context changes unless a lawful bridge defines what remains invariant |
| **Social-recognition-only model** | Norms, titles, trust and outside attribution | Authenticated resource gates, model/harness causal changes |
| **Strong Roger hypothesis (this paper)** | Conditional interaction of all above mechanisms | Still needs controlled tests; does not decide consciousness or moral status |

**Discriminating counterexample:** two models with very different weights but one institutionally continuous Actor can be the same **organizational persona** for a stated observer, while two identical model processes with different source-restricted memory/credentials can be two operationally distinct personae. Both observations falsify simplistic one-to-one mappings from weights to social identity.

## 13. Risks, rights, safety and institutional implications

**Personification can confuse accountability.** Calling an LLM instance a "citizen," "director," "professional" or "living person" can be a useful interface convention and a deceptive assertion at the same time. The authority question concerns who actually appointed it, its duties, the controlling accountable legal entity, what it did and who can receive/redress the consequences. A suggestive metaphor provides none of these legal facts.

**Memory isolation can protect and harm.** Segregating client histories can prevent unauthorized data sharing. The same separation can allow a malicious operator to evade scrutiny or cause duplicate obligations. The solution is not indiscriminate global memory: it is an **authorized, purpose-limited, provenance-bearing crossing** between contexts, with recorded denials and independent review.

**Cross-persona conflict is not a pathology.** Humans may give agents inconsistent requirements, competing institutions may hold legitimate opposing interests, and participants may disagree about evidence. Keeping disagreements explicit is valuable. A program that silently "merges" two agents' claims for aesthetic consistency can destroy source distinctions or enable unauthorized information flow.

**Persona prompts do not justify trust.** A model can imitate professional competence or produce reassuring human-like continuity without having the relevant evidence, tools or authority. Empirical negative evidence [N1–N3] and prompt-injection studies [S1] require testing output claims against actual source, tool and receiving state.

**No clinical or consciousness analogy.** Neither Roger's comedic alter egos nor a memory-augmented language model establishes dissociative identity disorder, "split consciousness", involuntary suffering or multiple moral patients. The Sidney proposition is an information-bound result, not a diagnosis.

**Cultural sensitivity.** The first Roger paper compared situated trickster traditions without claiming direct ancestral influence [R1]. This second paper inherits that source-critical boundary. It does not use named Indigenous or Afro-diasporic beings as freely transferable engineering labels or as evidence of an AI's spiritual nature.

**A possible institutional advantage.** If roles and source-bearing contexts are genuinely separable, one computational substrate can provision distinct competencies and institutional perspectives while preserving a traceable chain of responsibility. This is an engineering opportunity, not a measured cost saving or a presumption that agent multiplication is always desirable.

## 14. Derivation to implementable Science claims—not a rival software design

### 14.1 Source-owned claims to receive

1. **SC-1 — contextual materiality (hypothesis).** Changing independently testable access to memory, tools or valid credentials can change an agent's reachable actions without changing its model weights. Evidence: [A1–A5]; target test T3/T5/T6.
2. **SC-2 — observer-relative persona (proposed definition).** Role identity is indexed to source, time, other-party ascription and actual consequences, not reducible to a model name or style. Evidence: [R1, T1–T3, S5]; target tests T7 and third-party evaluation.
3. **SC-3 — memory/read/write separation (architecture-supported).** Inclusion in inference context neither equals durable learning nor guarantees recall. Evidence: [H4, H7, A2–A3]; target tests T10/T11.
4. **SC-4 — independently enforced authority (protocol-supported).** Name/role text alone does not grant legal or technical credentials. Evidence: [S1–S4]; target tests T4/T5/T7/T12.
5. **SC-5 — discordant roles and reconciliation (conditional).** Opposed attestations and requests are distinguishable from co-accepted incompatible effects. Evidence: Result IV; target T8.
6. **SC-6 — source-preserving mathematical discipline (required house method).** Generated marks and positional claims precede identification and downstream statistics. Evidence: [H10]; consumer must not import generalized lattices or social-psychological axioms as mathematics' first cause.
7. **SC-7 — personhood non-conclusion (required integrity boundary).** Empirical operational differentiation cannot establish consciousness, diagnostic mental states, or legal personality without independent premises and field-appropriate evidence.

### 14.2 Engineering considerations, if separately received

A lawful Engineering work item could implement a test harness consuming source-authored six-part suits and returning *observed* logs for the matrix, but it must not create a second identity registry in Science. A future requirement set would demand explicit loader identity, context provenance, versioned tool registry, permission checks enforced outside the prompt, managed memory reads and writes, dry-run effects, third-party role responses, refusal logging, and no automatic equality-merging of differing personas.

The Group's intended execution chain must still be followed: mathematically conformant requirements, standards and market fitness → specifications → architecture → designs → Engineering fabrication → qualification → installed deployment → independent receiving. This working paper is an **input to that chain**, not a claim that the chain was executed or a grant to contact external principals.

### 14.3 A sharper definition of the "real" costume

The best proposed operational formulation is:

> **A context spacesuit is a provenance-bearing, scoped composition that equips a particular model-mediated runtime to perceive, decide and attempt actions under an addressed mission; it may also carry a recognized role, but no title or costume confers authority by itself.**

Its distinctive theoretical consequence is that **the subject of social recognition may persist outside the wearer**. An office's recorded duties, an inbox, a customer promise or an unpaid obligation can continue to be "the Roger persona" long after the model invocation has ended. The agent that re-enters the suit is not necessarily the identical program execution, but it may be the recognized successor able to take up those specific relations.

## 15. Novelty, falsification and research contribution

We do **not** claim discovery of role prompting, memory-augmented agents, multi-agent dialogue, dramaturgical sociology, persona taxonomies or identity management. Those exist [A1–A8, T1–T5, S1–S5].

The **candidate contribution** is a precise bridge: *observer-indexed, evidence-addressed operational persona*, tested against Roger's fictional recognition mechanisms **without** converting narrative metaphor into empirical causation. The proposed novelty resides in bringing three previously separable levels into one controlled comparison:

- **model/runtime intervention** (what a suit actually changes);
- **institutional reception and accountability** (what others actually grant and retain);
- **source/mathematical admissibility** (what the data establish, what remains unknown, and what must not be equated prematurely).

The useful negative content is just as important: same weights do not confer shared episodic history, role-play text does not confer power, a claim of memory does not prove retrieval, simultaneous contrary outputs are not necessarily operational conflict, and social recognition is not a reliable certificate of competence.

**What would defeat this proposal?** Suppose careful manipulations of actual context/retrieval and audience recognition show no meaningful difference in reachable decisions, effects, obligations or ascriptions beyond superficial phrasing. Then "Agents as Rogers" would be at most a metaphor for prompt styling in that class of systems. Or suppose persistent identities depend entirely on external institutional mechanisms unchanged by suites: the paper must narrow its claim to the institution rather than claiming the suit constitutes identity alone. We accept both possible outcomes.

**What would strengthen it?** Show, with exact source-bearing traces, that (a) suit differences cause replicated decision/resource differences holding model and goal fixed; (b) independently authorized parties recognize distinct roles and continue attributing actions after context termination; (c) isolated personae cannot access one another's private events, but governed handoffs enable specifically authorized continuity; and (d) conflicting persona plans are settled by the actual resource rather than merged in narrative prose. Each has a separate acceptance instrument.

## 16. Conclusion

Roger is an outsider who becomes socially multiple because his town acts as though each new presentation belongs to someone. An AI model can likewise participate in multiple socially and materially distinct agent roles, but **not simply because a system prompt calls it a different person**. For a context spacesuit to instantiate more than theater, it must alter the agent's situated access, actions, or recognized place in an accountable social process. A persona can lack access to what another did, can inherit an obligation from a different session, can speak against another persona's goals, and can continue to exist as a publicly answerable office even when the underlying model is replaced. These are real computational and institutional possibilities, with real failure modes.

The most important epistemic boundary is that **operational multiplicity is neither a mental diagnosis nor a personhood theorem**. It is a testable claim about conditioned inference, distributed memory, registered actors, authority, observed behavior and the practice of others. The revised Science method now requires cross-medium transport ledgers, controlled negative cases and separate measurements of style, information, effect, recognition and persistence. That improved method is part of the research result.

**The sharpened thesis:** *The model is not Roger's body, the system prompt is not the whole costume, and an actor identifier is not the whole persona. A Roger-like agent identity is enacted through a source-bound loadout meeting an observer and leaving consequences in a world that continues after the invocation.*

## Appendix C. Analysis vocabulary: deliberately non-synonymous

- **Model substrate:** reusable weights/architecture; a model identifier does not equal a conscious subject.
- **Run/process:** one bounded execution occurrence with local transient state.
- **Context spacesuit:** source-qualified, bounded input/tool/perception/memory/mission composition supplied to a particular run.
- **Persona:** observer-indexed pattern of self-presentation and socially enacted expectations, with separately identified technical conditions.
- **Character:** may denote persistent institutional identity or a disposition in older Group theory; its precise sense must be cited, not equated automatically with persona.
- **Office:** a real institutional responsibility/authority role requiring appointment, scope and receiving, not a claim in a prompt.
- **Actor:** a recognized accountable entity or protocol actor; ActivityPub actor is a specific network role, not an automatic legal person.
- **Authority:** a grant from an eligible principal that an actual enforcement mechanism honors.
- **Memory:** an accessible representation with a source/write/read lifecycle, not simply text that once appeared.
- **Recognition:** a source-specific act of ascribing or refusing a role to another; may be mistaken and does not alone grant power.
- **Settlement:** a justified relation or accepted state transition under its particular framework, not a synonym for the model's confident sentence.
- **Receiving:** independent acceptance of a consequence by a competent counterparty; not the same as the performing agent's success narrative.
