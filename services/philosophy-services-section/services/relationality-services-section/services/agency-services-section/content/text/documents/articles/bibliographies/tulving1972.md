# Tulving 1972

Tulving, E. (1972). Episodic and Semantic Memory. In E.
Tulving & W. Donaldson (Eds.), *Organization of Memory*
(pp. 381-403). Academic Press.

The founding statement of the *episodic / semantic* distinction
in human memory. Tulving argues that memory of specific events
(episodic) and memory of general facts (semantic) are
functionally distinct memory systems with different encoding,
retrieval, and forgetting dynamics — not just different
contents of a unified store.

The distinction has structured cognitive psychology and AI
memory architecture for half a century. It reappears in
contemporary debates about whether LLMs have episodic memory
(they have something semantic-like in weights; whether they
have anything episodic-like is contested).
