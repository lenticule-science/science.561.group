## Annotation

A taxonomy of eleven methodological approaches to domain analysis (literature guides, special classifications, indexing and retrieval studies, empirical user studies, bibliometrics, historical studies, document and genre studies, epistemological and critical studies, terminological/database studies, structures and institutions, cognition/AI/expert systems). The paper's contribution is showing that no one approach captures a discourse community fully; each is a *projection* of the community's predicate vocabulary onto a different observable surface.

The predicated framing reads each of Hjørland's eleven approaches as a *closure-rule signature* — a sublanguage of [closure-rule-language](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/closure-rule-language.md) sufficient to reconstruct one face of the community's fiber algebra. Bibliometrics is closure over citation predicates; terminological studies are closure over name-equivalence predicates; institutional studies are closure over membership predicates. Each generates a distinct closure regime over the same underlying fiber, and federation of approaches is federation of closure regimes (covered by [federation-as-bilattice-colimit](../../../glossary/concepts/federation-as-bilattice-colimit.md)).

The paper licences a key methodological commitment of the framing: *no single closure regime fully characterises a discourse community*. The substrate must support multiple co-existing regimes over the same predicate vocabulary, with controlled translation between them — which the [predicate-category-stratification](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/archive/teaching/explanations/predicate-category-stratification.md) concept names.
