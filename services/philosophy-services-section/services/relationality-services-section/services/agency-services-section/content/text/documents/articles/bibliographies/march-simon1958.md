## Full citation

J.G. March, H.A. Simon. *Organizations.* Wiley, 1958.
(Second edition: Blackwell, 1993.)

## Status

Foundational.
Cited as canonical organizational-theory background.

## Annotation

March and Simon's account of organizations as *bounded-rational* coordinations of routines, programs, and decision premises is the disciplinary context for predicated-work's claim that work is *closure-driven*.
Their *programs* — recurrent decision sequences triggered by stimuli — correspond structurally to predicated-work's [recurrent-practice](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/content/glossary/terms/recurrent-practice.md): a closure-rule subsystem whose firings repeat at a cadence.
Their *decision premises* — the inherited frames within which choices are made — correspond to the [enterprise](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/enterprise.md)'s hard core $C$.

The relevance to [aim-generativity](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/social-theory-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/aim-generativity.md): March and Simon distinguish *programmed* from *non-programmed* decisions, where the latter are made under genuine uncertainty about what the alternatives are.
The non-programmed regime is exactly the aim-generative one — and per [aim-generativity-iff-aperiodic](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/social-theory-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/aim-generativity-iff-aperiodic.md), it is the aperiodic regime in the substrate-algebraic sense.
