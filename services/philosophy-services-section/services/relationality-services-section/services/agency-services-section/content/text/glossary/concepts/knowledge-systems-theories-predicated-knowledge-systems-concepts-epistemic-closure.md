# Epistemic closure

The community's commitment to "if I accept these premises and these rules, I accept whatever those premises and rules entail" is the substrate's [closure-rule system](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/closure-rule-system.md) applied to the [predication-settling](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) over the community's scope. *Epistemic closure* is not a separate philosophical principle layered on top of the substrate; it is the substrate's settlement dynamic.

The substantive substrate-level fact about epistemic closure: it is **regime-classified**. The [four-regime dichotomy](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/services/obsmetrological-services-section/lib/obsmetrologia/docs/verso/mark/content/utterance/articulating/content/articulation-cell/content/potential-articulation-cell/predicating/content/predication-cell/content/potential-predication-cell/positing/content/valuation/content/knowledge-least-valuation/settling/references/four-regime-dichotomy.md) classifies a community's epistemic closure as one of:

- **Strict** — the community's closure terminates in finite stages over any finite premise set. Every closure consequence is reachable in bounded time; the community's epistemic horizon is bounded by the finite stage it reaches before quiescence.
- **Scott-continuous** — the community's closure terminates at $\omega$, with each $\omega$-stage consequence derivable from a finite earlier stage. Standard Datalog-style monotone derivation; the community can in principle reach any consequence given enough time on a single premise-set.
- **Transfinite-bounded** — the community's closure terminates at some countable ordinal $\kappa < \omega_1$ but requires limit-stage consequences not derivable from any single earlier finite stage. Stratified rule systems with admissible quantification over derivation-stages.
- **Aperiodic** — the community's closure has firings at countably many limit ordinals, no two of which are reducible to each other. The community admits closure rules whose application requires synthesising across countably many prior derivations.

A community choosing to extend its closure-rule language can move between regimes; the move is not gradual — adding even one closure rule of the right shape can promote a Scott-continuous community to the transfinite-bounded regime. The community's regime is a substrate-level invariant that controls *what kinds of things the community can come to know*.

The traditional philosophical-logic question "is epistemic closure under classical logical entailment correct?" gets a substrate-level reframing: the question is *which closure-rule system* the community admits, not whether the community admits some closure-rule system. The substrate carries the closure-rule system as content; different communities can admit different systems on the same substrate; the substrate's [federation](../terms/federate.md) discipline handles cross-community closure-system reconciliation as a coherence-glue operation.

The contrast with the upstream literature (per [predication-logic-through-knowledge-systems](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/texts/predication-logic-through-knowledge-systems.md)): RDF-without-rules has no epistemic closure; OWL has classical-DL closure; Datalog has Scott-continuous fixpoint closure; description-logic-with-modal-extensions has transfinite-bounded closure under suitable axiom sets. The substrate is general over all of them — the closure regime is *chosen by the community*, not baked into the substrate.
