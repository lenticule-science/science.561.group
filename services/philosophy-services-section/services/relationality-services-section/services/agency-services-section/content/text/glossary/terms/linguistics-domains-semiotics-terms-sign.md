A sign is something that stands for something else to an interpreter — a three-place relation among sign, object, and interpretant.

A sign is something that stands for something else to an interpreter.

This is [Peirce&rsquo;s](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) formulation, and it is more careful than it sounds. The sign relation is triadic: it involves the sign itself (the [representamen](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/representamen.md)), the object the sign stands for, and the [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) — the meaning produced in the interpreter by the sign. Without all three, there is no sign: a mark without an interpreter produces no meaning, and a meaning without a mark is not yet communicable [@peirce_CollectedPapers_1931].

The [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) is the crucial third term. It is the functional effect the sign produces — the sign&rsquo;s &ldquo;meaning&rdquo; in the sense of what it does, what it allows the interpreter to do or infer or feel. The [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) is itself a sign (of the same object), which is why [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md)&rsquo;s [semiotics](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/index.md) is intrinsically dynamic: signs generate [interpretants](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) that are themselves signs that generate further [interpretants](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md), producing an open-ended chain of [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/semiosis.md) [@short_PeirceTheorySigns_2007].

## Types of signs

[Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) classified signs in multiple intersecting ways. Three basic types by the relation between sign and object:

- **[icon](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/icon.md)**: resembles its object (a portrait, a diagram)

- **[Index](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/index-sign.md)**: is causally or spatially connected to its object (smoke indexing fire, a finger pointing)

- **[symbol](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/symbol.md)**: is connected to its object by convention (most words)
