The cash conversion cycle (CCC) measures how many days pass between when a business pays its suppliers and when it collects cash from customers.
It answers: how long is my cash tied up in operations?

**CCC = Days inventory outstanding + Days sales outstanding − Days payable outstanding**

- **Days inventory outstanding (DIO)**: How long [inventory](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/inventory.md) sits before being sold.
  A restaurant using ingredients within 3–5 days has low DIO.
  A retailer holding merchandise for 45 days has high DIO.
- **Days sales outstanding (DSO)**: How long [accounts receivable](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-receivable.md) takes to collect.
  A cash-and-carry restaurant has DSO near zero.
  A catering company with Net 30 invoices has DSO around 30–40 days.
- **Days payable outstanding (DPO)**: How long the business takes to pay its [accounts payable](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-payable.md).
  If suppliers offer Net 30 terms and the business pays on day 28, DPO is 28.

**Example**: A catering business buys ingredients (pays supplier in 15 days), holds inventory for 3 days, delivers the catering order, and collects from the client in 35 days.

CCC = 3 (DIO) + 35 (DSO) − 15 (DPO) = **23 days**

For 23 days, the business has paid for ingredients but hasn't collected from the client.
That gap must be funded from [working capital](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/financing/content/cash-conversion-cycle/working-capital.md) or a [line of credit](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/financing/content/cash-conversion-cycle/line-of-credit.md).

A shorter CCC means less cash tied up in operations.
Strategies to shorten it: require deposits (reduce DSO), negotiate longer payment terms with suppliers (increase DPO), and minimize inventory on hand (reduce DIO).
