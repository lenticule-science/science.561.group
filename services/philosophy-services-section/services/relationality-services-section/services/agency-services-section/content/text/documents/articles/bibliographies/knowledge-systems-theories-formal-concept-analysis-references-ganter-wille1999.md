## Full citation

B. Ganter and R. Wille. *Formal Concept Analysis: Mathematical Foundations*. Springer, 1999. Translation of *Formale Begriffsanalyse: Mathematische Grundlagen*, Springer 1996.

## Status

Pending direct access; cited via standard FCA secondary literature and the textbook's wide circulation.

## Annotation

The consolidated mathematical treatment of formal concept analysis, by the two principal architects of the program. The textbook supplies the standard definitions, proofs, and notations that the FCA lineage uses. It is the canonical reference for the basic theorem (per [basic-theorem-of-fca](../../../glossary/concepts/basic-theorem-of-fca.md)), the implication-theory framework (per [attribute-implication](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/archive/teaching/explanations/attribute-implication.md)), the Duquenne–Guigues construction (per [implication-base](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/objects/implication-base.md)), conceptual scaling (per [scale](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/archive/teaching/explanations/scale.md)), and the operations on contexts (apposition, subposition, sub-, quotient-, and product-contexts).

Chapter structure (relevant to this subtheory):

- **Chapter 1**: formal contexts, concept lattices, the basic theorem.
- **Chapter 2**: determination of the concept lattice (algorithms).
- **Chapter 3**: parts of concept lattices (sub-concept-lattices and their extension).
- **Chapter 4**: decompositions of concept lattices.
- **Chapter 5**: constructions of concept lattices (apposition, subposition, etc.).
- **Chapter 6**: properties of concept lattices.

The textbook is mathematics-first; applications and methodology are addressed only briefly. Subsequent FCA literature (Belohlavek, Davey-Priestley, Carpineto-Romano) builds on Ganter-Wille's foundation.

For the predicated framing: Ganter-Wille is the standard reference for FCA-internal results that the predicated subtheory's [concept-lattice-as-degenerate-fiber](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/predicated-knowledge-systems/objects/concept-lattice-as-degenerate-fiber.md) presupposes. The FCA-internal results stand independently; the substrate-level reading does not modify them.
