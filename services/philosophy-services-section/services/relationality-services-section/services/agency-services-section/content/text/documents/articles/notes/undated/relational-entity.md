# relational-entity

A relational entity is a stable pattern constituted by relating — something that holds together and persists at some scale, not because it is a substance with intrinsic properties, but because the relations that compose it keep being sustained.
Remove the relations and there is no residual thing left over.
Generated by: relating — a relational entity is what relating produces when its activity reaches a configuration stable enough to recur and to be treated, by itself and by others, as a unit.

The concept is the bridge from the bare commitment of relational sociology (that relations come first) to the recognizable furniture of the social world (persons, groups, institutions).
It answers an obvious objection: if relations are prior to their terms, why does the world so plainly contain *things* — people, families, firms, states — that look like stable objects?
The answer is that stability is real but derived.
A whirlpool is nothing but a pattern in moving water, yet it is a definite thing with a location, a shape, and a history; it persists precisely as long as the flow that constitutes it persists, and not a moment longer.
Social entities are like this.
A university is not a substance but an arrangement of relations — teaching, examination, accreditation, employment, funding — durable enough to outlast any particular student or professor, and dependent for its existence on those relations being continually renewed.

What makes something an entity rather than a passing event is this kind of self-reproducing stability.
The pattern has to hold its shape against perturbation: people leave and are replaced, circumstances change, and still the arrangement reconstitutes itself.
This is why so much of sociology is really the study of *reproduction* — how the relations that make up a class, a profession, a household, or a state manage to reproduce themselves across time, and what happens when they fail to.
An entity that stops being reproduced does not leave behind a thing stripped of its relations; it simply ceases.

In this tree the relational entity is the parent of the social being, and through it of the whole catalogue of social forms.
Everything downstream — a person, an institution, a field — is a relational entity of some specific kind, constituted by some specific pattern of relating.
To say what one of them *is*, in this theory, is to say what relations constitute it and what keeps those relations in force.
