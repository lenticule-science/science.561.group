## Annotation

The technical report defining DOLCE (Descriptive Ontology for Linguistic and Cognitive Engineering), one of the four foundational ontologies in the WonderWeb library (with BFO, OCHRE, and DOLCE-Lite). DOLCE's signature commitment is the *endurant / perdurant* dichotomy — Masolo et al.'s rigorously axiomatised cousin of BFO's continuant/occurrent — together with explicit treatment of *qualities* and *quality-spaces* (every quality is in a quality-space; e.g., colour-quality lives in colour-space; relations between qualities are mediated by their quality-spaces).

For the predicated framing, DOLCE is the upper ontology that most directly anticipates the *predicate-category* construction: DOLCE's quality-spaces are *exactly* the fibers of the predication-fibration restricted to a fixed quality predicate. A "colour" quality of a subject is a cell whose predicate is colour-of and whose value lives in the colour-space lattice — and DOLCE's axioms on quality identity, quality dependence, and quality inheritance translate term-by-term into closure rules over those cells. The [upper-ontology-as-predicate-vocabulary](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/objects/upper-ontology-as-predicate-vocabulary.md) object names DOLCE as one canonical specification of *what predicate vocabulary an upper ontology supplies and what closure rules it commits to*.

DOLCE's choice to treat qualities as first-class entities (rather than as values of properties) is the move the framing inherits and generalises: the fiber algebra $A_p$ for quality-predicate $p$ *is* DOLCE's quality-space for $p$, equipped with bilattice structure rather than the order-theoretic structure DOLCE uses.
