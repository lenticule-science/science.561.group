# Discourse community as fiber

The structural reading of *discourse community* (Hjorland & Albrechtsen 1995) on the predicated substrate: a discourse community is a [scope](../../documents/articles/research/predicated-agentic-orchestration-platform/scope/scope.md) on the substrate, indexing a fiber of the [predication-fibration](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md) consisting of:

- A predicate-vocabulary subset $\mathcal{C}_D \subseteq \mathcal{C}$ — the predicates the community admits.
- A closure-rule subset $\mathcal{S}_D \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ — the inferential rules the community accepts.
- An entity-set subset $E_D \subseteq E$ — the entities the community talks about.
- A consistency commitment — what the community treats as a coherence violation requiring rejection vs. as a $\top$-valued tolerated contestation.

A discourse community's *knowledge* is its scope's settled cells under its closure-rule subset. Two communities differ when any of $\mathcal{C}_D$, $\mathcal{S}_D$, $E_D$, or the consistency commitment differs; the differences may be benign (different vocabularies for the same entities can be reconciled by federation) or fundamental (different closure-rule systems give different settlements over the same evidence and require explicit cross-community translation).

The eleven approaches Hjorland (2002) catalogued for *studying* a domain are eleven *projections* of the same fiber: bibliometric studies project onto citation predications; user studies project onto query-and-use predications; epistemological studies project onto closure-rule-system structure; etc. None of the projections is the fiber; the fiber is the union of what they jointly observe.

The substrate's [federation discipline](../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/federation-convergence.md) gives the operational interpretation of *cross-community reconciliation*: communities that share predicate-vocabulary and closure-rule subsets can federate without translation; communities that diverge on either require explicit cross-scope morphisms (per [federate](../terms/federate.md)) that map predications across the divergence.

The substrate names *what kind of mathematical object* a discourse community is — a fibered structure with explicit vocabulary, rules, entities, and consistency discipline — rather than treating "discourse community" as an unanalysed primitive imported from Hjorland's vocabulary.
