Predicated sociology identifies two grammars of political speech — [fascist grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/fascist-grammar.md) and [anarchist grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/04/27/2026-04-27-anarchist-grammar.md) — and one grammar's dialectical production of the other.
Neither is simply an ideology; both are *structures* of political speech, patterns of how claims are organized, how enemies are identified, how belonging is asserted, how crisis is narrated.
The apparatus runs both grammars simultaneously, as dialectical twins.

## Fascist grammar

[Fascist grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/fascist-grammar.md) is a six-step structure that recurs across political formations regardless of their specific ideological content:

1. **Crisis declaration.** A crisis is named that is so severe it cannot be addressed through ordinary political means.
  The crisis may be real or confected or both; its grammar-function is to suspend normal legitimacy requirements and create urgency.

2. **Pure community.** There is a community that, if unified and uncontaminated, could address the crisis.
  The community has a character — national, racial, class, religious, or otherwise — and its character is the source of its capacity.
  The community's potential power is blocked by its contamination.

3. **Corrupt enemy.** The contamination comes from an enemy who is corrupt in their essence — not merely in their interests — and who has achieved parasitic power over the pure community.
  The enemy's corruption is what explains why the crisis has not been resolved.

4. **Suffering as sacrifice.** The community's current suffering is not merely suffering; it is sacrifice — suffering with meaning, suffering that purifies, suffering that proves the community's devotion.
  The grammar requires suffering to be meaningful, because meaningless suffering produces neither commitment nor mobilization.

5. **Destiny.** The community has a historical destiny that the crisis imperils and the enemy is trying to deny.
  The destiny gives the struggle its ultimate stakes.
  The community is not fighting for interests; it is fighting for its historical role.

6. **Inevitability.** The struggle's outcome is inevitable, given the community's essential character.
  Victory is a matter of correctly expressing what the community already is.
  This grammar-step forecloses deliberation; there is no decision to be made, only an expression to be achieved.

This is not a list of fascism's beliefs.
It is a grammar — a structure that can be filled with any content.
National fascism fills the grammar with racial-national content.
Christian fascism fills it with theological content.
Left-populist movements fill it with class content (the working people vs. the oligarchs).
Wellness movements fill it with purity-of-body content (the toxin-free self vs. the industrial food system).
The grammar is the structure; the filling is ideology.

## Liberal grammar as response

Liberal political speech is organized as a structural response to fascist grammar.
Each step of fascist grammar has a liberal counter-move:

- Against crisis declaration: liberal grammar insists that problems are addressable through ordinary political processes — gradualism, expertise, dialogue, deliberation.
- Against pure community: liberal grammar insists on pluralism, on the equal standing of all persons regardless of identity.
- Against corrupt enemy: liberal grammar insists on charitable interpretation, on the principle that political opponents are mistaken rather than corrupt.
- Against suffering as sacrifice: liberal grammar insists on minimizing suffering, on welfare over devotion, on wellbeing as the political goal.
- Against destiny: liberal grammar insists on contingency, on the absence of guaranteed historical outcomes.
- Against inevitability: liberal grammar insists on democratic process, on the openness of political decisions.

Liberal grammar is thus organized entirely in relation to fascist grammar.
It does not have a positive content of its own; its content is the negation of fascist grammar's content.
This is what makes it *dialectically twin* to fascist grammar: it can only be stated as a counter-move, and a counter-move requires the presence of what it counters.

The consequence: when fascist grammar weakens, liberal grammar weakens with it.
Liberal politics is sustainably coherent only in the presence of the fascist politics it is organized against.
When the fascist grammar's specific threat recedes, liberal politics loses its defining purpose.
The post-Cold-War liberal order's structural confusion is partly explicable this way.

## Anarchist grammar

[Anarchist grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/04/27/2026-04-27-anarchist-grammar.md) is a third grammar that takes the liberal negation further: not just refusing fascist grammar's content but refusing the structure of political authority within which fascist and liberal grammars operate.

Anarchist grammar's distinctive moves: the refusal of all delegated authority as such (not just its abuses), the insistence on process-as-content (how decisions are made is as important as what decisions are made), the valorization of horizontal relation against vertical command, and the prefiguration of the desired world in the present practice.

As a *coherent subject-position*, anarchist grammar is metabolizable by the apparatus.
The [coherent confusion](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/coherent-confusion.md) of the activist who spends more energy on process than on action, the harm-governance collapse of the collective that cannot decide without full consensus, the platformal circuit that metabolizes anarchist aesthetics as content — all are metabolizations of anarchist grammar within the apparatus.

What cannot be metabolized, if anything, is the *practice* that anarchist grammar points toward but does not constitute: the actual relation, maintained across time, outside the legibility circuits of the platform, between people who act with each other without seeking institutional recognition.
Whether this practice is protected by the apparatus's structural limits or is itself metabolizable is the question the closure analysis holds open.

## The grammar that produces [soft surrealism](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/04/30/2026-04-30-soft-surrealism.md)

The interaction of these three grammars, under the conditions of platformal information architecture, produces a fourth phenomenon: [soft surrealism](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/04/30/2026-04-30-soft-surrealism.md).
When fascist grammar is widely deployed by actors who do not believe its content, liberal grammar is widely deployed by actors who have lost confidence in its institutions, and anarchist grammar is widely deployed by actors who cannot convert it into durable practice, the result is a general dissolution of the grammar-content connection.
Statements float free of their grammar.
The crisis-declaration move is made by actors who do not believe in the crisis (or who believe in many incompatible crises simultaneously); the pure-community move is made by actors who do not believe in the community's coherence; the corrupt-enemy move is made by actors who know that the enemy-figure is a genre rather than a person.

Soft surrealism is the condition of fluency in grammatical moves combined with loss of confidence in the moves' referential validity.
The subject knows how political speech works — knows the grammar — but no longer believes the grammar's deployment is tracking anything real.
This produces the characteristic contemporary phenomenon of political speech that is performed in full awareness of its own performance.
