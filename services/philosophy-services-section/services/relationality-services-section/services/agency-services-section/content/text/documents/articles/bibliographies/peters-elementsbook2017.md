# Peters, Janzing & Schölkopf, *Elements of Causal Inference* (2017)

A modern, ML-flavored textbook on causal inference.
Available open-access.

## What it covers

- Structural causal models with ML focus.
- Independence-based and functional approaches to
  causal discovery.
- LiNGAM, additive noise models, and identifiability
  through functional restrictions.
- Independent component analysis and causal
  discovery.
- Connections to ML: invariant prediction, causal
  representation learning foundations.
- Half-sibling regression, semi-supervised learning
  with causal assumptions.

## Why it matters

The bridge between Pearl's framework and ML. Where
Pearl's textbook is statistics-flavored, this book
emphasizes:

- Functional restrictions for identifiability.
- Connections to deep learning and representation
  learning.
- Algorithms that scale and work with continuous
  data.

The Schölkopf group's perspective on "causal
representation learning" largely originates here.

## How to read

Shorter than Pearl 2009; more accessible. Good
starting point for ML practitioners. Open-access
PDF available from MIT Press.
