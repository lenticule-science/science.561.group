# Movement phenomenology — the body that knows by moving

This text gathers the literature that lets `dancing` be read as an **agential** phenomenon rather than a metaphor: a body's movement is not the execution of a prior thought but a way of thinking, perceiving, and relating in its own right.

## Kinesthesia as a primary sense (Sheets-Johnstone)

Maxine Sheets-Johnstone, *The Primacy of Movement* (1999; expanded 2nd ed. 2011), argues that **movement is foundational, not derivative** — that animate life is "moving and being moved" before it is anything else, and that **kinesthesia** (the felt sense of one's own movement) is an originary sense, not a late add-on to vision and touch.
Her phrase **"thinking in movement"** (developed from her earlier *The Phenomenology of Dance*, 1966) names a cognition that happens *in* the moving, not one that movement merely carries out.
For dance this is the central claim: the dancer is not transmitting a pre-formed idea through the body; the dancing **is** the thinking.

## Motor intentionality and the body schema (Merleau-Ponty)

Maurice Merleau-Ponty, *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945), supplies the prior ground: the body is not an object in the world but the **standpoint from which there is a world**.
His **motor intentionality** — the "I can" that reaches, grasps, and orients without representing the goal propositionally — and the **body schema** (a pre-reflective postural and dynamic sense of the body's own configuration) describe an agency that is *lived from the inside of movement*.
The dancer's "knowing where the arm is" without looking is body-schematic, not inferential.

## The dance as virtual gesture (Langer)

Susanne K.
Langer, *Feeling and Form* (1953), reads dance as an art of **"virtual powers"**: what an audience sees is not literal physical exertion but a play of forces — gesture freed from its practical errand and made to **appear** as feeling-in-motion.
This is why a dance can be *about* something while denoting nothing: it presents the form of a feeling, not a proposition.
Langer gives the **danced artifact** (the `dance` node) its character — a bounded composed appearance — as distinct from the live **dancing** that produces it.

## Effort, shape, and notation (Laban)

Rudolf Laban's movement analysis (Laban Movement Analysis; *Choreutics*, posthumous 1966) shows that movement has **analyzable structure** — effort qualities (weight, space, time, flow), shape, and a notation (Labanotation/Kinetography) capable of recording a dance as a transmissible score.
This is the hinge by which **dancing** (live) settles into **dance** (a pattern that can be repeated, taught, and shared) — and why dance can fall into common time with others, which is where music begins.

## The dancer–dance unity (Yeats)

W.B. Yeats closes "Among School Children" (1928) with "**How can we know the dancer from the dance?**" — the canonical statement that in dancing the agent and the act are not separable.
There is no dancer standing behind the dancing who could be extracted from it; the agency *is* the movement.
This is the agential face in a line of verse.

## Vertigo as the play-root (Caillois)

Roger Caillois's category **ilinx** — vertigo, "the surrender of stable perception," the whirling dervish, the spinning child (already derived in-repo at `theories/agency/.../game/library/ludics/library/vocabulary/library/ilinx/ilinx.md`) — is the play-root from which dancing draws.
Where the other play-categories assert, accept, or adopt, ilinx **surrenders agency to physical sensation**.
Dance lives on the edge Caillois names: controlled loss of control, the body given over to its own motion within a held frame.

## What the literature licenses

Read together: movement is a mode of cognition and relation (Sheets-Johnstone, Merleau-Ponty), it composes into a presented form of feeling (Langer), that form is structured and transmissible (Laban), the agent is inseparable from the act (Yeats), and its play-root is the surrender of vertigo (Caillois).
That is enough to read **dancing as the agential turn**: agency exercised *as* and *between* moving bodies, prior to and productive of the shared field that becomes music.
