Fascism is difficult to describe with precision because the precision requires resisting the two gravitational errors that most accounts fall into.
The first error is premature essentialism: reducing fascism to a fixed list of content-properties (antisemitism, nationalism, leader-worship, paramilitary violence) that makes its structural form invisible and makes its recurrence appear as historical coincidence.
The second error is premature expansion: applying the term so broadly that it names everything the analyst dislikes, losing the specific structural content that makes fascism analytically distinct.

Predicated sociology describes fascism through [fascist grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/fascist-grammar.md): the structural sequence of political-speech moves that recur across formations regardless of their specific ideological content.
This is a deliberately formal approach — formal in the sense that it identifies structural rather than content properties.
A grammar can be filled with any content; fascist grammar can be, has been, and is filled with many different contents.
What makes a formation fascist is its grammatical structure, not its specific vocabulary.

## The six-step grammar

The grammar is described in detail in the [postliberal grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/postliberal-grammar.md) narrative.
To recap with attention to the descriptive problem: fascist grammar is a six-move sequence — crisis, pure community, corrupt enemy, suffering as sacrifice, destiny, inevitability — that can be analyzed in any specific political formation.
Describing a formation as fascist requires identifying all declared required moves, not just some of them.
A movement that has a crisis-narrative and a pure-community claim but lacks the corrupt-enemy move is not fascist in this sense; it is populist or millenarian.

The grammar approach resolves the premature-essentialism problem: different fascisms have different content (national socialism's antisemitic content, Italian fascism's Roman imperial content, Hindutva's Hindu-civilizational content, contemporary far-right's demographic-replacement content) but the same grammatical structure.
The grammar is what makes them instances of the same phenomenon.

The grammar approach also resolves the premature-expansion problem: not every political movement that the analyst considers bad or authoritarian is fascist in the grammatical sense.
Authoritarian governance without the fascist grammar is not fascism; populism without the corrupt-enemy move is not fascism; political violence without the pure-community claim is not fascism.

## What fascism does to [genring](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/genring.md)

Fascism does not merely use existing genre figures; it intensifies and operationalizes them.
The corrupt-enemy move of fascist grammar requires a figure for the enemy — a type that can be attributed corrupt characteristics, that can be identified in specific persons, and that can be made politically legible as the target of the movement's purifying violence.
Fascism's specific contribution to [genring](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/genring.md) is the *operationalization* of the genre figure: turning the literary type into a political-operational category whose identification in persons has direct consequences.

The antisemitic genring of Jewish people in Nazi Germany operationalized the existing anti-Semitic genre figure into an administrative category: the Jew was a genre figure, but the Nuremberg Laws translated the genre figure into a legal category, and the legal category determined who was subject to what treatment.
The genre became the administrative instrument.

This operationalization is what makes fascist genring qualitatively different from ordinary genring.
Both produce genre figures; fascist genring operationalizes the genre into an instrument of the political movement's pure-community/corrupt-enemy operation.

## The problem of liberal-fascist adjacency

One of the most important analytical problems in describing fascism is the adjacency between fascist grammar and liberal political speech.
As argued in the [postliberal grammar](../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/cybernetic-postliberalism-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/postliberal-grammar.md) narrative, liberal grammar is organized as a counter-move to fascist grammar: each step of fascist grammar has a liberal negation.
This means that liberal political speech is in constant structural reference to fascist grammar — it always knows where the grammar is and is always orienting against it.

The consequence: fascist grammar is the *condition of legibility* of liberal political speech.
Liberalism makes sense as liberalism in relation to fascism; remove the fascist grammar as the thing liberalism is negating, and liberalism's specific character becomes less defined.
This is not to say that liberalism is secretly fascist; it is to say that the two grammars are structurally entangled, and that the entanglement has consequences for what both grammars can and cannot do.

The practical consequence: political formations that invoke fascist grammar tactically — knowing that liberal opponents will predictably respond with liberal grammar — can use the response to sustain the fascist grammar's energy.
The liberal response confirms the crisis, the pure-community claim, the enemy's power (since the enemy's media apparatuses are now amplifying the crisis narrative), and the movement's destiny (since the enemy's reaction proves the movement is right).
The tactical deployment of fascist grammar, with the liberal response counted on as an amplifier, is a feature of contemporary political information dynamics.

## What predicated sociology contributes

Predicated sociology's specific contribution to the description of fascism is the analysis of how fascist grammar and [californication](../../../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/content/relating/content/relational-entity/content/social-being/content/social-relation/content/power/subjectifying/content/subject/content/possessive-individual/ontologizing/content/liberalism/analyzing/content/cybernetic-postliberalism/teaching/content/teaching/explanations/californication.md) interact.
Fascist grammar at the population level produces affective activation: the crisis declaration, the pure-community claim, the corrupt-enemy figure, and the sacrifice narrative all generate affective responses — fear, belonging, rage, righteousness, devotion.
This affective activation is the material that [californication](../../../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/content/relating/content/relational-entity/content/social-being/content/social-relation/content/power/subjectifying/content/subject/content/possessive-individual/ontologizing/content/liberalism/analyzing/content/cybernetic-postliberalism/teaching/content/teaching/explanations/californication.md) routes into personal self-regulation.

The subject who is activated by fascist grammar does not necessarily adopt the grammar's political positions.
What the subject does — within the apparatus that routes structural contradiction through personal affect — is metabolize the activation through the available affective genres.
The wellness-self metabolizes the fascist-grammar activation as anxiety to be managed through mindfulness practice.
The activist-self metabolizes it as righteous energy to be channeled into cause-advocacy.
The zen-fascist subject metabolizes it as evidence of the importance of maintaining inner serenity against the chaos.

In each case, the fascist grammar's activation produces affective material that the californication circuit routes into the apparatus-sustaining subject-formations.
This is not a defense of fascism — the grammar's content is not endorsed by the metabolizing subjects — but it is an analysis of how fascist grammar functions as a *fuel source* for the californication apparatus.
