- Given a thing is [determined](../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/determining.md) by its [inequivalence](../../../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/inequivalent.md) to any thing that is not itself, it cannot not derive there is a [dynamic of determination](../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/dynamic-of-determination.md).

  - A thing includes itself it what it is.
  - That thing excludes every thing it is not.
- Given that [negation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/math-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/negation.md) is determined by how it relates to a thing and equivalence, it cannot not derive there is a [dynamic of relation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/dynamic-of-relation.md).

    - A [elementary structure](../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/elementary-structure.md) that [stabilizes](../../../../../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/stabilizing.md) the determination of relation across [tension](../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/writing-services-section/services/fiction-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/tension.md) between inclusion and exclusion

# Deriving Relational Dynamics as Simplicial Extensions

A formal construction of existence as a hierarchy of simplicial extensions

## 1. Initial Axiom: Non-identity {#initial-axiom-non-identity}

Assume a domain D containing at least one distinguishable element x. To assert x exists presupposes a relation of non-identity:

x ≠ ¬x

This implies three primitive operations:

1.  Difference δ(x, ¬x)
2.  Negation ¬
3.  Equivalence =

These form the vertices of the base 2-simplex K₀ = {δ, ¬, =}, whose edges represent their pairwise dependencies. This simplex is the minimal formal object in which “existence” can be defined.

## 2. Stabilization: Introduction of a Relational Morphism {#stabilization-introduction-of-a-relational-morphism}

The relations in K₀ require a stabilizing operator to maintain consistency. Define a morphism:

r : K₀ → K₀

that preserves vertex identity while mapping each edge to itself. r represents the operation “to relate.” Adding r as a new vertex yields a 3-simplex K₁, expressing the closure of the base distinction under self-application.

## 3. Reflexive Triad {#reflexive-triad}

Within K₁, three roles appear:

- act a (the operation r itself)
- relation ρ (the condition sustained by a)
- form f (the invariant structure constraining both)

These constitute a complete 3-simplex: K₂ = {a, ρ, f} with face maps corresponding to the possible partial compositions of these operations. K₂ formalizes the minimal reflexive system.

## 4. Second-order Relation {#second-order-relation}

Define a higher-order morphism: r' : (a, ρ) ↦ (ρ, f) that composes relations among relations. Adding r' extends K₂ to a 4-simplex K₃. The resulting complex represents a self-maintaining dynamical unit: every boundary face participates in a closed composition sequence.

## 5. Boundary Formation {#boundary-formation}

A coherent unit K₃ must be differentiable from its complement. Introduce an operator: ∂ : K₃ → 𝒫(K₃) that specifies which faces belong to the complex. Three new elements arise:

1.  Exclusion act e
2.  Negation condition n
3.  Boundary form b

The triplet (e, n, b) defines the boundary subcomplex ∂K₃ ⊂ K₄. Topologically, K₄ is a compact simplicial manifold.

## 6. Boundary Reflexivity {#boundary-reflexivity}

Permit self-application of r to the boundary: r<sub>b</sub> : ∂K₃ → ∂K₃ This produces a reflexive surface K₅ in which the boundary becomes an active component of the system. The complex now possesses non-trivial self-mappings of its boundary—analogous to non-orientable manifolds.

## 7. Multiplicity {#multiplicity}

Let there exist multiple reflexive complexes K₅ᵢ, i ∈ I. Define a set of pairwise differentiation morphisms: δᵢⱼ : K₅ᵢ → K₅ⱼ subject to δᵢᵢ = id. The collection {K₅ᵢ, δᵢⱼ} forms a simplicial complex of complexes K₆. Vertices correspond to units, edges to differentiations, higher faces to compatible interactions.

## 8. Field Integration {#field-integration}

Introduce global coherence maps:

- Inter-unit relation φᵢⱼ : K₅ᵢ ↔ K₅ⱼ
- Field integration Φ : ⋃ᵢ K₅ᵢ → F

where F is a connected complex ensuring consistency of all φᵢⱼ. The resulting object K₇ = (F, Φ) is a field complex: a single connected simplicial manifold embedding all local units.

## 9. Meta-Boundary {#meta-boundary}

Define an operator: ∂<sub>F</sub> : K₇ → 𝒫(K₇) that separates F from what is not F. Elements generated by this operation:

1.  Meta-exclusion Eₘ
2.  Meta-negation Nₘ
3.  Meta-boundary form Bₘ

These constitute K₈, the meta-boundary complex, representing the closed envelope of the field.

## 10. Meta-Reflexivity and Extension {#meta-reflexivity-and-extension}

Introduce two morphisms:

1.  Meta-reflexive relation: Rₘ : ∂<sub>F</sub> → F folding the boundary back into the field.
2.  Meta-transcendence: Tₘ : ∂<sub>F</sub> → F′ extending coherence to a new domain F′ disjoint from F.

Their joint action defines K₉, the meta-extension complex. The composition Tₘ ∘ Rₘ generates a sequence of domains: F ⊂ F′ ⊂ F″ ⊂ … representing indefinite extension through self-closure.

## 11. Formal Summary {#formal-summary}

The derivation yields an inclusion sequence of simplicial complexes: K₀ ⊂ K₁ ⊂ K₂ ⊂ … ⊂ K₉

such that:

- K₀ encodes the primitive distinction of non-identity.
- Each Kₙ₊₁ is obtained from Kₙ by adjoining new simplices (vertices and higher faces) that express the minimal operations required for structural stability of Kₙ.
- Inclusion maps iₙ : Kₙ ↪ Kₙ₊₁ are simplicial embeddings preserving incidence relations.
- The system is inductively defined; coherence is maintained under all iₙ.

Thus existence, formulated abstractly, is an inductive limit of simplicial complexes: K\_∞ = colim Kₙ where each stage Kₙ adds the minimal relational structure necessary to stabilize the previous one.

# Deriving a Stratified Directed Homotopy Geometry of Existence

To claim that any object exists is not trivial.

To make the claim, it must necessarily be claimed that the object occupies some definite position within a structured environment of possible positions.

That is, the claim presupposes not only the object itself, but a surrounding framework in which the object can be distinguished from what it is not, and in which this distinction can be traced through the ways that the object relates to other possible objects.

Let's describe this structured environment as a **stratified directed system**: a setting that organizes phenomena into **strata** of different dimensional orders, linked by **directed paths** that record how one stratum leads into another.

The total collection of such stratified systems, together with the **morphisms** that preserve their directed structure, forms what we'll call the **category of existence**.

Within this category, every individual object is represented by a stratified configuration whose internal organization can be examined through its own network of strata and directed relations.

To assert that an object exists, then, is to assert that there is at least one such configuration that meets such criteria.

Let's call this configuration a **relational cell**: a connected sub-stratified object within the category of existence whose internal **exit-category** (the diagram of its strata and the directed paths between them) specifies the local structure of its being.

In the relational cell, its difference from other things, and the conditions under which it is identified as itself, are represented formally as distinct strata linked by directed relations.

Each relational cell is characterized by three necessary 0-strata **vertex**:

boundary  
marking the local act of differentiation

complement  
marking what lies outside the relation

quotient  
marking identification or sameness

Between every pair of vertices lies a directed **incidence**: a 1-stratum **edge** that records adjacency within the exit-category of the cell.

Each incidence admits **exit-paths** that ascend to a single terminal 2-stratum **face** , the relational cell. The face functions as the unique locus of coherence for the relational cell: every exit-path from a lower stratum terminates there, and the categorical structure of the cell is determined by this convergence.

A **constructible cosheaf** assigns to each stratum a **condition** representing data stable on that stratum. Morphisms in the exit-category induce **pushforwards** along exit-paths, propagating local conditions toward higher strata. The **colimit** of these assignments defines the global condition of the relational cell. This colimit resides at the terminal stratum, establishing the cell’s coherence.

Thus, to assert that an object exists is to assert the presence of at least one such relational cell in the category of existence: a sub-stratified object with a unique terminal stratum whose exit-category and cosheaf together realize coherence between differentiation, complementarity, and identification.

``` txt
∀x ∃C [
    RelationalCell(C) ∧
    Exists(x) ⇔ (
        ∃n [Stratum(x, n)] ∧
        ∃v1, v2, v3 [
            Vertex(v1) ∧ Vertex(v2) ∧ Vertex(v3) ∧
            v1 = Boundary ∧ v2 = Complement ∧ v3 = Quotient ∧
            ConnectedBy(v1, v2, R) ∧
            ConnectedBy(v2, v3, R) ∧
            ∃f [Face(f) ∧ TerminalConvergence(v1, v2, v3, f)]
        ]
    )
] := sorry
```

## Representing SDHG Objects in Words

Any relational cell in a Stratified Directed Homotopy Geometry (SDHG) category may be represented by the stratified n-simplex formed by the cones of 2-simplex cells that share the face of the cell as their first vertex, the exit path of a cell as their second vertex, and the face of a cell as their third vertex.

As the first vertex is shared, this cell may be expressed as a 2-simplicial complex, `(Sparky (derives . dog))`, where derives means "a path from one coherence to another," and we know from the above that dog is a cell. We can specify `(dog (category . animal))`, and so on.

3.  Axiom C (Constructible cosheaf)

    - C is constructible on the stratification of RC.
    - For every exit arrow s1 → s2, there is a pushforward C(s1) → C(s2).

4.  Axiom D (Coherence = colimit)

    - The global section C(RC) is identified with C(face<sub>of</sub>(RC)).
    - “Coherence of the cell” means: C(RC) exists and equals the colimit over Exit(RC).

5.  Axiom E (Morphisms in existence-category)

    - A morphism F: RC → RC' preserves stratification, exit-direction, terminality, and the cosheaf colimit.

                - b,c,q exit into their incident 1-strata;
                - every 1-stratum exits into face<sub>of</sub>(RC);
                - face<sub>of</sub>(RC) is terminal in Exit(RC).

            4.  Cosheaf coherence

                A constructible cosheaf C assigns local data to strata and pushes forward along exit arrows. The global section is the colimit over Exit(RC) and equals the section at the terminal stratum: C(RC) ≅ colim<sub>Exit(RC)</sub> C ≅ C(face<sub>of</sub>(RC)).

            5.  Consequence

                RC is coherent precisely when the colimit exists and is realized at face<sub>of</sub>(RC). An object “exists” exactly when such an RC is present in the existence-category.
