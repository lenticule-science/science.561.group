A habit is a tendency to act, think, or interpret in a particular way under particular circumstances. In [Peirce&rsquo;s](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) philosophy, habit is not merely psychological but cosmological: it is the general form of regularity, the way the universe acquires its laws and the way [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) acquire their meanings.

Peirce identified the **ultimate logical [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md)** — the final product of [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/semiosis.md) — as a habit-change: &ldquo;the deliberately formed, self-analyzing habit — self-analyzing because formed by the aid of analysis of the exercises that nourished it — is the living definition, the veritable and final logical interpretant&rdquo; (CP 5.491). An emotional [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) is a feeling; an energetic [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) is an action; a logical [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) is a habit or a modification of a habit.

Habit-change is therefore the mechanism by which [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/semiosis.md) stabilizes. The open-ended chain of [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) producing [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) producing [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) does not run forever in practice — it settles into habits of interpretation. These habits are not fixed: they are themselves subject to modification by further [semiosis](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/semiosis.md). Peirce&rsquo;s &ldquo;law of habit&rdquo; extends this beyond psychology to nature itself: all regularities are habits, and habits change by the habit of habit-change.

Habit belongs to [Thirdness](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/peirces-categories.md) in Peirce&rsquo;s categories — it is a general rule or tendency, not a particular feeling (Firstness) or brute reaction (Secondness).

## Formal correspondence

In the [semiotic universe](../../../../../../content/text/documents/articles/notes/semiotic-universe.md) construction, the modal closure operator jj formalizes habit-formation. An element aa of the meaning domain HH where j(a)=aj(a) = a is a meaning that has become habitual — its interpretation no longer changes under further examination. The gap j(a)>aj(a) > a marks meanings still undergoing semiosis, not yet settled into habit.
