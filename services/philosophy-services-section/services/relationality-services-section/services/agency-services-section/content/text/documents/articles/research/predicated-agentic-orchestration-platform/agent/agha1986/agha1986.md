## Source

MIT Press, 1986.
Originally MIT AI Lab Technical Report TR-844 (1985, MIT Ph.

## Form

Monograph (revised Ph.
D. thesis).
The mature mathematical treatment of the *actor model* introduced by [Hewitt–Bishop–Steiger (1973)](../hewitt-bishop-steiger1973/hewitt-bishop-steiger1973.md), with operational and denotational semantics and an analysis of fairness, deadlock, and observational equivalence.

## Content summary

Develops the actor model into a formal computational system.
An *actor configuration* is a finite multiset of actors (each with an address, a behavior, and a mailbox of pending messages).
A *transition* delivers one message from an actor's mailbox to its behavior, producing a new configuration with: the original actor replaced by its newly-designated behavior; any messages the behavior emitted added to the recipients' mailboxes; any newly-created actors added to the configuration.
The thesis develops the resulting transition system's properties: fairness conditions (every continually-pending message is eventually delivered), observational equivalence (when two actors are interchangeable from the system's perspective), the absence of explicit synchronization primitives in favor of message-pattern-based coordination.
The actor model's distinguishing engineering commitment — *encapsulation through asynchronous message-passing*, with no shared mutable state — is positioned against shared-memory concurrency and against synchronous message-passing (CSP).
The model has been instantiated in Erlang, Akka, Pony, and many other systems.
