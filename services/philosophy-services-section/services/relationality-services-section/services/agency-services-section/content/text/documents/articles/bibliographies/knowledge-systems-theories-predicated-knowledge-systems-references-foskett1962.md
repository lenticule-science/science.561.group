## Annotation

A first-decade history of the Classification Research Group (CRG), the British working group founded in 1952 to operationalise Ranganathan's faceted programme and Needham–Feibleman's integrative levels for practical document classification. Foskett records the CRG's central methodological move: *combining* faceted analysis (PMEST-style cross-cutting axes) with integrative levels (a level-stratified upper ontology) into a single classification scheme that is both horizontally faceted within a level and vertically stratified across levels.

For the predicated framing, the CRG synthesis is the historical precedent the [pmest-as-five-axis-decomposition](../../../glossary/concepts/pmest-as-five-axis-decomposition.md) and [predicate-category-stratification](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/archive/teaching/explanations/predicate-category-stratification.md) jointly recover: the predicate category has both a *fiber structure* (faceting within a level) and a *level structure* (the integrative-levels stratification). Federating across both axes is what the [federate](../../../glossary/terms/federate.md) morphism does in full generality.

The paper is also the historical record of the CRG's encounter with the *integration problem*: what to do when a document straddles two levels (e.g., biochemistry, social psychology, astrobiology). The CRG's pragmatic answer was *level priority by phenomenon of greatest integration* — an editorial heuristic. The framing makes the analogous structural commitment: a cell whose predicate spans two levels lives in the *colimit* of the two level-fibers under federation.
