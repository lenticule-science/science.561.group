The chart of accounts (COA) is the organizational structure of a business's financial records.
Every transaction — every sale, purchase, payment, and transfer — is recorded in one or more accounts.
The COA is the master list of those accounts, grouped into five categories:

1. **Assets** (what the business owns): Cash, [accounts receivable](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-receivable.md), [inventory](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/inventory.md), equipment, prepaid expenses
2. **Liabilities** (what the business owes): [Accounts payable](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-payable.md), loans, credit card balances, sales tax payable, [payroll](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/ledger/content/chart-of-accounts/_archive/payroll.md) taxes payable
3. **Equity** (owner's stake): Owner's capital, owner's draws, retained earnings
4. **Revenue** (money earned): Food sales, beverage sales, catering revenue, merchandise
5. **Expenses** (money spent): [Cost of goods sold](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/ledger/content/chart-of-accounts/_archive/cost-of-goods-sold.md), rent, utilities, wages, [insurance](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/business-insurance.md), marketing, [depreciation](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/ledger/content/chart-of-accounts/_archive/depreciation.md)

The COA should have enough accounts to produce useful reports — separating food cost from beverage cost, for example, so you can track [gross margin](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/ledger/content/chart-of-accounts/_archive/gross-margin.md) by category — but not so many that transactions are frequently miscategorized.
A small business typically needs 30–50 accounts.
More than that usually means accounts are too granular to be useful.

Each account has a number (assets: 1000s, liabilities: 2000s, equity: 3000s, revenue: 4000s, expenses: 5000s–7000s) that determines where it appears on financial statements.
The COA is set up during initial [bookkeeping](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/ledger/content/chart-of-accounts/_archive/bookkeeping.md) configuration and refined over the first few months of operation.
