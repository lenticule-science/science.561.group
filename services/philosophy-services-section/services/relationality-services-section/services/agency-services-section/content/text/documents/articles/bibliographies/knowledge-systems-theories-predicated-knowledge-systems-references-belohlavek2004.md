## Annotation

Generalises Formal Concept Analysis ([@ganter-wille1999]) from the boolean to the *fuzzy* (residuated-lattice-valued) case. Where classical FCA constructs a concept lattice from a boolean object–attribute incidence relation $I \subseteq G \times M$, Belohlavek's fuzzy FCA constructs a concept lattice from a residuated-lattice-valued incidence $I : G \times M \to \mathbf{L}$, with the two derivation operators (extent and intent) becoming residuated-lattice-valued Galois connections. The resulting concept lattice carries enriched-categorical structure indexed by the residuated lattice.

For the predicated framing, Belohlavek's construction is the technical bridge between Formal Concept Analysis and the bilattice-valued predication-fibration. Specifically: when the residuated lattice $\mathbf{L}$ is taken to be $\mathbf{2}$, Belohlavek's concept lattice reduces to the classical Wille-Ganter construction; when $\mathbf{L}$ is taken to be the *truth-order projection* of the bilattice $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$, the concept lattice becomes a valid degenerate fiber in the predication-fibration. The [concept-lattice-as-degenerate-fiber](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowing/content/knowledge-science/systems/objects/concept-lattice-as-degenerate-fiber.md) object names this construction and the [fca-concept-lattice](../teaching-articles/examples/fca-concept-lattice.md) example works out the fuzzy generalisation.

Belohlavek's paper is also significant for showing that the residuated-lattice generalisation *preserves the lattice-theoretic structure* (the Galois connection, the lattice operations, the basic theorem of FCA) while *enriching* the values along which incidence is recorded. This is the mathematical precedent for the framing's commitment that moving from $\mathbf{2}$ to $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}$ does not collapse the algebra; it only enriches the value-domain.
