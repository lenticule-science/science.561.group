# Jeffrey 1965

Jeffrey, R.C. (1965). *The Logic of Decision*.
McGraw-Hill.

The founding statement of **Evidential Decision Theory**.
Where Savage takes preferences over acts (with state-act independence assumed), Jeffrey takes preferences over *propositions* and uses conditional probability for both evidential and decision-theoretic reasoning.
The expected utility of an act $A$ is $\sum_s P(s \mid A) u(s \wedge A)$ — a quantity well-defined whether or not $A$ is causally exogenous to $s$.

Jeffrey's framework gives the natural EDT prescription on Newcomb's problem (one-box) but the contested EDT prescription on the Smoking Lesion (abstain).
The contrast with Causal Decision Theory frames the contemporary decision-theoretic debate.
