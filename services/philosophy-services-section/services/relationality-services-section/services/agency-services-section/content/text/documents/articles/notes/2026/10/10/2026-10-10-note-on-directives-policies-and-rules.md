# The theory

A **directive** is a subject-predicate-object triplet (S, P, O). Every subject and every object is a resource of the Group's system, and the predicate is an action an agent can make, if the agent is capable of it. The Leader issues directives.

A **policy** is the set of directives that are active over a resource.

A **rule** is a policy that applies under the preconditions of a program.

So rules derive from policies, and policies derive from directives. The Leader says the whole ruling system must be redone in this order (2026-09-11 19:43).

All data in existence is subject-predicate-object (2026-09-11 19:51). A directive is a norm because both are SPO. The consequence the Leader draws is that the Leader's prompts should be parsed into directives, and that the parsing, the storage and the derivation of policy and rule from them are one architecture and not a parallel set of record formats.

# What counts as a directive

A directive is only what the Leader said to an agent, in the current session or another. A statement an agent wrote in a file is not a directive, and an agent that cites its own file as the Leader's ruling misuses the term (2026-09-11 19:43). The existing glossary term directive (`social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/governance-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/directive.md`) in the governance section gives the speech-act sense (Austin, Searle): one dated, located, verbatim act, evidence of authority, and not yet a policy. The Leader's sense adds the form: the act is held as an (S, P, O) over resources, so that policy and rule can be derived from it by the same graph operations as any other data.

# Standing operating procedure

Standing operating procedure (SOP) for the 561 Group, "a real corporation", is a set of directives. The Leader's repeated complaint of 2026-09-11 is that the corrections reached agents as prose and were turned into notes in the agent's private journal, or into rule markdown specific to one installation, so that "99% of what I told you as rules, SOP, exist nowhere in any documentation" (22:07). The Leader's position is that making the corrections durable, as parts of SOP in the best form the agent can make them, outranks whatever the agent is spending credits on at that moment (23:29), and that rules hold typed, machine-readable, computational content and not raw prose (23:36). More prose cannot teach good form (23:32). A listing of the Leader's directives is rendered as positive RFC-style sentences in a copyable block when the Leader asks (23:33), and a listing of tasks is not a listing of directives (23:46).

The Leader's two further SOP statements of the same day: slow tests are indicative of bad tests (21:23), and "less wrapper, not more wrapper" (23:26).
