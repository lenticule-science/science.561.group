# McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995

McClelland, J. L., McNaughton, B. L., & O'Reilly, R. C. (1995).
Why There Are Complementary Learning Systems in the Hippocampus
and Neocortex: Insights from the Successes and Failures of
Connectionist Models of Learning and Memory. *Psychological
Review*, 102(3), 419-457.

The *complementary learning systems* (CLS) account: the
hippocampus and neocortex form two distinct learning systems
with complementary strengths. The hippocampus does fast,
sparse, episodic encoding; the neocortex does slow,
distributed, semantic integration. *Consolidation* is the
iterative replay of hippocampal traces to the neocortex,
solving the *stability-plasticity dilemma* — how to learn new
things without catastrophically overwriting old ones.

The framework directly influenced *experience replay* in
deep reinforcement learning (Mnih et al. 2015) and the broader
class of *continual-learning* algorithms in deep networks.
It also frames the contemporary question of whether LLM agents
need a CLS-like consolidation step (current agents largely
lack one).
