# Savage 1954

Savage, L.J. (1954). *The Foundations of Statistics*.
Wiley.

The founding statement of *subjective* expected-utility theory.
Where von Neumann-Morgenstern took probabilities as given (lotteries with labeled probabilities), Savage takes only preferences over *acts* (functions from states to outcomes) as primitive.
From axioms on these preferences — most famously the Sure-Thing Principle — Savage derives both a utility function and a subjective probability over states such that the agent's preferences are represented by subjective expected-utility maximization.

This is the central reference for the Bayesian decision- theoretic framework that dominates twentieth-century statistics, economics, and AI.
Critiques of Savage focus on the Sure-Thing Principle (Allais paradox, Ellsberg paradox) but the framework remains foundational.
