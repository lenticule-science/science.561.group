## What this node is

This node is generated by the morphism **predicate**, taken under **grammar**.
Grammar gives the abstract structure of well-formed expression — the categories,
combinatorics, and inference that hold of language as such. To predicate is to
assert that structure of a determinate subject: not "what makes any expression
grammatical," but "this is how *this* tongue actually combines." A particular
natural language is exactly grammar predicated — the general form of articulate
combination borne by a concrete, historically situated subject with its own
sound system, inventory of words, and attested texts.

So the move from grammar to *languages* is the move from a logical subject term
to a filled one. English is not a fresh principle; it is grammar said of a real
speech community. The corpus treats each language as such a predication, which is
why a particular phonological fact (the trajectory of rhoticity through Old, Middle,
and General American English) or a particular dialect (Kentish among the Old English
dialects) belongs here: they are the concrete content that predication supplies to
the otherwise empty grammatical frame.

## What it generates / contains

Under `content/` this node holds particular languages along a single lineage,
ordered as a chain of historical predication where each later tongue is the
ancestor's grammar carried forward and re-asserted of a new community:

- `proto-indo-european/` — the reconstructed root of the family.
- `proto-germanic/` — the Germanic branch descended from it.
- `old-english/` — the Anglo-Saxon stage, including its dialect divisions.
- `anglo-norman/` — the Norman-French contact layer that reshaped the lexicon.
- `english/` — Modern English and its varieties, with material on phonological
  history (rhoticity, the Great Vowel Shift's vowel effects), dialects such as
  Kentish, and collections of terms and texts.

The body, then, is concrete linguistic description and history rather than further
abstraction: it is grammar made particular and traced through time.

## Orientation

Up one step is the parent morphism node **grammar**, which supplies the structural
form these languages predicate. Its sibling generations under grammar are
**historical-linguistics** (the study of how that structure changes across
predications — the diachronic method these language histories instantiate) and
**semantics** (grammatical form on the side of meaning rather than concrete tongue).
Grammar itself stands under **language**, where the unfolding articulate ->
predicate -> posit -> settle is re-run at the base of language.

The `_archive/` directory holds un-canonicalized material — drafts and intake not
yet folded into the surfaces above. Treat it as source, not as settled structure.
