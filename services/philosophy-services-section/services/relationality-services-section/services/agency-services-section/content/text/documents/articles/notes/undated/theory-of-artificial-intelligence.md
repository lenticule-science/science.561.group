# The Theory of Artificial Intelligence

**Theorem.** The theory of artificial intelligence is a sub-theory of $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$ (continuous computation). Neural networks are $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable continuous functions; training by gradient descent is a $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable dynamical system on weight space; the Universal Approximation Theorem holds as an internal theorem of $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$; and the learnable function class — the functions approximable by finite networks trained on finite data — is exactly the class of $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable continuous functions on compact data manifolds.

**Proof.**

*Neural networks are $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable.* A feedforward neural network with $L$ layers, weight matrices $W^{(1)}, \ldots, W^{(L)}$, and activation function $\sigma: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ computes $f_W: \mathbb{R}^{d_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_L}$ as the composition $f_W = \sigma \circ W^{(L)} \circ \sigma \circ \cdots \circ \sigma \circ W^{(1)}$. When $\sigma$ is continuous (sigmoid, tanh, ReLU, softmax) and the weight matrices have computable real entries, $f_W$ is a composition of $\mathcal{K}_2$-computable continuous functions. The Kleene-algebra application structure of $\mathcal{K}_2 = (\omega^\omega, \cdot)$ — where elements are Baire-space functions and application is continuous function evaluation — provides a realizer for every such composition: the realizer for $f_W$ is the continuous function that, given an input $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_0}$ encoded as an element of $\omega^\omega$, computes the layer-by-layer evaluation. Therefore $f_W \in RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$.

*Gradient descent is $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable.* Let $\mathcal{L}(W) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell(f_W(x_i), y_i)$ be the training loss. Under standard smoothness assumptions (Lipschitz continuous gradients), the gradient descent update $W_{t+1} = W_t - \eta \nabla_W \mathcal{L}(W_t)$ defines a continuous dynamical system on weight space $\mathbb{R}^p$. The gradient $\nabla_W \mathcal{L}$ is computable from $W_t$ by backpropagation — a sequence of matrix-vector products and chain-rule applications, each a $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable operation. The trajectory $\{W_t\}_{t \geq 0}$ is a $\mathcal{K}_2$-computable sequence of real vectors. Therefore training is a $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable dynamical system.

*Universal approximation holds internally in $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$.* Cybenko (1989) and Hornik-Stinchcombe-White (1989): for any continuous $f: K \to \mathbb{R}$ on a compact $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, and any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a single-hidden-layer network $\sigma_W$ with $\|\sigma_W - f\|_\infty < \varepsilon$. The proof uses the Stone-Weierstrass theorem on $C(K)$ (the algebra of continuous functions on $K$): neural networks with sigmoidal activations separate points and contain constants, hence are dense in $C(K)$ in the sup norm. In $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$, $C(K)$ for compact $K \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is the object of $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable total functions from $K$ to $\mathbb{R}$ — precisely because $K$ is compact and every continuous function on a compact subset of Baire space is $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable (this is Brouwer's fan theorem, which holds in $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$). The density statement "neural networks are dense in $C(K)$" is therefore an internal theorem of $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$.

*The learnable class = $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable continuous functions on compact manifolds.* The converse of universal approximation is the learning-theoretic claim: any $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable continuous function on a compact manifold is learnable (in the PAC-learning sense, with sufficient data and a sufficiently large network). This follows from two facts. First, by the universal approximation theorem (above), there exists a network approximating $f$ to within $\varepsilon$. Second, the approximating network is finitely specifiable (by its weight vector $W$), and the training loss $\mathcal{L}(W)$ achieves its minimum near $W$ given sufficient data (by consistency of empirical risk minimization for uniformly continuous hypotheses on compact domains — a consequence of the uniform law of large numbers, which holds for $\mathcal{K}_2$-computable hypothesis classes). Therefore: a function is learnable from data on a compact manifold iff it is $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable and continuous.

*Why $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$ and not $\mathbf{Eff} = RT(\mathcal{K}_1)$.* Fixed-weight inference ($f_W$ for fixed $W$) is $\mathcal{K}_1$-realizable (finite arithmetic). But the theoretical content of AI — universal approximation, learnability, function representation — requires $\mathcal{K}_2$: the fan functional $\Phi: (C(\{0,1\}^\omega) \to \omega) \to \omega$ (which extracts the modulus of uniform continuity of any uniformly continuous function) is $\mathcal{K}_2$-realizable but not $\mathcal{K}_1$-realizable. Since the Universal Approximation Theorem's application to continuous functions on compact sets inherently invokes the modulus of uniform continuity (to bound approximation error uniformly), it requires the fan functional and hence $\mathcal{K}_2$. $\mathbf{Eff}$ cannot prove universal approximation for arbitrary continuous target functions; $RT(\mathcal{K}_2)$ can. $\square$
