# relational-state

A relational state is the configuration an entity occupies at a given moment within its web of relations — its standing or position right now.
Where a **relational-entity** is the durable pattern that persists across time, its relational state is the specific position that pattern is in at this moment: the ties currently potential, the recognitions currently granted or withheld, the standing the entity presently holds in the eyes of those it relates to.
Generated by: **relational-entity** — the state is the entity's momentary face, and the entity persists across many states as its relating continues.

The distinction between entity and state matters because social standing is not a fixed possession but a position that shifts as relations change.
A person is the same person across a lifetime, yet their relational state is continually in motion: a worker becomes unemployed, a defendant becomes a convict, a stranger becomes a friend, a citizen becomes a refugee.
None of these transformations changes the biological individual; each changes the configuration the individual occupies in the web of relations, and that configuration is what governs what they can do, claim, and expect.
The classic illustration is Max Weber's distinction between class and status: one's *status* is precisely a position in a structure of social honour and recognition, held relative to others and alterable as those relations shift.
A relational state is this kind of positional standing, generalized.

The crucial property of a relational state is that it is *momentary and revisable* rather than essential.
It is the answer to the question "where does this entity stand now?" — and that answer changes as relating goes on.
A reputation rises or falls; an alliance forms or dissolves; a group's place in a hierarchy is contested and re-settled.
Because the state is the current position rather than a permanent attribute, it carries no claim to be the entity's true or final nature.
Different relations place the same entity in different states at the same time — esteemed in one community, suspect in another — and no single state is privileged as the real one.
This is the positional, ever-shifting character that distinguishes a relational account of identity from an essentialist one.

The stakes connect directly to the branch's commitments.
If standing is a relational state rather than an essence, then it is **contingent** — reached one way, it could be reached otherwise, and it can be changed by changing the relations that produce it.
A person's degraded standing is not a property of their nature but a position assigned within relations of **witnessing** and recognition, which can be refused, contested, and re-witnessed.
Read against its neighbours, the relational-state is the **relational-entity** caught at a moment, the local and revisable counterpart of the entity's continuity, and a reminder that everything that looks like fixed social status is in fact a settlement of ongoing relating that the next moment of relating can unsettle.
