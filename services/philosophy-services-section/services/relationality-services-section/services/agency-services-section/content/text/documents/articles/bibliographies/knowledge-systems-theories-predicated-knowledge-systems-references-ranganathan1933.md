## Annotation

The first systematic *faceted* knowledge-organization scheme. Ranganathan's signature move is to refuse a single-axis hierarchical classification (Dewey, LCC) and instead decompose any subject into five fundamental categories — Personality, Matter, Energy, Space, Time (PMEST) — each contributing an axis along which a document is independently classified. A document's class number is then the colon-separated concatenation of facet values, one per axis.

The predicated framing recognises this as a *fibration with five distinguished fiber axes*: each PMEST category names a class of predicates the system is committed to; assertions about a document factor through the five axes; and reasoning over the classification is reasoning across the [predication-fibration](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md). The [pmest-as-five-axis-decomposition](../../../glossary/concepts/pmest-as-five-axis-decomposition.md) theorem makes this identification precise. Ranganathan's "phase relations" (between-, by-, with-, in-) are early sketches of what the predication-cell makes algebraically uniform.

What the predicated framing rejects: Ranganathan treats the five categories as universal across all knowledge. The framing instead treats the five-axis decomposition as one fibration choice — appropriate for library-scale document classification, but not the only valid stratification of a [predicate-category](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predicate-category.md).
