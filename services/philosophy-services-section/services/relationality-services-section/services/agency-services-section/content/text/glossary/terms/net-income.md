Net income is revenue minus all costs: [cost of goods sold](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/net-income/cost-of-goods-sold.md), [operating expenses](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/net-income/operating-expenses.md), interest on debt, and taxes.
It is the last line on the [income statement](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/net-income/income-statement.md) — the "bottom line."

**Revenue − COGS = Gross profit** **Gross profit − Operating expenses = Operating income** **Operating income − Interest − Taxes = Net income**

Net income tells you whether the business is profitable after everything is accounted for.
A positive net income means the business earned more than it spent.
A negative net income (net loss) means the opposite.

Net income is not the same as cash.
[Depreciation](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/net-income/depreciation.md) reduces net income but doesn't consume cash.
[Accounts receivable](../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/business-science-services-section/services/accounting-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/accounts-receivable.md) increases revenue (and net income) before cash is collected.
Capital expenditures consume cash but don't appear on the income statement (they appear on the [balance sheet](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/net-income/balance-sheet.md) and are depreciated over time).
This is why a profitable business can run out of cash, and why the [cash flow statement](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/endeavoring/content/endeavor/organizing/content/organization/resourcing/content/resource/recording/content/record/accounting/content/net-income/cash-flow-statement.md) exists as a separate document.
