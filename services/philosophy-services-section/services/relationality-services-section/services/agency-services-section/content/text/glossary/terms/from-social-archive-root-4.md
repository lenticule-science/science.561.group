Economies of abandonment, as theorized by [Elizabeth Povinelli](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/content/relating/content/relational-entity/content/social-being/content/social-relation/content/power/legitimating/content/authority/monopolizing/content/the-state/governing/content/biopower/general/people/elizabeth-povinelli.md) in *Economies of Abandonment: Social Belonging and Endurance in Late Liberalism* (2011), names the mode of governance in which [late liberalism](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/content/relating/content/relational-entity/content/social-being/content/social-relation/content/power/legitimating/content/authority/monopolizing/content/the-state/governing/content/biopower/abandoning/content/necropolitics/content/economies-of-abandonment/_archive/late-liberalism.md) manages populations and places not primarily through direct oppression but through withdrawal — the removal of resources, infrastructure, and recognition from those it deems unviable.

Abandonment is not neglect.
It is a governed process.
The state does not simply forget communities — it assesses them, classifies them, and determines their viability through administrative, economic, and demographic metrics.
When a community is found unviable — too small, too remote, too costly to service — the withdrawal of resources follows as a rational, procedural outcome.
The community is not attacked; it is abandoned.
The distinction matters because abandonment operates through the same procedural frameworks that late liberalism treats as neutral: cost-benefit analysis, demographic modeling, infrastructure planning.

This means that abandonment produces its own evidence.
The community that has been deprived of infrastructure appears to lack the capacity for self-governance.
The population that has been denied resources appears to have failed to thrive.
The withdrawal produces the conditions that retroactively justify the withdrawal — a feedback loop in which the managed exhaustion of a community becomes the proof that the community was unviable in the first place.

[Endurance](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/content/relating/content/relational-entity/content/social-being/content/social-relation/content/power/legitimating/content/authority/monopolizing/content/the-state/governing/content/biopower/abandoning/content/necropolitics/content/economies-of-abandonment/_archive/endurance.md) — the ongoing work of persisting under these conditions — becomes the central political act in economies of abandonment.
Not resistance in the heroic sense, but the refusal to disappear on the schedule that abandonment has set.
