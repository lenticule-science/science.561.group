**Ontogenring** is the process by which a subject is formed through genre: a being-template is installed iteratively across many micro-interactions, each one attributing genre-typical properties to the subject, until the subject inhabits the template as their ontological form.
Where [genring](../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/genring.md) operates on peoples at the level of collective discourse, ontogenring operates on individuals — it is the mechanism by which a person becomes, for themselves and for the apparatus, the genre figure their people has been assigned.

In predication-categories terms: ontogenring is a closure rule operating on the subject-predicate fibers.
For a subject $e \in E$, the ontogenric process is a sequence of attributions $a_1, a_2, \ldots$ each of which drives selected fiber values toward the genre-figure's canonical assignment $\hat{F}$.
The closure rule is genre-coherence: attributions that conform to $\hat{F}$ are reinforced (fiber values converge); attributions that deviate are corrected (either reversed by social feedback or left isolated as $\bot$).
Over sufficient iterations the subject's predicate-fiber bundle converges to $\hat{F}$ — not because they always had those properties, but because the closure rule has driven the settling to that fixed point.

## Relation to interpellation

Althusser's [interpellation](../../documents/articles/notes/undated/interpellation.md) is the moment ideology hails the subject and the subject turns: the police call *you, there*, and the act of turning installs the subject who can be addressed.
Interpellation is event-shaped — a punctual act that constitutes the subject at a moment.
Ontogenring is process-shaped: it is the cumulative settling that precedes and follows every interpellative moment, the distributed attribution that makes the subject available to be hailed at all.

The distinction matters for regime classification.
Under stable settled conditions (strict regime), interpellative moments are effective because the surrounding settling has already done most of the work: the subject is already genre-formatted, and the hail merely confirms what the settling has accumulated.
Under destabilized conditions (aperiodic regime), interpellative moments fail more often — the settling has not converged sufficiently to make the subject available to the genre's hail.
Ontogenring, as the underlying process, explains why interpellation works reliably in some regimes and unreliably in others.

## Relation to performativity

Butler's performativity (1990) describes subject-formation as the iterated repetition of regulatory norms: gender is materialized through the citation of feminine performance.
Both performativity and ontogenring treat subject-formation as ongoing accumulation rather than punctual installation.
The difference is emphasis: performativity foregrounds the *citation* of norms (which norms are cited, how citation succeeds or fails); ontogenring foregrounds the *genre-form* of the accumulation — the fact that the cited norms belong to a structured template with phases, arcs, affective registers, and narrative resolutions.
The being-template is the genre in its full narrative sense, not merely a norm.

## The being-template as fiber-fixed-point

The being-template $\hat{F}$ is what ontogenring is converging toward.
It is a comprehensive predicate-value assignment covering the genre figure's attributes, practices, affective dispositions, appropriate arcs, and social relations.
The trauma survivor's being-template includes: a relationship to a wound (the defining event), an arc (healing, recovery), a set of appropriate affects (grief that is structured, anger that is shared), and recognized trajectories (movement-building, becoming an educator).
The subject who inhabits this template does not experience it as external imposition; by the time the settling has converged, the template *is* their ontological form — the settled fibers are what they *are*, from the inside.

This is what the CPL corpus means by "ontogenring" as distinct from mere socialization or acculturation: not that the subject learns roles, but that the subject is ontologically constituted as the genre figure.
The social settling has driven the predicate-fiber bundle to a fixed point that is structurally identical to the genre's canonical assignment.
The subject may then act within the genre — make novel choices, face genuine dilemmas — but within a field whose shape the ontogenring has fixed.

## Stability and refusal

Ontogenric identities are stable precisely because the closure rule is ambient and continuous.
A subject who refuses the template — the trauma survivor who rejects the recovery arc, the recovered addict who refuses the relapse narrative — faces re-installation: every social interaction that treats them as a trauma survivor re-attributes the genre's canonical fiber values, every platform that surfaces genre-typical content reinforces the template's pressure.
The apparatus does not need to consciously enforce the template; the closure rule is distributed across all the micro-attributions of daily life.

Sufficient cumulative refusal can erode the template — enough attributions of alternative fiber values can shift the settled state away from $\hat{F}$.
But the condition for this is that there exist an alternative closure rule operating with comparable density and speed.
Communities of practice, counterinstitutions, and sustained relational forms can provide such alternative closing — they produce the counter-attributions at sufficient frequency.
This is the formal substrate of the political practice of prefigurative community-building: building the social conditions under which alternative ontogenring is possible.
