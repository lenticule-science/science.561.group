# Source — morphism/graph-theoretic sense

In a directed graph, the **source** of an edge \`e : u → v\` is the vertex \`u\` from which the edge departs. In category theory, the **domain object** of a morphism \`f : A → B\` is \`A\`. These are analogous selected coordinate roles within specified structures; the words \`domain\`, \`tail\`, \`input\` and \`source\` must not automatically be registered as universal synonyms. For example, a function's domain is generally a **set of permitted arguments**, not merely the source vertex of a particular graph edge; a transition's Petri-net preset can contain multiple places.

A graph edge carries a departure and an arrival **inside its nominated structure**. That orientation alone neither establishes a real-world cause, a content address, a witness of the edge's truth, nor the right to mutate a ledger. A categorical arrow should be typed by its own source/target objects and composition laws before using this glossary entry.

## Do not conflate source worlds

- A **documentary source** is the authored or acquired file, revision and span **from which text was read**, not the domain of an abstract morphism.
- An **evidential witness** is an admitted attributed event or record for or against a *typed claim*, not necessarily a graph source vertex.
- An **authority/source of law** names a rule-granting actor or instrument under a particular jurisdiction, not documentary evidence of the instrument's content.
- **Source custody** names the owning Science/Engineering/Operations/Group/Public section of an artifact. It does not give a graph-theoretic arrow or a social principal special rights.

The March 28 frontmatter discussion concerns **documentary attribution and local presentation**, so it should use the [source-bound frontmatter presentation term](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/information-science-services-section/services/language-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/frontmatter-presentation.md), **not** this graph-theoretic Source to stand in for its author or its truth.
