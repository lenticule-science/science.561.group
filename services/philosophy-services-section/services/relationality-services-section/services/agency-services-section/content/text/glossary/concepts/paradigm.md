# Paradigm

The *vertical axis of sign-combination*: the set of sign-vehicles that *could* substitute at a given position in a syntagm without rendering the sign-act incoherent. A paradigm is a *substitution-class absent* — what the producer chose against by choosing what they chose.

Saussure's *associative relations* (later renamed *paradigmatic* by Hjelmslev and the structuralist tradition; per [Saussure 1916](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/saussure1916.md)) are the alternatives that hover behind every present sign-vehicle. A paradigmatic analysis lists the substitutability classes; a syntagmatic analysis lists the present combinations; together they identify the [code](code.md)'s structural commitments.

Greimas's semantic square (per [Greimas 1966](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/greimas1966.md)) is a paradigmatic structure: four positions standing in mutual contrariety / contradiction / implication, generated by the binary semantic axis the analysis selects. The square is one of structuralism's load-bearing analytical instruments precisely because paradigmatic structure is *more* informative than syntagmatic structure for type-level analysis.

The substrate-level reading places a paradigm at the *predicate-category sub-fibration* over a fixed cell-position: the predicates that could replace $p$ in a cell $(s, p, o)$ while preserving the cell's syntactic well-formedness. The substrate's [specialize](../terms/specialize.md) morphism operates exactly on the paradigm: it refines a predicate to a more-specific predicate within the same paradigmatic class.
