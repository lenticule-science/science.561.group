A symbol is a [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) that represents its object by convention, habit, or rule. Most words are symbols: &ldquo;tree&rdquo; represents trees not because the word resembles a tree ([icon](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/icon.md)) or because trees caused the word ([index](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/index-sign.md)), but because English speakers have adopted the convention. Mathematical notation, traffic [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md), flags, and legal codes are all symbolic systems.

[Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) understood the symbol&rsquo;s connection to its object as mediated by an [interpretant](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/linguistics-services-section/services/semiotics-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/interpretant.md) — the convention that links them is itself a [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) (a &ldquo;legisign,&rdquo; or [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) of law) that must be learned and shared. Symbols therefore depend on a community of interpreters who maintain the convention through repeated use. This is why [Peirce](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) connected symbols to habit: the meaning of a symbol is stabilized by the habitual interpretations of the community that uses it [@peirce_CollectedPapers_1931].

Because symbols are conventional, they are the most flexible type of [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) — new symbols can be created, old ones can be repurposed, and the same symbol can carry different meanings in different communities. But this flexibility also makes them the most dependent on shared context and the most vulnerable to misinterpretation across communities [@short_PeirceTheorySigns_2007].
