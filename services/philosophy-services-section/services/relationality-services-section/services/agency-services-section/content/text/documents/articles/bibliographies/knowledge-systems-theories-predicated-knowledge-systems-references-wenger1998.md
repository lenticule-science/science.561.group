## Annotation

Wenger's mature elaboration of [@lave-wenger1991]. Develops a *social theory of learning* organised around four interlocked components: *practice* (the negotiated meanings, shared repertoire, and joint enterprise of a community), *community* (mutual engagement, joint enterprise, and shared repertoire), *meaning* (the ongoing negotiation of what counts as competent participation), and *identity* (the trajectory of an individual through a network of communities of practice). Introduces the *boundary object* — an artefact that participates in multiple communities of practice simultaneously, with potentially different meanings in each, and serves as the locus of inter-community translation.

For the predicated framing, Wenger's *boundary object* is the structural precedent for the [federate](../../../glossary/terms/federate.md) morphism. A boundary object is a single subject whose cells live in multiple fibers (one per community of practice it participates in); the cells need not agree across fibers (different communities may assert contradictory predicates of the same boundary object); the negotiation of meaning across the boundary is exactly what *federation* operationalises. The [federation-as-bilattice-colimit](../../../glossary/concepts/federation-as-bilattice-colimit.md) theorem characterises the colimit construction that produces the inter-community settled value from the per-community local values.

The framing also inherits Wenger's claim that *identity* is constituted through trajectories across communities, not through possession of intrinsic properties. This is what licenses the framing's structural commitment that subject identity is *by relations* (the cells whose subject it is, [aboutness](../../../glossary/concepts/aboutness.md)) rather than *by essence* — and the structural correlate that subjects can be *re-identified across fibers* without being *the same essence*.
