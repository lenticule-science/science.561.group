## Annotation

The standard collected edition of Peirce's published and manuscript writings. Foundational for three traditions the predicated framing draws on:

1. **Triadic semiotics**: every sign-process is a triad of *sign* (representamen), *object* (what the sign stands for), and *interpretant* (the further sign the original sign generates in a mind or community). Sign-relations are *not* dyadic. The predication-cell's subject–predicate–object triad is the structural cousin: the predicate plays the sign role, the cell's subject is the object, and the cell's *placement in a fiber within a discourse community* is the interpretant.
2. **Existential graphs**: a graphical syntax for first-order and modal logic (Alpha, Beta, Gamma graphs) that Peirce developed as a logical notation more revealing of inferential structure than algebraic notation. Existential graphs are the historical precedent for both Sowa's conceptual graphs and the framing's [predication-fibration](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md) — a graphical, relational notation in which inference is *operation on the graph* rather than symbol-manipulation.
3. **Pragmaticism**: the meaning of a concept is the totality of its practical bearings — the *consequences of holding the assertion true*. The framing inherits this in the [closure-as-fitting-fixpoint](../../../glossary/concepts/closure-as-fitting-fixpoint.md) commitment: the *meaning* of a cell is what closure under the rule-system makes derivable from it, not the cell's intrinsic content.

Peirce's triadic structure is what underwrites the framing's claim that the predication-cell is irreducibly three-place. Reductions to two-place relations (binary edges in a graph) lose the *interpretant* (which fiber, which discourse community); reductions to one-place predicates (typed nodes) lose the *object* (what the predicate is *about*).
