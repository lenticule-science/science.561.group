A claim entity asserts one proposition the theory takes to hold in the fully-settled state $v^T_\infty$.
In the [predication-fibration](predication-fibration.md) $(\mathbf{FOUR}, \mathcal{C}, E)$, claims are members of $E$ whose kind-classifier is `claims/`.

A claim is distinct from a [transformer](transformer.md): a transformer is a generator of the closure operator $\mathrm{Cl}^T$ (a rule or relation); a claim is a statement about the output of $\mathrm{Cl}^T$ (a theorem).
Asserted axiom data (initial valuation $v_0$) is also not a claim — it lives in entity fibers.
A claim is what the substrate has derived and committed to as true.

Claims may have status: open (stated but unproved), sketched (informal argument), or proved (full derivation).
Even open claims are substrate entities: recording them is the substrate's act of acknowledging an open research question about its own behavior.

Per AgOP's agential-self-generation invariant, the theorems about the substrate are themselves substrate entities.
A claim entity is the operative record that the substrate has asserted this theorem — accountable, auditable, and subject to the same predication-fibration algebra as all other entities in the [filestore](filestore.md).
