# volksgeist

*Volksgeist* — literally "folk spirit" or "national spirit" — is the supposed unique essence that romantic nationalism attributes to each people: an inner character, carried in a people's language, songs, myths, and customs, that makes it the distinct people it is.
The term is associated above all with Johann Gottfried Herder, who in his *Ideas for a Philosophy of the History of Humanity* (1784) argued that each *Volk* expresses its own genius through its particular tongue and inheritance, and that this particularity is the true unit of human life.
Generated by: **romantic-nationalism** — the volksgeist is the metaphysical centerpiece of that movement, the alleged thing that all the collected folklore is held to express.

The decisive thing to understand about volksgeist is that it presents as a timeless essence what is in fact a historically produced and politically curated construction.
The claim is that a people *has* a spirit, prior to and independent of anyone's choices, the way a person has a temperament.
But the spirit is never simply found.
It is assembled: someone collects the songs, codifies the costumes, edits the tales, and declares the result to be the authentic expression of the national soul.
Herder's framework, once adopted, generates the very evidence of its own truth — the folk tales get gathered, the festivals get organized, and the *Volk* duly becomes visible, as though it had been waiting there all along.
This is why the entry sits opposite **contingency**: volksgeist takes an arrangement that came about through specific history and recasts it as a necessary, eternal essence, which makes it far harder to question.

The political stakes follow directly.
If a people's spirit is a real, bounded essence, then deviation from it can be diagnosed as inauthenticity or contamination.
Volksgeist supplies the standard against which **the-folk** is measured and against which outsiders — Jews, Roma, urban cosmopolitans, the multilingual, the modern — are judged to be foreign to the national soul.
A people that is actually plural, mixed, and changing is rewritten as a single organic essence with a definite content, and whoever fails to embody that content becomes a problem for the nation rather than simply a member of a diverse population.
The concept moves easily from Herder's pluralist hope into exclusionary and, eventually, fascist registers, where the purity of the national spirit becomes a justification for expelling or destroying those said to defile it.

A grad student should note both Herder's intent and the concept's drift.
Herder meant volksgeist to dignify every people equally and to resist the flattening of cultures under one imperial standard; he was an anti-colonial thinker in his way.
But because the concept locates identity in a fixed inner essence rather than in living, ongoing relations, it lends itself to ranking, policing, and purification once a state takes it up.
Read against its neighbours, volksgeist is the essentialized core that **romantic-nationalism** elaborates, the thing **political-myth** narrates and the **invention-of-tradition** manufactures the evidence for.
Its alternative is to see a people not as the bearer of a spirit but as a web of relations and practices that could always have been, and could still become, otherwise.
