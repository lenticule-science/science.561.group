# Kyriarchial Politics

A theory of [politics](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/_archive/sociology/content/politics/_index.md) analyzed through the lens of [kyriarchism](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/social/_archive/critical-theory/theories/queer/theories/kyriarchism): politics as organized within and reproducing interlocking systems of domination (patriarchy, white supremacy, capitalism, heteronormativity, ableism) rather than as neutral governance.
Authority relations, decision-making processes, sovereignty claims, political institutions, and polis formation — the politics vocabulary's terms — are exhibited as specific structures produced through kyriarchal dynamics.

## What this theory commits to

Kyriarchial politics analyzes how political life is structured, valued, and contested within intersecting systems of power:

1. **Politics is never neutral** — all political activity occurs within and reproduces kyriarchal relations of power
2. **Authority is intersectional** — who counts as having legitimate authority varies depending on where actors sit in intersecting hierarchies of gender, race, class, sexuality, ability
3. **Sovereignty is contested** — claims to self-determination and governance emerge from specific positionalities within the kyriarchy and often challenge its foundations
4. **Institutions are isomorphic** — political structures mirror and reproduce broader societal power distributions
5. **Liberation requires dismantling** — transforming politics requires dismantling the interlocking systems themselves, not just reforming political practices

## Key Concepts

- **Interlocking authority**: How different forms of political authority (traditional, elected, appointed, claimed) reinforce each other across axes of domination
- **Positional political value**: How the legitimacy ascribed to identical political acts varies based on actor identity within kyriarchal hierarchies
- **Institutional kyriarchism**: How political institutions internalize and reproduce kyriarchal distributions of power and value
- **Intersectional sovereignty**: How self-determination movements emerge from specific kyriarchal positionalities and often challenge multiple domination systems simultaneously
- **Political solidarity fractures**: How kyriarchal divisions undermine collective political power and how solidarity can be rebuilt across differences

- **Distinct from predicated-politics**: While predicated-politics analyzes politics through formal predication mathematics, kyriarchial-politics analyzes politics through power relations
