Dialogism is the principle that every utterance is oriented toward other utterances — that meaning is constituted through the encounter between voices, not within a single speaker&rsquo;s intention.

The concept is central to the [Bakhtin Circle](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/semiotics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/bakhtin-circle.md)&rsquo;s semiotic theory. [Mikhail Bakhtin](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/humanities/domains/general/domains/people/mikhail-bakhtin.md) argued that no word arrives in a vacuum. Every word has already been used by others, carries their accents and intentions, and enters a field of existing discourse that shapes its meaning. To speak is always to respond — to prior utterances, to anticipated counter-responses, to the social languages that saturate every word with evaluative orientation [@bakhtin_DialogicImagination_1981].

Dialogism is not a property of some utterances (conversations, debates) as opposed to others (monologues, declarations). It is the constitutive condition of all language use. Even an apparently monologic text — a scientific paper, a legal statute, a sermon — is dialogic: it responds to prior positions, anticipates objections, and takes its meaning from the discursive field it enters. Monologue, in Bakhtin&rsquo;s analysis, is a socially enforced reduction of dialogue, not its absence.

## Heteroglossia

The social expression of dialogism is heteroglossia — the coexistence of multiple social languages within a single national language. A doctor&rsquo;s discourse, a merchant&rsquo;s discourse, a priest&rsquo;s discourse, a peasant&rsquo;s discourse: each carries its own worldview, its own evaluative accents, its own system of [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md). The novel, for Bakhtin, is the literary form that orchestrates heteroglossia, giving voice to multiple social languages without reducing them to a single authorial position.

## Multi-accentuality

[Valentin Voloshinov](../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/knowledge-services-section/services/research-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/valentin-voloshinov.md) extended the dialogic principle into a theory of the [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) as the arena of ideological struggle. Every [sign](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) carries multiple ideological accents held in tension. A ruling class attempts to make the sign &ldquo;uni-accentual&rdquo; — to fix its meaning and present that fixity as natural. But in periods of social upheaval, the multi-accentuality of [signs](../../documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/communicating/content/language/_archive/sign.md) becomes visible: the same word means different things to different classes [@voloshinov_MarxismPhilosophyLanguage_1929].
