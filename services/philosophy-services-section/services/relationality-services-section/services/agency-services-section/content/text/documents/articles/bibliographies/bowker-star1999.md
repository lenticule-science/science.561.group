## Annotation

Bowker and Star's analysis of classification systems — how they make some kinds of work visible and others invisible, how they encode standpoints that become naturalized, how exceptions and residual categories carry the work the system cannot otherwise represent — is the disciplinary critique that motivates predicated-work's bilattice-valued treatment of [operational-evidence](../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/operational-evidence.md).
The four-state reading $\{\bot, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \top\}$ is what gives the substrate language for the *residual categories* Bowker and Star identify: $\bot$ for the not-yet-classified, $\top$ for the contradictorily-classified, both as first-class operational stances rather than failures of the classification.

The relevance to [adjudication](../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/content/individual/content/agent/actuating/content/actuator/embodying/content/body/integrating/content/drone/swarming/content/swarm/content/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/adjudication.md): Bowker and Star's *boundary objects* — artifacts that mediate between communities with incommensurable classifications — are exactly the cells where adjudication happens, where $\top$-states surface and require repair across community standpoints rather than within one.
