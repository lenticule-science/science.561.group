**Adjudication** is the act of [governing](governing.md): the *receiving* dual of [speaking](speaking.md).
Where speaking *produces* a charge — one act, one stance toward one content — adjudication *holds and settles* the charges that many acts produce, forming a single state of the record at every point from accumulating, possibly conflicting, possibly partial sources.

## Forming one state from many sources

Most of what anyone holds rests on the say-so of others.
C.A.J. Coady's *Testimony* established that testimony is an irreducible ground of knowledge — we cannot rebuild our picture of the world from private observation alone — which means a knower's standing task is to form a settled view out of many received reports that need not agree.
Where those reports conflict, the problem is the one studied by the theory of *judgment aggregation* and by legal adjudication: how a body of contending inputs is brought to a single verdict that the record can carry.
Adjudication is that office, made general: the holding of a settled epistemic state over a whole field of contributions.

It must answer to three pressures at once, on one structure:

- **conflict** — when sources contradict, the state holds the contradiction as *contested* rather than collapsing.
  This is the paraconsistent discipline of Belnap's reasoner: a contradiction in the record must not let everything follow.
  The conflict is marked and kept live, not erased and not exploded.
- **partial information** — where nothing has been contributed, the state is *open*, not false.
  Absence of evidence is held as such; silence is not denial.
- **accumulation** — the state grows monotonically as contributions arrive, across time and across many contributors, converging without requiring every source to coordinate with every other.

## Holding, not inferring

What distinguishes adjudication from reasoning is that it *holds* a state rather than deriving new content from it.
Inference runs inside a single act, drawing out the consequences of what was said; adjudication persists *across* acts, maintaining the standing verdict as new acts land.
It is governance read epistemically: the settled law over the shared field that [teaching](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/library/teaching/docs/teaching.md) and [learning](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/library/learning/docs/learning.md) write to and read from, and from which a claim, once it stops moving, passes into [knowledge](../../research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/animating/content/becoming/doing/working/governing/knowledge/docs/knowledge.md).
