When the captain gives a directive that references mathematical structure (cellular closure, bilattice, holography, topos, projection), the first move is **research**, not synthesis.

**Why:** On 2026-04-05 the captain said the MUD directive needed "a tremendous amount of more math that we need to be drawing on" and specifically: "if your officers are not mathematically barcode shadows of holograms your math is not complex enough." I had produced an analysis doc, a new plan, and an edit to the sprint in under five minutes of reading three Teraum/TAB files. The artifacts were premature — they locked in a mapping before the math was grounded. The captain's framework is deep (GFRTU, cellular closure, fiber Heyting algebras) and any office/agent/room model needs to compose with it, not sit alongside it.

**How to apply:** When a directive references mathematical concepts:
1. Record the verbatim directive first.
2. Grep the library for the exact terms and related formalisms before producing anything.
3. Dispatch researchers to fill gaps BEFORE writing plans or analyses.
4. Hold synthesis until the math has been grounded in named, sourced formalisms.
5. "Barcode shadows of holograms," "fuzzy cells," "closure convergence," "bilattice affinity" are not metaphors — they name specific mathematical objects that must be located or defined before being used as plan vocabulary.

The MUD framing from the previous turn may still be right, but it needs to compose with the math, not be built in parallel with it.
