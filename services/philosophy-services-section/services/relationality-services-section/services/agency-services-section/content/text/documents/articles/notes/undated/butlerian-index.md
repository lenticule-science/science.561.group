## Butler

Judith Butler (1956–).
Butler's work spans gender theory (performativity, *Gender Trouble*) and political philosophy (grievability, precarity, *Frames of War*).
This subdomain covers the biopolitical side: the analysis of how populations are sorted into grievable and ungrievable lives through media frames, state rhetoric, and the differential distribution of vulnerability.

Butler's central argument in *Precarious Life* (2004) and *Frames of War* (2009) is that grievability is not a response to death but a precondition for recognizing life as life.
The question of who can be mourned is prior to the question of who can be killed — political and media structures frame certain lives as ungrievable before any violence is done to them.
This connects directly to biopolitics: the governance of which lives count is the governance of who can be killed with impunity.

Butler's gender theory (performativity) lives under queer theory in this library, where its meaning is constituted by questions about normativity and identity.
The biopolitical work lives here, where its meaning is constituted by questions about governance and the distribution of vulnerability.
