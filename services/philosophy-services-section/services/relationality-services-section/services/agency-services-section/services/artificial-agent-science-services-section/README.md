# Artificial Agent Science Services Section

Artificial agent science is the study of artificial agents — today, principally LLM-based agents — as agents:
their architectures, their use of tools and memory, how they are benchmarked, where they fail, and how multiple agents coordinate or fail to.
It seats under `agency-services-section` because that section's charter is the study of agency in this research program,
and its README already recognized social science as the science of *human* agents in relation;
this section is its sibling for the artificial case.

## Seating derivation

The former `agent-science-services-section` was founded 2026-08-21; it is now normalized as `agency-services-section`.
A library survey (2026-08-22) found no section owning contemporary artificial agents as a scientific subject:
`ai-science-services-section` (intelligence-science forest) held only operational character files and triage,
and the classical multi-agent-systems literature (Wooldridge and Jennings, contract nets, BDI) appeared nowhere.
The group operates on artificial agents daily — this library is itself maintained by them —
so the gap between implicit and explicit knowledge was largest exactly here.
This section is founded as agent-science's second subsection to close it.

## Relations to existing content (not moved)

- `ai-science-services-section` (intelligence-science forest) owns the underlying models — training, scaling, alignment, evaluation of the model itself; this section owns what is built *from* them as agents.
- `social-science-services-section` (sibling) owns human agents in relation; coordination phenomena that span human and artificial agents are related across the two, not duplicated.
- `agent-computer-system-engineering-services-section` (engineering forest) owns the group's own agent tooling as engineering; this section owns the science it should be reading.
- `news-media-science-services-section`'s accountable-agentic-newsrooms memo consumes this section's findings on agent reliability and multi-agent failure for its human-accountability boundary.

## Scope

This section owns the literatures for:

- Single-agent science: the classical lineage (agents as a program category, belief-desire-intention), LLM-agent architectures (reasoning-acting loops, tool use, reflection, memory), agent benchmarks, and documented failure modes.
- Multi-agent science: the classical coordination canon (contract nets, agent communication languages, mechanism design in MAS), LLM multi-agent systems, and the empirical literature on when multiple agents help and when they compound errors.

It does not own the underlying model science (ai-science), the group's operational agent code (engineering forest), or human social coordination (social science).

## Operational relations, not ownership

Empirical claims about provider reliability, tool use, coordination, refusal,
and failure belong here only as scientific questions and evidence-bounded
findings. The live mechanism that contracts and assays one inference act is
the engineering forest's `computer-inference-system-services-section`, with
`inference-work-lot-service` retaining work-lot, provider, compact, receipt,
and receiver-disposition evidence. The operations forest's Union Officer
Academy retains character formation and office doctrine: a provider response
can animate a session, but cannot appoint an officer or authorize a receiver
effect. Scientific notes should link those engineering receipts or operational
attestations instead of copying provider availability or deployment state into
this section.
