Belief-desire-intention (BDI) is a classical architecture for building rational agents, given its canonical statement in Anand Rao and Michael Georgeff's ["BDI Agents: From Theory to Practice"](https://cdn.aaai.org/ICMAS/1995/ICMAS95-042.pdf) (Proceedings of the First International Conference on Multiagent Systems, ICMAS 1995). An agent maintains three components: beliefs, its (possibly incomplete or wrong) model of the current state of the world; desires, the set of objectives it would like to bring about; and intentions, the subset of desires it has actually committed to pursuing, together with whatever partial plan it has adopted for pursuing them. The paper's contribution was to reconcile two traditions that had run in parallel without much contact — a quantitative, decision-theoretic formalization of rational commitment, and a symbolic, possible-worlds-semantics formalization — and to connect both to practical BDI interpreters (systems like PRS and dMARS) that actually ran an explicit belief-revision, option-generation, and intention-commitment cycle at each control loop.

BDI sits within the wider classical lineage surveyed by Michael Wooldridge and Nicholas Jennings's ["Intelligent Agents: Theory and Practice"](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0269888900008122) (*Knowledge Engineering Review*, 1995), which treats BDI as one deliberative architecture among the reactive and hybrid alternatives available to a designer.

The architecture's relationship to the 2023-era [LLM agent](llm-agent.md) turn is one of vocabulary surviving where formal machinery did not. Systems like ReAct, Reflexion, and Voyager are sometimes described informally in BDI's terms — a prompt's carried state as "beliefs," its objective as a "desire" or "goal," its committed next action as an "intention" — but none of them implement BDI's possible-worlds semantics, its explicit belief-revision function, or its formal intention-commitment and reconsideration policy; the loop is instead a natural-language observe-reason-act cycle with no discrete belief or intention data structure underneath the prose. A 2025 survey states this discontinuity directly: classical frameworks such as BDI "are often rhetorically applied but are fundamentally mismatched to the stochastic, non-symbolic, and context-driven nature of LLM-based agents" (Mohamad Abou Ali, Fadi Dornaika, and Jinan Charafeddine, ["Agentic AI: A Comprehensive Survey of Architectures, Applications, and Future Directions"](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10462-025-11422-4), *Artificial Intelligence Review*, published online 14 November 2025, peer-reviewed journal; first posted as arXiv:2510.25445, October 2025). An earlier draft of this entry misattributed this quotation to Zixiang Wang et al.'s "Multi-Agent Systems: From Classical Paradigms to Large Foundation Model-Enabled Futures" (arXiv:2604.18133, April 2026, since itself accepted at *IEEE/CAA Journal of Automatica Sinica*, May 2026) — that paper is a real, peer-reviewed survey of classical-versus-LLM multi-agent systems, but it does not contain this particular statement.
