## Why this is central

This study compares resting-state functional connectivity between
professional Baduk (Go) players and matched controls across four major
brain networks (default mode, left and right fronto-parietal, and
salience networks), identified by independent component analysis.
Its central finding — professional players show *lower* intrinsic
connectivity within a network's own canonical regions but *higher*
extrinsic connectivity to regions outside that network's traditional
boundaries — is read by the authors as evidence of expertise-driven
network reorganization: the regions gaining connectivity are ones
independently implicated in board-game expertise. This is one of the two
network-level studies (with [jung2013](jung2013.md)) [kim2025](kim2025.md)'s
2025 review draws on for its claim of "domain-specific neural
adaptations" in professional Go players, and it sits alongside
[chen2003](chen2003.md) as the resting-state counterpart to that earlier
task-activation finding.

## What it does not settle

Like [jung2013](jung2013.md), this is a cross-sectional expert-versus-
control design and cannot on its own separate training effects from
pre-existing individual differences that correlate with becoming a
professional player. The paper also does not address executive function
directly — its object is network topology, not behavioral or cognitive
outcome measures, which is the gap [kim2025](kim2025.md)'s review is
positioned to synthesize across studies that do measure them.

## Bridges

- [chen2003](chen2003.md) — the task-activation study this resting-state finding complements
