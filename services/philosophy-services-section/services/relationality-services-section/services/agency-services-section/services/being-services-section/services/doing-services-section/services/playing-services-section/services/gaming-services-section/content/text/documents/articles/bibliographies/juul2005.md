## Why this is central to the framework

Half-Real does the comparative definitional work for the field: Juul
reads the prior definitions (Huizinga, Caillois, Suits, Avedon &
Sutton-Smith, Crawford, Salen & Zimmerman) against each other and
distills the classic game model — six features: rules, variable and
quantifiable outcome, valorization of outcome, player effort, player
attachment to outcome, negotiable consequences. This section's
[game](../../../glossary/terms/game.md) term's four-feature framework is
a deliberate simplification of the same space, and Juul's survey is the
citation trail for anyone auditing that simplification. The book's core
thesis — that video games are half real rules and half fictional world,
playable at both levels — is also the cleanest statement of why
[rules](../../../glossary/terms/rules.md) and setting must be analyzed
separately even when one artifact carries both.

## What it does not settle

The classic game model is explicitly a model of the classic game: Juul
himself catalogues the borderline cases (open-ended simulations,
games of pure chance, role-playing) that sit outside it, and the model
is descriptive of a historical form, not a theory of play. The
activity-level questions — what playing is, what the lusory attitude
commits an agent to — remain with Suits and the playing section above.
