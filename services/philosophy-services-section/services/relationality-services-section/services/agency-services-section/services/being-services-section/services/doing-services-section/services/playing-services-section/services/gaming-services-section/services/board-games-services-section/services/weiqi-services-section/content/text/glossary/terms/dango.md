Dango ("dumpling") in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is a clump of stones bunched together with few [liberties](./liberty.md) relative to their number, an overconcentrated shape that gains little board coverage for the stones spent on it [@senseislibrary]. A dumpling shape is inefficient specifically because it duplicates work: several stones end up defending the same small set of liberties that fewer, better-placed stones would have secured.

Dango is not the same failure as being merely heavy. [Thickness](./thickness.md) is also a concentration of stones, but a thick wall faces outward and does useful work on the position around it; a dumpling shape faces nowhere in particular and is judged a mistake independent of context [@kageyama1978lessons]. Avoiding dango is one of the standard reasons to prefer [tobi](./tobi.md) or [keima](./keima.md) over a solid connection when either would hold the position.

## Related terms

- [thickness](./thickness.md) - Thickness is a concentration of stones that still does useful outward-facing work; dango is the same concentration without it.
- [tobi](./tobi.md) - Playing tobi instead of a solid connection is a standard way to avoid building dango.
- [keima](./keima.md) - Keima, like tobi, covers ground a solid connection would spend into dango.
- [sabaki](./sabaki.md) - Sabaki explicitly favors light shape over the heavy, clumped shape dango names.
