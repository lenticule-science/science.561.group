A modifier is a numerical bonus or penalty applied to a dice roll in a [tabletop role-playing game](../../../../README.md). Modifiers represent the influence of a [character's](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/character.md) abilities, circumstances, equipment, or other factors on the probability of success.

Sources of modifiers include: character attributes (a strong character gets a bonus to lifting), skills (a trained lockpick gets a bonus to picking locks), situational factors (attacking from higher ground), and equipment (a weapon with enhanced accuracy). Modifiers can be positive (bonuses) or negative (penalties), and most systems allow them to stack.

The magnitude of modifiers relative to the [dice mechanic](./dice-mechanic.md) determines how much character skill matters versus random chance. In a d20 system, a +5 modifier shifts the probability of success by 25% — character ability dominates. In a 3d6 system, the same +5 nearly guarantees success on moderate tasks because the bell curve concentrates results around the average. System designers calibrate modifier scales to produce the desired balance between competence and uncertainty.
