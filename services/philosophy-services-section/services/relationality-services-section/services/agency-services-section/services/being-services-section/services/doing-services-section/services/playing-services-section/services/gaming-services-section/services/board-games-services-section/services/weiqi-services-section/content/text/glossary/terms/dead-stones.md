Dead stones in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) are stones left on the board after both players [pass](./pass.md) that cannot avoid eventual [capture](./capture.md) under correct play, and are therefore removed as though captured before the board is counted, without either player having to actually play out the capture [@britgo2026howtoplaygo]. A stone or group is dead exactly when it fails the same test [life-and-death](./life-and-death.md) reading always applies: it cannot make two secure [eyes](./eye.md) and cannot escape or be rescued. A group that shares unresolvable liberties with the opponent, by contrast, is in [seki](./seki.md) and is never marked dead.

How dead stones are settled is one of the sharpest procedural differences between rulesets, and one the [rulesets comparison](../../documents/articles/notes/rulesets.md) records explicitly. Japanese-style rules settle status by mutual agreement after the two passes, which is fast but is the classic source of scoring disputes when players disagree about a group's fate [@nihonkiin1989rules]. Chinese-style area-scoring play resolves genuine disagreements by resuming alternating play until the disputed group is actually captured or clearly alive, which is slower but leaves no room for disagreement about the result.

## Related terms

- [life-and-death](./life-and-death.md) - A stone is dead exactly when life-and-death reading shows it cannot live.
- [pass](./pass.md) - Dead-stone settlement begins only after both players have passed in succession.
- [seki](./seki.md) - Stones in seki are explicitly not dead; they survive by sharing unfillable liberties.
- [area-scoring](./area-scoring.md) - Area-scoring rulesets typically settle disputed dead stones by resuming capture, not by agreement.
- [territory-scoring](./territory-scoring.md) - Territory-scoring rulesets typically settle dead stones by mutual agreement after passing.
