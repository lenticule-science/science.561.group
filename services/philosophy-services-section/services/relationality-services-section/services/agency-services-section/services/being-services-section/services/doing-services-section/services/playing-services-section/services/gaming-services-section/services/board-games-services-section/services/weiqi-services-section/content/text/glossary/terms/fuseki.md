Fuseki in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is the opening considered as a whole board, as distinct from [joseki](./joseki.md), the memorized locally-balanced corner sequences a fuseki is built from [@ishida1977joseki]. Where a joseki is judged by whether it is locally balanced, a fuseki is judged by whether the whole-board framework it builds serves the position — a joseki that is correct in isolation can still be the wrong choice for the fuseki around it [@senseislibrary].

Fuseki theory carries the same historical dispute as [influence](./influence.md): territorial orthodoxy took the corners first, then the sides, then the centre; the New Fuseki (*Shinfuseki*) movement of Go Seigen and Kitani Minoru, 1933, argued for centre-facing, influence-first opening play instead, and permanently changed what counted as a reasonable opening even though parts of it were later shown to be wrong. Choosing between these is the "direction of play" judgment the section's [Weiqi Strategic Theory](../../documents/articles/research-articles/2026-08-29-note-on-weiqi-strategic-theory.md) note names as the concept most resistant to local analysis and the one professionals most consistently identify as the amateur's real weakness.

## Related terms

- [joseki](./joseki.md) - A fuseki is built from joseki chosen to fit the whole board, not merely to be locally correct.
- [influence](./influence.md) - The New Fuseki movement is the historical dispute over how much influence a fuseki should stake on, versus territory taken directly.
- [thickness](./thickness.md) - A fuseki that builds thickness is choosing influence-first play over immediate territory.
