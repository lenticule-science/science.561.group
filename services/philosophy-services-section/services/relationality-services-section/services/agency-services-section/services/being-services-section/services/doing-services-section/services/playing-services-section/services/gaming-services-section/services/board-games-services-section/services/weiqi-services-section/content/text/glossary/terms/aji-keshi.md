Aji-keshi ("erasing the aji") in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is playing a move that forces the opponent to answer in a way that removes the player's own latent options — the residual [aji](./aji.md) a position was carrying [@senseislibrary]. It is one of the most characteristic strong-player errors: the move usually gains something locally, but it spends a possibility that was worth more unspent.

As a proposal in the section's own [Weiqi Strategic Theory](../../documents/articles/research-articles/2026-08-29-note-on-weiqi-strategic-theory.md) (§4), aji-keshi is a premature settlement: a move that drives a cell held at an unwitnessed value (⊥) to true or false and thereby destroys the option that cell was carrying, so it is not a bad move because it loses points locally — it usually gains points locally — but because it collapses a ⊥ that was worth more open than closed. Status: proposal.

## Related terms

- [aji](./aji.md) - Aji-keshi is the specific error of destroying aji rather than merely leaving it unused.
- [gote](./gote.md) - Aji-keshi sequences are frequently gote: the local gain does not carry the initiative forward.
- [honte](./honte.md) - Honte deliberately spends tempo instead of aji; aji-keshi spends aji instead of tempo.
