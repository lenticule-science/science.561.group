Tobi ("jump") in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is playing a stone one empty point away from an existing stone along a line, leaving the intersection between them unoccupied [@senseislibrary]. A two-space jump extends the same idea one point further; both are not physically connected the way an adjacent stone is, but are functionally connected in most fighting situations because the opponent cutting at the gap usually costs more than it gains.

Tobi is a light shape: it covers more of the board per stone than a solid connection does, at the cost of leaving a cutting point the opponent can sometimes exploit [@kageyama1978lessons]. Whether a given jump's gap is a real weakness or only a theoretical one is a local reading question, not something the shape settles by itself.

## Related terms

- [keima](./keima.md) - Keima is the other standard light-extension shape, covering a different offset than tobi at a similar cost in connection strength.
- [sabaki](./sabaki.md) - Jumps are the characteristic move of sabaki play, trading a firm connection for speed and flexibility.
- [dango](./dango.md) - Tobi is the light alternative to the heavy, over-connected shape dango names.
