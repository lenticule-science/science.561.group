## Why this is central to the framework

Caillois provides the classification Huizinga withheld: play divides
into agon (contest), alea (chance), mimicry (simulation), and ilinx
(vertigo), each running along the paidia–ludus axis from free
improvisation to rule-bound discipline. The four categories are the
working typology everywhere the Group analyzes what kind of engagement
a game offers — and they are one whole side of the open
[Girard–Caillois substrate-correspondence question](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/social-theory-science-services-section/services/ludics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/notes/questions-on-girard-caillois-substrate-correspondence.md),
which asks whether Caillois's cultural typology and Girard's
proof-theoretic ludics are two presentations of one algebraic object.

## What it does not settle

The categories classify; they do not explain. Why these four, whether
they are exhaustive, and what happens in games that mix them (poker is
agon and alea at once) are left open — Caillois himself treats the
combinations and corruptions of each category informally. The
paidia–ludus axis is likewise a description of degree, not a mechanism
of how free play acquires structure; that transition is exactly what
this section's [play](../../../glossary/terms/play.md) /
[playing](../../../glossary/terms/playing.md) / gaming seam is built to
hold.
