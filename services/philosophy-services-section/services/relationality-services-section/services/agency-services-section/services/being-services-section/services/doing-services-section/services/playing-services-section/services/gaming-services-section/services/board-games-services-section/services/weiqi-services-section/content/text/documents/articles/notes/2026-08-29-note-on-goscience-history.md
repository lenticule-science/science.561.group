## 1. The core doctrine's origin: "the engine is the executable form of the glossary"

The platform's organizing thesis is stated verbatim in `weiqi-platform-plan.md` (board-approved 2026-08-18):

> "Every major Go server treats playing and learning as separate products: you play on OGS or Fox, you learn on GoMagic or BadukPop or a book. This repository's weiqi corpus — a dependency-linked glossary, a sequenced lesson curriculum with a uniform worked-example/exercise template, and a formal OWL/SHACL ontology binding it together — is precisely the asset that lets us fuse them. The platform's differentiator is **glossary-linked play**: the rules engine recognizes the concepts the corpus defines... as they occur on the board, and the interface surfaces the corpus's own definitions and lessons at the moment they become relevant. The game explains itself while you play it. No incumbent does this, and no incumbent has the content architecture to do it."

And, the sharper formulation used everywhere downstream as house shorthand: **"the engine is the executable form of the glossary. Each glossary term corresponds to a named predicate or event in the rules kernel, documented in both directions."** This is not decoration — it is a literal engineering contract: every semantic event the kernel fires (`atari-given`, `capture`, `ko-started`, `connection`, etc.) carries a `termRef` field pointing at the exact glossary-term file that defines it, so the concept graph and the executable rules are kept mechanically in sync rather than drifting apart the way documentation usually does. The `weiqi-lsp` content checker exists specifically to police this contract (validating `termRef`s resolve, validating diagram claims against kernel recomputation) — the R9 diagram-transcription bug (an ASCII diagram silently disagreeing with its own `expected:` block) is treated in-corpus as proof the contract needs machine enforcement, not just good intentions. See [Engineering History](2026-08-29-note-on-engineering-history.md) §2.7 for the full incident.

The same thesis governs the **mastery/skill-tree model** in the platform plan: *"The concept graph is the skill tree. The ontology's `requires`/`teaches`/`defines` relations give us mastery tracking for free: a player's profile records which concepts they have been taught, exercised, and demonstrated in live play (the kernel's semantic events provide the evidence...)."* Progression, spaced-repetition scheduling, and lesson recommendations are all designed to read from this one graph rather than an ad-hoc XP/points system — this is stated as a deliberate rejection of gamification mechanics, not an oversight (see §4, Pillar 4).

**Per [Reframe and Standards](2026-08-29-note-on-reframe-and-standards.md):** this thesis survives the owner's 2026-08-29 course correction intact, but as one strand in a full feature web rather than the platform's whole definition. This note records the thesis's history and content-side execution as they actually happened; it should be read under that reframe, not as evidence the collapsed frame was the intended shape from the start.

## 2. Content architecture as it stood, and as it was extended

**Pre-existing, as of the 2026-08-18 plan:** four sequenced lessons (overview → living groups and eyes → komi and handicap, with history as a parallel entry) and ten dependency-linked glossary terms in three clusters — tactical (liberty–atari–capture–ko), scoring (territory–komi–handicap), structural (connection–eye–life-and-death) — plus a parent board-games/games curriculum and an **eight-lesson ludics (theory of play) curriculum** supplying the design vocabulary: the magic circle, paidia↔ludus, agon/alea, flow, and Sutton-Smith's rhetorics of play. A machine-checkable **OWL/SHACL ontology** — named in the source material as the **"Ludic ASR ontology"** — already typed content classes (`term`, `lesson`, `specification`, `text`, `topic`) and relations (`defines`, `requires`, `teaches`, `uses-mechanic`, `cites`).

**Explicitly named as missing** in the same plan: superko, suicide, seki, area-vs-territory scoring, pass/end-of-game procedure, dead-stone settlement, handicap placement conventions, standard komi values, and any strategy/training material at all — no joseki, fuseki, tesuji, yose, tsumego, rank-system explainer, annotated games, or SGF records.

**Authored 2026-08-19** (workflow `weiqi-phase0-foundation`, per the confirmed transcript): the rules-completeness suite closing most of that gap — `seki.md`, `false-eye.md`, `hoshi.md`, `weiqi-rulesets.md` (learner-facing ruleset comparison), `ko-and-superko.md`, `how-ranks-work.md`, `handicap-placement-and-komi-values.md`, `seki-and-false-eyes.md`, `ending-the-game.md` (a merge target, §2.2), and the `weiqi.md` topic/start-here page. The section's glossary grew from 10 to **20 terms** in this pass — a stale "10 terms" claim was later caught and corrected during the design-corpus adversarial review (§4).

**Final corpus shape**, per the current on-disk README: **17 glossary terms** in four clusters (tactical: liberty, atari, capture, ko, superko, suicide; scoring: territory, komi, handicap, area scoring, territory scoring, pass, dead stones; structural: connection, eye, life-and-death, seki) plus a five-lesson curriculum (overview, living groups and eyes, komi and handicap, how a game ends, history) and the implementer/learner rulesets pair (`rulesets.md` / `weiqi-rulesets.md`). (The drop from 20 to 17 terms between the Aug 19 pass and the final shape reflects later consolidation, not lost content — the corpus's own current README is the authority cited here.)

### 2.1 The rulesets document as literal kernel spec, not mere prose
`rulesets.md` is written to be **directly encodable** as kernel data: every cell of its five-ruleset × five-parameter comparison table (Chinese/Japanese/AGA/New Zealand/Ing × ko regime/suicide/scoring/komi/handicap placement) carries a stable identifier of the form `ruleset.<name>.<parameter>`. Its own framing: *"this document *is* the kernel's ruleset spec... as declared rule objects rather than code forks."* This is the concept-graph doctrine applied to rules content specifically: the document isn't "inspired by" or "informing" the code, it *is* the code's data source, addressed by the same identifiers the code uses. See [Engineering History](2026-08-29-note-on-engineering-history.md) §2.3 for the full ruleset table and the code side of this decision.

A deliberately preserved nuance rather than a false precision: traditional over-the-board Chinese rules historically left long ko cycles to ad hoc agreement (much like Japanese rules' "no result" convention) rather than a formal positional-superko ban; the document notes this and states `ruleset.chinese.ko_regime` "should be treated as configurable rather than fixed" for a kernel targeting strict traditional play, rather than silently picking one historically-contested answer.

### 2.2 The paired lesson merge: `ending-the-game.md` vs. `how-a-game-ends.md`
Two lessons covered near-identical ground for near-identical audiences — one fact-checked and corrected 2026-08-19, one an earlier draft. Per the 2026-08-19 kernel-completion workflow's confirmed outcome, `ending-the-game.md` was kept canonical and absorbed exactly three pieces of genuinely unique content from the older draft: a note that passing is never blocked by ko, a new paragraph treating **seki as a third settlement status** (distinct from simply "alive" or "dead"), and the entire **"Why area and territory rarely differ"** section — which turned out to be load-bearing, since `rulesets.md` and `regression-positions.md` both hold live anchor links directly into that section (`ending-the-game.md#why-area-and-territory-rarely-differ`), used to explain the roughly 1-point gap between Chinese-style area komi (7.5) and Japanese-style territory komi (6.5) as a real scoring-mechanism effect (see R9–R11 in the regression corpus, [Engineering History](2026-08-29-note-on-engineering-history.md) §2.7–§2.9) rather than an arbitrary historical accident. `how-a-game-ends.md` was then deleted (no git — `rm`, per the git-forbidden constraint, [Engineering History](2026-08-29-note-on-engineering-history.md) §2.2), and all dangling references repointed; a link-checker pass confirmed 384 links across 46 files still resolved afterward.

## 3. Provenance policy — the reasoning, and a real internal disagreement

`provenance-policy.md` states the policy plainly: live documents are singular (no `--archive-YYYY-MM-DD.md` or `--preexisting-*` siblings sitting next to the canonical file); anything historical moves into a section-local `recovered-research/` directory with a mandatory `provenance:` frontmatter field stating what the file is, where the live copy now lives, and why it was retired; nothing outside `recovered-research/` carries that field at all, because *"live documents are trusted as current by default; provenance bookkeeping is only needed where a reader could otherwise mistake an old copy for the live one."*

The stated motivation is explicitly about *scriptability*, not just tidiness: *"a contributor never has to guess which convention applies to a given file, and a script never has to special-case four shapes to find the live copy of anything"* — replacing four inconsistent legacy conventions (recovered-research dirs, `--archive-DATE` siblings, `--preexisting-glossary` siblings, and inline "Imported source" append blocks) that a predecessor session had left in simultaneous use.

**A genuine, confirmed doctrine collision:** the Aug 19 transcript mining turned up that this section-local `provenance-policy.md` was written the *same day* as a **corpus-wide** provenance policy (also literally titled `provenance-policy.md`, at the parent game-science level) — and the two took **opposite stances** on retroactive migration. The corpus-wide policy said existing four-convention variants should be left untouched, read-only, and merely interpreted by the new document going forward; the section-local weiqi policy actually **executed** a retroactive migration (comparing each `--archive-2026-08-17` glossary-term sibling against its live counterpart, folding in any unique content, and physically moving the archive copy into `recovered-research/` with a new `provenance:` field). Rather than silently overriding one with the other, the agent that found this added an explicit "Section-local documents" closing clause to the corpus-wide policy, stating that the weiqi document governs *only* where the two actually conflict — a real, small piece of house-doctrine conflict resolution, done by declaring precedence rather than picking a winner and deleting the loser.

Separately, the platform plan itself frames this hygiene work as a hard **Phase 0 gate**, not optional cleanup: *"Corpus debt bleeding into product [is a named risk]; Phase 0 is a hard gate; SHACL validation in CI keeps it fixed."*

## 4. Design pillars — the game-design constitution

`design-pillars.md` (2026-08-19, `services/weiqi-platform/content/text/documents/articles/design/`) states five pillars as binding constraints on every feature, with an explicit conflict-resolution rule: *"Where pillars conflict in a particular design, the earlier-numbered pillar wins, and the conflict gets written down in the relevant design document."*

1. **Do first, name after.** Vocabulary lands on lived experience — the app never explains a concept before the player has enacted it, and glossary terms are linked at the exact moment they become relevant, not pre-taught. *"We do not teach-then-test; we arrange for the experience, then hand over the word for it."* Decision test: can a first-time visitor act on the board within ten seconds, with no account/modal/video? This directly grounds the kernel's semantic-event layer's reason for existing: *"The kernel's semantic event layer exists precisely so the app knows *when the experience happened.*"*
2. **Feedback while the decision is still warm.** Named as Go's "pedagogical curse": consequences arrive fifty moves after causes, diffused across the whole board. *"A win-rate graph is not feedback; it is an autopsy without a finding."* Three specified instruments: the cause-chain post-mortem (a group's death told as a short story of decisive moments, not a percentage), counterfactual replay (branch into the unplayed move and play it out), and a rank-gated "legibility layer" translating engine judgment into glossary-vocabulary assertions.
3. **Opponents are teachers wearing masks.** Difficulty is a cast of characters, not a slider; *"the boss teaches the mechanic that defeats it."* An 8-opponent cast was designed (design-corpus workflow) spanning capture-go novice to single-digit-kyu, each with a named flaw stated in glossary vocabulary, mapped to an implementation ladder (scripted heuristics now → shaped GnuGo Phase 2 → KataGo human-style nets Phase 3).
4. **Calm by construction.** No streaks, lives, expiring rewards, variable-ratio reward boxes, or engagement-bait notifications; ratings are hidden by default for learners and surfaced only as plain-language trends; *"losing near half your games is presented — truthfully — as the healthy state of a person who is learning."* Motivation is meant to come from three sources only: visible mastery, story (canon/cast/own-games), and belonging (Pillar 5). Decision test: *"Would we still build this feature if we could not measure engagement?"*
5. **A place in the lineage.** The platform's job is induction into Go's actual 4000-year culture, not a proprietary substitute — teaching lineage (who-taught-whom) as a first-class visible graph, clubs outranking lobbies, deliberate ritual (nigiri, bow, a "quiet room" stripping all chrome but the board), and a playable canon of historical games. *"A player who outgrows us walks into any Go club on earth already at home."*

### 4.1 The adversarial design panel actually worked — real findings, not rubber-stamping
Eleven subordinate design documents (onboarding-screenplay, opponent-cast, legibility-layer, mastery-model, own-games-textbook, calm-technology, ritual-and-atmosphere, lineage-and-clubs, canon-and-broadcast, integrity-and-assist, playtest-protocol) were reviewed by a **4-chair adversarial panel** — an F2P-cynic ("calm technology is a euphemism for churn"), a 5-dan Go teacher, an advocate for a "bounced beginner" persona, and a staff engineer checking buildability against the actual stack. The panel is confirmed (Aug 19 transcript mining) to have caught genuine design flaws, not just style nits, including:
- A **deterministic, undisclosed matchmaking manipulation** on loss-streaks that the cynic chair flagged as *"the textbook shape of engagement-optimized matchmaking... undisclosed and unauditable"* — a direct violation of Pillar 4's own decision tests, caught by the panel process the pillars themselves mandated.
- A privacy leak in the calm-technology rating design: a `rating_visibility:"private"` toggle hid the *number* but not the equivalent signal, because automatic handicap-and-komi from rating gap broadcasts the rank difference to the opponent every game regardless of the toggle.
- Factual errors in the go-teacher-facing content: the Chunlan Cup cheating case was misrepresented as ending in "no finding" when it actually ended in a definite ruling (no cheating found, the accuser sanctioned); the Game of the Century's end date was wrong by 10 days; a stale claim that the corpus held "10 glossary terms" when it actually held 20 by that point.
- **Cross-document schema collisions** — two documents independently defined incompatible shapes for the same `Game.assist_mode`/`rated` fields; `own-games-textbook.md`'s `Assertion[]` collided with `legibility-layer.md`'s canonical `Assertion` type (resolved by renaming the former to `ConceptTag[]`).
- An **unbuildable enforcement mechanism**: `integrity-and-assist.md` specified a "second human reviewer" step that the staff-engineer chair flagged as impossible under the platform's own declared solo-operator model.
- `lineage-and-clubs.md` was singled out as the one document with **zero storage-layer specification** for six persistent multi-user entities it otherwise fully designed.

All of these were confirmed, then fixed by a dedicated revision pass, then synthesized into `design-index.md` (175 lines) — the corpus's own map of itself, recording cross-document canonical schemas and which panel findings were resolved vs. left open for the board (notably: Pillar 4's first decision test being fundamentally unauditable from outside, cross-feature signal leakage, and a completion-badge-style mechanic that three separate documents independently proposed and then independently refused for the same calm-technology reasons — treated as a signal the refusal is doctrinally sound, not a coordination failure).

This adversarial-panel pattern — write, then attack from multiple named, motivated, adversarial personas, then revise only confirmed findings — recurs identically in the kernel's code review (rules-lawyer / theory-fit / consumer lenses) and the LSP's false-positive/false-negative hunt (see [Engineering History](2026-08-29-note-on-engineering-history.md) §2.9). It is a consistent house methodology across both code and design content, not something invented once for weiqi.

## 5. Learn app content pipeline — pedagogy enforced mechanically, not just by review

The Phase 1 Learn app's content pipeline treats pedagogical correctness as a testable property, not just an editorial one:
- Glossary JSON and lesson-step JSON are **extracted from the markdown source by script** (`extract-content.mjs`), never hand-transcribed, so the app's data can never silently drift from the canonical corpus text.
- Every tsumego problem is **machine-verified against the kernel**: accept-move legality, refutation-line legality, and the claimed outcome (capture achieved / two eyes formed / correct point count) are all replayed through the real rules engine before a problem is allowed into the shipped set — described in workflow instructions as "the Equivo discipline" (author → verify → keep only reproducing ones), an explicit cross-reference to a naming convention used elsewhere in the house for the same author-then-adversarially-verify pattern.
- A dedicated **pedagogy review lens** (confirmed via Aug 19 transcript mining) audited the shipped lesson/tutorial data against the corpus doctrine and caught one real concept-ordering bug: a lesson's interactive board caption used the word "atari" one step before the lesson had formally defined it — fixed by rewording, not by deleting the caption, preserving Pillar 1's "do first, name after" ordering.
- A separate **accessibility review lens** caught several real defects entangled with pedagogy: color-only territory markers with no shape distinction (a color-only signal is explicitly forbidden by the platform plan's own accessibility requirements, not just general a11y best practice); a tsumego solved/refuted outcome that never reached screen readers; exercise feedback that never routed to an `aria-live` region; and light-theme amber text failing WCAG contrast (3.61:1, below the 4.5:1 threshold for body text). All were fixed, and the full 121-test suite re-verified green afterward.

## 6. Live concept recognition — the claimed competitive differentiator, and the bug that quietly broke it

The 2026-08-20 `weiqi-phase2-experience` workflow's mandate quotes the owner directly:

> "I'd like our Weiqi stuff to continue progressing. We really are looking to meet or beat real games, and I feel like so far we're very light on the graphics and game experience. and that sort of polish, once we get our engines and interfaces done right, is CHEAP."

Its governing spec, `weiqi-market-teardown.md`, is described as having confirmed via competitive research that **live concept recognition during play — the semantic-event-layer thesis surfaced as real-time, non-blocking UI toasts naming a concept with a glossary hover-card link — is UNCLAIMED by any competitor** (checked against the major Android Go apps and OGS), as is SRS-scheduled personalized tsumego. This is the clearest place the go-science content architecture (glossary + concept graph) is claimed, in-house, as the platform's actual product moat rather than merely its pedagogical nicety. See [Weiqi/Go App Landscape](2026-08-29-note-on-go-app-landscape.md) for the fuller, independently-run 2026-08-29 re-survey of this same claim against the current market.

**Confirmed via transcript mining: it shipped broken, and the break was exactly the kind of failure the concept-graph doctrine is supposed to prevent.** Live semantic events fired correctly from the kernel (verified by driving a real game through the R1 ko regression position and observing the correct sequence — `capture → ko-started → eye-formed → atari-given → ko-resolved → capture → ko-started` — each carrying the correct term and `termRef`). But the UI module wiring the toast to its glossary hover-card (`concept-toast.mjs`) passed the toast `<div>` itself as the *root* element to `attachTermSpans`, which internally calls `root.querySelectorAll(...)` — a call that by DOM definition can never match the root element it's called on. The practical effect: **every concept-recognition toast ever rendered was silently unhoverable to its own glossary definition**, directly contradicting both the module's own doc comment and the feature's status as the platform's headline claim in the market-teardown. It was caught by the same playtest pass that produced the win-matrix and parity verdict (see [Engineering History](2026-08-29-note-on-engineering-history.md) §2.11), fixed by wiring `mouseenter`/`focus` handlers directly to the term span, and reverified against `glossary.json`.

The significance for the content/doctrine record specifically: the corpus's central thesis — "the engine is the executable form of the glossary," each event carrying a `termRef` so the UI can surface the corpus's own definitions live — was, for some real span of development time, **true at the kernel layer and silently false at the UI layer**, with no test catching the gap until a dedicated playtest pass drove real games through the actual click-and-hover wiring rather than just asserting event shapes. This is a concrete illustration of why the corpus also built `weiqi-lsp` and an adversarial-review-before-landing habit (§4 above): doctrine holding at one layer of a stack doesn't imply it holds at every layer, and the failure mode here (a structurally-correct-looking call that is logically vacuous) is exactly the kind of thing that survives unit tests asserting "the event fired" without ever asserting "and a human could actually reach its definition."

## 7. Obsmetrologia and Go strategy — a real research inquiry, honestly negative on most fronts

A dedicated workflow (`obsmetrologia-go-strategy`, run under an Opus-tier investigator with a stated honesty bar — *"'no' is a fully successful answer... A reframing of known strategy in new vocabulary is NOT novel"*) asked whether a separate in-house mathematics research program (Obsmetrologia — Lean-formalized sheaf/cohomology/Chu-space structures) contributes anything genuinely new to Go strategy, checked against the real state of Go theory (Berlekamp–Wolfe combinatorial game theory, thermography/miai counting, engine-era ownership analysis) via literature search rather than memory.

**Result, confirmed on-disk** (`obsmetrologia-and-go-strategy.md`): **8 candidates assessed, 2 survived, 6 rejected**, and the surviving two are explicitly framed as *"questions and candidate structures, not results"* with **no worked positions constructed at all** — deliberately deferred until `weiqi-lsp` could machine-verify diagrams (not yet true at investigation time) and until someone chooses to extend the inquiry further.

- **Surviving candidate 1 — "coordination cohomology as an obstruction to whole-board decomposability."** CGT's disjunctive-sum machinery has no formal device for a single move whose effect is shared across otherwise-independent regions (a ladder-breaker serving two ladders, aji shared across two fights); a Lean-proved nerve construction (`CoordinationCohomology.lean`, Ȟ¹ = |E| − |V| + |C|) is conjectured to give a localized, computable measure of exactly where whole-board reading (vs. regional reading) matters — something neither CGT, classical thickness/aji heuristics, nor monolithic engine evaluation currently expresses. Falsification path: once Phase 3's engine-review service exists, compute the nerve's cycle rank over real games and test whether it predicts where regional and whole-board engine analysis disagree; no correlation kills it.
- **Surviving candidate 2 — "the inconsistency sheaf as a formalization of aji."** Aji is potential whose resolution depends on context not yet played — a local-to-global compatibility question. A Lean-formalized structure carrying evidence-for/evidence-against separately, with an explicit gluing obstruction, is conjectured to distinguish *"this resolves with deeper reading"* from *"this structurally depends on undetermined context elsewhere"* — a distinction the note claims current engines blur into scalar ownership-variance statistics. Falsification path: correlate a computed obstruction measure against real engine-review data (does deeper search change the local status where the obstruction should vanish, and not where it should persist).
- **Six rejected, each with a stated reason** — worth recording because each rejection is itself a small piece of Go-theory literacy: sheaf-gluing-as-miai (miai counting already fully captures the content; new vocabulary, no new predictive power); Chu-space/concurrent-game semantics for sente/gote (the house's own prior research note already frames this machinery as *subsuming* CGT, not exceeding it); occupation entropy as whole-board heat (collapses onto what KataGo's ownership variance and CGT's temperature already expose); germ orders/antisymmetry-thinness as "settledness" (real math, but proof-architecture hygiene, not a strategic claim in itself); a transfinite regime tower as a "reading-depth axis" (Go is finite/perfect-information/chance-free — the whole game sits at one point of the tower, and finite complexity results already cover settling hardness); a Caillois four-regimes reading of Go's phases (vocabulary import onto standard phase theory, no new evaluative content).

**The deferred worked-examples thread is now partly unblocked.** Of the two conditions the note deferred on, one is now satisfied: `weiqi-lsp` exists, is fully implemented against its rule catalog, and — per this session's own direct test run — passes its full suite (139/139), meaning mechanical diagram verification is a real, working capability as of this session, not an aspiration. The other condition, Phase 3's engine-review service, remains entirely unbuilt (see [Platform State](2026-08-29-note-on-platform-state.md), Open decision gates). This means the falsification paths the note names for its two surviving candidates — computing the nerve's cycle rank or the inconsistency sheaf's obstruction measure over real games and correlating against engine disagreement — cannot yet run end-to-end, since they need Phase 3's data. But the diagram-construction half of that work (building the worked positions themselves, verified for legality and consistency against the kernel, ready for whenever engine-review data exists to correlate them against) can now proceed with machine verification in hand, which was not true at investigation time. This is recorded here as a concrete, actionable unblocking, not a restatement of the original open thread.

This note is a useful house-methodology exemplar in its own right: it demonstrates the corpus's willingness to run a real speculative-research inquiry and publish a mostly-negative, explicitly-labeled-as-conjecture result rather than either suppressing the negative findings or overselling the two survivors — consistent with the same "code as a perfect model of the theory, never merely working" ethos applied to research writing rather than code.
