## Why this is central

This is a 2025 narrative review — not a primary neuroimaging study, and
labeled as such here rather than overstated — that synthesizes the
cognitive and neural evidence on Go training across novices, professional
players, and older adults, drawing on neuropsychological assessments,
fMRI and PET neuroimaging findings, and randomized-controlled-trial
behavioral outcomes. Its central claims are that Go training enhances
executive functions, memory, and visuospatial reasoning in trained
populations, and that professional players show domain-specific neural
adaptations including increased precuneus and cerebellar activation
during decision-making tasks. It is the most recent synthesis point for
the three primary studies also gathered in this bibliography
([chen2003](chen2003.md), [jung2013](jung2013.md), [sohn2017](sohn2017.md)),
and is the closest verified 2025 source this section found connecting Go
expertise specifically to executive function.

For this section, the review's executive-function claim gives an
independent empirical frame for treating strategic reading — the kind of
sustained, effortful whole-board evaluation [Obsmetrologia and Go Strategy](../research-articles/obsmetrologia-and-go-strategy.md)
and [Weiqi Strategic Theory](../research-articles/2026-08-29-note-on-weiqi-strategic-theory.md)
formalize mathematically — as cognitively demanding in a measurable,
domain-general sense, not merely a house metaphor.

## What it does not settle

As a narrative rather than systematic review, this paper does not itself
report new data, effect sizes, or a quantitative meta-analysis; its
strength is synthesis and framing, and its claims should be traced back
to the primary studies it cites (several of which — [chen2003](chen2003.md),
[jung2013](jung2013.md), [sohn2017](sohn2017.md) — this section holds
independently) rather than taken as a standalone empirical result. It
also does not address any of this section's own strategic or
combinatorial-game-theory vocabulary; its scope is cognition and
neuroscience only.
