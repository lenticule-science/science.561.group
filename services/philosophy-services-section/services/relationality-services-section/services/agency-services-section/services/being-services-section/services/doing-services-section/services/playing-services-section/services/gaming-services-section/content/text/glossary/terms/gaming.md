Gaming is structured [playing](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/playing.md): the activity of engaging in [games](./game.md) — play that has acquired agreed structure in the form of [rules](./rules.md), objectives, and outcomes. Where play needs nothing but voluntary engagement, gaming presupposes a shared frame the participants uphold together: what moves are legal, what counts as winning, when the game is over. That frame is what makes gaming social and repeatable — the same game can be played again, taught, varied by [house rules](./house-rule.md), and compared across tables and centuries.

Gaming spans traditions rather than media. The section's subsections carry the major ones — board games, card games, dice games, role-playing games, gambling — and the analytic vocabulary (game, [player](./player.md), [game state](./game-state.md), [mechanic](./mechanic.md), [turn](./turn.md), [win condition](./win-condition.md)) applies across all of them. The Group's formal line of work treats gaming's structure mathematically — concurrent game systems, strategies, and ludics, implemented as `@red-cup-engineering/gaming` on the registry — and its research frontier is surveyed in the section's notes ([2026-06-26 survey of ludic theory frontiers](../../documents/articles/notes/2026/06/26/2026-06-26-survey-of-ludic-theory-frontiers.md)).
