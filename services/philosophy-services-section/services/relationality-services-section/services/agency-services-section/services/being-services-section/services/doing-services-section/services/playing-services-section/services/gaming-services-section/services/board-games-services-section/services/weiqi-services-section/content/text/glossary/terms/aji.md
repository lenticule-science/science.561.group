Aji — literally "taste" — in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is latent potential: the residual possibilities in dead or weak stones, or in a defect in the opponent's shape, that are not currently playable but that constrain what both sides can do [@senseislibrary]. The standard maxim is that aji is used by not using it — a threat that has already been played is no longer a threat.

The section's [Weiqi Strategic Theory](../../documents/articles/research-articles/2026-08-29-note-on-weiqi-strategic-theory.md) note (§4) proposes the Obsmetrologia inconsistency sheaf as aji's formal home: aji as a cell held at an unwitnessed value (⊥) whose option value is a function of remaining unwitnessed, so that [aji-keshi](./aji-keshi.md) is the move that prematurely settles that cell and spends the option — status: proposal, that note's own.

As a proposal in [Weiqi as Arena, Chu Space, Predication, and Manifold](../../documents/articles/research-articles/2026-09-03-note-on-weiqi-as-arena-chu-space-predication-and-manifold.md), aji reads as a cell unsettled by the past reading and still changeable by the future reading — where territory is the settled case in both readings — so its worth lies in staying open rather than in whichever value it would take if forced — status: proposal.

## Related terms

- [aji-keshi](./aji-keshi.md) - Aji-keshi is the error of spending aji's option value for a small, immediate, and usually forced gain.
- [sente](./sente.md) - Unlike sente, whose value is spent the instant it is played, aji's value depends on staying unplayed.
- [honte](./honte.md) - Honte is the move that trades away the aji a lighter move would have left behind.
