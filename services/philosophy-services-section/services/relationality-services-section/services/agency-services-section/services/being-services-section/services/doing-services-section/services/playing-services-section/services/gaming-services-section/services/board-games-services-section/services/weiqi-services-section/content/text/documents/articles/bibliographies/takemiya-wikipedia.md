## Why this is central

Takemiya Masaki's "cosmic style" (*uchu-ryu*) is the second, closer
predecessor [Note on Lakota Weiqi Strategy](../notes/2026-09-04-note-on-lakota-weiqi-strategy.md)
§3 names for the owner's hypothesis, and this article is the direct
verification. Born 1951, professional under Kitani Minoru from 1965 (the
same teacher-lineage as Otake Hideo and, one generation earlier, Go
Seigen's Shin Fuseki partner), Takemiya built a style "focused on large
moyo in the center of the board," emphasizing influence and central
control over immediate territory, most visibly in his three-star
(*sanrensei*) openings. The article is explicit on a point load-bearing
for this survey's comparison table: amateurs know him for a "very thick
playing style," and his sanrensei openings "involve plenty of fights" —
this is a whole-board, center-first, fighting style, but a **thick** one,
not a light one. It also records a 1987 loss to Nie Weiping in the
China-Japan Challenge Series and two losses to the AI program Zen at
handicap play in 2012.

## What it does not settle

The article gives no quantitative account of Takemiya's shape lightness,
sacrifice rate, or encirclement ratio in
[National and Historical Play Styles](../research-articles/national-and-historical-play-styles.md)
§6's sense — "thick" and "involve plenty of fights" are qualitative
descriptions this survey reads as directional evidence, not measurements.
It also does not explain why the cosmic style is thick rather than light,
leaving open whether whole-board, moyo-first play necessarily trades
against shape lightness or merely happened to in Takemiya's own case.
