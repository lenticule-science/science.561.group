## Why this is central

Lee Chang-ho is the section's verified source for the "endgame" figure
this survey's task scope names by name. Born 1975, professional at
eleven in 1986 under Cho Hun-hyun, world #1 by ELO rating from 1991 to
2006 — the article's stylistic content is narrow but load-bearing: "his
tendency to prioritize securing his groups before going on the offensive
contributes to his unusually strong endgame attacks, as they gain power
from solid groups." This is a direct, sourced statement of a
solidity-first, endgame-strength style, distinct from both Cho's
fighting-first approach and Lee Sedol's risk-taking one.

## What it does not settle

The article's own text notes it lacks detail on Lee's popularly-known
"Stone Buddha" nickname or specific endgame techniques — the phrase
appears in this survey's own prose (echoing common usage) but is not
independently sourced to this article. The "solid groups first, offense
after" characterization is thin: one sentence, not a developed stylistic
account, and gives no numeric data on any of
[National and Historical Play Styles](../research-articles/national-and-historical-play-styles.md)
§6's seven features.
