Thickness (*atsumi*) in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is a strong, settled, outward-facing formation with no weakness the opponent can exploit locally [@kageyama1978lessons]. Three classical maxims describe what thickness is not: do not use it to enclose [territory](./territory.md), since walling off space wastes the wall's real value; do not play close to thickness, yours or the opponent's, since stones near a thick wall are cheap for its owner to attack; and thickness is a property of the position, not of the stones, so the same wall is strong against an empty board and worthless once the opponent has a living group settled behind it [@kageyama1978lessons].

That third maxim is the one with formal content: a wall's thickness can be destroyed without the wall itself being touched. If the opponent settles a group nearby, the stones of the wall are unchanged and its thickness is gone. A **wall** is the shape; thickness is the shape's value in context.

As a reading over the board fiber, thickness is an ordinary evaluation function on the settled position, no bilattice needed — a proposal of the section's own [Weiqi Strategic Theory](../../documents/articles/research-articles/2026-08-29-note-on-weiqi-strategic-theory.md) (§6), status: proposal.

## Related terms

- [influence](./influence.md) - Thickness is influence concentrated into one strong, settled wall.
- [honte](./honte.md) - Building thickness and playing honte are both trades of tempo for a position with no local weakness.
- [sabaki](./sabaki.md) - Sabaki is the light, expendable-stone strategy for operating near an opponent's thickness rather than confronting it directly.
- [territory](./territory.md) - Enclosing territory with a thick wall is the classical waste of thickness's real value.
