Keima ("knight's move") in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is playing a stone one point away from an existing stone in one direction and two points away in the perpendicular direction, the same offset a knight moves on a chessboard [@senseislibrary]. Like [tobi](./tobi.md), it is not a physical connection; it covers ground faster than a solid connection at the cost of a gap the opponent can, in principle, exploit.

The "knight's move cut," playing directly into the gap keima leaves, is the standard test of the shape: whether it actually works depends on the surrounding position, since the cutting stone itself often lands in trouble the keima's owner can exploit in turn [@kageyama1978lessons]. A keima that cannot be cut without loss is, for practical purposes, as solid as a direct connection while covering more board.

## Related terms

- [tobi](./tobi.md) - Tobi is the other standard light-extension shape, covering a straight-line offset rather than keima's diagonal one.
- [sabaki](./sabaki.md) - Keima's speed over solid connection makes it a characteristic move of sabaki play.
