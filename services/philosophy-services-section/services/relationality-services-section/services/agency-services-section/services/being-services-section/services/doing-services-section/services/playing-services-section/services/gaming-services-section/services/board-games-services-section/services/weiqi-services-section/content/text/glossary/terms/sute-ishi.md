Sute-ishi ("throw-away stones") in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) are one or more stones deliberately played to be [captured](./capture.md), because the exchange the [capture](./capture.md) forces is worth more than the stones would have been worth kept alive [@senseislibrary]. The player plans the loss before the opponent takes it; the stones are bait, not a mistake later rationalized as sacrifice.

A sacrifice earns its keep in one of a few standard ways: it draws the opponent into spending moves capturing low-value stones while the sacrificing player builds [thickness](./thickness.md) or [territory](./territory.md) elsewhere, it keeps [sente](./sente.md) by forcing a specific, predictable response, or it removes a cutting point the opponent would otherwise have exploited [@kageyama1978lessons]. Sute-ishi is a plan for the whole exchange, not a description of any single stone; the same stone that looks sacrificed in isolation may in fact need to survive if the surrounding reading changes.

## Related terms

- [sabaki](./sabaki.md) - Sabaki treats a group's own stones as expendable in the same way sute-ishi treats bait stones as expendable, applied to settling a whole group rather than one exchange.
- [aji](./aji.md) - A sacrificed stone frequently leaves aji behind even after capture, rather than disappearing from the position's latent options entirely.
- [sente](./sente.md) - A well-timed sacrifice is a common way to keep sente: the opponent must answer by capturing rather than tenuki.
