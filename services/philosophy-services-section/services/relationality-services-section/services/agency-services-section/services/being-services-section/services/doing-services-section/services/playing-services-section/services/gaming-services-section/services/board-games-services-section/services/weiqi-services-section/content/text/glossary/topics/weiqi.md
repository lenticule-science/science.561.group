Weiqi is a domain within Board Games.

This page is the start-here index for everything this section holds about weiqi — the sequenced lesson curriculum and the dependency-linked glossary — per the [Weiqi Platform Plan](../../documents/articles/notes/weiqi-platform-plan.md)'s "engine is the executable form of the glossary" thesis.

## Lessons

Read in this order; each lesson names what comes next.

1. [Weiqi Overview](../../documents/articles/teaching-articles/tutorials/weiqi-overview.md) — liberties, capture, territory, and ko, via one worked example.
2. [Living Groups and Eyes](../../documents/articles/teaching-articles/tutorials/living-groups-and-eyes.md) — why connection and eye shape determine whether a group lives or dies.
3. [Komi and Handicap](../../documents/articles/teaching-articles/tutorials/komi-and-handicap.md) — how even games and uneven games are balanced.
4. [Ending the Game](../../documents/articles/teaching-articles/tutorials/ending-the-game.md) — passing, dead-stone settlement, scoring both ways, and why area and territory rarely differ.
5. [History of Weiqi](../../documents/articles/teaching-articles/tutorials/weiqi-history.md) — origins in China, transmission across East Asia, and modern international play. (A parallel entry, not a prerequisite for the other four.)

## Bibliography

Verified sources this section's own research notes and constitutions cite
or depend on, one carrier per source, each carrying a "Why this is
central" section tying it back to the specific corpus documents that use
it.

- [Berlekamp & Wolfe 1994](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/berlekamp-wolfe1994.md) — *Mathematical Go: Chilling Gets the Last Point*, the combinatorial-game-theory endgame treatment [Weiqi Strategic Theory](../../documents/articles/research-articles/2026-08-29-note-on-weiqi-strategic-theory.md) cites for sente/gote/miai.
- [Silver et al. 2016](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/silver2016.md) — the AlphaGo paper, *Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search*.
- [Silver et al. 2017](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/silver2017.md) — the AlphaGo Zero paper, *Mastering the game of Go without human knowledge*.
- [Wu 2019](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/wu2019.md) — the KataGo paper, *Accelerating Self-Play Learning in Go*.
- [Hollosi 1997](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/hollosi1997.md) — the SGF FF[4] specification, this section's position-interchange boundary format.
- [Farnebäck 2002](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/farneback2002.md) — the Go Text Protocol version 2 specification, this section's engine-play boundary format.
- [KataGo Analysis Engine documentation](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/katago-analysis-engine.md) — this section's analysis boundary protocol.
- [Chen et al. 2003](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/chen2003.md) — the founding fMRI study of Go play.
- [Jung et al. 2013](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/jung2013.md) — gray-matter morphometry and resting-state connectivity in Baduk experts.
- [Sohn et al. 2017](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/sohn2017.md) — resting-state network connectivity in professional Baduk players.
- [Kim 2025](../../documents/articles/bibliographies/kim2025.md) — a 2025 review of Go training's cognitive and neural impacts, including executive function.
