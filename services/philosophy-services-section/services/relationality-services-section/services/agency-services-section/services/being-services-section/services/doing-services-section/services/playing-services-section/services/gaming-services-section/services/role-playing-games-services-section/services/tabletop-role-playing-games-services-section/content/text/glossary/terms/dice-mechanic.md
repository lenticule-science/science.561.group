A dice mechanic is a rule system for resolving uncertainty in a [tabletop role-playing game](../../../../README.md) using randomizers — most commonly dice, though some systems use cards, tokens, or other devices. When a [character](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/character.md) attempts something with an uncertain outcome, the dice mechanic determines success or failure.

Common approaches include: rolling a single die against a target number (d20 systems), rolling pools of dice and counting successes (World of Darkness, Shadowrun), comparing rolled dice to a fixed difficulty (Powered by the Apocalypse), and roll-under systems where players try to roll below their attribute score (Call of Cthulhu).

The choice of dice mechanic shapes the feel of a game. A d20 system produces a flat probability distribution where every outcome is equally likely; dice pools produce bell curves that cluster around average results. [Modifiers](./modifier.md) shift these probabilities, and the degree to which they matter depends on the underlying distribution. A +2 bonus on a d20 (10% shift) feels different from a +2 on 2d6 (disproportionately large near the average).
