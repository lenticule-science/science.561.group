Games gathers the analytic vocabulary that applies across every gaming tradition: [game](../terms/game.md) and [gaming](../terms/gaming.md) themselves, the structural terms ([rules](../terms/rules.md), [mechanic](../terms/mechanic.md), [turn](../terms/turn.md), [win condition](../terms/win-condition.md), [game state](../terms/game-state.md)), the participant ([player](../terms/player.md)), and the local variation ([house rule](../terms/house-rule.md)).

The topic sits under playing because a game is structured play — the vocabulary of the unstructured activity (play, lusory attitude) lives one level up in the playing section's glossary, and each tradition's own vocabulary (a hand of cards, a die's pips, a campaign's sessions) lives in the subsection that owns the tradition: board games, card games, dice games, role-playing games, gambling.

A second cluster of terms applies once a game stops resetting: a [persistent world](../terms/persistent-world.md) sustains standing [player types](../terms/player-types.md) population dynamics, a [virtual economy](../terms/virtual-economy.md), and [playbour](../terms/playbour.md) — activity that is play and labor at once. The same non-resetting property produces play with no session boundary at all, whether the player is scheduling returns to a shared world ([asynchronous play](../terms/asynchronous-play.md)) or absent entirely ([idle game](../terms/idle-game.md)), and it produces persistent worlds' standing governance and ownership problems: a designer-operator no player can depose (the [god problem](../terms/god-problem.md)) and objects that outlast any session and so behave like property ([virtual property](../terms/virtual-property.md)).
