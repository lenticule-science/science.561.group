## Why this is central

This is the direct source for the Shin Fuseki (New Fuseki) movement
[Note on Lakota Weiqi Strategy](../notes/2026-09-04-note-on-lakota-weiqi-strategy.md)
§3 names, unsourced at the time, as the nearest known predecessor to the
owner's hypothesis. The article confirms the movement's shape precisely:
led by Kitani Minoru and Go Seigen beginning in 1933, it inverted the
prevailing "corner, side, center" opening orthodoxy — which the article
states had left the center "unjustly under-emphasised" — by playing the
center point directly and emphasizing thickness and moyo (spheres of
influence) over immediate territory. Go Seigen himself later described it
as "a step on the way to the perfecting of go" that stimulated
"creativity of players beyond fixed josekis." The article also states the
movement's own limit: the radical experimentation "lasted only three or
four years" before its usable ideas were selectively absorbed into
mainstream theory rather than replacing it outright, and names Takemiya
Masaki explicitly as a later player who built a personal style on its
foundation.

## What it does not settle

The article gives no move-level statistics (opening line distribution,
sacrifice rate, or any of the other features
[National and Historical Play Styles](../research-articles/national-and-historical-play-styles.md)
§6 tabulates) — it is a historical and doctrinal account, not a
quantitative study of Shin Fuseki games. It also does not itself state
whether Shin Fuseki play favored light, sacrificial, or encirclement
tactics; those coordinates are marked not verified against this source in
this survey's table.
