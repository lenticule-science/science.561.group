Zenkyoku ("whole board") in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is evaluating a move by its effect on the balance of the entire board rather than judging the local exchange in isolation [@senseislibrary]. A move read as locally correct can still be the wrong move once its effect on a distant, seemingly unrelated group is counted; zenkyoku is the discipline of checking that effect before playing [@kageyama1978lessons].

As a proposal of this section's own [Whole-Board Reading as a Gluing Problem, and Lineages as Signatures](../../documents/articles/research-articles/whole-board-reading-as-gluing-lineages-as-signatures.md) (§1, §4), zenkyoku names exactly the reading a purely regional strategy lacks: the article reads a regional strategy's failure to account for a distant effect as a cover-nerve coordination obstruction, and reads whole-board play as a distribution over the readout space that couples regions a local reading would treat as independent. Status: proposal.

## Related terms

- [aji](./aji.md) - Zenkyoku frequently means holding a local option open specifically because of the effect it has elsewhere on the board.
- [miai](./miai.md) - Recognizing miai points requires the same whole-board comparison zenkyoku names, applied to two equivalent points rather than one distant effect.
- [sabaki](./sabaki.md) - Light, quickly-settled shape is what lets a player disengage from one region and act on a whole-board reading elsewhere.
