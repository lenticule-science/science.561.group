Kikashi in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) is a forcing move played for the value of the response it compels rather than for the stone's own local worth, kept as a quick exchange rather than developed further [@senseislibrary]. The player spends one move, the opponent must answer it in a fixed way, and the player keeps [sente](./sente.md) to play elsewhere — the exchange is judged complete the moment it is made.

Kikashi is the opposite outcome from [aji-keshi](./aji-keshi.md): both are forcing moves, but a kikashi is worth more played than left unplayed, while an aji-keshi is worth less played than left unplayed [@kageyama1978lessons]. Distinguishing the two requires reading whether the forced response removes an option the player would rather have kept open; a move that looks like kikashi in one position is aji-keshi in another, depending on what the position still needs from the point being forced.

## Related terms

- [sente](./sente.md) - Kikashi is specifically a forcing move whose value is realized entirely through keeping sente, not through the stone's local presence.
- [aji-keshi](./aji-keshi.md) - Aji-keshi is the failure mode kikashi is judged against: the same forcing shape, spent when it should have stayed unplayed.
- [sute-ishi](./sute-ishi.md) - A kikashi stone is frequently also a sacrifice stone, left to be captured once its forcing work is done.
