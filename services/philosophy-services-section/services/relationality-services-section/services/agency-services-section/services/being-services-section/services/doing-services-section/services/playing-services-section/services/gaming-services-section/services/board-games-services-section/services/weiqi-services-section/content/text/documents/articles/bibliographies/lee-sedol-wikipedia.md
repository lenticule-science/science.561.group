## Why this is central

Lee Sedol is the section's verified source for the named Korean
fighting-style figure this survey's task scope calls for, and for the
2016 AlphaGo match this section's AI-era account already leans on
(see [silver2016](silver2016.md)). The article records him turning
professional at twelve in 1995 (Korea Baduk Association, under Kweon
Kab-yong), his nickname "The Strong Stone," and his own self-assessment
that his "opening play" was "very weak" — compensated, by his own and
others' account, with tactical brilliance in the middle game. It names
his famous 2003 "broken ladder" formation as a defiance of conventional
wisdom that won, and his sole win against AlphaGo (Game 4, 2016) as
featuring a move widely called "a divine move."

## What it does not settle

The article's style characterization is anecdotal (a famous
game-winning tactic, a self-reported opening weakness, a widely-praised
single move) rather than a systematic account of his opening-line
distribution, sacrifice rate, or forcing ratio in
[National and Historical Play Styles](../research-articles/national-and-historical-play-styles.md)
§6's sense. His own "very weak" opening self-assessment is a subjective
claim, not verified against any external opening-strength measurement.
