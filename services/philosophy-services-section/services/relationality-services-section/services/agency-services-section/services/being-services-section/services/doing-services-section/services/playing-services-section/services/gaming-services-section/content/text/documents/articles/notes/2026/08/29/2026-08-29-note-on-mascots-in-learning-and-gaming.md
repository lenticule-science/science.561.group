# Mascots in Learning and Gaming

## 0. What this note is, and its evidence grade

Commissioned 2026-08-29, on the occasion of weiqi.561.group getting a mascot: **Keya**, a turtle
(Lakota *kéya* = turtle) with a go board for a shell. The owner wanted the mascot decision grounded
in real study rather than vibes. This note is that study — a survey of mascot design across
ed-tech and games, the design principles that survive contact with evidence, and a short,
deliberately incomplete closing section applying those principles to the Keya occasion. **It does
not design the character.** That is the owner's call.

Provenance, honestly stated: this is a search-synthesis note. Most claims below were gathered via
`WebSearch` result summaries and a small number of direct `WebFetch` reads (marked inline where a
source was fetched and quoted rather than summarized from a search snippet). Marketing-blog and
SEO-content sources (many mascot "case study" sites exist purely to sell mascot-design services)
are used only for facts that are independently corroborated or clearly labeled as their own claim;
numbers from that tier are flagged as unverified against Duolingo's own primary publications, since
several of the most-repeated engagement statistics ("+40% engagement," "7.2% D14 lift," "baby
schema effect") appear only on marketing/content sites with no visible primary citation and could
not be traced to a Duolingo research-lab or SEC-filing source in this pass. A future session with
more `WebFetch` budget should verify these against [Duolingo's own research blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/) and its
investor-facing metrics before treating them as settled.

## 1. The canonical case: Duo (Duolingo)

### 1.1 Design history

Duo began as a flat, simple green owl and evolved over more than a decade into a rounder, more
dimensional, more expressive character. Duolingo's own 2013 platform-redesign post states the
constraint the design team held fixed even while everything else changed: "Duo's color and his
species (a very rare green owl) were not going to change" — the redesign prioritized silhouette
first ("if the overall shape is not appealing, then the details within that shape can't compliment
it successfully"), then added animation and a slight quarter-turn once the silhouette read well
([Duolingo blog, "Reshaping Duo"](https://blog.duolingo.com/reshaping-duo/)). The lesson generalizes: mascot redesigns that survive
are the ones that hold identity constant (species, color, a couple of instantly-recognizable
shapes) while iterating everything else.

The species choice itself was not neutral: owls are cross-culturally coded for wisdom and learning
([multiple secondary sources](https://duolingoguides.com/why-is-duolingo-a-bird/), consistent with the general finding below that mascot species carries
pre-loaded symbolic freight before any character work begins — the same logic that makes a turtle
carrying a "cosmic diagram" shell not a blank slate either).

### 1.2 Streak mechanics and the passive-aggressive owl

Duo is not decorative. Duo is load-bearing infrastructure for the retention mechanic: streak
notifications are authored as coming from Duo personally, so a missed-day nudge reads as social
pressure from a character who "cares," not an algorithmic ping — [Choicehacking's analysis](https://www.choicehacking.com/2023/05/25/how-duolingo-used-psychology-to-make-learning-addictive/) frames
this directly as manufacturing a parasocial relationship that intensifies the felt cost of breaking
a streak. This is the mechanism behind the "passive-aggressive owl" meme: the character's persona
(guilt-tripping, thirsty, unhinged) is not a branding accident, it is the streak mechanic wearing a
face. The meme itself became free distribution — users posting about Duo's threats is marketing
Duolingo did not have to buy.

**Caveat on magnitude claims.** Specific lift numbers circulating for "Duo's effect on retention"
(a commonly repeated "+7.2% D14" figure, a "60% commitment increase" figure, a "12%→55% retention"
figure) all trace to marketing/content-site secondary sources in this search pass, not to a primary
Duolingo publication located and read directly. Treat the *mechanism* (mascot-as-streak-enforcer
manufactures parasocial accountability) as well-evidenced by the design logic and the meme's own
existence; treat the *specific percentages* as unverified.

### 1.3 The 2025 "Duo died" campaign

On 2025-02-11 Duolingo staged Duo's death (struck by a Cybertruck, driver kept a mystery), running
it across TikTok, X, and other channels. What began as a routine mascot-refresh post exploded once
the death notice generated an unexpectedly large comment/engagement wave, and marketing leaned into
it live rather than sticking to a pre-planned three-post schedule ([PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/duolingo-shares-pr-secrets-of-viral-death-of-duo-campaign/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91313082/duolingo-dead-duo-owl-social-media-campaign)). Reported
results: 144 million views on one X post, 558,000 likes / 20,000 comments on the TikTok eulogy,
1.7 billion cross-platform impressions over two weeks, a 25% Android user increase, 38% download
increase, 58% increase in web searches, and — notably — Duolingo's single highest iOS download day
of the year landing on the day Duo "died" ([PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/duolingo-shares-pr-secrets-of-viral-death-of-duo-campaign/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91313082/duolingo-dead-duo-owl-social-media-campaign)). These
figures come from PR-industry and business-press coverage of Duolingo's own disclosed campaign
results, a stronger evidence tier than the retention percentages above but still not a primary
Duolingo filing read directly in this pass.

**Why it worked, structurally.** A mascot with enough accumulated persona equity (years of the
passive-aggressive-owl meme) can be "killed" as a stunt precisely because the audience already has
a real parasocial relationship to protect — you cannot stage a beloved death for a mascot nobody
has feelings about yet. This is evidence *for* the value of memetic looseness accumulating over
time (see Design Principles §7.5), not evidence that a stunt like this is available to a new
mascot on day one.

### 1.4 3D redesign and ongoing evolution

Coverage of a 2025-era 3D/visual refresh is thinner and more concept-driven (several results were
unofficial redesign-concept projects on Behance/Pinterest rather than confirmed Duolingo
announcements); Duolingo has also run short-lived cosmetic variants (an illness-themed "sick Duo"
icon tied to a marketing moment, per [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/logos-icons/is-duolingo-okay)) distinct from any permanent redesign. The
pattern that is well-evidenced across both the 2013 redesign and later cosmetic variants: Duo's
core identity markers (species, color, silhouette) stay fixed while surface treatment flexes for
specific campaigns — the mascot has a stable "home" state and licensed excursions from it.

## 2. The cautionary case: Clippy

Clippy (Microsoft's Office Assistant) is the standard cited counter-example, and the sourced
material sharpens exactly why. Byron Reeves, one of the Stanford researchers involved in Clippy's
original design, is quoted identifying the core flaw directly: "the worst thing about Clippy was
that he interrupted" ([Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/504767/tragic-life-clippy-worlds-most-hated-virtual-assistant)). A 2003 study by researcher Luke Swartz formalized this: Clippy broke basic social
norms applied to a colleague — it interrupted without being asked, offered help after it was no
longer needed, and was "overly present, almost predatory," which read to users as disrespect for
their competence rather than assistance ([Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/504767/tragic-life-clippy-worlds-most-hated-virtual-assistant)). A second, independent failure mode: Clippy
could not learn individual user preferences or adapt to skill level, so its interruptions were
triggered by shallow pattern-matching rather than actual user intent, and even usability testing
before ship surfaced complaints about its "leering" eyes as unsettling ([Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/504767/tragic-life-clippy-worlds-most-hated-virtual-assistant)). It was
disabled by default in 2002, demoted in 2003, removed in 2007.

The precise contrast with Duo is instructive: both are anthropomorphized software agents that
intervene in the user's flow. Duo's interventions (streak notifications) are *requested* in the
sense that the user opted into the mechanic by starting a streak, are *predictable* (same trigger,
same channel, same persona every time), and are *outside* the work surface (a notification, not a
cursor-following presence watching the document). Clippy's interventions were *unrequested*,
*unpredictable* in trigger logic, and *inside* the work surface, visually present while the user
tried to concentrate. This maps directly onto the academic distinction below (§5.2): a companion
role versus an interrupting/intrusive role.

## 3. Other product and platform mascots — what work they do

- **GitHub's Octocat.** Started as a stock image, given a name and one governing rule: it never
  speaks, communicating only through action/pose ([1000logos](https://1000logos.net/github-logo/); [GitHub Brand Toolkit](https://brand.github.com/graphic-elements/mascots)). GitHub built the
  "Octodex," a gallery of 160+ community-remixed Octocat variants — the mascot's openness to
  remix, not its authored personality, is the mechanism: it converts brand identity into a
  participatory in-joke rather than a fixed character arc. Function: **brand/community identity**,
  not in-product pedagogy.
- **Discord's Wumpus.** Appears specifically at friction points — empty servers, errors,
  connection failures — reframing a moment of frustration as something charming rather than a bare
  error state; reported to generate substantial fan art ([search summary, unverified primary source](https://ziggle.art/best-brand-mascots)). Function:
  **error-state emotional buffering** — mascot as a softening layer over an otherwise negative UX
  moment.
- **Reddit's Snoo.** A simple orange-red alien (2005) whose defining mechanic is that every
  subreddit can reskin it. Ownership over the mascot's *appearance*, distributed to millions of
  communities, is credited with turning a generic character into something users feel is "theirs"
  ([search summary](https://ziggle.art/best-brand-mascots); primary confirmation not independently re-verified in this pass). Function: **identity
  substrate for sub-communities**, not a single fixed personality.
- **Khan Academy Kids.** Kodi Bear and a small cast of animal characters host activities and
  celebrate progress for early learners; Khan Academy's broader avatar system (32+ user-selectable
  avatars across 8 characters) lets *learners* pick a persona rather than only receiving one
  ([Khan Academy Help Center](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/360077758032-Update-32-new-avatars-available); [svgapp.ai](https://svgapp.ai/app-mascots/khan-academy-kids/)). Function: **stable social world across
  changing topic/pace** — the character cast is explicitly reasoned about as making constant
  subject-switching feel safe for young learners, a distinct pedagogical claim from Duo's
  streak-enforcement role.
- **Brilliant and Grammarly.** No confirmed, well-documented mascot character was located for
  either in this search pass (Grammarly uses a wordmark/icon-driven identity more than a named
  character in the sources surfaced; Brilliant likewise). Treat as an open gap rather than a
  negative finding — a targeted follow-up search focused on each brand individually, ideally with
  primary-site fetches, would be needed to confirm absence versus this pass simply missing it.
- **BadukPop (a Go-learning app).** Mascot is "Aji," described as a golden Go stone with wings
  ([search summary](https://badukpop.com/), not independently re-verified by direct fetch) — the nearest existing precedent in the
  Go-app space to what Keya would be. No turtle-mascot precedent was found among Go/weiqi/baduk
  apps in this pass (see §6.4 for the "Keya" name-collision check specifically).
- **Tux (Linux) / community-mascot pattern generally.** Not independently re-searched in this pass
  beyond background knowledge; grouped here only to note the broader pattern that
  open/community-governed projects tend toward mascots with *low* authored personality and *high*
  remixability (Tux, Octocat, Snoo), while consumer retention products tend toward mascots with
  *high* authored personality and persona consistency (Duo). This is a structural observation
  drawn from the pattern across the sources above, not itself independently sourced — flag as
  synthesis, not citation.

## 4. Gaming and idol-adjacent precedents

### 4.1 Hatsune Miku as a distributed idol

Miku is a Vocaloid voicebank and its official character design — Crypton Future Media's marketing
deliberately treats her as an open, crowdsourced canvas rather than a tightly authored character:
Crypton built Piapro.jp specifically so fans could upload derivative songs, art, and performances,
and the strategy is characterized as a deliberate balance between retaining copyright and enabling
fan production, aimed at a creator base "alienated from the production path of mainstream
commercial media" ([search summary](https://the-artifice.com/hatsune-miku-virtual-idol-inspired-creativity/); [Media Commons](https://mediacommons.org/imr/2011/04/25/global-culture-hatsune-miku)). The "distributed idol" framing
is apt: Miku's emotional authenticity to fans comes from the character being a shared, participatory
project, structurally close to Snoo (§3) rather than to Duo — ownership-through-remix rather than
ownership-through-consistent-persona.

### 4.2 VTubers as mascot-idol hybrids

Recent scholarship (2025) frames VTubers as "posthuman mediators of digital intimacies" built on
"affective liquidity, synthetic bonds" ([Hu & Ge, 2025, *SAGE*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448251403091)). A separate strand identifies a hybrid model beyond
classical parasocial theory: "parakin" reciprocity, where fans and the character/performer have a
financialized-but-felt-reciprocal relationship (superchats, subscriptions, merch) that mimics
kinship rather than the strictly one-directional parasocial relationship of, e.g., a TV celebrity
([research summary of VTuber fandom literature](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376663686_Virtual_presence_real_connections_Exploring_the_role_of_parasocial_relationships_in_virtual_idol_fan_community_participation)). Relevant caution: the same literature documents "public parasocial
grieving" when a VTuber "graduates" or is terminated — the intimacy a well-built mascot/character
generates is real enough to produce real grief when withdrawn ([arXiv 2504.13421](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13421)). A mascot that
becomes genuinely load-bearing for users' sense of companionship carries a corresponding
retirement/sunset responsibility.

### 4.3 Sports mascots and parasocial function

Search results here skewed toward athlete-fan parasocial research rather than mascot-specific
literature (a real gap in what this pass surfaced, not necessarily a gap in the literature itself).
One directly on-point 2025 finding: mascot *attractiveness* measurably affects attitudes toward the
sporting event it represents ([Scientific Reports, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-09406-z)) — i.e., the character-design-quality question is not
merely aesthetic, it has been shown to move an outcome variable (event attitude) in a controlled
study. Treat the broader "sports mascots create parasocial bonds analogous to athlete-fan bonds" as
plausible-by-extension from the athlete literature rather than independently confirmed for mascots
specifically in this pass.

### 4.4 Gacha/idol-game progression as a "fans/renown as score" mechanic

Idol-management and gacha games commonly instrument a "fans" resource directly: search results
describe mechanics where in-game fan count is a load-bearing stat — insufficient fans causes a
live performance to fail to reach a high score or for a character's special skill to not activate
([search summary](https://www.ldplayer.net/blog/idoly-pride-game-review-gacha-rates-rhythm-game-idol-manager.html)). This is the idol genre's structural move: convert the parasocial/audience relationship into an
explicit, numeric, spendable game resource, layered on top of standard gacha collection/leveling
mechanics (rarity tiers, leveling, "awakening," skill upgrades — [Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/gacha-mechanics-for-mobile-games-explained/); [Game Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/critical/study-gacha-stars)). Relevant to a
learn-track mascot: the genre already has a proven pattern for turning "does the player care about
this character" into a mechanic, which is a different design lever from Duo's streak-guilt lever
and worth distinguishing rather than collapsing together.

### 4.5 Hikaru no Go as the precedent for fiction-driven Go revival

*Hikaru no Go* (manga 1998–2003, anime 2001–2003) is repeatedly credited with a real, documented
youth Go boom: "a big increase in the popularity of Go in Japan, particularly among the young, due
to the successful cartoon called Hikaru no Go," crediting it with shifting Go's image from "a game
primarily for older individuals to one that appealed to a younger demographic," with effects
reported spreading to China, South Korea, and Taiwan, and cited by Go players generally as having
sparked renewed worldwide interest ([Wikipedia summary of secondary sourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikaru_no_Go); [British Go Association history](https://www.britgo.org/intro/history)). This is the
strongest available precedent that character-driven fiction — not a mascot exactly, but a cast of
characters embedded in a specific game — can move real enrollment/participation numbers for a
board game, not just brand sentiment. Caveat: none of the sources surfaced in this pass gave a
primary hard number (e.g., "X% increase in registered Nihon Ki-in youth players") — the claim is
consistently made in qualitative terms ("big increase," "dramatically increased") across secondary
sources, not sourced to a specific federation's enrollment statistics in what was fetched here. A
follow-up session should try to locate Nihon Ki-in, Korea Baduk Association, or Chinese Weiqi
Association youth-membership time series directly to convert this from "widely repeated claim" to
"cited number."

## 5. Scholarship: pedagogical agents, the persona effect, anthropomorphism

### 5.1 The persona effect (Lester et al.)

The foundational result: Lester et al. (1997) demonstrated what the field now calls the "persona
effect" — the mere presence of an animated, lifelike pedagogical agent, even a non-expressive one,
produced a strong positive effect on students' *perception* of their learning experience
([Semantic Scholar summary of Lester & Converse, "The Persona Effect"](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-persona-effect:-affective-impact-of-animated-Lester-Converse/4bf061f85d1495448f83b5e3bb6ec7e30bfe818d); [ACM DL, full text](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/258549.258797)). This is the
founding citation for "a mascot in a learning product measurably changes subjective experience,"
independent of whether it changes objective learning outcomes.

A follow-on study (Baylor & Ryu, cited via [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2190/V0WQ-NWGN-JB54-FAT4)) found a genuinely double-edged result worth carrying
into design: participants *learned significantly more* when the pedagogical agent was animated,
*but also reported* the agent as significantly *less* facilitative of learning when animated — i.e.
subjective perception and objective outcome can point in opposite directions for the same design
choice. A mascot's felt helpfulness and its actual pedagogical contribution are not the same
variable and should not be assumed to move together.

### 5.2 Pedagogical agents as learning companions vs. interrupting tutors

A distinct research line (Kim & Baylor and related work, per [Springer, "Pedagogical Agents as Learning Companions"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-006-8805-z); [ScienceDirect topic overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/pedagogical-agent)) draws
exactly the tutor-vs-companion distinction this note needs: a **tutor agent** plays a teacher role;
a **co-learner/companion agent** plays a peer role that appears to care about the learner's
progress, signaling "we're in this together" and increasing the learner's own caring about
progress. Interaction-style research within this line further distinguishes *proactive* agents
(which interject) from *responsive* agents (which react to learner-initiated triggers) — this is
the closest formal academic version of the Duo-vs-Clippy contrast in §1–2: a companion role, paired
with responsive (not proactive/interrupting) triggering, is the empirically favored combination;
Clippy was proactive and role-ambiguous (tutor-ish authority claims without companion warmth),
which is very close to the worst cell in this design space.

### 5.3 Anthropomorphism and motivation

General consumer-psychology findings: anthropomorphized mascots measurably raise purchase/adoption
intention, and the effect is mediated by the amygdala processing mascot faces similarly to human
faces ([summary citing multiple studies](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355543055_THE_EFFECT_OF_ANTHROPOMORPHIC_MASCOT_ON_THE_PURCHASING_INTENTION_OF_CONSUMERS_AN_EXPERIMENTAL_STUDY)). A specific, well-evidenced psychological driver: people denied ordinary
social relations tend to anthropomorphize non-human things *more*, i.e. anthropomorphism serves a
real, substitutable sociality-motivation function, not merely a decorative one ("sociality
motivation... if people are deprived of social relations, they tend to anthropomorphize even
non-human beings such as robots" — [research summary](https://research.mental-momentum.ai/r/impact-anthropomorphism-brand-mascots-z4wxy3)). Mascots designed with a *dual* personality signal
(both sincerity and competence) are reported to trigger stronger emotional attachment than
single-trait designs ([same source](https://research.mental-momentum.ai/r/impact-anthropomorphism-brand-mascots-z4wxy3)) — relevant to a mascot meant to be both a warm companion and a
credible carrier of go instruction.

## 6. Turtle symbolism: two independent traditions, one shape

### 6.1 Lakota *kéya*

Fetched directly from the Akta Lakota Museum & Cultural Center (a Lakota-affiliated cultural
institution, operated by St. Joseph's Indian School): the turtle, *kéya*, "represents Mother Earth"
and is "the guardian of life, longevity, and fortitude." Lakota women are traditionally associated
with the turtle specifically because of their life-giving role; traditional dance dress beadwork
includes turtle-by-the-shore-of-the-lake designs. The turtle's teaching is explicitly about pace:
"patience combined with purposeful movement... lasting progress often comes from moving slowly with
purpose" ([Akta Lakota Museum](https://aktalakota.stjo.org/lakota_spirit_animal/turtle-keya/)). A second source (Native Hope, a Lakota-founded nonprofit) adds a concrete
material practice: Lakota mothers traditionally make a leather turtle-shaped amulet (*keya cekpa
aknake*) for a newborn, sewing the child's umbilical cord inside it "for protection," keeping the
child "grounded and connected to its mother and *unci maka*" (grandmother earth) ([Native Hope blog](https://blog.nativehope.org/native-american-animals-turtle-k%C3%A9ya)). Neither
sourced page states an explicit caution against non-Lakota commercial use of the symbol or word —
see §6.5 for why that absence should not be read as clearance.

### 6.2 Turtle Island cosmology — adjacent but distinct

The specific "world on the turtle's back" creation narrative (Sky Woman falling to a turtle-borne
landmass) is most precisely a **Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy) and Anishinaabe (Ojibwe)**
tradition, not originally a Lakota one — though "Turtle Island" is now used pan-tribally by many
North American Indigenous nations to refer to the continent ([search synthesis](https://www.supersummary.com/the-world-on-turtles-back/summary/); [Atlas Obscura, world-turtle mytheme survey](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/world-turtle-cosmic-discworld)). This is a
precision worth keeping straight in any public-facing copy: kéya (the Lakota word and the
turtle-as-guardian/Mother Earth symbolism) is well-sourced to Lakota tradition specifically; the
"world sits on a turtle's back" cosmology proper is a different (related, overlapping, but not
identical) nation's story, and conflating them in marketing copy would be a real, checkable
inaccuracy, not just a sensitivity risk.

### 6.3 The Chinese Luoshu — a cosmic diagram literally on a turtle's shell

Independently, the Luoshu (Lo Shu) legend: as Yu the Great stood on the banks of the Luo River
(traditionally dated ~4,000 years ago), a turtle emerged bearing a 3×3 grid of dot-numbers on its
shell, later formalized as the Luoshu magic square — every row, column, and diagonal summing to 15,
regarded as the earliest known magic square and read at the time as "a supernatural sign of order
in the universe" ([MAA review of *Legacy of the Luoshu*](https://old.maa.org/press/maa-reviews/legacy-of-the-luoshu-the-mystical-mathematical-meaning-of-the-magic-square-of-order-three); [Wikipedia, Luoshu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoshu_Square)). Separately but in the same broad
cultural register, Shang-dynasty (~1600–1046 BCE) diviners inscribed questions onto turtle
plastrons (the underside of the shell) and ox scapulae, applied heat until the material cracked, and
read the crack pattern as an answer — "plastromancy" — leaving over 100,000 recovered inscribed
bones and the earliest known Chinese writing system ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Oracle_Bones/); [Smithsonian object record](https://si.edu/object/3d/inscribed-turtle-plastron%3A58be9e1e-9966-4f22-8eb8-b62a0205c697)).

The convergence worth naming plainly for the Keya occasion: **both traditions relevant to this
specific platform independently put a diagrammatic, cosmic, order-bearing pattern on a turtle's
shell** — the Luoshu grid in the Chinese tradition (the game's own cultural origin, weiqi being a
Chinese game), and, in a different register, the Lakota turtle as the carrier of grounded, worldly
order (Mother Earth on/as the turtle). A go-board-shell turtle is not importing an arbitrary
metaphor into either tradition; it is compatible with an existing "turtle carries the pattern" shape
in both, though it is also, honestly, a designed synthesis, not something either tradition itself
produced — the Luoshu shell carries numbers, not a 19×19 grid, and no source found here shows a
Lakota turtle-shell/board association specifically.

### 6.4 Spelling and existing-product collision check

Spelling: Lakota sources consistently use the diacritic form *kéya* in cultural/educational
material ([Akta Lakota Museum](https://aktalakota.stjo.org/lakota_spirit_animal/turtle-keya/); [Native Hope](https://blog.nativehope.org/native-american-animals-turtle-k%C3%A9ya)) and the plain form *Keya* in product/brand contexts. Search results
surfaced: **Keya Group**, a large Bangladeshi cosmetics conglomerate (no turtle mascot, no
apparent connection to Lakota culture — a likely-coincidental homophone from a different language
entirely); **Keya Lifestyle and Bedding**, an Indigenous-owned blanket/gift brand explicitly
described as selling "across Turtle Island" (closest existing brand-level use of the name in an
Indigenous-affiliated commercial context, though not turtle-mascot-branded specifically); and
several small independent-artist products literally titled "Keya" depicting turtles (a Coastal Art
Studio painting, a Wayanka Arts print, a Dakota Indian Foundation turtle-amulet product) — small-
scale, Indigenous-craft-adjacent, not platform/software products. **No existing Go/weiqi/baduk app,
learning platform, or software product named Keya, and no existing turtle-mascot Go app, was found
in this pass** (the nearest Go-app mascot precedent located, BadukPop's "Aji," is a winged Go stone,
not a turtle). This is a reasonably clean trademark/collision picture for software specifically,
though it is a search-based check, not a formal trademark search, and should not substitute for one
before any public launch.

### 6.5 Cultural-sensitivity considerations — reported, not resolved

Per this task's instructions, this section reports findings rather than resolving the question.
General guidance located on Indigenous-language use in commercial branding: legal/trademark
commentary consistently recommends verifying meaning and connotation directly with speakers from
the source culture before commercial use, and notes that voluntary disclosure of a name's cultural
origin, while not legally required, "can signal respect and cultural awareness" and may "enhance
both authenticity and consumer trust" in a climate of "increasing scrutiny over cultural
appropriation" ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=90abd863-de24-4526-93cc-1e665998c469); [Harris Sliwoski](https://harris-sliwoski.com/blog/united-states-trademarks-foreign-equivalents-and-indigenous-languages/)). A cited real-world precedent for how badly this can go when a
company does *not* do that verification: Air New Zealand's attempt to trademark "Kia Ora" (a Māori
greeting) for an in-flight magazine drew public accusations of disrespect toward the Māori
language and the airline withdrew the application ([Mewburn Ellis](https://mewburn.com/news-insights/kimono-controversy-can-you-trade-mark-culturally-sensitive-names)). Separately, broader
Indigenous-data-sovereignty scholarship documents active corporate interest in extracting
Indigenous-language material for commercial products and treats community consent and benefit-
sharing as the live governance question in that space ([Cambridge Core, Indigenous data sovereignty in ICH governance](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-cultural-property/article/indigenous-data-sovereignty-in-intangible-cultural-heritage-governance-a-complementary-approach-to-publicprivate-partnerships/5F9E115795FA06E77A05C8066D5A5D6B)).
Neither of the two Lakota-affiliated sources fetched directly for this note (§6.1) states an
explicit objection to non-Lakota use of *kéya* — but their silence on the question is not the same
as consent, and a platform whose own stated design philosophy is grounded in a relationality
program drawing on Lakota epistemology (per the task brief) is exactly the situation where "we
found no objection in two web pages" is the wrong bar. The owner is the one positioned to pursue
actual Lakota community input if the name proceeds; this note surfaces the consideration and stops
there.

## 7. Design principles extracted from the evidence

Each principle below is traced to the section that supports it.

1. **Companion, not interruptor** (§2, §5.2). The single sharpest, most convergent finding across
   both the Clippy failure case and the formal pedagogical-agent literature: a mascot that reacts
   to learner-initiated triggers, in a peer/companion role, outperforms one that proactively
   interjects in a tutor/authority role. Trigger *predictability and locus* (outside the work
   surface, e.g. a notification rather than an on-canvas presence) matters as much as trigger
   *frequency*.

2. **Mascot as mechanic-carrier, not decoration** (§1.2, §4.4). Duo's persona *is* the streak
   mechanic's social-pressure delivery system, not a coat of paint on top of it; idol/gacha games
   go further and make the audience relationship an explicit numeric resource. A mascot earns its
   keep by being wired into a real system (progress, reminders, unlocks, a "fans"-equivalent
   metric), not by sitting beside one.

3. **Memetic looseness as a feature, once identity is fixed** (§1.1, §1.3, §3's Octocat/Snoo
   pattern). Products that let a mascot be remixed, meme'd, or even narratively "killed" get real
   organic distribution from it — but this works *because* a small set of identity invariants
   (species, color, silhouette, in Duo's case) stayed fixed long enough to accumulate persona
   equity first. Looseness is a late-stage asset, not a day-one starting posture.

4. **Species and cultural symbolism are not a blank slate** (§1.1, §6.3). A mascot's species
   arrives pre-loaded with cultural connotation (owls = wisdom, before any character design work
   begins). This cuts both ways for Keya: a turtle arrives already carrying "cosmic diagram on
   shell" resonance in two traditions relevant to this specific platform (§6.3) — a genuine design
   asset — and also arrives carrying a real, specific, sourced meaning in Lakota culture (§6.1)
   that is not the designer's to redefine freely.

5. **Perceived helpfulness and actual pedagogical value can diverge** (§5.1). Baylor & Ryu's
   finding that an animated agent produced *more learning* but *lower reported facilitation* is a
   warning against optimizing a mascot purely for how warm or delightful it feels in testing;
   measure actual learning-track outcomes separately from affective/satisfaction ratings.

6. **Anthropomorphism is a real substitute for social connection, which is a responsibility as well
   as an opportunity** (§4.2, §5.3). People under-served on ordinary social relations
   anthropomorphize more readily; a mascot that becomes genuinely load-bearing for a user's sense
   of companionship (as VTubers demonstrably become) creates a retirement/discontinuation
   responsibility if the mascot or product is ever sunset.

7. **Dual-trait personas (warmth + competence) generate stronger attachment than single-trait ones**
   (§5.3). Relevant directly to a mascot meant to be simultaneously a comforting companion and a
   credible carrier of real go instruction — the literature suggests this combination is not in
   tension, it is the higher-attachment configuration.

8. **Community-remixable mascots and authored-personality mascots are different design families,
   not points on one spectrum** (§3's synthesis). Octocat/Snoo/Tux-style low-authorship,
   high-remix mascots serve brand/community identity; Duo-style high-authorship, persona-consistent
   mascots serve in-product pedagogy and retention mechanics. A single mascot can migrate from one
   family toward the other over time (Duo did, once meme culture took over parts of its voice), but
   starting with a clear sense of which family the mascot is being designed for avoids
   contradictory design decisions early.

9. **Fiction can move real participation numbers for a specific game, not just brand sentiment**
   (§4.5). Hikaru no Go is the strongest available existence proof that character-driven narrative
   about a specific board game can produce a documented enrollment effect for that literal game
   among youth, across multiple countries. This is a different, larger claim than "a mascot makes
   an app feel friendlier," and it argues for treating Keya's narrative surface (if any is built)
   as a potential enrollment lever in its own right, not only a UI/companion feature.

## 8. Application: Keya

This section records the occasion and flags open questions. It is deliberately not a character
design — that decision belongs to the owner.

**The concept as given.** Keya, a turtle, carrying a go board as its shell. The name is the Lakota
word for turtle (*kéya*), chosen in a platform whose broader relationality program is already
grounded in Lakota epistemology (per the task brief), applied to weiqi, a Chinese game.

**What the evidence in this note supports about the concept:**

- The turtle-shell-as-cosmic-diagram resonance is real and doubly sourced (§6.3) — Keya's board-
  shell is compatible with, not a violation of, the symbolic shape both the Chinese Luoshu
  tradition and (in a different register) Lakota kéya symbolism already carry. This is a genuine
  design asset, not merely a pun.
- No software/product-naming collision was found (§6.4); the trademark picture looks clean for a
  first pass, pending an actual trademark search.
- The design-principle literature (§7.1, §7.2) favors building Keya as a **companion role** wired
  to real learn-track mechanics (§7.2) — e.g., progress markers, a returning-player nudge, a
  reviewable "chronicle" of a learner's games — rather than as a proactive, in-canvas interruptor
  of actual gameplay, which is where Clippy-shaped failure lives.
- If a narrative/character layer is ever built around Keya (backstory, recurring appearances,
  story content teaching go concepts), the Hikaru no Go precedent (§4.5, §7.9) suggests this is
  worth taking seriously as a genuine growth lever, not just flavor — though converting that from
  "worth taking seriously" to "worth building" is a real scoping decision, not one this note makes.

**Open questions this note flags and does not resolve:**

1. **The relationality-precision question (§6.2).** "World on a turtle's back" cosmology proper is
   most precisely Haudenosaunee/Anishinaabe, not Lakota; if any public copy leans on Turtle Island
   cosmology specifically (rather than the more narrowly-sourced Lakota kéya/Mother-Earth framing),
   that attribution should be gotten right rather than blurred.
2. **The consent/consultation question (§6.5).** No sourced objection to non-Lakota commercial use
   of *kéya* was found, but absence of a found objection in two web pages is a weak bar for a
   platform whose stated philosophy draws on Lakota epistemology specifically. Whether and how to
   seek actual Lakota community input is an owner-level decision this note surfaces but does not
   make.
3. **Which mascot family Keya is being built for (§7.8).** A high-remix, community-owned mascot
   (Octocat/Snoo family) and a high-authorship, persona-consistent companion (Duo family) are
   different design projects with different tradeoffs; the concept as given (a specific named
   character with a specific symbolic design) reads closer to the Duo family, but this has not
   been explicitly decided.
4. **Whether Keya is meant to carry a mechanic or remain a presentational layer (§7.2).** The
   evidence favors wiring a learning-companion mascot into something real (progress tracking,
   spaced-review nudges, a return-after-absence greeting) rather than leaving it purely
   decorative — but which mechanic, if any, is unscoped here.
5. **Retirement responsibility (§7.6).** If Keya becomes genuinely load-bearing for how users
   experience the platform, the VTuber-fandom literature's finding on parasocial grief at character
   retirement (§4.2) argues for thinking now, lightly, about what a graceful sunset or evolution
   path would look like — not urgent, but cheap to note while the character doesn't exist yet.
