Hoshi in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) are the nine marked star points on a 19x19 board, used as fixed handicap-stone locations and as visual landmarks for opening play, with the center star point known as tengen [@wikipedia2026handicap]. The nine points sit at the 4-4 point in each corner, the 4-10 point on the midpoint of each edge, and the 10-10 point at the exact center of the board [@wikipedia2026handicap].

Hoshi matter for two distinct reasons. First, they are the standard locations for handicap stones: the fixed Japanese placement pattern seats every handicap stone, from two up to nine, on one of these nine points, and the tengen point is the fifth and seventh stone added as handicaps increase (see [Handicap Placement and Komi Values](../../documents/articles/notes/handicap-placement-and-komi-values.md) for the full placement order). Second, even outside handicap games, the corner hoshi mark a natural opening frame: a stone on the 4-4 point does not by itself secure the corner the way a 3-3 point does, trading that immediate territory for speed and center-facing influence, which is why it is one of the most common corner openings for beginners to learn even though the 3-4 point (komoku) is usually described as the more evenly balanced choice between territory and influence [@nagofed2026thisisgo; @gomagic2026komoku].

Tengen, the center hoshi, is treated differently from the other eight points. Because it touches no edge or corner, a stone there does not immediately stake out enclosed territory the way a corner stone does, so professional players rarely open there in even games; it appears far more often as the fifth or seventh stone of a handicap setup, where it works with the surrounding corner and side stones already on the board rather than alone.
