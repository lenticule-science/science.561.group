Dame in [weiqi](../topics/weiqi.md) are the neutral, empty points left on the board that border stones of both colors and belong to neither player's [territory](./territory.md) [@britgoagaexplain2026; @polgote2026rules].

Whether it is worth spending a move to fill a dame depends entirely on which scoring system is being used, which is why the term matters more than its small size suggests. Under **area scoring** (the family that includes Chinese rules), the final count adds up living stones on the board plus surrounded territory. A stone played on a dame is a stone on the board, so it is worth exactly one point, the same as a stone played anywhere else legal. Leaving dame unfilled when the opponent can still fill them is therefore a real, countable loss [@britgoagaexplain2026].

Under **territory scoring** (the family that includes Japanese rules), the final count adds up vacant points enclosed as territory plus captured prisoners; stones on the board are not counted directly, and playing an extra stone inside one's own territory actually costs a point by removing a vacant point from the count. A dame is nobody's territory, so playing on one neither gains nor costs a point under this system — it is scored as if it never happened. Dame still get filled in practice, but for a different reason: filling them removes any ambiguity about where one player's wall ends and the other's begins, which is needed before the players can agree on the [territory](./territory.md) boundary at all [@polgote2026rules].

This difference is a common source of confusion for players who learn one ruleset and then watch or play under the other. A player used to area scoring may reflexively fill every last dame out of habit, which costs nothing under territory scoring but also gains nothing once the boundaries are already clear. A player used to territory scoring who switches to an area-scored game and treats dame as free points to skip will find those skipped points quietly go to the opponent instead.

## Related terms

- [pass](./pass.md) - Passing before all dame are filled can cost a point under area scoring, one reason passing is timed carefully.
- [territory](./territory.md) - Dame are defined by what they are not: nobody's secured territory.
- [komi](./komi.md) - Because komi is a fixed score adjustment, it does not change how dame are counted, only what the resulting total is compared against.
- [capture](./capture.md) - A dame can turn into a live point for one side if a nearby capture changes which stones actually stand.
