## Why this is central

This is the section's verified source for Ke Jie's playing style and,
specifically, for the claim this survey's task scope calls for: that the
AI era measurably changed a leading professional's own approach rather
than only professional consensus in the abstract. The article
characterizes Ke Jie's pre-AI style as "accurate positional judgment and
reading" with calculation described as "reliable and fast," balancing
precise analysis against calculated, unconventional risk-taking. After
his 2017 matches against AlphaGo (see
[silver2017](silver2017.md), [wu2019](wu2019.md)), the article states he
"fundamentally reconsidered the game," adopting a more whole-board
perspective (explicitly naming AI's *tenuki* — playing elsewhere at a
critical local moment — as one adopted tactic) and incorporating
AI-discovered patterns into a hybrid classical/AI-era approach, in his
own words: "the possibilities of Go are immense."

## What it does not settle

The article is a general-audience retrospective, not a game-by-game
statistical study — it gives no numeric opening-line distribution,
encirclement ratio, or any of
[National and Historical Play Styles](../research-articles/national-and-historical-play-styles.md)
§6's seven features for either Ke Jie's pre- or post-AlphaGo play. Its
"tenuki adoption" and "hybrid approach" claims are qualitative and
unquantified, useful as evidence that a documented shift occurred, not as
a measured account of its size.
