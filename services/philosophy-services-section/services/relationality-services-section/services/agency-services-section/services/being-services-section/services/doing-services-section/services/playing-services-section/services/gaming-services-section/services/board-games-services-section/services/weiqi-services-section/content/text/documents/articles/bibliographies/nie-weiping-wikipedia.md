## Why this is central

Nie Weiping (1952-2026) is the section's verified source for the named
Chinese fighting-style player this survey's task scope calls for. The
article records his biography (began Go at nine, won the inaugural World
Amateur Go Championship in 1979, 9-dan in 1982) and his decisive role in
the China-Japan Supermatches of the 1980s, where he defeated a string of
leading Japanese professionals — including his own former teacher,
Fujisawa Hideyuki — earning the nickname "Steel Goalkeeper" for
repeatedly closing out matches as the last Chinese player standing. This
survives-under-pressure, fight-to-the-end reputation is this survey's
direct evidence for a semeai-engaging rather than semeai-avoiding
signature, the opposite pole from the Lakota hypothesis's predicted
avoidance.

## What it does not settle

The article's account of Nie's competitive record does not itself
characterize his move-level style — no direct quote here describes his
opening choices, sacrifice tendency, or shape preference; the "fighting"
characterization this survey's §2 draws on is inference from his
tournament role (last player standing, repeated decisive wins against
strong opposition under pressure) rather than a stated stylistic label in
this particular source.
