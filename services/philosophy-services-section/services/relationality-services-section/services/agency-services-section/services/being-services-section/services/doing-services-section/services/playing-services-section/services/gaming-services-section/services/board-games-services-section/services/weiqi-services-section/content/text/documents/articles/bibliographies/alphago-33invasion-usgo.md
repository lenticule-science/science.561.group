## Why this is central

This is the section's verified source for the specific tactical claim
this survey's task scope calls for: the 3-3 invasion and the AI-era
devaluation of moyo. Before AlphaGo, an early 3-3 invasion under a naked
4-4 (hoshi) stone was considered a beginner's error — the thickness White
gains from the standard joseki response was, and to a real degree still
is, regarded as more valuable than Black's small corner. AlphaGo's own
play statistics reversed the received teaching sharply: an early 3-3
invasion appeared in only two of sixty Master-series games, then in
roughly forty of a subsequent fifty-game series — a documented shift from
rare to routine within the same engine's own play, not a single
anecdotal game. Following this, leading professionals including Park
Junghwan (Korea) and Ke Jie (China) adopted the same early invasion in
their own tournament play (see [kejie-gomagic](kejie-gomagic.md) for
Ke Jie's own account of the broader reconsideration this fed into).

## What it does not settle

This is one American Go E-Journal report, useful for the specific
3-3-invasion frequency claim and its professional uptake, not a
comprehensive statistical study of moyo valuation shifts across the AI
era generally — it does not quantify how much moyo's expected value fell,
only that a previously-taboo direct-invasion tactic became common. Nor
does it address whether the shift represents a genuine devaluation of
whole-board framework play as such, versus a narrower correction to how
early an invasion into a specific framework can be played profitably;
[National and Historical Play Styles](../research-articles/national-and-historical-play-styles.md)
§4 states this distinction explicitly rather than collapsing it.
