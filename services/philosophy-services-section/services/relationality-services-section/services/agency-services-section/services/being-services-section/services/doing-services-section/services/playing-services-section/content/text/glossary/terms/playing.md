Playing is what an agent does when it engages a second party — an opponent, a partner, the world — across a bounded arena. It is distinguished among an agent's doings by being intrinsically two-party: communicating moves one's own meaning and knowing settles one's own knowledge, but playing acts *through* another. In the Group's formal treatment this is exact, not figurative: playing's characteristic morphism is the binary interaction of two dual strategies — Girard's cut, `Design ⊗ Design^⊥ → Interaction` — where a design is a strategy written as a polarized valuation over a tree of loci, and the interaction either converges (the daimon; orthogonality) or does not. The derivation is carried in the computer-science note [playing as the *-autonomous interaction faculty](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../engineering-science-services-section/services/computer-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/06/21/2026-06-21-playing-as-star-autonomous-interaction.md) and implemented in the registry package `@red-cup-engineering/playing`.

Culturally, playing is the activity whose quality is [play](./play.md): voluntary, intrinsically motivated engagement, bounded off from ordinary life (Huizinga), sustained by the [lusory attitude](./lusory-attitude.md) — the willing acceptance of constraints because they make the activity possible (Suits). The two readings meet at the boundary: the magic circle of cultural play theory and the arena of formal interaction both name the membrane across which an agent plays. Whether the correspondence is one structure or two is the open question held by the [Girard–Caillois substrate-correspondence note](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/social-theory-science-services-section/services/ludics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/notes/questions-on-girard-caillois-substrate-correspondence.md).

Playing becomes [gaming](../../../../services/gaming-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/gaming.md) when the engagement acquires agreed structure — rules, objectives, win conditions. The gaming section below this one carries that structured form and its traditions; the cultural theory of play (Caillois's categories, deep play, flow) is social-theory custody, at the ludics-science section's game-ludics subsection.
