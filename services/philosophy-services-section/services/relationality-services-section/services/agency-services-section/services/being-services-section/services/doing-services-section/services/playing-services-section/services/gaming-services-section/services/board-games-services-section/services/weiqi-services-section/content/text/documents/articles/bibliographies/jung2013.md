## Why this is central

This study combines three neuroimaging methods — gray matter morphometry,
resting-state functional connectivity, and graph-theoretical network
analysis — on professional Baduk (Go) experts against controls, testing
the hypothesis that expertise produces morphological differences in
occipitotemporal and parietal regions (visuospatial processing, spatial
attention) and in the somatic-marker circuitry involved in emotion-based
decision-making. It is one of the two structural/network studies (with
[sohn2017](sohn2017.md)) that [kim2025](kim2025.md)'s review cites for its
claim that "professional Go players show domain-specific neural
adaptations."

Together with [chen2003](chen2003.md)'s task-activation finding, this
study extends the evidence that Go expertise is measurable in the brain
beyond simple activation, into resting-state network organization and
gray-matter structure — the kind of long-term, training-induced
adaptation this section's own player-development framing (professional
players who have "trained for" many years, per this study's own subject
criteria) takes as a background empirical fact rather than an assumption.

## What it does not settle

Cross-sectional expert-versus-novice comparisons of this kind cannot by
themselves distinguish training-induced plasticity from pre-existing
traits that predispose someone to Go expertise in the first place — a
limitation general to this entire literature, not specific to this study,
and one [kim2025](kim2025.md)'s review notes randomized controlled
training studies are needed to resolve.
