Read in English translation (Bloomington edition).

## Annotation

Eco's *Theory of Semiotics* is the discipline's most influential structural synthesis after Hjelmslev. For the framing this theory commits to, three Eco-1976 threads are load-bearing:

1. **Code as foundational primitive.** Eco treats *code* as the system-level primitive: a semiotic system *is* a (possibly composite) code, and semiotic competence is the ability to operate codes. This grounds the [code](teaching/explanations/code.md) concept and the [code-vs-system-distinction](questions/code-vs-system-distinction.md) open question on whether code and system are the same object viewed from two registers.
2. **Encyclopedia (vs. dictionary) bound on semiosis.** §2.7 distinguishes the *dictionary* (closed list of necessary properties) from the *encyclopedia* (open network of cultural-historical relations); working semiotic practice operates an encyclopedia, which bounds the interpretant chain in practice without closing it in principle. This grounds the substrate-level *Scott-continuous regime* claim of [unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint](../theorems/unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint.md).
3. **Sign-production typology.** §3 catalogues the kinds of work (recognition, ostension, replica, invention) by which sign-vehicles come into use; the typology is structural rather than historical, and informs the [code](teaching/explanations/code.md) and [translation](../morphisms/translation.md) concepts.
