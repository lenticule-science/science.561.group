Anarchist TTRPGs are a loose school of tabletop role-playing design that treats play as a place to rehearse anti-authoritarian social relations.
These games often distrust centralized mastery, distribute authority across the table, and center community survival, mutual aid, or shared responsibility over heroic command.

This is not a single lineage.
Some anarchist TTRPGs are explicit in name and politics.
Others are better described as anti-authoritarian or prefigurative: they do not announce anarchism loudly, but they redesign play around many of the same commitments.

## Core commitments

### Authority should be distributed deliberately

One of the clearest anarchist design moves in TTRPGs is refusal of a single sovereign controller.
Avery Alder's [Belonging Outside Belonging](https://buriedwithoutceremony.com/belonging) engine makes this explicit: no dice, no masters, distributed control over setting elements, and collaborative worldbuilding through a community worksheet.
Joshua Benjamin's thesis is useful because it explains how these structures change the rhetoric of play rather than merely adding variety.

### Community matters more than command

Anarchist TTRPGs often care less about mission execution and more about what it takes to keep a vulnerable community alive.
[Dream Askew](https://buriedwithoutceremony.com/dream-askew) is a strong example.
Its question is not how a party clears a dungeon under a GM's command.
Its question is how a queer enclave survives, negotiates scarcity, and builds forms of care amid collapse.

### Procedure can prefigure politics

These games are often prefigurative.
The social relations of play are part of the point.
If the game imagines a freer, less coercive world, it often tries to organize the table in a freer, less coercive way too.
That does not remove conflict.
It changes where conflict lives: in scarcity, desire, competing needs, and community tension rather than in obedience to an external master.

### Publishing culture matters too

Anarchist tendencies also appear in circulation and licensing practices.
Some indie creators pair anti-authoritarian design with open or anti-capitalist sharing norms, community copies, sliding-scale access, or explicit rejection of dominant platform ethics.
This publishing layer does not by itself make a game anarchist, but it often travels with anarchist design commitments.

## Common design patterns

Anarchist TTRPGs often include some combination of the following:

- shared or rotating scene authority
- GM-less or GM-light procedures
- community worksheets or mapmaking as co-governance tools
- safety and consent procedures built into setup
- economies of scarcity, care, and obligation rather than loot accumulation
- settings focused on enclaves, collectives, fugitives, rebels, or precarious communities

## Relationship to decolonial and Indigenous work

Anarchist TTRPGs overlap with [Decolonial TTRPGs](./decolonial-ttrpgs.md) and [Indigenous TTRPGs](./indigenous-ttrpgs.md) where they challenge domination, militarized adventure, and sovereign mastery.
But the traditions are not the same.
A GM-less game can still ignore colonial power.
An anti-authoritarian mechanic does not automatically produce accountable worldbuilding.
The strongest anarchist TTRPG practice recognizes that hierarchy is racialized and colonial as well as procedural.

## Examples

The Belonging Outside Belonging family is the clearest contemporary cluster.
[Dream Askew](https://buriedwithoutceremony.com/dream-askew) centers queer community in apocalypse.
[Dream Apart](https://buriedwithoutceremony.com/dream-apart) centers Jewish communal life under threat.
Both distribute authority and focus on belonging, precarity, and self-organization. *Zephyr: An Anarchist Game of Fleeting Identities* is notable as an explicitly anarchist recent title rather than an adjacent one.

## Limits

Not every collaborative game is anarchist.
Not every game about rebels is anarchist.
The term is useful only when a game's politics and procedures converge: authority is genuinely redistributed, coercion is not naturalized, and collective life becomes more important than mastery.
