## Annotation

A systematic introduction to process philosophy that makes the historical case for process as a *recurring* rather than marginal commitment in Western philosophy: Heraclitus, Leibniz's *monads*, Bergson's *duration*, James's *radical empiricism*, Whitehead's *actual occasions*, Hartshorne's *neoclassical theism*. Rescher distils a *process metaphysics* whose central commitments are: (i) *primacy of process over substance* — substance is a stability-pattern of processes, not vice versa; (ii) *internal relatedness* — every process is constituted by its relations to other processes; (iii) *novelty and emergence* — process generates genuinely new kinds, not merely rearrangements; (iv) *temporal directionality* — process metaphysics is asymmetric in time, distinguishing past (settled) from future (open) in a way substance metaphysics does not.

The predicated framing reads Rescher's four commitments as the metaphysical underpinning of the substrate:
- Process-primacy ≈ the predication-cell as unit of *becoming-asserted*, not unit of *static record*;
- Internal relatedness ≈ subject identity by [aboutness](teaching/explanations/aboutness.md), not by essence;
- Novelty/emergence ≈ the closure regime can produce derived cells whose predicates are not in the asserted predicates' immediate vocabulary, with [federation-as-bilattice-colimit](../theorems/federation-as-bilattice-colimit.md) producing genuinely new fibers;
- Temporal directionality ≈ the *settling* dynamics of [predication-settling](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) is monotone in the [knowledge-order](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md), distinguishing the past-as-settled from the future-as-open.

Rescher's book is the single most useful entry point for situating the framing's metaphysical commitments within the longer process tradition — it shows that the substrate's commitments are *not idiosyncratic to one school* but recur across the process literature.
