A context spacesuit is the bounded package of identity, constraints, capabilities, perception, memory, and mission framing loaded around an agent before it acts. It is not merely prompt text. It is the local world the agent can perceive and compute within.

The library source [Context Spacesuits in Agentic AI](/library/information/computing/artificial-intelligence/multiagent-systems/archive/context-spacesuits-in-agentic-ai.md) compares current agent systems, military briefing kits, RPG loadouts, and process environments as convergent solutions to the same problem: an actor needs a structured suit before a task becomes legible.

For research agents, the practical rule is to keep the suit small enough to act and rich enough to preserve authority, constraints, available tools, and relevant memory. The guide [Witness Propagation for Agent Teams](/library/information/computing/artificial-intelligence/multiagent-systems/guides/witness-propagation-for-agent-teams.md) uses this term as the entry point for agent-team witness work.

## Use

Use this term when explaining what an agent was equipped to see or do at the moment it submitted, transformed, or adjudicated a witness. A failed action may be a bad model response, but it may also be a malformed context spacesuit: missing authority, missing source memory, excessive irrelevant context, or the wrong tools in scope.
