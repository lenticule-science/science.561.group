# Arrow 1951 — Social Choice and Individual Values

Arrow, K.J. (1951). *Social Choice and Individual Values*.
Wiley.
(2nd edition with new preface and extended commentary, Yale University Press, 1963.)

The book proving Arrow's impossibility theorem and founding modern social choice theory.
Arrow showed that no voting rule with three or more alternatives satisfies all of universal domain, Pareto, IIA, and non-dictatorship.

The result reshaped welfare economics, political philosophy, and (much later) AI alignment theory.
Demonstrating that *no perfect aggregation rule exists* forced normative analysts to confront tradeoffs rather than pursue a "best" rule.

Arrow received the Nobel Prize in Economics (1972) in substantial part for this work.
The 1963 second edition includes extensive philosophical commentary on the theorem's significance and implications.

The theorem's reach extends beyond voting: any procedure aggregating individual preferences (jury decisions, group recommendations, multi-stakeholder AI alignment) faces the same constraints.
