# Pearl, *Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference* (2009)

The definitive textbook on Pearl's causal framework.
Second edition (2009) of the 2000 original.

## What it covers

- Structural causal models and graphical models.
- D-separation, Markov property, faithfulness.
- Do-calculus and identifiability.
- Counterfactual semantics and inference.
- Potential outcomes and translation between
  frameworks.
- Mediation, direct/indirect effects.
- Causal discovery foundations.

The 2nd edition adds substantial material on
counterfactuals, mediation analysis, and the
Shpitser-Pearl identification completeness results.

## Why it matters

This book *is* the field's standard reference. Pearl's
framework — graphs, do-operator, do-calculus,
counterfactual three-step procedure — is presented
authoritatively here. Anyone doing serious technical
work in causal inference works with the contents of
this book.

## How to read

Substantial mathematical density. For first exposure,
*The Book of Why* (2018) is more accessible.
*Elements of Causal Inference* (Peters et al. 2017)
is shorter. *Causality* is the reference once you
need depth.

## Related works

- Pearl-Mackenzie 2018 — popular exposition.
- Peters-Janzing-Schölkopf 2017 — alternate textbook.
- Hernán-Robins 2020 — potential-outcomes-focused
  textbook.
- Shpitser-Pearl 2008 — identification completeness
  results.
