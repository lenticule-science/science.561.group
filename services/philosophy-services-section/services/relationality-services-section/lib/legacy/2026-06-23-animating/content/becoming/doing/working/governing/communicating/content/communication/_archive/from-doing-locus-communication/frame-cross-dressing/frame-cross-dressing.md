Frame cross-dressing is what happens when an artifact dresses one kind of unit in the costume of another. A speculative claim presented in the typographic form of a theorem. A pedagogical illustration presented as a worked proof. A remark presented as a definition. The frame says one thing about the unit's epistemic status — what is being claimed, what is being assumed, what closure is being asked for — and the content does another.

The defect is not stylistic. It is substantive: the reader, trained to read frames as commitments, will treat the unit according to its frame. They will cite the speculation as a result. They will use the illustration as a deductive step. They will quote the remark as a definition. The artifact has used the social authority of one kind of unit to launder content that does not belong in that kind of unit.

## Examples across registers

In mathematics: a paper writing as a Theorem something the author hopes is true but has not actually proved, with the proof being a hand-wave. In software documentation: a snippet labeled "example" that actually carries the load of being the canonical pattern, but is presented as if it were optional. In policy: a statement framed as a definition that is actually a normative commitment. In a slide deck: a chart framed as data that is actually an editorialized projection.

The remedy is [frame-discipline](teaching/explanations/frame-discipline.md): the practice's machinery for keeping each frame's commitments visible and honoring them. Strong practices catch frame cross-dressing in review — they have norms that say a Theorem must be a theorem, a Definition must be a definition, an Example must be exemplifying. Weak practices accumulate cross-dressed units until the practice's frames stop carrying meaning.

## Distinguishing cross-dressing from honest weakening

It is honest to mark a result as conjectural and present it as such. It is honest to mark an example as illustrative and refuse it the load of being canonical. It is honest to mark a proof as a sketch. The defect is not weakening — it is *concealing* the weakening behind a frame whose authority the unit has not earned.
