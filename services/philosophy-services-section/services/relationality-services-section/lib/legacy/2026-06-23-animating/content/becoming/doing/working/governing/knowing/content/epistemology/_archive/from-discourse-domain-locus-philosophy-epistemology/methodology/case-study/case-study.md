# Case study

*The method in which a single case — a community, an organization, an event, a movement, a policy episode — is studied in depth as itself, with the analytic claim that what is learned about this case bears on a class of cases or on a theoretical question the case instantiates.*

The case study is older than sociology; the discipline inherited it from law, medicine, and history. Its modern sociological form was developed in the Chicago School (Whyte's *Street Corner Society*, Lynd and Lynd's *Middletown*) and in the comparative-historical tradition, where each case in a multi-case comparison is itself a case study before the comparison begins.

Yin's typology distinguishes single-case from multiple-case designs and exploratory, descriptive, and explanatory cases. The substantive sociological question is rarely "what is true of this case" alone; the case is studied because it is taken to bear on some larger claim. A *paradigmatic* case (Flyvbjerg's term) instantiates a class. A *deviant* or *negative* case tests a generalization. A *critical* case is one in which a hypothesis must hold if it holds anywhere. A *typical* case is the workhorse middle.

The method's commitments. The case must be bounded — what is and is not part of this case is a methodological choice with consequences. Multiple sources of evidence (interviews, observation, documents, archival material, sometimes survey data) are typically combined; the method does not depend on any single instrument. Causal inference inside a case is process-tracing — the analyst follows the chain of events and asks what would have had to be different for the outcome to differ.

Critiques. The method is sometimes dismissed as anecdotal, but the dismissal usually rests on the assumption that statistical generalization is the only legitimate form of inference; case studies aim at structural inference, which is a different question. The method genuinely struggles with claims about *frequencies* in a population — if the question is how common something is, a case study cannot answer it.

The case study sits at the intersection of [ethnography](ethnography.md) (when conducted through participant observation), [archival method](archival-method.md) (when historical), [comparative-historical analysis](comparative-historical-analysis.md) (when the unit of comparison), and [discourse analysis](discourse-analysis.md) (when the textual record is central). It is less a discrete method than a mode of organizing the others around a bounded object of study.
