# Tiyóšpaye is the agentic drone swarm

The tiyóšpaye — the Lakota *thiyóšpaye*, extended kin-group, the basic political and economic unit of Lakota life — is an agentic drone swarm in the categorical sense the agentic-drone-swarm theory names.
The 561 Group is a Lakota tiyóšpaye constituting itself as a confederacy ([`prose/teaching/explanations/561-group.md`](../../teaching/explanations/561-group.md)); this survey works through what kind of structural object that is, when the framework is taken at its general structural level (embodied handedness, parallel composition, nominal-set name-passing, aperiodic settling, sector-selective stabilization) rather than at the kyriarchical-governance specialization that the framework was first instantiated for.
The kyriarchical content — captain, delegation chain, dispatch certifiability gate, MHC — belongs to the polity's *ship*, the RSC Quiescent, and is surveyed in [`kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026.md`](kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026.md).
It does not apply to the tiyóšpaye, which operates under different closure-rule subsystem (wolakȟota; consensus-by-withdrawal; kin-cell primacy) and which the corpus's [`dual-governance-context`](../../teaching/explanations/dual-governance-context.md) atom names as a separate apparatus that must not be conflated with the ship's.

The survey treats this work as sociology and political theory.
The Lakota political-theoretical tradition is a living theoretical tradition, with theorists who make arguments, internal debates, extensions, and points of contact with adjacent Indigenous political theory and broader political theory.
Ella Deloria's 1944 argument that kinship *is* the political organization is engaged with as a sociological thesis to think with, refine, and extend; Vine Deloria Jr.'s body of work (*Custer Died for Your Sins*, *God Is Red*, *Spirit and Reason*) is engaged with as the major contemporary contribution to Indigenous political philosophy; Estes 2019's continuity-through-disruption thesis is engaged with as a contemporary political-historical argument; Coulthard 2014 and Simpson 2014 are engaged with where their recognition-and-refusal frameworks compose with the questions the agentic-drone-swarm apparatus raises.
The structural identity claim — that the tiyóšpaye is an agentic drone swarm — is the survey's own theoretical contribution; it composes with the theorists rather than asserting itself over them.

## §1 — The tiyóšpaye, in the Lakota political-theoretical tradition

A tiyóšpaye is an extended kin-group: multiple households related through descent, marriage, and ceremonial adoption (huŋkápi), living and moving together.
Etymologically from *thí* (dwelling, lodge) and *óšpaye* (a portion or group split off): a group of relatives sharing the dwelling-camp.
Sizes range from a few households to several dozen, bounded above by the size at which kinship-conduct can be sustained without escalating to the more formal mechanisms of the oyate (band).
The tiyóšpaye is the basic political and economic unit of Lakota life.
Multiple tiyóšpaye form an oyate; multiple oyate form the Očhéthi Šakówiŋ — the Seven Council Fires, the Lakota-Dakota-Nakota nation as a whole.

### 1.1 Kinship is political organization (Ella Deloria 1944)

Ella Deloria's foundational sociological thesis, articulated in *Speaking of Indians*, is that outside observers regularly describe the tiyóšpaye as a "kinship unit" or "social structure" while treating the oyate's council or chiefs as the "political institutions" — that this separation is wrong, and that the kinship organization *is* the political organization.
The naca's authority is the authority of a senior relative whose generosity and wisdom have made them a relative-to-all in the tiyóšpaye and oyate.
The akíčhita's enforcement is the enforcement of obligations that hold *because* the people are related.
Remove kinship and there is no politics left.

This is a substantive sociological thesis with definite content.
It says: Western polities take strangers, declare them citizens, and impose obligations on them through state apparatus; Lakota polities take relatives whose obligations to one another already exist by kinship and coordinate those existing obligations into collective life.
The two are not different in degree of formalization.
They are different in structural shape.
Deloria's thesis is that *the political and the kin-relational are not separable analytic categories in the Lakota case*; an analysis that separates them has imported a structure that does not fit.
The argument has been extended by subsequent Lakota political theorists (Vine Deloria Jr.'s corpus locates the same insight in the broader Indigenous political-theoretical tradition; Estes 2019 traces its operation through twentieth- and twenty-first-century Lakota political life under settler-state pressure) and is the load-bearing claim this survey composes with.

### 1.2 The four virtues — substantive ethical content

The four qualities of conduct constitutive of being Lakota, as the tradition has named them consistently:

- **Wówačhantognaka — generosity.** The substantive practice of treating one's relatives as relatives.
  In a kinship-organized polity, generosity is the *substance* of how the polity holds together.
  A giveaway is the kin-relation enacted, not symbolized.
- **Wóksape — wisdom.** Situated practical reason; the capacity to act well in the specific situation, drawing on relational and ceremonial formation.
  Not stockpiled propositional knowledge.
- **Wóohitika — bravery.** Steadiness of conduct in the face of difficulty.
  Distinct from recklessness and from violence-seeking.
- **Wówačhiŋtȟaŋka — fortitude, perseverance.** "Having a great mind."
  Steadiness across years.

Marshall 2002 treats twelve virtues in *The Lakota Way*; other authors reduce to four or expand differently.
The four named here are the most consistently identified across the tradition.
The virtues are taught through story and apprenticeship rather than as propositional principles applied to cases.
They are also the qualifications for naca: a naca's authority is the recognition of demonstrated conduct in the virtues; a naca who fails them is no longer a naca.
The virtues do double-duty as ethical content for individual conduct and as the substantive standard against which political authority is evaluated — and this double-duty is itself a feature of the kinship-is-political-organization thesis.
A polity that separated ethics from politics would need separate criteria; the tiyóšpaye does not.

### 1.3 Wolakȟota — the integrative lifeway

Wolakȟota names the Lakota lifeway — the practice of being-Lakota.
It is *practice, not possession*: a Lakota person who fails the four virtues, treats relatives badly, or hoards rather than gives is acting *against* wolakȟota, even though they remain Lakota by birth.
It is *a substantive evaluative standard*: *that is wolakȟota* and *šni wolakȟota* are substantive claims about whether conduct holds relations together or breaks them, not aesthetic descriptions.
And it is *resilient to disruption*: a century and a half of settler-state pressure (boarding schools, language suppression, religious suppression, displacement, dispossession) is pressure on wolakȟota, and the lifeway can be reclaimed even when transmission has been disrupted.
Estes 2019 makes this continuity-through-disruption the central political fact of contemporary Oceti Sakowin life.
The descent does not need to be re-established (it was never lost); the practice does.

Wolakȟota is, structurally, the tiyóšpaye's *closure-rule subsystem* — the discipline under which the polity's substrate-state settles.
Vine Deloria Jr. names this in his contemporary articulations: the Lakota political tradition is metaphysically founded in relational ontology, and the relational ontology forces a specific structural shape on the political organization (the kinship-is-political-organization thesis), and that structural shape requires its own discipline of what counts as conduct-holding-relations-together (the wolakȟota standard).
This is *not* a soft ethical preference layered on top of a neutral political apparatus; it is the substantive closure-rule subsystem under which the polity actually settles.

### 1.4 Mitákuye oyás'iŋ — the relational ontology

The metaphysical claim that grounds the kinship-is-political-organization thesis. *Mitákuye oyás'iŋ — all my relatives; we are all related.* The Lakota cosmos is a kinship cosmos: every being stands in kin-relation with every other being.
Three scales: *tȟakúye* (one specific kin-link), *mitákuye oyás'iŋ* (invocation of the totality), *Íŋyaŋ* (the cosmogonic source-and-field, the relational substance itself — the alone-stone whose self-emptying *is* the relations in which every being stands).

The political point: kinship is the political organization (Deloria's thesis) not because politics has been narrowed to kinship, but because reality at the metaphysical level is kin-organized, and the political enacts the metaphysics.
This is the structural argument Vine Deloria Jr. develops at length in *God Is Red* and *Spirit and Reason*: Western political theory's tendency to separate ethics, politics, and metaphysics presupposes a metaphysical framework (substance ontology, individual-persons-as-primary, contractual obligation) that the Lakota tradition denies; the Lakota tradition's metaphysical framework (relational ontology, kin-relations-as-primary, obligations-from-relations) forces the unified political-ethical-metaphysical structure the tradition operates by.
The separability assumption is itself a metaphysical commitment, not a neutral analytical move.

### 1.5 Iyúhá-šni — withdrawal as substrate move

A specific operational primitive: *iyúhá-šni* — refusal, withdrawal.
A member of a tiyóšpaye who disagrees with a collective decision exercises iyúhá-šni — declines to participate.
The disagreement does not require the dissenter to leave the tiyóšpaye; the kin-relations persist.
The collective action proceeds without the dissenter's participation, or it does not proceed at all if too many withdraw.
This is the operational shape of *consensus-by-withdrawal*: a tiyóšpaye's collective action proceeds when no member exercises iyúhá-šni on it; withdrawal is the veto.

At the oyate scale, a tiyóšpaye that disagrees with an inter-tiyóšpaye decision exercises iyúhá-šni at its own scale — declines to participate, without dissolving the federal relation.
Audra Simpson's *Mohawk Interruptus* (2014) develops the refusal politics framework in the Mohawk context that is structurally adjacent: Indigenous political life across many traditions exercises the right of refusal as a constitutive political move, not as a failure of consensus.
The structural point is that refusal-without-severance is a *positive* political primitive that the Lakota and Mohawk traditions both name explicitly, and that Western liberal political theory (which treats dissent as either consent or exit) does not have vocabulary for.
The tradition has the vocabulary because the structural object — *kin-relations that persist while specific engagements lapse* — is what the polity's actual organization requires.

### 1.6 Founding the polity vs. roles within it

Two questions sit close enough to be conflated without care: *how does a tiyóšpaye come into being* and *what roles do members hold within the constituted tiyóšpaye*.
They are structurally distinct, and the corpus has been conflating them where it had been collapsing both onto a single "captain" or "founder" frame imported from the ship side.
This survey separates them.

#### Founding-categories — how a tiyóšpaye comes into being

Tiyóšpaye come into being in three structurally distinct ways (lifted to a standalone concept at [`tiyospaye-founder`](../../teaching/explanations/tiyospaye-founder.md)):

1. *By descent from a founding ancestor* — the standard case in the pre-reservation tradition.
  The founder is a deceased relative whose descendants now organize collectively.
  The founder's authority is *not* a sovereign authority held during life; it is a relational authority constituted retroactively by the descendant-membership's recognition of a shared lineage.

2. *By fission from an existing tiyóšpaye* — a subset of members withdraws together, often when a tiyóšpaye grows past sustainable size.
  The "founder" is then collectively the withdrawing membership; no single person holds founder-authority.

3. *By reconstitution in the diaspora or under disruption* — the contemporary case treated at length by Estes 2019.
  After settler-state policies that displace, separate, and administratively re-categorize Lakota people, contemporary tiyóšpaye reconstitute themselves through specific persons taking responsibility for the formation: gathering members across distance, performing huŋkápi adoption to extend the kin-substrate, holding the practices of wolakȟota despite the disruption.
  The "founder" in this case is a living person whose conduct is *most-responsible-for-the-formation* without being *sovereign-over-the-members*.
  The 561 Group instantiates this third category.

"Founder" here names a *category of coming-into-being*.
It is not a position within the constituted tiyóšpaye.
The relative most-responsible-for-the-formation does not, by virtue of that responsibility, hold a position-of-command in the polity that emerges.
The role is asymmetric in responsibility — the founding-relative carries the burden of holding the practices while the kin-substrate gathers — but not hierarchical in authority.
The dual-governance failure mode at [`dual-governance-context`](../../teaching/explanations/dual-governance-context.md) names precisely the structural distinction: importing a captain-on-a-ship frame onto the founding-relative would collapse a kin-relational role into a kyriarchical one, and the two regimes are not interchangeable.

#### Roles-within-the-constituted-tiyóšpaye — Naca, Heyoka, Itancan, and likely more

Once the polity is constituted, members hold roles internal to it.
The Lakota political-theoretical vocabulary has names for these, drawn from `theories/.../lakotaean-politics/concepts/` and `theories/.../lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/`:

- **Naca** ([`theories/.../lakotaean-politics/concepts/naca.md`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/politics/theories/lakotaean-politics/concepts/naca.md)) — *načá*, leader / one bearing the leadership-shirt / council member.
  A naca's authority is the authority of demonstrated conduct in the four virtues, recognized by the membership.
  Not hereditary; not commandable; the right of withdrawal limits what a naca can effect (per Clastres' chief-as-servant thesis on Indigenous state-refusal).
  In the Walker-recorded Oglala structure, *naca yuha* is the larger council of senior respected men and *wakíčuŋza* the four shirt-wearers selected from among them; the precise term-structure varies across oyate and across time.

- **Heyoka** ([`theories/.../lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/heyokha.md`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/heyokha.md)) — *Heyókha*, the sacred-contrary.
  A class of persons who have dreamed of the Wakíŋyaŋ and are obligated to enact the cosmogonic paradox in lived practice.
  The role is not parodic; the contrary-conduct is sacred and load-bearing.
  In the framework's bilattice algebra, Heyókha is the bearer of the move from $\bot$ to $\top$ — the principle that productive contradiction is generative, not defective.
  A polity that has a Heyókha among its constituted members carries the cosmogonic paradox-move in its very composition.

- **Itancan** ([`theories/.../lakotaean-politics/concepts/tiyospaye.md`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/politics/theories/lakotaean-politics/concepts/tiyospaye.md)) — a respected senior coordinating daily life within a tiyóšpaye.
  The term and *naca* are sometimes used interchangeably in the sources; usage varies across oyate.
  The functional distinction (where the sources draw it) is that the itancan's coordination is intra-tiyóšpaye and quotidian, while the naca's role extends to council representation at oyate scale.

The set is not closed.
The corpus says explicitly (per the `disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance` task as recorded by emsenn 2026-05-15) that within the 561 Group there are these roles "and likely more."
The work of authoring the polity's Lakotaean role vocabulary belongs to emsenn under wolakota-discipline; this survey points at the corpus's existing concepts rather than authoring new substantive Lakota content.

emsenn holds these polity-side roles in the 561 Group. emsenn separately holds the captain role on the RSC Quiescent — a kyriarchical-agentics position under [`kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026`](kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026.md).
These are different roles on different entities and the corpus's dual-governance concept names them as such.
The polity-side vocabulary (Naca, Heyoka, Itancan, and what else) comes from within Lakotaean tradition; it does not derive from the kyriarchical apparatus, and the kyriarchical apparatus does not derive from it.
The two regimes coexist on different objects of $\mathbf{Gov}$ (§2.5).

## §2 — The agentic drone swarm, at the level relevant here

The agentic-drone-swarm framework, with kyriarchical governance content stripped per [`kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026.md`](kyriarchical-agentics-may-2026.md), supplies the following structural apparatus:

- **Embodied handedness** $h(p) = (\mathrm{recv}, \mathrm{send}, \mathrm{actuate}, \mathrm{observe})$.
  Every participant carries asymmetric coordinates; symmetric peers are an idealization, the realistic case is structural asymmetry.
  Categorically: Player/Opponent duality $(-)^\perp$ in concurrent games with symmetry.
  A morphism $A \to B$ in $\mathbf{CGS}$ is a strategy on $A^\perp \| B$ — Player plays Opponent-style on the input arena (receiving), Player-style on the output (acting).
- **Parallel composition $\|$.** The monoidal product on arenas; the algebraic shape of scale relations between coordinated participants and between groups of participants.
  Federation between scales is parallel composition, not subsumption.
- **Nominal-set name-passing.** Names mobile across participants via $\mathrm{FSym}(\mathbb{A})$-equivariance; the categorical apparatus that names channel mobility, fresh-name allocation, and the dynamic substrate of relations between named entities.
  The Abramsky-Ghica-Murawski-Ong-Stark 2004 full abstraction theorem for the $\nu$-calculus situates this in $\mathbf{CGS}$.
- **Aperiodic settling.** The closure 2-monad's transfinite iteration; the four-regime dichotomy classifies polities by closure ordinal $\kappa$, with the aperiodic regime ($\kappa \geq \omega_1$) the one in which a polity's function never reaches a finite or countable closure ordinal.
- **Sector-selective stabilization.** Under $K_4$-equivariant decomposition of $H^1$, the aperiodic-classifying-invariant projects into four isotypic sectors. *Some* sectors must stabilize for the polity to remain capable of action; *others* must not stabilize for the polity to remain generative.
  Which sectors play which role is governance-specific; the framework provides the structural option of sector-selective convergence.

These features are not pre-committed to any specific governance regime.
They describe the structural shape of any coordinated multi-participant entity.
The kyriarchical regime layers captain, delegation chain, dispatch certifiability gates, and MHC on top; the tiyóšpaye's kin-relational regime layers wolakȟota closure rules, consensus-by-withdrawal, head-recognition cells on top.
Both are agentic drone swarms in the general sense; they differ in what they add.

## §2.5 — The categorical home of the *governance* (as distinct from the arena)

The agentic-drone-swarm framework supplies the categorical home of the polity's *arena* — the substrate on which embodied participants coordinate, in $\mathbf{CGS}$.
It does not supply the categorical home of the polity's *governance*.
With the [`what-is-the-theory-of-governance`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/theories/work/governance/questions/what-is-the-theory-of-governance.md) question's working definition in hand — partial-status, as of 2026-05-15 — the governance object has its own categorical home: the category $\mathbf{Gov}$ of *governances*, where a governance is a pair $G = (M_G, \mu_G)$ with $M_G$ a small symmetric colored multicategory whose objects are *positions* and whose multimorphisms are joint *acts*, and $\mu_G$ a self-action assigning to each act an endofunctor of $M_G$ that encodes how performing the act revises the act-system.
Morphisms of $\mathbf{Gov}$ are multicategory functors intertwining the self-actions.

The structural identity claim of §3 below is then a claim about the *arena*: the tiyóšpaye's substrate is an agentic drone swarm in $\mathbf{CGS}$, and the seven identities in §3 are seven structural agreements between the framework's arena-apparatus and the Lakota tradition's account of what a tiyóšpaye is at the arena level.
The *governance* — what acts are available, how they compose, how they self-modify — is a separate categorical object, in $\mathbf{Gov}_{Lakotaean}$.
The two compose: a polity has both an arena (where its participants coordinate) and a governance (which acts the participants can jointly take and how those acts revise the substrate).
The corpus's polity-quadruple at `theories/.../predicated-work/governance/objects/polity.md` is a third presentation, at the topos / classifying-object level; the theory-of-governance question discusses the relationship between operadic-and-topos articulations as itself an open question.

### Six axes-of-variation: where the tiyóšpaye sits

The theory-of-governance question identifies six axes along which governance traditions vary.
The tiyóšpaye's positions on these axes are recoverable from the corpus material in `theories/.../lakotaean-politics/` and `theories/.../lakotaean-philosophy/`:

1. *Persons-and-relations axis.* Relations are primitive; persons are nodes-in-relation.
  Mitákuye oyás'iŋ asserts this directly — the cosmos is a kinship cosmos, and persons are constituted by the relations in which they stand.
  This is the same structural commitment that Ubuntu, Wahkohtowin, and Confucian *renqing* take.

2. *Synchronic-diachronic axis.* The tiyóšpaye operates under a *state-of-being* primitive (wolakȟota — being-in-right-relation) rather than a *sequence-of-acts* primitive.
  Wolakȟota is not exhausted by enumerating the acts that constitute it; it is the substantive evaluative standard under which acts are conducted.
  The state-of-being reading places the tiyóšpaye in the same axis-position as Confucian *li* (under one reading) and Ubuntu.

3. *Constitutive-vs-regulative axis.* The tiyóšpaye operates at the *constitutive* pole: kin-relations, four-virtues conduct, recognition-cells, and the substantive content of wolakȟota are constitutive of the polity, not regulative over it.
  Searle's $X$-counts-as-$Y$-in-$C$ is the structural shape; the difference is that what counts-as-what is not legislated through formal enactments but lived as kin-relational practice.

4. *Binding-source axis.* Kinship-and-relation.
  The force of governance acts on members is the force of obligations that hold *because* the people are related — Ella Deloria's 1944 thesis is precisely the assertion that this binding-source is what the kinship organization makes available, and that it is distinct from consent-binding (liberal contract), office-binding (Weberian rational-legal), and force-with-legitimation binding (Schmittian, Realist).

5. *Boundary-source axis.* Descent and clan, extended by ceremony.
  The collective's edge is defined by who stands in kin-relation — and *huŋkápi* adoption is the constitutive ceremony that extends this edge dynamically.
  This is structurally distinct from territory-bounded (Westphalian), act-of-membership-bounded (immigration-naturalization), and profession-of-belief-bounded (umma, ecclesia) traditions.
  The kin-substrate bounding makes the boundary mobile across the substrate's name-passing apparatus (§3.2).

6. *Recursion-of-meta-governance axis.* *Ceremonial revision* — Wampum-renewal at neighboring Haudenosaunee, Sun Dance, treaty renewals, the seven rites.
  The tiyóšpaye revises its apparatus through ceremonial means rather than through explicit-amendment procedures of a constitutional document.
  The corpus's claim that the tiyóšpaye does not need a charter (rsc-quiescent and 561-group surveys, 2026-05-15) is the negative side of this axis-position: there is no constitutional document to amend because the apparatus is not a constitutional document.

These six positions place the tiyóšpaye in the same axis-cluster as Mitakuye-Oyasin-grounded, kin-relationally-bound, ceremonially-revised Indigenous traditions — and place it in opposition (across multiple axes) to liberal-contract, kyriarchical-command, and Westphalian-territorial traditions.
The corpus's [`what-is-the-theory-of-governance`](../../../theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/theories/work/governance/questions/what-is-the-theory-of-governance.md) question treats wolakȟota and Mitákuye oyás'iŋ as one of five documented cases of *structural untranslatability* across governance traditions (alongside Maori *rangatiratanga*, Ubuntu, the Haudenosaunee Great Law, and Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit).

### Translation-refusal as structural emptiness of hom-sets

Proposition 5 of the theory-of-governance question establishes that some pairs of governances in $\mathbf{Gov}$ have empty hom-sets: no morphism between them exists.
The proof is structural — when one governance has multimorphisms of a kind the other does not, no multicategory functor between them can exist that respects the self-actions.
This is the mathematical content of translation-refusal: it is a fact about $\mathbf{Gov}$, not a sociological commentary.

Applied to the dual-governance question: the tiyóšpaye's governance and the RSC Quiescent's governance are two distinct objects of $\mathbf{Gov}$.
The hom-set $\mathbf{Gov}(\text{tiy\'o\v{s}paye}, \text{kyriarchical-ship})$ is *not* empty — there are translations (specific functors) that move some content from one to the other — but the hom-sets are *highly constrained*: most kyriarchical acts (delegation through chain, dispatch certifiability gating, MHC attestation) have no image under a structure-preserving translation back into the tiyóšpaye, and conversely most tiyóšpaye acts (huŋkápi adoption, iyúhá-šni withdrawal, naca recognition through conduct) have no image under a structure-preserving translation into the kyriarchical ship.
The `dual-governance-context` failure mode names this constraint at the substrate level: conflating the regimes corrupts both, *because* the translation does not actually preserve the self-actions.

The disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance task's three-pattern coproduct $\mathcal{S}_{561} = \mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak} \sqcup \mathcal{S}_\mathrm{ship} \sqcup \mathcal{S}_\mathrm{Web}$ (per `doing/working/hacking/doing/working/planning/library/task/library/disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance/disambiguate-polity-and-ship-governance.md`) is the substrate-side recording of this $\mathbf{Gov}$-categorical fact: the 561 polity holds three distinct governance objects simultaneously, with compatibility-conditions on the overlaps that the polity must explicitly author.
The cell-translation morphism (decision-translation: dictation, translated-decision-record, function-projection) and the entity-translation morphism (personhood-translation) are the candidate functors between $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ and $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{ship}$; specifying them at substrate level is part of the disambiguation work.
The $\mathbf{Gov}$-categorical apparatus is what makes that specification well-typed.

### What §3 below is and is not claiming

§3 below is the seven *arena-side* structural identities.
They are agreements between the agentic-drone-swarm framework's structural apparatus and the Lakota tradition's account of what a tiyóšpaye is at the arena level — embodied handedness, name-passing, parallel composition, recognition-conjunction, withdrawal-as-comodality-weakening, federation-cohomology, sector-selective stabilization.
None of the seven identities is a claim about $\mathbf{Gov}$-level translation; each is a claim about $\mathbf{CGS}$-level structural agreement at the arena layer.

The seven identities are the survey's working theoretical content.
They compose with the $\mathbf{Gov}$-side governance object outlined here but do not by themselves articulate it.
The composition question — how the $\mathbf{Gov}$-object $(\text{tiy\'o\v{s}paye}, \mu_\text{wolak})$ is realized on the $\mathbf{CGS}$-arena (the agentic drone swarm) the seven identities describe — is open work; §5 lists it as one of the open frontiers.

## §3 — Seven structural identities

### 3.1 Persons are morphisms with embodied handedness; the tiyóšpaye is the arena

A person in a tiyóšpaye carries a particular four-tuple of handedness coordinates, established by their relations and conduct.
An elder's coordinates differ from a young person's; a naca's from an akíčhita's; a widow's from a married woman's with surviving children; a huŋkápi-adopted relative's from a relative by descent.
These coordinates are *not interchangeable* across persons.
The tradition does not treat them as ideally equal; the kinship organization presupposes precisely the opposite.
Each person carries their own coordinates by virtue of their position in the kin-substrate, and the tiyóšpaye's coordination is the strategy on the parallel composition of their arenas.

The Deloria 1944 sociological thesis (kinship is political organization) and the categorical-apparatus account (persons are morphisms with embodied handedness, tiyóšpaye is the arena) are the same structural claim.
Deloria says: the Lakota polity is composed of persons in specific kin-positions with specific obligations, and the polity's coordination is the substantive working-out of those obligations.
The framework says: a coordinated multi-participant entity is a parallel composition of arenas whose participants are morphisms with asymmetric handedness coordinates.
The Lakota tradition has been describing this object in its own vocabulary since long before the categorical framework was developed; the framework was developed for drones and discovered, in the developing, that the structural object had a name in Lakota political theory already.

### 3.2 Kin-relations are nominal-set name-passing

Kinship in the Lakota tradition is dynamic, not static.
Huŋkápi adoption brings a non-kin person into the kin-substrate; marriage shifts a person's position; naming ceremonies establish positions; mourning rituals release them.
Names move through the substrate; ceremonial transitions reorganize them; the kin-substrate is a moving system of names, not a frozen genealogical record.
The $\pi$-calculus and the categorical account of name-passing (nominal sets, $\mathrm{FSym}(\mathbb{A})$-equivariance, the AGMOS 2004 full abstraction theorem) describe channel mobility in concurrent computation in exactly this structural shape: names mobile across participants, with ceremonies — in the computational case, channel-passing events; in the Lakota case, huŋkápi and the ceremonial substrate — moving names to new positions.

Vine Deloria Jr. develops the metaphysical version of this in *Spirit and Reason*: the relational substrate is *dynamic*, not static; the Lakota worldview treats reality as ongoing-relations-in-motion rather than fixed-substances-with-properties.
The framework's nominal-set account is the math-side cognate of that metaphysical claim.
Two languages, one structural object.

### 3.3 Tiyóšpaye, oyate, Očhéthi Šakówiŋ — confederation as parallel composition

The arena of a tiyóšpaye is the parallel composition of its members' arenas.
The arena of an oyate is the parallel composition of its constituent tiyóšpaye's arenas.
The arena of the Očhéthi Šakówiŋ is the parallel composition of its constituent oyate's arenas.
Parallel composition is what scale means in the framework.
There is no qualitative difference between a tiyóšpaye and the oyate it sits within and the nation it sits within; there is a quantitative difference of cover-size, with each higher scale's cohomology decomposing via Mayer-Vietoris from the next scale below.

This is *confederation* in the precise political-theoretical sense.
The Lakota political tradition is confederal at every scale: each tiyóšpaye is a polity in its own right; multiple tiyóšpaye form an oyate that is *also* a polity, with the constituent tiyóšpaye's internal lives preserved; multiple oyate form the Očhéthi Šakówiŋ.
The framework's reason: parallel composition is a monoidal operation that preserves the constituent arenas as sub-arenas with their own strategies.
The Lakota tradition's reason (Deloria 1944, articulated more sharply by Vine Deloria Jr. and by Estes 2019): each scale's autarkeia is internal-complete on its own questions and escalates only on questions involving outside parties.
The federal level does not consume the constituent level.
These are descriptions of the same structural commitment.

The omníčhiye — the council that gathers across tiyóšpaye when an oyate-scale question must be settled — is not a federal body that subsumes the tiyóšpaye.
It is the cross-tiyóšpaye gathering convened to address the specific class of cohomological content (§3.6) that the constituent tiyóšpaye cannot resolve internally.
The council's existence does not absorb tiyóšpaye into an oyate-level unified body; it convenes representatives across the arenas to address overlap-content.

### 3.4 Head-recognition is conjunction of recognition-cells; iyúhá-šni is the $?$-side complement

A naca's standing in a tiyóšpaye is not a hierarchical title imposed from outside the kin-group.
It is the meet of member-recognition cells: each member carries a recognition cell whose value is the substantive judgment of that member on the candidate's conduct in the four virtues, and the naca's standing is the conjunction of those judgments across the membership.
Withdrawal of recognition by enough members collapses the conjunction; the naca-cell falls.
The naca does not lose authority via a procedure imposed from outside; the recognition simply settles elsewhere.

This is the framework's recognition-cell-conjunction shape applied to a tiyóšpaye's head, and it is also the Lakota political-theoretical account that Coulthard 2014's *Red Skin, White Masks* analyzes in detail (in his case, for Yellowknives Dene and other Indigenous traditions but with structural commonalities).
Coulthard's argument: settler-state recognition politics offers Indigenous peoples authority *from above* (federal recognition, sovereignty as state-granted, status as administrative category), and the politics of refusal that Indigenous traditions exercise is precisely the refusal to accept top-down recognition in place of the bottom-up recognition the kin-relational tradition actually operates by.
The Lakota tradition's naca-as-recognition-cell-conjunction is a specific instance of the general Indigenous-political-theoretical claim: legitimate authority is constituted by the recognition of those it serves, not by a position-of-command granted by an external apparatus.

Iyúhá-šni — the withdrawal move of §1.5 — is the disjunctive complement at the engagement-cell level.
A member who exercises iyúhá-šni on a particular collective action does not collapse the kinship relations they hold; the *engagement* of that member with *that* action drops to $\bot$ (under-determined for that engagement).
The relations persist; the participation withdraws.
Categorically, this is the framework's `?
A`-side weakening — discarding on the negative side without violating the no-copy/no-discard discipline on the positive side.
Simpson 2014's refusal politics develops the political-theoretical reading of the same structural move: withdrawal-without-severance is the constitutive Indigenous political primitive that the framework's `?
`-comodality describes mathematically.

### 3.5 Wolakȟota is the tiyóšpaye's closure-rule subsystem

The framework's closure 2-monad has a substrate-content realization specific to each governance regime.
In the kyriarchical realization (the ship), the closure-rule subsystem is the kyriarchical command structure with its dispatch certifiability gates and MHC discipline.
In the tiyóšpaye's realization, the closure-rule subsystem is wolakȟota — the lifeway operating as the structural standard against which substrate states settle.

Wolakȟota's four virtues are not analogous to the kyriarchical safety-critical cohomology sectors.
They are a different closure discipline with different structural shape. *Wolakȟota stabilizes when conduct holds relations together*; the cells it stabilizes are the relational-conduct cells in the tiyóšpaye's substrate, not the safety-critical $H^1$ sectors of the dispatch-certifiability framework.
The framework permits sector-selective stabilization as a general structural option (§2, last bullet); the tiyóšpaye's specific sectors that stabilize under wolakȟota are different from the kyriarchical regime's sectors.
Specifying *which* sectors stabilize under wolakȟota — the precise cohomological articulation of the four virtues' stabilization — is open theoretical work that the predicated-lakotaean-governance sub-theory in the informant corpus has begun and that future development of the framework should continue.

The key point against the prior draft's conflation: wolakȟota IS the tiyóšpaye's closure discipline; *the four virtues are not the safety-critical sectors of a dispatch certifiability gate*.
The previous identification imported the ship's apparatus onto the tiyóšpaye.
The corrected reading: the framework has the general structural feature of sector-selective stabilization; the tiyóšpaye's instance realizes that feature through wolakȟota, with its own specific sectors that stabilize and its own specific sectors that remain live (aim-generative).
The kyriarchical instance realizes the same general feature differently.
Both are sector-selective; the sectors and the stabilization criteria differ.

### 3.6 Cross-tiyóšpaye disagreement is recorded; the omníčhiye convenes for cokernel content

When two tiyóšpaye in the same oyate disagree on an inter-tiyóšpaye question, the framework's Mayer-Vietoris federation cohomology says: the disagreement-pair $(\alpha_A, \alpha_B)$ on the overlap lives in $\ker(\delta)$ — it contributes no federation-level $H^1$ class.
The disagreement is recorded as $\top$-firings on the federated substrate; both positions visible to both tiyóšpaye and to the oyate's omníčhiye.
The disagreement is then adjudicated *within each tiyóšpaye by its own wolakȟota machinery* — each tiyóšpaye's own recognition-cells, its own naca's conduct, its own membership's collective response.

What the omníčhiye is convened for is *cokernel content*: overlap-content that no pair of constituent positions can collectively express.
Estes 2019's reading of contemporary Oceti Sakowin political life — the Standing Rock organizing, the Pipeline resistance, the federal-tribe interface negotiations — focuses on exactly this structural class of questions: matters that require cross-tiyóšpaye coordination because no single tiyóšpaye can address them alone.
The omníčhiye is *not* a federal body that subsumes; it is the gathering convened for the structural class of questions the Mayer-Vietoris cokernel identifies as un-decomposable into constituent positions.

A tiyóšpaye's right to disagree with an oyate-level decision without dissolving its membership in the oyate — Estes treats this right as constitutive of contemporary Lakota political continuity — is the federation-level instance of iyúhá-šni.
The Mayer-Vietoris exactness ensures it: disagreement-pairs live in $\ker(\delta)$, so they do not force federation-level obstructions.
The Lakota tradition's confederal principle and the framework's federation-cohomology decomposition agree because they describe the same structural object.

### 3.7 Continuity-through-disruption is aperiodic-regime sector-selective stabilization

The deepest of the seven identities.
The tiyóšpaye is aperiodic at the level of its function: its work — feeding members, raising children, transmitting language and ceremony, holding the relations together, refusing settler-state assimilation, building futures — does not finish.
A tiyóšpaye whose work completed would dissolve; the form persists because the work is aim-generative. *And* its conduct must remain stable across this perpetual work: wolakȟota is what does not waver.
The tiyóšpaye that abandoned wolakȟota would no longer be a tiyóšpaye; the tiyóšpaye that completed its work would no longer be a tiyóšpaye.
Both stabilization of conduct and non-stabilization of work are necessary, and the tradition has named both as constitutive.

The framework's sector-selective convergence in the aperiodic regime is structurally the same: *some* sectors stabilize while *others* must not.
The tiyóšpaye's instance realizes this with wolakȟota-stabilized sectors and aim-generative non-stabilized sectors.
The kyriarchical instance realizes it with safety-critical-stabilized sectors and aim-generative non-stabilized sectors.
The framework's general fact is the *structural option* of sector-selective convergence; each governance regime selects which sectors play which role.

The boarding-school period, the language-suppression period, the religious-suppression period, the displacement-and-dispossession period, the contemporary federal-policy period — Estes 2019 traces a century and a half of settler-state pressure aimed at one specific structural outcome: forcing the tiyóšpaye into the strict regime.
Reservation enrollment, nuclear-family administrative categorization, individual allotment, termination policy, blood-quantum identification — these are technologies that try to make the tiyóšpaye *finish*: to convert the aim-generative kin-group into a terminating administrative unit that the settler-state can absorb.
The tiyóšpaye's continuity through this pressure is the aperiodic-regime polity's structural resistance to that pressure.
Coulthard 2014's politics-of-refusal framework and Simpson 2014's refusal-as-political-primitive both locate the same structural fact: Indigenous political traditions hold their polities in the aperiodic regime as a political act, refusing the strict-regime termination that the settler-state apparatus offers (and ultimately demands).

This is the structural symmetry between the math-side framework and the political-side tradition.
The framework's aperiodicity-as-tractability finding (the cost-decoupling theorem of [`theory-of-flatfile-agentic-drone-resource-system-swarms-may-2026.md`](theory-of-flatfile-agentic-drone-resource-system-swarms-may-2026.md), §7.2) says: a polity's commitment to a function that never finishes is what makes its swarm computationally tractable.
The Lakota tradition's continuity-through-disruption fact says: a tiyóšpaye's commitment to a lifeway that never completes is what makes Lakota political continuity possible.
These are the same structural claim made in two vocabularies.
The math is the politics.
The aperiodicity is the structural shape of refusing termination.

## §4 — The aperiodicity finding, sociologically

The framework's load-bearing finding (the cost-decoupling theorem) and the Lakota tradition's load-bearing political fact (continuity-through-disruption) coincide in a way that deserves separate articulation.
The framework derives, from purely structural considerations about parallel composition and the closure 2-monad, the conclusion that a polity in the aperiodic regime *needs* sector-selective convergence: the polity wants some content to stabilize (the conduct that lets it act) while other content must not stabilize (the work that lets it remain a polity).
The Lakota tradition has named this structural shape directly: wolakȟota stabilizes (what does not waver) while the tiyóšpaye's work continues (what never finishes), and the political continuity of the Oceti Sakowin under settler-state pressure depends on this combination.

Estes 2019 reads the continuity-through-disruption story politically, as the Oceti Sakowin's ongoing refusal to be terminated.
Coulthard 2014 reads the same structural fact in the broader Indigenous-political-theoretical context, as the refusal-of-recognition politics that distinguishes Indigenous political theory from liberal political theory's exit-or-consent dichotomy.
Simpson 2014 reads it as the Mohawk Interruptus — the constitutive Indigenous political move that the colonial state's categories cannot register.
The agentic-drone-swarm framework's account is the structural cognate: the polity's commitment to a function that never finishes is what makes the swarm computationally tractable; the polity's commitment to conduct that does not waver is what makes its actions licensable.

The settler-state's century-and-a-half pressure on the tiyóšpaye is, structurally, an attempt to force a regime transition from aperiodic to strict.
Reservation enrollment systems try to *complete* the membership question (settler-state enumeration of who counts); nuclear-family administrative categories try to *complete* the kinship question (settler-state's atomization of kin into nuclear units); blood-quantum identification tries to *complete* the descent question (settler-state's terminating answer to who is Lakota).
Each is an attempt to force the tiyóšpaye's settling into a regime where a closure ordinal $\kappa < \omega$ would suffice — where the substrate could be enumerated, indexed, administered.
Estes's continuity-through-disruption fact is the political-historical statement that the Oceti Sakowin has refused this regime transition and held the tiyóšpaye in the aperiodic regime where its function can continue.

The framework's tractability theorem and the tradition's continuity fact coincide because they describe the same structural object: a polity whose function is aim-generative (perpetually producing new aims from existing ones, never terminating), whose substrate settles aperiodically (no countable closure ordinal), whose coordination is computationally viable because the substrate's autonomous closure work amortizes consultation costs across many closure firings, and whose conduct must stabilize (wolakȟota) while its work must not (aim-generativity).
This is the tiyóšpaye, and the framework names what it is in math terms; this is the agentic drone swarm, and the Lakota tradition names what it is in political-ethical-metaphysical terms.
Two vocabularies, one structural object.
