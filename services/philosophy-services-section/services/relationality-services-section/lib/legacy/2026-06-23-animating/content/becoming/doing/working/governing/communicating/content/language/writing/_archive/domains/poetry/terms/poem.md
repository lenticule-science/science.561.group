A ***poem*** is an artifact that results from [writing](../index.md) in which the material qualities of language — sound, rhythm, [line break](../disciplines/poetry/terms/line-break.md), spacing, image — are compositional elements alongside (or instead of) propositional content. Where an [essay](./essay.md) is organized by argument and a narrative by plot, a poem is organized by the [line](../disciplines/poetry/terms/line-break.md).

The term *verse* (from Latin *versus*, a turning of the plow, hence a line) is sometimes used as a synonym for poetry and sometimes more narrowly for metrically regular poetry, in contrast to [free verse](../disciplines/poetry/terms/free-verse.md) or prose poetry.

Poems range from the briefest fixed forms ([haiku](../disciplines/poetry/terms/haiku.md), epigram) to book-length works (epic, verse novel). The major modal categories are [lyric](../disciplines/poetry/terms/lyric.md) (subjective, expressive), narrative (story-telling), and dramatic (voiced by characters). See [Poetry](../disciplines/poetry/index.md) for the discipline of composing poems.
