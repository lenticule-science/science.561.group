This is a working procedure for identifying [moral displacement](../terms/moral-displacement.md) in a specific media artifact — a film, a recruitment video, a wellness campaign, a journalism piece, an advertisement, a platform-native short.
Moral displacement is the rerouting of moral attention from a structural condition the artifact acknowledges onto an individual question about character, choice, attitude, or personal practice.
The procedure walks through how to recognize the operation in a concrete case.

This how-to assumes familiarity with the term [moral displacement](../terms/moral-displacement.md), the broader analytical layer in [introduction to moral physics](./introduction-to-moral-physics.md), and the related operations [californication](../terms/californication.md) and [harm governance](../terms/harm-governance.md).

## What you'll need

- The artifact itself, accessible for repeated viewing or reading.
- A way to take notes.
- About 30–60 minutes for a single short artifact; longer for feature-length material.
- Background knowledge of the structural condition the artifact addresses.
  The procedure cannot be completed without this.
  Moral displacement is recognizable as the gap between the structural condition (which you must already understand) and the personal question the artifact substitutes for it.
  If you do not already have a reasonable grasp of the structural condition, read about it before proceeding.

## Step 1: Identify the structural condition the artifact acknowledges

Watch, read, or examine the artifact carefully.
What structural condition does it acknowledge?
The condition will usually appear early in the artifact — in an opening voiceover, a framing scene, an establishing shot, a problem statement.
The acknowledgment is often surprisingly direct.

Examples of structural conditions artifacts commonly acknowledge:

- That soldiers are sent into wars whose moral character is uncertain.
- That workers are exhausted by extractive labor regimes.
- That platform users are subjected to influence operations.
- That climate change is destroying the conditions of social life.
- That the institutions audiences live inside have failed.
- That the protagonist's industry, profession, or community is structurally compromised.

Write down the structural condition the artifact acknowledges.
Be specific.
Not "things are bad" but "the U.S. military's recent campaigns have ambiguous moral standing"; not "work is hard" but "the platform-economy labor regime extracts unsustainable amounts of attention from its workers."

If the artifact does not acknowledge a structural condition at all, moral displacement is not the right framework — that artifact is operating in a different register (denial, depiction without analysis, pure entertainment in a surface sense).
Moral displacement specifically requires the acknowledgment.

## Step 2: Identify the moral question the artifact actually attends to

Now identify the moral question the artifact is actually inviting the viewer to attend to.
This is rarely the structural question.
It is usually a personal question that arises *given* the structural condition.

Common substitutions:

- *Given* that war is morally compromised, **does the protagonist personally find meaning in service?**
- *Given* that work is exhausting, **does the worker have a sustainable personal practice?**
- *Given* that influence operations exist, **are you clever enough to be the one doing them?**
- *Given* that climate change is happening, **how is the protagonist personally processing their grief?**
- *Given* that institutions have failed, **does the hero personally maintain integrity?**

Write down the personal question the artifact attends to.
Note the rhetorical structure: the artifact often *uses* the structural acknowledgment to frame the personal question, treating the structure as the *condition under which* the personal question becomes interesting.
The structural acknowledgment is doing rhetorical work — it is establishing the seriousness of the situation in which the personal question is being asked.
This makes the personal question feel substantive even when it has displaced the structural one.

## Step 3: Compare

Hold the structural question and the personal question side by side.
The structural question is the one the structural condition raises.
(If soldiers are sent into morally compromised wars, the structural question is whether the apparatus that sends them should exist in this form, what would have to change, who is accountable.)
The personal question is the one the artifact is actually attending to.
(Whether the protagonist personally finds meaning.)

Note the difference.
The structural question and the personal question are *not* answers to each other.
A protagonist personally finding meaning does not address whether the apparatus should exist; the apparatus continuing to exist does not depend on whether the protagonist personally finds meaning.
The two questions are operating at different levels and in different registers.

The gap between them is moral displacement.
The wider the gap, the more aggressive the displacement.

## Step 4: Track the artifact's treatment of the structural question

Now track what the artifact does with the structural question once it has been acknowledged.
The structural condition does not usually disappear — it remains in the frame.
What does the artifact do with it?

Common patterns:

- **Background residence.** The structural condition becomes the scenery of the personal drama.
  It is referenced when needed for atmospheric weight but does not actually constrain the narrative.
- **Repeated acknowledgment without engagement.** The structural condition is named several times across the artifact, in slightly different language, in a way that performs gravity but does not advance any analysis or change any choice.
- **Implicit treatment as natural.** The structural condition is presented as a feature of the world rather than as a result of decisions and arrangements that could be otherwise.
  It is the weather, not a policy.
- **Casting the structural condition as a personal challenge.** The structural condition becomes the *test* the protagonist's personal character is being measured against.
  The condition has been transformed into setting for the personal narrative.

Write down which of these (or which other pattern) describes the artifact's treatment.
The pattern is itself diagnostic.

## Step 5: Identify the unaskable question

Now identify what the artifact *cannot ask* under its displacement.
This is often the most useful step.
It surfaces the political work the displacement is doing.

The unaskable question is usually a structural question with teeth — a question that, if asked seriously, would require the artifact to take up the structural condition rather than route attention away from it.
Questions of this kind:

- Should this apparatus exist in its current form?
- What would have to change for this condition to be different?
- Who benefits from this condition continuing?
- What is the audience's responsibility, beyond the level of personal practice?
- What politics, not just personal posture, is implied by this analysis?

Write down the unaskable question.
The artifact will often have *some* version of this question circulating in its frame, but at a register that has been defanged — gestured at without being followed up, named in a way that signals seriousness without inviting answer.

The question, asked in its real form, is what moral displacement has displaced.

## Step 6: Identify the affective payoff

Moral displacement does not just suppress the structural question; it offers an affective reward for accepting the substitution.
Identify the reward.

Common rewards:

- The audience leaves the artifact feeling that they have engaged with a serious matter and emerged with a workable response (which is the personal practice the artifact recommended).
- The audience identifies with a protagonist whose personal character is admirable, and feels that identification has been morally substantive.
- The audience experiences the artifact as having delivered honesty (because the structural acknowledgment was made openly), without being asked to do anything that would change the structure.
- The audience experiences a sense of insider access — they have been let in on the difficulty of the structural condition and given a sophisticated personal response to it.

Write down the affective reward the artifact offers.
The reward is what makes the displacement palatable; without it, audiences would notice the substitution.
With it, the substitution feels like the whole experience was the right experience to have.

## Step 7: Track the audience's positioning

Note where the artifact positions the viewer in the moral economy it has produced.

The standard positioning of moral displacement: the viewer is positioned as a *moral evaluator of the protagonist's personal answer*.
The viewer's job is to judge whether the protagonist's personal practice, integrity, attitude, or character is admirable.
The viewer's own moral agency is engaged at exactly the level the artifact has displaced the question to: the personal level.

This is part of why moral displacement is so effective.
The viewer is not passive; the viewer is *actively engaged in moral evaluation*.
The engagement is real.
It is just engaged at the displaced level.

Note this positioning explicitly.
Write down: *the viewer is invited to morally evaluate X*.
X is almost always a personal answer.

## Step 8: Write the finding

Compose a short finding (a few sentences) that names:

- The structural condition the artifact acknowledged.
- The personal question it substituted.
- The unaskable structural question its displacement defends against.
- The affective reward it offers.
- The level at which the viewer's moral agency is engaged.

This is the moral-displacement reading of the artifact.

## A worked example, in compressed form

Consider a recruitment video for an elite U.S. military unit that openly acknowledges the unit conducts psychological-influence operations and dramatizes the moral weight of doing so.

- **Structural condition acknowledged.** The U.S. military conducts psychological-influence operations against foreign and domestic information environments.
- **Personal question substituted.** Are *you*, the viewer, the kind of intelligent, perceptive, complex person who could thrive inside this unit?
- **Treatment of the structural question.** Treated as the dramatic backdrop that makes the personal question meaningful.
  Acknowledged repeatedly in voiceover, never followed up with any analysis.
- **Unaskable question.** Should this apparatus exist?
  What is the political relation between U.S. influence operations and the audiences they target?
  How is the production of this very recruitment video itself an instance of the apparatus the video acknowledges?
- **Affective reward.** A sense of being addressed as sophisticated and morally serious; an invitation into a sophisticated insider community whose work is morally weighty.
- **Viewer positioning.** Asked to evaluate, at the level of personal aspiration, whether one is the kind of person who belongs.

The recruitment video is performing moral displacement: the structural condition is acknowledged with surprising openness, but the moral attention this acknowledgment ought to mobilize is rerouted onto a personal question about audience worthiness.

## Common difficulties

**The artifact really does engage the structural question.** Sometimes what looks like moral displacement is a sincere engagement with the structural condition, with the personal narrative serving as illustration rather than substitution.
The diagnostic is whether the artifact's analysis of the structural condition would survive if the personal narrative were removed.
If yes, the artifact is doing structural work and using the personal as illustration.
If the analysis collapses without the personal narrative, displacement is operating.

**Multiple displacements are occurring at once.** Some artifacts displace at several levels — structural to institutional, institutional to personal, personal to affective.
The procedure works for each level.
Run it more than once if needed.

**The "structural" condition the artifact acknowledges is itself already displaced.** Some artifacts acknowledge a thinned-down version of the structural condition (e.g., "soldiers face hard challenges" rather than "the U.S. military's recent campaigns have ambiguous moral standing").
The thinning is itself a kind of displacement, prior to the rerouting onto the personal.
Note it as a separate finding.

## What this procedure is not

This procedure does not produce a moral judgment of the artifact's makers.
It produces an analytical reading of what the artifact is doing.
The makers may have produced a moral-displacing artifact deliberately or because they were trained inside a moral-physics regime in which moral displacement is the available form for serious work.
Both are findings.
Neither is a personal indictment.
The reading is for understanding the operation, which is a precondition for choosing whether and where to deploy or refuse it.
