# Replay buffer

A **replay buffer** is a finite-capacity store of past
state-action-reward-next-state transitions $(s, a, r, s')$ from which
training mini-batches are drawn. Introduced by Lin (1992); central to
Deep Q-Networks (Mnih et al. 2015) and to most off-policy deep-RL
algorithms.

## Specification

- **Capacity** $N$ — the maximum number of transitions stored. When
  full, the oldest transition is overwritten (FIFO; standard) or the
  lowest-priority transition is overwritten (prioritized variants).
- **Insertion**: at each environment step, append the new transition.
- **Sampling**: draw a mini-batch of $B$ transitions, either uniformly
  (uniform replay) or with probability proportional to TD-error
  magnitude (prioritized replay; Schaul et al. 2016).

## Why replay buffers exist

Two engineering motivations and one structural motivation:

**Decorrelation.** Successive transitions in a single trajectory are
strongly correlated. SGD on correlated minibatches has higher gradient
noise and worse convergence behavior. Sampling uniformly from a buffer
of many trajectories breaks the correlation.

**Sample efficiency.** Each environment step is reused many times
across training updates rather than once.

**Off-policy transformation.** A buffer storing transitions from many
past behavior policies turns the problem into an off-policy learning
problem. This is the structural reason for the
[deadly-triad](teaching/explanations/deadly-triad.md): replay buffers force
off-policy learning, which combined with bootstrapping and function
approximation breaks convergence guarantees.

## Variants

**Prioritized replay** (Schaul et al. 2016). Sample with probability
$\propto |\delta|^\alpha$ for TD-error $\delta$. Compensate the resulting
bias with importance weights.

**Hindsight replay** (Andrychowicz et al. 2017). For goal-conditioned
tasks, relabel failed trajectories with the goal that *was* achieved.
Effective for sparse-reward environments.

**Distributional / quantile buffers**: store distributional information
about transitions to support distributional RL algorithms.

## Limits

A replay buffer of capacity $N$ implicitly assumes that transitions
collected $N$ steps ago are still relevant for the current policy's
training. When the policy changes substantially, older transitions are
generated by a behavior policy very different from the current target
policy, and importance ratios become unstable. Capacity must therefore
be tuned to the rate of policy change.
