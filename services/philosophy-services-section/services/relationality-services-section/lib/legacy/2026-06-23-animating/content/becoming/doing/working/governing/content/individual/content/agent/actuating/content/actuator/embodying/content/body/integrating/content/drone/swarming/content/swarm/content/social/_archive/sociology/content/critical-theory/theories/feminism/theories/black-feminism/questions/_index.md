Open work for the `black-feminism` subdomain.
The page holds two registers of question: the **substantive** questions the tradition itself is still working out, and the **structural** curation questions about how the subdomain should be organized in this library.

## Substantive questions the tradition is working out

These are live questions in the contemporary literature, not questions the subdomain has settled.

### On intersectionality

- **Intersectionality as analytic vs. intersectionality as identity description.** The analytic Crenshaw introduced and Collins systematized is a structural analysis of how systems of domination interlock.
  The reception has often reduced it to a demographic checklist of identity categories.
  The contemporary literature (notably Collins, Jennifer Nash, Sirma Bilge, Vivian May) is largely an argument over what intersectionality is and how to recover its analytical force from its institutional dilution.
  The subdomain treats this as live.
- **Intersectionality and political economy.** How fully does intersectionality, as currently developed, integrate analysis of capital accumulation?
  The Combahee statement is explicitly socialist; some contemporary intersectional work has lost that thread.
  The question of how to articulate intersectionality with the racial-capitalism analytic in the sibling [`black-radical-tradition`](../../../../black-radical-tradition/_index.md) subdomain is open.

### On the relation to other Black radical traditions

- **Black feminism and the Black radical tradition.** The two subdomains share figures (Davis, Spillers, Hartman) and analytical resources, but Black feminism foregrounds the gendered-sexual constitution of the structure that the BRT analyzes principally as racial capitalism.
  Whether the two are best read as complementary, as one nested in the other, or as two distinct lineages with overlapping membership is debated.
  The BRT's [`questions/`](../../../../black-radical-tradition/questions/_index.md) page raises the same question from its side.
- **Black feminism and Black queer studies.** Substantial overlap (Lorde, Spillers, Cathy Cohen, Roderick Ferguson, the Combahee Collective itself was Black lesbian feminist) but the subdomain boundaries have not been worked out.
  The relation to the sibling [`queer`](../../../../queer/_index.md) subdomain is curatorial work pending.

### On standpoint and epistemology

- **Standpoint theory after the critique of essentialism.** Collins's standpoint epistemology has been criticized for risks of essentializing Black women's experience.
  Collins's response (in *Fighting Words* and later) is that standpoint is structural-positional rather than essentialist.
  The methodological question — how to take standpoint seriously without essentializing — remains live.
- **The wayward and the archive.** [Hartman](../figures/saidiya-hartman.md)'s critical fabulation has been taken up widely and also contested: where the line between speculative reconstruction and projection sits is itself a methodological question.
  The contemporary methodological literature (Marisa Fuentes, Jennifer Morgan, Tiya Miles) extends and complicates Hartman's framing.

### On transnationalism and the diaspora

- **Black feminism beyond the U.S.** The strongest theoretical work in the subdomain currently centers U.S. Black feminism.
  The transnational Black-feminist literature (Carole Boyce Davies, Hazel Carby, Beverley Bryan / Stella Dadzie / Suzanne Scafe in the U.K., Lélia Gonzalez in Brazil, the Caribbean Association for Feminist Research and Action) is uneven in the subdomain's current vocabulary.
  A future pass should extend the figure set and the analytical vocabulary to include the diasporic and global tradition more fully.
- **Pan-Africanism, Afrofuturism, and Black-feminist futures.** The relation between Black feminism and the Pan-African and Afrofuturist literatures (from Sun Ra to Octavia Butler to the contemporary Afrofuturism studies field) is a research direction the subdomain has not yet taken up.

### On contemporary uptake and risk

- **The risk of recuperation.** "Intersectionality," "standpoint," and "self-care" (Lorde's term, in *A Burst of Light*) have circulated widely outside the contexts where they were developed and have been recuperated by institutions whose structural commitments the concepts were designed to critique.
  The contemporary literature's diagnosis of the dilution of intersectionality, in particular, is part of the subdomain's working methodological problem.
  How to use the concepts without participating in their hollowing is itself an open question.

## Structural / curation questions

The discipline-level open work for the `black-feminism` subdomain — questions whose answers, once worked out, would bring the subdomain closer to theory status under [theory-discipline.md](../../../../../../../../../science/theories/information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/markdown-vault-knowledge-system/theories/predicated-markdown-vault-knowledge-system/specifications/theory-discipline.md).

- **Which figures should the subdomain add to its figure set?** The current pass adds pages for the [Combahee Collective](../figures/combahee-river-collective.md), [Lorde](../figures/audre-lorde.md), [hooks](../figures/bell-hooks.md), [Collins](../figures/patricia-hill-collins.md), [Spillers](../figures/hortense-spillers.md), and [Hartman](../figures/saidiya-hartman.md).
  A next pass should consider Sojourner Truth, Anna Julia Cooper, Ida B.
  Wells, Claudia Jones, Frances Beal, Barbara Smith, Toni Cade Bambara, Kimberlé Crenshaw, [Sylvia Wynter](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/sylvia-wynter.md), Christina Sharpe, Jennifer Nash, and Carole Boyce Davies.
- **Which works should the subdomain promote from inline mention to reference notes?** The current pass adds reference notes for the four canonical works named in the front door's frontmatter (Combahee 1977, Lorde 1984, Collins 1990, Spillers 1987).
  A next pass should add reference notes for hooks 1981 (*Ain't I a Woman*), Crenshaw 1989 / 1991 (the foundational intersectionality essays), Davis 1981 (*Women, Race & Class*), Hartman 2008 ("Venus in Two Acts") and Hartman 2019 (*Wayward Lives*).
- **Which figures cited here lack reference notes at [`library/references/people/`](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/_index.md)?** The current pass surfaces this for the Combahee Collective (no per-author notes for Barbara Smith, Beverly Smith, or Demita Frazier), Audre Lorde, Patricia Hill Collins, and Saidiya Hartman.
  Adding those is a follow-up.
- **What is the relation between `black-feminism` and the sibling [`black-radical-tradition`](../../../../black-radical-tradition/_index.md) subdomain?** The two carry overlapping figure sets and analytical resources.
  The question of where the boundary sits and how the cross-references should be structured is curatorial work that should be worked out across both subdomains' next passes.
- **What is the relation between this subdomain's vocabulary ([`terms/`](../terms/_index.md)) and the discipline's broader concept layer ([`sociology/concepts/`](../../../../../../teaching/explanations/_index.md))?** Where the same analytical role is occupied by a term here and a concept at the discipline level, the relation should be made explicit.
