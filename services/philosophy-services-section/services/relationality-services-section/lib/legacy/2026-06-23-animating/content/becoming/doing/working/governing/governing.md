The shared field of working is open and revisable — anyone may contribute, and what is contributed can be read, weighed, and overturned. **Governing** is what happens when that open field is brought under a bound: a settled order of rules that caps the work, resolves its conflicts, and holds.
Governing is the fourth and most settled moment of existence — where the unbounded process of working reaches a fixed order it can persist under.

## Bounded order over a shared field

Governance is not a fifth field added beside working; it is working's own field *settled into rule*.
Elinor Ostrom's *Governing the Commons* shows the move in its plainest form: a community sharing a common resource is a working field — many actors drawing on and adding to one medium — and what keeps it from exhaustion or deadlock is a settled set of rules the community itself crafts and enforces: who may take what, who adjudicates, how disputes close.
Governance is exactly this: the bound a shared field places on itself so that work can continue.
Where political philosophy since Hobbes asks how a multitude becomes one body able to act and endure, the answer named here is governing — the settling of an open plurality into a bounded, law-bearing order.

This is the *finite* turn in the plainest sense: a process that was open and ongoing reaches a fixed point, a state that no longer revises under the contributions in hand.
The law is the cap; what it caps is the work.

## The individual face

Read as agency, governing wears the face of the **individual**.
The order it settles is also the place where a someone is finally bounded into a distinct, recognized self — the *person* of law, the one to whom acts are imputed and rights and duties attach.
Governance is where agency meets law: where the bounded individual is recognized, capped, or denied — "the denial of agency is one of the operations of power."
So the settled order and the settled self are two faces of one fixed point: an order is bounded by recognizing its members as individuals, and an individual is bounded by standing under an order.

## What it generates

Governing generates governance, and as its agency-face it settles into the [individual](../library/individual/docs/individual.md) — one bounded someone, glued out of the shared field and persisting under law.
Its proper acts are [adjudication](../library/adjudication/docs/adjudication.md), the closing of conflicting contributions into a held verdict, and [knowledge](../library/knowledge/docs/knowledge.md), the claim settled past further revision.
