# Bearer tokens are bound evidence vectors, not derivation attestations

**Claim.** A [bearer token](teaching/explanations/bearer-token.md) — opaque session token, JWT, PASETO, OAuth access token, or signed cookie — is structurally an [evidence vector](../../teaching/explanations/evidence-vector.md) per [evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation](../../../theorems/evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation.md): a tuple of canonical-form digests over (issuer, subject, audience, scope, freshness window, nonce) with a [signature binding to identity](../../../theorems/signature-binds-witness-to-identity.md). Verifying a bearer token is *canonical-form recompute and signature-check* against the trusted issuer's [identity key](../../teaching/explanations/identity-key.md). The verification *binds* the request to the issuer's claim — it does not *attest* that the issuer's underlying authorization decision was sound. The two attestation strengths are structurally distinct, and conflating them is the substrate's most common authorization-discipline failure.

## Two representational shapes for the same evidence vector

Bearer tokens come in two structural shapes that *differ in where the canonical-form bytes live* but agree in the underlying evidence-vector content:

- **Self-contained tokens** (JWT, PASETO, signed cookies, Macaroons, Biscuits). The canonical-form bytes of each evidence-vector entry — issuer, subject, audience, scope, freshness window, nonce — are encoded *in the token itself* (typically as base64url-canonicalized JSON or CBOR). The signature over the encoded vector is also in the token. The verifier reconstructs the evidence vector from the token's bytes alone and checks the signature against the issuer's public [identity-key](../../teaching/explanations/identity-key.md). No substrate-side state is consulted for the binding check itself (revocation may consult external state, but binding does not).
- **Handle tokens** (opaque session tokens, OAuth opaque access tokens). The token is a *handle* — typically a high-entropy random string — that names a substrate-recorded [principal-session](../../teaching/explanations/principal-session.md) entry; the evidence-vector entries live in that substrate entry, not in the token. Verification dereferences the handle to the entry and reads the canonical-form bytes from the entry. The substrate's own audit discipline plays the role of the signature: the entry's existence and integrity at the recorded substrate state is what the substrate vouches for.

Both shapes are evidence vectors per [evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation](../../../theorems/evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation.md); they differ in *the locus of the canonical-form bytes* and *the binding mechanism* (cryptographic signature vs substrate-recorded entry-integrity). The structural claims of this theorem hold uniformly across both shapes; differences in revocation discipline and federation portability follow from the locus difference and are noted below.

## Why the binding holds

Per [evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation](../../../theorems/evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation.md), an evidence vector is a tuple of canonical-form-hash entries that, by [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../theorems/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md), uniquely identifies the canonical-form bytes of each component. A bearer token's structural decomposition matches this exactly (for self-contained tokens, the bytes are in the token; for handle tokens, the bytes are in the dereferenced session record):

- **Issuer (`iss` in JWT, server-known for opaque tokens).** Canonical-form-hash of the issuer's identifier (substrate principal-record reference, OAuth issuer URL).
- **Subject (`sub`, the principal the token represents).** Canonical-form-hash of the subject's principal-record reference.
- **Audience (`aud`, the substrate the token is addressed to).** Canonical-form-hash of the substrate's audience identifier; tokens with `aud` not matching the verifier are refused.
- **Scope (`scope`, the granted capabilities).** Canonical-form-hash of the capability claims the issuer attached.
- **Freshness window (`iat`, `exp`).** Substrate-clock ordinals bounding the token's necessity along the time-accessibility relation per [cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility](cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility.md). Tokens past `exp` are *outside the issuer's necessity claim* and the substrate refuses them.
- **Nonce (`jti`).** Canonical-form-hash of a per-issuance random handle, providing replay-distinguishability.
- **Signature (self-contained tokens) or substrate-recorded entry (handle tokens).** Per [signature-binds-witness-to-identity](../../../theorems/signature-binds-witness-to-identity.md), self-contained tokens carry a signature over the canonical-form encoding of the entire vector using the issuer's [identity key](../../teaching/explanations/identity-key.md); handle tokens are bound by the substrate's own recording discipline — the substrate's [witness](../../../objects/witness.md) chain over the session entry is the binding analogue. In both shapes, the binding artefact (signature or substrate-entry) is what makes the canonical-form bytes recipient-verifiable.

The two shapes differ in *federation portability*: a self-contained token can be verified by any holder of the issuer's public identity-key, including substrate-instances that have never communicated with the issuer; a handle token can only be verified by a substrate-instance with access to the issuing substrate's session store (directly or via an introspection protocol per RFC 7662). The choice between shapes is an engineering trade-off between revocation latency (handle tokens revoke instantly via session-record deletion; self-contained tokens revoke only at `exp` or via additional revocation-list lookup) and verification independence (self-contained tokens do not require the issuer to be online at verification time; handle tokens do).

Per [evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation](../../../theorems/evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation.md), verification of this vector is purely canonical-form recompute and hash/signature comparison. A verifier that obtains the issuer's public identity-key, reconstructs the canonical-form encoding of (header + payload), and confirms the signature has *bound* the request to the issuer's claim: the issuer committed, at the moment of token issuance, to those exact canonical-form bytes. By the underlying signature's existential-unforgeability, only a holder of the issuer's signing key could have produced that signature; per [identity-key](../../teaching/explanations/identity-key.md), the issuer's identity is bound to the key.

The opaque-session-token case is the same structure with one indirection: instead of carrying the canonical-form bytes inline, the token is a handle into a substrate-recorded [principal-session](../../teaching/explanations/principal-session.md) record whose contents are the evidence vector. The substrate's lookup of the session record is the substrate's canonical-form recompute; the session record's existence and integrity are the substrate's signature-equivalent (the substrate vouches for what it stored, by its own audit discipline).

## Why the binding does not attest derivation

The bearer token attests *that the issuer said this*. It does not attest *that what the issuer said is justified*. Concretely, the verifier sees `scope: ["transfer:funds"]` on a token signed by issuer $I$, and confirms that $I$ committed to that scope claim. The verifier does *not* learn:

- That $I$ verified the principal's identity correctly before issuing the token (the issuer's authentication discipline).
- That $I$ had authority to grant `transfer:funds` to this principal (the issuer's authorization discipline).
- That $I$'s policy at issuance time still holds at request time (the issuer's revocation discipline).
- That $I$ itself is trusted by the substrate to grant that scope (the verifier's federation-trust discipline).

These are the *derivation attestations*. They are not in the evidence vector. The verifier must obtain them separately:

- **Issuer trust.** Per [federation-trust-bilattice-graded](../../../theorems/federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md), the verifier's trust in $I$ is a `FOUR`-valued accessibility cell. The token's binding is in $\mathbf{t}$ only when *both* signature-verification succeeds (binding) *and* $R_{\mathrm{fed}}(\mathrm{this}, I) = \mathbf{t}$ (trust). A token bound to a $\bot$-trust issuer is not authorization; a token bound to a $\mathbf{f}$-trust issuer is grounds for refusal regardless of signature validity.
- **Revocation.** The issuer may have revoked the token after issuance (added it to a revocation list, expired the principal's session). The token's `exp` provides a self-contained necessity-window bound; per [cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility](cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility.md), within the window the issuer has committed to the token's validity, but the verifier may *additionally* consult a revocation source — a revocation list, an introspection endpoint per RFC 7662 — that may strengthen the verifier's verdict from $\bot$ (have-not-checked) to $\mathbf{t}$ or $\mathbf{f}$.
- **Authorization decision.** The verifier confirms that the token's `scope` covers the requested operation. This is a substrate-local check against the verifier's policy; the token only contributes the bound scope claim.

## The four-corner verification verdict

Composing token binding, issuer trust, freshness, and revocation produces a `FOUR`-valued verdict on the token's authorization for this request:

- $\mathbf{t}$ — *valid authorization*: signature binds, issuer trusted ($\mathbf{t}$), within freshness window, not revoked, scope covers operation. Wire: substrate proceeds with the request under the principal's authority.
- $\mathbf{f}$ — *explicit refusal*: signature binds but issuer is denied ($\mathbf{f}$), or the token is past `exp`, or the token is on a revocation list, or the scope does not cover the operation. Wire: `401 Unauthorized` (signature problem, freshness, revocation) or `403 Forbidden` (scope insufficient).
- $\bot$ — *binding failure*: signature does not verify, the token is malformed, the issuer is unknown ($\bot$-trust). Wire: `401 Unauthorized` with `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer error="invalid_token"`.
- $\top$ — *contested*: the issuer's trust is at $\top$ (federation-disputed), or two revocation sources disagree, or the token is bound but the substrate's revocation list is in $\top$ for this token. Wire: `5xx` until [repair-as-top-cell-resolution](../../../theorems/repair-as-top-cell-resolution.md) resolves.

These four match exactly the [status-code-classes-as-four-bilattice-values](status-code-classes-as-four-bilattice-values.md) decomposition of HTTP responses, applied to the authorization sub-question.

## Engineering consequence

- **JWT verification is canonical-form discipline.** A JWT's signature is over the base64url-encoded canonical form of (header || "." || payload). The substrate's canonical-form discipline must agree with the issuer's: identical JSON canonicalization (key ordering, whitespace, number formatting), identical base64url alphabet, identical signing algorithm. Per [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../theorems/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md), divergence in canonical-form discipline produces verification failures even on legitimate tokens.
- **`alg: none` is a binding-discipline failure, not a feature.** A JWT with `alg: none` carries no signature; the evidence vector is structurally absent. Implementations that admit such tokens have effectively replaced binding with $\bot$-trust on every issuer claim. Per the substrate's discipline, `alg: none` tokens are refused unconditionally.
- **Confused-deputy attacks are issuer-trust failures.** A token issued by issuer $I_1$ for audience $A_1$ being accepted by audience $A_2$ (because $A_2$ shares the trust-list with $A_1$) is the substrate's failure to distinguish *this verifier's* `aud` cell from *another verifier's*. The `aud` claim is the canonical-form bytes that bind the token to a specific audience; the substrate must verify `aud == self`, not just `aud ∈ trust-list`.
- **Token exchange composes the binding.** OAuth token exchange (RFC 8693) produces a new token whose evidence vector cites the prior token: the new token's issuer is the exchange server, the new subject inherits the prior subject, the new scope is the exchange-policy's restriction. Per [evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation](../../../theorems/evidence-vector-as-canonical-binding-attestation.md), this is canonical evidence-vector composition; the audit chain (per [witness-chain-as-proof-object](../../../theorems/witness-chain-as-proof-object.md)) preserves the binding history.
- **Macaroons / Biscuits add caveat-attenuation as additional vector entries.** A macaroon or biscuit is an evidence vector with caveat entries (third-party caveats, predicate caveats) that further restrict the token's authorization. Each caveat is a canonical-form-hash entry; verification recomputes each caveat's hash and confirms the substrate's local state satisfies the caveat predicate. This is *additional* to the issuer's binding, not a replacement.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Treating signature validity as authorization sufficiency.** A substrate that admits any request bearing a valid signature from any issuer in its trust list — without checking `aud`, `exp`, scope, or revocation — has elided the derivation-attestation step entirely. The signature only attests *that the issuer said this*; the substrate must additionally check *that what the issuer said authorizes the request*.
- **`exp`-less tokens.** A bearer token without an explicit freshness window is the issuer's claim that the binding holds *forever*. Per [cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility](cache-control-as-modal-necessity-along-time-accessibility.md), this is a maximally-strong necessity claim that is rarely the issuer's actual intent. Conformant issuers always set `exp`; conformant verifiers refuse `exp`-less tokens unless an out-of-band agreement justifies the unbounded necessity (e.g., long-lived API keys with separate revocation discipline).
- **Storing bearer tokens unencrypted at rest in the substrate.** A bearer token in possession is the credential; the substrate that records bearer tokens it has *seen* (e.g., logging request `Authorization` headers) is recording credentials. Per [encryption](../../teaching/explanations/encryption.md) and [identity-key-custody](../../teaching/explanations/identity-key-custody.md), tokens must be encrypted at rest, redacted from logs, and treated as the credential they are.
- **Reflecting tokens across audiences.** A verifier that proxies a request to a downstream substrate by *forwarding the bearer token* to the downstream is sharing a credential bound to itself (`aud == self`) with another principal. The downstream's `aud == self` check should refuse it. Per the substrate's discipline, multi-hop authorization uses token-exchange (a fresh token per hop) or sender-constrained tokens (DPoP, mTLS-bound), not credential forwarding.

## Position

This theorem closes the substrate's account of the HTTP authorization triple: bearer tokens are evidence vectors per the parent's canonical-binding theorem, the issuer's identity-key is the binding root per [signature-binds-witness-to-identity](../../../theorems/signature-binds-witness-to-identity.md), and the verifier's authorization verdict composes binding with federation-trust, freshness, revocation, and scope per the four-corner decomposition above. Together with [cors-as-bilattice-graded-origin-accessibility](cors-as-bilattice-graded-origin-accessibility.md) (origin admission) and [rate-limiting-as-cost-quantale-residuated-authority-gate](rate-limiting-as-cost-quantale-residuated-authority-gate.md) (consumption gating), it grounds the web-server-platform's three middleware-canonical disciplines — authentication, cross-origin admission, rate limiting — in the same predication-categories apparatus the substrate uses for federation, governance, and cost. The substrate's middleware is not a heuristic gate-pile; it is a structured composition of evidence-vector binding, accessibility-relation modal restriction, and cost-quantale residuation.
