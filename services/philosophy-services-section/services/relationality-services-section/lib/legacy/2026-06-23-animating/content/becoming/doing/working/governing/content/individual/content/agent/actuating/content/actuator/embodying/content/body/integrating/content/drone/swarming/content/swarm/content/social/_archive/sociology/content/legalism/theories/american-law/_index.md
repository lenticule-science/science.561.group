The sociological analysis of American law is the analysis of a specific institutional formation.
American law is not law-in-general; it is the historically specific arrangement of common-law inheritance, adversarial procedure, federalism, the constitutional design of judicial review, the role of juries, the distribution of authority between state and federal jurisdictions, and — constitutively — the role of the carceral apparatus in absorbing the contradictions the rest of the system produces.
Reading the analysis as if it were about law-in-general loses the substance.

The subdomain's terms — *adversarial procedure*, *adversarial structure*, *character evidence*, *legal personhood*, *legal precedent*, *prosecutorial discretion* — name the analytical pieces the substantive texts develop.
The *adversarial structure* is one of the most consequential features: by routing legal truth-finding through a contest between two professional advocates, American law produces a specific kind of evidentiary process whose strengths (testing of claims through cross-examination) and pathologies (the asymmetry of prosecutorial resources, the criminalization of legal poverty) are both structural. *Legal personhood* is the analytical category through which the law sorts who counts as a person for legal purposes — and the historical record of how that category has been used to exclude (slaves, women, Indigenous peoples, children) and to extend (corporations) is part of the discipline. *Prosecutorial discretion* names the locus where most of the actual sorting of the carceral system happens — not in courts, but in charging decisions, plea offers, and the cascade of consequences that follow.

The texts in this subdomain develop the analysis at length.
The text [*law-as-governmentality*](texts/law-as-governmentality.md) reads American law through Foucault's frame; the text [*evidence-as-translation*](texts/evidence-as-translation.md) develops the analysis of how the courtroom converts events into evidence; the [*prairieland-trial-as-cultural-governance*](texts/prairieland-trial-as-cultural-governance.md) is a specific case study; the [*counterinsurgency-and-legal-infrastructure*](texts/counterinsurgency-and-legal-infrastructure.md) text develops the analysis of how legal forms have been used to manage dissent; the [*open-problems*](texts/open-problems.md) text is the visible front for what the school has not yet resolved.

The subdomain reads against the abolitionist analysis (in [`abolition/`](../../../critical-theory/theories/abolition/)): the carceral state is the operational endpoint of much of what American law does, and the sociology of law cannot honestly be done in abstraction from the carceral analysis.
It also reads against critical legal studies and against the contemporary scholarship on law and racial capitalism (Ahmed, Bell, Crenshaw, Spade), which the texts here engage in passing.
