# Kyriarchial Governance

A theory of [governance](../../../_index.md) analyzed through the lens of [kyriarchism](../../../../../../critical-theory/theories/queer/theories/kyriarchism/): governance as organized within and reproducing interlocking systems of domination (patriarchy, white supremacy, capitalism, heteronormativity, ableism) rather than as neutral authority relations.
Jural relations, offices, normative systems, charters, accountability mechanisms, and decision-making processes — the governance vocabulary's terms — are exhibited as specific structures produced through kyriarchal dynamics.

## What this theory commits to

Kyriarchial governance analyzes how authority, accountability, and decision-making are structured, valued, and contested within intersecting systems of power:

1. **Governance is never neutral** — all governance occurs within and reproduces kyriarchal relations of power
2. **Authority is intersectional** — who counts as having legitimate authority varies depending on where actors sit in intersecting hierarchies of gender, race, class, sexuality, ability
3. **Accountability is situated** — what counts as a violation, who is held accountable, and what remedies are considered just depend on kyriarchal positionalities
4. **Institutions are isomorphic** — governance structures mirror and reproduce broader societal power distributions
5. **Transformation requires dismantling** — liberating governance requires dismantling the interlocking systems themselves, not just reforming governance practices

## Key Concepts

- **Interlocking authority structures**: How different forms of authority (traditional, bureaucratic, charismatic, claimed) reinforce each other across axes of domination
- **Positional authority value**: How the legitimacy ascribed to identical authority claims varies based on actor identity within kyriarchal hierarchies
- **Kyriarchial norm-conflict**: How competing normative systems (state law, customary law, religious law, workplace policy) produce different outcomes for different identity positions
- **Intersectional accountability**: How responsibility assignment and remedy formulation vary across axes of domination
- **Governance solidarity fractures**: How kyriarchal divisions undermine collective governance power and how solidarity can be rebuilt across differences

## Relationship to Other Governance Theories

- **Distinct from predicated-governance**: While predicated-governance analyzes governance through formal predication mathematics, kyriarchial-governance analyzes governance through power relations
- **Complementary to predicated-governance**: A predicated-kyriarchial-governance synthesis would apply predication mathematics to kyriarchial governance structures
- **Informs critical governance studies**: Builds on feminist governance theory, critical race theory approaches to governance, queer theory of the state, and disability studies of governance
