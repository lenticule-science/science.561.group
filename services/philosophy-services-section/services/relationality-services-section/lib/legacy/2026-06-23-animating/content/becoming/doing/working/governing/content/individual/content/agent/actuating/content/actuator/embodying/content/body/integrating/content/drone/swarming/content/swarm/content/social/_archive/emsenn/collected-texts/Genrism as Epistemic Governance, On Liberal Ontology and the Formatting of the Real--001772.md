## **Genrism as Epistemic Governance: On Liberal Ontology and the Formatting of the Real**

### **Abstract**

This paper introduces the concept of *genrism* as a formal mechanism through which liberal ontology governs the conditions of the real.
Genrism names the epistemic and ontological operation by which genre becomes not merely an aesthetic or narrative category, but a structuring apparatus for making beings, relations, and futures intelligible.
Liberalism, we argue, does not simply tolerate multiplicity; it conditions emergence through a doctrine of linearity and legibility, formatting life into recognizable patterns.
Genrism is the infrastructure of this conditioning — the recursive operation through which form becomes both ontological container and epistemological filter.
The paper develops this thesis through engagements with critical theorists including Foucault, Berlant, Butler, Ahmed, and Povinelli, and concludes by contrasting genrism with recursive-relational models of coherence.

---

## **1. Introduction: Genre Beyond Narrative**

Genre is commonly understood as a classificatory tool for organizing representations — a way of identifying, anticipating, and interpreting cultural artifacts.
However, under liberalism, genre functions as more than a media heuristic.
It becomes an **epistemic condition** of appearance, a template through which life must pass in order to be rendered intelligible.
We name this phenomenon **genrism**: the ontological and epistemological governance by genre.

Whereas genre organizes texts, **genrism organizes reality**.
It ensures that lives, events, and relations must unfold within prescribed aesthetic and temporal forms in order to be recognized by liberal institutions.
To be “out of genre” is not merely to be misread — it is to be misrecognized as incoherent, irrational, or unreal.

---

## **2. Liberal Ontology and the Doctrine of Linearity**

Liberal ontology is founded on a set of interlocking assumptions: that subjects are autonomous, that time progresses teleologically, and that recognition is achieved through narrative development.
This ontology is not simply philosophical; it is operational.
It governs the production of knowable life by presupposing that coherence arises through **linear progression** — from rupture to redemption, from harm to healing, from exclusion to inclusion.

This commitment to linearity is not limited to temporality; it is **ontological**.
It determines what counts as a coherent object, what counts as a legible relation, and what futures can be oriented toward. **Genrism functions as the mechanism of this ontological ordering** — the technique by which linearity is imposed upon experience, affect, and social life.

---

## **3. Genrism as Epistemic Tool: Formatting the Real**

Drawing from Michel Foucault’s account of power as productive rather than repressive, we can understand genrism as a **productive epistemology**: it does not merely sort representations; it **produces the real** by organizing perception and constraining emergence.
It tells us what a story is, what a subject must endure, how a life becomes meaningful.

In this way, genrism performs a **double operation**:

1. **It formats emergence** — shaping how events and selves must appear in order to be seen;

2. **It filters recognition** — determining which configurations of being and relation can be validated.

Lauren Berlant’s concept of genre as “an affective event of encounter” offers a crucial insight: genre is not just form, but **form with consequences**.
It informs what kinds of optimism are possible, what forms of belonging are imaginable.
Genrism, then, is the **sedimentation of this affective infrastructure into a disciplinary schema** — a way the state, society, and even intimate life “know” what is true.

---

## **4. Examples of Genrism in Practice**

Genrism manifests across domains.
In healthcare, the trans subject must narrate a linear story of dysphoria, diagnosis, and transformation in order to gain access to care.
In settler-colonial contexts, Indigenous resurgence is recognized only when it conforms to the genre of reconciliation.
In queer visibility politics, legitimacy accrues through narratives of trauma and triumph that echo liberal moral arcs.

In all cases, genrism reduces complex, recursive, and ambient lives into **legible templates**.
It renders alterity readable only by depriving it of its structural difference.
Jasbir Puar's notion of homonationalism reveals how queerness, when formatted by state-acceptable genre, becomes not insurgent but instrumental.
Genrism is the process by which that formatting becomes **epistemic common sense**.

---

## **5. Recursive Coherence as Anti-Genrism**

Against genrism stands an alternative mode of knowing: **recursive coherence**.
In your relational calculus, coherence is not achieved through linear progression, but through the stabilization of relation across rupture.
It is not narrative, but field.
Not character arc, but recursive act.
This form of worlding allows for forms of being that do not resolve, do not climax, and do not obey genre — but are nonetheless real, and internally coherent.

This recursive epistemology does not refuse form — it refuses **formatting as governance**.
It insists that intelligibility can emerge from within relation, rather than be imposed from without.
It is not anti-structure; it is **anti-template**.

---

## **6. Conclusion: Genrism as a Problem of Ontological Control**

Genrism is not merely a cultural preference.
It is a **technology of ontological control** — a liberal apparatus for regulating what may be known, how it must appear, and in what order it must unfold.
It is genre at the level of being.
And as such, it must be confronted not just through critique of media, but through the construction of alternative epistemologies — recursive, relational, and non-linear.

In deconstructing genrism, we do not abandon coherence.
We refuse the colonial terms by which coherence has been monopolized.
