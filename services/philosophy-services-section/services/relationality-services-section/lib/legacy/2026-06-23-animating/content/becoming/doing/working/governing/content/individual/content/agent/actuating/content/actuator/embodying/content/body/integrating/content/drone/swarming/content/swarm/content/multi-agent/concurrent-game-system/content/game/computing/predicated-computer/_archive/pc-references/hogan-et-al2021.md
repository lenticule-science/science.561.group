## Source

A. Hogan et al. "Knowledge Graphs." *ACM Computing Surveys*, 54(4), Article 71, 2021. Also available as a Synthesis Lectures on Data, Semantics, and Knowledge book (Morgan & Claypool, 2021).

## Form

Survey paper. Considered the canonical comprehensive reference for the knowledge-graph engineering tradition.

## Content summary

Surveys the knowledge-graph field across its data models (RDF triples, property graphs), schema layers (ontologies, shapes, type systems), inference machinery (rules, ontology entailment, embedding-based completion), provenance and trust (named graphs, signed assertions, source attribution), and engineering practices (ingestion, identifier management, query languages, federation). Organizes the field around the unifying view that a knowledge graph is *a graph of data, intended to accumulate and convey real-world knowledge, whose nodes represent entities of interest and whose edges represent relations between these entities*. Surveys named systems (DBpedia, Wikidata, YAGO, schema.org), commercial knowledge-graph platforms, and the academic literature on each layer.
