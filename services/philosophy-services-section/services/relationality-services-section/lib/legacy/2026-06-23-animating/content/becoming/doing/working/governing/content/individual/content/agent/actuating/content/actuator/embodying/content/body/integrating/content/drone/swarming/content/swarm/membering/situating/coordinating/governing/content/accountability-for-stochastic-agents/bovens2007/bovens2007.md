# Bovens (2007) — Analysing and Assessing Accountability

**Full citation:** Bovens, M.
"Analysing and Assessing Accountability: A Conceptual Framework." *European Law Journal*, 13(4), 447–468, 2007.

## Summary

Bovens provides the foundational modern accountability framework.
He defines accountability as a social relationship between an agent and a forum, in which the agent is obliged to explain and justify conduct, the forum can pose questions and pass judgment, and the agent may face consequences.

The framework distinguishes accountability from responsibility: responsibility is causal (you caused it), accountability is positional (you hold the role, therefore you answer).
This distinction grounds the agents theory's proof that delegation does not transfer accountability.

Bovens also distinguishes five forms of accountability relevant to public and private organizations: political, legal, administrative, professional, and social.
For multi-agent systems, administrative accountability (documented procedures and decision traces) and legal accountability (named responsible individuals) are the most directly applicable.

## Contribution to the agents theory

Bovens' definition of accountability as a relational structure requiring a forum, an agent, an explanation, and consequences is the basis of the argument that agentic systems cannot bear accountability (they cannot appear before a forum, cannot receive consequences, cannot explain).
The relational structure implies that accountability must attach to a person.

The distinction between responsibility and accountability grounds the [delegation-does-not-transfer-accountability](../theorems/delegation-does-not-transfer-accountability.md) theorem's Part 2 (the positional argument).

Bovens' requirement that accountability be answerable — that there be a forum — grounds the [accountability-chain-must-be-total](../theorems/accountability-chain-must-be-total.md) theorem's Part 1 (accountability gaps leave the forum empty).
