## Annotation

Fleck's foundational case study of the development of the Wassermann reaction for syphilis. The book's load-bearing claim is that *a scientific fact* is constituted through the work of a *thought collective* (*Denkkollektiv*) operating within a *thought style* (*Denkstil*) — both of which precede, constrain, and partially determine what counts as a fact, an observation, or an explanation in the relevant community. Fleck distinguishes the *esoteric circle* (the small group of producing experts) from the *exoteric circle* (the larger group of consuming users) and traces how facts pass between them, transforming as they do.

For the predicated framing, Fleck is the founding source of the [discourse-community-as-fiber](teaching/explanations/discourse-community-as-fiber.md) reading. The thought collective is the discourse community; the thought style is the closure-rule signature operative in the community's fiber; the esoteric/exoteric split is the layered structure within a fiber whereby central participants ([@lave-wenger1991]) and peripheral participants assert with different bilattice-evidence weights. The transformation of facts as they cross from esoteric to exoteric is the [specialize](../morphisms/specialize.md) morphism (refinement to context) composed with the [federate](../morphisms/federate.md) morphism (cross-community settling).

Fleck's underlying epistemological commitment — that *what counts as a fact is community-internal* — is the framing's reason for refusing a global, universal predicate vocabulary. The substrate is *fibered* precisely so that the framing can take seriously the existence of multiple, partially-incommensurable thought collectives without either reducing them to a single substrate or treating them as separate worlds.
