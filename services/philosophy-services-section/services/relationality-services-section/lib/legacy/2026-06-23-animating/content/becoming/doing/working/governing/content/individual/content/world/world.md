A **world** is the meaningful whole an existent finds itself within: the totality relative to which its things show up as the things they are.
Not "all that exists," not the cosmos — a *world* in the sense the phenomenological tradition gives the word: always *someone's* world, the horizon of one existent's life.

## Welt, Umwelt, Lebenswelt

Three closely related notions fix the term.
Heidegger (*Being and Time*) takes *being-in-the-world* as the basic structure of existence: a self is never first a subject that afterwards encounters objects, but always already amid a *Welt* of things that matter, ready-to-hand, organized by what the self is up to.
Husserl's *Lebenswelt* — the lifeworld — is the pre-given world of ordinary experience that all theorizing presupposes and never gets behind.
Jakob von Uexküll's *Umwelt* makes the point biological: each creature inhabits a world carved to its own senses and concerns — the tick's world, the world of a thing for which warmth and butyric acid are the salient realities — so that there is no single neutral environment but as many worlds as there are kinds of life.
In all three, a world is the *disclosed* totality within which entities appear, and it is indexed to whose world it is.

## Prior to its things, and self-reproducing

A world is not built up out of the things in it; it is *prior* to them, the field of sense within which anything can show up as a determinate thing at all.
This is the Simondonian point read at the scale of the whole: the world is the metastable field of potential out of which individuals emerge.
And it is *self-reproducing*: living in a world reproduces the world — the activity carried on within it sustains the very totality that makes the activity possible.
Maturana and Varela named this circularity *autopoiesis*: a system that produces and maintains the conditions of its own continuation.
The world makes the existents that make the world.

This is why world is held *by* the [individual](../../../docs/individual.md) rather than being a rung of any descent.
A single descent settles a claim, an act, a self; the world is the standing setting in which descents happen.
The individual and its world are one self-producing whole seen from two sides: what comes to rest as a bounded self, and the totality within which it is at home.
Distinct individuals, with distinct givens and concerns, inhabit overlapping but non-identical worlds — there is no world that is the view from nowhere.
