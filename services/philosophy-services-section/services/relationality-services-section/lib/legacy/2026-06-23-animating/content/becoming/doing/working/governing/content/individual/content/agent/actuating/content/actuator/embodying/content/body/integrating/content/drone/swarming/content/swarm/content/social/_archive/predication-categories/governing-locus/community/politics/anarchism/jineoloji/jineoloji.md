Jineoloji (from Kurmanji *jin*, "woman," and Greek *-loji*, "science") is the Kurdish women's movement's indigenous feminist theory: the "science of women" or, more precisely, the science of free life grounded in the liberation of women.
It is not an import of Western feminism into Kurdish politics but an intellectual production of the Kurdish women's movement itself, developed through decades of armed struggle, political education, and theoretical debate within the PKK and its affiliated organizations.

The core claim is that the domination of women is the first and foundational form of domination — the template from which all other hierarchies (class, state, colonialism) derive.
Abdullah Ocalan (2013) argues that the Neolithic transition — the shift from egalitarian gathering communities to hierarchical agricultural societies — was accomplished through the subjugation of women, and that every subsequent structure of domination reproduces this original subjugation.
The liberation of women is therefore not one political goal among others but the precondition for all liberation.

This analysis produces specific institutional forms.
In Rojava's [democratic confederalism](../../anarchism/terms/democratic-confederalism.md), every leadership position is held jointly by one man and one woman (co-chairship).
Women's communes and women's councils operate in parallel to mixed-gender institutions, with autonomous decision-making authority over matters affecting women.
The YPJ (Women's Protection Units) is not merely a women's military unit but an armed expression of jineoloji's claim that women's self-defense — against patriarchy, against the state, against fascism — is the foundation of collective self-defense.

Jineoloji is taught in academies across Rojava and in Kurdish diaspora communities.
Its curriculum includes history, ethics, aesthetics, economics, ecology, and demography — all reread through the lens of women's experience and women's liberation.
The theory insists on its own intellectual autonomy: it does not position itself as a Kurdish application of Western feminist theory but as an independent tradition that arrives at convergent conclusions through different historical and philosophical paths.
