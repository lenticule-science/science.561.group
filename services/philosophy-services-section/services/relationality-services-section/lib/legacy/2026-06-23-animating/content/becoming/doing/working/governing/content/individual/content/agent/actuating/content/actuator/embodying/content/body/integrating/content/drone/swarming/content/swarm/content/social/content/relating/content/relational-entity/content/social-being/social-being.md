# social-being

A social being is a relational entity capable of entering social relations — of orienting its conduct toward others and having theirs oriented toward it in turn.
It is the founding presupposition of sociology: the kind of entity whose relations *are* social, in the sense that their content is meaningful to the parties and shapes how each acts.
Generated by: relational-entity — a social being is a relational entity that has become capable of mutual orientation, so that what it does answers to what others do, and others' doing answers to it.

The qualifier "social" does real work.
Not every relation a being stands in is social.
A rock stands in physical relations to everything around it but orients its conduct toward nothing; an animal may orient toward others without the meaningful, interpretable quality that Weber made the criterion of social action.
A social being is one whose action "takes account of the behaviour of others and is thereby oriented in its course" — one for whom others are not just objects in the environment but parties whose expected conduct enters into its own.
This is the threshold sociology begins at: the point where relations become mutual orientations rather than mere causal contacts, and where, in consequence, they can carry meaning, expectation, obligation, and contestation.

It is worth being careful about what a social being is and is not.
It is not yet a *person* in the full sense — personhood, in this theory, is a further achievement, the individuation and social recognition of a social being as a named, accountable unit, and it is treated downstream.
Nor is a social being necessarily a single human body: a collective — a community, an organisation, a people — can be a social being too, able to act and be addressed as one.
The social being is the more basic category, the bearer of the capacity for social relation as such, prior to the distinctions (individual and collective, recognised and unrecognised, dominant and subordinate) that the rest of the theory draws.

From the social being everything else in the social tree unfolds.
Because two or more social beings can orient toward one another, there can be social relations; because social relations can be reciprocal or asymmetric, there can be solidarity and domination; because they settle into durable patterns, there can be structures, institutions, and the whole apparatus of social life.
The social being is the hinge between the general claim that relations constitute their terms and the specific subject matter of sociology.
