# The federation-network operator is a strong symmetric monoidal functor; the binary case is its parallel-composition coherence

**Claim.** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the federation viewed as a [quantale-enriched sheaf](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/quantale-enriched-sheaf.md) over the substrate's [cost quantale of fuel](../objects/cost-quantale-of-fuel.md) $\mathcal{V} = (V, \otimes, k, \bigvee)$, with peers as $\mathcal{V}$-points and federation edges as $\mathcal{V}$-graded restrictions. Let $N : \prod_w \mathrm{Pred}_w \to \prod_w \mathrm{Pred}_w$ be the substrate's *federation-network operator* — the endofunctor on the product of per-peer predication fibrations that admits a federation envelope per [federation-envelope](teaching/explanations/federation-envelope.md) and applies the receiver's [interfacer counit law](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md).

Then $N$ is a **strong symmetric monoidal functor** $(N, \mu, \mu_0)$ from the per-peer fibrations' product-monoidal structure (with $\otimes_{\mathrm{peer}}$ the per-peer fiber-tensor and unit the per-peer empty cell-set) to itself, with:

1. **Tensorator (binary coherence).** A natural isomorphism $\mu_{X,Y} : N(X) \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} N(Y) \xrightarrow{\sim} N(X \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} Y)$ recording that admitting two envelopes and then tensoring their effects is naturally isomorphic to admitting the tensor envelope.
2. **Unitator.** $\mu_0 : I_{\mathrm{peer}} \xrightarrow{\sim} N(I_{\mathrm{peer}})$ — the empty admission is admitted as the empty admission.
3. **Pentagon and triangle coherence.** $\mu$ satisfies Mac Lane's pentagon (3-fold associativity) and triangle (unit-tensor) axioms.
4. **Symmetry.** $N(\sigma_{X,Y}) \circ \mu_{X,Y} = \mu_{Y,X} \circ \sigma_{N(X),N(Y)}$ — admitting two envelopes is symmetric under swap.
5. **Graded compatibility.** Per [federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity](federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity.md), the $\mathcal{V}$-grade of $\mu_{X,Y}$ is the $\otimes$ in $\mathcal{V}$ of the per-component grades:
   $$\mathrm{grade}(\mu_{X,Y}) \;=\; \mathrm{grade}(N(X)) \otimes \mathrm{grade}(N(Y)).$$

The *binary parallel-composition coherence* — placeholder #2 component 2b in the dev-doc placeholder audit — is exactly the tensorator $\mu_{X,Y}$ together with its $\mathcal{V}$-grade compatibility. No new substrate primitive, no new envelope shape, and no new conformance check beyond those already named in [federation-envelope](teaching/explanations/federation-envelope.md), [federation-trust-bilattice-graded](federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md), and [federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity](federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity.md).

## Why this holds

**Part 1: $N$ is a functor.** The per-peer fibration product $\prod_w \mathrm{Pred}_w$ is a fibration whose morphisms are per-peer admissions. Per [interfacer-counit-law-uniformity](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md), every federation envelope's admission at the receiver is the application of a faithful interfacer functor $I_w : \mathcal{S}_w \to \mathrm{Pred}_w$ followed by the closure step. $N$ acts on a per-peer admission by routing it through the federation transport (per [federation-envelope](teaching/explanations/federation-envelope.md)'s wire format) and applying $I_w$ at the receiver. Functoriality (preservation of identity admissions and composition of admissions) is the conjunction of [interfacer-counit-law-uniformity](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md) (per-peer) with the route-composition associativity of the underlying transport — which is the substrate's own composition, not a transport contingency.

**Part 2: $N$ is monoidal — the tensorator.** Two independent envelopes $X, Y$ traversing the federation graph (possibly along different paths) reach their receivers and apply per-peer interfacer admissions. The product of those admissions in $\prod_w \mathrm{Pred}_w$ is $N(X) \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} N(Y)$. Conversely, the *paired envelope* $X \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} Y$ — admitted as a single substrate cell carrying both payloads (per the federation envelope's ability to carry compound substrate cells per [federation-envelope](teaching/explanations/federation-envelope.md)'s `payload` field) — applies as $N(X \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} Y)$.

The tensorator $\mu_{X,Y}$ identifies these two routes. Its naturality is: for any per-peer admissions $f : X \to X'$ and $g : Y \to Y'$,

$$\mu_{X',Y'} \circ (N(f) \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} N(g)) \;=\; N(f \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} g) \circ \mu_{X,Y}.$$

Both sides are admissions of the paired envelope's image under the paired admission. They agree because per-peer admissions commute with envelope-pairing: the receiver's interfacer per [interfacer-counit-law-uniformity](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md) acts componentwise on a paired payload, and pairing commutes with componentwise application by definition of $\otimes_{\mathrm{peer}}$.

The tensorator is *invertible* because envelope-pairing is invertible at the substrate level: given a paired admission $N(X \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} Y)$, the substrate recovers the per-component admissions by the [federation-envelope](teaching/explanations/federation-envelope.md) `payload` projection — which is functorial per the same counit-law uniformity.

**Part 3: $N$ is monoidal — the unitator.** The empty per-peer cell-set $I_{\mathrm{peer}}$ has trivial admission (no envelope sent, no closure step triggered). $N(I_{\mathrm{peer}})$ is again the empty admission. The unitator $\mu_0 : I_{\mathrm{peer}} \to N(I_{\mathrm{peer}})$ is the identity admission — invertible and unique up to the unique morphism between trivial admissions.

**Part 4: Pentagon coherence.** For three admissions $X, Y, Z$, the two routes around the pentagon

$$N(X) \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} (N(Y) \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} N(Z)) \;\rightrightarrows\; N(X \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} Y \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} Z)$$

(left-associated then tensorated, vs. right-associated then tensorated, vs. partial-tensor then full-tensor) all agree because the receiver's componentwise interfacer admission is *associative* per [interfacer-counit-law-uniformity](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md). The associator on the per-peer product structure is the substrate's own associator on the fiber tensor; $\mu$ commutes with it because $N$ is the application of a single counit law, not a sequence of distinct operations.

**Part 5: Triangle coherence.** $\mu_{I,X} \circ (\mu_0 \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} 1_{N(X)})$ and the corresponding right-unit triangle commute by the same counit-law associativity together with the unit law of the per-peer interfacer: admitting an empty payload alongside a non-empty payload is the same as admitting just the non-empty payload, and $\mu$ records this naturally.

**Part 6: Symmetry.** Federation envelopes carry no payload-order information beyond what their substrate cells encode; per [federation-envelope](teaching/explanations/federation-envelope.md)'s canonical-JSON discipline, the canonical form of a paired payload is order-independent (cells sorted by canonical hash). The braiding $\sigma_{X,Y}$ on the per-peer product is symmetric ($\sigma^2 = 1$) and $N$ preserves it because the canonical form is preserved by per-peer interfacer admission.

**Part 7: Graded compatibility.** Per [federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity](federation-fuel-as-tensor-distributivity.md), parallel federation paths' fuel costs combine by the cost quantale's $\otimes$. Per [federation-path-metrics-multiplicative](federation-path-metrics-multiplicative.md), per-edge grades compose multiplicatively in $\mathcal{V}$. Two parallel admissions $N(X), N(Y)$ along independent paths have grades $\mathrm{grade}(N(X)), \mathrm{grade}(N(Y)) \in V$; the paired admission $N(X \otimes_{\mathrm{peer}} Y)$'s grade is the parallel composition $\mathrm{grade}(N(X)) \otimes \mathrm{grade}(N(Y))$. The tensorator $\mu_{X,Y}$ is therefore graded by exactly this parallel-$\otimes$, which is what the dev-doc placeholder #2 component 2b asks the substrate to commit to.

## Engineering consequence

- **No new "parallel-network" primitive.** Two federation messages sent in parallel compose by the same substrate operator that sequential messages compose under, with the difference being the choice of $\otimes$ in $\mathcal{V}$ (parallel) vs. sequential composition $\cdot$ in $\mathcal{V}$ (sequential, per [federation-path-metrics-multiplicative](federation-path-metrics-multiplicative.md)). When $\mathcal{V}$'s $\otimes$ and $\cdot$ coincide (e.g., the additive-latency quantale where parallel latency = max and sequential = sum, modeled by separate quantales) the dev-doc may parameterise the choice; otherwise, a single tensorator suffices and parallel-vs-sequential is a quantale-choice decision per [cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale](cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale.md).
- **Bulk admission is structurally one admission.** A receiver admitting a batched envelope carrying $n$ paired payloads applies the tensorator $\mu_{X_1, \dots, X_n}$ — a single faithful-interfacer admission per [interfacer-counit-law-uniformity](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md), not $n$ admissions interleaved. Per the pentagon, the order in which the substrate decomposes the batched payload for closure-step processing is unobservable; the closure result is identical.
- **Federation-graph's monoidal structure is the substrate's.** Per [federation-trust-bilattice-graded](federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md), the federation graph's $\mathcal{V}$-grading is the substrate's bilattice-trust grading. The monoidal structure on the graph is the substrate's own per-fiber tensor lifted to the per-peer product — no separate "network monoidal structure" needs to be specified.
- **Bulk-admission grade is the parallel $\otimes$ of components' grades.** Per Part 7, an auditor verifying a bulk admission's fuel cost uses $\otimes$ over the per-payload contributions; per [witness](../objects/witness.md), each component's grade is recorded in the envelope. The bulk-admission cost is reproducible from the envelope's content, not derived from runtime measurement.
- **SDK-import composition is the symmetry case.** Per [sdk-import-non-amplifying-via-modal-restriction-composition](sdk-import-non-amplifying-via-modal-restriction-composition.md), composing two SDK imports gives a non-amplifying composite. The symmetry of $\mu$ (Part 6) says: the order in which the consumer imports two SDKs does not change the composite capability. A consumer importing $A$ then $B$ has the same effective capability as importing $B$ then $A$, modulo each SDK's own modal restriction — the substrate's symmetric tensorator forbids order-dependent capability amplification.
- **One conformance vector covers parallel admission.** Per the conformance discipline, the substrate's parallel-admission-conformance reduces to verifying the tensorator's invertibility on a representative envelope-pair, plus the grade-$\otimes$ identity on the recorded grades. No separate parallel-protocol conformance check is required.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Non-symmetric parallel admission.** A federation that admits envelope pair $(X, Y)$ to a different effective substrate cell than envelope pair $(Y, X)$ violates Part 6. Per the canonical-JSON discipline, this cannot occur in a substrate respecting the envelope's canonical form; an implementation that violates symmetry is non-conformant.
- **Order-dependent batched admission.** A receiver that produces different closure results depending on the within-batch order of paired payloads violates the pentagon (Part 4). The substrate's closure step is associative on paired admissions; an order-sensitive receiver is non-conformant per [interfacer-counit-law-uniformity](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md).
- **Parallel-only or sequential-only cost models.** A federation cost model that handles only parallel composition (no sequential) or only sequential (no parallel) cannot be the substrate's $\mathcal{V}$. Per [cost-quantale-of-fuel](../objects/cost-quantale-of-fuel.md)'s commutative-unital-quantale requirement, $\otimes$ supports both; an asymmetric cost model is non-conformant.
- **Implicit serialisation of "parallel" admissions.** A substrate that internally serialises a parallel admission and reports the *sequential* grade (per [federation-path-metrics-multiplicative](federation-path-metrics-multiplicative.md)'s $\cdot$) when the envelope was admitted as a tensorator $\mu_{X,Y}$ violates Part 7's grade identity. An auditor can detect this by comparing the recorded grade against the $\otimes$ of the per-component grades.
- **Bulk-admission re-tagging at the receiver.** A receiver that re-tags a bulk-admitted payload's bilattice-tag differently from the per-component tags violates the tensorator's naturality (Part 2). Per [federation-trust-bilattice-graded](federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md), the bilattice tag is determined by the federation accessibility relation; the receiver applies the same modal restriction componentwise.

## What the theorem requires

- The federation graph is a $\mathsf{Q}$-enriched sheaf with $\mathsf{Q} = \mathcal{V}$ (cost quantale) per [quantale-enriched-sheaf](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/quantale-enriched-sheaf.md), with parallel-composition $\otimes$ in $\mathcal{V}$ explicitly named (distinct from or coincident with the sequential $\cdot$ depending on the chosen quantale).
- The federation envelope carries paired payloads in canonical-JSON form per [federation-envelope](teaching/explanations/federation-envelope.md); the canonical form is order-independent.
- Every receiver implements the per-peer interfacer admission per [interfacer-counit-law-uniformity](interfacer-counit-law-uniformity.md); admission acts componentwise on paired payloads.
- Per-edge grades and per-payload grades are recorded as substrate content per [witness](../objects/witness.md); the parallel-$\otimes$ identity is verifiable from recorded predications.

The substrate's federation network is not an external transport with its own algebra — it is the substrate's own per-peer fibration product equipped with the cost quantale's $\otimes$ as parallel-composition operator and the receiver's faithful interfacer as admission. Two federation messages in flight are one substrate cell; their parallel cost is one substrate computation; their bulk admission is one closure step. The theorem is what makes "the federation IS the substrate" structurally precise at the parallel-composition surface.
