Allovonic Cosmology does not resolve mystery.
It formalizes its structure — offering a grammar for when systems exceed their own explanatory frame, and modulation becomes the condition of legibility itself.

### 2.3 Infrons and Modulons

Infrons and modulons describe how systems behave when shaped by forces that are not fully contained within them.

An **infron** is a structural shift — a moment where a system begins acting out of pattern, as if responding to a signal it doesn’t generate.
This can appear as a sudden asymmetry, a change in field behavior, or a shift in how patterns stabilize.
What matters is not the form, but the _departure_: the moment local rules no longer account for what the system is doing.

A **modulon** is what unfolds from that departure.
It is not a single event, but the spread of new structure — a change that echoes outward, reshaping behavior over time and scale.
Where infrons mark the start of modulation, modulons carry it forward, sometimes subtly, sometimes visibly.
Their influence may show up in curvature, fluctuation, emergence, drift — any shift that behaves as though it belongs to a broader logic than the system alone can explain.

These are not new particles or energies.
They are ways of recognizing when a system is under recursive stress — when something outside its apparent bounds is shaping what it becomes.

Read together, infrons and modulons help identify not just when a system changes, but _how_ that change carries structural memory.
They let us trace the signature of modulation, not just in isolated events, but in patterns that persist, amplify, and recombine — often far from where they began.
