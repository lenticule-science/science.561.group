# HTTP method semantics are predication-fibration generator actions

**Claim.** The HTTP methods $\{\texttt{GET}, \texttt{HEAD}, \texttt{OPTIONS}, \texttt{POST}, \texttt{PUT}, \texttt{PATCH}, \texttt{DELETE}\}$ are not a discretionary verb list. Each method is a *named action on the [predication fibration](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md)* drawn from a small structural vocabulary: reindexing $f^*$, the bilattice quantifier-adjoint $\exists_f^k$, the post-reindex insertion $\eta_p \circ f^*$, the post-reindex sublocale-bounded retraction $\rho_p \circ f^*$, and the converse-self-adjoint introspection $f_{\mathrm{conv}}^*$. The HTTP method dispatch in [handle-request](../morphisms/handle-request.md) is therefore not heuristic; it is *generator selection on the substrate's predicate-category morphism*.

## Method-to-generator-action assignment

For an [endpoint](teaching/explanations/endpoint.md) bound by [routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change](routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change.md) to a predicate-category morphism $f : p \to q$ — where $q$ is the parent predicate and $p$ is the route-scoped predicate, with $f$ a composite of the four generators (specialization, converse, subject-projection, object-projection) — the HTTP method determines which fibration action the endpoint performs:

| HTTP method | Substrate action on fiber $A_p$ | Predication-category content |
|---|---|---|
| `GET`, `HEAD` | $f^* : A_q \to A_p$ then read | Pure reindexing; per [cost-discipline-reads-free](../../../theorems/cost-discipline-reads-free.md), no successor valuation |
| `OPTIONS` | $f_{\mathrm{conv}}^* \circ f^* : A_q \to A_p$ then read | Self-introspection via converse-self-adjoint per [predicate-converse-quantifier-adjoints](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/predicate-converse-quantifier-adjoints.md); reads the route-table cell that *mentions* the resource rather than the resource itself |
| `POST` | $\exists_f^k : A_p \to A_q$ then [admit](../../../morphisms/admit.md) | Existential-quantifier left adjoint per [predicate-subject-projection-quantifier-adjoints](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/predicate-subject-projection-quantifier-adjoints.md) — *introduces a new predication* under $f$, which is non-idempotent because the subject-row $s$ in the diagonal cell is freshly chosen each call |
| `PUT` | [admit](../../../morphisms/admit.md) of total fiber-value at the route's scope | In-fiber [insertion](../../../morphisms/insertion.md) of a complete value $v \in A_p$ — admissible because $\vee_k$ is idempotent on equal $v$ |
| `PATCH` | [admit](../../../morphisms/admit.md) of partial fiber-update | In-fiber insertion of a partial value $u$ to be joined with $v$ via $\vee_k$ |
| `DELETE` | $\rho_p \circ f^*$: route to fiber, then sublocale-bounded [retraction](../../../morphisms/retraction.md) | Sublocale-nucleus change at the route's fiber, *not* a substrate-level deletion |

## Why this holds

By [routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change](routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change.md), routing already binds each $(method, URL)$ pair to a predicate-category morphism $f$. The fibration's generator action data (axiom A2 of [predication-fibration](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md)) determines $f^*$ uniquely from the generator class of $f$. What [routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change](routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change.md) leaves implicit is the *direction-of-action*: $f^*$ is the reindex from $A_q$ down to $A_p$, but the substrate also admits the left-adjoint $\exists_f^k : A_p \to A_q$ (per the quantifier-adjoint theorems), and any in-fiber [insertion](../../../morphisms/insertion.md) or sublocale-bounded [retraction](../../../morphisms/retraction.md) at $A_p$ is a third possibility distinct from both. The HTTP method is the substrate's choice among these three directions plus the converse-self-adjoint introspection move.

**`GET` and `HEAD` are reindex-and-read.** Both apply $f^*$ then return — no in-fiber update, no quantifier-fold. Per [cost-discipline-reads-free](../../../theorems/cost-discipline-reads-free.md) this advances no settling ordinal. `HEAD` differs from `GET` only at [emit-response](../morphisms/emit-response.md): the body is suppressed but the status, headers, and the witness's read-content-hash are identical. Their substrate-side identity is what HTTP's specification records as "HEAD must be identical to GET except for the response body."

**`OPTIONS` is the converse-self-adjoint introspection.** An `OPTIONS` request asks "what can be done at $f$?" — i.e., which methods does the substrate's [endpoint-record](../objects/endpoint-record.md) declare for the route bound to $f$? Per [predicate-converse-quantifier-adjoints](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/predicate-converse-quantifier-adjoints.md), the converse $f_{\mathrm{conv}}^*$ is its own adjoint and acts by $(s, o) \mapsto (o, s)$. Composed with the route's $f^*$, it reads the *predications-about-the-route* (the substrate cells whose object position is the route's resource and whose subject position is the endpoint-record), recovering the method-list as cell content. The `OPTIONS` response's `Allow:` header is the substrate's projection of those cells.

**`POST` is the existential left-adjoint $\exists_f^k$.** Per [predicate-subject-projection-quantifier-adjoints](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/predicate-subject-projection-quantifier-adjoints.md), $\exists_f^k(w)(s, s) = \bigvee_k\{w(s, o') : o' \in E\}$ folds the row at $s$ onto the diagonal. `POST` to a collection URL freshly chooses the subject $s$ — the new resource's identity — and inserts the request body as $w(s, o')$ at the chosen subject. Two `POST`s with the same body select two distinct fresh subjects $s_1 \neq s_2$; $\exists_f^k$ produces two distinct diagonal cells. *This is why `POST` is not idempotent*: the left-adjoint freshly enters the fiber at a freshly chosen index. Mathematically: a left adjoint preserves colimits but does not in general preserve the equalizer of $(\mathrm{id}, \mathrm{id})$ — replaying the morphism reproduces an equivalent insertion, not the identical cell.

**`PUT` is full-value in-fiber insertion.** A `PUT` to `/users/{id}` with body $v$ sets the cell at the fixed subject $s = \mathrm{id}$ to value $v$. The action is [insertion](../../../morphisms/insertion.md) at a fixed cell, which the [insertion](../../../morphisms/insertion.md) morphism establishes as $G[\![s, p, o \mapsto G[\![s, p, o]\!] \vee_k v]\!]$. Idempotence comes from $v \vee_k v = v$ (knowledge-join idempotence in the bilattice). The detailed proof of `PUT` idempotency is [idempotent-methods-via-closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](idempotent-methods-via-closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md).

**`PATCH` is partial in-fiber insertion.** A `PATCH` body $u$ is admitted as a partial value to be joined: the fiber result is $v \vee_k u$. Per [insertion](../../../morphisms/insertion.md)'s monotonicity, this raises $v$ in the knowledge order without lowering it. `PATCH` is *not* idempotent in general — replaying $u$ against an evolved $v'$ may produce a different result if other writers have moved $v$ between the two `PATCH`es. A `PATCH` whose body fully determines the cell value is structurally a `PUT`.

**`DELETE` is sublocale-bounded retraction post-reindex.** Per [retraction](../../../morphisms/retraction.md), arbitrary deletion is forbidden; only repair-retraction or sublocale-bounded retraction is admissible. A `DELETE` is the latter: the route's resource is removed from the application sublocale's nucleus, not from the underlying [predication-graph store](../../../objects/predication-graph-store.md). Replaying the same `DELETE` is a no-op against a nucleus from which the cell is already absent — the operation is idempotent because the nucleus-modification is itself a knowledge-join move on the nucleus algebra.

## Engineering consequence

- **Method-mismatch is a fibration violation, not a convention violation.** A handler that processes `GET` by mutating a substrate cell is not just violating REST etiquette — it is naming itself as $f^*$ (per the route-table) but acting as the in-fiber insertion. The conformance check at [run-conformance-test](../../../morphisms/run-conformance-test.md) detects this structurally: per [cost-discipline-reads-free](../../../theorems/cost-discipline-reads-free.md), a handler labeled `GET` cannot produce settling-ordinal advances; if its witness records one, the handler's method-tag is wrong.
- **`405 Method Not Allowed` is a generator-class mismatch.** When the route resolves to $f$ but $f$'s generator class is incompatible with the requested method (e.g., a route bound to a converse-only morphism cannot accept `POST` because $\exists_f^k$ is not defined for converse), the substrate returns `405`. The set of `Allow:`-header methods is the set of methods whose generator-action is well-defined on $f$.
- **Method extension proceeds by generator extension.** Adding a new HTTP method (e.g., `WebDAV`'s `MKCOL`, `PROPFIND`) requires assigning it to a fibration action — typically a composite of existing generators with a fixed in-fiber move. A new method that has no fibration assignment is rejected at the route table; per [self-representation](../../teaching/explanations/self-representation.md), the substrate's method table is itself substrate content.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Methods bound to opaque effects.** A method whose action is "whatever the handler does" is not a method in this discipline; it is an undisciplined RPC dispatch and lies outside the [predication-fibration](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md) frame. The substrate refuses to register such an endpoint.
- **`GET`-with-side-effects.** A `GET` that admits content into the substrate is mis-typed: per [cost-discipline-reads-free](../../../theorems/cost-discipline-reads-free.md), `GET`'s commitment is no settling-ordinal advance. If the side effect is essential, the method is `POST` (or `PUT`/`PATCH`/`DELETE` per its in-fiber discipline), and the URL design has confused naming with action.
- **Idempotent `POST`.** A `POST` whose effect is idempotent because the application-layer dedups by request-body-hash is engineering on top of the non-idempotent generator-action; the substrate-side `POST` remains $\exists_f^k$ and remains non-idempotent. Idempotent-write requirements should be expressed as `PUT` against a client-chosen identity, which is the discipline `PUT`'s generator-action makes available.

## Position

This theorem promotes the REST bullet in [routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change](routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change.md) into a standalone result and supplies the structural vocabulary needed by [idempotent-methods-via-closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](idempotent-methods-via-closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md) (which uses the in-fiber insertion identification to derive `PUT`/`DELETE` idempotency) and by [status-code-classes-as-four-bilattice-values](status-code-classes-as-four-bilattice-values.md) (which uses the action-direction to fix the bilattice corner of the response). Together the three give the web-server-platform a closed account: routes are base-change ($f$), methods are generator-actions on $f$ (this theorem), responses are bilattice-graded outcomes of those actions.
