This procedure enables the analyst to identify [genring](../teaching/explanations/genring.md) in a specific media artifact — a film, news article, advertisement, social media post, documentary, novel, game, or any other media production.
The procedure has five steps.
Each step has specific outputs; completing each step prepares the next.

## Before you begin

Identify your artifact clearly.
The artifact is a specific, bounded media production — not "the Western genre" but *The Searchers* (1956); not "news coverage of refugees" but a specific news segment with a broadcast date and publication.
Working from a clearly bounded artifact is essential because genring identification requires close reading of specific representational choices.

You also need a working hypothesis about which people the artifact may be genring.
[Genring](../teaching/explanations/genring.md) is always genring *of someone specific*.
Without a hypothesis about who the genre figure represents, you cannot identify what features are being selected or what template is being assembled.

## Step 1: Map the people represented

Read, watch, or listen to the artifact attentively and produce a list of all the groups of people represented — directly depicted, referenced, or implied.
For each group, note:

- How many individuals from the group appear, with how much screen time or textual space
- Whether individuals are named and characterized individually, or appear as unnamed and undifferentiated members of the group
- What affective register the representation assigns to the group (threatening, sympathetic, pitiful, comic, noble, exotic, etc.)

The group you are investigating for genring is your hypothesis group.
But do not limit your mapping to that group — the genring of one group always involves the genring (or the non-genring) of other groups, and the contrast is analytically important.

## Step 2: Identify the selected features

For the hypothesis group, identify specifically which features of the group are represented.
Be precise: not "the film shows indigenous people" but "the film shows indigenous people in specifically these ways" — list the visual signifiers, behavioral attributes, speech patterns, physical settings, narrative roles, and affective registers that appear.

Then ask: of the full heteroglossia of this living group — all the internal diversity, disagreement, complexity, modernity, history, and situation that the group actually contains — what is in the representation, and what is absent?

Make a two-column list: features that appear, features that are absent.
The features that appear are candidates for the selected features through which [genring](../teaching/explanations/genring.md) operates.
The features that are absent identify what the genring suppresses.

## Step 3: Assess template assembly

Look across the selected features and ask: do they assemble into a coherent character template?
A template is coherent if:

- The features are mutually consistent (they do not produce an internally contradictory character)
- The features can be recognized across representations (the character would be recognizable to an audience with prior exposure to the genre)
- The features have narrative utility (they serve specific functions in the artifact's narrative — the noble savage's dignity authorizes the protagonist's respect; the threatening other's menace authorizes the protagonist's response)

If the selected features assemble into a coherent template, [genring](../teaching/explanations/genring.md) is likely operating.
If the features are inconsistent with each other or do not seem to serve narrative functions, the representation may be something other than genring — it may be incoherent representation, or it may be a more complex representation that resists genre assembly.

## Step 4: Identify what the template substitutes for

If a coherent template is present, identify what the template substitutes for: what specific aspects of the living group's actual situation, history, complexity, and internal heteroglossia are made invisible or illegible by the template's operation?

The key analytical question: does the artifact represent the group's [temporal middle](../teaching/explanations/temporal-middle.md) — the position of inheriting, transforming, and continuing a tradition — or does the representation require the group to occupy a static position (pre-modern, timeless, essentially unchanged, defined by a fixed attribute) that eliminates the temporal middle?

If the temporal middle is absent, [genring](../teaching/explanations/genring.md) is strongly indicated.
The temporal middle's absence is the most reliable indicator because it is structural: any representation that requires a people to be frozen in a type is performing the genring operation's defining function.

## Step 5: Identify the political function

Every genre figure serves a political function in the artifact's context.
Identify what function the genre figure serves:

- Does it authorize a specific course of action toward the genred group (protection, reform, exclusion, removal)?
- Does it produce a specific affective relationship for the presumed audience (sympathy, fear, respect, contempt, exoticism)?
- Does it anchor some other identity against which the genre figure is defined (the settler-hero against the Noble Savage; the civilized against the primitive; the modern against the traditional)?
- Does it resolve a narrative problem that would be more complex if the group were represented with full heteroglossia (the plot requires the indigenous character to be authentically traditional; internal complexity in the indigenous community would make the plot unworkable)?

The political function is the genring's stake.
Understanding the function is what makes the genring analysis useful for political analysis of the artifact.

## What to do with the analysis

The five-step procedure produces a structured analytical account of the genring — what features are selected, how they assemble into a template, what the template substitutes for, and what political function it serves.
This account is the genring analysis.
It is not an accusation and should not be presented as one; it is a structural reading of the artifact's representational operation.

The analysis should be presented with the specific evidence from steps 1-5 — the artifacts' actual content, specific and quotable — and with explicit acknowledgment of what you have not analyzed (other artifacts in the same tradition, the artifact's reception, the actual living group's relation to the representation).
Genring analysis of a single artifact is always partial; it identifies the operation in one case and should not be extrapolated without the further work of analyzing the tradition.
