# Host-projection round-trip laws partition into three canonical-side regimes

**Claim.** Let the host carrier (filesystem, OS process tree, network endpoint set) be presented as a category $\mathcal{H}$ whose objects are host-addressed positions (paths, ports, sockets) and whose morphisms are host-structural relations (containment, sibling-order, mount). Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the substrate's [predication fibration](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md). The substrate's host-projection driver presents an [interfacer](teaching/explanations/interfacer.md) `extract`

$$ R : \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{S} \qquad \text{(read host position into substrate predications)} $$

paired with an `apply`

$$ L : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{H} \qquad \text{(render substrate predications onto host positions)} . $$

When the driver satisfies the [interfacer counit law](interfacer-counit-law.md) at predicate $\pi$ — i.e. `extract` and `apply` are inverse on $\pi$-content up to substrate-irrelevant host detail — there are *natural transformations* $\eta_\pi : 1_\mathcal{S} \to R \circ L$ and $\epsilon_\pi : L \circ R \to 1_\mathcal{H}$ at $\pi$. (We do *not* claim $L \dashv R$ as a global adjunction here; the engineering content is the existence and behavior of these two natural transformations at each $\pi$ independently — equivalently, the two round-trip laws.)

For any predicate $\pi$ encoded by a host-position convention (filename, leaf path-segment, mount-point label, port number), the substrate's view of $\pi$ is determined by which round-trip law holds. The natural-transformation behavior at $\pi$ falls into one of three regimes, mutually exclusive at any single $\pi$ and independently choosable across distinct $\pi$:

- **K — substrate-canonical.** $\eta_\pi$ is a natural iso (the substrate→host→substrate round trip is the identity on $\pi$-cells). Host-side mutation that contradicts the substrate's $\pi$-cell is a $\mathrm{con}$-failure under [two-store-coherence](teaching/explanations/two-store-coherence.md), surfaced as drift.
- **L — host-canonical.** $\epsilon_\pi$ is a natural iso (the host→substrate→host round trip is the identity on $\pi$-positions). Substrate-side mutation that contradicts the host's $\pi$-reading is overwritten on next scan; the substrate's $\pi$-cell is a derived view.
- **M — bilateral with disagreement witness.** Neither $\eta_\pi$ nor $\epsilon_\pi$ is iso; both sides author independently and the substrate runs a closure rule asserting their agreement. Disagreement produces a [witness](teaching/explanations/witness.md) at predicate `kind-projection-disagreement` (or its analogue), captain-resolved through the witness-resolution lifecycle. Round-trip is not identity by construction; the disagreement is the surface that records the loss.

The trichotomy is exhaustive *modulo* the degenerate K-and-L-simultaneously case (both round-trips iso), in which $\pi$-cell and $\pi$-position are byte-identical and the choice of canonical side is structurally vacuous.

## Why this holds

The argument is structural in three parts and depends only on the existence of the two round-trip natural transformations, not on a global adjunction.

**Part 1: $\eta_\pi$ and $\epsilon_\pi$ exist when the interfacer is faithful at $\pi$.** Per [doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor](doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor.md), an interfacer $R : \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{S}$ is faithful at $\pi$ iff its extract-apply pair satisfies the [interfacer counit law](interfacer-counit-law.md) on $\pi$-content. The substrate→host→substrate round trip is then a natural transformation $\eta_\pi : 1_\mathcal{S}|_\pi \to R \circ L|_\pi$ (its components are determined by `apply` followed by `extract` on each $\pi$-cell, and naturality follows from `apply` and `extract` being functors of the substrate's $\pi$-fiber); similarly $\epsilon_\pi : L \circ R|_\pi \to 1_\mathcal{H}|_\pi$ is the host→substrate→host round trip. We do not need either to be an iso, nor do we need triangle identities to hold globally; the two natural transformations exist as soon as the counit law is satisfied.

**Part 2: Each round-trip is independently iso, identity, or non-trivial.** $\eta_\pi$'s components are endo-arrows in $\mathcal{S}|_\pi$ on each $\pi$-cell. They are either iso (round-trip recovers the cell exactly), strictly identity (the iso is identity, the degenerate sub-case where host and substrate share representation), or non-trivial endo-arrows that do not factor as iso (round-trip loses or augments cell content). The same trichotomy holds independently for $\epsilon_\pi$. The K/L/M labelling is the diagonal of this 3×3 grid:

- $\eta_\pi$ iso, $\epsilon_\pi$ not iso ⇒ K — the substrate's $\pi$-cell uniquely determines the host's $\pi$-rendering, and re-reading recovers it. The host is the projection; the substrate is canonical.
- $\epsilon_\pi$ iso, $\eta_\pi$ not iso ⇒ L — the host's $\pi$-position uniquely determines the substrate's $\pi$-cell, and re-rendering recovers it. The substrate is the projection; the host is canonical.
- Neither iso ⇒ M — both round-trips are non-trivial; both sides carry $\pi$-content the other does not preserve. The substrate's discipline is to record, not silence, the non-triviality.

**Part 3: The fourth grid-cell collapses to the degenerate equivalence.** $\eta_\pi$ iso *and* $\epsilon_\pi$ iso means $L|_\pi$ and $R|_\pi$ are an equivalence of categories on $\pi$-content; substrate and host then carry byte-identical $\pi$-content and the canonical-side election is structurally vacuous (any choice is correct). In practice the host carries information the substrate ignores (file mtime, inode number, terminal whitespace) and the substrate carries information the host cannot represent (multi-valued cells, witnesses, retraction stratum), so the equivalence case is rare. The remaining "fourth election" candidates — "neither side authors $\pi$, the substrate infers it" or "the substrate ignores $\pi$" — are M with a degenerate inference rule (the inference is itself a substrate-side authoring step) and not-an-election-at-all (removing $\pi$ from both $\mathcal{S}$ and $\mathcal{H}$), respectively. The K/L/M trichotomy is exhaustive on the predicates $\pi$ where the question of canonical side actually arises.

## Engineering consequence

- **Path-projected leaf entity-kind admits a substrate-side answer.** When the host path's leaf segment encodes the entity's kind by convention (e.g., `<office>/personnel/<name>.md` encoding `kind = personnel`), the substrate's $\pi = \mathrm{kind}$ election is the K/L/M choice this theorem characterises. Per [self-representation](teaching/explanations/self-representation.md) and [driver-substrate-split](teaching/explanations/driver-substrate-split.md), K is the discipline-aligned default: the substrate authors the kind cell; the driver renders the host path *from* the cell. Renaming a host directory then surfaces as a witness, not a silent substrate mutation.
- **The host-projection driver is verifiable per $\pi$.** For each predicate $\pi$ the host carries by convention, the driver's election (K, L, or M) is recordable as a substrate cell on the driver entity (e.g., `(driver:host-projection, canonical-side-for-predicate, kind) = K`). A conformance check enumerates the cells and tests the corresponding triangle identity on each: K requires `extract(apply(cell)) = cell` for $\pi$-cells; L requires `apply(extract(host-position)) = host-position` for $\pi$-positions; M requires the disagreement-witness predicate to be defined and the closure rule to fire on disagreement.
- **Election is per-predicate, not per-driver.** A single host-projection driver may elect K on `kind`, L on `last-modified-at-ord`, and M on `cited-by-text` — the adjunction is a family of $\pi$-fibrewise elections, not a global flag. Per the [predication fibration](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md), each predicate is its own fiber, and the canonical side is fiberwise.
- **The election composes with [storage-as-projection](storage-as-projection.md).** Under K, the host position is a [storage-as-projection](storage-as-projection.md) projection of the substrate's $\pi$-cell — the host disk layout is one of the substrate's cell-store presentations. Under L, the substrate's $\pi$-cell is a projection of the host position — the host is itself a storage; the substrate caches a view. Under M, neither projects the other; both are stored and the substrate runs the disagreement closure rule.
- **Federation discipline is K-only at $\pi$ for federation-shipped predicates.** A predicate the substrate ships across federation must be substrate-canonical (K) on the shipping side, otherwise the receiving peer would receive a derived view whose canonical content lives in the sender's host filesystem — unobservable to the receiver. Per [cross-peer-stratum-projection-via-naturality](cross-peer-stratum-projection-via-naturality.md), the substrate's ship-and-receive content is the substrate's authored cells; L and M predicates are not first-class federation content.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Implicit-canonical-side elections.** A host-projection driver that does not record its election for each conventional $\pi$ has an unstated discipline: a reader cannot tell whether a host-side rename should fire a drift witness or be absorbed silently. The substrate refuses; per [self-representation](teaching/explanations/self-representation.md), the election is recorded as substrate content on the driver entity.
- **Cross-cutting elections.** A single $\pi$ cannot be K *and* L on the same substrate without collapsing to the equivalence case: that would require both round-trips to be iso, which (per Part 3) reduces to byte-identity of $\pi$-cell and $\pi$-position. The substrate's discipline is to either accept the byte-identity reduction (in which case the choice is structurally vacuous) or to choose K, L, or M.
- **Silent-overwrite L.** An L election whose substrate-side $\pi$-cell mutations are *not* overwritten by the next host scan — i.e., the substrate carries a $\pi$-cell that disagrees with the host and the host scan does not refresh it — is not honoring $\epsilon_\pi$ iso. Per Part 2, this is not L; it is M with a degenerate witness rule. The substrate either repairs the L discipline (force-overwrite on scan) or upgrades to M with an explicit disagreement witness.
- **K without drift detection.** A K election whose substrate-cell mutations do not produce a host-side rerender, or whose host-side $\pi$-position changes are not surfaced as drift, is not honoring $\eta_\pi$ iso. Per Part 2, the substrate's K conformance requires the round-trip-iso property to be operationally tested in both directions.

## What the theorem requires

- The host-projection driver is presented as an interfacer $R : \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{S}$ with declared `apply` operation $L$ ([driver-substrate-split](teaching/explanations/driver-substrate-split.md)).
- $R$ satisfies the [interfacer counit law](interfacer-counit-law.md) on the predicates $\pi$ at which an election is being made.
- The host carrier is presentable as a category $\mathcal{H}$ — host positions form a set, host-structural relations form composable morphisms. Filesystems, process trees, and network-endpoint sets all qualify.
- The substrate's $\pi$-cell shape is fixed (no schema drift on $\pi$ during the period the election is in force); a schema change at $\pi$ retires the prior election and admits a fresh one.

## Position

This theorem is the structural account of the substrate-vs-host canonical-side question that arises whenever the predicated computer projects content onto a host carrier with its own conventional reading. It generalises the path-leaf-as-kind question: any host convention that *could* be read as the substrate's authoring of a predicate is governed by the K/L/M trichotomy. Under [driver-substrate-split](teaching/explanations/driver-substrate-split.md), the discipline-aligned default is K — the substrate authors content, the driver renders host positions — but the trichotomy is the structural invariant, and the captain-elected fallback to L or M is admissible when the host carrier's authoring authority is the operational ground truth (e.g., a third-party-managed filesystem the substrate must follow rather than lead). The theorem stays inside the natural-transformation surface — `extract`/`apply` and their two round-trip endo-natural-transformations — and does *not* claim a global $L \dashv R$ adjunction; whether the interfacer pair extends to a full adjoint pair is a separate (and stronger) categorical question outside the scope of this engineering claim. Composes with [doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor](doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor.md) (faithfulness is the precondition for the round-trip natural transformations to exist), with [storage-as-projection](storage-as-projection.md) (each election determines which side is the projection), and with [two-store-coherence](teaching/explanations/two-store-coherence.md) (the M-disagreement witness is the d-frame $\mathrm{con}$-failure, surfaced rather than silenced).
