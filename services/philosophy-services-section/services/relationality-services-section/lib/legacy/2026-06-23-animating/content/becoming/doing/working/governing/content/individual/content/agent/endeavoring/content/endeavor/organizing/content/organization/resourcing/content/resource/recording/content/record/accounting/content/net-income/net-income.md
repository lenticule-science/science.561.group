Net income is revenue minus all costs: [cost of goods sold](./cost-of-goods-sold.md), [operating expenses](./operating-expenses.md), interest on debt, and taxes.
It is the last line on the [income statement](./income-statement.md) — the "bottom line."

**Revenue − COGS = Gross profit** **Gross profit − Operating expenses = Operating income** **Operating income − Interest − Taxes = Net income**

Net income tells you whether the business is profitable after everything is accounted for.
A positive net income means the business earned more than it spent.
A negative net income (net loss) means the opposite.

Net income is not the same as cash.
[Depreciation](./depreciation.md) reduces net income but doesn't consume cash.
[Accounts receivable](./accounts-receivable.md) increases revenue (and net income) before cash is collected.
Capital expenditures consume cash but don't appear on the income statement (they appear on the [balance sheet](./balance-sheet.md) and are depreciated over time).
This is why a profitable business can run out of cash, and why the [cash flow statement](./cash-flow-statement.md) exists as a separate document.
