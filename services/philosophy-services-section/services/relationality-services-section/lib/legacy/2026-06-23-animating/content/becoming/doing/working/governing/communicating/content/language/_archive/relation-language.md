# Language

## What this node is

Language is [relation](../../relation.md) evaluated **where the marks are linguistic**. It is a sibling unfolding to [predication](../predication/predication.md) (which gives the pure mathematical structures) and [encoding](../encoding/encoding.md) (which writes a relation down as a term): here the same generator `articulate → predicate → posit → settle` re-runs over signs, and the result is the structure of speech and meaning. This placement is derived from the corpus's own research, not assumed: the predicated-computing research factors a performed act as a Hoare triple `{P} B {Q}` whose *positing* body carries illocutionary force indexed by Belnap's four-valued bilattice — so a speech act is, first-class, a positing morphism, and language is the base at which positing wears illocutionary force.

A node here is reached by descending the morphisms exactly as in mathematics, but each morphism is read at the language base:

- **articulate → [semiotics](content/semiotics/)** — the triadic sign relation (a vehicle stands for an object to an interpretant). The bare fact of something *signifying*, before grammar or force.
- **predicate → [grammar](content/grammar/grammar.md)** — saying something well-formed *of* signs: syntax and the well-formedness constraints a settled utterance must satisfy, and, reading the settled structure, [semantics](content/grammar/content/semantics/) (meaning as a measure over the parse — the settle face).
- **posit → [speech-acts](content/speech-acts/speech-acts.md)** — value and force enter: locution, illocution, perlocution. Saying-as-doing (assertive, directive, commissive, expressive, declaration) and the felicity that licenses it. Per the corpus's research, the four illocutionary force-classes are exactly the four corners of the bilattice over a predication cell; abstention is itself an act.
- **settle → common ground** — accepted assertions close into shared meaning and resolved reference; the discourse fixed point.

## Why it matters

Language is where this corpus's relational law becomes *pragmatic*: an utterance is the related made to signify, grammar is an ordered (Lambek) predication where a verb is `np \ s / np`, and an assertion is a posit that moves a common ground — a Hoare triple with an illocutionary body. Reading a sentence is the same descent as reading a filepath — premise to conclusion, sign to settled meaning — which is why the language base hangs off the same `relation` root as mathematics rather than sitting in a separate domain. The grounding here is the research library's own treatment of speech acts (Austin 1962, Searle 1969, the illocutionary-body claim) together with first-class linguistic reasoning, not any program's source.
