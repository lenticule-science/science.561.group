## Annotation

Introduces the **Duquenne–Guigues base** of attribute implications for a finite formal context — the canonical minimum-cardinality implication base, generated by the *pseudo-intents* of the context. The paper proves soundness, completeness, and minimum cardinality of the construction; the proof structure walks the pseudo-intents in induction on size, showing that each is implication-essential and that the base derives every implication holding in the context via the Armstrong rules.

The Duquenne–Guigues theorem (per [theorems/duquenne-guigues-completeness](../theorems/duquenne-guigues-completeness.md)) is the FCA lineage's canonical contribution to the theory of *minimum implicational bases*. It generalises and refines Armstrong's (1974) work on functional-dependency bases in relational databases, and is the structural foundation of attribute exploration (Ganter 1984) — the FCA lineage's interactive knowledge-acquisition algorithm.

For the predicated framing: the Duquenne–Guigues base is, in substrate language, the *minimum closure-rule presentation* of the FCA-substrate's Galois closure. Whether the construction generalises to richer value-domains and richer predicate vocabularies is open; see [duquenne-guigues-beyond-binary](questions/duquenne-guigues-beyond-binary.md).
