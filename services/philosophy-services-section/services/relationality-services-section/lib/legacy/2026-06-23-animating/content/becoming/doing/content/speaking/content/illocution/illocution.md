# illocution

**Illocution** is the second layer of [speaking](../../../docs/speaking.md): *the force* — Austin's illocutionary act, "what is done *in* saying" something.
Where [locution](../../locution/docs/locution.md) fixes the content, illocution fixes what is done with it: the same content can be asserted, denied, asked, promised, ordered, or warned.
Austin called this the act's *force*; Searle systematized it as *illocutionary force*, the conventional, intended point of the utterance.

This is the layer where the act takes on its stance.
To utter a content *as an assertion* is to take responsibility for its truth; *as a question*, to call for its settling; *as a denial*, to rule it out.
The force is not added decoration on a neutral content — it is what makes the utterance an act of one kind rather than another.

## What generates it

Illocution is generated by locution: there is no force without something to give force to.
It acts within the content locution has selected, charging it with a stance.

## What it generates

Illocution generates a *charged utterance* — a content now put forward with a definite force — and hands it to [perlocution](../../perlocution/docs/perlocution.md), whose work is the effect that charge then has.
Illocutions sort into kinds by *which* stance they take; the four basic stances toward a content's truth are the illocutionary [forces](../library/forces/docs/forces.md).
