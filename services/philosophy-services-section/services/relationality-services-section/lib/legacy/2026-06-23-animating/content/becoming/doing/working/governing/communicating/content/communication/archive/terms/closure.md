Closure is what a reader does in the [gutter](teaching/explanations/gutter.md): the active completion that joins one framed unit to the next. Scott McCloud's central observation in *Understanding Comics* (HarperCollins, 1993) is that the artifact never depicts the transition. The artifact depicts the units. The transition is the reader's work. A panel of a man raising an axe followed by a panel of a city skyline: the reader supplies the swing, the impact, the time elapsed, the consequence. Without closure, the page is a pile of stills. With closure, it is a story.

Closure is not metaphor. It is the actual cognitive act of joining units. McCloud catalogues six kinds of transition (moment-to-moment, action-to-action, subject-to-subject, scene-to-scene, aspect-to-aspect, non-sequitur), each demanding different completion work and tolerating different degrees of underdetermination. The relevance of his observation outside comics is that all sequential communicative practice — proofs, lectures, software documentation, signage routes, presentations, conversation — relies on the same operation. The artifact frames units. The reader closes between them. The artifact's responsibility is to make the closures it asks for performable and accountable.

## The act, distinct from the concept

This is the term *closure*. The corresponding parametric pattern is the concept [communicative-closure](teaching/explanations/communicative-closure.md). The concept is what every subtheory must instantiate to specify what counts as legitimate completion in its register; the term is the operation itself, what readers actually do.

## Where closure fails

Closure fails in two directions. It can be impossible — the reader has no way to perform it because the artifact has not provided enough constraint, and the gap is not licensed by shared training the audience has. This is [unaccountable-closure](unaccountable-closure.md): the artifact extracts work the reader cannot do correctly. It can also be unnecessary — the artifact has framed adjacent units so identical that no completion is required, and the page becomes a wall of restatement that exhausts the [closure-budget](closure-budget.md) without earning it.

The discipline lives between these failures: the artifact must demand closure that is performable, license the closures it asks for, and not extract closure-work it has not earned.
