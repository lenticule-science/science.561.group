Predicated sociology has multiple adjacent theoretical formations, each of which captures part of the circuit.
What follows positions predicated sociology against six adjacent schools — neoliberalism critique, post-liberalism proper, surveillance capitalism analysis, post-structuralism, left-populism, and autonomist Marxism — identifying what each captures, where each reaches its limit, and where predicated sociology takes the analysis further.

## Adjacent formation 1: neoliberalism critique

Neoliberalism critique (Harvey, Brown, Fraser) argues that the post-1970s reorganization of capitalism displaced the welfare-state framework in favor of market logics extended into all domains of social life, with the result that citizens become market actors, rights become commodities, and the collective dimensions of social provision are privatized or eliminated.

What this captures: the account of marketization is analytically accurate and historically well-documented.
The extension of market logics into education, healthcare, social relations, and self-conception is an empirical phenomenon the analysis tracks well.
Wendy Brown's account of neoliberalism as reason — as a rationality that restructures subjects' self-understanding — is particularly close to predicated sociology's account of [ontogenring](../teaching/explanations/ontogenring.md).

Where it reaches a limit: neoliberalism critique tends to treat the marketization as the fundamental transformation, with the cultural and affective effects following from it.
Predicated sociology inverts the emphasis: the affective-cultural apparatus is doing the metabolizing work, and the market is the energetic substrate that funds the apparatus.
The limit of neoliberalism critique is visible in its treatment of the platform: surveillance capitalism analysis (Zuboff, 2019) is a neoliberalism-critique extension that treats the platform primarily as a new form of extraction.
What it underspecifies is the *subject-formation* work the platform is simultaneously doing — the [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md) that is not reducible to data extraction.

## Adjacent formation 2: post-liberalism proper

Post-liberalism proper (Patrick Deneen, Adrian Pabst, various Christian Democratic traditions) argues that liberalism has produced a moral anthropology that is now producing its own disintegration: the liberal subject's radical autonomy and disembeddedness from tradition, community, and authority has corroded the conditions of its own possibility.

What this captures: the account of liberal atomization is empirically well-supported.
The dissolution of mediating institutions — church, union, civic association, extended family — under conditions of atomizing-liberalism is documented.

Where it reaches a limit: post-liberalism proper's proposed response is a communitarian restoration — a renewal of thick, tradition-embedded community against liberal atomism.
What it cannot account for is that the communities to which it appeals have themselves been extensively formatted by the apparatus.
The church community that post-liberalism appeals to as a corrective is, in many of its instantiations, an apparatus of [zen fascism](../teaching/explanations/zen-fascism.md), [harm governance](../teaching/explanations/harm-governance.md), and [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md).
The community is not prior to the apparatus; it has been constituted by the apparatus's prior operations.
This is what the ecclesial-recurrence analysis establishes.

## Adjacent formation 3: post-structuralism and discourse analysis

Post-structuralism (Foucault, Derrida, Laclau/Mouffe) argues that meaning is produced through difference and contest, that power operates through discourse and subject-formation, and that political identity is constructed rather than given.

What this captures: Foucault's genealogy of the confession, the pastoral, and governmentality is directly constitutive of predicated sociology's analytical framework.
The account of subject-formation through power-knowledge is foundational.

Where predicated sociology extends it: post-structuralism's commitment to discourse-as-constitutive tends to underspecify the material-energetic substrate within which discourse operates.
Foucault's accounts of institutions — the prison, the clinic, the school — presuppose the energetic surplus that funded those institutions without analyzing the surplus as a variable.
Predicated sociology's energetic-substrate analysis is what places the discursive-subject-formation analysis inside a material-historical account of the conditions that made the discursive operations possible and what is changing as those conditions change.

## Adjacent formation 4: left-populism

Left-populism (Chantal Mouffe, Ernesto Laclau, applied in Podemos, various Bernie-style movements) argues that the appropriate political response to neoliberalism is the construction of a populist political identity that aggregates the demands of subordinate groups against an oligarchic elite.

What this captures: the analysis of hegemony and counter-hegemony is well-specified.
The account of political identity as constructed rather than given is accurate and politically consequential.

Where it reaches a limit: left-populism's framework depends on the availability of political subjects who can be aggregated into a populist formation.
The savior-slave subject — who metabolizes structural contradiction through personal cultivation rather than political identity — is not easily aggregatable, not because of false consciousness but because the [coherent confusion](../teaching/explanations/coherent-confusion.md) that characterizes their condition is specifically not a condition from which political identity easily crystallizes.
Left-populism understates how thoroughly the apparatus has formatted the would-be populist subjects.

## Adjacent formation 5: autonomist Marxism

Autonomist Marxism (Negri, Hardt, Virno) argues that the post-Fordist capital extracts value not from industrial labor but from the general social capacities — communication, affect, cognition — of living subjects.
The concept of "immaterial labor" names the labor that produces immaterial products: communication, relationships, affects.

What this captures: the account of value-extraction from social capacities is analytically important.
The platform does extract value from the social labor of its users — this is what surveillance capitalism analysis documents and what immaterial-labor analysis predicted.
The affective and communicative work of platformal subjects is labor in a meaningful sense.

Where predicated sociology extends it: autonomist Marxism tends to find in immaterial labor the seeds of a new mode of production that could supersede capital.
Predicated sociology is more skeptical about the seed-of-liberation reading.
The same immaterial labor that is extractable by capital is also the metabolizing labor of [californication](../teaching/explanations/californication.md).
The subject who produces content, builds community, and performs emotional labor on the platform is simultaneously producing surplus value for the platform and performing the self-regulation the apparatus requires.
These are not two separate operations; they are one operation running on two registers.

## What predicated sociology adds

Predicated sociology's specific contribution against all these adjacencies is the integration of three registers — the cybernetic-feedback architecture of the platform, the subject-formation operations of californication, and the energetic-substrate analysis — into one account of how late-liberal governance reproduces itself without coordination.

None of the adjacent formations integrates all three.
Neoliberalism critique has the economic register but underspecifies the affective-subject-formation register.
Post-liberalism has the subject-formation concern but lacks the cybernetic and energetic analysis.
Post-structuralism has the subject-formation register but lacks the material-energetic substrate.
Autonomist Marxism has both the economic and the social-production registers but underspecifies the apparatus's closure.
Left-populism has the political-construction apparatus but underspecifies the apparatus's capacity to format the would-be political subjects.

Predicated sociology integrates by running the three registers through the same analytical framework.
The result is an account in which the apparatus's self-reproduction is simultaneously economic (the platform's extraction model), cultural (californication's affective formatting), material-energetic (the substrate's contraction driving platformalization), and structural (the closure's metabolizing of coherent subject-positions).
No single-register account is sufficient.
