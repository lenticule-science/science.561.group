# The bounded modal-Horn fragment of the substrate's predication algebra is substrate-checkable; clauses outside it are captain-judged

**Claim.** Let a *charter clause* be a textual policy commitment authored on a polity entity per [substrate-policy](teaching/explanations/substrate-policy.md). Let $\mathrm{ClosureRule}(\mathcal{S})$ be the set of substrate [closure rules](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md) expressible in the substrate's algebra: premise pattern over substrate cells, optional guard expression in the substrate's message language, conclusion pattern over substrate cells. A charter clause $\phi$ is *substrate-checkable* iff there exists a finite set of closure rules $\{r_1, \ldots, r_n\} \subseteq \mathrm{ClosureRule}(\mathcal{S})$ whose conjoint firing on the substrate's settled state produces a witness cell $w_\phi$ exactly when $\phi$ is violated and produces no such cell exactly when $\phi$ holds.

This theorem makes two claims:

- **Sufficiency (proved):** Every charter clause expressible in the **bounded modal-Horn fragment** of the substrate's predication algebra (defined below) is substrate-checkable, by an explicit compile to closure rules.
- **Lower bound on the un-checkable side (proved):** Charter clauses requiring unbounded universal quantification over substrate-unenumerable sets, negation-as-failure over an open universe, premises referring to substrate-external content, or modal iteration beyond the substrate's accessibility-relation depth bound, are *not* substrate-checkable by any finite set of closure rules in $\mathrm{ClosureRule}(\mathcal{S})$ as currently constituted.

We do **not** claim the bounded modal-Horn fragment is the *exact* substrate-checkable fragment. Auxiliary-predicate encodings — introducing a derived predicate that monotonically witnesses a checked condition, then writing the clause against the derived predicate — can sometimes lift a clause out of one of the un-checkable categories. The claim in this direction is that every clause failing one of the four conditions above has no *direct* expression in $\mathrm{ClosureRule}(\mathcal{S})$, and any encoding that does check it must augment the substrate's vocabulary with new predicates whose update discipline is itself within the bounded modal-Horn fragment.

The bounded modal-Horn fragment is defined by:

- **Horn-shaped.** $\phi$ is a finite conjunction of premises (each a [knowledge-join](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/knowledge-join.md)-positive cell pattern with bind variables) implying a single conclusion (a positive cell pattern over the same bind variables, or a violation-witness cell).
- **Bounded-modal.** Premises and conclusion may invoke the [modal-necessity operator](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/modal-necessity-operator.md) $\Box_R$ along a [bilattice accessibility relation](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/bilattice-accessibility-relation.md) $R$ that is itself substrate content (an entity with cells defining the relation), with the world-set finite or admitting the finite model property per [modal-finite-model-property](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/modal-finite-model-property.md).
- **Closed under the substrate's value-domain algebra.** Premise predicates and conclusion predicates are drawn from the substrate's vocabulary; values are drawn from the substrate's [predication fiber algebra](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md); the guard expression is a message-language expression evaluable by the substrate's runtime.

A charter clause is *not* substrate-checkable when it requires any of:

- **Quantification over an unbounded world-set** (e.g., "every possible action by any future agent"). The substrate's enumeration is unbounded; the closure rule does not terminate.
- **Reference to substrate-external content the substrate cannot read** (e.g., "actions consistent with the captain's intent" where intent is not authored in the substrate). The premise has no cells to fire on.
- **Negation of an open formula** (e.g., "no agent has ever asserted X without the captain's permission" where the existential is unbounded). The Horn-shape is broken; the substrate cannot enumerate the absence.
- **Higher-order modality** (e.g., "necessarily, possibly, X holds" with the modal operators iterated beyond the bound the substrate's accessibility-relation depth supports). Per [modal-complexity-bounds](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/modal-complexity-bounds.md), iterated modality without finite-model-property witnesses exceeds the substrate's decidability envelope.

A clause failing substrate-checkability remains a first-class polity-entity record but is enforced through the *captain-judged* lifecycle: the substrate emits a candidate-violation [witness](teaching/explanations/witness.md), the captain resolves it through the witness-resolution discipline. The substrate carries the clause; it does not check it.

## Why this holds

The argument has two directions.

**Part 1 (sufficiency): bounded modal-Horn clauses compile to closure rules.** Per [closure-rule](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md), a closure rule is a premise pattern over substrate cells with bind variables, an optional guard, and a conclusion pattern. A bounded modal-Horn clause $\phi = (\bigwedge_i \alpha_i) \to \beta$ (each $\alpha_i$ a positive premise, $\beta$ a positive conclusion or violation-witness) compiles directly:

- Each premise $\alpha_i$ becomes a cell pattern in the rule's premise list; bind variables shared across premises unify under the substrate's standard closure-rule activation discipline.
- The conclusion $\beta$ becomes the rule's conclusion pattern; if $\beta$ is a violation-witness, the conclusion writes a cell at the witness predicate (e.g., `(action:<id>, violates-charter, clause:<slug>) = t`).
- A modal premise $\Box_R \alpha$ at world $w$ compiles to the closure rule "for every $w'$ accessible from $w$ via $R$, the cell pattern $\alpha[w']$ holds" — an iteration whose termination is per [governance-decision-modal-decidability](governance-decision-modal-decidability.md) bounded in PSPACE for local consequence and EXPTIME for global consequence on a finite accessibility relation.
- The guard, when present, encodes the message-language expression that the substrate's runtime evaluates against the bind context per [closure-rule](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md).

The compiled rule fires under the substrate's settlement discipline; per [settlement-via-closure-2-monad](settlement-via-closure-2-monad.md), the firing terminates and reaches a deterministic fixed point. The witness $w_\phi$ is present iff the violation conditions hold.

**Part 2 (lower bound on the un-checkable side): clauses with the four un-checkable features have no direct expression as closure rules.** A closure rule's premise is a finite conjunction of cell patterns; a closure rule's conclusion is a finite list of cell templates. Per [closure-rule](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md), the rule has no facility for unbounded existential quantification (the substrate cannot enumerate "for every action that has ever been taken" without bound) and no facility for negation-as-failure over an open universe (the substrate's settlement is monotone; per [knowledge-join](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/knowledge-join.md) the value-domain join is $\vee_k$, which never retracts evidence). A clause requiring such a feature cannot be directly expressed; an indirect expression must reify the missing facility as a new substrate predicate (per the "auxiliary-predicate encoding" caveat above), and the new predicate's update rule is itself a closure rule — hence subject to the same fragment constraints. The four directly-unexpressible features:

- *Unbounded universal quantification.* The closure rule's premise pattern unifies bind variables across substrate cells. If the universal quantifier ranges over a set the substrate does not finitely-enumerate, the rule has no finite premise pattern to match. The substrate's enumeration discipline is bounded by the substrate's content; clauses ranging over substrate-unrepresentable sets cannot fire.
- *Negation of open formulas.* "No $x$ such that $\alpha(x)$" is the absence of a witness; the closure rule cannot fire on absence in a monotone fixed-point system. The substrate's response is to maintain the contrapositive — a positive witness asserting "every checked $x$ satisfies $\neg \alpha(x)$" — which is bounded only if the universe of $x$ is bounded. For unbounded $x$, no closure rule expresses the original.
- *Substrate-external premises.* A premise referring to content the substrate does not author (e.g., a person's intent, a third-party system's state) cannot be matched against substrate cells. The premise has no firing surface.
- *Iterated modality beyond the depth bound.* Per [modal-complexity-bounds](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/modal-complexity-bounds.md), modal-formula evaluation is EXPTIME for global consequence; a clause requiring deeper modal iteration (e.g., a fixed-point of the modal operator over an infinite accessibility chain) exceeds the substrate's decidability envelope and does not terminate as a closure rule.

Combining the two directions: the bounded modal-Horn fragment is a subset of the substrate-checkable fragment, and any clause outside the substrate-checkable fragment fails one of the four un-checkable features. Whether the substrate-checkable fragment *strictly* exceeds the bounded modal-Horn fragment depends on which auxiliary-predicate encodings the captain is willing to author; the engineering content of this theorem is that the bounded modal-Horn fragment is the *direct-compile* surface and clauses outside it require either auxiliary-predicate reification or captain judgement.

## Engineering consequence

- **Charter clauses partition into substrate-checkable and captain-judged.** The captain authoring a charter can be told, per clause, which fragment the clause falls in: bounded modal-Horn ⇒ substrate-checkable ⇒ closure rule compiled and active; outside the fragment ⇒ captain-judged ⇒ closure rule emits candidate-violation witness for captain resolution. The partition is mechanical; the captain need not pre-judge whether the clause is "operational" or "aspirational."
- **The seed-set choice is the choice of which fragment-residents to compile first.** A seed set of operational-discipline clauses (dispatch, capability rotation, spec-edit gate) is a seed of bounded modal-Horn clauses with single-premise patterns over already-authored substrate state — the lowest-attention compile path. A seed of governance/federation clauses (operations-account approval, federation receive scope, decision-record provenance) is bounded modal-Horn with one or two additional cell-shape definitions. A seed of function-commitment clauses (Black Liberation, Indigenous Sovereignty) is *outside* the fragment and lives in the captain-judged lane regardless of seed prioritization; the substrate's compile of these clauses produces only the inference-trigger closure rule, not the violation-checker.
- **The captain's authoring discipline acquires a pre-publish check.** For each clause the captain proposes, the substrate runs a fragment-membership check: does the clause's stated premise compile to a closure-rule premise pattern? Does its conclusion compile to a closure-rule conclusion pattern? Is its modal depth within the substrate's accessibility-relation envelope? The check returns "compilable / requires-witness-only / not-expressible" before the clause is published, per [substrate-policy](teaching/explanations/substrate-policy.md)'s pre-approval discipline.
- **The substrate's audit surface witnesses the partition.** Per [self-representation](teaching/explanations/self-representation.md), the partition is recorded as substrate content: each charter clause carries a `(clause:<slug>, substrate-checkability, full | partial | captain-only) = t` cell. A federation peer reading the polity's charter learns not only the clauses but also which the substrate enforces structurally and which are carried as commitments awaiting captain judgement.
- **Outside-fragment clauses still benefit from the substrate's discipline.** A captain-judged clause carries a candidate-violation [witness](teaching/explanations/witness.md) with full substrate audit trail: the witness records the agent's inference heuristic output, the closure-rule firing context, the bind values that triggered the candidate. Per the witness-resolution discipline, the captain's resolution becomes substrate content (`accepted` / `discarded` with rationale), and the substrate accumulates a corpus of resolved candidates that may, over time, reveal a bounded modal-Horn refinement of the original clause that *is* compilable. The fragment boundary is a structural constraint, not a permanent fence.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Charter clauses that pretend to be substrate-checkable but require unbounded enumeration.** A clause stating "no agent has ever taken action X" requires enumeration over the substrate's full event history; per Part 2, the closure rule does not terminate as the history grows without bound. The substrate refuses such clauses at the pre-publish check; the captain either bounds the clause (e.g., "no agent has taken action X in the current session") or accepts it as captain-judged.
- **Per-clause runtime feature flags.** A scheme that compiles a clause to a closure rule only "if FEATURE_X is enabled" introduces runtime-resident program logic, in violation of [driver-substrate-split](teaching/explanations/driver-substrate-split.md). The substrate refuses; the activation condition must itself be a substrate cell per [closure-rule](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md).
- **Closure rules over substrate-external state.** A clause whose premise references the captain's prose-document of intent or a third-party system's state has no firing surface in the substrate. The captain must either reify the external state into substrate cells (via an interfacer with declared faithfulness per [doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor](doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor.md)) or accept the clause as captain-judged.
- **Negation-as-failure over the open universe.** A clause asserting "no agent without authority X has performed action Y" requires checking the absence of a witness across an open authority-set; per Part 2 this breaks the Horn-shape. The substrate's response is to require the contrapositive form: "every agent who performed action Y carries authority X" — a Horn clause expressible if the agent-set on action Y is bounded by substrate enumeration (which it is, per [self-representation](teaching/explanations/self-representation.md), because every action emission is a substrate-recorded event).

## What the theorem requires

- The substrate's [closure-rule](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md) pattern and [substrate-policy](teaching/explanations/substrate-policy.md) discipline are in force (the closure-rule shape and message-language are fixed).
- The accessibility relation $R$ is a finite [bilattice accessibility relation](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/bilattice-accessibility-relation.md) on a finite [world set](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/world-set.md), or admits the finite model property per [modal-finite-model-property](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/modal-finite-model-property.md).
- The substrate's runtime implements the closure-rule firing per [settlement-via-closure-2-monad](settlement-via-closure-2-monad.md) (terminating, deterministic, lax-idempotent).
- A pre-publish fragment-membership check is part of the charter-authoring flow (per [substrate-policy](teaching/explanations/substrate-policy.md)).

## Position

This theorem characterises the structural boundary between charter clauses the substrate enforces and charter clauses the substrate carries-but-cannot-enforce. It is the answer to "which charter clauses become substrate-checkable first" at a structural level — the bounded modal-Horn fragment, with seed-set choices ordered by attention-cost within the fragment and function-commitment clauses always residing outside, in the captain-judged lane. The theorem does not adjudicate the seed-set ordering itself (that is a captain choice over which fragment-residents to compile first); it adjudicates which clauses are *in principle* compilable. Composes with [governance-decision-modal-decidability](governance-decision-modal-decidability.md) (the decidability-envelope of governance decisions is the same envelope; the theorem is the static-clause analogue of the per-decision decidability claim), with [substrate-policy](teaching/explanations/substrate-policy.md) and [closure-rule](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md) (the operational compile path), and with [witness](teaching/explanations/witness.md) (the captain-judged lane for clauses outside the fragment).
