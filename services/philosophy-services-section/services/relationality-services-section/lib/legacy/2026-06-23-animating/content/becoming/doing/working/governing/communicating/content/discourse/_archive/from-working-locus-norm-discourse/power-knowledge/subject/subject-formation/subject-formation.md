# Subject formation

The social process by which a person becomes a *subject* — acquires a subject-position, internalizes the norms and predicates of that position, and comes to act and feel in ways consistent with it. Not the making of a biological individual but the making of a *socially intelligible* individual: one whose actions are legible within the predicate structure of the social field, and whose legibility is itself the condition of their social existence.

Althusser's interpellation — the hailing that recruits individuals into subject-positions — names one mechanism. Foucault's subjectification — the incitement to produce oneself through confession, examination, and self-regulation — names another. Cybernetic postliberalism's subject-formation — the production of savior-slave subjects through affect governance — is a third, historically specific case. Predicated sociology treats all three as variations on a common substrate pattern.

## Substrate realization

Subject formation is the settling of subject-predicate cells: the process by which cells of the form

$$\langle e, \texttt{recognizes-self-as}, s \rangle, \quad \langle e, \texttt{regulated-by-norm}, n \rangle, \quad \langle e, \texttt{inhabits-subject-position}, \sigma \rangle$$

move from $\bot$ (no information about $e$'s subject-position) to $\mathbf{t}$ (the subject-position is settled) via the accumulation of witnessing events — institutional acts of recognition, successful interpellations, affective formatting events, confessional utterances, and self-regulatory practices.

The morphism [interpellation](../morphisms/interpellation.md) is the canonical subject-formation event: a single witnessing event that, when successful, initiates the settlement of subject-position cells. Subject formation is complete (in the work-vocabulary sense) when the subject-predicate cells have settled to $\mathbf{t}$ and are stable under the social settling's closure rules — meaning the subject now fires closure rules that reproduce their own subject-predicate cells.

## Self-reproducing subject positions

The key structural property of formed subjects is that they become *closure-rule participants*: they fire rules that reproduce the social settling, including the rules that settled their own subject-position cells. Mathematically, this is the closure-rule analogue of a fixed point:

$$v(\langle e, \texttt{inhabits-subject-position}, \sigma\rangle) = \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{$e$ fires rules in $\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}$}$$

where $\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}$ is the closure-rule subsystem associated with subject-position $\sigma$. The subject doesn't just *occupy* a position — they *enact* the rules that make that position a stable feature of the social settling. This is the substrate-algebraic content of Foucault's claim that subjects are not merely subjected but are produced as productive participants in their own subjectification.

## Subject formation and affect governance

[Affect governance](affect-governance.md) is one mechanism of subject formation: the settling of affective predicate cells installs the subject in a specific affective orientation that reproduces structural conditions. The subject who routes crisis through personal affect is formed as a subject who does not fire collective-action rules — the affective formatting is simultaneously a structural formatting.

The formed subject of cybernetic postliberalism — the [savior-slave subject](../../../cybernetic-postliberalism/terms/savior-slave-subject.md) — is substrate-algebraically a subject whose action-predicate cells are structured by two simultaneously active closure-rule regimes: a high-determination regime on collective-structural predicates (no routes to structural transformation) and an aperiodic-regime drive on personal-affect predicates (endless generation of new affective aims without resolution). The subject is always settling on something — but what they are settling on is not their own liberation.
