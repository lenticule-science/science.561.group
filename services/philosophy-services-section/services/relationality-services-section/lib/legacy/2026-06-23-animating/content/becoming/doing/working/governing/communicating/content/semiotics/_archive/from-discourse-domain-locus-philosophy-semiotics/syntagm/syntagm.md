# Syntagm

The *horizontal axis of sign-combination*: how sign-vehicles co-occur within an actual utterance, sequence, image, or composed sign-act. A syntagm is a *combination present* — what the producer of the sign-act has actually committed to placing together.

Saussure (per [Saussure 1916](references/saussure1916.md)) introduced the distinction between *syntagmatic* (combinatorial) and *associative* (later: *paradigmatic*, see [paradigm](paradigm.md)) relations as the two structural axes of langue. A linguistic utterance is a syntagm of phonemes, morphemes, and words; a film shot is a syntagm of compositional elements; a corpus note is a syntagm of frontmatter relations and prose.

The structural content of *syntagm* is that *what is together is together for a structural reason*: co-occurrence is meaningful, not incidental. The discipline holds that any sign-act admits syntagmatic analysis once its compositional units are identified, and that the rules governing licit syntagms are part of the [code](code.md).

The substrate-level reading places a syntagm at the *cell-cluster* of co-asserted predications within a single source/scope/timestamp triple — the predications a community asserts *together* form a syntagm in the substrate's predication-graph. The closure-rule subset's combination-rules are the substrate-level realisation of syntagmatic constraints.
