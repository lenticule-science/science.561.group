# Locality is a substrate commitment, not a performance optimization

A common reading of [wavefront](../teaching/explanations/wavefront.md) propagation is that it is a clever implementation trick: instead of evaluating closure rules over the whole graph at once, the runtime walks a frontier and reaches the same fixed-point with less memory. This reading is engineering-true but substrate-false. Locality is not a runtime optimization layered on top of a global semantics; it is *the substrate's commitment to what a computational primitive is allowed to depend on*.

The negation is sharp. A closure rule whose premises range over the whole [predication graph](../objects/predication-graph-store.md) — "if every entity in the substrate satisfies $\varphi$, conclude $\psi$" — is not admissible substrate content. Such a rule cannot be applied within a bounded wavefront. It can only be applied by loading the entire graph, evaluating $\varphi$ globally, and emitting $\psi$. The runtime that admits such rules has accepted a contract its substrate cannot satisfy at scale: as the graph grows, the rule's evaluation cost grows with it, and the relationship between rules-declared and work-performed is no longer bounded by structural locality but by graph size.

The substrate's discipline rules this out at the level of *what a closure rule is*. Per [closure-rule](../teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md), a rule's premises must be expressible as a bounded-radius graph pattern from a single anchor entity. The radius is part of the rule's specification — substrate content, not runtime configuration. A rule with declared radius $r$ examines at most $O(d^r)$ predications where $d$ is the substrate's average degree; the substrate's runtime can apply the rule at any entity by loading only that entity and its $r$-radius neighborhood. The wavefront is then the natural execution shape: each application is local, memory is bounded by wavefront width, and the global fixed-point emerges from local applications.

The engineering consequence is that a predicated computer's effective scale is not bounded by RAM. A substrate with billions of entities can be settled by a runtime with megabytes of working memory if the wavefront width stays bounded — which it does whenever the closure rules genuinely respect their declared locality. The pathological cases (a rule whose effective radius is unbounded, a join operation whose intermediate result is graph-sized) appear as concrete violations of the locality contract. Per [unbounded-radius-rules](../learning/questions/unbounded-radius-rules.md), the substrate's response is either to reject such rules at admission time or to factor them into bounded-radius rules whose composition produces the same conclusions.

Several engineering disciplines fall out:

- **No global "load-the-graph" operation.** The substrate's read primitives are scoped: load one entity, load one entity's neighborhood, load entities matching a small pattern. A primitive that returns "the whole graph" is out of substrate. If the runtime offers such an operation as a convenience for offline analysis, that is a host-level affordance, not a substrate primitive.
- **Closure-rule application is the smallest unit of work.** [Settle](../morphisms/settle.md) is composed of closure-rule applications, not of global iterations. Per-application cost is bounded by the rule's radius; total cost is bounded by `applications × per-application-cost`, where applications is bounded by [fuel-bound](../teaching/explanations/fuel-bound.md).
- **Wavefront width is the operational throttle.** The runtime can pause a wavefront, persist its state, resume it later. Per [branched-substrate-state](../teaching/explanations/branched-substrate-state.md), it can fork a wavefront across branches. The wavefront is itself substrate content during settlement; the substrate's discipline of recording what is in flight makes this possible.
- **Prior-art confirms the shape.** Tick-based simulation engines, sheaf-theoretic models of distributed computation, dataflow runtimes, and CRDT-based replication all converge on the same locality contract: bounded radius per step, monotonic effects, fixed-point as the meaning of "settled." The predication substrate makes this contract its central commitment rather than a performance hint, and the result is a computer whose work is bounded by structural locality regardless of substrate size.

The substrate's locality contract is what makes [federation](../theories/agential-orchestration-platform/concepts/federation.md), [replication](../teaching/explanations/replication.md), and [branched substrate state](../teaching/explanations/branched-substrate-state.md) cheap. Two substrates federating are two locality-bounded wavefronts exchanging predications at their interface; the cost is bounded by the interface, not by the participants' total content. A replica catching up is a wavefront over the replication delta; the cost is bounded by the delta's width. A branch's settlement is independent of all other branches' settlement. None of these properties require additional engineering — they are direct consequences of every substrate operation respecting locality at the primitive level.
