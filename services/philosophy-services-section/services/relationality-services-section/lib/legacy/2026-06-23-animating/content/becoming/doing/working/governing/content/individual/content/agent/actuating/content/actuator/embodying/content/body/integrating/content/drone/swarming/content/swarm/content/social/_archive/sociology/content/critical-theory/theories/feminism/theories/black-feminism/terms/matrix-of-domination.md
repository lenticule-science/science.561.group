The *matrix of domination* is [Patricia Hill Collins](../figures/patricia-hill-collins.md)'s name, developed in *Black Feminist Thought* (1990), for the structure the [Combahee River Collective](../figures/combahee-river-collective.md) called interlocking.
Domination is organized through the intersection of multiple systems (race, class, gender, sexuality, nation, age) operating across multiple domains of power.
The framing refuses both the additive reading of oppressions (race + class + gender) and the reduction of the structure to any single axis.

In Collins's most worked-out version, the matrix operates through four domains:

- The **structural** domain — the macro-level institutions: economy, polity, family, education, law, healthcare.
  Where the major distributive arrangements are made and where the categorical inequalities are reproduced at scale.
- The **disciplinary** domain — bureaucratic rule, surveillance, the institutional protocols that govern movement through the structural domain.
  Where the structural inequalities are translated into individual experience through procedures that present themselves as neutral.
- The **hegemonic** domain — the cultural production (media, schooling, religion, common sense) that legitimates the structural and disciplinary arrangements and renders them as natural rather than as produced by the structure.
- The **interpersonal** domain — the everyday encounters in which the structure is enacted between persons.
  Where individuals occupy positions in the matrix and act on each other from those positions.

The four domains operate together.
A given case (the Black woman worker who experiences workplace harassment, say) is structured at all four levels simultaneously: by the structural arrangements that put her in the position she occupies, by the disciplinary apparatus that processes her complaint or fails to, by the hegemonic frames that determine whether her account is heard as legitimate, and by the interpersonal encounters in which the harassment occurs.
Single-axis analysis cannot describe the case; multi-domain analysis can.

The framing converges with the [`kyriarchism`](../../../../queer/theories/kyriarchism/_index.md) analysis (interlocking systems of domination as a single structure of rule) and with the [`intersectionality`](intersectionality.md) analytic (categories produced through their intersection rather than summable from their separation).
The three terms describe the same object from three points of entry: kyriarchism from feminist theology, intersectionality from Black-feminist legal theory, and the matrix of domination from Black-feminist sociology.

In this library the matrix-of-domination framing is also one of the analytical heuristics used in the sibling [`abolition`](../../../../abolition/_index.md) subdomain (the abolitionist analysis of the carceral system requires reading across all four domains) and in the [`povinellian`](../../../../povinellian/_index.md) subdomain's analysis of organized abandonment.
