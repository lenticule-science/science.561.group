# Codeswitching

The *per-utterance* operation by which a sign-act produced under one [code](teaching/explanations/code.md) within a federated semiotic system invokes a portion produced under a different code, without translating between the two codes — the recipient is presumed to operate both.

The classical setting is bilingual conversation, where a speaker switches between languages mid-utterance; the discipline reads codeswitching as the same operation across any two semiotic systems that share a [semiosphere](teaching/explanations/semiosphere.md): a film that switches between visual and verbal codes, a code that switches between formal and informal registers, a corpus note that switches between prose and equation.

## Operational signature

$$\mathrm{codeswitch}: \mathcal{S}_{12} \times V_1^* \times V_2^* \times \cdots \to \mathrm{Utterance}_{\mathcal{S}_{12}}$$

— given a federated semiotic system $\mathcal{S}_{12}$ and per-source sequences of sign-vehicles drawn from each component system, codeswitching produces an utterance whose [syntagm](teaching/explanations/syntagm.md) interleaves the components without translating them.

## Substrate-level structural facts

**Codeswitching is scope-restriction-per-cell.** The substrate-level reading places codeswitching as the operation that asserts cells *each in a specific scope* (per the [predicated-computing scope](../../../../information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/concepts/discourse-community-as-fiber.md) discipline) within a federated knowledge system. The federated system's settling integrates the per-scope assertions; the recipient who operates both codes settles each portion under that code's closure rules.

**Codeswitching presupposes the federated system.** Codeswitching is internal to $\mathcal{S}_{12}$; it does not federate $\mathcal{S}_1$ and $\mathcal{S}_2$ at the moment of utterance, it presupposes the federation has already happened (in the recipient's competence). When the recipient does not operate both codes, codeswitching collapses into untranslated sign-vehicles — not a different semiotic operation, but a failure of the codeswitching's competence-presupposition.

**Codeswitching is asymmetric in the semiosphere's core/periphery.** Per [Lotman 1990](references/lotman1990.md), codeswitching from a peripheral to a central code is more frequent than the reverse; central codes have higher prestige and stability, and switching toward them carries different semiotic load than switching away.

**Halliday's register and field as codeswitching axes.** Per [Halliday 1978](references/halliday1978.md), the register/field/tenor/mode axes of social semiotic are the parameter-space along which codeswitching takes place: a switch in register is a codeswitch within the same denotational language but across two registerial codes.

## Comparison

- **Vs. translation.** Translation invokes the identity-mapping to reconcile sign-objects across two systems; codeswitching does not — it preserves each portion *in its own code*. A translated utterance is mono-coded in the target; a codeswitched utterance is multi-coded.
- **Vs. specialization.** Specialization refines within a single code; codeswitching crosses two codes at the syntagmatic level.
