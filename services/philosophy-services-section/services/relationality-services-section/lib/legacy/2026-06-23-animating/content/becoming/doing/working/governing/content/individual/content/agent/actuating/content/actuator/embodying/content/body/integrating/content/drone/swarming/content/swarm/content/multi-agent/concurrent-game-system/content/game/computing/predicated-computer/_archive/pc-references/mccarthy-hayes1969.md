## Source

J. McCarthy, P. J. Hayes. "Some Philosophical Problems from the Standpoint of Artificial Intelligence." In *Machine Intelligence 4*, B. Meltzer and D. Michie (eds.), Edinburgh University Press, 1969, pp. 463–502.

## Form

Book chapter / programmatic paper. The founding paper of the situation-calculus tradition and the source of the *frame problem*.

## Content summary

Introduces the *situation calculus* for representing actions and change: the world has *situations* (snapshots of the state of affairs); *fluents* (situation-dependent predicates); *actions* (functions producing new situations from old ones). Names and articulates the *frame problem*: an axiomatization of action effects must specify not only what changes when an action is performed but also what *does not* change, and naive axiomatizations require a quadratic number of "frame axioms" in the number of fluents and actions. The paper also introduces the *qualification problem* (an action's preconditions can have unboundedly many qualifications) and the *ramification problem* (an action's effects can propagate transitively through the world). These three problems became the load-bearing problem-statements for fifty years of follow-up work in knowledge representation, culminating in [Reiter's 2001 textbook](reiter2001.md).
