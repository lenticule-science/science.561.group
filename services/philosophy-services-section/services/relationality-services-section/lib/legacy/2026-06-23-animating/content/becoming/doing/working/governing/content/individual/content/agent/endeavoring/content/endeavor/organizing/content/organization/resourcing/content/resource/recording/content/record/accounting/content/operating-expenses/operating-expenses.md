Operating expenses (OpEx) are the costs of running the business that are not directly attributable to producing goods or services.
They include rent, utilities, [insurance](./business-insurance.md), administrative salaries, marketing, office supplies, software subscriptions, professional fees, and [depreciation](./depreciation.md).

The distinction between [cost of goods sold](./cost-of-goods-sold.md) (direct costs) and operating expenses (indirect costs) matters for understanding profitability:

- **Revenue − COGS = Gross profit** (how efficiently the business produces)
- **Gross profit − Operating expenses = Operating income** (how efficiently the business runs overall)

A business can have strong [gross margins](./gross-margin.md) (efficient production) but still lose money if operating expenses are too high — too much rent, too many administrative staff, too much spent on marketing relative to revenue.

Operating expenses are often categorized as **fixed** (don't change with sales volume — rent, insurance, loan payments) or **variable** (change with sales volume — utilities, supplies, part-time labor).
Understanding which expenses are fixed vs. variable is essential for [break-even analysis](./break-even-analysis.md) and for knowing how much revenue decline the business can absorb before losing money.
