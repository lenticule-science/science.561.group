# Predicated computer

A **predicated computer** is a computer whose primary substrate is the [predication](teaching/explanations/predication-substrate.md): data is stored as $(s, p, o)$ [predication cells](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/predication-cell.md) carrying values in the substrate's value-domain, and computation is the addition, removal, and traversal of those cells. The substrate choice is ontological — what *kind* of computer this is, at the level of what data primitively is and what computation primitively does — not architectural. A graph database layered on a boolean computer is not a predicated computer in this sense; the addressing, the storage primitives, and the operator semantics on a predicated computer all proceed in terms of predication cells rather than memory cells.

A predicated computer's content is a [predication valuation](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-valuation.md) $v$ into [predication fiber algebras](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md), one per [predicate](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/predicate.md), assembled into a [predication settling](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) as the runtime advances. Its closure machinery is a [closure operator](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-operator.md) generated by a [closure-rule system](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/closure-rule-system.md). The substrate is fibered over its [predicate category](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predicate-category.md) as a [predication fibration](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md), refined to a [predication hyperdoctrine](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-hyperdoctrine.md) when the substrate's quantifier adjoints, Frobenius reciprocity, and Beck-Chevalley coherence hold.

## How a predicated computer differs from a boolean computer

A predicated computer contrasts with a boolean computer — one whose data is two-valued and whose computation is the manipulation of those values held at memory addresses. Most architectures realized in physical hardware to date are boolean computers. The substrate choice is ontological, not architectural: layering a graph database on a boolean-computer store does not produce a predicated computer, because storage, addressing, and operations still presume the boolean substrate underneath. Two contrasts make the distinction concrete:

- **A query is a structured traversal, not a fetch-from-address.** Starting from an entity, one follows edges of named predicates to reach related entities. There is no `fetch(address)` primitive — the closest analog is "what is related to entity $e$ by predicate $p$?", a question the substrate answers by enumerating the predication cells incident to $e$ under $p$.
- **A write is the addition or retraction of a predication, not a value-mutation.** There are no stored values at addresses to mutate. Editing a predicated computer's content is admitting a new predication, retracting an existing one (per the substrate's [retraction](../morphisms/retraction.md) discipline), or — when the value-domain admits it — raising an existing predication's value monotonically toward fixed-point in the [knowledge order](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md) $\leq_k$.

The engineering content of predicated computing is the study of what such a computer is: what its operators are, how its state persists, how it handles concurrency and federation, what closure operations it supports, what guarantees follow from the substrate choice. This object is the engineering target the corpus characterizes; the surrounding [concepts](teaching/explanations/), [objects](./), and [morphisms](../morphisms/) develop the structure it commits to.

## What the substrate commits to

A predicated computer commits to three engineering invariants that follow from the substrate being a fibered predication-valuation:

- **The unit of storage is the predication cell, not the memory cell.** What lives in storage is $(s, p, o)$ triples carrying values in the predication fiber algebra at $p$. A cell is identified by the triple, not by an address. The store has no fetch-from-address primitive.
- **The unit of operation is the relational primitive.** The substrate offers primitive operations on cells — variants of *assert this predication*, *retract this predication*, *traverse from this entity along this predicate*. Higher-level operators are compositions of these primitives. An operator with a richer signature must decompose into the primitives, or it is not an operator on the predication substrate.
- **Identity is relational.** An entity is constituted by the predications it participates in, not by an address or a stored value. Two entities are the same when their incident-predication structure is the same; an entity persists when its predications persist; an entity is observed by traversing its predications.

These three are not independent — the predication cell as the storage unit forces the operators to act on cells, which forces identity to be relational. Together they determine what the engineering theory characterizes.

## Completeness criteria

A predicated computer (or a sub-region of one) is *complete* in the substrate's sense when it satisfies the seven [closure conditions](teaching/explanations/closure-conditions.md) within its scope: bare admission, identity closure, composite closure, reference closure, constraint classification, procedure closure, and coherence closure. A complete sub-region is a [locale](locale.md) — a predicated computer at its scope. A predicated computer at the top scope is the locale of itself; a sub-region that has not yet satisfied all seven conditions is at an earlier development stage and is admissible (it can still operate) but is *less than* a complete sub-computer.

The completeness criteria are what makes the predicated computer auditable: a region claiming to be complete is checkable against the seven conditions; a region with declared gaps (missing shapes, missing operations, vocabulary debt) is checkable for what it lacks.

## What the substrate does NOT commit to

The substrate does not commit to a particular [residuated lattice](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/residuated-lattice.md) $\mathbf{L}$ for the value-domain (Boolean $\mathbf{2}$, the four-valued $\mathbf{FOUR} = \mathbf{2}^\bowtie$, larger residuated lattices are all admissible), to a particular operator vocabulary beyond the primitives, to a particular storage realization (file-based, network-attached, in-memory, federated are all admissible realizations), or to a particular closure-rule-language fragment (different fragments give different [regimes](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/four-regime-dichotomy.md) of computational behavior). These are degrees of freedom for particular predicated computers — what a specific implementation chooses — and the engineering theory's job is to characterize what differs and what stays invariant across the choices.

## Subtheories

[Agential orchestration platform](theories/agential-orchestration-platform/_index.md) is a subtheory specializing the parent: a predicated computer where agents (human and AI) are the operative actors and where their skills, runbooks, and shapes are themselves entities in the same store. Other subtheories may specialize differently — a CRDT-replicated multi-instance computer with no agential layer, a single-machine epistemic computer, a federated computer with no central store — each commits to additional structure beyond the substrate.
