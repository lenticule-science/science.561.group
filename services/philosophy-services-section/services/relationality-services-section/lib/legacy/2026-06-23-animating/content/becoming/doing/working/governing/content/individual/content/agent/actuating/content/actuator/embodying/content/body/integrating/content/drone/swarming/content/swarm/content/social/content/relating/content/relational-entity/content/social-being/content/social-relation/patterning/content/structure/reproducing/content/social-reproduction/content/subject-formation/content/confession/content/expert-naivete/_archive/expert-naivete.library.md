**Expert naivete** is the formation in which a public producer — a scholar, journalist, or commentator — is induced by some crisis to begin producing content on an adjacent domain in which they have not developed contributory expertise, while their standing in their original domain causes the audience to receive the new content as expert.
The capital is transferred (the audience's recognition follows the producer into the new domain); the knowledge is not (the producer is learning in real time, at platform-velocity, without the depth that contributory expertise requires).
The formation is not charlatanism — the producer is typically sincerely learning — but the distortion at the audience-end is structurally similar to its effects.
The [platformal liberalism](../objects/platformal-liberalism.md) configuration accelerates this by making prior-domain cultural capital visible and portable across domains, while the confessional-tonal mode of platform content makes the learning-in-progress *appear* as honest disclosure rather than as underdevelopment.

## The three-feature structure

**Capital-transfer rather than knowledge-transfer.** Bourdieu's (1984) cultural capital is transferable: capital accumulated in one domain (academic credentials, journalistic platform, professional reputation) circulates as capital in others.
Expert naivete is the operational expression of this transferability in the crisis-induced commentary economy: the expertise is not transferred (no actual knowledge moves), but the *capital* is, and the audience reads the deployed capital as expertise.

**Crisis-induction.** The producer is typically pulled into the new material by a structural event that makes the adjacent domain feel urgent.
Crisis-induction produces the velocity: the producer is learning in real time and is incentivized to produce content from the learning before it has matured.
The incentive is not cynical — the urgency is real, and the producer genuinely believes they have something to contribute — but the platform rewards velocity over depth, and the confession-of-learning is itself a genre-compliant performance.

**Confessional-tonal compatibility.** Expert naivete is compatible with — and frequently inhabits — confessional honesty: the producer narrates their own learning, their own previously-unthought edges, their own questions.
Following Foucault's (1978) analysis of confession as power-mechanism, the confessional mode is itself a form of incitement to discourse that binds the speaker to the epistemic formation they are confessing within.
The audience reads the confession as authenticity — and authenticity is the genre-compliant form that expertise takes in the [platformal liberalism](../objects/platformal-liberalism.md) configuration.

## Collins and Evans: interactional versus contributory

Collins and Evans (2007) supply the analytical apparatus that locates expert naivete precisely.
Their typology distinguishes *interactional expertise* (the ability to converse competently within a domain) from *contributory expertise* (the ability to do original work in the domain).
Expert naivete typically has interactional expertise — the producer can talk plausibly about the topic, engage its literature, participate in its discourse — without contributory expertise: they cannot evaluate, extend, or correct primary work.
The audience reads the interactional expertise as evidence of contributory expertise.
The gap between the two is what expert naivete inhabits.

The [neurotic platformal intellectual](neurotic-platformal-intellectual.md) is the subject-position that produces expert naivete as its characteristic content.
The neurotic platformal intellectual is incited into expertise-performance under anxiety; expert naivete is the epistemic formation that results when this performance is read as delivery rather than as performance.

## Relation to industrial intellectualism

Expert naivete and [industrial intellectualism](industrial-intellectualism.md) are related but distinct formations.
Industrial intellectualism is the structural condition in which intellectual labor is organized as a production process, optimizing for genre satisfaction rather than material correspondence.
Expert naivete is a specific instance of this: the production of genre-satisfying expert content from an underdeveloped epistemic position.
Industrial intellectualism describes the structural condition; expert naivete names the epistemic configuration that the condition tends to produce in the commentary economy.

## Political effects

Expert naivete sustains [coherent confusion](coherent-confusion.md) at the level of intellectual production.
A crisis produces urgent questions; expert naivete produces content that appears to address those questions at authoritative volume; the content sustains the questions' salience while failing to resolve them; the audience remains in the confused-but-engaged state that coherent confusion requires.
The production is not a failure — from the platform's perspective, the content is doing exactly what the platform needs.
From the epistemic perspective of the audience, it is a systematic misallocation of the epistemic trust that the audience extends. ---
