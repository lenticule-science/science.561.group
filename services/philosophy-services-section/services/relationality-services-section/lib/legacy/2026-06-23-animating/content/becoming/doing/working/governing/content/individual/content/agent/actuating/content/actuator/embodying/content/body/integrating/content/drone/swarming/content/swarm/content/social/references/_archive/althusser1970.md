# Althusser 1970 — Ideology and Ideological State Apparatuses

**Althusser, Louis.
(1970).
"Idéologie et appareils idéologiques d'État." *La Pensée*, 151.
(Trans.
Ben Brewster, 1971, in *Lenin and Philosophy and Other Essays*.
Monthly Review Press.)**

## Annotation

The foundational text for the theory of interpellation and the analysis of ideological state apparatuses (ISAs).
Althusser distinguishes repressive state apparatuses (the army, police, prisons — which function by violence) from ISAs (schools, churches, media, families — which function by ideology).
ISAs reproduce the conditions of production not through coercion but through the installation of subjects who recognize themselves in their position: the process he calls *interpellation*.

The iconic image: a police officer calls "Hey, you!" in the street; the individual who turns around has already performed the act of subjection.
By turning — by recognizing themselves as the one addressed — the individual has been installed as a subject of the law.

For predicated sociology, the interpellation mechanism is formalized as the [interpellation morphism](../morphisms/interpellation.md): a single-increment event that, when the self-recognition counter-event fires, installs subject-position predicate cells.
The Althusserian insight that subjects are not pre-given but *produced* through ISAs is realized in the settling dynamics: the subject-position cells begin at $\bot$ (no information about the entity's subject-position) and accumulate toward $\mathbf{t}$ through iterated interpellating events.

Althusser's analysis of material ideology — that ideology exists in practices and apparatuses, not merely in ideas — is given substrate form by the closure-rule structure of the ISAs: the school, the church, the media platform fire interpellation-closure rules as part of their normal operation, not through any special ideological act.
