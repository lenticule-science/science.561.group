# Does the holographic-projection framing have engineering content this corpus has not yet captured?

The older relational-system specs treat a holographic projection $\mathrm{Stab} : H \to H^*$ as load-bearing engineering structure. The projection takes the construction sheaf $H$ to its boundary $H^* = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ — the *settled* sub-sheaf where both nuclei stabilize. The projection becomes well-defined for a sub-region only once the sub-region has been equipped with a Grothendieck topology (the "charter" stage in the older progression).

The corpus's current engineering content captures *what stays settled*:

- The substrate's value-domain monotonicity preserves settled content.
- [Settle](../morphisms/settle.md) drives content toward fixed-point.
- A [locale](../objects/locale.md) is a region whose substrate state has reached the seven [closure-conditions](teaching/explanations/closure-conditions.md), including coherence-closure (the sheaf-condition equivalent).
- [Settling-as-computation](teaching/explanations/settling-as-computation.md) frames quiescence-as-normalization.

What the corpus does *not* capture is the holographic-projection framing's specific engineering claim: that there is a *boundary view* of a substrate region — distinct from the region's full content, computable as a fixed-subset of the region — that carries the region's "what would survive if the substrate stopped advancing now" picture. The framing suggests this boundary view is operationally useful: it is what cross-region observers should consult, what federation handshakes should sign, what governance decisions should be checked against.

The question is whether *this specific engineering content* — the substrate-recorded "boundary as fixed sub-region" — is captured anywhere in the corpus, refined enough that an implementer could realize it. Current candidates that *partially* cover the territory:

- [Snapshot](../objects/snapshot.md) records the canonical content of a region at a moment — but the snapshot is the *full* content, not the boundary fixed-sub-region.
- [Locale](../objects/locale.md) declares a region complete — but completeness is the seven closure conditions, not the fixed-subset distinction.
- [Settling-quiescence](teaching/explanations/settling-as-computation.md) names the fixed-point — but as a property of the whole region, not as a separately-extractable boundary.

The set-aside framing of the question: the holographic-projection framing came from older homebrew math (modal-comonadic Heyting structure with $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ as two compatible Lawvere–Tierney topologies); spatiotemporal-knowledge has not yet articulated whether this construction has a clean modern realization. The engineering content may survive in some form once the math program catches up; until then, the question is open.
