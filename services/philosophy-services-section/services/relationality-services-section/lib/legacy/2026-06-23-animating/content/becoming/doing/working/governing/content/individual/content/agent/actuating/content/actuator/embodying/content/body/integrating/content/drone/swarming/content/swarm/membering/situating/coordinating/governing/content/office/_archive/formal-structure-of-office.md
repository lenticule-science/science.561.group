An [office](../terms/office.md) is a normative position that persists independently of any particular holder.
This text develops the formal model: the office as a normative structure with required predicates whose joint settlement constitutes full incumbency, the correspondence with Roman legal categories (*munus*, *honor*, *dignitas*), Kantorowicz's body politic as the structural form, and Madison's separation of powers as structural insulation between offices.

## Formal tuple

An Office $O$ is a quadruple $(P, A, D_O, I^+)$:

$$O = (P,\; A,\; D_O : A \to \{\mathrm{power}, \mathrm{duty}, \mathrm{privilege}, \mathrm{immunity}\},\; I^+ : \mathrm{Investiture})$$

where: - $P$ is the **office's normative structure** — the structural form that persists across all histories, defining the required predicates the incumbent must settle (recognition predicate, induction predicate, etc.) and the laws the office is bound by; this is the *body politic* — the normative form whose structure governs the office and exists whether or not any element inhabits it - $A$ is the **action domain** — the set of acts and relations the office governs - $D_O : A \to \{\mathrm{power}, \mathrm{duty}, \mathrm{privilege}, \mathrm{immunity}\}$ is the **deontic profile** — the function assigning each action a Hohfeldian position independently of any holder - $I^+ : \mathrm{Investiture}$ is the **installation morphism** — the constitutive speech act that begins the [settlement](../../../../math/theories/voidmath/terms/settlement.md) of the incumbency proposition $\iota$ on the office's required predicates

**Compliance space.** The compliance space of the office at history $t$ is the set of incumbency claims whose required predicates have all reached affirming settlement at $t$.
An element $\iota$ is in the office as soon as installation has begun.
It is fully compliant iff all of the office's required predicates over $\iota$ have reached affirming settlement simultaneously.
The *obligation gap* of a candidate $\iota$ is the distance from the current settlement of each required predicate to its affirming-determination position.

**Persistence.** The office's normative structure is defined over all of $T$ — not as an element at any particular history.
Vacating the office means no element is currently being evaluated; the structure persists.
This is the formal content of *dignitas non moritur*: the dignity does not die because the dignity IS the normative structure, not any element in any history.

## The munus/honor/dignitas trinity

The Roman law of public service encoded in Digest Title 50.4 ("*De muneribus et honoribus*") provides the purest external grounding for the structural distinction between the office's required predicates.

**Munus**: Callistratus (Dig.
50.4.14) defines *munus* as administration of public affairs *with expense but without title of dignity* (*"cum sumptu sine titulo dignitatis"*).
In framework terms: the induction predicate has reached affirming settlement (forward obligation is in place) but the recognition predicate has not (the office's recognized standing has not been conferred).
The leitourgos of Athens was compelled to fund the trireme (*munus*) without thereby acquiring the *honor* of holding office.

**Honor**: The *honor municipalis* is administration *with a grade of dignity* (*"cum dignitatis gradu"*).
The key addition: the recognition predicate has reached affirming settlement — the accumulated normative history treats the incumbency as recognized at dignitary grade.
Honor carries recognition; munus carries forward obligation.

**Dignitas**: The qualifier distinguishing honor from munus — the constitutional grade of standing.
Rilinger's analysis (*Ordo und Dignitas*, 2007): *dignitas* is inherently comparative and relational — position within a ranked order, constituted by relation to the rest of the system.
In framework terms: full settlement on both required predicates simultaneously, with the recognized standing conferring a position in the system's ordered hierarchy.

**Modestinus's priority rule** (Dig.
50.4.10): *"Honorem sustinenti munus imponi non potest: munus sustinenti honor deferri potest."* One holding an honor cannot have a munus imposed; one holding a munus may be given an honor.
In framework terms: if both predicates have settled affirmingly (full honor), the induction predicate is already affirmed and no new munus can impose an additional demand.
But if only the induction predicate has settled (munus-only), the recognition predicate may subsequently be brought to affirming settlement, completing the incumbency.

The **cursus honorum** — quaestor → praetor → consul — is a chain of office structures with progressively richer required predicates: each higher office's structural commitments include those of the lower, plus additional ones.

## The King's Two Bodies

Kantorowicz's reconstruction of Tudor legal theory (Plowden's *Commentaries*, c.
1571):

> "The King has in him two Bodies, viz., a Body natural, and a Body politic... his Body politic is a Body that cannot be seen or handled, consisting of Policy and Government... utterly void of Infancy, and old Age, and other natural Defects."

**Body politic** IS the office's normative structure $P$ over all of $T$.
It is not an element of any history.
It "cannot be seen or handled" because it is not a fiber element — it is a structural form that governs incumbency claims rather than being one of them.

**Body natural** is the current occupant — an Entity at $t_{\mathrm{now}}$, indexed to that history, mortal in the precise sense of being a particular individual whose existence does not automatically persist to $t' \neq t_{\mathrm{now}}$ without an explicit transition.

*Dignitas non moritur*: the office's structural commitments are defined over all of $T$.
They are not consumable by any act of an incumbent.
No element's departure from any history can modify them.
The formal grounding: the office's required predicates and laws are structural features of the office, not elements of any single history; they exist for all $t$ by the definition of the office.

Kantorowicz's genealogy: the phrase is medieval canonist formulation (not classical Roman).
Baldus de Ubaldis and Johannes Andreae coined it to solve the succession problem.
The answer: the *dignitas* never died; the authority passed instantaneously to the successor.
Because the office's structure is not an element of any one history, succession is instantaneous — no waiting for a new element, because the structure was never attached to the old element.

## The interregnum and vacancy

An office is vacant at $t$ when its structure is defined but no incumbency claim is currently in joint affirming settlement on the required predicates.
The structure persists; no element currently satisfies both predicate conditions.

The Roman **interrex** held a partial incumbency: the induction predicate was settled (forward obligation was in place) but the recognition predicate was not.
The interrex's authority was structurally a *munus* without *honor* — pure forward obligation to reconstitute the full office.

The **patrum auctoritas** (Senate's collective authority during interregnum) corresponds to the framework's covering structure on histories — what families of facts count as valid in the system.
The covering structure persists regardless of which elements inhabit any history; it is the "rule of recognition" (Hart) at the base of the office.

## The structural opposition of offices

Madison (*Federalist No. 51*):

> "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition.
> The interest of the man must be connected with the constitutional rights of the place... the private interest of every individual may be a sentinel over the public rights."

**Constitutional opposition** between offices $O_1$ and $O_2$ is the condition that their structural commitments are mutually insulated:

1. No unilateral act of the holder of $O_1$ can advance a proposition into the affirming settlement of $O_2$'s required predicates
2. No unilateral act of the holder of $O_2$ can advance a proposition into the affirming settlement of $O_1$'s required predicates

This is the framework formulation of separation of powers: each branch's required predicates are settled by its own witnessing structure, not by the other branch's.
Joint settlement of cross-branch propositions requires a constitutional procedure that traverses both branches' witnessing simultaneously — the analog of a covering family requiring both branches' witnessings before gathering into a unified record.

Madison's additional point — "each department should have a will of its own" — corresponds to each office having its own required predicates and its own witnessing structure, not shared with other offices.
A system where all branches operated under the same predicates would not constitute separation of powers; it would be a single office with multiple occupants.

The connection of personal interest to the office's prerogatives: the gap between the current settlement of an office's required predicates and full affirming settlement motivates the incumbent to reduce it.
Madison's mechanism converts personal ambition into structural self-defense.

## Installation: the constitutive speech act

Installation has two stages corresponding to the two required predicates:

1. The installing authority issues a status function declaration (Searle 1995, pp.
  43–51): "X counts as holder of O in institution N."
  This begins to settle the recognition predicate over $\iota$ in the affirming direction — first-stage installation.
2. Forward-stability: the induction predicate over $\iota$ has reached affirming settlement — the incumbency is stable in continuations of $t$.
  This completes installation.
3. Full installation: both required predicates have reached affirming settlement — the incumbency is doubly stable.

The declaration must satisfy felicity conditions (Austin 1962, pp.
14–15): appropriate authority, appropriate procedure, and completeness.
An attempted installation by an entity lacking the power fails the felicity condition — the declaration does not advance the recognition predicate's settlement regardless of the words used.
