**The liberalism legitimacy crisis** is the structural-condition object that arises when liberalism's constitutive contradictions can no longer be routed into institutional management — when the closure rules of the late-liberal social settling can no longer resolve the contradictions they produce fast enough to prevent their accumulation as experienced incoherence.
It is the object-instance of [destabilization](teaching/explanations/destabilization.md) in the contemporary late-liberal formation: what destabilization looks like in the specific predicate-fiber space of liberal political life.

In predication-categories terms: the liberalism legitimacy crisis is the settling $\mathcal{S}_{LLC}$ — a sub-configuration of the late-liberal social base — characterized by an aperiodic fiber-settling regime in the political-legitimacy predicate dimensions.
Specifically:

- The predicates *liberalism delivers on its promises* and *liberalism can manage its contradictions* are held at $\top$ (overdetermined: simultaneously affirmed in official discourse and denied in experienced reality)
- The settling closure rules have shifted from *resolution-promising* (the predicates will converge to $\mathbf{t}$: the promises will be kept) to *regulation-promising* (the predicates will be managed: the symptoms will be addressed)
- Legitimacy itself becomes a manageable variable: the discourse concerns not whether liberalism delivers but whether subjects perceive it as delivering — moving the settling target from truth-value to perception-value

## Three defining features

**Constitutive contradictions become experientially insoluble.** Liberalism's core contradictions — universalism versus individualism, progress versus exhaustion, formal equality versus substantive inequality — have always been in tension.
Under non-crisis conditions, the tensions are routed into negotiation, deferral, or local resolution.
Under the liberalism-legitimacy-crisis settling, the routings stop converging: the contradictions become available to subjects as continuous experience rather than occasional anomalies.
The predicate *the system is working* is simultaneously confirmed (the institutions are functioning) and denied (the outcomes are deteriorating), producing persistent $\top$ in the political-legitimacy fiber.

**Resolution-promising shifts to regulation-promising.** The settling target changes: the system no longer drives political-legitimacy predicates toward $\mathbf{t}$ (liberalism will deliver full employment, equality, prosperity) but toward a regulated $\top$ state (employment volatility will be smoothed, inequality will be tracked, growth will be optimized).
The promise has changed its modal character: from *will be achieved* to *will be managed*.
This shift is the formal mark of the liberalism-legitimacy-crisis settling.

**Legitimacy becomes a manageable fiber.** The system's response to its own legitimacy crisis is to manage perceptions of legitimacy — through communication, expert framing, platform-mediated narrative — rather than to address the conditions producing the crisis.
In the predication-categories model: the operative closure rule no longer targets the truth-value of the political-legitimacy predicates but their epistemic accessibility.
If the $\top$-valued predicates can be kept at [coherent confusion](teaching/explanations/coherent-confusion.md) — ambiguous enough that subjects keep interpreting rather than withdrawing — the system can function without resolving its contradictions.

## Historical instances

**The 1970s rupture.** The end of Bretton Woods, the 1973 oil shocks, and the breakdown of the welfare-state consensus mark the onset of the contemporary liberalism-legitimacy-crisis settling.
Streeck (2014) reads the subsequent response — inflation management, then debt expansion, then austerity — as a series of crisis-management instruments rather than resolutions: each instrument addresses the crisis's symptoms while the underlying settling regime remains aperiodic.

**The 2008 collapse and non-resolution.** The 2008 financial crisis exposed the structural unsustainability of the post-1970s management regime.
The institutional response (bank rescue, quantitative easing, austerity) addressed symptoms without resolving the contradictions; the post-2008 decade continued the destabilized settling under successive management instruments, confirming the aperiodic-regime characterization.

**The post-2016 political reorganization.** Brown (2019) reads the widespread emergence of nationalist, far-right, and crisis-populist political formations as effects of the liberalism-legitimacy-crisis settling, not its causes: the system's inability to deliver liberal resolutions has produced political formations that abandon the liberal framework.
In predication-categories terms: when the system's political-legitimacy predicates have been held at $\top$ long enough, large subject-populations shift to alternative closure rules that do not require the $\mathbf{t}$ values to be recoverable.

## Relation to Fisher's capitalist realism

Fisher (2009) names the affective register of the liberalism-legitimacy-crisis settling *capitalist realism*: the widespread sense that it is easier to imagine the end of the world than the end of capitalism.
In predication-categories terms: capitalist realism is the subjective settling-state that arises when the *system-transformation is possible* predicate is persistently held at $\mathbf{f}$ (confirmed-false) — not because subjects have been argued into this position but because the settling dynamics have converged on it through the accumulated closure-rule operations of [genre calibration](../morphisms/genre-calibration.md), [californication](../morphisms/californication.md), and [coherent confusion](teaching/explanations/coherent-confusion.md). ---
