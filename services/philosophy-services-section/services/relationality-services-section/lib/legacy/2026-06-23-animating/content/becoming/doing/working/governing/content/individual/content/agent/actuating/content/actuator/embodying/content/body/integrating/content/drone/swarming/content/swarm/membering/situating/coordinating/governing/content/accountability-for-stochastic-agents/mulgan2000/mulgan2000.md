# Mulgan (2000) — 'Accountability': An Ever-Expanding Concept?

**Full citation:** Mulgan, R.
"'Accountability': An Ever-Expanding Concept?" *Public Administration*, 78(3), 555–573, 2000.

## Summary

Mulgan traces the historical expansion of "accountability" in public administration literature and provides a core analytical definition.
He distinguishes accountability from related concepts: responsibility, transparency, responsiveness, and controllability.

Key contribution: Mulgan establishes that accountability is backward-looking, retrospective, and sanctioning — it concerns past actions that must be explained and for which consequences may follow.
This contrasts with prospective control mechanisms.
The retrospective character of accountability is why the question "who is accountable?" is meaningfully answered after the fact by tracing the delegation chain.

Mulgan also identifies the accountability diffusion problem: when accountability is distributed across multiple parties, each party can attribute primary accountability to others, resulting in no effective accountability for anyone.
The resolution is scope-exclusivity: within a given domain, one and only one party is accountable.

## Contribution to the agents theory

The accountability diffusion problem is the basis for the [accountability-chain-must-be-total](../theorems/accountability-chain-must-be-total.md) theorem's Part 2 (accountability conflicts are incoherent — the scope-exclusivity invariant).

Mulgan's five forms of accountability (political, legal, administrative, professional, social) are referenced in the [accountability-tracing](../texts/accountability-tracing-in-multi-agent-systems.md) text as the forms that multi-agent systems must implement (administrative and legal, at minimum).
