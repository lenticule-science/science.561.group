# Saussurean semiotic system

A worked instance of [semiotic-system](../objects/semiotic-system.md) under Saussure's *Cours* (per [Saussure 1916](references/saussure1916.md)).

## Components

- $V$ — *signifier inventory*: phonemes and their licit combinations, drawn from the langue.
- $O$ — *signified range*: the conceptual content the langue admits.
- $I$ — *interpretant repertoire*: the langue-as-system supplies the third place implicitly. The recipient's interpretation is constrained by the langue's structural differences (the *valeur* of a sign is its position in the system, not its intrinsic content).
- $K$ — *code*: the langue's signifier-signified pairings, established by social convention. Pairings are *arbitrary* in the strict sense — there is no natural relation between signifier and signified that is *not* an artifact of the convention.
- $R$ — *rule system*: the syntagmatic rules of licit combination ([syntagm](teaching/explanations/syntagm.md)) and the associative/paradigmatic rules of substitutability ([paradigm](teaching/explanations/paradigm.md)).
- $\Sigma$ — *semiosphere*: the speech-community whose synchronic langue is the system's setting.

## Distinctive features

- **Dyadic at the face**, with langue-as-third-place. The signifier-signified pair is the visible face; the langue-as-system supplies the structural third place implicitly.
- **Synchronic primacy.** The langue is studied at a synchronic cut; diachronic change is a separate study (linguistics, not semiotics-of-the-langue).
- **Valeur is positional.** A sign's value is its differential position in the langue, not an intrinsic property; the langue is a system of differences with no positive terms.

## Substrate reading

The Saussurean system instantiates predicated knowledge system under the *Scott-continuous* regime restricted to the langue: closure within the synchronic langue terminates at $\omega$ because the langue is finite-of-arbitrary-size and its rules are bounded-arity. The langue's *valeur* corresponds to the substrate's *predicate-category structure*: a sign's structural position is the morphisms-to-and-from it in the predicate category.

## Bridge to substrate operations

- *Specialise* — runs along the lexical specialisation hierarchy (`mammal → animal → being`).
- *Translate* — between two langues; load-bearing for cross-language reception. Eco's *On Translation* operationalises this directly.
- *Codeswitch* — within a multilingual community sharing two langues at the speech-act level.

## Distance from Peirce

The Saussurean system *reads as* a Peircean system once the langue's implicit third place is made explicit; the difference is presentation, not substrate. The two systems' convergent readings on a shared corpus (the same set of sign-acts) coincide modulo the explicit-vs-implicit difference.
