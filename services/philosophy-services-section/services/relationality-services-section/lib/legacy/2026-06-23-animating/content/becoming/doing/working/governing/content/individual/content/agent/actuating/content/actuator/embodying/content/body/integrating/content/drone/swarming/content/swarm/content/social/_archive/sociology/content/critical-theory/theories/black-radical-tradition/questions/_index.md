Open work for the `black-radical-tradition` subdomain.
The page holds two registers of question: the **substantive** questions the tradition itself is still working out (what the tradition is unsettled about as an intellectual project), and the **structural** curation questions about how the subdomain should be organized in this library.
Both are kept here because in this subdomain — as in any tradition organized around a continuing political project rather than a closed body of doctrine — the line between substantive and curatorial is porous.

## Substantive questions the tradition is working out

These are the live questions in the contemporary literature, not questions the subdomain has settled.
The framing of each is the subdomain's; the disagreements are real.

### On racial capitalism as concept

- **Is racial capitalism transhistorical or periodizable?** Robinson reads racial logic as constitutive of capitalism from its origins; some of the post-2010 literature reads racial capitalism as a periodizable formation that takes different forms across the slavery / colonial / industrial / financialized phases of capital.
  The subdomain currently leans toward Robinson's reading but treats this as live.
  See the discussion essay [`racial-capitalism-after-robinson`](../texts/racial-capitalism-after-robinson.md).
- **Can the concept generate predictions at the meso scale?** The frame describes how racial-capitalist crisis takes form in retrospect (Gilmore on the California prison fix).
  Whether it can generate forward-looking claims about which forms of crisis-management will emerge in which conjunctures is open.
- **What is the relation between racial capitalism and other capital-modifier concepts?** Surveillance capitalism, platform capitalism, climate capitalism, racial capitalism — are these dimensions of one structure, regional analytics, or competing accounts?
  The literature has not converged.

### On abolition

- **What is the political-economic theory of the transition?** The abolitionist horizon is articulated; the path from carceral capacity to its replacement is articulated programmatically (non-reformist reforms, build-and-divest); the political-economic theory of how the transition becomes self-sustaining at scale is less developed.
- **What does abolition mean outside the U.S. carceral context?** The strongest theoretical work has been done on the U.S. prison-industrial complex.
  Whether and how abolitionist analysis transfers to other carceral geographies (immigration detention in Europe, the Israeli occupation, the Chinese reeducation camps, post-colonial African and Asian carceral systems) is an open theoretical and political question.
  Some of the work exists; more is needed.
- **Abolition and the police function.** What replaces the social functions police currently absorb (response to interpersonal violence, mental-health crises, traffic enforcement, child welfare) at the necessary scale, and on what political-economic basis?
  The movement literature is rich; the systematic theoretical articulation is uneven.

### On fugitivity and the limits of recognition

- **The constructive content of refusal.** Both Black-radical fugitivity and Indigenous-resurgence refusal pair refusal with the construction of alternative forms of life.
  The tradition has not fully theorized the relation between refusal and construction — when refusal is enabling and when it becomes its own form of capture.
  See the discussion essay [`fugitivity-and-the-limits-of-recognition`](../texts/fugitivity-and-the-limits-of-recognition.md).
- **Fugitivity and scale.** Fugitive practice is theorized at the scale of the small group, the maroon community, the undercommons.
  Whether the analytic scales to the polity, the region, the planet — or whether fugitivity is specifically a politics of the small scale that breaks down when scaled — is contested.
- **The limits of the archive.** Hartman's critical fabulation is the methodological response to the archive's structural absences.
  The question of what the method warrants — what counts as a fabulation rather than a falsification, where the line between speculative reconstruction and projection sits — is itself a live methodological question.

### On the relation to Marxism

- **Is the tradition a critic of Marxism, an extension of Marxism, or an alternative to Marxism?** Robinson's framing is "precedes and exceeds."
  The contemporary literature contains all three readings, sometimes within the same author.
  The question is not principally academic; it bears on the political-strategic question of which alliances are available.
- **Class analysis after racial capitalism.** If race is structural to capital accumulation, what does class analysis look like inside that frame?
  The orthodox Marxist class-first / race-second framing is rejected; the substantive replacement is in development.

### On the relation to Black feminism

- **The integration of [Hortense Spillers](../../../../../../../../references/people/hortense-spillers.md), [Sylvia Wynter](../../../../../../../../references/people/sylvia-wynter.md), and [Angela Davis](../figures/angela-davis.md)'s analysis of race-class-gender into the tradition's racial-capitalism analytic is uneven.** The substantive work to make the categories analytically primary together (rather than additively) is ongoing.
  Davis's *Women, Race & Class* is foundational; the post-2010 literature has extended this; a fully synthetic account is not yet stabilized.

### On the relation to Indigenous thought

- **The tradition's relation to Indigenous-resurgence and settler-colonial analysis is being actively developed and is not yet stable.** [Coulthard](../../../../../../../../references/people/glen-coulthard.md), Audra Simpson, [Leanne Simpson](../../../../../../../../references/people/leanne-simpson.md), and the broader Indigenous-studies literature share analytical resources with the Black radical tradition (critique of recognition, refusal as politics, the inadequacy of liberal-multicultural categories) and diverge on others (land, sovereignty, the status of the nation form).
  The subdomain takes the convergence seriously and tracks the divergences in [`fugitivity-and-the-limits-of-recognition`](../texts/fugitivity-and-the-limits-of-recognition.md).

### On contemporary uptake and risk

- **The risk of recuperation.** "Racial capitalism," "abolition," and "fugitivity" have circulated widely outside the contexts where they were developed.
  The risk that institutional uptake hollows the concepts — that they become brand language for institutions whose structural commitments the concepts were designed to critique — is a live concern in the literature.
  Moten and Harney's analysis of the undercommons is in part an analysis of this risk; Hartman's refusal of recovery is another.
  The question of how to use the concepts without participating in their hollowing is a working methodological question for the subdomain itself.

## Structural / curation questions

The discipline-level open work for the `black-radical-tradition` subdomain — questions whose answers, once worked out, would bring the subdomain closer to theory status under [theory-discipline.md](../../../../../../../science/theories/information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/markdown-vault-knowledge-system/theories/predicated-markdown-vault-knowledge-system/specifications/theory-discipline.md).

- **What does this subdomain commit to substantively?** An earlier curation pass classified most sociology subdomains as staging-areas — collections of analytical resources that have not yet stabilized into a single research program with shared method and shared object.
  The first-order question for `black-radical-tradition` is whether the existing material can stabilize into such a program, or whether what currently lives here is better understood as a regional collection routed through the school whose name it carries.
  The current pass (the build-out into [`figures/`](../figures/_index.md), additional essays in [`texts/`](../texts/_index.md), and the substantive open-question list above) is intended to make the substantive shape of the subdomain visible enough that the staging-area-vs-theory question can be answered next.
- **What is the relation between this subdomain's vocabulary ([`terms/`](../terms/_index.md)) and the discipline's broader concept layer (`sociology/concepts/`)?** Where the same analytical role is occupied by a term here and a concept at the discipline level, the relation should be made explicit (via `realizes:` or `see-also:` edges).
- **Which writers and works should the subdomain cite, and which of those need notes in `sociology/references/` or [`library/references/people/`](../../../../../../../../references/people/_index.md) rather than ad-hoc inline mentions?** The current pass adds [`figures/`](../figures/_index.md) pages for Du Bois, James, Robinson, Davis, Gilmore, Hartman, and Moten.
  The next pass should produce reference-note entries at `library/references/people/` for the four figures who lack them (Du Bois, James, Robinson, Davis, Hartman) and add figure pages for Wynter, Spillers, hooks, Fanon, and Glissant if the subdomain decides to maintain that scope.
- **What relations to neighboring subdomains are structural rather than incidental?** The convergences with [`abolition`](../../abolition/_index.md), [`povinellian`](../../povinellian/_index.md), and [`feminism`](../../feminism/_index.md) flagged in the new texts suggest that several subdomain boundaries are curatorial.
  A future pass should make the `see-also:` edges explicit and bidirectional.
