The military-command subdomain analyzes command authority in a specific institutional substrate: the ship's company.
The four terms the subdomain carries — *captain*, *first officer*, *crew*, *ship* — name the position structure that the analysis works with.
The choice of substrate is not incidental.
The ship is one of the few command institutions that has been worked out in operational detail across centuries of practice and that maps cleanly onto a small, intelligible position structure: a single accountable command authority (the captain) with a designated second-in-command and execution officer (the first officer), a crew with role-differentiated duties, and a bounded shared environment (the ship) inside which the authority operates.

The substrate has been adopted by the project as the working operational metaphor for orchestrated multi-agent work — see the project's own [`operations/command/group/`](../../../../../../../../../../../operations/command/group/) directory and the personnel/ship/office structure it scaffolds.
The sociological analysis here gives that operational vocabulary its analytical grounding rather than leaving it as bare metaphor. *Captain* is the command authority — the position with both the highest decision-rights and the highest accountability for the ship's conduct. *First officer* is the second-in-command — the position with delegated authority for execution and for command-continuity in the captain's absence. *Crew* is the role-differentiated body of personnel who carry out the ship's work under the command structure. *Ship* is the bounded operational substrate inside which the command structure is operative.

The subdomain reads against the broader literature on military sociology (Janowitz, Huntington, the more recent literature on civil–military relations, the social-historical work on the Royal Navy and the merchant marine), but it is not a substantive contribution to that literature.
It is the analytical scaffolding for an operational practice the project itself uses.
As an analysis of command authority in general, it is intentionally narrow; the broader analysis lives in the neighboring [`hohfeldian/`](../hohfeldian/) (positions and powers in general), [`jurisdiction/`](../jurisdiction/) (scope conditions for authority), and [`governance/`](../../) subdomains.

The relation to anarchism.
The subdomain's analytical commitment to studying command authority in a specific substrate does not entail an endorsement of command-authority arrangements in general.
The project's broader political commitments lie in the [`anarchism/`](../../../../../critical-theory/theories/anarchism/) subdomain.
The military-command analysis is an analysis of how a particular command form works, undertaken in part so that the project can use the form deliberately and in part so that the form's operations can be analyzed and challenged.
