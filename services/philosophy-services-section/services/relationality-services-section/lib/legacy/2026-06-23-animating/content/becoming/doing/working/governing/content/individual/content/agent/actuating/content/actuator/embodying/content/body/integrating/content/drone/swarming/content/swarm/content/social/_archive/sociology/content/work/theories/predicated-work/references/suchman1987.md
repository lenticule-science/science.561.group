## Annotation

Suchman's distinction between *plans-as-resources-for-action* and *plans-as-prescriptions-for-action* is the canonical critique of the AI-planning tradition's assumption that plans determine activity.
For predicated-work, Suchman's reading aligns precisely with the [plan](../objects/plan.md) object as a closure-rule subsystem: the plan does not *execute* — it constrains the closure rules that fire as situated witnesses arrive.
The plan-completion condition is not "the plan ran to its end" but rather [the meet of subtask completions](../theorems/plan-completion-as-meet.md) — a substrate-state, not a prescription-following.

The relevance to [closure-driven-coordination](teaching/explanations/closure-driven-coordination.md): Suchman's point that coordination emerges from situated action rather than from plan-execution is structurally what predicated-work's closure-driven account formalizes.
Coordination rules fire on the evidence the working community records; the plan is the rule-subsystem, not a prescription.
