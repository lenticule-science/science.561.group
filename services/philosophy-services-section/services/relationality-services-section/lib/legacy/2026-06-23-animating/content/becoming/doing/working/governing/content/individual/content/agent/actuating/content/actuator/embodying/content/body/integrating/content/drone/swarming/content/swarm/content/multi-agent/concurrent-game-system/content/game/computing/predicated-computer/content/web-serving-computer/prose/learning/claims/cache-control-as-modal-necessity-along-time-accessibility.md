# Cache-control is the modal-necessity operator along the time-accessibility relation

**Claim.** A `Cache-Control: max-age=N` declaration on an HTTP response is *the substrate's normative freshness commitment*: a substrate-side declaration that, for the duration of $N$ substrate-clock ticks from the response's settling ordinal, the substrate undertakes that the response value $u_w$ remains its committed answer for the request's fiber. Structurally, this commitment is the [modal necessity operator](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/modal-necessity-operator.md) $\Box_{R_{\le N}}$ applied to the response cell along a [time-accessibility relation](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/bilattice-accessibility-relation.md) $R_{\le N}$ that admits worlds $w'$ with $\tau(w') - \tau(w) \le N$ — i.e., the substrate asserts $\Box_{R_{\le N}} u = u$ at the request's $w$, and a cache that reuses $u$ within the window is *evaluating that necessity claim by reading the cached cell*. The claim is normative on the substrate's response projection (what it commits to return), not ontological on substrate state (which may continue to change underneath); a substrate that violates its own `max-age` declaration is doctrine-fidelity-failing per [doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor](../../../theorems/doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor.md), even if its underlying state has not actually changed. Headers other than `max-age` modulate the accessibility relation; `Vary` declares the fiber over which $u$ is indexed; `ETag`/`Last-Modified` provide the equality witness used when a cache re-validates after the necessity window closes.

## Why this holds

A response value $u$ from an [endpoint](teaching/explanations/endpoint.md) is the substrate's projection of a [predication fiber algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md) value at fiber $A_p$, where $p$ is the route-scoped predicate per [routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change](routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change.md). The substrate-state at request time is one [world](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/world-set.md) $w$; future substrate-states at later substrate-clock ticks are other worlds $w' \in W$ in the world-set indexed by [time-and-event-ordinals](../../teaching/explanations/time-and-event-ordinals.md).

Define the **time-accessibility relation $R_{\le N}$** valued in `FOUR`:

$$R_{\le N}(w, w') := \begin{cases} \mathbf{t} & \text{if } 0 \le \tau(w') - \tau(w) \le N \\ \bot & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Per [modal-necessity-operator](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/modal-necessity-operator.md), the necessity of $u$ at $w$ along $R_{\le N}$ is

$$(\Box_{R_{\le N}} u)_w(s, o) \;=\; \bigwedge_t \{ R_{\le N}(w, w') \to u_{w'}(s, o) : w' \in W \}.$$

When the substrate issues `Cache-Control: max-age=N`, it is declaring $\Box_{R_{\le N}} u = u$ at $w$ — the value at $w$ holds throughout the $N$-second window. Per the strong-implication semantics on `FOUR`, this is the claim that for every $w'$ reachable in $\le N$ seconds, $u_{w'}(s, o) \ge_t u_w(s, o)$ — the cell's truth-component does not retreat. By [cache-coherence-via-functorial-projection](../../../theorems/cache-coherence-via-functorial-projection.md), a cache reusing $u_w$ for a request at $w'$ within the window is reading the substrate's $\Box_{R_{\le N}}$-witnessed claim, not a stale projection.

Header-level reductions:

- **`max-age=0` plus `must-revalidate`.** $R_{\le 0}$ is the identity world-relation; $\Box_{R_{\le 0}} u = u$ trivially. The cache must re-fetch on every request — no necessity window is admitted.
- **`no-cache`.** Equivalent to `max-age=0, must-revalidate`. The substrate makes no necessity claim; the cache may store $u$ but must re-validate before reuse.
- **`no-store`.** Stronger than `no-cache`. The substrate forbids the cache from holding $u$ at all — the necessity claim is *defective*: the substrate cannot itself assert $u$ holds for any future $w'$, so it forbids any party from caching $u$. Common for responses containing $\top$-cells (mid-repair state) or for responses that are intentionally per-request fresh.
- **`s-maxage=N`.** A *shared-cache-only* necessity declaration. The cache's audience axis is part of the world-set: shared caches and private caches are at different fiber coordinates. The substrate may make a necessity claim along $R_{\le N}$ at the shared-cache coordinate while leaving the private-cache coordinate unconstrained.
- **`stale-while-revalidate=M`.** The necessity window is $R_{\le N}$, but the substrate additionally admits *paraconsistent reuse* in the window $(N, N+M]$ — the cache may serve $u$ even after necessity expires, marking the response as $\top$-class (told-this, told-this-is-stale) until the revalidation completes. Per [paraconsistency-as-substrate-discipline](../../../theorems/paraconsistency-as-substrate-discipline.md), this is the substrate's admission that the cell may be in $\top$ during the revalidation window.

## `Vary` declares the fiber-indexing axes

The `Vary` header declares the request coordinates over which the response is indexed. `Vary: Accept, Accept-Language, Authorization` says the response's $u$ is at fiber $A_p$ where $p$ depends on the (Accept, Accept-Language, Authorization) triple of the request. Two requests differing on a `Vary` axis are at *different fibers* — different $u$s, different necessity claims, different cache keys.

Cache-control's $\Box_{R_{\le N}}$ is per-fiber: the necessity is along the time accessibility, *holding the fiber fixed*. Conflating two fibers (caching across a `Vary` axis the substrate declared) violates the fibration's coherence — per [routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change](routing-as-predication-fibration-base-change.md), each fiber's $f^*$ is its own base-change, and a cache that returns one fiber's $u$ for a request landing in another fiber is non-functorial.

## `ETag` and `Last-Modified` are the equality witness for re-validation

When the necessity window closes ($\tau(w') - \tau(w) > N$), the cache's $u_w$ is no longer covered by the substrate's necessity claim. The cache may still be *correct* — the substrate's value may not have changed — but the substrate has stopped guaranteeing it. To recover the necessity, the cache issues a *re-validation*: an `If-None-Match: <ETag>` or `If-Modified-Since: <Last-Modified>` request that asks the substrate "is the value at the new world $w'$ still equal to the value $u_w$ I cached?"

Per [conditional-get-as-stalk-equality](conditional-get-as-stalk-equality.md), this is precisely a stalk-equality query at the bitopological-settling-site: the substrate's response is `304 Not Modified` iff the canonical-form-hash of the fiber stalk at $w'$ matches the cached `ETag`. A `304` re-extends the necessity window — the substrate now asserts $\Box_{R_{\le N'}} u_w$ at $w'$ for the new `max-age=N'` it returns. A `200` overrides the cached $u$ with a new value $u'$ and a fresh necessity window.

The two validators differ in which equality is witnessed:

- **`ETag` (strong).** Canonical-form-hash equality on the fiber-algebra value. Per [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../theorems/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md), `ETag` equality witnesses byte-equal canonical encoding — the strongest equality on the fiber.
- **`Last-Modified` (weak).** Substrate-clock equality on the fiber's last-write tick. A finer-grained edit that did not advance the recorded write-tick would not be detected; conversely, a re-write that did not change the value would be falsely reported as modified. `Last-Modified` is admissible as a re-validator when the substrate's clock-tick discipline tracks fiber-edit precisely.

## Engineering consequence

- **`Cache-Control` violations are doctrine-fidelity violations.** A substrate that returns a value differing from $u_w$ for a request at $w'$ within the necessity window has produced a response inconsistent with its own $\Box_{R_{\le N}}$-claim. Per [doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor](../../../theorems/doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor.md), this is a wire-side coercion: the substrate's audit trail records the new value while the necessity claim still covers the old one. Conformance per [run-conformance-test](../../../morphisms/run-conformance-test.md) detects such cases by issuing two requests within the declared window and checking byte-equality of the response.
- **Necessity is monotone in the truth-order.** A substrate may *strengthen* its commitment within the window (return a value with a strictly higher truth-component — e.g., resolve a $\bot$-cell to $\mathbf{t}$); per the strong-implication semantics, this is admissible. The substrate may not *retreat* (return a value lower in the truth-order). The asymmetry is what `stale-while-revalidate` formalizes — the cache may return its stored $u$ even when the substrate's value has risen, accepting a known-stale lower bound for the duration of the revalidation.
- **Federation-aware caching.** Per [federation-convergence](../../../theorems/federation-convergence.md), the value $u$ at a federated request's response is the $\vee_k$ of peers' values; per [federation-trust-bilattice-graded](../../../theorems/federation-trust-bilattice-graded.md), the necessity along $R_{\le N}$ is the $\wedge_k$-meet of the peers' necessity claims — a cache may only assert necessity within the window all peers agreed to. The substrate's effective `max-age` on a federated response is the minimum of per-peer `max-age`s, which the substrate must compute and surface in the wire response.
- **`Age` header is the substrate's $\tau$-distance witness.** The `Age: A` header on a cached response declares $A$ seconds have elapsed since the substrate computed $u_w$. Combined with `max-age=N`, the remaining necessity window is $N - A$ seconds. The arithmetic is the substrate's witness of its position in $R_{\le N}$.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Caches without a substrate-side necessity claim.** A cache that stores responses lacking `Cache-Control` (relying on heuristic freshness based on `Last-Modified` age, browser defaults, or proxy guesses) is operating *outside* any substrate-issued $\Box_{R_{\le N}}$ commitment. Such reuse is admissible only if the cache treats the response as $\bot$-class — a hint, not a substrate commitment. Per the substrate's [self-representation](../../teaching/explanations/self-representation.md), wire reuse without substrate-side necessity is a discipline failure on the cache's side, not on the substrate's.
- **`Cache-Control: max-age=N` returned with response classes that cannot be necessitated.** A `5xx` response (substrate at $\top$ — paraconsistent contradiction per [status-code-classes-as-four-bilattice-values](status-code-classes-as-four-bilattice-values.md)) cannot legitimately carry a positive `max-age`: the substrate is in a $\top$-cell, has no settled value to necessitate, and any cache of the response would extend the contradiction. Conformant substrates emit `no-store` (or omit cache-control entirely, defaulting to no-store) on `5xx`.
- **`Vary: *`.** Declaring every request coordinate as a fiber-axis is the substrate's admission that it cannot determine the response's fiber-indexing, which is structurally equivalent to refusing to cache. The substrate is more honest emitting `no-store` than `Vary: *`.
- **Tunneling caching via response-body envelopes.** A pattern of returning `200 OK, Cache-Control: max-age=3600` with a body envelope `{"cached_at": ..., "max_age": ...}` that the application code respects but caches do not is a doctrine violation: the substrate's cache-control commitment is at the wire layer, not the body layer. Per [doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor](../../../theorems/doctrine-fidelity-via-faithful-interfacer-functor.md), the wire cache-control must accurately report the substrate's necessity claim.

## Position

Together with [conditional-get-as-stalk-equality](conditional-get-as-stalk-equality.md) (the equality witness used at re-validation) and [content-negotiation-as-modal-possibility-projection](content-negotiation-as-modal-possibility-projection.md) (the dual modal operator on the representation axis), this theorem completes the web-server-platform's modal account of HTTP response shape: cache-control is necessity along time, content-negotiation is possibility along representation, conditional GET is the equality query that re-extends necessity. The substrate's HTTP response is, in every coordinate, a modal projection on the predication fiber — necessity in time, possibility in representation, equality at the stalk.
