Enclosure is the process of converting [commons](./commons.md) — collectively governed shared resources — into private [property](./property.md).
The English enclosure acts of the fifteenth through nineteenth centuries are the paradigmatic case: parliamentary legislation and landlord action converted common pastures, forests, and fields into privately owned parcels, displacing the rural population from the land that sustained them.
This dispossession created a class of people who, lacking independent access to subsistence, had no choice but to sell their [labor](../../../../../../../terms/labor.md) for wages.

[Karl Marx](../../../../../../../../../../references/people/karl-marx.md) analyzed enclosure as part of [primitive accumulation](../../../terms/primitive-accumulation.md) — the historical process that created the preconditions for capitalist production.
Enclosure was not a natural evolution from feudalism to [capitalism](./capitalism.md) but an act of organized dispossession backed by law and violence.
E.P. Thompson documented how enclosure destroyed not just economic arrangements but entire ways of life: customary rights, communal governance, seasonal rhythms of work and rest.

Enclosure is not only historical.
Contemporary forms include the privatization of public services, the expansion of intellectual [property](./property.md) regimes (patents, copyrights, trademarks) over knowledge and genetic material, corporate land grabs in the Global South, and the commodification of digital spaces.
Each converts shared or public resources into privately controlled assets, reproducing the same structural logic.

The connection to [settler colonialism](../../../../povinellian/theories/settler-colonialism/terms/settler-colonialism.md) is direct: the colonization of Indigenous lands is enclosure on a continental scale.
The conversion of collectively stewarded territory into private [property](./property.md) through treaties, allotments, and outright theft follows the same logic as the English enclosures, extended through racial and imperial violence.
