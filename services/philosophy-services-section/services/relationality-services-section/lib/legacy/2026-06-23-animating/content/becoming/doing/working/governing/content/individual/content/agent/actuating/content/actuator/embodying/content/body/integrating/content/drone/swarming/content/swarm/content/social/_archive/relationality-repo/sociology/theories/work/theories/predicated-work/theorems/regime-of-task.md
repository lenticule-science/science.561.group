# Regime of a task

A [task cell system](../objects/task-cell-system.md) $T = (G_T, \mathcal{S}_T, v^T_0)$ embedded in an [operations settling](../objects/operations-settling.md) is in:

- the **strict regime** iff $\mathcal{S}_T$ is finite, $G_T$ is finite, and the entity references in $\mathcal{S}_T$'s antecedents are bounded by a finite sub-set of $E_{\mathrm{op}}$;
- the **Scott-continuous regime** iff $\mathcal{S}_T$ is Scott-continuous as a closure-rule-language fragment but its antecedent-references admit countably-infinite entity sub-sets;
- the **transfinite-bounded regime** iff $\mathcal{S}_T$ contains rules with non-Scott-continuous antecedents (e.g., "if every prior cycle has shown $P$") and the antecedent-aggregation cofinality is countable;
- the **aperiodic regime** iff the antecedent-aggregation cofinality is uncountable.

## Sketch

By the [four-regime dichotomy](../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/four-regime-dichotomy.md), the regime of any settling is determined by the closure ordinal of its closure operator.
For a task cell system embedded in an operations settling, the *task-restricted* closure operator is $\mathrm{cl}^{\mathcal{S}_T}$ — the closure generated by the task-local subsystem $\mathcal{S}_T$.

By [predicate-generator exhaustiveness](/predication-categories/library/predication-cell-complex-site/content/explanations/exhaustiveness-of-four-predication-cell-complex-site-generators.md), the task-restricted closure operator's regime depends on:

1. The cardinality of the task's predication-cell sub-set ($|G_T| + $ ancestor cells in the antecedents).
2. The Scott-continuity of $\mathcal{S}_T$'s closure-rule-language fragment.
3. The cofinality of the antecedent-aggregation if non-Scott-continuous.

Applying the dichotomy's equivalences regime-by-regime:

- **Strict.** Finite $G_T$, finite $\mathcal{S}_T$, finite antecedent-references give finite ascending chains in the task-restricted fiber algebras. $\kappa < \omega$.
- **Scott-continuous.** Countable antecedent-references with Scott-continuous closure rules give $\kappa = \omega$.
  The task settles "in the limit" — the goal cell carries its target value at the $\omega$-th stage.
- **Transfinite-bounded.** Non-Scott-continuous closure rule with countable cofinality. $\kappa$ is some specific countable ordinal $> \omega$.
  The task involves aggregating across countably-many prior stages.
- **Aperiodic.** Non-Scott-continuous closure rule with uncountable cofinality. $\kappa \geq \omega_1$.
  The task does not stabilize at any countable ordinal; it carries cohomological content. $\square$

## Examples per regime

- **Strict-regime task**: "review this PR" — the goal cell is a single `completed-by` cell, the closure rule is the trivial transition open → active → completed, finite computation.
- **Scott-continuous-regime task**: "tag every open-source advisory in the past 30 days" — the goal cell aggregates across a countably-infinite candidate set, but each candidate's check is finitary.
  Settles at $\omega$.
- **Transfinite-bounded-regime task**: "after every prior weekly cycle has produced at least one Routine Read post, conclude the persona has stabilized" — the closure rule's antecedent quantifies across all prior cycles.
  Settles at some countable ordinal depending on cycle structure.
- **Aperiodic-regime task**: not a task in the work vocabulary's sense; this regime is for [endeavor-functions](function-as-aperiodic-settling.md), not for individual tasks.

## Corollary — most operational tasks are strict-regime

The work vocabulary's [task lifecycle](../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/terms/lifecycle.md) (open → active → completed/failed/abandoned) terminates in finite time per the lifecycle's *constrained state machine* structure: each transition fires on a single witness, the state space is finite, no transition aggregates across an infinite history.
This places typical operational tasks in the strict regime.

## Corollary — recurrence is non-strict

A [recurrent practice](../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/terms/recurrence.md) (return of a pattern with novelty across cycles, per Prigogine's irreversible-time arrow) is *not* a single task but a closure rule that fires once per cycle, with the cycles indexed by a countable ordinal sequence.
The recurrent practice's settling lives in the Scott-continuous regime if the cycles' content is independent (each cycle's settling depends on the increment for that cycle alone) and in the transfinite-bounded regime if cycles' content depends on aggregations of prior cycles (e.g., a cumulative review).
