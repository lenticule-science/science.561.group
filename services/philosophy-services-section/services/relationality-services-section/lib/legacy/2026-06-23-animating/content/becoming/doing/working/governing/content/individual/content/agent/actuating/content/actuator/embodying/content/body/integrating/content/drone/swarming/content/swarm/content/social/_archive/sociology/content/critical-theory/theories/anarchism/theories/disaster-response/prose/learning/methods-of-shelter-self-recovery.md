Shelter self-recovery uses a distinctive set of methods that try to support rebuilding without displacing household and community agency.
The main methods are [accompaniment](../terms/accompaniment.md), technical guidance, owner-driven incremental repair, local focal points, and simple risk-reduction messages that people can use within real resource constraints [@pathways2022; @twigg2021; @ahmedparrack2022].

## Accompaniment

Pathways Home describes support for self-recovery as accompanying a process rather than delivering a finished solution [@pathways2022].
This matters because outside actors do not control the whole recovery pathway.
They support people who are already making decisions under conditions of loss, scarcity, and urgency.

## Incremental owner-driven repair

A key method in this school is staged, owner-driven rebuilding.
Ahmed and Parrack show that in Vanuatu people moved through their own repair pathways using different combinations of labor, materials, assistance, and technical advice [@ahmedparrack2022].
This method treats recovery as a sequence of household decisions rather than a single external delivery event.

## Technical guidance and build back safer messages

Technical guidance is another core method.
The Shelter Cluster's build back safer key messages were explicitly designed for owner-driven incremental recovery, giving households simple accessible advice rather than assuming an ideal but unaffordable reconstruction model [@buildbacksafer2019].
