# σ/Δ pass decomposition

The substrate's settlement is the iteration of *two distinct nuclei* on the fiber algebra to a joint fixed point. The two nuclei are:

- **σ-nucleus:** the rule-saturation closure operator, generated by the substrate's [closure-rule system](../objects/closure-rule-system.md). Iterates by *firing closure rules* until no new conclusion is producible. Output stratum: `"saturated"`.
- **Δ-nucleus:** the [bilattice stabilization nucleus](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/bilattice-stabilization-nucleus.md), iterating per-cell composite steps $T_a(x) = a \Rightarrow (a \odot x)$ to per-cell fixed points. Output stratum: `"settled"`.

The substrate's full settlement of a fiber is then the joint fixed point

$$H^*_t \;=\; \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \,\cap\, \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta),$$

obtained by alternating σ-passes and Δ-passes until both nuclei are simultaneously fixed.

## The two senses of "σ" and "Δ" in this corpus

The notation σ/Δ appears in two structurally different roles in this corpus, and conflating them produces confusion that has surfaced in dev specifications more than once. The two roles are:

| Role | σ | Δ | Where used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pass decomposition** (this concept) | Rule-saturation closure | Bilattice-stabilization nucleus | Settlement engine, per-respond pass shape, per-cell fuel bounds |
| **Bigraded modality** ([substrate-operations-are-bigraded-modal](../theorems/substrate-operations-are-bigraded-modal.md)) | Closure 2-monad as necessity-side modality | Dualized closure 2-comonad as possibility-side modality | Closure-kind classification, modal calculus on substrate operations |

In the **pass-decomposition** sense (this concept), σ and Δ are two operators that act on the fiber algebra and are iterated by the substrate's runtime. Their joint fixed point is the settled fiber. The pass-decomposition σ and Δ are *operationally distinct* — they produce different output strata, consume different fuel budgets, and have different termination conditions.

In the **bigraded-modal** sense (the existing [substrate-operations-are-bigraded-modal](../theorems/substrate-operations-are-bigraded-modal.md) theorem), σ and Δ are the necessity-style and possibility-style modalities on the truth-axis and knowledge-axis respectively, used to classify operations into the four [closure kinds](closure-kind.md). The bigraded-modal σ and Δ are *structural classifiers* on operations, not iterative passes.

The two senses are related but distinct. The pass-decomposition σ-nucleus, restricted to the truth-axis behavior of the closure 2-monad, is the bigraded-modal σ. The pass-decomposition Δ-nucleus is **not** the bigraded-modal Δ — the pass-decomposition Δ is a knowledge-axis stabilization on individual cells, while the bigraded-modal Δ is the dualized 2-comonad acting as a possibility-style modality on the entire fiber. When a specification or implementation uses "Δ" without context, the surrounding text tells the reader which sense applies; this concept records the distinction so that the ambiguity is documented.

## Structural distinctness of the two passes

The σ-pass and Δ-pass are not operationally fused into a single loop, even though both nuclei act on the same fiber algebra. Three structural facts make the separation load-bearing:

1. **Different termination conditions.** The σ-pass terminates when a layer of rule firings produces no new conclusion (a rule-system fixed point). The Δ-pass terminates when every moved cell's $T_a$-orbit reaches a per-cell fixed point. Conflating the two into a single loop hides which condition exhausted fuel when fuel runs out, making post-hoc diagnosis impossible.
2. **Different fuel disciplines.** Per [delta-pass-bounded-by-bilattice-orbit](../theorems/delta-pass-bounded-by-bilattice-orbit.md), per-cell Δ-fuel is structurally bounded ($\leq 2$ substeps per cell on $\mathbf{FOUR}$). Per the substrate's regime classification, σ-fuel is regime-dependent and may grow with rule count and seed size. The two budgets compose by a Lawvere quantale per [cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale](../theorems/cost-composition-via-lawvere-quantale.md) but are not comparable as raw counts.
3. **Different output strata.** σ-firings produce cells at the *saturated* stratum (rule-derivable conclusions); Δ-stabilizations produce cells at the *settled* stratum (per-cell bilattice fixed points). The two strata are observable substrate content recorded by the [witness](../objects/witness.md) chain; the substrate's audit and projection machinery distinguishes them. A single-loop implementation that emits all closure-derived cells at one stratum loses the per-cell witness chain's structural information.

## Joint fixed point and outer alternation

After the σ-pass reaches its fixed point, the Δ-pass may stabilize cells in ways that match previously-unsatisfied closure-rule premises. When this happens, the substrate runs another σ-pass, then another Δ-pass, alternating until both nuclei are simultaneously fixed.

The outer alternation has its own bound, separate from σ-fuel and per-cell Δ-fuel. In the strict regime with finite rule sets and finite fibers, the outer alternation is bounded by the number of distinct value transitions a single cell can undergo, which on $\mathbf{FOUR}$ is at most three (the number of edges in the cell's value's $\leq_k$-upset traversal). Empirically, conforming rule systems stabilize the outer alternation in 1–2 round-trips; the structural bound is an [open question](questions/sigma-delta-outer-alternation-bound.md) whose answer would let substrate cost models forecast outer-fuel consumption deterministically.

## Why two nuclei rather than one

A single closure operator on the predication fiber algebra would *also* settle the fiber: the algebra is a closure-operator-admitting structure per [closure-operator](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-operator.md), and the composition of σ and Δ as a single nucleus is itself a nucleus by the closure-of-composition argument. The two-nucleus decomposition is an engineering choice, not a mathematical necessity.

The reason for the decomposition is that the two nuclei have different operational profiles:

- σ-firing is a *worklist-driven* operation: enumerate rules whose premises match, fire each, append conclusions. The cost is dominated by premise matching, which is a graph-traversal problem.
- Δ-stabilization is a *cell-local* operation: pick up a cell's value, apply $T_a$ until fixed, write back. The cost is dominated by per-cell substep count, which is bounded by the bilattice's structural convergence rate.

A single-nucleus implementation that interleaves rule firings with bilattice stabilization on a per-conclusion basis pays the worklist-management overhead at every $T_a$-substep, which is wasteful. The two-pass decomposition amortizes the worklist work over the σ-pass and the per-cell work over the Δ-pass, producing a substantially cheaper runtime in the strict regime.

## Position

This concept records the substrate's commitment to a two-nucleus settlement engine and the operational distinctness of the two passes. It is the conceptual ground for [delta-pass-bounded-by-bilattice-orbit](../theorems/delta-pass-bounded-by-bilattice-orbit.md) (the per-cell Δ-bound) and the [outer-alternation question](questions/sigma-delta-outer-alternation-bound.md) (the structural bound on σ↔Δ alternation).

It also disambiguates the two senses of σ/Δ that coexist in the corpus, so that future references — in dev specifications, in audit tooling, in conformance vectors — name which sense they invoke. A reference to "the σ-pass" invokes the pass-decomposition sense; a reference to "$\Box \sigma$ on this operation's output" invokes the bigraded-modal sense. The two are structurally compatible (the bigraded-modal σ classifies operations whose σ-pass output is fixed) but not interchangeable.
