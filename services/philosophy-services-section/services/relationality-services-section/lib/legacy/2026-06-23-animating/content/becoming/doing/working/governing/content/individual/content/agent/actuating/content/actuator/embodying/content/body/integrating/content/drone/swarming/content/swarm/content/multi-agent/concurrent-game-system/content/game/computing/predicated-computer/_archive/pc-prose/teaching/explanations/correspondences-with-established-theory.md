# Eleven correspondences (plus three structural promotions): locating the predicated computer against established theory

The predicated computer is not a new computational object — it is a familiar object stated under a different mathematical reformulation, with a substrate that happens to be the *relation* rather than the addressed memory cell. The predicated-computing literature inherits a substantial inheritance from automata theory, cybernetics, software architecture, sheaf theory, and thermodynamics; this narrative locates the substrate against eleven established traditions so a reader familiar with any of them can find the substrate's machinery in vocabulary they already know.

The correspondences are not metaphors. They are structural equivalences: each tradition formalizes some aspect of computation that the predicated computer reformulates within its substrate. Reading the substrate against the eleven traditions is one of the more efficient ways to verify that the substrate's commitments are not arbitrary — each commitment turns out to be a natural reformulation of a commitment some prior tradition made, sometimes for reasons that made the prior tradition's framing limit further generalization.

## Turing machine

A Turing machine has a tape, a head, a finite control, and a transition function that updates the tape and the head's position based on the current symbol. The predicated computer's correspondence:

| Turing | Relational |
|---|---|
| Tape | The substrate's [predication-graph store](../objects/predication-graph-store.md) |
| Head | The substrate's [wavefront](../teaching/explanations/wavefront.md) — the region currently being settled |
| Finite control | The substrate's closure-rule system |
| Transition function | The substrate's [settle](../morphisms/settle.md) operator advancing one wavefront layer |
| Halting | Quiescence — the wavefront propagates no further |

The reformulation: the Turing tape is a single-dimensional addressed memory; the substrate's store is the *relation*-indexed analog. The Church-Turing thesis at the substrate level becomes "what the substrate can compute is what its closure machinery can settle" — exactly the [settling-as-computation](../teaching/explanations/settling-as-computation.md) commitment.

## Mealy and Moore automata

A Mealy automaton produces output as a function of state and input; a Moore automaton produces output as a function of state alone. The substrate's per-step output (the newly settled predications produced by admitting one port-typed message) corresponds to Mealy output. The substrate's per-fiber observation (querying the substrate's settled state at a given history) corresponds to Moore output. The two are different observation modes of the same substrate — Mealy observation is "what changed because of this admission," Moore observation is "what's true now."

## Von Neumann stored program

In Von Neumann's architecture, code and data share the same memory — the program is data the CPU consumes. The substrate's [self-representation](../teaching/explanations/self-representation.md) discipline is Von Neumann's principle made structural: the closure rules, shapes, scopes, witnesses, and operator specifications are all substrate predications, indistinguishable from "ordinary content" except by which closure rules apply to them. There is no separate "instruction memory"; the substrate is its own program store.

## Shannon channel

Shannon's channel has a capacity bounded by `log` of the size of its output alphabet per unit time. The substrate's analog: the per-fiber capacity is bounded by the value-domain's size; the per-step capacity is bounded by the wavefront width times the closure-rule count. The substrate's [cost discipline](../teaching/explanations/cost-discipline.md) — *reads are free, only settling pays* — is Shannon's principle restated: information that is *transmitted* (admitted, settled) costs; information merely *available* (queried) does not.

## Eilenberg variety

Eilenberg's variety theorem identifies a recognized language with the syntactic monoid of its automata. The substrate's parallel: the substrate's history monoid is the typed step set $\Sigma$ modulo the [port](../teaching/explanations/port.md)'s independence relation $I$ — a Mazurkiewicz trace monoid. The substrate's recognized "language" is the set of fixed-point-stable predications across all histories — what survives, across every fiber, the [closure operators](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-operator.md) generated by the substrate's [closure-rule system](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/closure-rule-system.md).

## Manes-Arbib bialgebra

Manes and Arbib formalized state-transition systems as bialgebras: an algebra structure (input behavior) and a coalgebra structure (output behavior) on the same carrier. The substrate's correspondence is direct: the boundary's input morphism is the algebra component (admitting steps); the [stepping](../teaching/explanations/stepping.md) discipline is the coalgebra component (advancing state under each step); the [engine](../teaching/explanations/engine.md)'s settle operator is the bialgebra structure map composing the two.

## Wiener cybernetics

Wiener's cybernetic system has a feedback loop that compares output against a reference and adjusts behavior to close the gap. The substrate's settle operator *is* a negative-feedback operator: at each closure-rule application, the substrate's [closure operator](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-operator.md) measures the gap between the current [predication fiber algebra](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md) valuation and its closure, and the next application closes part of that gap. The substrate's quiescence — the wavefront reaching fixed point, formalized as [settling quiescence](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-quiescence.md) — is the cybernetic system reaching homeostasis. The substrate is *constitutively* cybernetic, not metaphorically.

## Conant and Ashby's Good Regulator theorem

The Good Regulator theorem (Conant and Ashby 1970) states that any regulator of a system must contain a model isomorphic to that system. The substrate's [doctrine-fidelity](../teaching/explanations/doctrine-fidelity.md) discipline is the substrate-level statement: every interfacer admitting external content must carry an isomorphic representation of the source's structure, not a quotient or projection. The substrate's response to "be a faithful regulator of the source" is to *be* a faithful regulator — to construct the doctrine-isomorphic copy as substrate content before reasoning about the source.

## Landauer's erasure principle

Landauer (1961) showed that erasing one bit of information requires at least $k_B T \ln 2$ joules of energy. The substrate's analog: each closure-rule application that produces a new fiber costs in proportion to the *spectral mass* of the kernel — the predications newly fixed in the new fiber that cannot be recovered from the old fiber. The substrate's [cost discipline](../teaching/explanations/cost-discipline.md) makes the analog operative: reads are free (no irreversible computation); settlement is the only billable operation (the only place irreversible computation happens). Bennett (1973) showed that any irreversible computation can be made reversible by preserving the erased history; the substrate's [event log](../objects/event-log.md) is exactly that preservation.

## Marx's machinery

Marx's analysis of machinery (Capital, Vol. 1, Ch. 15) distinguished the *motive force* of a machine from the operator who tends it: a machine has its own motive force; the operator's role is to feed it raw material. The substrate's analog: the [engine](../teaching/explanations/engine.md) is the substrate's motive force — it settles the wavefront on its own, given the substrate's content; the operator's role (per [driver-substrate-split](../teaching/explanations/driver-substrate-split.md)) is to admit content at the boundary, not to perform the settlement. The substrate has its own motive force; the operator does not power it.

## Searle's Chinese Room

Searle (1980) argued that a system manipulating symbols according to formal rules does not thereby understand what the symbols mean. The substrate's response is structural: the substrate's [settling-as-computation](../teaching/explanations/settling-as-computation.md) settles syntactic predications into doubly-stable form; whether the predications "mean" what they assert is *not* a substrate-level question. The substrate carries the structure of meaning (per doctrine-fidelity); whether the structure is grounded in something outside the substrate is for the substrate's downstream — agents, sensors, federation peers — to negotiate. The substrate is a Searle-good syntactic regulator; semantic grounding is downstream of substrate operation.

## What the correspondences clarify

Reading the substrate against the eleven correspondences makes several substrate commitments more precise:

- The substrate is *not* a graph database with computation layered on it. It is a Turing-equivalent computational object whose substrate is the relation, with closure-driven settlement playing the role of the Turing machine's transition function.
- The substrate's reads-free / settlement-pays cost discipline is not arbitrary. It is the substrate's reformulation of Landauer's principle, made operative by the substrate's audit trail (Bennett's reversibility argument).
- The substrate's [interfacer](../teaching/explanations/interfacer.md) discipline is not arbitrary. It is the substrate's reformulation of the Good Regulator theorem, applied to every external interface.
- The substrate's [self-representation](../teaching/explanations/self-representation.md) is not arbitrary. It is the Von Neumann principle pushed to its logical conclusion: not just that code and data share memory, but that *every* operative element — closure rules, shapes, witnesses, operations themselves — is substrate content.
- The substrate's two-closure architecture (the $\sigma/\Delta$ pair of [closure operators](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-operator.md), per the math reformulation) is not arbitrary. It is the substrate's reformulation of cybernetic feedback, made structurally definite: the two operators correspond to closure-against-the-past and closure-against-the-future on the [predication fiber algebra](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md), and their composition is the substrate's homeostatic operator.

The correspondences also reveal what the substrate's reformulation has *gained* over the prior traditions. The Turing machine has no built-in audit trail; the substrate has one in the event log. Mealy and Moore automata have no notion of compatible-section gluing; the substrate has it through closure condition 7. Cybernetic feedback as Wiener formulated it has no built-in cost accounting; the substrate has Landauer-grade accounting through the cost discipline. Each correspondence is both a re-statement and a refinement.

## Three structural promotions from the predication-categories math reformulation

Beyond the eleven established-theory correspondences, the substrate's engineering library now carries three *structural promotions* from the predication-categories math reformulation. Each promotes a math object or theorem to substrate content — not as a correspondence ("the substrate resembles X") but as a substrate-content claim ("the substrate's content *is* X, materialized").

### The four-regime stratification as substrate parameter

Per [four-regime-dichotomy](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/four-regime-dichotomy.md), every closure-driven predication settling falls into exactly one of four regimes (strict, Scott-continuous, transfinite-bounded, aperiodic) classified by the closure ordinal. Per [regime-classification-for-substrate](../theorems/regime-classification-for-substrate.md), this lifts to substrate engineering: every conformant predicated computer sits in one regime. The promotion adds:

- The [regime-record](../objects/regime-record.md) object: regime as substrate-resident, queryable, version-tracked content.
- Four [regime engineering envelope theorems](../theorems/strict-regime-engineering-envelope.md) — one per regime — bounding the substrate's fuel, audit, federation, and snapshot disciplines from the regime declaration alone.
- The [rule-language-realizability-determines-regime](../theorems/rule-language-realizability-determines-regime.md) theorem tying the substrate's rule-language fragment to its regime via the four PredCat per-regime realizability theorems, resolving the previously-set-aside [rule-language question](../learning/questions/rule-language.md) at the engineering level.
- The [regime-discipline](regime-discipline.md) narrative walking through what each regime obliges and how regime-distinguished substrates federate.

### The closure 2-monad realized as the closure-rule system

Per [closure-2-monad](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-2-monad.md) and [closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md), the substrate's closure machinery is structurally the closure 2-monad on the predication fibration. Per [settlement-via-closure-2-monad](../theorems/settlement-via-closure-2-monad.md), the substrate's settlement is the iteration of $\mathrm{Cl}$ to fixed point. The promotion adds:

- The [closure-rule-system-realizes-closure-2-monad](../theorems/closure-rule-system-realizes-closure-2-monad.md) theorem: the substrate's [closure-rule system](../objects/closure-rule-system.md) is the closure 2-monad, materialized — its unit, multiplication, monad laws, and lax-idempotence are substrate-content properties.
- The [substrate-operations-are-bigraded-modal](../theorems/substrate-operations-are-bigraded-modal.md) theorem: the four [closure-kinds](../teaching/explanations/closure-kind.md) (Process, Derivation, Procedure, Inquiry) are the four positions in the bigraded modal lattice $(\sigma, \Delta)$ of the substrate's two disentangled KZ structures (per [two-disentangled-kz-structures](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/prose/teaching/explanations/two-disentangled-kz-structures.md)).
- This resolves the previously-open [modal-operators-on-substrate question](../learning/questions/modal-operators-on-substrate.md): the substrate realizes a bigraded modal lattice whose four cells are the closure-kinds; modal proof rules are inherited from the KZ structure; modal-decision evaluation is regime-determined.

### The inconsistency sheaf as substrate object

Per [inconsistency-sheaf](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/inconsistency-sheaf.md), the substrate's $\top$-cells (cells receiving contradictory predications) form a sheaf $\mathcal{F}_U^{(\top)}$ whose cohomology classifies the substrate's contradictions. Per [audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology](../theorems/audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology.md), substrate audit content lives in this cohomology. The promotion adds:

- The [inconsistency-sheaf-record](../objects/inconsistency-sheaf-record.md) object: the inconsistency sheaf as substrate-resident content, with $\top$-cells indexed by step ordinal, witness citations, source classifications, and a cohomology cache.
- The [query-inconsistency-cohomology](../morphisms/query-inconsistency-cohomology.md) morphism: the substrate's audit-cohomology primitive that operates on the record. Per the regime-engineering envelope theorems, the operator's cost is regime-determined: polynomial-time $H^0$-counts in stabilizing regimes, firing-ordinal-traversal $H^1$-enumeration in non-Scott-continuous regimes, direct-limit assembly with declared bound in the aperiodic regime.

### What the three promotions clarify

Reading the three promotions against the eleven correspondences makes the substrate's relationship to the math reformulation more precise:

- **The substrate is not a model of the math.** It does not "implement" the predication-categories framework as an external system. The substrate's content *is* the math objects, materialized. The closure-rule system is the closure 2-monad; the regime record is the four-regime classification; the inconsistency-sheaf record is the inconsistency sheaf.
- **Engineering decisions are math results.** The four [regime engineering envelope theorems](../theorems/strict-regime-engineering-envelope.md) translate per-regime cohomology and realizability theorems into substrate-level fuel, audit, federation, and snapshot disciplines. The substrate's engineering envelope is structurally determined by the math.
- **Math abstractions become substrate primitives.** The cohomology of the inconsistency sheaf — a structural mathematical operation — becomes a substrate primitive ([query-inconsistency-cohomology](../morphisms/query-inconsistency-cohomology.md)) that engineers invoke. The KZ structure's modal calculus becomes a substrate-content modal classification (the closure-kinds). Math abstractions and substrate operations are the same content viewed from different angles.

The three promotions together establish the substrate's claim to *be* a categorical computer — not just to resemble one. The substrate's engineering rests on its math, and the math's structural results are the substrate's engineering content.
