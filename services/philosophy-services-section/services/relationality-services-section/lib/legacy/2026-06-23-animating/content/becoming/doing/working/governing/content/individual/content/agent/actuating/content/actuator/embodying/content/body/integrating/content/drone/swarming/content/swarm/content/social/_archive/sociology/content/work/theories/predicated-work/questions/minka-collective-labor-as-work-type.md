# Is minka collective labor a distinct work type on the operations-settling?

The Andean ayllu's *minka* (collective labor obligation for community projects) creates a work obligation that the predicated-work typology may not fully capture.
Existing work types — task, plan, workflow, recurrent-practice — are all individually grounded: the assignment morphism places $\langle T, \texttt{assigned-to}, A\rangle = \mathbf{t}$ on a specific agent.
Minka obligation is asserted by the community's governance layer over the polity as a whole, distributing labor without individual pre-assignment.

## What minka looks like on the substrate

Per [ayni-reciprocity](theories/predicated-governance/objects/ayni-reciprocity.md), minka creates a collective-obligation cell:

$$\langle \mathrm{Pop}, \texttt{owes-minka-for}, \mathrm{Project}\rangle = \mathbf{t}.$$

Individual contribution is then allocated by a distribution rule within the polity's closure-rule subsystem.
The allocation structure is:

1. *Post-obligation*: the collective obligation is asserted first; individual contributions are determined after.
  This inverts standard task-assignment (assign individual → individual has obligation).
2. *Collectively witnessed*: completion is attested by the community recognizing that the project is complete, not by individual agents reporting task-completion.
3. *Reciprocally embedded*: minka participation builds community standing in the ayni-debt network; non-participation generates a community-standing deficit rather than a Hohfeldian sanction.

## Specific questions

1. **Is minka a plan?** A [plan](../objects/plan.md) is a composed collection of task cells whose joint completion constitutes a higher-level objective.
  Could minka be represented as a plan where each individual's contribution is a task cell, and the plan completes when all tasks complete?
  Or does the collective-obligation structure require something the plan object cannot represent — specifically, that the obligation predates and determines the task assignments, rather than the tasks constituting the plan?

2. **Is the distinction substrate-relevant or merely social?** The predicated-work framework allows closure-rule parameters to represent governance-pattern-relative practices.
  Is minka's distinctiveness fully captured by a governance-pattern parameter on an existing work type, or does it require a new object?

3. **Does minka create a new regime?** Minka obligations often span generations — community infrastructure projects undertaken across multiple lifetimes.
  Does a minka-like collective obligation with intergenerational scope push the work type into the transfinite-bounded or aperiodic regime, analogously to the seventh-generation extension in [consensus-closure](theories/predicated-governance/objects/consensus-closure.md)?

4. **How does collective witnessing interact with the witness-as-increment morphism?** Standard [witness-as-increment](../morphisms/witness-as-increment.md) records individual evidence of task progress.
  Minka completion is a collective recognition event, structurally closer to the consensus-closure settlement rule than to individual witnessing.
  Does collective witnessing require a modified witness morphism, or is it handled by the consensus-closure closure rule firing over individual contribution cells?

5. **What is the connection to $S_{\mathrm{shared}}$?** The [shared-closure-rule-vocabulary](teaching/explanations/shared-closure-rule-vocabulary.md) concept formalizes how community governance rules enter the general intellect.
  Does minka, as a community-wide work obligation pattern, enter $S_{\mathrm{shared}}$ (available to all agents as background expectation) or remain in $S_{\mathrm{charter}}$ (specific to this governance pattern and its polity)?

## Notes for the answering researcher

This question checks whether the predicated-work work-typology covers collective-obligation forms.
Minka is the canonical instance, but collective labor obligation appears across traditions: barn-raising, leitourgia in ancient Athens (already formalized in [leitourgia](theories/predicated-governance/objects/leitourgia.md)), contemporary community mutual aid.
If the typology gap is real, the resolution should be substrate-algebraic (not merely a relabeling) and should draw on community-authorized accounts of how minka is governed and completed in practice.
