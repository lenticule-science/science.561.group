# Brachman & Levesque, *Knowledge Representation and Reasoning* (2004)

The standard textbook on classical knowledge
representation.

## What it covers

- First-order logic as KR foundation.
- Resolution and theorem proving.
- Frames, semantic networks, description logics.
- Default reasoning, circumscription, autoepistemic
  logic.
- Inheritance, taxonomies.
- Reasoning under uncertainty.
- Knowledge representation in expert systems.

Comprehensive coverage of pre-LLM KR.

## Why it matters

The standard textbook reference for the symbolic KR
tradition. Authoritative coverage from two of the
field's founders. Shapes how KR is taught in CS
curricula.

## How to read

Substantial textbook; can be read selectively.
Description logic and frames sections particularly
useful for ontology/KG work.

## Related works

- Sowa 2000 — alternative textbook.
- Baader et al. 2003 — description logic depth.
