In the [relationality](../_index.md) framework, **object** names a position in a directed act of [relating](relating.md): the relatum *toward which* the predicate runs.
With [subject](subject.md), the object completes the directed triadic structure of relating.
What makes the object an object — rather than the subject — is the directionality of the relating: the predicate is asserted *of* the subject *in relation to* the object.

The object is not a thing-with-properties, an external world, or a target in the intentional-realism sense.
An object in this framework is constituted by the relating in which it stands — it is what the predicate runs *toward*, and it would not be that object outside the relating.
Like the [subject](subject.md), it is a *position*, constituted by the directed relating that gives the position content.

## Asymmetric pairing

Object and subject are not interchangeable.
In a directed predication cell $(s, p, o)$, the predicate $p$ holds of subject $s$ in relation to object $o$, not the reverse.
The asymmetry is structural: the converse relation (where $p^c$ would hold of $o$ in relation to $s$) is a *different* relation, related to the original by the [predicate converse](../../../../math/theories/voidmath/terms/predicate-category.md) generator.

In the special case where a predicate equals its own converse — a *symmetric* predicate, in the math-side vocabulary — the subject and object positions become indistinguishable for that predicate.
In all other cases, *being* the object is structurally distinct from *being* the subject.

## Distinction from grammatical and realist object

Three readings of *object* should be kept apart:

- **Grammatical object** — the noun phrase in object position of a sentence.
  A surface feature of language.
- **Realist object** — the thing-out-there that perception or thought tracks; the world's furniture, conceived independently of perceivers.
  Substance-metaphysical; the relationality framework refuses this substrate.
- **Relationality object** — a position in a directed relating, constituted by the relating itself. *This* reading.

The three coincide in particular cases but the framework's term means the third.

## Object as second relatum, not as opposition

In some philosophical traditions, *object* connotes opposition (a subject opposes or objectifies an *other*).
The framework's use is more austere: object is just the second coordinate of the directed cell.
The position carries no inherent valence — neither superior, inferior, distant, nor opposed.
It is what stands at the receiving end of the directed relating, nothing more.

This is significant for the framework's politics: *objectification* in the moral-philosophical sense is not what the framework's *object* names.
Objectification names a particular practice that treats relational entities as if they had only object-position content (no subject-side, no other relatings); the framework's *object* is just the second coordinate of any directed predication cell, including in relatings where the *same entity* simultaneously occupies subject positions in many other cells.
