# The substrate's algebra is the federation protocol

When two systems federate, the engineering question is what guarantees the federation makes about the combined behavior. The traditional answer is a protocol: a specification of message formats, ordering rules, conflict-resolution policies, retry semantics, and authentication procedures, layered on top of whatever the participating systems internally do. The protocol is the federation; the systems are the participants.

This shape has a recurring pathology. The protocol's guarantees are about messages, not about state. Two participants exchanging messages "successfully" can still end up disagreeing about state, because the protocol's correctness conditions don't reach into the participants' internal semantics. Conflict resolution at the protocol layer is often *positional* — last-write-wins, primary-replica-wins, ordered-by-vector-clock — which works as long as the application's state is positional, but breaks as soon as the application's state is genuinely contested or genuinely partial. The result is a stack of protocols, each addressing the previous protocol's weakness, with the participants still doing the real semantic work themselves.

A predication substrate cuts the stack by making the substrate's value-domain the protocol's semantic content. Per [merge](../morphisms/merge.md), the substrate's join operation is commutative, associative, and idempotent. Two substrates exchanging predications and merging them into their local stores converge on the same combined content regardless of message order, regardless of repeated delivery, regardless of which side initiated the exchange. The convergence is not a protocol guarantee — it is a property of the substrate's value-domain. The protocol layer has no semantic work left to do; its sole job is transport and authentication.

This is what is meant by *the substrate's algebra is the federation protocol*. Federation between two predication substrates is two locality-bounded wavefronts that exchange predications at their interface, run merge against each other's contributions, and re-settle. There is no "federation layer" with its own semantics; the substrate's algebra is the only semantics in play. Per [replication](../teaching/explanations/replication.md), the same property makes intra-system replication work, and the federation case generalizes to inter-system: two substrates federating are structurally just two replicas of an interface region, with the rest of each substrate being non-shared.

The engineering consequences are concrete:

- **Out-of-order delivery is not a problem.** Messages arriving in any order produce the same merged result. The substrate doesn't need a vector clock or a sequence number to be safe; it needs only its own join.
- **Partition tolerance is structural.** Two substrates separated by a network partition continue to record predications independently. On reunion, a sync round merges the partitioned content; the merged state is a deterministic function of the union. There is no protocol step that "recovers" from the partition; the partition's effect is just the substrate's normal merge applied to a longer-than-usual delta.
- **Conflict resolution is the substrate's repair discipline.** When two substrates record different values for the same predication, the merged state is the substrate's value-domain join — typically a contested predication that the substrate's [coherence-glue](../morphisms/coherence-glue.md) recipe handles per the substrate's [repair-catalog](../objects/repair-catalog.md). Resolution is auditable, replayable, and substrate-content rather than a protocol-layer decision.
- **Authentication is signed-witness.** Per [witness](../teaching/explanations/witness.md), every event is witnessed; signed witnesses cross substrate boundaries with their authentication intact. The receiving substrate verifies the signature against its known identity predications and either admits or drops the event. This is the only thing a federation protocol has to specify that the substrate's algebra does not already supply, and the substrate's discipline absorbs it: the verification is a substrate operation, the result is a witness, the audit trail is substrate content.
- **Adapters are substrate entities.** When two substrates speak different vocabularies, a *bridge* — a substrate entity that records the translation between the two — is added at the federation boundary. Per [self-representation](../teaching/explanations/self-representation.md), the bridge is itself substrate content: editable, witnessed, replicable. Federation between mismatched vocabularies without a declared bridge is rejected at the inbox, with the rejection itself a substrate event.

The result is a federation discipline in which two systems' interoperation is determined by their shared substrate algebra and their declared bridge content, with no separate protocol stack accumulating semantic responsibilities the substrate could have handled. The protocol has shrunk to transport: how bytes get from one substrate to another. Every other concern — order, conflict, partition, authentication, vocabulary translation — is substrate content, evaluated by the substrate's algebra, witnessed in the substrate's audit trail.

Comparable patterns appear in distributed systems where convergence is treated as a value-domain property: CRDT-based collaborative editors, version-control systems with three-way merge, peer-to-peer networks with hash-based content addressing. Each makes a similar move at its specific layer. The predication substrate's contribution is making the move at the *substrate level*, so that every higher-level concern (applications, agents, governance, federation between organizations) inherits the convergence property without re-deriving it.

## ActivityStreams as a federation wire format

Among the available wire formats, ActivityStreams 2.0 / ActivityPub deserves explicit treatment: the substrate's federation discipline is structurally what ActivityPub specifies, and a substrate can publish to and consume from the broader fediverse by exposing an ActivityStreams projection of its content.

The mapping is straightforward:

- **Actors** are substrate entities carrying agential identity predications. Their public profile predications (per the [agent-record](../theories/agential-orchestration-platform/objects/agent-record.md) discipline) become the Actor's profile object.
- **Objects** are substrate entities carrying content predications — notes, articles, documents, observations. The substrate's [shape](../theories/agential-orchestration-platform/objects/shape-record.md) discipline supplies the type information ActivityStreams requires.
- **Activities** are the substrate's [witnesses](../teaching/explanations/witness.md) — the audit-chain entries recording state transitions (admission, settlement, repair). Each witness becomes an ActivityStreams Activity (Create, Update, Delete, Follow, Like, etc.).
- **Threads and contexts** are scope-traversal predications: ActivityStreams' `inReplyTo` and `context` correspond to substrate predications relating one entity to another within a [scope](../teaching/explanations/scope.md).

The substrate's algebra absorbs ActivityPub's semantic concerns: out-of-order delivery is handled by the value-domain join; conflict resolution is the [coherence-glue](../morphisms/coherence-glue.md) discipline; partition tolerance is the merge applied to longer-than-usual deltas. What ActivityPub specifies that the substrate doesn't have is pure transport: JSON-LD wire encoding, inbox/outbox HTTP semantics, WebFinger discovery — all of which are driver-level concerns per [driver-substrate-split](../teaching/explanations/driver-substrate-split.md), not substrate algebra.

A substrate that publishes to the fediverse exposes an ActivityStreams projection at its observation surface; a substrate that consumes from the fediverse admits ActivityStreams JSON-LD through an interfacer and runs the substrate's normal admission discipline. Per [correspondences-with-knowledge-representation](correspondences-with-knowledge-representation.md), the same substrate content also admits an RDF/OWL view at the same time, with no separate store — the wire format is the projection choice; the substrate is the source.

The pattern composes with [topology](../teaching/explanations/topology.md) and [replication](../teaching/explanations/replication.md): a federated network of substrates is a topology of replicas, with bridges where vocabularies differ, and the substrate's merge running across the whole. With [branched substrate state](../teaching/explanations/branched-substrate-state.md): a federated substrate may carry branches across peers, and the cross-peer branch relationships are substrate content. With [agential-self-generation](../theories/agential-orchestration-platform/concepts/agential-self-generation.md): an agent operating across federated substrates carries the same authorization fiber from one to the other, with the destination substrate composing the agent's claim against its local capability content. None of these requires additional protocol; each is a direct consequence of substrate-level merge being the federation's semantic content.
