# reflexivity

Reflexivity is the social's capacity to take itself as its own object — to turn the activity of knowing, describing, or relating back upon itself.
In a reflexive situation the knower is part of what is known: the observer belongs to the world being observed, the describer is shaped by the description, and the act of studying society is itself a piece of society.
Generated by: **relating** turned upon itself — relating that takes other relating, including its own, as what it relates to.

The term has a precise career in sociology.
Alvin Gouldner, in *The Coming Crisis of Western Sociology* (1970), called for a *reflexive sociology* that would make sociologists examine their own social position, interests, and assumptions rather than pretending to a view from nowhere.
The sociologist studying class is themselves of a class; the institution funding the research has interests; the categories used to describe the world were forged in particular struggles.
Reflexivity is the discipline of holding all of that in view instead of bracketing it as irrelevant to the findings.
Pierre Bourdieu radicalized this into a methodological program: *participant objectivation*, the turning of the tools of analysis back onto the analyst and the academic field itself, so that the unexamined "scholastic point of view" — the comfortable distance of someone who studies a game they are not desperately playing — becomes part of the data rather than a hidden distortion.

At the level of social theory, Anthony Giddens made reflexivity constitutive of modernity itself.
In *The Constitution of Society* (1984) and later work, he argues that modern social life is characterized by the routine incorporation of knowledge about society back into society: we act on descriptions of ourselves, and those descriptions then change what we are.
Economic forecasts move markets; crime statistics reshape policing and thereby the next year's statistics; a diagnosis alters how the diagnosed person lives.
Giddens calls this the "double hermeneutic": the objects of social science, unlike rocks or stars, talk back, read the findings, and revise their conduct in light of them.
There is no stable, indifferent object waiting to be described, because describing it is one of the forces acting on it.

The stakes are both epistemic and political.
Reflexivity dissolves the fantasy of a detached social science that mirrors a society sitting still to be photographed; it insists that inquiry is a relation *within* its object, which is the strong form of the **immanence** this branch is committed to — the knower cannot step outside the relations being studied.
Concretely, this is why a census category like "race" does not merely record pre-existing groups but helps constitute them, why publishing a theory of how elites reproduce themselves can become a manual elites use, and why the very concepts a discipline circulates loop back into the lives it studies.
Reflexivity is closely tied to **witnessing**, which can itself be witnessed, and to **polyvalence**, since a reflexively self-aware actor can hold several readings of their own situation at once.
Far from a paradox to be eliminated, it is the basic condition of any social knowledge that is honest about being part of what it knows.
