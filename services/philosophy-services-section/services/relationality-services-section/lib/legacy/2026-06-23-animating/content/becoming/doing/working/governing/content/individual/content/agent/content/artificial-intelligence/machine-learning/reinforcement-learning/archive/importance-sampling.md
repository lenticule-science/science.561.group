# Importance sampling

**Importance sampling** (IS) is the reweighting technique that converts
a Monte-Carlo estimate under one distribution (the **behavior**
distribution) into an unbiased estimate under another (the **target**
distribution). In RL it is the standard mechanism for **off-policy
evaluation**: estimating quantities under target policy $\pi$ from data
generated by behavior policy $\beta$.

## The basic identity

For any function $f$ and any $a \sim \beta(\cdot \mid s)$ with
$\beta(a \mid s) > 0$ wherever $\pi(a \mid s) > 0$,

$$\mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi}[f(a)] = \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \beta}\!\left[\frac{\pi(a \mid s)}{\beta(a \mid s)}\, f(a)\right].$$

The ratio $\rho(s, a) = \pi(a \mid s) / \beta(a \mid s)$ is the
**importance weight**.

## Trajectory-level IS

For a trajectory $\tau = (s_0, a_0, r_0, \dots)$ sampled under $\beta$,
the **per-decision importance ratio** at step $t$ is

$$\rho_{0:t} = \prod_{k=0}^{t} \frac{\pi(a_k \mid s_k)}{\beta(a_k \mid s_k)}.$$

The IS estimator of the target-policy value is

$$\hat{V}^\pi = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{\tau \in \text{batch}} \rho_{0:T-1}(\tau) \cdot G(\tau).$$

Unbiased; can have catastrophic variance if some $\rho_{0:t}$ become
very large or very small.

## Variance control

**Weighted IS** (self-normalized): divide by $\sum \rho_{0:T-1}$ rather
than by $M$. Biased but consistent and lower-variance.

**Per-decision IS** (Precup et al. 2000): exploits the fact that future
rewards do not depend on past action probabilities; reduces the
variance contribution of long trajectories.

**Doubly-robust estimators** (Jiang–Li 2016): combine IS with a
value-function baseline; variance is controlled by the quality of the
baseline.

**Clipping** (PPO-style): clip $\rho$ to a fixed range $[1 - \epsilon,
1 + \epsilon]$. Biased but very stable in practice; the standard
mechanism in modern policy-optimization pipelines.

## Coverage assumption

IS requires the **absolute-continuity** condition $\pi \ll \beta$:
the target policy can only assign positive probability where the
behavior policy does. Violations make the IS estimate undefined; in
practice they make it numerically unstable. For deep-RL applications
where $\pi$ moves substantially during training, the absolute-continuity
condition is the formal reason replay-buffer capacity must be tuned to
the rate of policy change.

## Connection to the deadly triad

Off-policy evaluation via IS is one leg of the
[deadly-triad](teaching/explanations/deadly-triad.md). The high variance of IS
in long-horizon problems is one of the practical reasons that off-policy
deep RL is hard.
