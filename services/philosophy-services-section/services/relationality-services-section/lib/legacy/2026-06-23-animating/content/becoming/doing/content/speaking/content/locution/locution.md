**Locution** is the first layer of [speaking](../../../docs/speaking.md): *what is said* — Austin's locutionary act.
It is the production of a meaningful utterance with a definite content: this subject, this predicate, this reference.
Austin decomposes it further (the phonetic, phatic, and rhetic acts — making sounds, making words in a grammar, making them mean and refer), but the point that matters here is the whole of it: locution fixes *what the utterance is about*, prior to any force.

Locution carries no force of its own.
To settle what is being spoken of is not yet to assert it, deny it, ask it, or promise it — that is the next layer's work.
This is why the same locutionary content, "the door is shut," can be put with different forces: stated, questioned, or ordered.
Locution selects the content; the force enters at [illocution](../../illocution/docs/illocution.md).

## What generates it

Locution is generated by speaking the moment the act picks out what it will address — the content drawn from the field of everything [doing](../../../../../docs/doing.md) might speak of.
It is the selecting step: it changes nothing yet, it only fixes where a force will land.

## What it generates

Locution generates a *content held ready* — what is to be spoken of, not yet charged with any force.
It hands that content to illocution, which gives it a force.
