# From Peirce to Lotman — and what each step bought

The line from Peirce's pragmaticist semiotic (1860s–1910s) to Lotman's semiosphere (1980s) and Sebeok's biosemiotic (1960s–1990s) is not a coincidence and not a parade of competing schools. Each step on the line was taken in service of a real *structural problem* in the analysis of standing-for; every step that survived did so because the next problem in the line could not be solved without it; the discipline as it stands is what the accumulated commitments look like when read structurally.

This narrative traces the line in the spirit of the [from-frege-to-fars](../../../../information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/narratives/from-frege-to-fars.md) lineage on the substrate side: each step named, the problem it solved spelled out, what its successor would have lacked without it.

## Peirce solves the *triadic-irreducibility* problem

The pre-Peircean way of writing standing-for was as a name-and-thing pair: the word *cat* stands for the animal *cat*. The structural problem: this picture has no place for the *interpretation* the standing-for serves. A name-and-thing pair is metaphysics, not semiotics; the standing-for that matters happens in a mind or a community, and the picture has no slot for it.

Peirce's move was to write standing-for as the triadic structure $(V, O, I)$ of [sign-vehicle](teaching/explanations/sign-vehicle.md), [sign-object](teaching/explanations/sign-object.md), and [interpretant](teaching/explanations/interpretant.md). The interpretant is the further sign that the sign-vehicle generates; without it the structure collapses to a dyadic name-and-thing pair, which is *not* a sign-relation. This is the structural origin of every triadic semiotic that followed.

The discipline's [sign-relation](teaching/explanations/sign-relation.md) concept is Peirce's triad. The substrate-side predication-cell triad $(s, p, o)$ is the structural cousin: the predicate plays the sign-vehicle role; the cell's subject and object play the sign-object role; the cell's placement in a discourse-community fiber is the interpretant. Peirce's solution to triadic-irreducibility is what makes the substrate's cell *meaningful* rather than merely combinatorial.

## Saussure solves the *system-of-differences* problem

Once standing-for is triadic, the question becomes: how does a sign-vehicle have its content? Pre-Saussurean answers grounded the content in the sign-vehicle's *intrinsic properties* (sounds resembling things, names motivated by their referents). The structural problem: most signs are *not* iconically motivated; the convention is arbitrary, and so the content cannot lie in the sign-vehicle's intrinsic properties.

Saussure's move was to ground the content in the *differential structure* of the langue: a sign-vehicle's *valeur* is its position in a system of differences, not an intrinsic property. The structural argument is the substitution-test: replacing one phoneme with another produces a different word; the word's meaning is the system's response to the replacement. This is the structural origin of [paradigm](teaching/explanations/paradigm.md) and [syntagm](teaching/explanations/syntagm.md) and of all structuralist analysis that followed.

The discipline inherits Saussure's commitment to *systematic structure as the locus of content*. Without it, the discipline could not name [code](teaching/explanations/code.md) as the structural primitive that pairs sign-vehicles with sign-objects.

## Hjelmslev solves the *form-substance* problem

Once content is differential, the question becomes: what *medium* does the differential structure live in? Saussure answered by gesturing at the langue without separating the langue's *structural form* from its *material substance*. The structural problem: the same form (e.g., the structural opposition between voiced and unvoiced consonants) can be realised in different substances (different phonologies); analyses that conflate form with substance lose what is structurally invariant.

Hjelmslev's move was to split each face of the sign-relation — both the expression-plane and the content-plane — into *form* and *substance*. The form is what the discipline studies; the substance is the parameter the form is realised in. This gives the four-by-four structural analytical instrument of *expression-form, expression-substance, content-form, content-substance*. The connotative iteration follows: a connotative semiotic takes a complete denotative semiotic as its expression-plane, and the form-substance split iterates up the connotative tower.

The discipline inherits Hjelmslev's [denotation](teaching/explanations/denotation.md) and [connotation](teaching/explanations/connotation.md) distinction, and the connotative-iteration mechanism that grounds the substrate-level reading of unlimited semiosis as iterated closure.

## Greimas solves the *semantic-axis* problem

Hjelmslev gave the form-substance split, but the *internal structure of the content-form* was not yet articulated. The structural problem: how is content-form *organised*? An analysis that treats the content-form as a flat list of distinctions does not name the structural relations among the distinctions; an analysis that orders the distinctions by ad-hoc means is not structural at all.

Greimas's move was the *semantic square*: a four-position structure on a binary semantic axis, with the positions related by *contrariety* (the two original poles), *contradiction* (each pole's logical denial), and *implication* (each contradictory implies the other pole's contrary). The square's structural completeness is the proof: no further positions can be added on a single axis without redundancy.

The discipline inherits Greimas's commitment to *structural relations on paradigmatic content*; the [paradigm](teaching/explanations/paradigm.md) concept's substantive operationalisation is the semantic square's class of structural instruments.

## Eco solves the *bound-on-semiosis* problem

By the 1970s the discipline had Peirce's open interpretant chain and Saussure's structurally-bounded system, with no clean account of how the two related. The structural problem: an open chain in principle and a bounded system in practice cannot both be primary; one must be derivative.

Eco's move was the *encyclopedia*: a culturally-constituted body of admissible interpretants that bounds the chain in practice. The encyclopedia is *not* a closed dictionary; it is the network of cultural-historical relations the chain draws on. The bound is the structural fact that *working* semiotic practice operates a bounded encyclopedia rather than the in-principle-open chain. Eco (per [Eco 1976](references/eco1976.md), [Eco 1984](references/eco1984.md)) develops the discipline of distinguishing the in-principle from the working-bounded.

The discipline inherits Eco's bound as the substrate-level *Scott-continuous regime* commitment per [unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint](../theorems/unlimited-semiosis-as-fixpoint.md). The Peircean open chain is the substrate's *transfinite-bounded* regime; both regimes are admissible, and the discipline does not collapse one into the other.

## Lotman solves the *semiosphere* problem

Eco bounded the chain by the encyclopedia, but the *structural locus* of the encyclopedia was not yet named. The structural problem: where does the encyclopedia *live*? An analysis that places it in individual interpreters cannot account for cross-interpreter convergence; an analysis that places it in language-as-such cannot account for the cultural-historical specificity of working semiotic communities.

Lotman's move was the *semiosphere* — a bounded cultural-historical region whose internal structure (core-periphery asymmetry, accumulated memory, internal poly-coding, boundary-translation activity) is what carries the encyclopedia. The semiosphere is the *condition of possibility for sign-life*; outside a semiosphere, sign-vehicles are inert.

The discipline inherits the [semiosphere](teaching/explanations/semiosphere.md) concept and its identification with the substrate's [discourse-community-as-fiber](../../../../information/theories/knowledge-systems/theories/predicated-knowledge-systems/concepts/discourse-community-as-fiber.md). The boundary, the core-periphery asymmetry, and the translation-as-load-bearing-operation read directly onto the substrate's scope discipline.

## Halliday solves the *social-meaning* problem

The structuralist line gave the langue and the structural relations within it, but the *social* dimension of meaning — how meaning is made in, for, and by social groups — was not yet a structural object. The problem: meaning is not produced in isolation; it is produced in *registers* keyed to *fields* of activity, *tenors* of social relation, and *modes* of communication.

Halliday's move was the *systemic-functional* analysis: register, field, tenor, mode are independent structural variables that jointly determine the social meaning of an utterance. Each variable is a paradigmatic axis; the utterance's meaning is its joint position on the four axes. The proof is the systematic-functional analysis of natural utterances: variation along any axis produces a structurally distinguishable utterance.

The discipline inherits Halliday's commitment to *register* as a per-utterance parameter. The [codeswitching](../morphisms/codeswitching.md) morphism's substrate-level reading absorbs Halliday's register-as-axis directly.

## Sebeok solves the *biosphere-scope* problem

The line through Lotman and Halliday concerned human semiotic communities; the question of whether the discipline's primitives are specific to humans was unresolved. The problem: if sign-life is specific to humans (or to symbol-using systems), then the discipline is a sub-branch of anthropology; if it is broader, the discipline must say *how broad* and *why*.

Sebeok's move was the *biosemiotic* extension: sign-life is *biospheric* in scope; every organism operates a semiotic; the discipline's primitives are general across the biosphere. The cellular signal-transduction networks, the immune-system pattern-recognition, the inter-species communication of bees and birds, the territorial marking of mammals — all are semiotic systems in the discipline's sense, with sign-relations, codes, interpretants, and (organism-bounded) semiospheres.

The discipline inherits Sebeok's biospheric scope as a *parameter* of the discipline rather than a commitment of its substrate. The substrate is open over the kind of interpreter; the biosemiotic instances populate the discipline's example-space alongside the human-cultural ones.

## What the line accumulates

Reading from Peirce to Lotman-and-Sebeok, the discipline accumulates eight structural commitments:

| Step | Problem | Commitment | What the successor would lack |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peirce | name-and-thing collapses interpretation | sign-relation is irreducibly triadic | no place for interpretation in the structure |
| Saussure | content cannot lie in intrinsic properties | langue is a system of differences | no structural primitive for paradigm/syntagm |
| Hjelmslev | form vs. substance conflated | each face splits into form and substance | no structural account of the connotative tower |
| Greimas | content-form unstructured | semantic square on binary axis | no structural template for paradigmatic analysis |
| Eco | open chain unbounded in working practice | encyclopedia bounds the chain | no account of what working communities operate |
| Lotman | encyclopedia has no structural locus | semiosphere as bounded cultural-historical region | no structural account of cross-community translation |
| Halliday | social dimension absent | register / field / tenor / mode as four axes | no structural primitive for codeswitching |
| Sebeok | scope unspecified | biospheric extension | no structural account of non-human sign-life |

Each step is required for what comes after. The accumulated commitment is what the discipline *is*; each commitment becomes a substrate-level operation under the predicated-knowledge-systems substrate (per the [composability narrative](composability-with-predicated-knowledge-systems.md)).

The discipline as it stands is not a parade of competing schools; it is the line of accumulated commitments that, taken together, constitutes structural semiotics on a working substrate.
