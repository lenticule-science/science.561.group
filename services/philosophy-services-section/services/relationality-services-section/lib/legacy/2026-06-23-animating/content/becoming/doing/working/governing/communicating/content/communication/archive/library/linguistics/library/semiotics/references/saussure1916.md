## Annotation

The posthumously-edited *Cours* is the foundational document of structural linguistics and, by extension, of all structural semiotic. For the structural-semiotic framing, three Saussurean threads are load-bearing:

1. **Dyadic sign with langue-as-third-place.** The signifier-signified pair is the visible face of the sign; the third place (the recipient's interpretation) is supplied implicitly by the langue's structural differences. This grounds the [saussurean-system](../examples/saussurean-system.md) example and the structural argument that Saussurean and Peircean systems differ in *presentation* of the third place rather than in its presence.
2. **Valeur as positional.** A sign's value is its differential position in the langue, not an intrinsic property. The substitution-test is the proof: replacing one phoneme with another produces a different word. This grounds the discipline's commitment to *systematic structure as the locus of content*.
3. **Syntagmatic / associative axes.** Sign-vehicles relate along a horizontal (combinatorial) axis and a vertical (substitution-class) axis. Hjelmslev later renamed the associative axis *paradigmatic*, by which name it is now standard. This grounds the [syntagm](teaching/explanations/syntagm.md) and [paradigm](teaching/explanations/paradigm.md) concepts.

Saussure's commitment to *synchronic primacy* and *arbitrariness of the sign* are *parameters* of his system, not commitments of the structural-semiotic discipline; see the [inheritance ledger](teaching/explanations/what-semiotics-inherits.md) on what does not inherit.
