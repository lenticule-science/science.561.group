**Settlerism** is the structural formation of settler colonialism: the three-phase operation of *displacement* (the removal of indigenous populations from their lands and relations), *replacement* (the installation of a settler population and its institutions in the cleared space), and *legitimation* (the erasure of the prior presence so that the replacement appears natural, necessary, and complete).
Wolfe's formulation — settler colonialism is a structure, not an event — is the key theoretical commitment: settlerism is ongoing, self-reproducing, and present wherever the three phases are active, regardless of historical distance from original founding moments.
Each institutional iteration — legal title, national narrative, historical curriculum, cultural form — re-applies the legitimation operation.
The settling is never complete: the erasure of prior presence must be continuously re-confirmed because living Indigenous peoples, their relations with the land, and their assertions of sovereignty persistently contest it.

## The three operations

**Displacement** is the ongoing removal of indigenous populations from their lands, relations, and governance structures.
Wolfe's insistence on the structure-not-event framing is most important here: displacement is not something that happened once and was complete.
It is a continuously operating structure — legal mechanisms, resource extraction, environmental dispossession, urban removal — that continues to sever indigenous populations' relation to land.

**Replacement** is the installation of settler institutions, legal orders, economic forms, and population on the cleared space.
Replacement is not the presence of settlers but the installation of a social formation that treats the cleared space as its natural substrate — operating as if the displacement had produced a neutral clearing rather than a structured absence.

**Legitimation through erasure** is the most analytically significant phase.
For the replacement to function smoothly, the displacement must be made invisible: the absence of prior presence must become the background assumption of the replacement formation, not a contested position requiring continuous re-confirmation.
Legal fictions (*terra nullius*, the doctrine of discovery), national narratives (the frontier myth, the Californian dream), educational institutions, and cultural forms all participate in this legitimation.
The [genring](genring.md) operation is central: indigenous peoples are genre-figured as *historically prior* and therefore *presently absent*, while settlers are genre-figured as *present* and *legitimate*.
The genre-figure naturalizes the settlement.

## Relation to liberal-ontologizing

Settlerism requires [liberal ontologizing](liberal-ontologizing.md) as its metaphysical precondition.
Only if the land can be treated as a substance with inherent properties (ownable, marketable, improveable) independent of the relational web of indigenous presence does the legitimation operation make sense.
Indigenous relational ontologies — which treat land as a network of relations, responsibilities, and kinship rather than as a property-bearing substance — are precisely what liberal ontologizing forecloses.
The foreclosure is not incidental to settlerism; it is structural.
The replacement settling can only function if the prior relational ontology has been epistemologically, and not merely physically, displaced.

## Relation to californian-projection

The [Californian projection](californian-projection.md) is settlerism's specific operation at the Pacific terminal coast: the apparatus that has converted the conditions of a specific settler-colonial clearing into successive global-scale subject-formatting exports.
The erasure operations that the Californian projection depends on — of Indigenous Californian peoples, of Mexican-Californian dispossession, of Black and Asian populations — are instances of the legitimation-through-erasure phase of settlerism.
The projection's test-and-export logic operates on the cleared space that settlerism produces.

## Crisis-settlerism as intensified mode

[Crisis-settlerism](crisis-settlerism.md) is the intensification of settlerism under conditions of [destabilization](destabilization.md): when the routine legitimation operations begin to fail (as the prior presence becomes more visible, as indigenous sovereignty assertions gain traction, as the settler social settling loses its equilibrium), the formation responds with overt assertion rather than tacit erasure.
The move from concealment to assertion is the defining mark of the crisis-settlerist intensification. ---
