A disciplinary gap is one the reader closes by invoking a body of shared apparatus that the discipline has explicitly built and maintains: a named lemma the reader has been trained to apply, a normal form the reader has been trained to compute, a diagram chase the reader has been trained to perform. Andersen, in "Acceptable gaps in mathematical proofs" (*Synthese*, 2020), singles these out from broader [acceptable-gaps](acceptable-gap.md) because they are visible: the discipline can point at exactly what apparatus the gap relies on.

The visibility matters. When a proof says "by Yoneda" or "by Sard's theorem" or "by a standard ε/3 argument," the gap is closed by something the reader can be sent to look up. The author has not done the work of the closure but has named the closure-machinery, and the reader's act of closure becomes inspectable: did the writer name the right machinery, does the machinery actually apply, did the writer respect its hypotheses? An undisciplined gap leaves the reader with nothing to check. A disciplinary gap leaves the reader with a specific check to perform.

## Disciplinary gaps as infrastructure

A field is, in part, the inventory of disciplinary gaps it licenses. To be a category theorist is to be allowed certain omissions. To learn the field is, in part, to learn which omissions you are now licensed to leave open and which ones you must still spell. The same is true of any communicative practice — software engineering, medicine, woodworking, signage design — though many practices have not catalogued their disciplinary gaps as systematically as mathematics has.

The danger in any practice is the *unmaintained* disciplinary gap: a closure that depended on shared apparatus the practice has stopped teaching, so that practitioners now invoke it without being able to discharge it. The gap was once disciplinary, but the discipline has drifted, and the omission now extracts work the reader can no longer perform. This is one route to [unaccountable-closure](unaccountable-closure.md).
