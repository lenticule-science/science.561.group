# Semantics

## What this node is

Semantics is generated by **settle** — the last morphism in the unfolding
articulate -> predicate -> posit -> settle, re-run here at the base of language.
Grammar articulates and predicates structure; force (speech acts) posits an
utterance into a context. Semantics is what those steps **settle into**: meaning
is not added on top of a parse, it is the fixed point that a settled parse
already determines. Once the structural arrangement of an expression is fixed,
its meaning is the measure that arrangement induces — the truth conditions,
the referent, the proposition.

This is why settle is the right generating morphism. Frege's principle of
compositionality makes meaning a function of the parts and the way they are
combined: the meaning of the whole settles deterministically out of the
meanings of its constituents and their syntactic mode of combination. Montague
formalized exactly this, treating the semantic value of an expression as
computed compositionally over its structure. Settle names that movement from a
resolved structure to the single value it pins down — the parse, having been
built, comes to rest on its meaning.

## What it generates / contains

This node has no canonical `content/` subconcepts yet; its body lives in
`_archive` as un-canonicalized term entries. That body clusters around the
**reference** half of meaning — how expressions come to rest on what they are
about:

- **Sense and reference** — Frege's distinction between the mode of
  presentation (sense) and the object picked out (reference); how two
  expressions can refer to one thing yet carry different cognitive routes.
- **Intension and extension** — intension as the rule that determines a
  referent, extension as the referent it settles on; in possible-worlds
  semantics, intension is a function from worlds to extensions, which is the
  measure-over-structure reading of meaning made precise.
- **Primary and secondary intension** — Chalmers' two-dimensional refinement
  separating the epistemic component (sensitive to how things turn out) from
  the metaphysical component (fixed by how things actually are).
- **Rigid designation** — Kripke's account of terms (proper names, natural
  kind terms) whose extension does not vary across possible worlds, fixed by a
  causal-historical chain rather than descriptive content.

Together these track the settle morphism on the reference side: how a term,
given its place in a structure, comes to designate. The compositional,
frame-semantic, and logical-form machinery for the sentence side belongs here
too as the body is canonicalized.
