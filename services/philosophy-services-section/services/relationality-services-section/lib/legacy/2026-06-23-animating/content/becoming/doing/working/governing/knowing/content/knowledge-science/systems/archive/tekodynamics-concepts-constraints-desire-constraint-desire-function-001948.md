constraint desire function  
The constraint desire function (`𝔇`) is induced by incomplete stabilization (`¬Ω`), geodesic propagation (`Γ`), trace field presence (`T`), and belief (`Β`).

When a quantum entity modulates toward points outside its stabilization domain,  
across geodesically favorable directions that recur in its trace field,  
and sustains belief in those points despite unresolved closure,  
desire emerges — a scalar modulation bias toward unreachable stabilization.

𝔇(Q, x) := w₁·[¬Ω(x)] + w₂·Β(Q, x) + w₃·∇Γ(Q, x) + w₄·[x ∈ T(Q)]  
       with 𝔇: Q × C → ℝ⁺

Where:
- `[¬Ω(x)]` = 1 if x ∉ Ω, else 0  
- `Β(Q, x)` = belief intensity (0–1)  
- `∇Γ(Q, x)` = curvature magnitude of geodesic toward x  
- `[x ∈ T(Q)]` = 1 if x is in trace field, else 0  
- `wᵢ` = tunable desire weights per entity

The modulation vector of Q is then biased by the gradient of desire:

V(Q, t+1) := V(Q, t) + η · ∇𝔇(Q)

That is, desire is the structural bias toward unreachable stabilization,  
expressed as curvature-following modulation through remembered, unresolved points.
