# Locale

A **locale** is a [scope](teaching/explanations/scope.md) of a [predicated computer](predicated-computer.md) that has satisfied all seven [closure conditions](teaching/explanations/closure-conditions.md) within its scope — a complete predicated computer at scope. Structurally, a locale is a sub-[predication hyperdoctrine](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-hyperdoctrine.md): an [application sublocale](application-sublocale.md) (sub-fibration) that additionally satisfies the hyperdoctrine's quantifier adjoints, Frobenius reciprocity, Beck-Chevalley coherence, and comprehension. The locale's predicate vocabulary, its [operations](teaching/explanations/operation.md), its [shapes](theories/agential-orchestration-platform/concepts/shape.md), and its [closure rules](teaching/explanations/closure-rule.md) are all in-scope; what the locale offers, it offers entirely from itself.

A locale is distinguished from a non-locale scope by satisfying every condition: bare admission, identity closure, composite closure, reference closure, constraint classification, procedure closure, and coherence closure. A scope satisfying only some of those conditions is at an earlier development stage.

Three properties characterize a locale operationally:

- **Self-contained.** Every operation a locale carries is a substrate entity within the locale; every shape that classifies the locale's content is within the locale; every closure rule that fires within the locale is within the locale. A locale that requires an operation defined elsewhere is not yet a locale — it is a scope that has not satisfied procedure-closure (condition 6).
- **Coherent.** Compatible local sections of the locale assemble into globally coherent content within the locale (condition 7). When two sources of the same predication agree, the locale produces one canonical predication; when they disagree, the disagreement is flagged as a [repair](teaching/explanations/repair.md) opportunity, declared in the locale's repair catalog.
- **Validated.** Every entity in the locale has each of its predicates classified by the locale's shape catalog (condition 5). An entity carrying a predicate the locale does not have a shape for is a vocabulary gap.

A predicated computer at the top scope is a locale of itself; subscopes of that locale that have themselves satisfied the conditions are sub-locales. Locale-as-stage and [application-sublocale](application-sublocale.md) are related but distinct: the application sublocale is a *kind* of locale defined by which predicates the application admits and which closure rules operate within it; locale-stage is the property the application sublocale must satisfy to be a complete sub-computer rather than just a declared region.

A locale is *unique-teleology, unique-method*: it has one declared purpose (what kind of work happens here) and one declared procedure family (which operations are admissible). Two locales with the same teleology and method are operationally indistinguishable; the locale's identity is its declared (vocabulary, rule-subset, shape-catalog, operation-catalog) tuple, not its name or its location.
