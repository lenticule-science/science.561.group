An **office** in the 561 Group's governance is a formalized position of authority constituted through the predicated governance framework, grounded in Lakota relational ontology and realized through the double-settlement form required by the bilattice's bigraded common-knowledge condition.

An office is not the person holding it (that is the *officer* relation), nor is it merely a functional role—it is a recognized institutional entity that persists across incumbencies and carries specific authority-cells that govern how closure rules may be invoked, who has standing to initiate governance operations, and how normative states may be modified.

## Distinguishing Predication-Shape

An office entity $O \in E^\mathrm{off}$ carries the following minimal constitutive predications:

- $\langle O, \texttt{recognition-cell}, \rho \rangle$ — the bigraded common-knowledge cell representing polity-wide recognition of the office's legitimacy
- $\langle O, \texttt{induction-cell}, \iota \rangle$ — the cell representing the ceremonial or procedural installation that activates the office
- $\langle O, \texttt{authority-gate}, \alpha \rangle$ — the closure-rule subsystem that governs which actions may be performed under the office's authority
- $\langle O, \texttt{jurisdiction-scope}, J \rangle$ — the bounded domain (territorial, functional, or relational) within which the office's authority operates
- $\langle O, \texttt{succession-rule}, \sigma \rangle$ — the morphism that governs how incumbency transfers when the office becomes vacant
- $\langle O, \texttt{revocation-condition}, \nu \rangle$ — the condition under which the office may be revoked or its authority suspended

Additionally, offices may carry pattern-specific predications depending on their governance pattern (Hohfeldian, consensus, rotational, restorative, etc.).

## The Double-Settlement Requirement

Per the recognition-as-bigraded-common-knowledge theorem, an office can only be considered installed when both: 1.
The recognition-cell $\rho$ is settled at $\mathbf{t}$ (truth-designated/Wí) across the relevant populace 2.
The induction-cell $\iota$ is settled at $\mathbf{t}$ through a wakȟáŋ-bearing induction morphism

This double-settlement form arises by construction from the bilattice $\mathbf{FOUR}$'s requirement for bigraded common-knowledge: an office cannot exist as a governance entity without both communal recognition *and* formal installation.
Attempts to exercise office-authority without both cells at $\mathbf{t}$ constitute a different morphism (such as pretension, usurpation, or de facto authority) that does not install the office in the categorical sense.

## Authority-Gate Pattern

All office authority operations instantiate the authority-gate pattern: an action $X$ may be performed under office $O$'s authority if and only if the authority-gate closure rule $\alpha$ fires on the cell $\langle O, \texttt{authorizes}, X \rangle$.
This pattern unifies: - Installation (who may install an office) - Delegation (how office authority may be delegated) - Jurisdiction (how office authority transports across base-changes) - Revocation (under what conditions office authority may be withdrawn) - Novation (how office authority may be transferred to a new office)

## Jurisdiction and Scope

An office's jurisdiction $J$ defines the boundary of its authority-cell firings.
Jurisdiction may be: - **Territorial**: bound to specific places (e.g., office:science authority over the science work facet) - **Functional**: bound to specific work types (e.g., office:theory-quality authority over theory audits) - **Relational**: bound to specific relationships (e.g., office:mediation authority over conflicts between specific offices) - **Temporal**: bound to specific durations or cycles (e.g., seasonal offices)

Jurisdictional operations (invocation, declination, submission, excess, vires) govern how office authority interacts with jurisdictional boundaries.

## Succession and Continuity

An office persists across incumbencies through its succession-rule $\sigma$, which governs how the recognition-cell and induction-cell transfer to a new incumbent.
Succession may be: - **Hereditary**: recognition and induction flow through kinship lines - **Elective**: recognition emerges from populace consent; induction follows election procedures - **Meritocratic**: recognition flows from settled virtue-cells; induction follows certification procedures - **Rotational**: recognition and induction follow predetermined cycles - **Charismatic**: recognition flows from settled wakȟáŋ-cells; induction follows vision-procedures

The office object itself remains constant; only the officer relation (the incumbent-of tuple) changes.
