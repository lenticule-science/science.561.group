**Anarchist grammar** is the rhetorical sequence that anarchist political speech follows regardless of surface content — a six-step structure that mirror-inverts the corresponding steps of [fascist grammar](../morphisms/fascist-grammar.md).
Where fascist grammar declares crisis, names a pure community, denounces enemies, reframes suffering as sacrifice, promises destiny, and asserts inevitability, anarchist grammar refuses each move with a structurally specific countermove.
The predicated-sociology analysis is that this inversion does not constitute an exit from the operational closure within which fascist grammar operates; anarchist grammar inhabits the same genre metabolism as another available political register.

## The mirror inversion

The six steps of fascist grammar are inverted by anarchist grammar in structurally specific ways:

1. **Refuse crisis as urgency.** Where fascist grammar declares crisis to authorize emergency measures, anarchist grammar refuses the genre of crisis-urgency, insisting that the conditions named as crisis are continuous structural features that emergency measures do not address.
  The crisis has always been here; nothing about its current visibility licenses concentrated action.

2. **Refuse pure community as identity.** Where fascist grammar names a pure community whose survival is at stake, anarchist grammar insists on multiplicity — the irreducible plurality of communities, the heteroglossia of voices, the refusal of any single identity-claim to organize political action.

3. **Refuse the enemy as a population.** Where fascist grammar identifies enemies as agents of decay located in a particular population, anarchist grammar locates the antagonist in *the system* — capital, state, hierarchy, settler-formation — rather than in any group of people.
  Enemies, when named, are structural rather than personal.

4. **Refuse suffering as sacrifice.** Where fascist grammar reframes the community's pain as evidence of its moral standing and sanctifies its martyrs, anarchist grammar refuses the sacrificial calculus: suffering is not redemptive, martyrdom is not currency, and political work is not legitimated by anyone's pain.

5. **Refuse destiny as inevitability.** Where fascist grammar promises destiny and asserts that history's arc guarantees the community's restoration, anarchist grammar refuses destiny.
  There is no preordained arc, only the contingent practice of acting otherwise.

6. **Refuse inevitability as agency.** Where fascist grammar asserts that "we are the storm" — that the community's action is itself the inevitable arc made visible — anarchist grammar refuses the conflation of action with inevitability.
  Action is what does not arrive on its own; the storm metaphor is precisely what anarchism rejects.

## Definitional structure

Three features mark a piece of political speech as performing anarchist grammar.

**Mirror-structural inversion.** The speech inverts the corresponding fascist-grammatical move at each of the six steps.
The inversion is specific, not arbitrary: each anarchist-grammatical move is the negation of the corresponding fascist-grammatical move along the dimension that fascist grammar makes operative.

**Negation as the principal mode.** Anarchist grammar tends to operate through refusal rather than positive construction.
Where fascist grammar asserts (*we are the community*, *they are the enemy*, *the storm is coming*), anarchist grammar refuses (*we are not a single community*, *there is no enemy-as-population*, *no storm is coming on its own*).
This negation-mode is structurally consequential.

**Recognizable genre.** Anarchist grammar produces a recognizable genre with its own affective register (refusal, irony, dark humor, prefigurative care) and its own subject-positions (the comrade, the affinity-group, the autonomous individual, the prefigurative practitioner).
The genre is reproducible and recognizable across different historical conditions and movements.

## The post-anarchist limit

Newman (2005) and the post-anarchist tradition provide theoretical resources for understanding a structural problem the mirror-inversion generates: political modes organized primarily through negation tend to reproduce the structures they negate, because negation takes the structure as its reference point and cannot fully exit it.
Anarchist grammar names its object (the fascist-grammatical sequence), refuses each step, and in refusing each step keeps the sequence operative as its defining reference.

The predicated-sociology interest is not in whether anarchist grammar is politically correct but in how it functions as a genre: how its affective register gets installed through [ontogenring](ontogenring.md), how its subject-positions are calibrated through [genre calibration](../morphisms/genre-calibration.md), and how it interacts with the broader social-settling conditions that determine whether prefigurative refusal produces durable structural effects.
The framework's descriptive claim — that anarchist grammar inhabits the same genre metabolism as fascist grammar — is a finding about how political speech circulates, not a conclusion about the desirability of anarchist politics.

## Relation to fascist grammar and genre coherence

[Fascist grammar](../morphisms/fascist-grammar.md) is the morphism anarchist grammar mirror-inverts.
The two grammars are structurally coupled: each requires the other as its constitutive reference.
A social field in which fascist grammar is dominant produces the conditions under which anarchist grammar is the legible refusal; a social field in which anarchist grammar is dominant (if such existed at scale) would produce different refusal forms.

[Genre coherence](genre-coherence.md) operates differently in the two grammars.
Fascist grammar enforces genre coherence for the folk figures it names.
Anarchist grammar, through its insistence on multiplicity and its refusal of any pure community, formally resists genre coherence as a political value — but produces its own genre coherence in the form of the recognizable anarchist subject-position, whose attributes are as stable and enforceable as those of any other genre-figure in a social field.
