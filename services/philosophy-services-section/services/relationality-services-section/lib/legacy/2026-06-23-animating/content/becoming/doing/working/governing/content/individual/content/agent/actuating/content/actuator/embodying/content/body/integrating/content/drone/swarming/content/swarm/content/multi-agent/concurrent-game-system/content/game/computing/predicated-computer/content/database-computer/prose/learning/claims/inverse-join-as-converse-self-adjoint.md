# Inverse joins are correctness-free for the planner — the converse is its own existential and universal lifting

**Claim.** Let $p$ be a substrate predicate and $f : p^c \to p$ its [converse](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/predicate-converse.md). The induced fiber map $f^* : A_p \to A_{p^c}$ on the [predication fiber algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md) is its own [knowledge-order](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/knowledge-order.md) *and* [truth-order](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/truth-order.md) adjoint:

$$\exists_f^k \;=\; f^* \;=\; \forall_f^k, \qquad \exists_f^t \;=\; f^* \;=\; \forall_f^t.$$

**Engineering consequence.** A substrate query planner faced with an inverse traversal of $p$ — answering a query whose free variables are bound along $p^c$ rather than $p$ — has *no correctness choice to resolve*. The four candidate liftings ($\exists$ and $\forall$ in each of the two bilattice orders) are the same operator. The planner does not need to reason about which lifting is appropriate; the converse is self-adjoint and the substrate's [federation-convergence](../../../theorems/federation-convergence.md) discipline applies symmetrically to both traversal directions.

## Why this holds

Per [predicate-converse-quantifier-adjoints](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/predicate-converse-quantifier-adjoints.md), the converse acts on cells as the swap $(s, o) \mapsto (o, s)$, which is its own inverse. A bijective involution on a poset is self-adjoint in any order it preserves; the swap preserves both $\leq_k$ and $\leq_t$, so all four liftings collapse onto $f^*$ itself. Per [closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/closure-2-monad-lax-idempotence.md), the substrate's closure cascade commutes with $f^*$ in both directions, so a query answered through the inverse traversal at quiescence is the same value as the query answered through the forward traversal at quiescence.

## Engineering implications

- **Single-copy data discipline.** The substrate does not maintain "the predicate" and "the inverse predicate" as two persisted records. Per the converse self-adjointness, the inverse traversal reads the same fiber-algebra entries as the forward traversal; the substrate's storage discipline records $p$ once and the [inverse-join](../morphisms/inverse-join.md) operator serves both directions from that record.
- **Reverse indexes are a cost optimization, not a correctness requirement.** A traditional database engine often maintains a separate B-tree on the reverse direction of a foreign key for query speed. Per the converse self-adjointness, *correctness* of inverse-direction queries does not depend on that reverse index. The substrate may add a reverse [secondary-index](teaching/explanations/secondary-index.md) for locality reasons under [secondary-index-as-alexandrov-cover](secondary-index-as-alexandrov-cover.md) — adding an Alexandrov-open up-set to the index cover — but the absence of one does not change the answer.
- **Cost-equivalence at the hyperdoctrinal level.** Per [graded-dispatch-via-q-enriched-fusion](../../../theorems/graded-dispatch-via-q-enriched-fusion.md), the substrate grades operations by quantale cost; the converse swap is grade-preserving (it does not amplify the cost-quantale value). Two query plans that differ only in choosing forward vs inverse traversal of $p$ have the same hyperdoctrinal cost. Cost differences observed at runtime are storage-locality artifacts, not structural cost differences.
- **Federation symmetry.** Two federated substrates that have, between them, materialized the predicate in either direction give the same answer to an inverse query. Per [federation-convergence](../../../theorems/federation-convergence.md) and the converse self-adjointness, neither substrate needs to ship the converse representation to the other.
- **Witness symmetry.** Per [witness](../../../objects/witness.md), an inverse-join witness records the traversal direction as a witness datum. The directional information is preserved in the audit log even though the resulting fiber-algebra section is the same; this lets [audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology](../../../theorems/audit-content-via-inconsistency-sheaf-cohomology.md) track which direction was actually traversed for performance attribution, while the structural correctness claim is direction-independent.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Plan space inflation by traversal direction.** A planner that enumerates "forward existential lift", "forward universal lift", "inverse existential lift", "inverse universal lift" as four distinct candidates is enumerating non-distinct candidates for the converse case. Per the theorem, the four collapse onto two distinct operators (forward $f^*$ and inverse $f^*$, where $f^*$ is the swap). The planner must recognize and quotient.
- **Isolation-direction asymmetry.** A discipline that admits "the inverse traversal is allowed to read at a different $\leq_k$-position than the forward traversal" violates the symmetry; the converse self-adjointness applies pointwise on the fiber and inherits the chosen $\leq_k$-position from [transaction-isolation-as-knowledge-order-position](../theorems/transaction-isolation-as-knowledge-order-position.md) symmetrically.
- **Federation flows that ship the converse separately.** A federation protocol that sends both $p$ and $p^c$ as separate predication payloads is shipping redundant content. The substrate's discipline ships $p$ and reconstructs $p^c$ at the recipient through the converse swap.

## What the theorem does not claim

- **Storage-level cost symmetry.** The hyperdoctrinal cost is symmetric, but page-layout, cache-line, and disk-stripe characteristics may favor one direction. Those are below the engineering claim and are the proper subject of [graded-dispatch-via-q-enriched-fusion](../../../theorems/graded-dispatch-via-q-enriched-fusion.md) at finer cost-quantale grades.
- **Symmetry for non-converse morphisms.** The theorem applies to the converse $f : p^c \to p$ and only to the converse. A general [predicate-specialization](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/predicate-specialization.md) $g : q \to p$ has distinct $\exists_g \dashv g^* \dashv \forall_g$ per [specialization-hyperdoctrinal-adjoints](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/specialization-hyperdoctrinal-adjoints.md); the planner *does* face a correctness-relevant choice between extension-by-$\bot$ and extension-by-$\top$ in that case.

## Position

This theorem joins the database-platform's structural query-rewrite family alongside [predicate-pushdown-via-frobenius](../../../theorems/predicate-pushdown-via-frobenius.md) (filters past quantifiers) and [join-reordering-via-beck-chevalley](../../../theorems/join-reordering-via-beck-chevalley.md) (quantifier ordering). The first two are non-trivial rewrites that preserve correctness; this third is the limiting case where the rewrite is *transparent* — the operator is the same operator. The three together cover the rewrites induced by the predication-hyperdoctrine's structural maps (Frobenius, Beck-Chevalley, the converse); other rewrites (e.g., those from the substrate's twist-product fusion $\odot$, recursive-CTE evaluation via the closure-2-monad, or cost-driven plan choice within a fixed correctness frame) are governed by separate theorems and are not in this family's scope.
