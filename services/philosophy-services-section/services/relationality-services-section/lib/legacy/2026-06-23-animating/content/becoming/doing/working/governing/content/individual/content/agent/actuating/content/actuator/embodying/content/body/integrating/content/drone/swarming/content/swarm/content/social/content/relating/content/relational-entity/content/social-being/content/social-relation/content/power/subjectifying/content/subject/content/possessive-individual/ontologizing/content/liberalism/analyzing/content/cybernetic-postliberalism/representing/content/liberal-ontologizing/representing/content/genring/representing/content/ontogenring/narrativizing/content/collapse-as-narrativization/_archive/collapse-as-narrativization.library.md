**Collapse as narrativization** is the genre operation by which [destabilization](destabilization.md) — the systemic condition of lost equilibrium — is formatted into recognizable personal and collective narrative arcs rather than recognized as structural.
Where destabilization names the condition of a system that has lost equilibrium (aperiodic, non-converging, losing ground to overdetermination), collapse-as-narrativization names what that condition *looks like* to subjects inside it: not system failure, but a story of crisis, challenge, transformation, and potential healing.
The collapse is real; the narrativization is the operation that converts it into a medium for subject formation rather than a site of structural analysis.
The settlement does not resolve the structural contradiction; it produces a story about the structural contradiction that subjects can inhabit.

## Three modes of narrativization

**Narrativization.** The structural collapse is formatted as a healing arc, a journey, a story of personal transformation through adversity.
The [savior-slave subject](savior-slave-subject.md) inhabits their exhaustion as meaningful experience: the weight of caring, the significance of witnessing.
Povinelli's (2011) quasi-event names the structural form — a disturbance that appears to open possibility but is already embedded in its own containment.
Collapse-as-narrativization is the quasi-event at the level of collective self-understanding.

**Monetization.** The narrative is converted into markets: survivalism products, resilience consulting, wellness infrastructure, crisis content.
The subject is not simply told a story about collapse; they are invited to consume the story as a product.
The material conditions of collapse generate economic activity through the narrative that contains them.
The containment and the market are the same operation.

**Aestheticization.** Collapse is made available as content — doomscrolling, crisis aesthetics, solidarity performance, therapeutic media.
The aesthetic makes the collapse *beautiful*, and beauty sustains attachment.
A subject who is aesthetically engaged with collapse is doing affective work that the platform collects as engagement substrate; they are also sustaining their attachment to the formation that produces the collapse.

## Structural incommensurability

The CPL corpus identifies the key political consequence: there are more structures in place to sustain crisis than to end it.
Collapse-as-narrativization produces this structural imbalance.
The narrativization creates economic incentives (survivalism, resilience), affective pathways (the healing arc, solidarity performance), and governance mechanisms (feedback loops processing crisis as ordinary input) that are all oriented toward *inhabiting* collapse rather than ending it.
Resolution would deprive the system of its operating material.

This produces a structural incommensurability between *wanting to resolve collapse* and the systems, affects, and incentives that sustain it.
The felt result is [capitalist realism](../../../../../../references/people/mark-fisher.md) (Fisher 2009): the foreclosure of alternatives without the cessation of wanting them.
Transformation is desired but appears impossible; the desire persists as affect without converting into action.

## Relation to coherent confusion

[Coherent confusion](coherent-confusion.md) is the epistemic mode that collapse-as-narrativization depends on.
For the narrativization to work, subjects must be generating interpretive labor — engaging with the collapse as a thing to understand, feel, and narrate.
Coherent confusion ensures this by keeping the structural signals overdetermined enough to sustain interpretation without ever resolving in a direction that would end the interpretive engagement.
Collapse-as-narrativization provides the narrative containers for that engagement; coherent confusion provides the inexhaustibility of the signal that fills them. ---
