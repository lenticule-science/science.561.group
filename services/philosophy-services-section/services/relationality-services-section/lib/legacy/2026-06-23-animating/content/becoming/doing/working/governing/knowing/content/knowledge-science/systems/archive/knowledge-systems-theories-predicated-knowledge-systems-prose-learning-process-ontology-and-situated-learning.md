A knowledge system makes social commitments. The predicate vocabulary it admits, the closure rules it accepts, the discourse communities whose conflicts it resolves (and whose conflicts it does not) — none of these are mathematically neutral. The four upper ontologies covered in [upper-ontologies-as-predicate-vocabularies](upper-ontologies-as-predicate-vocabularies.md) cover what *kinds of things* a predicated knowledge system can talk about; this essay covers what *kinds of knowing* it commits to and what *social arrangements* it presumes about its users.

The lineage is process philosophy on one side (Whitehead → Rescher → Seibt; Peirce as a parallel and earlier stream) and sociology of knowledge on the other (Fleck → Lave-Wenger → Knorr-Cetina). The two streams converge: process philosophy says *being is becoming* (categories track patterns of activity, not finished things); sociology of knowledge says *knowing is participating* (knowledge is not a possession but a way of being in a community of practice). A predicated knowledge system, by adopting the predication-cell substrate, takes on both commitments structurally.

## 1. Whitehead, Rescher, Seibt: process ontology

Whitehead's *Process and Reality* (1929) inverted the standard substance ontology. The fundamental units are not enduring things but **actual occasions** — momentary, finite, atomic events of becoming. An enduring thing is a pattern of inheritance across many actual occasions; the appearance of stability is the appearance of recurrence in a pattern of becomings. Rescher (1996) gave the modern textbook treatment and Seibt (2018) the Stanford Encyclopedia entry that systematises the contemporary process-philosophical literature.

Three commitments matter for the predicated framing:

- **Categories track patterns of activity.** A category does not sort finished things; it names a recurrent pattern of becoming. The directory-types of the four-group register (governing, engaging, situating, defining; methods, practices, standards; learning, researching, applying; etc.) are *gerunds* — verb-forms — for this reason. Categories on a process ontology name what is *being done*, not what *is*.
- **Identity is constituted by participation.** A thing's identity is its history of participations in actual occasions. This is the philosophical ancestor of the substrate's [identity-by-relations](../../../../../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/concepts/identity-by-relations.md) discipline: an entity *is* the predications it participates in, not a class declaration or a stored type.
- **Closure is becoming.** What process philosophy calls "concrescence" — the actual occasion's coming-to-be from its prehensions — is, in the substrate's language, [closure-driven settling](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md). Each settlement step is an actual occasion of substrate becoming; the substrate's history is a chain of such occasions.

The predicated knowledge system inherits Whitehead's process commitment: the substrate is *what becomes*, not what *is*. The cells do not sit; they are the trace of inferential becoming. The substrate's [event log](../../../../../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/objects/event-log.md) is not a side-channel for audit; it is the substrate's primary structure under the process reading. The fiber algebra is what the becoming becomes *into*.

## 2. Peirce: Firstness, Secondness, Thirdness

Peirce's triadic semiotics (Collected Papers vol. 1, esp. CP 1.300–353) classified the modes of being:

- **Firstness**: the mode of pure quality, possibility, what-could-be. The redness of red, considered apart from any red thing.
- **Secondness**: the mode of brute reaction, encounter, this-against-that. The collision of one thing with another.
- **Thirdness**: the mode of mediation, habit, lawfulness, generality. The pattern that connects encounters and makes them types of encounter.

Peirce's triad is the philosophical ancestor of the substrate's bilattice value-domain in a structurally precise way: $t$ (positive evidence affirmed) and $f$ (negative evidence affirmed) are Secondness — the brute encounter of source-with-claim. $\bot$ (no evidence) and $\top$ (positive *and* negative evidence asserted) are *not* Secondness; they are conditions on the system's Thirdness — they are facts about the *pattern* of encounters across sources, not facts about any one encounter. The substrate's distinction between $\leq_t$ (truth ordering) and $\leq_k$ (knowledge ordering) maps onto Peirce's distinction between *what the substrate's encounters say* (truth ordering = Secondness aggregated) and *how much pattern the substrate has accumulated* (knowledge ordering = Thirdness measured).

The triadic semiotics also gives the substrate's natural reading of its own settlement dynamic: closure-rule application is mediation (Thirdness applied to Secondness to produce more Secondness), and the substrate's stability under iterated closure is the substrate's habit-formation in Peirce's sense. A predicated knowledge system *develops habits* in Peirce's strict sense; the substrate's converged settlement is its accumulated habit-structure.

## 3. Lave & Wenger: situated learning, legitimate peripheral participation

Lave and Wenger's *Situated Learning* (1991) and Wenger's *Communities of Practice* (1998) are the canonical sociology-of-learning treatment. Their thesis: learning is not the transfer of content from a knower to a learner; it is the learner's progressive **legitimate peripheral participation** in a community of practice. The learner moves from peripheral to central participation by doing the community's work, in increasingly central roles, with the community's tools and norms.

Three commitments matter for the predicated framing:

- **Knowledge is constituted in practice, not held in a head.** The substrate's content is not "what is known" abstractly; it is the predication-record of what the practitioners *do*. A community's knowledge-system is the substrate-region they jointly assert, traverse, and close.
- **Communities of practice have boundaries set by participation, not by topic.** A discourse community is not "everyone interested in topic X"; it is "everyone whose participation in the community's work is recognised by the community as legitimate." The substrate represents this via [scope](../../../../../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/concepts/scope.md): a community's scope is the substrate-region in which its predications are admitted, with admission gated by the community's membership discipline.
- **Boundary objects mediate between communities.** Wenger's term for artifacts that have meaning in multiple communities, with each community reading them under its own conventions. In the substrate, a boundary object is a substrate entity that participates in predications under multiple scopes — the entity's identity is the same across communities, but the predications attached differ per community. The substrate's federation discipline handles boundary-object reconciliation as a coherence-glue operation.

The predicated knowledge system inherits situated learning's sociological commitment: the substrate is not "what is true"; it is "what the participating communities have asserted, denied, derived, and contested in the course of doing their work." The substrate's social legitimacy is grounded in the discourse-community discipline, not in a separate epistemic-correctness layer.

## 4. Knorr-Cetina: epistemic cultures

Knorr-Cetina's *Epistemic Cultures* (1999) extended the situated-learning analysis comparatively, studying how *different* knowledge-producing communities (her case studies were high-energy physics and molecular biology) develop *different* "machineries of knowing" — different ways of producing, validating, circulating, and trusting knowledge claims. Her finding: there is no single epistemic culture that all sciences share; the apparent universality of "scientific method" is a back-formation from the diversity of working epistemic cultures.

The predicated translation: each epistemic culture is a different choice of *closure-rule system*. High-energy physics admits closure rules that derive new claims from statistical aggregations of detector events with specific significance thresholds; molecular biology admits closure rules that derive new claims from experimental reproducibility with specific community-acceptance criteria. Both communities use the same substrate (predication-cells, bilattice values, predicate-fibration); they differ in the closure-rule subset they admit. The substrate's [closure-rule system](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/closure-rule-system.md) is parameterised per community; the substrate is universal, the closure system is cultural.

Knorr-Cetina's contribution to the predicated framing is the *anti-monism* about epistemic standards: a substrate that supports only one closure-rule system is a substrate that has chosen one epistemic culture and excluded all others. The four-regime classification (strict / Scott-continuous / transfinite-bounded / aperiodic) is what lets a single substrate carry multiple epistemic cultures — different communities' rule-systems can land in different regimes, and the substrate's classification of which is in which is the structural fact about how the cultures relate.

## 5. Fleck: thought collectives and thought styles

Fleck's *Genesis and Development of a Scientific Fact* (1935) is the earlier ancestor of both Lave-Wenger and Knorr-Cetina. Fleck distinguished the **thought collective** (the community of practitioners) from the **thought style** (the perceptual and inferential style the collective enforces on its members). A thought collective generates a thought style; the thought style determines what the collective can perceive as a fact.

The predicated translation: the thought collective is a discourse community (a substrate scope); the thought style is the closure-rule system the scope admits. A thought collective's facts are the substrate's $t$-valued cells in that scope; an outsider's facts are not facts in that scope, and the substrate makes the perspectival difference explicit through scope-bound traversal.

Fleck adds a piece neither Lave-Wenger nor Knorr-Cetina add: **historical succession of thought styles**. A thought collective's thought style changes over time; what was a fact becomes not-a-fact (and vice versa). The substrate represents this as scope-history: the substrate's [substrate-state](../../../../../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/concepts/substrate-state.md) is indexed by history, and a scope's closure-rule system at history $t$ may differ from its closure-rule system at history $t' > t$. Fleck's "scientific revolution" becomes a substrate-level event: a discontinuous change in a scope's admitted closure-rule subset, after which the scope's $t$-valued cells differ from what they were before.

## 6. The four-group register as social commitment

The four-group directory layout — governing / engaging / situating / defining — is a social-commitment claim, not just an organisational convenience. Each group names a class of activity that any knowledge community has to do:

- **Governing** (methods / practices / standards): the community's normative discipline — how its work is supposed to be done, how it is actually done, what counts as adequate.
- **Engaging** (learning / researching / applying): the community's transactions with knowledge — how knowledge enters, how it is reorganised for transmission, how it is applied to concrete situations.
- **Situating** (disciplines / schools / lineages): the community's intellectual placement — its institutional jurisdiction, its methodological style, its derivational chain back to other communities.
- **Defining** (topics / concepts / terms): the community's vocabulary — what it talks about, the units of knowledge it has, the linguistic handles.

The predicated framing recognises this as a [four-group-register-as-fibration](../objects/four-group-register-as-fibration.md): the four groups are *predicate-category strata* of any community's working substrate. The community's substrate carries predications in all four strata; the four-group layout is the visible surface of the predicate category's stratification. This is what the [theorem on the four-group register as a regime](../theorems/four-group-register-as-regime.md) (status: sketch) names — the social-commitment claim and the fibration-structural claim are the same claim.

## 7. What predicated knowledge systems commit to socially

A predicated knowledge system inherits the following commitments from the process-and-situated-learning lineage:

- **Categories track activities.** Per Whitehead and the gerund convention of the four-group register, the substrate's content is what the participating communities *do*, not what they *have*.
- **Identity is participation.** Per Whitehead's actual-occasions and the substrate's identity-by-relations discipline, an entity is the predications it participates in across the substrate's history.
- **Closure is mediation.** Per Peirce's Thirdness and Fitting's bilattice fixpoint, closure-rule application is the substrate's habit-formation.
- **Knowledge is community-bounded.** Per Lave-Wenger and the substrate's scope discipline, $t$-valued cells are $t$-valued *in a scope*; cross-scope reconciliation is an explicit substrate operation.
- **Epistemic standards differ across communities.** Per Knorr-Cetina, different closure-rule systems serve different epistemic cultures; the substrate carries the cultural variation as content rather than imposing one culture.
- **Thought styles change historically.** Per Fleck, scope-level closure-rule systems are versioned, and the substrate's history records the changes; what was a fact in scope $S$ at time $t$ may not be a fact in scope $S$ at time $t' > t$.
- **Facts are settled, not given.** Per the entire lineage, a $t$-valued cell is the *outcome* of the substrate's settling under the scope's closure rules; the substrate has no direct access to "what is the case" independent of the settlement that produced the cell.

The predicated knowledge system is, on this reading, what a process-aware and community-aware knowledge system looks like when it has been given a mathematical substrate. The process-philosophical and sociology-of-knowledge commitments were already present implicitly in any working knowledge system; the substrate is what makes them explicit, checkable, and federable across communities that adopt them.

The system's social legitimacy follows: a predicated knowledge system is legitimate to the extent that the participating communities accept the closure-rule system, the predicate vocabulary, and the scope discipline operating in their region of the substrate. Legitimacy is not asserted; it is settled, in exactly the sense the substrate settles its cells.
