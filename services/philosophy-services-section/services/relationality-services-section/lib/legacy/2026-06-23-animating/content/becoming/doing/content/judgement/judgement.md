# judgement

**Judgement** is the claim taken up and weighed: predicated, oriented, held up for assessment — but not yet settled to a verdict on the evidence.
It is what a [question](../../../../library/question/docs/question.md) becomes once it is actively taken up. *Judging* is the act; *judgement* is what that act holds in hand — a content made assessable, the weighing underway, the verdict not yet in.

## To judge is to predicate

In the tradition running through Kant, to judge is to *predicate* — to join a subject and a predicate and hold the join up for assessment.
Kant makes judgment (*Urteilskraft*) the power that brings a particular under a concept, the act in which a content first becomes the kind of thing that can be true or false at all.
Before judgement there is only a content raised; in judgement it is oriented — *this* is asserted of *that*, against *these* alternatives — so that it can be weighed.
Judgement lays out the claim's possible shapes; it does not yet choose among them.

A word of care, because "judgement" is smeared across two moments. **Judging-as-weighing** — the act of active consideration — is what this term names: the claim made assessable, still value-free. **Judgment-as-verdict** — the reached, settled ruling — is a different, later thing; it belongs with the settled order, and is exactly what [adjudication](../../../working/governing/library/adjudication/docs/adjudication.md) performs and what [knowledge](../../../working/governing/library/knowledge/docs/knowledge.md) holds.
The doxastic act of judgement is the weighing, not the verdict.

## What generates it

Judgement is generated by **judging**: taking the raised question and predicating it — orienting the content as a determinate claim, fixing what is being weighed against what, without yet fixing how it comes out.
This is the move, in the arc of inquiry, from a bare question to a *live possibility*: a claim whose shapes are laid out so that evidence can begin to bear on them.

## What it generates

Judgement generates [belief](../../../working/library/belief/docs/belief.md).
Once a claim has been oriented for assessment, evidence can weigh it — and the evidence-weighted held position that weighing yields is belief.
Judgement makes the claim assessable; belief is what assessment-on-evidence then holds.
