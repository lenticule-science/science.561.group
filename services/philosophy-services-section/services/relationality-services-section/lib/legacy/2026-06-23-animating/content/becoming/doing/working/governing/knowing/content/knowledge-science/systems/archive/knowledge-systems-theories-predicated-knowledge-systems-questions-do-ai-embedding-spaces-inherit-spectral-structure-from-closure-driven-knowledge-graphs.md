# Do AI embedding spaces inherit spectral structure from closure-driven knowledge graphs?

The source question is not merely whether closure-driven graphs have spectral invariants. The source question is whether human-machine knowledge infrastructures generate spectral structures that are later inherited by artificial intelligence embedding spaces.

## The question

When a knowledge graph grows through repeated human edits, ontology constraints, and machine inference, does the resulting closure-driven structure leave a spectral signature that later appears in learned embedding spaces?

Equivalently: are some features of artificial intelligence representation geometry downstream of the recurrence dynamics of human knowledge systems?

The source triage material proposes a semantic-spectral alignment conjecture: linked-data graph structure, recurrence dynamics of human-machine co-evolution, and artificial intelligence embedding spaces may be governed by shared spectral attractors. The predicated-knowledge-systems version should retain that real question while breaking it into testable substrate-level components.

## Predication restatement

A Semantic Web graph can be read as a finite shadow of a predicated knowledge system: entities, predicates, objects, closure rules, and settling steps. Its Boolean adjacency matrix is only one projection of that richer substrate. A learned embedding space is another projection, produced by training processes that may or may not preserve the relevant structure.

The spectral-inheritance question therefore becomes:

1. What operator is actually native to the predication substrate?
2. Which graph projection produces the spectrum under study?
3. What information is lost when bilattice-valued predication is collapsed to Boolean adjacency?
4. Which recurrence dynamics are produced by human editing, ontology closure, and machine inference?
5. Under what conditions would a learning system inherit any of those spectral features?

## Answer-enabling questions

Do closure-driven predication graphs exhibit stable spectral invariants? This is the first technical prerequisite. If repeated closure does not produce stable operator shadows, there is nothing stable for an embedding space to inherit.

Which operator should be studied? Candidate operators include predicate-indexed adjacency operators, collapsed adjacency matrices, graph Laplacians, normalized Laplacians, incidence operators, cohomological boundary operators, bilattice-valued operators, and d-frame-valued shadows. A Boolean adjacency operator may be useful but lossy.

Does ontology closure behave like a nonnegative propagation operator? Some Semantic Web rules look monotone, inflationary, and idempotent, but Perron-Frobenius analysis requires more than a suggestive analogy. The operator, state space, and update dynamics have to be specified.

Do human edits introduce recurrence? The source material proposes that human-machine co-editing may create second-order recurrence. This should be treated as a hypothesis about actual revision histories, not assumed from the form of the model.

Can embeddings inherit graph spectra? Some embedding methods approximate spectral filtering over co-occurrence, adjacency, or Laplacian-like operators. But a general large language model embedding space is not automatically a spectral image of a linked-data graph. The inheritance path has to be demonstrated through training data, preprocessing, model class, and measurable alignment.

## What would resolution require

A minimal resolution would choose one finite knowledge graph, one closure-rule system, one sequence of graph states, and at least two operator shadows: a Boolean graph operator and a predication-aware operator. It would then compare their spectral behavior across closure steps and identify what each operator preserves and erases.

A stronger resolution would compare those spectra with embeddings trained directly on the graph or on text generated from the graph. The comparison should use explicit metrics such as principal angles, eigenvector alignment, spectral gap stability, neighborhood preservation, or cohomological diagnostics.

A critical resolution would determine whether spectral inheritance is a real mechanism, a useful approximation for some engineered knowledge systems, or an overextension of graph-spectral language into artificial intelligence geometry.

## What this question should not collapse into

This question should not be reduced to "do closure-driven graphs have spectral invariants?" That is only the first answer-enabling question.

It should also not inherit the strongest claims of the triage source without proof. The claims about Perron-Frobenius convergence, Fibonacci-like recurrence, and embedding alignment should be treated as conjectural until the substrate, operator, recurrence, and inheritance path are specified.
