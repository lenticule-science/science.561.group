The companion canonical Du Bois work to *The Souls of Black Folk* (Du Bois 1903): a historical sociology of the Reconstruction era that, against the dominant Dunning-school historiography, reads emancipated Black workers as the central political agent of the period and reads the failure of Reconstruction as the structural foundation of subsequent American racial capitalism.
The book is one of the load-bearing prior works for the [`black-radical-tradition`](theories/critical-theory/theories/black-radical-tradition/) subdomain — Cedric Robinson's account of racial capitalism, the analytical work on the post-Reconstruction wage and incarceration regime that runs through Davis (Davis 2003) and Gilmore (Gilmore 2007), and the abolitionist reading of the present all draw on its argument.
