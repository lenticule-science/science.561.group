**Destabilization** is what [saturation](saturation.md) induces.
The social settling loses the prior equilibrium that had been absorbing its constitutive contradictions; the closure rules that previously routed contradiction into personal affect, deferral, or institutional management stop converging; what had been routine — the daily reproduction of subjects, infrastructures, and expectations — begins to register continuously as crisis.
Destabilization is not an event.
It is the ambient condition in which events arrive.

In predication-categories terms: a settling $\mathcal{S}$ is *destabilized* when its closure rules fail to drive the overdetermined fiber density back below the saturation threshold.
The settling continues to iterate but each iteration produces net overdetermination rather than net resolution.
Feedback layers that previously dampened perturbations begin amplifying them: the interpretive layer generates more contradictions than it absorbs; the affective-governance layer formats crisis faster than subjects can metabolize it; the institutional layer produces management instruments whose side effects exceed their corrections.

## Ambient versus punctual

Destabilization must be distinguished from *crisis* in the event-shaped sense.
A crisis is a moment — a market collapse, a pandemic onset, a political rupture — that can in principle be resolved by institutional response.
Destabilization is the ambient condition that makes such moments arrive as unresolvable.
The same event arriving in a non-destabilized settling is metabolized as a perturbation; arriving in a destabilized settling, it propagates.
The framework's claim is that much of what appears as discrete crisis in contemporary discourse is better read as destabilization-made-legible: the event is a probe that reveals the destabilized settling's inability to restabilize.

This distinction carries a formal analog.
In ecological regime theory (Holling 1973), a system in a stable basin of attraction returns to equilibrium after perturbation.
A destabilized system has left its basin; perturbations no longer return it but instead shift it further.
The sociological translation is exact: a destabilized social settling no longer absorbs perturbations through closure — it translates them into further overdetermination.

## The system becoming visible to itself

Under non-destabilized conditions, the social settling's operation is taken for granted by its participants.
They follow the genre's arcs, apply the feeling rules, and the everyday reproduction of structure proceeds invisibly.
Under destabilization, the operation becomes perceptible: the genre arcs fail to resolve; the feeling rules contradict each other; the institutional promises diverge from institutional deliveries.
Subjects notice that they are in a system — that the system is not delivering — without yet having vocabulary for what they are noticing.

This visibility does not produce resolution.
In [cybernetic postliberalism](../../cybernetic-postliberalism/), the system's perception of its own destabilization is itself metabolized: converted into content, therapeutic engagement, political commentary, and interpretive labor that feeds the platform's engagement metrics.
[Coherent confusion](coherent-confusion.md) is the governance mode that instrumentalizes this visibility: the subjects are confused, they know they are confused, and their interpretive labor under confusion is what sustains system function.

## Relation to liberalism-legitimacy-crisis

The [liberalism-legitimacy-crisis](../objects/liberalism-legitimacy-crisis.md) is the named object-instance of destabilization in the contemporary late-liberal formation: the specific settling-configuration that arises when liberalism's constitutive contradictions can no longer be routed into institutional management.
Destabilization is the underlying process; the liberalism-legitimacy-crisis is what that process looks like instantiated in the predicate fibers of the late-liberal social base.
