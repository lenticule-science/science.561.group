# Language and Literature

Language is the medium through which knowledge is expressed, stored, and transmitted. Literature is the art that pushes language to its limits — fiction, poetry, and narrative that explore what it means to be alive.

This root holds two domains:

**Language** covers linguistics in its formal and social dimensions: phonology, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, sociolinguistics, and the political history of languages — including language death, revitalization, and the relationship between language and power.

**Writing** covers writing as a practice and craft: the history of writing systems, rhetoric, composition, and the techniques of non-fiction and scholarly prose. The focus is on writing as a skill and a discipline, not on literature as an object of study.

Literature as a body of texts — fiction, poetry, drama — is collected under the relevant cultural and linguistic traditions where those traditions have enough material to warrant a home.
