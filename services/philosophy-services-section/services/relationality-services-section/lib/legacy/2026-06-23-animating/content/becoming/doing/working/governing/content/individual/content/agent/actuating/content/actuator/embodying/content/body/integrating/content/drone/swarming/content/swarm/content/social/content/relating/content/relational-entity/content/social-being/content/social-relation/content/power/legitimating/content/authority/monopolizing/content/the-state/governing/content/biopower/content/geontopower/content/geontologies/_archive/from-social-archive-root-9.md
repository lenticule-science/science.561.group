Audience: readers who have completed the [overview](./overview.md).

Learning goal: explain how [geontologies](../terms/geontologies.md) and [geontopower](../terms/geontopower.md) operate through three figures and how they extend [biopolitics](../../biopolitics/terms/biopolitics.md).

[Povinelli](../../../../../../../../references/people/elizabeth-povinelli.md) argues that [late liberal](../terms/late-liberalism.md) governance manages the Life/Nonlife boundary through three figures:

- **The Desert**: Nonlife threatening to overwhelm Life.
  This is the figure of ecological catastrophe, resource exhaustion, and extinction — the fear that the inert world will reclaim the living one.
  Climate [discourse](../../foucauldian/terms/discourse.md) often operates through this figure.
- **The Animist**: the figure that refuses the Life/Nonlife distinction altogether.
  Indigenous ontologies that recognize rivers, rocks, and ancestors as alive and agentive represent the Animist — not as a "belief" to be tolerated but as an ontological challenge to the framework that makes [extractivism](../../../../../../../science/theories/ecology/disciplines/climate/terms/extractivism.md) possible.
- **The Virus**: the figure that crosses the boundary, neither fully alive nor fully inert.
  The Virus disrupts classification from within — it is the uncanny entity that the Life/Nonlife distinction cannot accommodate.

[Geontopower](../terms/geontopower.md) is the form of power that manages these threats to the boundary.
It operates not through dramatic acts of sovereignty but through the quiet maintenance of [ontological](../../../../../../../science/theories/information/terms/ontology.md) categories — the classifications embedded in [property](../../anarchism/terms/property.md) law, environmental regulation, and scientific taxonomy.

Check for understanding: How does the figure of the Animist challenge geontological governance?
