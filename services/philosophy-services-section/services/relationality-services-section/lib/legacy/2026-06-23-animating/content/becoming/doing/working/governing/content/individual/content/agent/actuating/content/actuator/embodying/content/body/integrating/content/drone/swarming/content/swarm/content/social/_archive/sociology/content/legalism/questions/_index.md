Open work for legalism at the subdomain level.
School-specific questions live in each school's own subdomain.
What is held here are the questions whose scope crosses schools.

## Open structural questions

- **What is the relation between the sociology of law (this subdomain) and the abolitionist analysis of the carceral state (in [`abolition/`](../../critical-theory/theories/abolition/))?** The carceral state is, operationally, the largest single thing American law does; a sociology of law that does not centrally engage the abolitionist analysis is partial.
- **How should the subdomain read the contemporary literature on law and racial capitalism?** Crenshaw, Bell, Spade, Harris's *Whiteness as Property*, the broader critical-race-theoretical tradition — none of these have their own subdomain inside `legalism/` and the question of where they should live (here? under `critical-theory/`? as their own subdomain?) is open.
- **What are the discipline-wide structural commitments of the [`american-law/`](theories/american-law/) subdomain that should be lifted to legalism level?** Many of the analytical moves in the American-law texts (adversarial structure, precedent-and-propagation, evidence-as-translation) are not specifically about American law; they are general moves about how common-law systems work.
  The structural question is which to keep at the American-law level and which to lift.

## Charter-alignment open work

- The relation to Indigenous legal orders — and to the analysis of how settler-colonial legal systems treat Indigenous law — is currently absent from the subdomain.
  The forward work is registered.
