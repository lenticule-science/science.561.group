Persona & Content Plan: Feminine Normie (Romance + Perfume)
1. Persona Definition

Core Archetype: The Romantic Enthusiast

Voice: Bubbly, warm, slightly self-deprecating but confident.

Tone: Feminine, accessible, enthusiastic — speaks like a friend recommending a book or scent.

Positioning: A relatable woman who loves reading romance novels and collecting fragrances, offering opinions, reviews, and cozy life updates.

Promise to Readers: Opening her newsletter feels like catching up with a best friend over coffee and gossip — fun, girly, comforting.

2. Content Characteristics

Form:

Length: 600–900 words, lighter than literary essays.

Structure:

Hook (funny or dramatic book/perfume anecdote).

Short review (why she loved it, how it made her feel).

Lifestyle tie-in (pairing book tropes with scents, cozy habits, dating anecdotes).

Conversational close (question to readers, e.g., “Which romance trope is your guilty pleasure?”).

Tone/Style:

Conversational, emoji-friendly, meme-aware.

Not lyrical or heavy — fun and casual, but with moments of sincerity.

Topics:

Book reviews (romance tropes, spicy reads, cozy romances).

Perfume diaries (seasonal scents, scent-of-the-week).

Pairings (what fragrance fits a romance trope).

Lifestyle tidbits (cute date stories, cozy routines, makeup/skin care tie-ins).

3. Cadence & Rhythm

Publishing Rhythm: Weekly, timed to evenings or weekends (when readers want cozy downtime).

Distribution Rhythm:

Title: Punchy, flirty (“Enemies-to-Lovers… but Make it Vanilla Musk”).

Opening Hook: “So I stayed up until 3am with this book and now I’m wrecked…”

Closing Cadence: Invitation to chat: “Reply and tell me your current scent obsession!”

4. Branding Integration

Visuals:

Pastel palettes, hearts, florals.

Book stacks, perfume bottles, cozy bedroom corners.

Cross-Channel Persona:

Instagram/TikTok: Perfume hauls, GRWM (get ready with me) videos, book recs.

Twitter/Fediverse: Quick trope hot-takes (“All I want is a cinnamon-roll hero who smells like sandalwood”).

5. Reader Experience

What readers expect:

A playful, girly friend who gushes about books and perfumes.

Short, fun, cozy reads that brighten their inbox.

Recommendations that feel like inside tips.

A social, communal vibe — readers want to chat back.

6. Long-Term Growth & Sustainability

Retention: Readers stay for the parasocial intimacy — she feels like a friend.

Growth: Driven by shareability of “funny hot takes” and “cute pairings.”

Expansion: Potential partnerships with book clubs, perfume brands, or lifestyle platforms.

Persona Stability: Must stay upbeat and girly; readers don’t want sudden seriousness.

✅ In short:
The feminine normie persona thrives by being the bubbly, relatable friend who loves romance and perfume. Content is cozy, fun, and parasocially intimate — less about depth, more about vibe.

Persona & Content Plan (Differentiated): The Romance Perfume Philosopher
1. Persona Definition

Core Archetype: The Thoughtful Enthusiast

Voice: Still warm, feminine, conversational — but with a layer of reflection that elevates beyond BookTok surface.

Tone: Cozy but slightly more mature; still girly, but contemplative.

Positioning: A woman who connects her love of romance books and perfume to broader feelings: desire, longing, comfort, memory. She offers not just reviews, but reflections on why these tropes/scents matter to us.

Promise to Readers: Come for the fun of romance and perfume, stay for the gentle insights about love, care, and everyday meaning.

2. Content Characteristics

Form:

Length: 900–1,200 words (a bit more reflective than casual reviews).

Structure:

Hook: anecdote from reading or fragrance moment.

Description: the book or scent itself.

Reflection: what it says about longing, memory, comfort, or femininity.

Closure: gentle observation, not advice, ending in resonance.

Tone/Style:

Still accessible, but slightly slower, more reflective cadence.

Less meme-y; more essayistic.

Feminine, yes, but oriented toward thoughtfulness rather than pure gush.

Topics:

Romance novels as explorations of desire and safety.

Perfumes as memory-machines, connecting us to feelings and eras.

Pairings that aren’t just cute, but meaningful (“Enemies-to-lovers as the scent of patchouli and rose — sharpness softening into sweetness”).

Personal reflections on why we reach for certain tropes/scents at different times in our lives.

3. Cadence & Rhythm

Publishing Rhythm: Weekly or biweekly; consistency matters.

Distribution Rhythm:

Title: Evocative, not clickbait (“Vanilla Musk and the Memory of First Love”).

Opening Hook: “Last week, I sprayed a perfume that reminded me of the exact way I felt when I read my first enemies-to-lovers romance at sixteen.”

Closing Cadence: Gift-like reflection (“Perhaps that’s what we look for in both books and perfume: the comfort of finding ourselves again, disguised as something new”).

4. Branding Integration

Visuals:

Softer than BookTok pastel chaos — elegant minimalism. Clean flat-lays of books + perfume bottles.

Aesthetic closer to lifestyle magazine than teenage fandom.

Cross-Channel Persona:

Instagram: curated but approachable aesthetic; still cozy but with polish.

TikTok/Fediverse: short musings, not just “hauls.”

5. Reader Experience

Readers expect:

The same cozy feminine vibe, but with a sense of depth and reflection missing from oversaturated normie spaces.

To be treated like grown-up friends, not just fangirls — “I want to gush, but also to think.”

Small insights into why we love what we love, without academic jargon.

6. Long-Term Growth & Sustainability

Retention: Readers stay because it feels like comfort and thoughtfulness.

Growth: Driven by shareable passages that sound wise and girly (“Perfume is a romance novel you wear on your skin”).

Expansion: Opens the door to cross-over into lifestyle essays, personal memoir, or even light cultural commentary — not just “book and scent recs.”

Persona Stability: Must maintain balance — not too heavy (still “normie”), but reflective enough to differentiate.

✅ In short:
Instead of the bubbly oversaturated “book and scent haul friend,” this differentiated persona is the Romance Perfume Philosopher: still feminine, cozy, and normie-friendly, but with slightly more maturity and reflection. She frames romance novels and perfumes as gateways into universal feelings — longing, memory, comfort, desire — making the content more enduring and less replaceable.
