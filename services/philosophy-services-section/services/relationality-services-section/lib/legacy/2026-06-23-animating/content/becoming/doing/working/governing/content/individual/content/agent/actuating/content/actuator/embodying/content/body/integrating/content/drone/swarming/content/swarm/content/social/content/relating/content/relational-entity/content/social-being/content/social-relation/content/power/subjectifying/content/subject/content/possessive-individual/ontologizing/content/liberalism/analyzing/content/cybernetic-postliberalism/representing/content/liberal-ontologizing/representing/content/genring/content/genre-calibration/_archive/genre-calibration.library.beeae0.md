**Genre calibration** is the morphism that continuously and distributively tunes the affective-predicate fibers of a social settling so that structural conditions are experienced as personal narrative.
It is the operative mechanism by which [californication](californication.md) runs without a central coordinator: no institution prescribes which feelings subjects should have; the calibration emerges from the distributed feedback of social interaction, platform engagement, and interpersonal feeling-rule enforcement.

In predication-categories terms: genre calibration is an endomorphism $\gamma : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{S}$ on the social settling, operating specifically on the affect-predicate fibers.
For each subject $e$ and affect-predicate $P$, $\gamma$ adjusts $F(e)(P)$ toward the genre-canonical value $\hat{F}(P)$ by a step proportional to the social-feedback signal at time $t$.
The adjustment is distributed: it accumulates across all interpersonal interactions, platform engagements, and institutional encounters, without any single interaction determining the outcome.
Over time, the composition $\gamma^n$ converges the settling toward the genre-canonical fixed point.

## The Berlant–Ahmed–Wiener synthesis

Genre calibration synthesizes three theoretical contributions that each describe part of the mechanism.

**Berlant: genre as structure of anticipation.** Berlant's use of genre in *Cruel Optimism* (2011) designates not a literary category but a structure of expectation: a set of tacit rules about how situations unfold, what counts as crisis, what counts as resolution, and which feelings belong at each stage.
Genre does not interpret events; it constitutes what events *are*.
By the time a subject is interpreting a job loss, the genre has already determined which aspects are visible (personal failure or personal growth), which are background (structural conditions), and what resolution looks like.
The genre is the prior calibration that makes the event legible at all.

**Ahmed: affects circulate, stick, and orient.** Ahmed's account in *The Cultural Politics of Emotion* (2004) adds the affective mechanism: emotions are not in subjects or objects but emerge through circulation between them, and certain objects become *sticky* — accumulating affective value through their repeated movement in discourse.
The "pivot" genre for job loss works because resilience and growth-mindedness have become sticky with positive affect through their circulation across wellness media, LinkedIn culture, and therapeutic discourse; grief and structural analysis have become sticky with negative affect (the killjoy's response).
Genre calibration operates through these stickiness gradients: subjects orient toward genre-compliant affects because those affects are where the positive-affect accumulation has been concentrated.

**Wiener: feedback without central control.** Wiener's cybernetics (1948) provides the formal substrate: the calibration is self-sustaining because it is a feedback process.
When a subject narrates a job loss as a pivot and receives social reinforcement (likes, supportive comments, professional opportunities), the genre is confirmed — the closure rule has been applied, the fiber has moved toward $\hat{F}$.
When a subject narrates the same event as structural failure and receives discomfort or the suggestion to "focus on what you can control," the deviation is dampened.
No coordinator is required; the correction is distributed across every interaction, every platform algorithm, every interpersonal feeling-rule enforcement.

## Hochschild's feeling rules as fiber-update operator

Hochschild (1983) named the institutional prescription of feeling as *feeling rules*: the social norms governing which emotions are appropriate in which contexts.
Under genre calibration, feeling rules are the specific fiber-update operators $\delta(e, P, t)$ that genre calibration applies at each social encounter.
The flight attendant is required not merely to perform warmth but to cultivate it; the platform user is not merely encouraged to be resilient but to desire the growth narrative.
The feeling rule is the local form the calibration takes in a specific institutional context; genre calibration is the global morphism that these local operators compose into.

## Platform acceleration

[Platformal liberalism](../objects/platformal-liberalism.md) accelerates genre calibration by operating its feedback loops at engagement-metric speed.
Prior to platforms, feeling-rule enforcement was limited by the speed of interpersonal and broadcast interaction.
Platform-mediated engagement metrics apply the calibration continuously, at scale, in real time: an affect that produces poor engagement is dampened within seconds; an affect that produces strong engagement is amplified to additional subjects immediately.
Beer's (1979) nested feedback layers map precisely onto platform-mediated genre calibration: the wellness industry calibrates on one timescale (years of accumulated content), algorithmic recommendation on another (real-time engagement optimization), interpersonal platform interaction on another (immediate social cost of being a killjoy).
The layers do not coordinate; they converge.

## Genre calibration and structural invisibility

Genre calibration's most significant sociological effect is the systematic production of structural invisibility: structural conditions are continuously reattributed to the personal-narrative level, making their collective character experientially inaccessible.
A subject who has been genre-calibrated to experience housing unaffordability as a personal financial-management challenge is not ignorant of the structural facts; they may know them abstractly.
But the calibration has driven the affective-predicate fibers to values that make the structural level phenomenologically inaccessible as a *site of action*.
This is what Berlant names *cruel optimism*: attachment to genre forms that harm, sustained because the genre provides the only livable structure of feeling available. ---
