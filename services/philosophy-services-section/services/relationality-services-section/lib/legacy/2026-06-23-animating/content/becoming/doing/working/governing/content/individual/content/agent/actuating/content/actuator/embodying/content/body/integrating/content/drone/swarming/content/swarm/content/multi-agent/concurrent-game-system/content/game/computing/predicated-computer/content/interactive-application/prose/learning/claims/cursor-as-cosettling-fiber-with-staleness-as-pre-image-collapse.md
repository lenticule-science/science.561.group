# Cursor cells inhabit a closure-invariant sub-fiber of the document, with edit-driven coordinate-resolution collapse as the structural cleanup mechanism and idle timeout as a separate policy layer

> Filename retained for stable cross-references. The theorem's actual grounding is sub-fiber invariance under the document's closure operator, not the [cosettling](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/cosettling.md) object — see "Position" below for why cosettling does not in fact apply to cursor cells.

**Claim.** Let $D$ be a [document](teaching/explanations/document.md) under [collaborative editing](teaching/explanations/collaborative-editing.md) with closure operator $T_D$ on the document's [predication-fiber-algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md). Let $w_D = T_D^*(\bot)$ be the document's settled content (the [settling-quiescence](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-quiescence.md) value). Decompose the document's predication-fiber into two sub-fibers indexed by predicate kind:

- $A^{\text{edit}}_D$ — the *edit-content sub-fiber*, containing all cells whose predicate is an edit-content predicate (`text-span-content`, `span-author`, `span-position`, `next-span`, etc.). $T_D$'s rules consume and produce cells in $A^{\text{edit}}_D$.
- $A^{\text{cursor}}_D$ — the *cursor-presence sub-fiber*, containing cells whose predicate is `has-cursor-at`. No rule of $T_D$ has any cursor cell as an output, and no rule has a cursor cell as a relevant antecedent.

Let $C = \{\phi_{u_1}, \ldots, \phi_{u_k}\}$ be the live [cursor](teaching/explanations/cursor.md) cells from currently-connected users; each $\phi_u = (u, \mathrm{has\text{-}cursor\text{-}at}, \pi)$ lies in $A^{\text{cursor}}_D$ with $\pi$ a coordinate in $w_D$'s span-tree. Let $w_C := w_D \cup C$ be the *cursor-augmented document state* — what the substrate's display layer reads.

Then:

1. **The cursor sub-fiber is closure-invariant.** $T_D(w_D \cup S) = w_D \cup S$ for every $S \subseteq C$. In particular $T_D(w_C) = w_C$, so $w_C$ is closure-stable. Every $w_D \cup S$ is itself a quiescence point; no edit-rule fires on cursor cells.

2. **Multi-cursor display is sub-fiber join.** The substrate's display state $w_C$ is the join in $A^{\text{cursor}}_D$ of the individual cursor cells $\{\phi_u\}_{u}$ unioned with the edit-content quiescence $w_D$. Per [predication-fiber-algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md), this join exists componentwise: edit cells from $w_D$ plus cursor cells from $C$, with no cross-fiber interaction. All live cursors coexist in the substrate state without conflict because the cursor sub-fiber's join is just set-union of distinct $(u, \mathrm{has\text{-}cursor\text{-}at}, \pi)$ cells across users.

3. **Edit-driven structural cursor collapse.** When an edit advances $w_D \mapsto w_D' = T_D^*(w_D \cup \{e\})$, the substrate's coordinate-resolution rule for cursor cells re-evaluates each $\phi_u \in C$: if the position $\pi_u$ no longer designates a coordinate in $w_D'$'s span-tree (the span containing $\pi_u$ was deleted, or the offset within the span exceeds the new span's length), then $\phi_u$ is *coordinate-inadmissible* under $w_D'$ and the substrate drops $\phi_u$ from $C$, yielding $C' \subseteq C$. The new display state is $w_{C'} = w_D' \cup C'$. The collapse is structural: it follows from the substrate's well-formedness rule that cursor positions must resolve, applied to the new edit state, *independently* of any closure rule on cursor cells.

4. **Ephemeral persistence preserves replay.** Cursor cells may be marked ephemeral (no event-log entry) without compromising substrate-replayability of the document. Per [snapshot-replay-fidelity](../../../theorems/snapshot-replay-fidelity.md), replay reconstructs $w_D$ from edit-event log entries — the trajectory $\bot \subseteq v_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq w_D$ lies entirely in $A^{\text{edit}}_D$. Cursor cells, being in the disjoint $A^{\text{cursor}}_D$ sub-fiber and inert under $T_D$, contribute nothing to the trajectory; omitting them from the event log does not alter $w_D$'s reconstruction. Live cursors after replay are re-populated from currently-connected clients per [federation-convergence](../../../theorems/federation-convergence.md).

5. **Idle-timeout pruning is policy, not structure.** The substrate's discipline of dropping cursor $\phi_u$ when $u$'s `last-updated` ordinal is older than threshold $\theta$ is *not* derivable from $T_D$ alone (which contains no ordinal predicate) nor from the coordinate-resolution rule of claim 3 (which considers only span-tree validity, not time). It is a policy layered atop structural state, motivated by the substrate's cost of maintaining live cursors for users whose replicas have gone silent. The policy is a heuristic upper-bound on uncertainty: it may drop a cursor whose position would still resolve in $w_D$ (and which would *not* be structurally collapsed by claim 3), accepting a false-positive rate to bound substrate cost. A platform that wished to make timeout-cleanup structural would augment $T_D$ with an ordinal-keyed cursor-staleness rule, lifting the policy into a closure rule; the present subtheory does neither.

This grounds [cursor](teaching/explanations/cursor.md)'s "ephemeral, high-frequency, optionally-persistent" character in the math parent's predication-fiber-algebra (sub-fiber decomposition, closure invariance, settling quiescence) — *with* an explicit policy/structure boundary that prior framings collapsed.

## Why this holds

**Sub-fiber decomposition is well-defined.** Per [predication-fiber-algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md), the fiber over a base $B$ at a given predicate is the bilattice of cells with that predicate. The document's full fiber is the product over predicates of the per-predicate fibers; sub-fiber decomposition by predicate-set is the projection onto the chosen sub-product. Decomposing into edit-content predicates and the cursor predicate gives two disjoint sub-fibers whose product is the full fiber.

**Closure invariance.** A predication-rule of $T_D$ specifies antecedent cells (in some sub-fiber) and a consequent cell (in some sub-fiber). The collaborative-editing rule set for documents — edit admission, span-merge, span-split, ordering-by-fractional-index, etc. — has antecedents and consequents entirely within $A^{\text{edit}}_D$. No rule has the `has-cursor-at` predicate as an antecedent or as a consequent. Hence for any state $v$ with edit-content $v^{\text{edit}}$ and cursor-content $v^{\text{cursor}}$, $T_D(v) = (T_D|_{A^{\text{edit}}_D}(v^{\text{edit}})) \cup v^{\text{cursor}}$ — the closure operator acts only on the edit sub-fiber and leaves the cursor sub-fiber untouched. Setting $v = w_D \cup S$ with $w_D$ closure-stable: $T_D(w_D \cup S) = w_D \cup S$, establishing claim 1.

**Sub-fiber join.** Per [predication-fiber-algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md), the cursor sub-fiber's join over users is set-union of distinct $(u, \mathrm{has\text{-}cursor\text{-}at}, \pi)$ cells (each user's cursor is a distinct cell because of the user-coordinate). The display state is the join across the full fiber: edit-content from $w_D$ plus cursor-content from $\bigsqcup_u \{\phi_u\}$. Because the two sub-fibers are disjoint as predicate-spaces, the join is a clean Cartesian-product join — no cross-fiber compatibility constraint, no $\top$-witness possible from cursors-vs-edits, no inconsistency. This establishes claim 2.

**Edit-driven coordinate-resolution collapse.** Per [cursor](teaching/explanations/cursor.md), a cursor's position $\pi$ is `(span-id, character-offset)` or a fractional-index, both interpretable only against a span-tree. The substrate's well-formedness rule for cursor cells requires $\pi$ to designate a valid coordinate in the *current* edit state $w_D'$. After an edit, the substrate re-evaluates this rule for each live cursor; cursors whose position no longer resolves are dropped. This rule is part of the substrate's display-layer correctness discipline (a cursor pointing nowhere has no rendering); it is independent of $T_D$, which doesn't manipulate cursor cells. The rule's application is deterministic given $w_D'$ and the current $C$, establishing claim 3.

The key qualification: "no longer resolves" is the only structural collapse condition. A cursor at position $\pi$ in a span that the edit *modified but did not delete* still resolves (the position translates to the new offset under the standard fractional-index displacement); only cursors whose target span was destroyed or shrunk past their offset collapse.

**Replay fidelity.** The trajectory $\bot \subseteq v_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq w_D$ of the document's forward closure consumes only edit-rule applications, all firing in $A^{\text{edit}}_D$. The trajectory's intermediate states $v_i$ contain only edit cells. Cursor cells are admitted externally (by client cursor-update operations) and persist in $A^{\text{cursor}}_D$ but contribute nothing to the trajectory's forward arrows. Per [snapshot-replay-fidelity](../../../theorems/snapshot-replay-fidelity.md), replay applies the edit-event log to reconstruct $v_i$ in sequence and recover $w_D$. Cursor cells may be omitted from the log without altering $w_D$'s reconstruction; the recovered document state is $w_D$ on the edit sub-fiber, with the cursor sub-fiber empty. Live clients re-populate cursors on connection per [federation-convergence](../../../theorems/federation-convergence.md). This establishes claim 4.

**Idle-timeout policy.** A cursor $\phi_u$ whose user $u$ has been disconnected for time $\theta$ may still satisfy the coordinate-resolution rule (its $\pi$ may still designate a valid coordinate in the current $w_D$). The substrate's cost of maintaining $\phi_u$'s display across federation is non-zero (each connected client renders the cursor in its UI; replicas of the document pin the cursor cell in memory). When $u$ is silent, the substrate cannot determine cheaply whether $u$ has merely paused, has lost network connectivity, or has abandoned the session. The substrate's $\theta$-timeout discipline is a *cost-bounded heuristic*: drop the cursor after $\theta$ to bound display-side cost, accepting that some users who would have returned within $\theta$ lose their cursor visibility briefly until they re-emit a position update.

The policy's relationship to claim 3's structural collapse: timeouts may drop cursors that would *not* structurally collapse (the position still resolves), and structural collapse may drop cursors before $\theta$ has elapsed (the user is active but their target span was edited away). The two cleanup mechanisms are *independent*; the substrate runs both. Neither subsumes the other. This establishes claim 5 honestly.

## Engineering consequence

- **Cursor throttling is mathematically licensed.** A client batching cursor updates every $N$ ms admits cursor cells at coarser cadence than keystrokes. Per claim 1, batched cursor cells are inert under $T_D$; missing intermediate positions don't affect substrate-document state. Throttling discards cursor history without loss to document content.
- **Cursor ephemerality preserves [snapshot-replay-fidelity](../../../theorems/snapshot-replay-fidelity.md).** Per claim 4, a snapshot of the edit sub-fiber is byte-identical regardless of whether cursor cells are persisted. Ephemeral cursors are a *correct optimization*, not a fidelity compromise.
- **Edit-driven cursor cleanup is structural and predictable.** Per claim 3, when an edit removes or shrinks a span containing a cursor's position, the cursor's collapse follows from the substrate's coordinate-resolution rule applied to the new state. The platform's "the user's cursor disappears when their text was deleted" semantics is grounded in substrate well-formedness, not heuristic.
- **Idle-timeout cleanup is a policy decision the platform must declare.** Per claim 5, the $\theta$-timeout is *not* implied by closure structure or coordinate resolution; it is the substrate's chosen cost-bounded heuristic. Per [self-representation](../../teaching/explanations/self-representation.md), the platform should expose $\theta$ as queryable substrate content so users and auditors can reason about its behavior.
- **Multi-cursor display has no cross-cursor inconsistency.** Per claim 2, the cursor sub-fiber's join is union; no two cursors can produce a $\top$-witness against each other (distinct users have distinct cells). The substrate displays all live cursors structurally; collapsing to a single cursor is information loss with no consistency justification.
- **Cursor and edit cells live in one fiber algebra but disjoint sub-fibers.** Per [predication-fiber-algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md), the document's value-domain factors as the product of edit-fiber and cursor-fiber. Edit cells grow monotonically per [settling-monotonicity](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/settling-monotonicity.md); cursor cells are non-monotone (admitted and dropped per the live-presence discipline). One fiber, two sub-fibers, two regimes; the math parent's fiber-algebra accommodates both without modification.

## What the theorem rules out

- **Cursor cells in the document's canonical form.** Per [document-canonicalization-via-settling-quiescence](document-canonicalization-via-settling-quiescence.md), the canonical form is the byte-encoding of $w_D$ on the edit sub-fiber. Cursor cells are in $A^{\text{cursor}}_D$, hence not in the edit-fiber canonical form. A platform including cursor positions in the document's canonical hash makes the hash non-stable under cursor movement — violating [hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form](../../../theorems/hash-injectivity-under-canonical-form.md).
- **Stale-cursor "extrapolation" presented as recovery.** Per claim 3, structurally collapsed cursors do not refine into nearby surviving positions. A platform offering "cursor recovery" by interpolating to a nearby position is producing a *new* cursor cell from heuristic, not re-admitting the dropped one. Permissible as a UX feature; misrepresented if framed as substrate-state preservation.
- **Cursor persistence as a replay-fidelity requirement.** Per claim 4, treating cursor cells as required for replay conflates live-state with substrate content. A platform requiring cursor-stream persistence has elevated a UX preference into a fidelity requirement.
- **Single-cursor-per-document semantics.** Per claim 2, the sub-fiber's join supports multi-cursor display structurally. A platform displaying only the most-recent cursor (last-mover-wins) loses this without consistency justification.
- **Treating timeout-cleanup as structural.** Per claim 5, a platform documenting its $\theta$-timeout as if it were a closure-rule consequence misrepresents the policy/structure boundary. Auditors are then unable to determine whether the timeout is an implementation choice or a substrate invariant — an interoperability hazard for federated platforms.
- **Closure rules with cursor antecedents.** A rule of $T_D$ that fires based on cursor presence (e.g., "if a user's cursor is at position $\pi$ for $T$ seconds, auto-insert content") would break sub-fiber invariance, coupling the cursor sub-fiber to the edit sub-fiber. Such a rule is admissible only if the platform accepts that cursor cells participate in the edit sub-fiber's monotonicity discipline (per claim 1's converse: rules that consume cursors make them no longer inert).

## What the theorem requires

- The document's value-domain is a distributive bilattice per [predication-fiber-algebra](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fiber-algebra.md).
- The closure operator $T_D$ is the standard collaborative-editing closure; no rule has cursor cells as antecedents or consequents.
- Cursor cells use a predicate (`has-cursor-at` or equivalent) distinct from any edit-content predicate.
- The substrate's display layer reads the union $w_C = w_D \cup C$; the canonical-hash and replay layers read $w_D$ alone.
- The substrate enforces a coordinate-resolution well-formedness rule on cursor cells against the current $w_D$, dropping unresolvable cursors.
- The substrate's $\theta$-timeout policy, if used, is declared as policy per [self-representation](../../teaching/explanations/self-representation.md) — not asserted as a closure-rule consequence and not assumed to coincide with structural collapse.

## Position

This theorem is the third structural pillar of the subtheory's collaborative-document story:

- [collaborative-edit-convergence](collaborative-edit-convergence.md) — *forward dynamics on the edit sub-fiber*: edits converge under $T_D$ across users.
- [document-canonicalization-via-settling-quiescence](document-canonicalization-via-settling-quiescence.md) — *fixed-point semantics on the edit sub-fiber*: canonical form exists exactly at $w_D = T_D^*(\bot)$.
- This theorem — *cursor sub-fiber invariance, edit-driven structural cleanup, and explicit policy/structure separation*: the cursor sub-fiber is closure-invariant; cursor cleanup has two independent mechanisms (structural coordinate-resolution collapse on edits, and policy-driven idle timeout); the two are not equivalent and the platform must declare both.

**On the original cosettling framing.** This theorem's filename and earlier prose suggested cursor cells live in the [cosettling](../../../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/cosettling.md) $T_D^{-1}(w_C)$ of the cursor-augmented state. That framing does not survive scrutiny: because $T_D$ has no rule producing cursor cells, the single-step cosettling of $w_C$ is degenerately $\{w_C\}$, and the iterated cosettling is also $\{w_C\}$ (no closure step ever adds cursor cells). The cosettling object does not in fact contain "off-trajectory pre-images with partial cursor extensions"; that intuition was overreach. The honest grounding for cursor presence in the math parent is sub-fiber invariance (this theorem) — a distinct math fact than cosettling, and one that does apply.

The cosettling object remains relevant to the subtheory for *other* phenomena: any predicate whose cells *are* derived by closure rules but whose intermediate "potentially-precursor" states the substrate wants to enumerate (e.g., undo-history, partial-form-completion-recovery, branched-edit-merge-candidates) is genuinely cosettling content. The interactive-application-platform should anticipate cosettling-grounded theorems for those phenomena specifically, distinct from the present sub-fiber-invariance grounding for cursors.

This also opens cross-subtheory connections: any subtheory with "live presence state" sharing the *inert externally-admitted* character of cursors (online indicators, typing indicators, focus state, viewport-position state) can be grounded analogously by exhibiting a closure-invariant sub-fiber for the presence predicates, with structural and policy cleanup mechanisms made explicit. The subtheory should anticipate analogous theorems for typing-indicators on documents, focus-state on forms, and active-window state on user-sessions.
