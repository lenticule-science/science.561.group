# Application sublocale

A view of a [predicated computer](predicated-computer.md) — a [scope](teaching/explanations/scope.md) of the substrate equipped with a particular *predicate vocabulary*, a particular *closure-rule subset*, and a particular *nucleus* that projects out the application's visible region of the predication graph and admits new content only when it passes the application's closure-rule subset. Structurally, an application sublocale is a sub-[predication fibration](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-fibration.md) $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{app}} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{E}$ where the sub-base is a sub-[predicate category](../../../../../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predicate-category.md) $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{app}} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}$ defined by the application's predicate vocabulary, with a sub-fibration's closure machinery restricted to the application's rule subset.

The application sublocale is the engineering specialization of [scope-as-region](teaching/explanations/scope.md) for the case where the scope corresponds to a coherent application — a MUD, an ERP, a publishing system, a knowledge base. Two applications running on the same predicated computer with disjoint predicate vocabularies do not see each other's predications: they share the substrate's storage, the [event log](event-log.md), and the operator vocabulary, but their nuclei project out non-overlapping sections of the predication graph.

Properties of application sublocales:

- **Sublocales compose.** Per [closure-conditions-composability](../theorems/closure-conditions-composability.md), the intersection of two application nuclei is itself a nucleus, defining a third application sublocale whose vocabulary is the intersection of the two component vocabularies and whose closure rules are the rules common to both subsets. This composition is the engineering content of "applications can be combined" — the combined application is itself a sublocale, not a glued-together pair of applications.
- **Sublocale closure inherits.** A sublocale's closure machinery is the restriction-and-projection of the substrate's full closure machinery. A sublocale's convergence behavior is no harder than the substrate's — restricting to a smaller vocabulary cannot introduce divergence the substrate did not already have, though it can simplify cases that the full substrate has not yet resolved.
- **Sublocale boundaries are configuration, not architecture.** A sublocale's specification — its predicate vocabulary, its closure-rule subset, its nucleus — is itself a substrate entity, per [self-representation](teaching/explanations/self-representation.md). Editing the specification adds a predicate to the sublocale, or removes a closure rule, or revises the nucleus's projection rule. The substrate reads the new specification and the application now operates on a different region; no deployment step is required.
- **Sublocales may be writable or read-only.** A sublocale's nucleus may admit substrate edits (writable: insertions, retractions, and closure-rule applications within the sublocale produce new substrate content) or only project a view of substrate content (read-only: queries return predications, but the sublocale rejects edits). The writability discipline is a sublocale property, not a substrate property; the same predication graph may be writable through one sublocale and read-only through another.
- **Projection sublocales are reversible.** When a sublocale's nucleus is a projection — its visible content is derived from a base sublocale's content via a deterministic query rule — edits within the projection sublocale can be propagated back to the base by inverting the projection. The reversibility is an engineering property of the projection rule.

An application sublocale is fully specified by its (vocabulary, rule-subset, nucleus) triple. Two applications with the same triple are operationally indistinguishable; an application's identity is its triple, not its name or its location. When the triple has additionally satisfied all seven [closure conditions](teaching/explanations/closure-conditions.md) within its scope, the application sublocale is a [locale](locale.md) — a complete predicated computer at the application's scope.

Application sublocales are the substrate-level realization of "code reuse is structural, not architectural": two applications that share a predicate vocabulary share the substrate's content for those predicates automatically. There is no API surface to maintain between them; the predication graph is the surface, and the nuclei are the views.
