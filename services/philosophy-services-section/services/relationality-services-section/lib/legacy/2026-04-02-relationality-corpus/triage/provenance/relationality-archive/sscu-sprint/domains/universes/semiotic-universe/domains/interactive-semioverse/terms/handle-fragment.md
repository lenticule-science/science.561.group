The handle fragment $F_{\tau,X}$ of a handle $\tau$ in state $X$ is the least [fragment](../../semiotic-universe/terms/fragment.md) containing the semantic interpretations of $\tau$'s [interaction surface](./interaction-surface.md): $F_{\tau,X}$ = the least fragment containing $\{\llbracket t\rrbracket : t \in \mathrm{Ix}_X(\tau)\}$. It is the smallest modal–trace [Heyting](../../../../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md) subalgebra of $H$ generated by the handle's interaction denotations — the bounded interpretive context generated by a handle's [semiotic](../../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) encounters.

The handle fragment captures everything that can be inferred locally from the handle's interactions, before applying the full [closure operator](../../../../teaching/explanations/closure-operator/index.md). It is monotone in the [interaction surface](./interaction-surface.md): adding [interaction terms](./interaction-term.md) can only enlarge the handle fragment. The handle fragment sits inside H and is subject to all the fragment-level reasoning tools -- fragmentwise equality, hereditary extensionality, and the [sheaf condition](../../../../disciplines/higher-topos-theory/terms/sheaf-condition.md).

Closing the handle fragment under the state's operators produces the [footprint](./footprint.md): Foot_X(tau) = S_X(F_{tau,X}). The handle fragment is the raw local data; the [footprint](./footprint.md) is what remains after closure stabilizes it. The [footprint](./footprint.md) is always bounded by the core semioverse state X*: nothing escapes the [least fixed point](../../../../teaching/explanations/fixed-point/index.md).
