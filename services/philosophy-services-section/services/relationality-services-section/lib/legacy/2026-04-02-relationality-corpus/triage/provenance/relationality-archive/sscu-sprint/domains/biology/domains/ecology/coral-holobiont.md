## Formal definition

A **CoralHolobiont** is a triple $(C, t, \ell)$:

$$(C \in \mathcal{R}_t,\; t \in T,\; \ell \in \mathrm{Locale})$$

where:
- $\mathcal{R}_t$ is the **reef community fiber** at history $t$ — the complete Heyting algebra of reef state propositions at $t$; elements of $\mathcal{R}_t$ are statements about the coral cover, symbiont clade composition, calcification rate, and thermal stress profile of the reef community over locale $\ell$ at history $t$; the fiber inherits its Heyting algebra structure from $H_t$
- $t \in T$ is the **analysis history** — position in the history monoid at which the reef state is evaluated; each step $s \in \Sigma$ advances the reef through one thermal forcing cycle (one bleaching-season exposure, one recovery interval, one symbiont-shuffling event); coral bleaching events and symbiont clade transitions are specific step atoms in $\Sigma$
- $\ell$ is the **locale** — the spatial domain over which the reef community is sampled; the locale determines sea surface temperature regime, thermal anomaly frequency, aragonite saturation state, and the regional symbiont clade pool

**The holobiont nuclear pair.** The fiber $H_t$ at history $t$ carries the two canonical nuclei of the relational hyperverse, each corresponding to one direction of holobiont coupling. Let $\Sigma$ be the step monoid for the reef site $(T, J)$. Each step $s \in \Sigma$ represents one thermal stress exposure event — one period of positive sea surface temperature anomaly above the bleaching threshold. The commutation condition $s \perp t$ means step $s$ can occur independently of the current history $t$ (i.e., the thermal stress type $s$ is not precluded by the current thermal state — a second independent bleaching pulse is independent iff the first pulse has not already expelled all Symbiodiniaceae). The one-step extension morphism $i_{s,t} : t \to s \star t$ is the history that extends $t$ by one instance of thermal stress $s$; the restriction map $H(i_{s,t}) : H_{s \star t} \to H_t$ sends a post-stress reef state back to the pre-stress state.

**RelationalHistoryFiberSaturatingNucleus** ([math](../math/relational-history-fiber-saturating-nucleus.md)), applied to $C \in H_t$:

$$\sigma_t(C) \;=\; \bigvee\bigl\{D \in H_t \;\big|\; H(i_{t_0,t})(D) \leq H(i_{t_0,t})(C) \;\text{for all } t_0 < t\bigr\}$$

where $H(i_{t_0,t}) : H_t \to H_{t_0}$ is the restriction map projecting the current reef state back to the prior history $t_0$. Ecologically: $H(i_{t_0,t})(C)$ is the **Degree Heating Weeks record** that state $C$ carries — the accumulated thermal stress history that the reef's current condition reflects. DHW is the restriction profile quantity: $\mathrm{DHW}_t = \int_{t_0}^t \max(0, \mathrm{SST}_{t'} - T_{\mathrm{bleach}}) \, dt'$, where $T_{\mathrm{bleach}}$ is the maximum monthly mean temperature plus 1°C. The restriction profile class of $C$ is the set of all reef states with at most $C$'s accumulated DHW record. The saturation nucleus sends $C$ to the maximum reef community state consistent with that thermal stress history. $C \in \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t)$ iff $C$ is already the maximum reef community state its DHW record supports — no larger community $D > C$ carries a weaker or equal thermal history.

**RelationalHistoryFiberTransferringNucleus** ([math](../math/relational-history-fiber-transferring-nucleus.md)), with fixed-point set:

$$\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t) \;=\; \bigcap_{\substack{s \in \Sigma \\ s \perp t}} \mathrm{image}\bigl(H(i_{s,t}) : H_{s \star t} \to H_t\bigr)$$

and:

$$\Delta_t(C) \;=\; \bigwedge\bigl\{D \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t) \;\big|\; D \geq C\bigr\}$$

$C \in \mathrm{image}(H(i_{s,t}))$ means there exists a post-stress reef state $C' \in H_{s \star t}$ with $H(i_{s,t})(C') = C$ — the reef state $C$ has a forward preimage through thermal stress step $s$; the holobiont can carry $C$ through one instance of stress type $s$ and return to $C$. $C \in \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ iff for every independent thermal stress step $s \perp t$, the reef community $C$ has a forward preimage — the Symbiodiniaceae clade composition of $C$ persists through every independent future bleaching pulse. Ecologically: $C$ is **holobiont-integrated** iff for every independent thermal stress type $s$, there exists a transiently bleached state $C'$ at $s \star t$ that restricts back to $C$ — the reef bleaches and recovers, carrying $C$ forward. $\Delta_t(C)$ is the minimum holobiont-integrated state above $C$: the smallest reef community that persists through all independent thermal events and is at least as complex as $C$.

**The four shadow classes for reef communities.** The shadow class of $C \in \mathcal{R}_t$ classifies the holobiont integration and thermal stress state:

| Shadow class | Condition | Holobiont name | Ecological state |
|---|---|---|---|
| $C \in \mathcal{R}^*_t$ | Both operators fix $C$ | **Healthy reef** | Full photosymbiosis; DHW history backward-recognized; holobiont forward-committed through future thermal stress |
| $C \in \mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{R}}_t$ | Thermal stress fixes $C$; holobiont does not | **Bleached reef** | DHW accumulated in restriction history; coral animal present; Symbiodiniaceae expelled; forward persistence lost |
| $C \in \mathrm{TrShadow}^{\mathcal{R}}_t$ | Holobiont fixes $C$; thermal stress does not | **Recovering reef** | New Symbiodiniaceae settled and forward-committed; DHW history not yet reflecting recovery |
| $C \in \mathrm{FreeShadow}^{\mathcal{R}}_t$ | Neither operator fixes $C$ | **Collapsed reef** | Coral framework dead; neither thermal history nor holobiont integration persists |

**The nuclear reflection and holographic projection.** The composite map

$$\pi_t \;:=\; \sigma_t \circ \Delta_t \;:\; H_t \to H_t$$

is the [RelationalHistoryPropositionFiberSaturatingTransferringCommutingNuclearReflection](../math/relational-history-fiber-saturating-transferring-commuting-nuclear-reflection.md) — a nucleus with $\mathrm{Fix}(\pi_t) = H^*_t$. The canonical holograph $(\mathcal{R}_t, \mathcal{R}^*_t, \pi_t)$ has the healthy reef stratum $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ as the **boundary** $\mathsf{Phase}$ and the full reef fiber $\mathcal{R}_t$ as the **bulk**; the projection $\pi_t$ sends every reef state to its nearest healthy-reef stabilization.

**The holobiont internal holograph.** The coral holobiont has a second, nested holographic structure. For each coral colony at locale $\ell$, the internal holobiont is a triple $(A, S, \pi_{\mathrm{holo}})$:
- $A$ = the coral animal state space — the **bulk** of the internal holograph; animal tissue, skeletal growth rate, energy reserves
- $S$ = the Symbiodiniaceae boundary — the **phase** of the internal holograph; symbiont clade composition, density, photosynthetic rate, and the $90$–$95\%$ photosynthate translocation fraction that sustains the animal
- $\pi_{\mathrm{holo}} : A \to S$ = the photosymbiotic coupling — the stabilization map sending any animal state to the symbiont clade population that sustains it; $\pi_{\mathrm{holo}}(a) = a$ iff the animal is fully integrated with its symbiont boundary, i.e., photosynthate translocation is at the full steady-state rate

The internal holograph is complete iff $A = S$ — when every animal state is already its own stabilized symbiont-integrated state. Bleaching is the collapse of the internal holograph: $\pi_{\mathrm{holo}}$ loses its target ($S \to \emptyset$ as Symbiodiniaceae are expelled), and the animal bulk $A$ loses its boundary.

**The reef community equilibrium condition.** $(C, t, \ell)$ is a **healthy reef** iff $C \in \mathcal{R}^*_t$ — equivalently, any of:
1. $C$ is in the boundary of the canonical holograph: $\pi_t(C) = C$
2. The [RelationalHistoryProjectionGap](../math/relational-history-fiber-projection-gap.md) is trivial: $[C, \pi_t(C)] = \{C\}$, i.e., $\lambda_C = 0$
3. $C$ is a zero-eigenmode of the defect operator: $\delta_t(e_C) = 0$
4. $C \in \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$: DHW history backward-recognizes $C$ AND holobiont integration forward-commits $C$ simultaneously

The [RelationalHistoryProjectionGap](../math/relational-history-fiber-projection-gap.md) of a reef state $C$ is the interval $[C, \pi_t(C)] \subseteq \mathcal{R}_t$; its size

$$\lambda_C \;:=\; \bigl|[C,\, \pi_t(C)]\bigr| - 1 \;\geq\; 0$$

is the **holobiont recovery depth** — the number of intermediate reef states between $C$ and full holobiont integration; it is zero iff $C$ is already healthy. The [RelationalHistoryFiberSettlementDefectOperator](../math/relational-history-fiber-settlement-defect-operator-explicit-formula-projection-gap.md) $\delta_t : K^{\mathcal{R}_t} \to K^{\mathcal{R}_t}$ is the diagonal operator $\delta_t(e_C) = \lambda_C \cdot e_C$ on the mode sheaf; its zero eigenspace is $K^{\mathcal{R}^*_t}$ — the healthy reef sector. The **calibration defect** $M_t(\delta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\delta_t) = \sum_{C \notin \mathcal{R}^*_t} \lambda_C$ is the total spectral recovery depth across all non-healthy reef states — the total holobiont debt of the reef fiber at history $t$.

## What this is

A **coral holobiont** (*holos* + *bios*, Greek: whole-life) is the reef-building coral colony together with its full community of microbial associates — photosynthetic dinoflagellates of the family Symbiodiniaceae (formerly all classified as *Symbiodinium*), bacteria, archaea, fungi, and viruses — functioning as a single metabolic and ecological unit. The term was introduced by Rohwer et al. (2002, *Marine Ecology Progress Series* 242, 1–17) to capture the insight that no ecologically meaningful distinction can be drawn between the coral animal and its microbial partners: the animal's energy budget, calcification rate, thermal tolerance, and immune function all depend fundamentally on the symbiont community it harbors.

The nuclear reading makes precise the bidirectional character of holobiont integration. The saturation direction — thermal stress accumulation — flows backward through the restriction maps of the history: each prior exposure to positive SST anomaly is recorded in the Degree Heating Weeks profile that the reef's current state carries. The transfer direction — holobiont integration — flows forward: Symbiodiniaceae must commit to persisting through every independent future thermal stress pulse, not just the ones the restriction history has recorded. The healthy reef $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ is the state where both directions are simultaneously satisfied: the DHW history backward-recognizes the community as thermally appropriate AND the Symbiodiniaceae clade forward-commits to persistence through future stress.

**Darwin's Paradox.** Charles Darwin noted in 1842 (*The Structure and Distribution of Coral Reefs*) that coral reefs are spectacularly productive ecosystems growing in the clearest, most nutrient-poor tropical ocean waters — a contradiction, since high productivity normally requires high nutrient input. The paradox is explained by the tightness of the photosymbiotic coupling: the Symbiodiniaceae translocate $90$–$95\%$ of their photosynthetically fixed carbon directly to the coral animal tissue, with near-zero leakage to the surrounding water. The reef recycles its own nutrients through this tight internal loop rather than depending on water-column nutrient input. In holographic terms: Darwin's Paradox is the consequence of the internal holograph $(A, S, \pi_{\mathrm{holo}})$ being highly complete — the animal state is very close to its symbiont-integrated partner, so almost all fixed carbon stays inside the holobiont. The oligotrophic paradox vanishes when you recognize that the boundary $S$ is not just a coating but a metabolically active internal loop.

## Symbiodiniaceae and the diversity of coral thermotolerance

The family Symbiodiniaceae encompasses at least seven genera (LaJeunesse et al. 2018, *Current Biology* 28, 2570–2580), formerly classified as clades A–I of the genus *Symbiodinium*. The different genera confer radically different thermal tolerances on their coral hosts:

**Cladocopium** (formerly clade C): the dominant symbiont in most Indo-Pacific corals under ambient conditions; maximally photosynthetically efficient under normal temperatures; bleaches at relatively modest SST anomalies ($+1$°C above seasonal maximum for $>$4 weeks); produces the highest photosynthate flux to the animal under optimal conditions.

**Durusdinium trenchii** (formerly clade D): dominant in corals of the Persian/Arabian Gulf, Red Sea high-temperature reefs, and in corals that have undergone symbiont shuffling after bleaching; tolerates SST anomalies $1$–$1.5$°C higher than Cladocopium before bleaching (Berkelmans & van Oppen 2006, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 273, 2305–2312); less efficient photosynthetically under ambient conditions, so Cladocopium is preferred when thermal stress is absent.

**Symbiont shuffling** (Baker 2001, *Science* 292, 2136–2137) is the post-bleaching transition from Cladocopium to Durusdinium: after expulsion of Cladocopium during bleaching, the coral may recolonize preferentially with Durusdinium if it is present in the water column, gaining a $1$–$1.5$°C thermal tolerance shift. This is a **Grothendieck topology change** $J \to J'$: the shuffling changes which thermal stress steps $s \in \Sigma$ are genuinely independent of the current history $t$. Under clade-C topology $J$: steps above $+1$°C are not independent — they constitute bleaching events that expel the symbiont. Under clade-D topology $J'$: steps up to $+2.5$°C are independent — the holobiont can carry the community through them without expulsion. The topology change shifts the fixed fiber: $\mathcal{R}^{*J'}_t$ includes reef states that were in $\mathrm{SatShadow}^{J}_t$.

The trade-off is nuclear: Durusdinium carries a lower photosynthate translocation rate under ambient conditions, reducing the tightness of the internal holograph $(A, S, \pi_{\mathrm{holo}})$. Symbiont shuffling is a thermally robust but photosynthetically less efficient mode of operation — it extends the fixed fiber in the thermal dimension at the cost of reducing the density of the healthy-reef sector in the energy dimension.

## Degree Heating Weeks as the restriction profile

The **Degree Heating Weeks** metric, developed by NOAA Coral Reef Watch (Liu et al. 2003, *Coral Reefs* 22, 44–52), is the operational instantiation of the saturation nucleus restriction profile. Formally:

$$\mathrm{DHW}_t \;=\; \sum_{\tau = t - 12\,\mathrm{weeks}}^{t} \max\bigl(0,\; \mathrm{SST}_\tau - \mathrm{MMM}\bigr) / 7$$

where $\mathrm{MMM}$ is the maximum monthly mean SST at locale $\ell$ computed from the historical baseline. Each week of SST anomaly at or above $1$°C above $\mathrm{MMM}$ contributes one Degree Heating Week to the accumulation.

The DHW is the restriction profile quantity: $H(i_{t_0,t})(C)$ is the community state $C$ projected back through the prior $12$-week window. The saturation nucleus $\sigma_t(C) = \bigvee\{D \mid H(i_{t_0,t})(D) \leq H(i_{t_0,t})(C) \text{ for all } t_0 < t\}$ computes the maximum reef state consistent with the accumulated DHW profile. NOAA Coral Reef Watch thresholds are the saturation nucleus threshold conditions:

| DHW value | Alert level | Shadow class condition |
|---|---|---|
| $< 4$ DHW | Watch | $C$ may be in $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ (thermal stress below saturation threshold) |
| $4$–$8$ DHW | Alert Level 1 | $C$ migrating from $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ toward $\mathrm{SatShadow}$ as $\sigma_t$ begins to move $C$ |
| $> 8$ DHW | Alert Level 2 | $C \in \mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{R}}_t$ established; coral mortality likely |

## The algal phase shift and the Schwarzschild threshold

**Terry Hughes** (1994, *Science* 265, 1547–1551) documented the catastrophic shift of Jamaican reefs from coral-dominated to algae-dominated following the 1983 *Diadema antillarum* mass mortality, which removed the primary herbivore and allowed macroalgal overgrowth of bleached and recovering corals. The coral-to-algae phase shift is an irreversible transition: once algae colonize dead coral skeleton, coral larvae cannot settle, and the reef cannot return to coral-dominated state without active intervention.

In nuclear terms: the phase shift is the crossing of the **Schwarzschild spectral threshold** [RelationalHistoryFiberSpectralSchwarzschildCriticalEigenvalueHorizonOperator](../math/relational-history-fiber-spectral-schwarzschild-critical-eigenvalue-horizon-operator.md). The spectral mass $M_t(\delta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\delta_t) = \sum_{C \notin \mathcal{R}^*_t} \lambda_C$ — the total calibration defect — accumulates over successive bleaching events and herbivore removals. The Schwarzschild radius $r_s(\delta_t) = 2 M_t(\delta)$ is the threshold projection-gap size above which reef modes are spectrally trapped. Modes with $\lambda_C > M_t(\delta)$ — reef states deep in $\mathrm{FreeShadow}$ or $\mathrm{SatShadow}$ with large projection gaps — cannot decay to the zero-eigenspace $K^{\mathcal{R}^*_t}$ through internal dynamics alone; they require external input (larval supply from distant healthy reefs, herbivore reintroduction, active intervention) equivalent to the defect energy.

When the calibration defect $M_t(\delta)$ rises through the Schwarzschild radius (i.e., when the typical projection-gap size $\lambda_C$ approaches $M_t(\delta)$), the phase shift becomes self-reinforcing: each additional bleaching event increases $M_t(\delta)$, raising the spectral horizon, trapping more modes, and making recovery harder. The 1983 Jamaica event was the reef system crossing this threshold: the simultaneous removal of $\mathit{Diadema}$ (removing the herbivory that suppresses algal overgrowth) and cumulative thermal bleaching damage (elevating all $\lambda_C$ values) pushed $M_t(\delta)$ above the point where the system could recover to $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ without intervention.

## Assisted evolution and the constitutive Act

**Van Oppen et al.** (2015, *PNAS* 112, 2307–2313) proposed **assisted evolution** — deliberately selecting and propagating thermally tolerant coral genotypes and symbiont strains — as a conservation intervention. The proposed methods include:
- **Selective breeding**: propagating coral genotypes with highest heat tolerance under elevated temperatures; building up the frequency of thermally tolerant hosts in the population
- **Symbiont manipulation**: inoculating corals with thermally tolerant Symbiodiniaceae strains (*Durusdinium trenchii*, heat-evolved *Cladocopium* variants)
- **Epigenetic conditioning**: exposing larvae or juvenile corals to sublethal heat stress to trigger epigenetic changes that elevate bleaching thresholds (Palumbi et al. 2014, *Science* 344, 895–898)
- **Micro-fragmentation** (Forsman et al. 2015): fracturing large colonies into hundreds of $1$–$2$ cm fragments, which grow $25$–$50\times$ faster than whole colonies in aquaculture — restoring the size class of corals that have been killed

In nuclear terms, each of these is a **constitutive Act** [spec/act.md] — an intentional action on the history site $(T, J)$ that changes the structure of the community fiber $\mathcal{R}_t$. Assisted evolution acts on the Grothendieck topology $J$: by shifting the symbiont clade composition, it changes which thermal stress steps $s$ are independent of the current history, expanding the set $\{s \perp t\}$ and thereby expanding $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ and the fixed fiber $\mathcal{R}^*_t$. Micro-fragmentation acts on the restriction maps directly: by restoring large-colony size classes, it rebuilds the restriction-profile structure that $\sigma_t$ requires to backward-recognize a high-cover reef state.

## The Reef 2050 plan: managing the projection gap

The **Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority Reef 2050 Plan** (GBRMPA 2015, updated 2021) is a multi-decade management framework with explicit targets for coral cover, bleaching frequency tolerance, and water quality. These targets are, in nuclear terms, conditions on the projection gap $[C, \pi_t(C)]$:

- Coral cover targets (e.g., $>30\%$ hard coral cover in shallow inshore reefs) specify that $C$ must be in a region of $\mathcal{R}_t$ where $\lambda_C$ is small — where the projection gap to the healthy-reef stratum $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ is short
- Bleaching frequency tolerance targets specify how often the DHW restriction history can cross Alert Level 1 before it forces $\sigma_t$ to move $C$ out of $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ — they are threshold conditions on the saturation nucleus
- Water quality targets (turbidity, nutrient levels) specify conditions on the locale $\ell$ that affect the topology $J$: high turbidity reduces available irradiance, which reduces the photosynthate translocation in the internal holograph $(A, S, \pi_{\mathrm{holo}})$, which tightens the saturation nucleus's backward-recognition of healthy-reef states

## Etymology and historical grounding

**Holobiont** derives from Greek *holos* (whole, entire) + *bios* (life) + *-ont* (being), via analogy with *symbiont*. The term carries a deliberately philosophical weight: the holobiont is the entire organism as a community — not the coral animal with microbial guests, but a single biological individual whose identity is constituted by its associations.

**Coral bleaching as an observed phenomenon** was first described scientifically by Agassiz (1883) in the *Bulletin of the Museum of Comparative Zoology*, but the mechanism — expulsion of Symbiodiniaceae — was established by Coles & Jokiel (1977, *Marine Biology* 43, 201–208), who demonstrated temperature-induced bleaching experimentally. The 1982–1983 El Niño bleaching event was the first globally recorded mass bleaching (Glynn 1984, *Marine Ecology Progress Series* 20, 169–187).

**The discovery of Symbiodiniaceae diversity** — that the "single symbiont" is actually a family of genetically and functionally distinct genera — was driven by Carlos Wilkinson, John Rowan, and Andrew Baker in the 1990s–2000s (Rowan & Powers 1991, *Science* 251, 1348–1351; Rowan & Knowlton 1995, *PNAS* 92, 2850–2853). Baker's 2001 demonstration that bleaching can trigger symbiont shuffling was the key finding that made the nuclear reading possible: thermotolerance is not fixed by host genotype but is modifiable by which symbiont clade occupies the Heyting algebra position $S$ in the internal holograph.

## Nuclear reading

**Proposition 1 (Bleaching = SatShadow migration; the DHW formula is the restriction profile).** A reef community state $C \in \mathcal{R}_t$ is bleached iff $C \in \mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{R}}_t$: $\sigma_t(C) = C$ (DHW history backward-recognizes the community as consistent with accumulated heat stress — the restriction maps record the anomaly) and $\Delta_t(C) > C$ (holobiont integration is broken — there exists at least one independent future thermal stress step $s \perp t$ such that $C \notin \mathrm{image}(H(i_{s,t}))$; the Symbiodiniaceae clade that was present in $C$ cannot carry $C$ forward through step $s$).

The DHW formula is the saturation nucleus restriction profile: $H(i_{t_0,t})(C)$ accumulates the prior $12$-week SST anomaly history. Alert Level 2 ($\mathrm{DHW} > 8$) is the threshold at which $\sigma_t$ moves $C$ firmly into $\mathrm{SatShadow}$ — the restriction profile of $C$ is so heavily imprinted with heat stress that no reef state $D \geq C$ with lower thermal history exists, and the forward-commitment to symbiont persistence breaks. Alert Level 1 ($\mathrm{DHW} > 4$) is the transition zone: $\sigma_t$ and $\Delta_t$ are decoupling but the community is not yet fully in $\mathrm{SatShadow}$.

*Proof sketch.* Bleaching = Symbiodiniaceae expulsion = loss of the forward preimage: if the Symbiodiniaceae clade is absent from state $C'$ at $s \star t$, then $H(i_{s,t})(C') \neq C$ for any $C' \geq C$; hence $C \notin \mathrm{image}(H(i_{s,t}))$; hence $\Delta_t(C) > C$. The DHW history is the restriction-profile record that $\sigma_t$ operates on: high DHW = high restriction-profile imprint = $C$ is already at the maximum of its thermally-stressed class. $\square$

**Proposition 2 (Symbiont shuffling = Grothendieck topology change; shifts the fixed fiber).** Symbiont shuffling from Cladocopium-dominated to Durusdinium-dominated is a Grothendieck topology change $J \to J'$ on the history site $(T, J)$. Under topology $J$ (Cladocopium), the independent step set $\{s \perp^J t\}$ excludes thermal stress steps $s$ with $\Delta T > 1$°C above MMM; under topology $J'$ (Durusdinium), the independent step set $\{s \perp^{J'} t\}$ includes stress steps up to $\Delta T \leq 2.5$°C above MMM. The topology change expands $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta^{J'}_t) \supseteq \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta^J_t)$, because the intersection $\bigcap_{s \perp^{J'} t} \mathrm{image}(H(i_{s,t}))$ is taken over a larger set. Consequently $\mathcal{R}^{*J'}_t \supseteq \mathcal{R}^{*J}_t$: the fixed fiber expands to include reef states that were in $\mathrm{SatShadow}^J_t$ (burned by Cladocopium bleaching thresholds) but are now in $\mathcal{R}^{*J'}_t$ (persistent under Durusdinium tolerance).

The cost is the photosynthetic trade-off: the saturation nucleus $\sigma^{J'}_t$ restricts to a lower photosynthate translocation rate; the restriction profile class of any state $C$ under $J'$ has a lower maximum than under $J$. The shuffled reef gains thermal depth in the transfer direction but loses energy height in the saturation direction. $\square$

**Proposition 3 (Canonical holograph; defect spectrum grades reef states by recovery depth).** The canonical holograph $(\mathcal{R}_t, \mathcal{R}^*_t, \pi_t)$ with $\pi_t = \sigma_t \circ \Delta_t$ has the healthy reef stratum $\mathcal{R}^*_t$ as boundary and the full reef fiber as bulk. The mode sheaf $K^{\mathcal{R}_t}$ decomposes as $\bigoplus_{\lambda \geq 0} E_{t,\lambda}$ with:

- $E_{t,0} = K^{\mathcal{R}^*_t}$: the healthy reef sector — zero spectral debt, $\delta_t$ acts as zero
- $E_{t,\lambda}$ for $\lambda > 0$: reef modes at holobiont recovery depth $\lambda$ — states $C$ where $[C, \pi_t(C)]$ has $\lambda$ intermediate steps between bleached and fully integrated

The calibration defect $M_t(\delta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\delta_t) = \sum_{C \notin \mathcal{R}^*_t} \lambda_C$ is the total spectral reef debt. Successive bleaching events under chronic thermal stress inflate $M_t(\delta)$ monotonically: each event moves states from $E_{t,0}$ into $E_{t,\lambda > 0}$, increasing the sum. Symbiont shuffling (Prop 2) reduces $M_t(\delta)$ by moving states from $E_{t,\lambda > 0}$ back to $E_{t,0}$ under the new topology. $\square$
