## Executive summary

The classical light cone is the null cone of a Lorentzian metric: at each event, it separates timelike directions from spacelike directions, and its boundary consists of lightlike directions.
In modern mathematics, that picture is still central, but it is no longer the whole story.
The frontier is increasingly about treating the cone itself as primitive: a local partial order, a propagation law, a characteristic relation, a commutativity constraint, a reconstruction interface, or a generalized positivity cone.

The short version is:

> **The frontier is the passage from “spacetime has a metric, hence light cones” to “cone/order/propagation data may be enough to define, recover, or replace spacetime.”**

That slogan cuts across several fields that are not always read together.

In **synthetic and low-regularity Lorentzian geometry**, the question is how much causality, time separation, curvature, global hyperbolicity, and null hypersurface theory survive when the underlying space is not a smooth manifold or the metric is too rough for classical tangent-cone arguments.
Lorentzian length spaces, Lorentzian metric spaces, timelike Ricci curvature bounds, Gromov-Hausdorff-like convergence, null hypersurfaces, and Lorentzian Cartan-Hadamard/splitting-type theorems are part of this active zone [@kunzinger-saemann-2018; @bykov-minguzzi-suhr-2025; @mondino-saemann-2025; @mondino-ryborz-saemann-2026; @cavalletti-manini-mondino-2025; @eros-gieger-2026].

In **inverse problems and reconstruction**, the light cone is treated as a measurement interface.
If one sees where light emitted from hidden events reaches a boundary, or if one measures nonlinear wave interactions, one can sometimes recover topology, smooth structure, conformal geometry, metric data, or even gauge/connection data [@hintz-uhlmann-2019; @kurylev-lassas-uhlmann-2018; @lassas-uhlmann-wang-2018; @oksanen-zhang-2025].

In **microlocal analysis**, the light cone is the characteristic cone of hyperbolic PDE.
Singularities propagate along null bicharacteristics, so the light cone is the geometry of where nonsmooth information can travel.
This connects geometric analysis, black-hole scattering, null infinity, Feynman propagators, Hadamard states, and the sheaf-theoretic notion of singular support [@vasy-2026; @kashiwara-schapira-1990].

In **algebraic quantum field theory and factorization algebra**, the cone determines which spacetime regions are causally independent and hence which algebras commute, factorize, or embed into one another.
The light cone becomes an algebraic syntax for locality, time-slice behavior, covariance, and the interface between BV quantization and AQFT [@benini-musante-schenkel-2024; @bunk-macmanus-schenkel-2025; @dul-2025; @finberg-2025].

In **causal set theory and order-theoretic spacetime**, causal precedence is primary and the continuum is emergent or approximate.
A locally finite partial order is asked to carry the same information as a Lorentzian spacetime: order gives causal structure, counting gives volume, and together they may recover geometry [@surya-2019; @hu-meyer-meyer-2025].

In **hyperbolicity cones, Lorentzian polynomials, and convex algebraic geometry**, the “light cone” abstraction detaches from spacetime and becomes a structure of signature, hyperbolicity, and positivity.
Hyperbolic polynomials define convex cones; Lorentzian polynomials generalize the “one positive direction, many negative directions” pattern into combinatorics, Hodge theory, matroids, log-concavity, and optimization [@guler-1997; @branden-huh-2020; @branden-leake-2025; @baker-huh-kummer-lorscheid-2025; @scheiderer-2025].

In **twistor theory, null infinity, and celestial holography**, null rays and lightlike asymptotics are promoted to a primary encoding of scattering and gravitational data.
The frontier here is not just spacetime causality; it is the possibility that the deep structure of flat-space quantum gravity is best described at null infinity, on the celestial sphere, or in twistor/ambitwistor geometry [@mason-skinner-2014; @zhu-2026; @kmec-mason-ruzziconi-sharma-2025; @jarov-2025].

The deepest unifying claim of this memo is that **light cones are becoming a general mathematical grammar of admissible continuation**.
At a point, event, region, algebra, sheaf, polynomial, or boundary cut, a cone tells us what can propagate, what can be ordered, what can be reconstructed, what can interact, and what must remain independent.

## 1. Classical backbone: the cone, the order, and the conformal metric

The usual smooth model starts with a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold \((M,g)\), where \(g\) has signature \((- + \cdots +)\) or \((+ - \cdots -)\).
At each point \(p\), the tangent space \(T_pM\) is divided by the quadratic condition

\[ g_p(v,v)=0. \]

Vectors satisfying this equation form the **null cone**.
Vectors inside the chosen future lobe are future-timelike or future-causal; vectors outside are spacelike.
A causal curve is a curve whose tangent remains within the future causal cone almost everywhere.
One then defines:

- \(p \ll q\): \(q\) lies in the chronological future of \(p\), connected by a future-directed timelike curve;
- \(p \leq q\): \(q\) lies in the causal future of \(p\), connected by a future-directed causal curve or equal to \(p\);
- \(J^+(p)\): causal future;
- \(I^+(p)\): chronological future;
- causal diamonds \(J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)\);
- time separation \(\tau(p,q)\), often the supremum of Lorentzian lengths of causal curves from \(p\) to \(q\).

The old insight that remains foundational is that causal order is extremely rigid.
Kronheimer and Penrose axiomatized causal spaces in terms of causal and chronological orderings [@kronheimer-penrose-1967].
Hawking, King, and McCarthy showed that a topology incorporating causal, differential, and conformal structure can be built from spacetime causality [@hawking-king-mccarthy-1976].
Malament sharpened this into the famous theorem-family: for sufficiently distinguishing spacetimes, the causal structure determines the topology and conformal metric structure [@malament-1977].
Modern causality theory refines the hierarchy of causal conditions and can also be framed in terms of cone distributions and differential inclusions [@minguzzi-2019].

This classical backbone matters because it already says something radical: the light cone does not merely decorate the metric; it almost determines the metric up to conformal scale.
The cone tells us which vectors are zero with respect to \(g\), and in Lorentzian signature that null structure is so restrictive that it determines angles and causal topology in a way no Riemannian analogue does.
A Riemannian metric has no light cone; all directions are locally comparable as lengths.
A Lorentzian metric has an order-generating boundary at every point.

The standard reconstruction slogan is:

\[ \text{causal order} \quad + \quad \text{volume scale} \quad \approx \quad \text{Lorentzian geometry}. \]

The conformal class fixes the cones.
A volume measure, time separation, or counting density can restore the conformal factor.
This is the spine of causal set theory, causal reconstruction, and Lorentzian metric geometry.

The contemporary frontier extends this classical backbone in two directions at once.
One direction weakens the smooth assumptions: what survives when no smooth tangent space exists?
The other direction strengthens the algebraic and analytic payload: what else besides the conformal metric can be recovered from cone-guided propagation?

## 2. The new primitive: cone/order/propagation geometry

A light cone has several mathematical readings.
These readings are equivalent or tightly coupled in smooth Lorentzian geometry, but they separate in generalized settings.

First, a light cone is a **quadratic null cone**:

\[ \mathcal{N}_p = \{v\in T_pM : g_p(v,v)=0\}. \]

Second, it is a **cone field**: an assignment \(p\mapsto C_p\subset T_pM\) of future causal cones.
This is the natural language of causality theory, differential inclusions, and low-regularity spacetimes.
A causal curve is a solution of the inclusion

\[ \dot{\gamma}(t) \in C_{\gamma(t)}. \]

Third, it is an **order generator**.
The cone field integrates to the relation \(p\leq q\).
In this view, global spacetime is the transitive closure of local admissible continuations.

Fourth, it is a **characteristic variety**.
For the wave operator \(\Box_g\), the principal symbol is

\[ \sigma(\Box_g)(x,\xi)=g_x^{-1}(\xi,\xi), \]

and the characteristic set \(g_x^{-1}(\xi,\xi)=0\) is the cotangent null cone.
Singularities propagate along the Hamiltonian flow on this characteristic set.

Fifth, it is a **commutativity/factorization constraint**.
In algebraic QFT, spacelike separation means the associated algebras should commute.
The cone is thus not merely geometric; it controls algebraic independence.

Sixth, it is a **convex positivity structure**.
The Lorentz cone

\[ \{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}: x_0 \geq \sqrt{x_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2}\} \]

is the model example of a hyperbolicity cone.
Generalized hyperbolicity cones and Lorentzian polynomials extend this signature logic into optimization and combinatorics.

Seventh, it is a **null-boundary encoding**.
In twistor/celestial geometry, null rays, asymptotic directions, and cuts of null infinity can replace local spacetime points as the meaningful variables.

These seven readings give the central table of this memo:

| Reading of light cone | Primary object | What it controls | Frontier question |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Lorentzian null cone | \(g(v,v)=0\) | conformal metric | How much metric data is determined by null data? |
| Cone field | \(C_p\subset T_pM\) | admissible curves | What survives at low regularity? |
| Causal order | \(p\leq q\) | topology, chronology, domains | Can order replace spacetime? |
| Characteristic cone | \(\sigma(P)=0\) | wavefront propagation | What can singularities reveal? |
| AQFT locality cone | spacelike separation | commutativity/factorization | How does causality become algebra? |
| Hyperbolicity cone | polynomial positivity component | convexity, optimization, Hodge theory | How far does Lorentzian signature generalize? |
| Null asymptotic cone | null infinity / twistor space | scattering and holography | Can the bulk be reconstructed from null boundary data? |

The point is not that all these fields are secretly the same.
The point is that the same structural move recurs: replace a symmetric metric notion with an asymmetric, directed, cone-constrained, order-producing one.

That is why light-cone mathematics is now a frontier for more than relativity.

## 3. Synthetic and low-regularity Lorentzian geometry

### 3.1 Why this area is moving

Riemannian metric geometry has a mature synthetic theory: length spaces, Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, Alexandrov curvature bounds, CAT spaces, Ricci curvature lower bounds via optimal transport, measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, and so on.
Lorentzian geometry is much harder.
Time separation is not a distance: it is not symmetric, it can vanish for distinct points, it obeys a **reverse triangle inequality** along causal chains, and it is only meaningful for causally related pairs.

The Lorentzian analogue of a metric space therefore cannot simply replace \(d\) by \(\tau\).
It needs at least:

- a topology;
- causal and chronological relations;
- a time-separation function \(\tau\);
- compatibility between curves, causality, and \(\tau\);
- conditions such as global hyperbolicity, strong causality, or causal plainness;
- localizability assumptions allowing small neighborhoods to behave like local spacetimes.

Lorentzian length spaces were introduced to supply such a framework [@kunzinger-saemann-2018].
They model smooth spacetimes but also admit much rougher objects.
Since then, the field has rapidly developed the causal hierarchy, time functions, global hyperbolicity, curvature bounds, convergence, and singular/nonsmooth hypersurface theory [@ake-cabrera-solis-2020; @burtscher-garciaheveling-saemann-2024].

The frontier is not merely “make Lorentzian versions of Riemannian metric geometry.” The real lesson is that Lorentzian geometry does not behave as a naive sign-flipped Riemannian geometry.
Causal diamonds replace balls.
Timelike maximizers replace shortest geodesics.
Null directions are boundary cases rather than ordinary directions.
Ricci lower bounds and splitting theorems may fail or require extra hypotheses in ways that reveal genuine Lorentzian asymmetry [@mondino-ryborz-saemann-2026].

### 3.2 Lorentzian metric spaces and the two-point distance program

A related line asks whether one can define a spacetime using only a two-point Lorentzian distance.
In the smooth setting, the time-separation function \(\tau(p,q)\) is zero when \(q\) is not in the causal future of \(p\), and otherwise records maximal proper time.
Unlike Riemannian distance, \(\tau\) satisfies a reverse triangle inequality on causal chains:

\[ p\leq q\leq r \quad \Rightarrow \quad \tau(p,r) \geq \tau(p,q)+\tau(q,r). \]

Recent work on Lorentzian metric spaces and Gromov-Hausdorff convergence abstracts this structure.
Bykov, Minguzzi, and Suhr formulate Lorentzian metric spaces using conditions such as reverse triangle inequality, continuity of the distance, compactness of chronological diamonds, and a distinguishing condition, then develop a Gromov-Hausdorff-type convergence theory [@bykov-minguzzi-suhr-2025].
Bykov and Minguzzi further show how global hyperbolicity, topology, and causality can be recovered from the Lorentzian distance in settings designed not to presuppose smooth manifold topology [@bykov-minguzzi-2026].
Minguzzi’s 2026 review consolidates this “two-point distance” approach [@minguzzi-2026-results].

This is mathematically important because it directly attacks the issue of primitives.
If \(\tau\) is primitive, then the cone is implicit in the vanishing and positivity pattern of \(\tau\):

\[ p\leq q \quad \text{roughly when} \quad \tau(p,q)>0 \text{ or } p=q, \]

modulo null-related subtleties.
Thus, causal order and temporal length are encoded in a single asymmetric function.

### 3.3 Lorentzian Gromov-Hausdorff convergence

One current frontier is the right notion of convergence for Lorentzian spaces.
In Riemannian geometry, Gromov-Hausdorff convergence compares compact metric spaces by embedding them into common metric spaces and measuring Hausdorff distance.
But Lorentzian spaces use causal diamonds, time separation, and partial order. “Balls” are no longer the natural local objects; diamonds are.

Mondino and Sämann develop Lorentzian Gromov-Hausdorff convergence using causal diamonds, \(\varepsilon\)-nets, and time-separation distortion, and they prove precompactness theorems for globally hyperbolic Lorentzian length spaces [@mondino-saemann-2025].
Che and Perales define a timed Gromov-Hausdorff distance and show stability of causality under convergence, connecting this to intrinsic flat convergence [@che-perales-2026].
Ketterer develops an \(\ell\)-convergence framework for Lorentzian pre-length spaces and studies stability of curvature bounds under convergence [@ketterer-2026].

This area matters because it supplies a mathematical bridge between smooth general relativity, rough/impulsive gravitational waves, discrete spacetime models, and limiting processes.
If a sequence of spacetimes converges, what happens to causal cones?
Do causal relations collapse?
Do timelike geodesics survive?
Does global hyperbolicity pass to the limit?
Does synthetic curvature pass to the limit?
These are not technical side-questions; they are the conditions under which “spacetime by light cone” becomes a robust mathematical category.

### 3.4 Synthetic timelike Ricci curvature and nonsmooth null hypersurfaces

Another major frontier is curvature.
Synthetic Ricci curvature in Riemannian geometry is often formulated through optimal transport, entropy convexity, or Brunn-Minkowski-type inequalities.
Lorentzian analogues are subtler because timelike geodesics maximize rather than minimize length, and null boundaries matter.

Farooqui proves an equivalence between a timelike Ricci curvature condition and a timelike Brunn-Minkowski inequality in synthetic Lorentzian settings [@farooqui-2026].
Mondino, Ryborz, and Sämann prove stability of synthetic timelike Ricci curvature bounds under \(C^0\) limits and apply the framework to impulsive gravitational waves; they also identify a counterexample to a Lorentzian splitting analogue, emphasizing a sharp divergence from Riemannian expectations [@mondino-ryborz-saemann-2026].

Null hypersurfaces are especially important because they are the geometric realization of light fronts.
Cavalletti, Manini, and Mondino introduce a synthetic framework for null hypersurfaces in nonsmooth spacetimes, working with intrinsic data that can capture null energy conditions and support synthetic forms of classical results such as area and singularity theorems [@cavalletti-manini-mondino-2025].

The key conceptual shift is this: in smooth Lorentzian geometry, the light cone at a point lives in the tangent space.
In nonsmooth Lorentzian geometry, one often cannot begin there.
Instead, lightlike structure is inferred from causal relations, time separation, null hypersurfaces, diamonds, and limiting behavior.
The cone becomes less a literal tangent cone and more a **behavioral constraint** on admissible propagation.

### 3.5 Lorentzian Cartan-Hadamard, splitting, and total curvature

The Riemannian Cartan-Hadamard theorem says that a complete simply connected Riemannian manifold of nonpositive sectional curvature has strong global geodesic and topological properties.
Lorentzian analogues are much more delicate because causal geodesics can maximize time and because topology interacts with chronology.
Recent synthetic Lorentzian Cartan-Hadamard work proves existence and uniqueness of timelike geodesics under global hyperbolicity and future one-connectedness assumptions [@eros-gieger-2026].

Barton, Beran, Che, Gieger, Röhrig, and Rott develop first variation formulae and a splitting theorem for Lorentzian pre-length spaces with global non-positive timelike curvature [@barton-beran-che-gieger-rohrig-rott-2026].
Erös, Rott, and Xu introduce synthetic timelike total curvature and prove sharp lower bounds for lengths of timelike curves in Lorentzian length spaces [@eros-rott-xu-2026].

These results are part of a bigger effort to build Lorentzian metric geometry as a real peer of Riemannian metric geometry while respecting the deep asymmetries of time.

### 3.6 Open problems in the synthetic frontier

The core open problems are not only theorem-level; they are also category-level.

1. **Find the right category of Lorentzian spaces.** Smooth globally hyperbolic spacetimes, continuous cone fields, Lorentzian length spaces, Lorentzian metric spaces, timed metric spaces, causal sets, and spaces with synthetic curvature bounds do not yet sit in a single universally accepted category.

2. **Control null structure synthetically.** Timelike curves are often easier than null curves because \(\tau>0\) detects them.
  Null relatedness lives on the boundary where \(\tau=0\), making it fragile.
  A good synthetic theory of null geodesics, null hypersurfaces, and caustics is still emerging.

3. **Develop stable convergence theorems.** The frontier needs convergence notions that preserve causality, global hyperbolicity, curvature bounds, and physically meaningful singular limits.

4. **Understand tangent objects.** Blow-ups, tangent cones, spaces of directions, and local models are subtler in Lorentzian signature.
  At a nonsmooth point, what is “the light cone” after rescaling?

5. **Build sheaf/categorical versions.** A Lorentzian length space naturally has causal diamonds and propagation relations.
  It should support sheaves, cosheaves, factorization structures, and causal sites.
  But the general theory is not as mature as its manifold-based counterpart.

For the purposes of your own mathematics, this is the branch to watch most closely if you care about light cones as local-to-global gluing constraints.

## 4. Reconstruction: light cones as measurement interfaces

### 4.1 From “cones determine conformal geometry” to operational reconstruction

The classical causal reconstruction theorem says that, under strong enough hypotheses, causal structure determines conformal structure.
Modern inverse problems ask a harder and more operational question:

> If we do not know the spacetime, but we can observe where light or waves arrive, what can we reconstruct?

This reframes the light cone as an interface between hidden geometry and observed data.
The observable may be a set of arrival times on a boundary, a family of light observation sets, a response operator for a wave equation, or singularities produced by nonlinear interactions.

Hintz and Uhlmann reconstruct Lorentzian manifolds from boundary light observation sets, showing how the topological, differentiable, and conformal structures of certain regions can be recovered from observations of future light cones at a boundary [@hintz-uhlmann-2019].
In that framework, one does not directly inspect the metric; one records how null geodesics from interior events reach an observation surface.

The intuition is simple but powerful.
An event is known by its light projection.
If two hidden events have different future light observation sets, the boundary can distinguish them.
If the family of such sets is rich enough, one can reconstruct the interior.

### 4.2 Nonlinear wave interactions

A second reconstruction method exploits nonlinear wave equations.
In linear wave equations, singularities propagate independently along null geodesics.
In nonlinear equations, interacting waves can create new singularities at intersection points.
Those artificial singularities then propagate outward and become detectable.

Kurylev, Lassas, and Uhlmann used nonlinear hyperbolic equations to solve inverse problems on Lorentzian manifolds [@kurylev-lassas-uhlmann-2018].
Lassas, Uhlmann, and Wang further developed inverse problems for semilinear wave equations [@lassas-uhlmann-wang-2018].
Uhlmann and Zhai survey this nonlinear inverse-problem approach [@uhlmann-zhai-2021].

Oksanen and Zhang push this into gauge/connection territory: for semilinear cubic wave equations on globally hyperbolic Lorentzian manifolds, they recover Hermitian connections by analyzing nonlinear wave interactions and a non-abelian broken light-ray transform [@oksanen-zhang-2025].

The conceptual pattern is:

1. send waves into a region;
2. let their singularities propagate along light cones;
3. use nonlinear interactions to create new singularities;
4. observe those singularities at the boundary;
5. reconstruct geometry, coefficients, or gauge data.

This makes the light cone a computation device.
It routes wavefronts through the unknown geometry, and the interaction pattern encodes the hidden structure.

### 4.3 Causal metric reconstruction

A related order-theoretic reconstruction question asks how much metric data can be recovered from causal separations alone.
The causal metric hypothesis says that the distances between causally related pairs may determine spacelike distances and hence the full Minkowski structure in certain settings [@hu-meyer-meyer-2025].
This is directly relevant to causal sets: if causal order plus enough scale information determines spacelike metric data, then discrete causal data may have more reconstructive power than it first appears.

This matters because spacelike distance is not directly given by causal order.
Two spacelike separated events are incomparable.
Recovering their separation from the pattern of common futures, common pasts, causal intervals, or timelike distances is a deep problem at the edge of order geometry.

### 4.4 Open problems in reconstruction

The active questions include:

1. **Stability under noise.** Inverse theorems often reconstruct geometry exactly from ideal data.
  Realistic or computational versions need quantitative stability estimates.

2. **Low regularity.** Can the light-observation and nonlinear-interaction methods survive when the metric is only continuous or belongs to weak regularity classes?

3. **Caustics and conjugate points.** Null geodesics can focus.
  Observation sets can develop singularities.
  Reconstructing through caustics remains subtle.

4. **Gauge fields and non-abelian data.** Recovering connections through wave interactions suggests a frontier where light cones recover not just metric geometry but internal field data.

5. **Discrete reconstruction.** Can causal set data support analogues of light observation sets, wavefront propagation, or broken light-ray transforms?

6. **Algorithmic reconstruction.** Many inverse results are analytic existence theorems.
  Turning them into implementable algorithms is a major frontier.

For a sheaf-oriented program, the reconstruction viewpoint is especially suggestive.
Observation sets form a family of subsets.
Events can be reconstructed from the incidence pattern of these subsets.
That is already a sheaf-like or cosheaf-like local-to-global story: hidden points are recovered from how their light cones restrict to observation domains.

## 5. Microlocal analysis: light cones as singular-support geometry

### 5.1 Characteristic cones

For a differential operator \(P\), the characteristic variety is where the principal symbol vanishes.
For the wave operator on a Lorentzian manifold,

\[ P = \Box_g, \qquad \sigma(P)(x,\xi)=g_x^{-1}(\xi,\xi). \]

Thus the characteristic variety is the cotangent light cone.
The propagation of singularities theorem says, roughly, that singularities of solutions to \(Pu=f\) propagate along null bicharacteristics, the Hamiltonian flow lines of \(\sigma(P)\) on the characteristic set.

This is the analytic form of light-cone causality.
Smooth information may disperse, but singular information follows the null geometry.

### 5.2 Wavefront sets and the covector version of causality

The wavefront set \(\operatorname{WF}(u)\) of a distribution records not merely where \(u\) is singular, but in which cotangent directions it is singular.
Light cones therefore appear in cotangent space: singularities propagate along null covectors.
This is crucial for:

- hyperbolic PDE;
- scattering theory;
- black hole stability and decay;
- Feynman propagators;
- Hadamard states in QFT;
- inverse problems;
- sheaf-theoretic singular support.

Vasy’s review of general relativity and microlocal analysis describes how microlocal tools have become central to wave propagation in general relativity, including null infinity, black hole backgrounds, and the Einstein equation itself [@vasy-2026].

### 5.3 Sheaf-theoretic microlocalization

Kashiwara and Schapira’s microlocal sheaf theory attaches to a sheaf its singular support, a conic subset of the cotangent bundle that describes where and in what directions sections fail to propagate uniquely [@kashiwara-schapira-1990].
This is not “light cones” in the physical sense by default, but it is structurally adjacent: a conic subset of \(T^*M\) constrains admissible propagation.

For Lorentzian geometry, one can imagine sheaves whose singular support lies in a causal cone or its dual.
Then the light cone becomes a microsupport constraint.
In this language, causality can be phrased as a restriction on which covectors a sheaf is allowed to see.

That is a very strong bridge to the mathematics you have been building.
A light cone can be read as a **local admissibility relation**.
A sheaf is a local-to-global gluing machine.
A sheaf with constrained singular support is a local-to-global gluing machine whose failures, obstructions, or propagations are restricted by a cone.

### 5.4 Nonlinear microlocality and interaction calculus

The inverse-problem literature shows why nonlinear interactions matter.
Four distorted plane waves can interact to create a new singularity at an interior point; that singularity then propagates along the future light cone and can be measured outside [@kurylev-lassas-uhlmann-2018; @lassas-uhlmann-wang-2018].
The light cone is not merely where information travels.
It is also where information can be made to collide.

This suggests a general principle:

> **A light cone is not only a propagation relation; it is an interaction scheduler.**

Where null hypersurfaces intersect, nonlinear terms can generate new signals.
The geometry of those intersections encodes the metric, connection, or coefficients of the equation.

### 5.5 Open microlocal questions

1. **Rough Lorentzian backgrounds.** How much of the propagation-of-singularities machinery survives for nonsmooth metrics or synthetic Lorentzian spaces?

2. **Microlocal sheaves on causal spaces.** Classical microlocal sheaf theory assumes smooth manifolds.
  Can a meaningful analogue exist for Lorentzian length spaces or causal sets?

3. **Nonlinear interaction algebras.** Can one algebraize the calculus of wavefront interactions as an operad, factorization algebra, or sheaf/cosheaf over causal diamonds?

4. **Null boundary microlocality.** At null infinity, singularities asymptote to boundary data.
  How should one formulate the microlocal category of radiation fields?

5. **Computational wavefront reconstruction.** The inverse-problem theorems are sophisticated but often nonconstructive.
  Making them computational is an open applied-math frontier.

This field is likely the most technically powerful source for a “light cone as propagation constraint” formalism.

## 6. Algebraic quantum field theory and factorization: cones as locality syntax

### 6.1 Region-to-algebra, not point-to-value

Algebraic quantum field theory replaces the assignment “point \(\mapsto\) field value” with “region \(\mapsto\) algebra of observables.” A typical AQFT assigns to each suitable spacetime region \(O\) an algebra \(\mathcal{A}(O)\), satisfying properties such as:

- **isotony**: if \(O_1\subseteq O_2\), then \(\mathcal{A}(O_1)\to\mathcal{A}(O_2)\);
- **Einstein causality**: if \(O_1\) and \(O_2\) are spacelike separated, their observables commute;
- **time-slice axiom**: data on a suitable Cauchy neighborhood determines the algebra of the larger domain;
- **local covariance**: embeddings of spacetimes induce compatible maps of algebras.

The light cone determines spacelike separation.
Therefore it determines the commutativity graph of the theory.
In AQFT, the cone becomes an algebraic rule.

### 6.2 Factorization algebra and causal factorization

Factorization algebras assign algebraic data to opens and encode how observables on disjoint opens multiply.
In Euclidean contexts, disjointness and inclusion organize the structure.
In Lorentzian contexts, causal order and time-orderability matter.

Benini, Musante, and Schenkel compare quantization of Lorentzian free BV theories in the languages of factorization algebras and AQFT, using retarded and advanced Green homotopies and distinguishing time-orderable prefactorization from algebraic quantum field theory [@benini-musante-schenkel-2024].
Dul develops causal factorization algebras for linearized gravity, fitting Lorentzian causal structure into categorical language and relating globally hyperbolic coverages to perturbative gravitational observables [@dul-2025].

This area is conceptually rich because it treats causal geometry as a kind of topology or coverage.
The relevant local patches are not merely open sets; they are causally meaningful regions.
Gluing is constrained by light cones.

### 6.3 Functorial field theory under AQFT

Bunk, MacManus, and Schenkel show that every AQFT has an underlying functorial field theory on a pseudo-category of globally hyperbolic Lorentzian bordisms [@bunk-macmanus-schenkel-2025].
This captures time evolution while not necessarily encoding all spatial locality.
That distinction is important.
Time evolution and spacelike locality are both light-cone-derived, but they are not the same algebraic datum.

Finberg’s work on covariance problems in AQFT highlights another frontier: local covariance, Einstein causality, and Bell-type correlations can pull against simple classical intuitions about common causes and screen-off conditions [@finberg-2025].
Thus the light cone supplies a locality grammar, but quantum theory makes that grammar nonclassical.

### 6.4 Open AQFT/factorization questions

1. **Gravity and background independence.** AQFT usually assumes a background spacetime.
  Perturbative gravity and locally covariant frameworks move beyond fixed backgrounds, but full background-independent algebraic locality remains difficult.

2. **Causal covers and descent.** What is the right Grothendieck topology or coverage for Lorentzian causality?
  Causal diamonds?
  Globally hyperbolic opens?
  Cauchy developments?
  Time-orderable families?

3. **Factorization vs commutation.** How exactly should factorization algebra structures and AQFT commutation structures be compared in interacting and gauge theories?

4. **Null boundaries.** Can AQFT/factorization structures be transported cleanly to null infinity, horizons, or celestial spheres?

5. **Quantum causal order.** Can causal cones themselves become quantum, indefinite, or noncommutative while preserving a usable locality axiom?

For your work, the major takeaway is that light cones can be formalized as the relation controlling which local algebras may be glued, multiplied, or required to commute.
That is a very direct bridge between causal geometry and sheaf/fiber algebra.

## 7. Causal sets and order-theoretic spacetime

### 7.1 Order plus number

Causal set theory proposes that spacetime is fundamentally a locally finite partially ordered set.
The order \(x\preceq y\) represents causal precedence.
Local finiteness says that intervals

\[ [x,y] = \{z : x\preceq z\preceq y\} \]

contain finitely many elements.
The guiding slogan is:

\[ \text{order} + \text{number} = \text{geometry}. \]

The order gives causal/conformal structure; counting elements gives volume.
Surya’s review emphasizes that causal sets are rooted in the Lorentzian fact that causal order captures much of spacetime geometry, while local finiteness supplies discreteness [@surya-2019].

This is the purest version of light-cone primacy.
There are no tangent-space cones at the fundamental level.
There are only relations of causal precedence.
The cone is the downset/upset pattern around an event.

### 7.2 Manifoldlikeness and the Hauptvermutung

The central difficulty is manifoldlikeness.
Most posets do not resemble Lorentzian manifolds.
A causal set intended to approximate a manifold is usually obtained by a random sprinkling into a spacetime, with order inherited from causal order.
The hard questions include:

- How can one recognize manifoldlike causal sets intrinsically?
- How can one estimate dimension, curvature, and topology from order?
- How unique is the continuum approximation?
- How does one define dynamics over causal sets?
- Can different manifolds give rise to macroscopically indistinguishable causal sets?

The causal set Hauptvermutung asks, roughly, whether a causal set can be well approximated by two macroscopically distinct spacetimes.
Recent metric reconstruction results, such as the causal metric hypothesis, are relevant because they strengthen the idea that causal data may determine more of geometry than expected [@hu-meyer-meyer-2025].

### 7.3 Discrete light cones and triangulations

Other discrete Lorentzian models use simplices, triangulations, or Regge-style discretizations.
Vertex causality in Lorentzian triangulations studies how light cones intersect simplicial building blocks and what causal types result [@asante-borgolte-2025].
The mathematical issue is that a smooth cone field, when discretized, becomes combinatorial incidence data: which vertices, edges, faces, or simplices lie in which causal relation?

This is another way the cone becomes order/combinatorics.

### 7.4 Open order-theoretic questions

1. **Causal completion and boundary.** What is the best boundary theory for causal posets and causal sets?
  Can null infinity be recovered order-theoretically?

2. **Sheaves on causal posets.** A causal set is a category: an arrow \(x\to y\) exists when \(x\preceq y\).
  Sheaves, cosheaves, persistence modules, and incidence algebras should be natural tools, but a standard “microlocal causal-set sheaf theory” is not yet mature.

3. **Wave propagation without differentiability.** How should one define a wave equation, Green function, or singularity propagation law on a causal set?

4. **Continuum recovery.** Which invariants of the poset recover dimension, topology, volume, and approximate metric?

5. **Dynamics.** What is the correct quantum dynamics of causal order itself?

For your purposes, causal sets are less about physics speculation and more about the categorical reduction of light cones: local cones integrate to a preorder; a preorder is a thin category; and geometry may be reconstructed from the incidence pattern of that category plus scale data.

## 8. Hyperbolicity cones and Lorentzian polynomials

### 8.1 From spacetime cones to algebraic cones

The standard Lorentz cone is a convex cone:

\[ L_{n+1} = \{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}: x_0 \geq \sqrt{x_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2}\}. \]

It is one sheet of the cone defined by the quadratic form

\[ x_0^2 - x_1^2 - \cdots - x_n^2 \geq 0. \]

Hyperbolicity cones generalize this.
A homogeneous polynomial \(p\) is hyperbolic with respect to a direction \(e\) if \(p(e)>0\) and for every \(x\), the univariate polynomial \(t\mapsto p(x+te)\) has only real roots.
The connected component of \(\{p\neq 0\}\) containing \(e\) is a hyperbolicity cone.
Güler showed that hyperbolic polynomials yield convex cones useful for interior point methods [@guler-1997].

This area is not about physical light propagation.
But it is about the same algebraic skeleton: a cone selected by a hyperbolic form, with a distinguished positive/time-like component.

### 8.2 Lorentzian polynomials and combinatorial Hodge theory

Brändén and Huh introduced Lorentzian polynomials as a class of homogeneous polynomials with deep log-concavity and Hodge-theoretic properties [@branden-huh-2020].
The name “Lorentzian” comes from the signature pattern: Hessians or associated quadratic forms have exactly one positive eigenvalue in the relevant region.
This generalizes the Lorentzian signature pattern from quadratic forms to higher-degree polynomial and combinatorial settings.

The theory connects:

- matroids;
- negative dependence;
- log-concavity;
- Hodge-Riemann relations;
- volume polynomials;
- stable polynomials;
- M-convexity;
- tropical geometry.

Brändén and Leake extend Lorentzian polynomial theory to cones, developing hereditary characterizations and proving Hodge-Riemann and Alexandrov-Fenchel-type results in a more general cone setting [@branden-leake-2025].
Baker, Huh, Kummer, and Lorscheid study the topology of spaces of Lorentzian polynomials over the triangular hyperfield, showing how the theory continues to spread through tropical and hyperfield mathematics [@baker-huh-kummer-lorscheid-2025].

Scheiderer’s work on smooth hyperbolicity cones and second-order cone representability is part of a parallel convex-algebraic frontier: classify which hyperbolicity cones admit tractable conic representations [@scheiderer-2025].

### 8.3 Why this belongs in a light-cone memo

This branch belongs here because it asks what remains of light-cone mathematics when “time-like” and “space-like” become abstract positivity signatures.

The pattern is:

\[ \text{one positive direction} \quad + \quad \text{many negative directions} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{rigid convex/combinatorial consequences}. \]

In spacetime, this signature creates causal cones.
In combinatorics, it creates log-concavity, Hodge-Riemann relations, and matroidal constraints.
In optimization, it creates hyperbolic programming cones.

So the word “Lorentzian” has become a transportable algebraic technology.
It no longer only names spacetime geometry; it names a signature regime that produces order, convexity, and rigidity.

### 8.4 Open algebraic questions

1. **Representability.** Which hyperbolicity cones are spectrahedral?
  Which are spectrahedral projections?
  Which are second-order cone representable?

2. **Computational complexity.** What are the efficient algorithms for optimization over generalized Lorentzian/hyperbolicity cones?

3. **Hodge-theoretic universality.** How far do Lorentzian polynomial methods extend in combinatorics and tropical geometry?

4. **Categorical meaning.** Is there a categorical account of “Lorentzian signature” that unifies spacetime cones, hyperbolic polynomials, and Hodge-Riemann forms?

5. **Bridge to causal geometry.** Can hyperbolicity-cone methods inform synthetic Lorentzian geometry, or are the analogies mostly formal?

For your work, this branch is valuable because it suggests that “light-cone-like” structure is not tied to spacetime.
It may be an algebraic signature pattern for constraint systems in general.

## 9. Twistor theory, null infinity, and celestial holography

### 9.1 Null rays as primary objects

Twistor theory begins from the insight that conformal and null structures may be more fundamental than metric points.
Instead of treating null geodesics as curves inside spacetime, one builds spaces whose points correspond to null rays, spinors, or complexified geometric data.

Ambitwistor theory pushes this further: ambitwistor space is, roughly, the space of complex null geodesics.
Mason and Skinner’s ambitwistor strings connect holomorphic maps to such spaces with scattering equations and amplitudes [@mason-skinner-2014].

This is light-cone mathematics in a different register.
The null cone is not just a causal boundary of timelike motion; it is the structure from which scattering and field data can be encoded.

### 9.2 Null infinity and celestial holography

Celestial holography studies flat-space quantum gravity by reorganizing scattering data at null infinity.
Massless particles reach null infinity and are labeled by points on a celestial sphere plus energy-like data.
The S-matrix can be recast in a basis adapted to Lorentz symmetry and null asymptotics [@zhu-2026].

This connects:

- asymptotic symmetries;
- BMS transformations;
- soft theorems;
- memory effects;
- celestial conformal field theory;
- twistor space;
- chiral algebras;
- Carrollian geometry;
- null boundary reconstruction.

Kmec, Mason, Ruzziconi, and Sharma relate celestial S-algebras to twistor space, Yang-Mills scattering, operator product expansions, radiative phase space at null infinity, and twisted holography [@kmec-mason-ruzziconi-sharma-2025].
Jarov develops higher-genus twistor spaces and celestial chiral algebras, extending the null/twistor toolkit into more elaborate geometric settings [@jarov-2025].

### 9.3 The frontier meaning of null infinity

In asymptotically flat spacetime, null infinity is where light rays end.
Thus it is the natural boundary for radiation, scattering, and massless degrees of freedom.
The frontier question is:

> Can bulk spacetime physics be reconstructed from data organized at null infinity?

This resembles the inverse-problem question, but at an asymptotic boundary rather than a finite observation boundary.
It also resembles AQFT/factorization: observables are reorganized by causal boundary structure.
And it resembles twistor geometry: null rays, not spacetime points, become primary.

### 9.4 Open null/twistor questions

1. **Mathematical definition of celestial CFT.** Celestial holography has many strong structural clues, but a fully rigorous nonperturbative mathematical object remains unclear.

2. **Bulk reconstruction.** How exactly does null-infinity data reconstruct bulk geometry, especially beyond self-dual or perturbative regimes?

3. **Gravity beyond toy sectors.** Self-dual Yang-Mills and self-dual gravity are mathematically tractable; full quantum gravity is harder.

4. **Twistor sheaves and categories.** Twistor theory is already sheaf-cohomological.
  Its relationship with modern factorization, derived geometry, and microlocal sheaves remains fertile.

5. **Null boundaries in low regularity.** How do celestial or twistor techniques adapt to nonsmooth spacetimes or synthetic Lorentzian geometry?

For your purposes, this branch suggests that light cones are not merely local.
Their null generators reach boundary structures that may encode the whole field theory.

## 10. A unifying mathematical schema

Here is the synthesis I would use as a working map:

\[ \begin{array}{c} \text{local cone} \\ \downarrow \\ \text{admissible curve / causal relation} \\ \downarrow \\ \text{causal diamond / region system} \\ \downarrow \\ \text{sheaf, algebra, PDE, or observation functor} \\ \downarrow \\ \text{global geometry / dynamics / reconstruction} \end{array} \]

The local cone determines which infinitesimal or local continuations are allowed.
Integrating those constraints yields causal curves and causal order.
Intersections of futures and pasts yield causal diamonds.
Diamonds become the natural local regions over which one builds sheaves, algebras, wave propagators, measurements, or discrete intervals.
Global structure is recovered by gluing those local data.

This leads to six meta-principles.

### Principle 1: the cone is more primitive than the metric in many problems

A Lorentzian metric gives a cone, but causal reconstruction suggests the cone can recover the conformal metric.
Lorentzian metric spaces suggest two-point time separation can recover topology and causality.
Causal set theory suggests order plus counting can recover geometry.
AQFT suggests cones control algebraic locality independent of pointwise fields.

### Principle 2: the cone is a propagation constraint

In PDE, singularities propagate along null bicharacteristics.
In sheaf theory, singular support constrains propagation of sections.
In AQFT, causal independence constrains algebraic commutation.
In inverse problems, wavefront propagation makes geometry observable.

### Principle 3: causal diamonds are Lorentzian balls

In Riemannian geometry, balls localize the metric.
In Lorentzian geometry, causal diamonds localize causal interaction.
The synthetic frontier increasingly treats diamonds as the natural compactness, convergence, and gluing units.

### Principle 4: null structure is the hard boundary

Timelike structure is often easier because it has positive time separation.
Null structure sits at \(\tau=0\) but is still causally related.
It is a boundary case analytically, geometrically, and categorically.
That is why null hypersurfaces, caustics, horizons, null infinity, and celestial spheres are all frontier objects.

### Principle 5: Lorentzian signature generalizes beyond spacetime

Hyperbolicity cones and Lorentzian polynomials show that “one positive direction” is a deep algebraic/combinatorial constraint.
The light cone is the geometric ancestor of a broader family of signature-controlled positivity structures.

### Principle 6: reconstruction is the new test of understanding

The strongest modern theorems do not merely describe light cones; they reconstruct hidden structure from them.
If a formalism can recover topology, conformal geometry, metric data, wave coefficients, connections, or algebras from cone-guided observations, it has captured something real.

## 11. How this connects to sheaves, fibers, and relational mathematics

The light-cone frontier is unusually friendly to sheaf-theoretic and categorical thinking.

A smooth cone field \(C\) assigns to each point a set of allowed directions.
This is already fiberwise structure:

\[ p \mapsto C_p \subset T_pM. \]

A causal relation integrates that field:

\[ p \leq q \iff \text{there exists a future causal curve from }p\text{ to }q. \]

The resulting preorder can be read as a thin category.
A sheaf or cosheaf on that category encodes how information restricts forward, backward, or across causal intervals.
A factorization algebra on spacetime regions encodes how local observables multiply or commute.
A microlocal sheaf with singular support in a causal cone encodes which discontinuities can propagate.

This suggests a general formal package:

1. **Base:** a causal site, poset, Lorentzian length space, or manifold.
2. **Fibers:** local state spaces, observables, claims, measurements, or propositions.
3. **Cone constraint:** admissible transitions or propagation directions.
4. **Gluing:** sheaf/cosheaf/factorization rules over causal diamonds.
5. **Dynamics:** PDE, rewriting, inference, game move, or algebraic evolution.
6. **Observation:** boundary restriction, null-infinity transform, or quotient projection.
7. **Reconstruction:** recover hidden local/global structure from observed restrictions.

In this language, a light cone is not an object in spacetime.
It is an operator on possible continuations.
It says: from here, these are the directions in which consistency, influence, or singularity may move.

That is why it belongs near modal logic.
The future cone of \(p\) is an accessibility relation.
But unlike ordinary Kripke accessibility, it carries geometry, convexity, smooth approximation, and analytic propagation.
It is a modal relation with differential and microlocal substance.

It also belongs near bilattice and paraconsistent thinking.
Spacelike separated regions are neither causally ordered one way nor the other.
Null-related regions are boundary-related.
Timelike-related regions are ordered.
The causal relation is not a total order, and its incomparabilities are meaningful rather than missing information.
This is a geometry where partiality is structural.

## 14. Concrete research directions to watch

### 14.1 Causal sites over Lorentzian length spaces

A promising mathematical object would be a site whose opens are causally meaningful regions: diamonds, globally hyperbolic neighborhoods, causal completions, or domains of dependence.
Covering families would not merely be ordinary topological covers; they would preserve causal development.
A sheaf on this site would encode data that glues causally.

The question:

> What Grothendieck topology is naturally generated by light cones?

This would connect synthetic Lorentzian geometry, AQFT, factorization algebras, and sheaf theory.

### 14.2 Causal microsupport

Microlocal sheaf theory already uses conic subsets of \(T^*M\).
Lorentzian geometry supplies physically meaningful cones in \(T^*M\).
A “causal microsupport” formalism would study sheaves whose singular support lies in future/past causal covectors.

The question:

> Can propagation of sheaf sections be constrained by Lorentzian cones in a way that recovers hyperbolic PDE propagation and AQFT locality?

This could unify PDE, sheaf theory, and field theory.

### 14.3 Light observation cosheaves

Boundary light observation sets form a functor from interior events to boundary subsets.
One could try to encode this as a cosheaf of observations or a nerve construction over observed light cuts.

The question:

> Can Lorentzian inverse problems be reformulated as reconstruction from a sheaf/cosheaf of light observations?

This would make inverse problems more categorical and potentially more computational.

### 14.4 Null boundary factorization

Celestial holography and AQFT both want algebraic structures at null boundaries.
Factorization algebras already encode local-to-global observables.
A null-boundary factorization theory would organize radiative observables by cuts of \(\mathscr{I}^+\), the celestial sphere, or Carrollian boundary geometry.

The question:

> What is the factorization algebra of null infinity?

### 14.5 Lorentzian polynomial semantics for constraint systems

Lorentzian polynomials show that Lorentzian signature produces powerful combinatorial rigidity.
It may be possible to use this as a semantics for systems with one “time-like” aggregation direction and many “space-like” variation directions.

The question:

> Can generalized Lorentzian signature classify consistency, propagation, or optimization constraints outside geometry?

This is speculative, but likely fertile for mathematical computing.

### 14.6 Causal set sheaf theory

A causal set is a poset and hence a category.
Sheaves on posets, incidence algebras, persistence modules, and derived category methods could organize local data over causal intervals.

The question:

> What is the right sheaf theory of locally finite causal orders?

A strong answer could connect causal set reconstruction, discrete PDE, and computational topology.

## 15. Strongest “frontier claims”

I would condense the memo into the following claims.

1. **Light cones are being promoted from derived metric features to primitive mathematical structure.**

2. **Lorentzian synthetic geometry is now a real frontier, not just a wishlist.** The 2024-2026 literature on Lorentzian metric spaces, Gromov-Hausdorff-type convergence, synthetic timelike Ricci bounds, null hypersurfaces, Cartan-Hadamard/splitting results, and total curvature shows rapid maturation.

3. **Null structure is the bottleneck.** Timelike order is comparatively manageable.
  Null geodesics, null hypersurfaces, caustics, horizons, and null infinity are where the hardest boundary phenomena live.

4. **Inverse problems turn light cones into sensors.** Boundary light observations and nonlinear wave interactions reconstruct hidden geometry by exploiting the routing and collision structure of null propagation.

5. **Microlocal analysis gives the sharpest analytic version of light-cone propagation.** Wavefront sets and characteristic varieties are the correct high-resolution language for “where information travels.”

6. **AQFT and factorization algebra turn light cones into algebraic locality.** Spacelike separation becomes commutation; causal development becomes time-slice equivalence; causal covers become descent data.

7. **Causal sets are the most radical order-first version.** They ask whether the cone-integrated order, plus counting, can replace the manifold.

8. **Lorentzian polynomials show that light-cone logic escapes spacetime.** The signature pattern behind light cones has become a tool in combinatorics, Hodge theory, and optimization.

9. **Twistor/celestial geometry makes null rays and null infinity primary.** Instead of using light cones inside spacetime, these programs encode physics on spaces of null directions or their asymptotic boundary.

10. **The unifying mathematical object is a directed local-to-global propagation system.** Whether the fibers are tangent vectors, covectors, algebras, observables, events, polynomials, or boundary cuts, the cone says which continuations are admissible.

## 16. Bottom line

The mathematical frontier of light cones is no longer merely “the geometry of null vectors in relativity.” It is a multi-field convergence around **directed admissibility**.

A light cone can be:

- the null boundary of a Lorentzian metric;
- the generator of causal order;
- a time-separation relation;
- a characteristic variety for waves;
- a singular-support constraint;
- a commutativity relation for quantum observables;
- a discrete reachability order;
- a convex hyperbolicity cone;
- a Lorentzian signature condition in combinatorics;
- a null asymptotic boundary for scattering.

That breadth is not accidental.
The light cone is one of the deepest mathematical devices for saying:

> here are the continuations that matter, here are the ones that are forbidden, here are the incomparable ones, and here is how local constraint becomes global structure.

For your own program, the most productive synthesis is probably:

\[ \textbf{light cone} = \textbf{fiberwise admissibility relation}

after gluing,

\[ \textbf{spacetime/order/algebra} = \textbf{global settlement of those local admissibilities}. \]

That reading places light cones directly beside sheaves, modal accessibility, predication frames, causal sites, and microlocal singular support.
It also gives a concrete research path: define the local cone-like constraint, define the fiber data it acts on, define the causal/gluing site, then study what global structure can be reconstructed from the resulting propagation system.
