# Research memo: the frontier in coloring aperiodic tiles **Date:** June 25, 2026 **Scope:** “Coloring” is used broadly here: graph colorings of tiling adjacency graphs; colored/decorated matching rules; Wang-tile edge colors; symbolic codings and Markov partitions; color-weighted control points for diffraction; and, briefly, physical/material realizations where “colors” become species, chirality, labels, or states.

## Executive synthesis

The frontier has shifted from “can this tiling be colored?” to “what information does a coloring encode?” In the older graph-theoretic sense, the field is still surprisingly young: exact vertex, edge, and face chromatic numbers are known only for a limited set of substitution tilings beyond classical Penrose cases.
The strongest systematic recent paper gives optimal vertex/edge/face colorings for the chair, Ammann–Beenker, rational pinwheel, and pinwheel tilings, with explicit algorithms rather than mere existence proofs.
([cdm.ucalgary.ca](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/76852/58532/271840))

The most active frontier is broader: colors as **finite labels that force or reveal hierarchy**.
This includes decorated matching rules, Wang tiles, symbolic dynamics, and coding constructions.
The newest notable development I found is Vereshchagin’s June 2026 preprint, which strengthens the Goodman-Strauss/Mozes tradition by proving broad sofic/matching-rule results for substitution and hierarchical tilings under a finite-local-complexity condition formulated via finitely many “crowns.” ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25005v1))

The Hat/Spectre revolution has also turned colorings into a practical coding language.
Smith’s 2024 rhombille construction recasts the Hat/Turtle/Spectre hierarchy as a coloring game on a rhombille tiling, while Labbé–Selinger’s April 2026 Markov-partition construction generates Hat tilings by superimposing a triangular grid on a specially colored image and reading tile orientations; conversely, every valid Hat tiling arises from that construction in their precise sense.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01911?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

For Wang tiles, the frontier is no longer just the minimal number of tiles.
Jeandel–Rao settled the celebrated 11-tile/4-color minimal aperiodic set problem, and newer work by Labbé and by Kari–Labbé–Mostert is building parameterized aperiodic Wang systems controlled by metallic means, toral dynamics, Markov partitions, and prescribed quadratic-irrational stripe densities.
([advancesincombinatorics.com](https://www.advancesincombinatorics.com/article/18614-an-aperiodic-set-of-11-wang-tiles))

Finally, in diffraction and long-range-order theory, “color” often means a weight assigned to tile types or control points.
Hat and Spectre tilings are now being analyzed as pure-point, cut-and-project-like systems with fractal windows and computable diffraction spectra; recent renormalization work aims to compute such spectra with high precision.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05639?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## 1. Terminology: five meanings of “coloring”

In this research area, “coloring” can mean at least five different things.

**Proper graph coloring.** Vertices, edges, tiles, or faces are colored so adjacent objects differ.
This is closest to the Four Color Theorem and Vizing’s theorem, but for infinite tiling graphs with substitution or quasiperiodic structure.

**Decorative or matching-rule coloring.** Tiles are given colors, labels, arrows, edge markings, or side decorations that force local compatibility.
These colors are not necessarily visible in the final undecorated tiling; they can encode hidden hierarchy.

**Wang-tile edge coloring.** Square tiles carry colored edges, and adjacent edge colors must match.
This is the classic symbolic-dynamics formulation of local constraints and aperiodicity.

**Coding/Markov-partition coloring.** A colored partition of some ambient symbolic, toral, or geometric system is used to generate tilings.
Here colors are states in a dynamical coding.

**Spectral/diffraction coloring.** Tile types or control points receive weights, often complex or real “scattering strengths,” and one studies how those weights affect diffraction, dynamical spectrum, or long-range order.

These meanings overlap.
A decorated matching rule can be translated into Wang tiles; a Wang shift may be a symbolic cover of a substitution tiling; a color-weighted control-point set can reveal or suppress spectral information; and a proper graph coloring can sometimes be viewed as a factor map from the tiling dynamical system to a finite shift.

## 2. Proper graph colorings of aperiodic tilings

### State of the art

The central paper here is **Evans, Gawlak, Ramsey, Strungaru, and Trang, “Colourings of aperiodic tilings.”** It gives explicit optimal vertex, edge, and face colorings for several substitution tilings: chair, Ammann–Beenker, rational pinwheel, and pinwheel.
The authors frame the problem as determining the chromatic number, chromatic index, and face-coloring number of infinite graphs associated with aperiodic tilings.
They note that general upper bounds are available from the Four Color Theorem for planar graph colorings and from Vizing-type edge-coloring theory, but exact optimality is much more delicate.
([cdm.ucalgary.ca](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/76852/58532/271840))

Their exact values are:

| Tiling | Vertex chromatic number | Edge chromatic number | Face/tile chromatic number |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Chair | 2 | 4 | 3 |
| Ammann–Beenker | 3 | 8 | 2 |
| Rational pinwheel | 3 | 8 | 3 |
| Pinwheel | 3 | 8 | 3 |

The paper’s importance is not just the numbers; it gives explicit coloring algorithms and explains why different tiling geometries require different mechanisms.
For example, the pinwheel case is harder for edge coloring because irrational rotations prevent the simpler direction-by-direction method that works in more rigid settings.
([cdm.ucalgary.ca](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/76852/58532/271840))

For the Hat/Spectre family, the most explicit colorings I found are still community or computational rather than a single definitive journal treatment of exact chromatic numbers.
A MathOverflow construction colors reflected Hat tiles with a fourth color and then 3-colors a remaining hexagonal-like quotient; for Spectre-like cases, a related construction colors a special class of tiles and 3-colors the rest.
Simon Tatham’s implementation notes give concrete algorithms for generating and four-coloring Hat/Spectre patches, but these should be treated as technical notes/software documentation rather than peer-reviewed theorems.
([mathoverflow.net](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/443377/how-can-one-construct-a-four-coloring-of-a-tiling-of-the-plane-with-smith-myers))

### Frontier questions

The obvious frontier is to turn the Hat/Spectre coloring folklore into a formal, optimal theory.
The questions are:

**Are four colors always necessary for Hat and Spectre tilings under the natural tile-adjacency graph?** Finite Hat patches obstruct 3-colorability in known examples, but the clean research target is a published theorem specifying the graph model, adjacency convention, and exact chromatic number for every valid tiling or every hull element.

**Can proper colorings be made local, canonical, or mutually locally derivable?** A coloring exists by compactness or by a global construction may not be locally derivable from the tiling.
For aperiodic tilings, the distinction between “there exists a coloring” and “there is a finite-radius coloring rule” is central.

**Can chromatic invariants distinguish tiling spaces?** The raw chromatic number is crude, but the space of all proper colorings, the minimal radius of a local coloring rule, or the entropy of compatible colorings may distinguish substitution tilings that share simpler invariants.

**Can there be a general substitution-tiling coloring theorem?** For primitive finite-local-complexity substitutions, one might hope to compute vertex/edge/face chromatic numbers from supertile collar data.
The Evans et al. paper is a model for this, but the general algorithmic decidability and complexity picture is not yet settled.

**What happens in non-FLC or infinite-orientation cases?** Pinwheel tilings already show that infinitely many orientations complicate edge coloring.
The rational pinwheel/pinwheel comparison suggests a broader project: isolate exactly which coloring questions depend on finite local complexity, finite orientation, or substitution recognizability.

## 3. Coloring as construction: Hat, Turtle, Spectre, and Markov partitions

### State of the art

The Hat/Spectre discoveries created a new wave of constructive colorings.
The original monotile work by Smith, Myers, Kaplan, and Goodman-Strauss exhibited the Hat family and then a strictly chiral Spectre monotile, using hierarchical substitution structures to prove aperiodicity.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10798?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

James Smith’s 2024 paper **“Turtles, Hats and Spectres: Aperiodic structures on a Rhombic tiling”** reframes these monotiles through rhombi with matching rules.
Its abstract describes a “colouring game on a Rhombille tiling,” gives a simpler substitution-based proof of aperiodicity, connects Turtle tilings with a Fibonacci/Sturmian-like construction, and gives new substitution rules for Spectres.
This is important because it shows that the Hat/Spectre hierarchy can be moved from polygon geometry into a colored combinatorial substrate.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01911?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The newest major construction I found is **Labbé and Selinger, “A construction of the hat tilings by a Markov partition,”** posted in April 2026.
They construct Hat tilings by placing a triangular grid over a specially colored image and reading off tile orientations.
Their abstract states both directions: the construction produces valid Hat tilings, and every valid Hat tiling is obtainable in the appropriate sense.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20964))

### Why this matters

This is not merely a prettier way to draw Hats.
It suggests that Hat tilings may be understood as symbolic codings of a relatively simple dynamical object, analogous to how Sturmian sequences code irrational circle rotations or how model sets code toral/cut-and-project dynamics.

The frontier is therefore moving toward a dictionary:

| Geometric object | Colored/coded object |
|---|---|
| Hat/Turtle/Spectre tile | state, orientation, or color symbol |
| metatile hierarchy | substitution or automaton |
| local matching rule | finite-type constraint |
| tiling hull | symbolic dynamical system or toral factor |
| tile orientation | readout from colored partition |
| aperiodicity | nonperiodicity/minimality of coded dynamics |

### Frontier questions

**Can the Markov-partition construction be extended from Hats to Spectres and the full monotile family?** The Hat family and Spectre family are related but not identical; the Spectre is strictly chiral and has its own substitution structure.
A unified Markov-partition or symbolic-coding construction would be a major simplification.

**What is the minimal symbolic alphabet?** The existing constructions use colors, orientations, metatile labels, or partition regions.
A natural problem is to minimize the number of symbols while preserving recognizability and local derivability.

**Are these color codings mutually locally derivable from the original tilings?** Some codings may be local decorations; others may require global choices.
Determining which constructions are MLD, finite-to-one factors, almost-one-to-one factors, or nonlocal covers is a key dynamical question.

**Can colored-image or Markov-partition methods generate random, finite, or boundary-controlled patches efficiently?** This matters both for computation and for physical design.
If one can sample valid finite patches with controlled boundary conditions, one can test coloring conjectures, diffraction, defects, and material models.

**Can the Hat/Spectre codings be placed in the same formal family as Sturmian, cut-and-project, and Wang-tile codings?** Smith’s “2D Sturmian” analogy and Labbé–Selinger’s Markov partition point in this direction.
A precise equivalence would unify several currently separate languages.

## 4. Colored matching rules and sofic substitution tilings

### State of the art

The classical theorem behind this area is Goodman-Strauss’s result that, under suitable conditions, substitution tilings can be forced by finite local matching rules.
In practice, “matching rules” often mean adding colors, arrows, notches, edge labels, or collared decorations to prototiles so that local legal adjacency forces global hierarchical structure.
Goodman-Strauss’s 1998 paper remains the foundational reference.
([annals.math.princeton.edu](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/12903?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The current frontier is sharpening the hypotheses and making the construction more general and transparent.
Vereshchagin’s 2025 **“Goodman-Strauss theorem revisited”** revisits the older theorem, emphasizing explicit conditions and tricks such as taking powers of substitutions or grouping tiles.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02842?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

More importantly, Vereshchagin’s June 2026 preprint **“Matching Rules for Substitution and Hierarchical Tilings for any Substitution with Finite Local Complexity”** states a broad new theorem: for every finite-local-complexity substitution, both substitution tilings and hierarchical tilings are sofic, meaning they can be obtained from decorated tilings satisfying finite local rules and then forgetting the decorations.
The paper explicitly treats decorations as colors and side decorations as matching colors along shared edges.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25005v1))

### Conceptual shift

The old question was:

> Given a substitution tiling, can local rules force it?

The newer question is:

> How much finite colored information is needed to force, recognize, or cover the hierarchy?

That changes the emphasis from existence to complexity.
A theorem may say finite colors suffice, but the research frontier asks how many colors, how local, how canonical, how computable, and how robust under deformation.

### Frontier questions

**Minimal decorated matching rules.** Given a substitution, what is the smallest number of colors, edge labels, or decorated prototiles that force its tiling space?
This is a direct analogue of minimal Wang-tile problems.

**Algorithmic synthesis.** Can one input a substitution rule and automatically output finite matching rules with provable correctness and reasonably small complexity?

**Collaring versus coloring.** One standard method is to collar tiles by local neighborhoods.
This often works but can explode the alphabet.
The frontier is to replace brute-force collars with smaller semantic colors that encode just enough hierarchy.

**Beyond finite local complexity.** Vereshchagin’s 2026 theorem is powerful precisely for FLC substitutions.
Pinwheel-like infinite-orientation tilings and non-FLC systems remain harder.
Understanding what kind of colored matching rules survive without FLC is a natural next step.

**Undecorated enforcement.** The Hat and Spectre are exciting because their geometry itself enforces aperiodicity.
A deep problem is to characterize when colors are essential and when shape alone can encode the same information.

**Sofic covers and invariants.** Once a tiling space is represented as a factor of a decorated finite-type system, the cover is not unique.
Comparing minimal covers, canonical covers, and cohomological/dynamical invariants of covers is becoming important, especially alongside newer work on covers of tiling spaces.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14276?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## 5. Wang tiles and edge-color symbolic dynamics

### State of the art

Wang tiles are square tiles with colored edges, placed without rotations or reflections, with adjacent edge colors required to match.
This model is foundational because it connects tilings with symbolic dynamics, computation, undecidability, and local constraints.
Jeandel and Rao’s 2021 result is the modern benchmark: they constructed an aperiodic set of 11 Wang tiles with 4 colors and proved that no aperiodic Wang set can have at most 10 tiles or at most 3 colors.
([advancesincombinatorics.com](https://www.advancesincombinatorics.com/article/18614-an-aperiodic-set-of-11-wang-tiles))

Since that minimality result, the frontier has broadened toward structured infinite families and dynamical classification.

Labbé’s **metallic mean Wang tiles** build a family \(T_n\) with \((n+3)^2\) tiles.
The associated Wang shifts are self-similar, aperiodic, and minimal, with inflation factor the positive root of \(x^2 - nx - 1\); the \(n=1\) case is equivalent to Ammann’s 16 Wang tiles.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03652))

Labbé’s second metallic-mean paper develops a more dynamical picture: the tiles behave like “computer chips” carrying integer-vector input/output data, and the Wang shifts admit toral descriptions via Markov partitions and almost one-to-one factor maps to a two-dimensional torus.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03197))

The newest result I found is Kari–Labbé–Mostert’s June 2026 preprint **“An aperiodic set of Wang tiles for every quadratic irrational.”** It gives a nonperiodicity criterion using vertical and horizontal stripe constraints, proves nonperiodicity via irrational stripe densities, and constructs finite aperiodic Wang-tile sets for irrational pairs in the same quadratic number field.
It also applies the method to Penrose encodings and metallic-mean Wang tiles.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24693))

### Frontier questions

**From isolated examples to parameterized arithmetic families.** The new work suggests that one can prescribe arithmetic data—metallic means, quadratic irrationals, toral translations, stripe densities—and build finite colored tile systems realizing it.

**Tile-count and color-count optimization.** Jeandel–Rao solved the absolute minimum for aperiodicity, but for structured families the optimization question reappears: given a target slope, inflation, or toral system, what is the smallest Wang alphabet?

**Classifying Wang shifts by dynamical invariants.** Minimality, entropy, nonexpansive directions, toral factors, eigenvalues, and substitution structure are replacing “aperiodic or not?” as the main invariants.
Labbé’s work on metallic means and Jeandel–Rao-related systems is part of this shift.
([aimsciences.org](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/dcds.
2023046?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Bridging Wang tiles and geometric monotiles.** One can encode Penrose tilings, substitution tilings, or monotile hierarchies into Wang systems, but the encodings may introduce many colors or lose geometric transparency.
Finding small, canonical Wang encodings of Hat/Spectre-like systems is a natural frontier problem.

**Decidability with structure.** The unrestricted Wang tiling problem is undecidable, but many structured subclasses are partially decidable or classifiable.
A current research direction is to identify which color constraints make tiling spaces computationally tractable without trivializing them.

**Statistical mechanics and random tilings.** Once Wang systems are viewed as shifts of finite type, one can ask about entropy, Gibbs measures, defects, and phase transitions.
This connects aperiodic coloring with probability and physics rather than only exact deterministic tilings.

## 6. Long-range order, diffraction, and color-weighted tilings

### State of the art

In diffraction theory, “coloring” often means assigning weights to tile types or marked control points.
Different weightings can reveal, hide, or split Bragg peaks, change extinction rules, and distinguish local derivability classes.
This is a different but highly relevant meaning of color: color becomes an observable.

For the Hat family, Baake, Gähler, and Sadun showed that the family admits a four-dimensional space of shape deformations, that the corresponding tiling spaces are topologically conjugate, and that a self-similar CAP representative has pure-point dynamical spectrum and a cut-and-project description with two-dimensional internal space.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05639?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

For Spectres, Baake, Gähler, Mazáč, and Sadun analyze long-range order in a complex two-dimensional family.
Their work gives pure-point dynamical spectrum, topologically conjugate dynamics up to rescaling/rotation, a \(4{:}2\) cut-and-project description, and Rauzy-fractal windows.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15503?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Baake, Gähler, Mazáč, and Mitchell then compute diffraction for Hat, Spectre, and related tilings using model-set representatives and reprojections.
The key point for coloring is that diffraction depends on the chosen control points and weights; exact formulas require understanding fractal windows and renormalization in internal space.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03268?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

A June 2026 renormalization paper by Baake, Gähler, Klick, Mañibo, and Mazáč pushes this computational frontier further.
Its abstract describes exact renormalization techniques for inflation systems, including computation of window covariograms and diffraction patterns for the new monotile tilings with arbitrary precision.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19645?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Frontier questions

**Color-dependent diffraction.** For a multi-type tiling, different color weights can produce very different observed spectra.
A major practical problem is to characterize all possible diffraction measures under all physically meaningful weightings.

**Extinctions and hidden order.** Some colorings may cancel Bragg peaks; others may reveal hidden eigenvalues.
This is central for comparing mathematical spectra with experimental scattering.

**Inverse problems.** Given diffraction data, can one infer the coloring, tile weights, or hidden decoration?
This is difficult because different colored tilings can be homometric, meaning they share diffraction.

**Relation to proper graph colorings.** A proper tile coloring produces a colored point set.
Does the graph-theoretic coloring have recognizable spectral signatures?
Can chromatic constraints be detected from diffraction?
This seems underexplored.

**MLD classification and chirality.** Spectre tilings are especially interesting because chirality, reflection classes, and shape deformation interact with local derivability and spectral data.
New invariants such as orbit separation dimension, tiling-space covers, and asymptotic-composant methods are being developed to distinguish systems beyond cohomology alone.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03541?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## 7. Physical and materials frontier

This is less “coloring theory” in the strict combinatorial sense, but it is increasingly relevant because physical tiles often carry species, chirality, orientation, or binding-state labels.

A 2025 Nature Communications paper reports an aperiodic chiral tiling formed by molecular self-assembly on a silver surface.
The authors observe a dense chiral triangular tiling with random mirror-isomers and defects; they emphasize that the observed tilings are aperiodic, while also noting that whether the molecular constraints strictly enforce aperiodicity remains subtle.
([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55405-5))

Another emerging direction is electronic/topological physics on aperiodic tilings.
A 2025 preprint on a family of aperiodic tilings with tunable quantum geometric tensor argues that real-space aperiodic geometry can tune topological phases and disorder-driven topological Anderson behavior.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13304?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Frontier questions

**Can colored matching rules be physically implemented?** In molecular or metamaterial settings, colors become binding sites, chirality, charges, connector types, or molecular conformations.
The research challenge is to design local interactions that enforce a desired aperiodic hull.

**Can one engineer defects rather than merely tolerate them?** Mathematical aperiodicity often assumes perfect legal tilings.
Physical systems have defects.
A frontier question is whether colored aperiodic rules can produce useful controlled defect statistics.

**Can spectral colorings predict material response?** If tile colors correspond to atomic species, resonators, hopping amplitudes, or refractive indices, then mathematical color weights become physical observables.
This links diffraction theory, electronic spectra, and combinatorial coloring.

## 8. Cross-cutting research program

The most promising research directions sit between the subfields rather than inside one of them.

### A. Formalize Hat/Spectre graph coloring

A clean project would define several adjacency graphs for Hat and Spectre tilings—edge-adjacency of tiles, vertex-adjacency of control points, edge graph of the tiling skeleton—and prove exact chromatic numbers for the entire hull.
The community constructions suggest four colors for natural tile-adjacency colorings, but a fully published proof package would need optimality, existence, locality, and compatibility with substitution hierarchy.
([mathoverflow.net](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/443377/how-can-one-construct-a-four-coloring-of-a-tiling-of-the-plane-with-smith-myers))

### B. Build an algorithm from substitution to optimal coloring data

Evans et al. show how rich explicit algorithms can be for individual examples.
A next step is software that takes a primitive substitution tiling, computes collared approximants, searches for proper colorings, proves lower bounds via forced patches, and outputs local rules when they exist.
([cdm.ucalgary.ca](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/76852/58532/271840))

### C. Compare three encodings of the same tiling

For Hat/Spectre-like systems, one may have:

1. a substitution/metatile description,
2. a Markov-partition or colored-image coding,
3. a Wang-tile or finite-type cover.

A major unification problem is to prove which of these are conjugate, MLD, finite-to-one factors, or merely related by nonlocal maps.
Labbé–Selinger’s Markov partition and Vereshchagin-style sofic covers make this comparison newly feasible.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20964))

### D. Minimize colors under different notions of equivalence

“Fewest colors” means different things in different categories:

| Problem | Minimize |
|---|---|
| Proper coloring | number of graph colors |
| Matching rules | decorated tile types or side colors |
| Wang systems | tile count and edge-color count |
| Markov coding | partition atoms or symbolic alphabet |
| Diffraction | number of weighted species needed to distinguish order |

A serious theory would compare these minima.
For example, a tiling might need many decorated matching-rule colors but only two diffraction weights to reveal its pure-point spectrum.

### E. Use spectral invariants to study colorings

Given a colored tiling dynamical system, one can ask whether the coloring changes the maximal equicontinuous factor, eigenvalue group, diffraction, cohomology, or orbit separation dimension.
This would connect graph coloring and matching-rule coloring to the modern spectral analysis of Hat/Spectre tilings.
([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05639?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### F. Develop inverse and experimental pipelines

The applied version is: given an image, STM scan, diffraction pattern, or finite patch, infer the hidden coloring or hierarchy.
This would combine computer vision, symbolic dynamics, finite patch enumeration, diffraction, and local-rule inference.

## 9. High-value open problems

Here are the problems I would put at the top of a frontier memo.

1. **Exact Hat/Spectre chromatic numbers.** Prove optimal vertex, edge, and face/tile chromatic numbers for the Hat and Spectre hulls under standard adjacency conventions.

2. **Local versus global proper colorings.** Determine when a proper coloring of an aperiodic tiling can be chosen as a local derivation from the uncolored tiling.

3. **Minimal decorated matching rules for FLC substitutions.** Vereshchagin-type existence is powerful; the next problem is minimizing and computing the decorations.
  ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25005v1))

4. **Markov partitions for Spectres.** Extend the Labbé–Selinger Hat construction to Spectre tilings and compare it with known substitution and cut-and-project descriptions.
  ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20964))

5. **Canonical Wang encodings of monotiles.** Build small Wang-tile systems for Hat/Spectre tilings that preserve hierarchy, chirality, and local derivability as cleanly as possible.

6. **Arithmetic classification of Wang shifts.** Extend metallic-mean and quadratic-irrational constructions to a broader arithmetic taxonomy of aperiodic Wang systems.
  ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03652))

7. **Color-weighted diffraction classification.** For Hat/Spectre systems, classify diffraction measures as tile weights vary, including extinction phenomena and homometry.

8. **Coloring and cohomology.** Relate proper colorings and decorated matching-rule covers to Čech cohomology, tiling-space covers, and newer invariants such as orbit separation dimension.
  ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03541?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

9. **Non-FLC coloring theory.** Develop matching-rule and proper-coloring methods for infinite-orientation systems such as pinwheel-type tilings.

10. **Physical enforcement of colored aperiodicity.** Translate colored local rules into molecular, colloidal, photonic, or mechanical interactions and prove when the physical rules actually enforce aperiodicity rather than merely favoring it.
  ([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55405-5))
