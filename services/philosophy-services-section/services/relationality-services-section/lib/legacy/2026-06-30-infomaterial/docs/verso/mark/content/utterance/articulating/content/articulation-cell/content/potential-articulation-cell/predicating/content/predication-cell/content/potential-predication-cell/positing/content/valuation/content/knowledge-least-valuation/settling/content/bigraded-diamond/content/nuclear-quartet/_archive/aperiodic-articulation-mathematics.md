The **aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-category** is the category generated by [commutating](../../commutating.md). Objects are [commutative nuclear squares](../../../library/aperiodic-articulation-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-articulation-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/hacking/library/variety/library/lattice/library/closure-operator/library/nucleus/library/commutative-nuclear-square/commutative-nuclear-square.md) $(A, \sigma, \Delta)$ — Heyting algebras equipped with two commuting nuclei.

## Objects

Each object $(A, \sigma, \Delta)$ carries the **four-corner structure**:

|                            | $\Delta$-quiescent              | $\Delta$-free        |
|----------------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------|
| **$\sigma$-quiescent**     | $A^* = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ | $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$ |
| **$\sigma$-free**          | $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$           | $A$                  |

The four corners are connected by the nucleus-arrows: moving from $A$ to $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$ applies $\sigma$; moving to $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ applies $\Delta$; reaching $A^*$ applies both (in either order, by commutativity). The joint projection $\pi = \sigma \circ \Delta = \Delta \circ \sigma$ is itself a nucleus with $\mathrm{Fix}(\pi) = A^*$.

## Morphisms

A morphism $(A, \sigma_A, \Delta_A) \to (A', \sigma_{A'}, \Delta_{A'})$ is a Heyting algebra homomorphism $f : A \to A'$ satisfying both nucleus-preservation conditions:

$$
f \circ \sigma_A = \sigma_{A'} \circ f, \qquad f \circ \Delta_A = \Delta_{A'} \circ f.
$$

Equivalently, $f$ commutes with both nuclei. This forces $f(A^*_A) \subseteq A^*_{A'}$ and similar containments for the individual fixed-point sub-algebras.

## The four corner-cells

Each corner of the quartet is a Heyting sub-algebra of $A$ derived from the nucleus structure. Internally, the four-corner structure manifests as the four cells inside this category's `library/`:

- `library/free-corner/` — the carrier $A$ (no quiescence imposed). Maps to the **potential** phase-cell of articulation.
- `library/saturation-quiescent-corner/` — $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$. Maps to the **active** phase-cell.
- `library/transfer-quiescent-corner/` — $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$. Maps to the **stable** phase-cell.
- `library/joint-quiescent-corner/` — $A^*$. Maps to the **absurd** phase-cell. This is the [aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell](library/joint-quiescent-corner/library/aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell/aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell.md).

## Aperiodicity is a theorem

Global aperiodicity is not a geometric hypothesis on this category — it is a consequence of fiberwise variation of the nuclear pair across the indexing history category $T$. The **nucleus-aperiodicity theorem** (held at `docs/dev/theorems/`) proves: when the nuclei $(\sigma_t, \Delta_t)$ vary across fibers in a way that faithfully encodes each history's distinct relational past, global aperiodicity follows. A constant nuclear pair would satisfy all other coherence axioms but fail the Axiom of History Separation: any automorphism of $T$ would be invisible to $H^*$.

Concretely: the saturation and transfer nuclei are the internal witnesses that each history is genuinely different from every other, at every scale.

## Connection to the saturation–transfer dyad

Each object of this category is the doubly-equipped form of a Heyting algebra: it has both a [saturating-nuclear-dyad-category](../../../saturating/library/saturating-nuclear-dyad-category/saturating-nuclear-dyad-category.md) presentation (forgetting $\Delta$) and a [transferring-nuclear-dyad-category](../../../transferring/library/transferring-nuclear-dyad-category/transferring-nuclear-dyad-category.md) presentation (forgetting $\sigma$). The quartet-category is the pullback of these two dyad-presentations under the commutativity axiom.

## Pairwise instance

The fiber Heyting algebras $H_t$ over a history category yield specific quartet objects $(H_t, \sigma_t, \Delta_t)$, one per history. The aggregate functor $H : T^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{nucl}}$ lands in the aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-category, sending each history to its nuclear-quartet fiber.

## Regime shadows

The full quartet structure manifests at the aperiodic regime. Lower regimes carry shadows in increasingly degenerate forms:

- **Transfinite-bounded (predicating)**: nuclear square — the (A, σ, Δ) structure survives but fiberwise variation is bounded below $\omega_1$, weakening History Separation. Held inside `library/joint-quiescent-corner/library/aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell/predicating/...`
- **Scott-continuous (positing)**: further collapse — possibly $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) = \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ at the $\omega$-limit, indistinguishing the off-diagonal corners.
- **Strict-finite (settling)**: most degenerate — nuclei become identity; quartet collapses to underlying algebra.

**Commutating** is the morphism that combines [saturating](../saturating/saturating.md) and [transferring](../transferring/transferring.md) on a shared Heyting algebra carrier under the commutativity axiom

$$
\sigma \circ \Delta \;=\; \Delta \circ \sigma.
$$

The output is a [commutative nuclear square](../../library/aperiodic-articulation-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-articulation-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/hacking/library/variety/library/lattice/library/closure-operator/library/nucleus/library/commutative-nuclear-square/commutative-nuclear-square.md) $(A, \sigma, \Delta)$, the carrier of an [aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-cell](library/aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-category/library/aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-cell/aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-cell.md).

## What commutating does

The two nuclei $\sigma$ (saturating, backward-facing) and $\Delta$ (transferring, forward-facing) act on the same Heyting algebra $A$. Without commutativity, the composite $\sigma \circ \Delta$ need not be a nucleus: it may fail idempotence. With commutativity, the composite $\pi = \sigma \circ \Delta = \Delta \circ \sigma$ is itself a nucleus, and $\mathrm{Fix}(\pi) = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta) =: A^*$. (Proof at [commutative-nuclear-square](../../library/aperiodic-articulation-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-articulation-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/hacking/library/variety/library/lattice/library/closure-operator/library/nucleus/library/commutative-nuclear-square/commutative-nuclear-square.md).)

The commutativity axiom asserts: the path $\sigma \circ \Delta$ (transfer from future, then saturate to past) and the path $\Delta \circ \sigma$ (saturate to past, then transfer from future) reach the same doubly-quiescent result.

## The saturation–transfer dyad

The pair $(\sigma, \Delta)$ subject to commutativity is the **saturation–transfer dyad**, which commutating constitutes. Both nuclei are inflationary ($a \leq \sigma(a)$, $a \leq \Delta(a)$). The opposition is not between inflation and deflation but between two directions of expansion:

|              | Nucleus | Looks toward                | Fixed points |
|--------------|---------|-----------------------------|---------------|
| Saturation   | $\sigma$ | Sub-histories (the past)   | Saturation-stable |
| Transfer     | $\Delta$ | Extensions (the future)    | Transfer-stable   |

The convergence

$$
A^* \;:=\; \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)
$$

is the **doubly quiescent layer**: elements both saturation-stable (the past has nothing further to add) and transfer-stable (the future has already sent everything back). Non-trivial: contains at least $\top$ and $\bot$, and is itself a Heyting sub-algebra of $A$ closed under both nuclei.

## Why commutativity is axiomatic

Commutativity is not derivable from σ and Δ alone. Given two arbitrary nuclei on the same algebra, their composite may fail idempotence (see commutative-nuclear-square's discussion of the four-corner coherence). The commutativity axiom

$$
\sigma \circ \Delta = \Delta \circ \sigma
$$

is what guarantees:
1. $\pi$ is a nucleus.
2. $\mathrm{Fix}(\pi) = A^*$ is itself a Heyting algebra.
3. The four corners $(A, \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma), \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta), A^*)$ cohere as a commuting square of Heyting sub-algebras.

It is the fiber-level instance of the coherence condition required for the nuclear-quartet to make sense as a single derived object.

## At the empty history

For $t = \epsilon$ (the empty history), no proper sub-histories exist, so $\sigma_\epsilon = $ constant $\top$ (everything saturates to $\top$). No independent steps yet exist either, so $\Delta_\epsilon = \mathrm{id}$. The commutativity condition $\sigma_\epsilon \circ \Delta_\epsilon = \Delta_\epsilon \circ \sigma_\epsilon$ reduces to $\sigma_\epsilon = \sigma_\epsilon$, holding trivially. The doubly-quiescent layer is $\{\top_\epsilon\}$.

## Generated category

The category generated by commutating is the [aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-category](library/aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-category/aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-category.md), whose objects are commutative nuclear squares $(A, \sigma, \Delta)$ and whose morphisms are Heyting algebra homomorphisms preserving both nuclei. Each object has the four-corner structure $(A, \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma), \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta), A^*)$ as derived structure; the joint quiescent corner $A^*$ is the [aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell](library/aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-category/library/aperiodic-nuclear-quartet-cell/library/aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell/aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell.md) of that object.

## Connection to articulation phases

Under commutating, the four corners of the resulting commutative nuclear square correspond to the four phase-cells of articulation:

| Corner               | Sub-algebra | Phase-cell |
|----------------------|-------------|------------|
| both axes free       | $A$ | potential  |
| $\sigma$-quiescent   | $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$ | active |
| $\Delta$-quiescent   | $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ | stable |
| both quiescent       | $A^*$ | absurd |

The Chu-shape `(token-or-void, claim-or-void)` partition of articulation is the same 2×2 as the `(σ-quiescent?, Δ-quiescent?)` partition of the nuclear quartet. Commutating is what makes this correspondence coherent: without commutativity, the "absurd corner" wouldn't be a well-defined Heyting algebra.

The **aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell** is the [joint-quiescent corner](../../joint-quiescent-corner.md) $H^* = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ of a [commutative nuclear square](../../../../../../library/aperiodic-articulation-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-articulation-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/hacking/library/variety/library/lattice/library/closure-operator/library/nucleus/library/commutative-nuclear-square/commutative-nuclear-square.md) $(A, \sigma, \Delta)$, taken together with $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ themselves as part of the cell's content.

## Definition

An aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell is the triple

$$
\bigl(\,H^*,\; \sigma,\; \Delta\,\bigr)
$$

where:

- $H^* = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta) \subseteq A$ is the doubly-quiescent sub-Heyting-algebra (the cell's invariant **organization**).
- $\sigma, \Delta : A \to A$ are the saturation and transfer nuclei whose joint fixed-point set is $H^*$ (the cell's producing-**processes**).
- The nuclei $\sigma, \Delta$ are themselves **part of the cell's content**: $\sigma, \Delta \in H^*$ in an appropriate enriched sense (the cell carries its own producing-machinery).

The extension over [joint-quiescent-corner](../../joint-quiescent-corner.md) is the inclusion of $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ as internal data: the cell knows how it was produced.

## Self-production: organization vs. structure

The aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell distinguishes two layers:

- **Organization** (invariant): the relation-structure of $H^*$ — the Heyting algebra operations, the nucleus-fixed-point conditions, the lattice-ordered structure of doubly-quiescent elements. This does not change across the cell's lifetime.
- **Structure** (continuously replaced): the specific elements populating $H^*$ at each history $t$. These turn over as the nuclear pair $(\sigma_t, \Delta_t)$ varies fiberwise.

The cell is self-producing because: every element $a \in H^*_t$ is produced by the action of $\pi_t = \sigma_t \circ \Delta_t$ on some $a' \in A$, and $\pi_t$ itself is part of the cell's content. The cell makes itself by applying its own internal closures.

## Operational closure

The cell's operations refer to its own states: both $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ are endomaps of $A$ that act non-trivially only on the complement of $H^*$ (they are identity on $H^*$ itself). Each element of $H^*$ is the image, under $\pi$, of elements in $A \setminus H^*$ — and $\pi$ is constructed from $\sigma$ and $\Delta$, which are themselves in $H^*$.

There is no external operator: the cell's closure is generated entirely from data internal to the cell.

## Structural coupling

The cell is structurally coupled to its environment through fiberwise variation. At each history $t$, the nuclei $(\sigma_t, \Delta_t)$ have their specific shape — the saturation closure depends on what sub-histories $t_0 < t$ exist, and the transfer closure depends on what independent extensions $s \perp t$ exist. Across histories, these vary; the cell's content at history $t$ depends on what relational past the cell has accumulated.

The environment perturbs the cell: a new history $t'$ contributes new restrictions $\rho_{t,t'}$ and new extensions, altering $(\sigma_{t'}, \Delta_{t'})$. The cell interprets the perturbation internally — through its own nuclei — and the new $H^*_{t'}$ is what the cell produces from that perturbation, not what the perturbation directly imposes.

## Aperiodicity

The family $(H^*_t)_{t \in T}$ across the history category $T$ is **aperiodic** at the $\kappa \geq \omega_1$ regime: the specific structure $H^*_t$ never settles (the elements turn over across histories) even though the organization (the Heyting algebra shape) stays invariant.

Aperiodicity is a theorem-consequence, not an axiom. The [nucleus-aperiodicity theorem](../../../docs/dev/theorems/nucleus-aperiodicity.md) (held at the parent quartet-category's `docs/dev/theorems/`) proves that fiberwise variation of $(\sigma_t, \Delta_t)$ — when faithful — forces global aperiodicity via the Axiom of History Separation.

## Maturana-Varela autopoiesis identified

The four signature properties of autopoiesis (Maturana–Varela 1972) are:

| Maturana-Varela property | Identification |
|---|---|
| Continuous self-production of components and processes | $\pi$ produces $H^*$ elements; $\sigma, \Delta$ are in $H^*$ |
| Organization invariant; structure continuously replaced | Heyting structure of $H^*$ invariant; fiberwise variation of elements |
| Operationally closed | $\sigma, \Delta$ act on $A$; closure refers only to internal data |
| Structurally coupled with environment | Fiberwise variation interprets perturbations through internal nuclei |

The biological cell as paradigm: $H^*_t$ is the cell's instantaneous propositional state at history $t$; $\sigma_t$ and $\Delta_t$ are the metabolic processes maintaining it; the cell at $t'$ has the same organizational structure but different specific propositions.

## Regime shadows

The aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell carries shadows at the lower three regimes, each more degenerate:

- **Transfinite-bounded (predicating)**: nuclear square — the (A, σ, Δ) structure survives but fiberwise variation is bounded below $\omega_1$. Held inside `library/potential-aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/...`
- **Scott-continuous (positing)**: nuclear-dyad shadow — possibly $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) = \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ at the $\omega$-limit. The semiotic-universe-least-fixed-point lives at this regime.
- **Strict-finite (settling)**: nuclear-collapse shadow — nuclei become identity. Textbook closure-operator theory recovers as this fully-collapsed shadow.

The **joint-quiescent corner** of a [commutative nuclear square](../../../../library/aperiodic-articulation-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-articulation-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/hacking/library/variety/library/lattice/library/closure-operator/library/nucleus/library/commutative-nuclear-square/commutative-nuclear-square.md) $(A, \sigma, \Delta)$ is the joint fixed-point sub-algebra

$$
A^* \;:=\; \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta) \;=\; \mathrm{Fix}(\pi).
$$

Elements here are simultaneously saturation-stable (past has nothing further to add) and transfer-stable (future has already sent everything back). The doubly quiescent layer.

## Position in the quartet

| | $\Delta$-**quiescent** | $\Delta$-free |
|---|---|---|
| $\sigma$-**quiescent** | **$A^*$** | $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$ |
| $\sigma$-free | $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ | $A$ |

The joint-quiescent corner is the top-left: both nuclei have reached their fixed-points; neither can push further.

## $A^*$ is a Heyting algebra

$A^*$ is a sub-Heyting-algebra of $A$, closed under all Heyting operations, because it is the fixed-point set of the joint nucleus $\pi = \sigma \circ \Delta = \Delta \circ \sigma$. Both inclusions $A^* \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \hookrightarrow A$ and $A^* \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta) \hookrightarrow A$ have left adjoints. Non-trivial: contains at least $\top$ and $\bot$.

## The canonical projection

The joint projection $\pi : A \to A^*$ is the canonical retraction:

$$
\pi(a) \;=\; \min\bigl\{b \in A^* \mid b \geq a\bigr\}.
$$

$\pi$ is itself a nucleus on $A$ (the composite of two commuting nuclei). For $a \notin A^*$, $\pi(a) > a$ strictly — $A^*$ is reached by genuine collapse, not reorganization.

## Correspondence to absurd

The joint-quiescent corner corresponds to the **absurd** phase-cell of articulation: both token-axis (σ) and claim-axis (Δ) articulated. Chu-shape pairing reads `(token, claim)`. Both axes are saturated; the system is self-maintaining under both closure-orientations.

## The aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell

The joint-quiescent corner contains the [aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell](library/aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell/aperiodic-autopoiesis-cell.md) — the cell where the system's articulating-process is part of its content, and where both saturating and transferring have nothing left to add. The fiberwise variation of $(A^*_t)$ across histories — when the nuclear pair $(\sigma_t, \Delta_t)$ varies in a way that faithfully encodes each history's distinct relational past — is what makes the joint quiescent corner globally aperiodic (nucleus-aperiodicity theorem).

In the fiber instance $(H_t, \sigma_t, \Delta_t)$, the joint quiescent corner is $H_t^*$ — the fixed fiber at each history. The fixed-fiber assignment $t \mapsto H_t^*$ is the central invariant that aperiodicity guards.

| | $\Delta$-quiescent | $\Delta$-**free** |

## Structure

For any $a \in A$ that is not already in $A^*$, at least one of $\sigma(a) > a$ or $\Delta(a) > a$ holds. The free corner contains the entire algebra including elements where the nuclear closures have non-trivial work remaining.

## Reflections to the other corners

The three other corners receive $A$ under nucleus-application:

$$
A \xrightarrow{\sigma} \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma), \qquad
A \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta), \qquad
A \xrightarrow{\pi = \sigma \circ \Delta} A^*.
$$

Each nucleus is left adjoint to the corresponding inclusion, making the corner a reflective sub-algebra.

## Correspondence to potential

The free corner corresponds to the **potential** phase-cell of articulation: neither token-axis ($\sigma$) nor claim-axis ($\Delta$) is yet articulated. The Chu-shape pairing reads `(void, void)`. The substrate's room-to-work cell, where downstream regimes (predicating, positing, settling) take place.

The **saturation-quiescent corner** of a [commutative nuclear square](../../../../library/aperiodic-articulation-cell/library/potential-aperiodic-articulation-cell/predicating/library/transfinite-predication-category/hacking/library/variety/library/lattice/library/closure-operator/library/nucleus/library/commutative-nuclear-square/commutative-nuclear-square.md) $(A, \sigma, \Delta)$ is the σ-fixed sub-algebra

$$
\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \;=\; \{a \in A : \sigma(a) = a\}.
$$

Elements here are **saturation-stable**: the past has nothing further to add. They are the largest elements of $A$ with their restriction profiles — fully determined by what they look like from every shorter history.

## Position in the quartet

| | $\Delta$-quiescent | $\Delta$-**free** |
|---|---|---|
| $\sigma$-**quiescent** | $A^*$ | **$\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$** |
| $\sigma$-free | $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta)$ | $A$ |

The σ-quiescent corner is the top-right: σ has reached its fixed-point; Δ may still move elements upward.

## Sub-Heyting-algebra structure

$\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$ is a sub-Heyting-algebra of $A$ (closed under $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\Rightarrow$, $\bot$, $\top$). The inclusion $\iota : \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \hookrightarrow A$ has left adjoint $\sigma : A \to \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$, giving a reflective adjunction.

The transfer nucleus $\Delta$ restricts to $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$: by commutativity $\sigma \circ \Delta = \Delta \circ \sigma$, so $\Delta(\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)) \subseteq \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$. Within this sub-algebra, $\Delta$ continues to act non-trivially toward the joint fixed-point.

## Further reduction to the joint corner

Applying $\Delta$ to elements of $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$ reduces further to the joint-quiescent corner:

$$
\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \xrightarrow{\Delta} A^* = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta).
$$

This is the second leg of the canonical retraction $A \to \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma) \to A^*$ (which composes to $\pi$).

## Correspondence to active

The σ-quiescent corner corresponds to the **active** phase-cell of articulation: token-axis articulated (σ has settled), claim-axis still free. Chu-shape pairing reads `(token, void)`. The saying has stabilized; the said is not yet committed.

This is the other leg of the canonical retraction: $A \to \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta) \to A^*$ (which composes to $\pi = \Delta \circ \sigma = \sigma \circ \Delta$).

## Correspondence to stable

The Δ-quiescent corner corresponds to the **stable** phase-cell of articulation: claim-axis articulated (Δ has settled), token-axis still free. Chu-shape pairing reads `(void, claim)`. The said is committed across all futures; the saying has not yet stabilized at this corner alone.

# Settlement

Fix a base `(L, C, E, λ)` — a residuated lattice `L`, a predicate-category `C`, an entity set `E`, and a settling-bound `λ` (an ordinal).

A **settlement** over `(L, C, E, λ)` is a family

$$v \;=\; (v_\tau)_{\tau < \lambda}$$

in which each `v_τ` is a predication-valuation (a function assigning to each predicate `p` a value `v_τ(p) ∈ A_p` in the predication-fiber-algebra), and the family satisfies the **settling-monotonicity** condition: for all `σ ≤ τ < λ` and every `p`,

$$v_\sigma(p) \;\leq_k\; v_\tau(p)$$

pointwise in `A_p`.

## What it IS

A settlement is the **orbit** of a settling-endofunctor `F_U` starting from some initial valuation `v_0`:

$$v \;=\; \bigl(F_U^\tau(v_0)\bigr)_{\tau < \lambda}.$$

At each successor step, `F_U` applies the closure-2-monad (saturating under derivable consequences) and then merges in the next external evidence from the increment-sequence `(u_τ)_{τ<λ}`. At each limit step (when settling is continuous), the value is the directed knowledge-order join of values at preceding steps.

The settlement therefore packages four pieces of structure into one indexed family:

1. The initial valuation `v_0`.
2. The closure-operator `cl = (cl_p)_p` driving inflation.
3. The increment-sequence `(u_τ)_τ` driving external evidence injection.
4. The settling-bound `λ` capping the index.

## Why "settlement" — terminology correction

The archive vocabulary was `predication-settling`. That name conflates two distinct regimes: "predication" (the object at predicating tier — what predicating generates) and "settling" (the morphism at settling tier — what produces this object). In the four-object grammar, the orbit produced by settling is the regime-correct object **settlement**.

Each of the four primitive morphisms generates one named object at its own regime:

- articulating generates **articulation** (at aperiodic-articulation-category).
- predicating generates **predication** (at transfinite-predication-category).
- positing generates **posit** (at scott-continuous-posit-category).
- settling generates **settlement** (at finite-settlement-algebra) — this $thing.

The archive's `predication-settling` mixed an upstream object name with the downstream morphism name. Translate it to `settlement` in current grammar.

## Lives at settling tier — and only fully here

A settlement is the *settled* form of an orbit: a settling that has reached quiescence at some `τ*` and remains identical to its `τ*`-value through the rest of `λ`. In the strict-finite regime (`κ < ω`), every settlement quiesces in finitely many steps and is a finished, computable object.

In higher regimes, the *orbit shape* exists but quiescence may not be reached within the regime's ordinal bound:

- **Scott-continuous regime** (`κ = ω`): the orbit is a posit at scott-continuous-posit-category. Quiescence at ω.
- **Transfinite-bounded regime** (`ω < κ < ω₁`): the orbit is a predication at transfinite-predication-category. Quiescence at some countable ordinal.
- **Aperiodic regime** (`κ ≥ ω₁`): the orbit is an articulation at aperiodic-articulation-category. No countable quiescence; the orbit is genuinely aperiodic.

The four-regime tower is exactly the stratification of orbits by *when settling quiesces*. A `settlement` is what an orbit becomes once it has reached `κ < ω` — the strict-finite endpoint of the stratification.

## Cross-tier identity

Operationally, a single ongoing orbit spans all four tiers:

| Tier | What the orbit IS at this tier |
|---|---|
| articulating | the open articulation — no closure guaranteed |
| predicating | the indexed family of predication-valuations |
| positing | the closure-rule-system + increment-sequence that drives it |
| settling | the settlement — the settled identity of the orbit |

So while the **object** named `settlement` lives at settling tier, the underlying *orbit* it identifies spans the chain. Lower regimes' orbits are not yet settlements; they become settlements when they finish.

## Worked example: a strict-finite settlement

Take `L = 2` (Boolean), `C` a single-predicate category with `p`, `E = {a, b}`, `λ = 3`. Initial `v_0(p)(a, b) = ⊥`. Increment-sequence: `u_1(p)(a, b) = t`, `u_2(p)(a, b) = ⊥`. Closure: identity.

- Step 0: `v_0(p)(a, b) = ⊥`.
- Step 1: `v_1 = cl(v_0 ∨_k u_1) = id(⊥ ∨_k t) = t`.
- Step 2: `v_2 = cl(v_1 ∨_k u_2) = id(t ∨_k ⊥) = t`.

Quiescence at `τ* = 1`. Closure ordinal `κ = 1 < ω`. This is a finite-step settlement with carrier value `t` (told positive) at cell `(a, p, b)`.

## Worked example: a higher-regime orbit (not yet a settlement)

Same base, but increment-sequence supplies `u_n(p)(a_n, b_n) = t` for new entity pairs `(a_n, b_n)` at each step `n`, indefinitely. Closure: identity.

- Each `v_n` has more cells set to `t` than its predecessor.
- The orbit grows monotonically but never quiesces (every step adds new content).
- Closure ordinal `κ = ω` (Scott-continuous).
- This is a **posit**, not a settlement: the orbit is at scott-continuous-posit-category, not at finite-settlement-algebra.

The orbit becomes a settlement only if `E` is finite and the increments eventually stop introducing new entities — at that point closure reduces to finite-step.
