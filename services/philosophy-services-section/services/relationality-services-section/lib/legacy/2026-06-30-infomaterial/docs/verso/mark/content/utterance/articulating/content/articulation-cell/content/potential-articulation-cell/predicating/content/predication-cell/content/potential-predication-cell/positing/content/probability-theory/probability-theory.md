## What this node is

Probability theory is generated by the **posit** morphism — the third stage of articulate → predicate → posit → settle, where value enters predication by evidence. A bare predication says only *that* an event holds; a probability says *with what standing*, and here that standing is a number in [0, 1]. A probability is a valuation: a measure that assigns graded charge to events of a sample space, with the certain event weighted 1, the impossible event 0, and disjoint events adding. It is positing made quantitative — evidence admitted not as one bit but as a [0,1]-valued degree.

## What it generates / contains

No subconcept directories exist under `content/` yet. The body, drawn from the material in `_archive/`, covers:

- **Sample spaces and events** — outcomes Ω, events as subsets forming a σ-algebra, and the probability measure on them (countable additivity).
- **Measure** — the general notion that grounds probability, integration, and statistics: a function from sets to the reals preserving zero and additivity.
- **Distributions** — probability mass functions for discrete variables and density functions for continuous ones; random variables and their patterns of value.
- **Conditional probability and Bayes** — P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B), prior updated by likelihood into posterior; the foundation of Bayesian inference.
- **Independence** of events and variables.
- **Descriptive statistics** — mean, median, mode, variance, and standard deviation as summaries of a distribution.
- **Sampling, bias, and inference** — populations and samples, how biased sampling misrepresents a population, and the basics of statistical inference.
