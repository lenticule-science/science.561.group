## What this node is

A **lattice** is an [order](../../order.md) in which every pair of elements has
both a **meet** $a \wedge b$ — their greatest lower bound, the largest element
$\le$ both — and a **join** $a \vee b$ — their least upper bound, the smallest
element $\ge$ both. It is order with the one thing added that lets any two
elements be combined in each direction; nothing more.

Each bound is unique when it exists, by [order](../../order.md)'s antisymmetry: two
greatest lower bounds are each $\le$ the other, hence equal. An order in which only
meets exist is a meet-semilattice; only joins, a join-semilattice; both, a lattice.

## Generated from order

Order gives the relation $\le$; the lattice adds the demand that the two bounds
*exist for every pair*. This is the refinement [settling](../../../../../../settling.md)
already needs: the fixed point settling reaches is a **join** (a least upper
bound of the graded chain), so the value structure it ascends is at least a
lattice. Meet and join are the operations that distinguish a lattice from a bare
order — they are this node's constitutive structure.

## The four laws, and order recovered

As algebra, $\wedge$ and $\vee$ each satisfy **commutativity**, **associativity**,
and **idempotence**, and the two are tied by **absorption**:
$$a \wedge (a \vee b) = a, \qquad a \vee (a \wedge b) = a.$$
Absorption is what makes the two operations one structure rather than two: combining
an element with something above it and meeting back recovers the element. From the
operations the order returns —
$$a \le b \iff a \wedge b = a \iff a \vee b = b,$$
so the order-theoretic and algebraic descriptions of a lattice are the same object.
Meet is conjunction / intersection, join is disjunction / union; the two are dual,
every meet-statement becoming a join-statement under reversal of $\le$.

## What it generates

Refining the lattice in turn:

- **bounded** — a top $\top$ (join of everything) and bottom $\bot$ (meet of
  everything);
- **complete** — meets and joins of *all* subsets, not only pairs; this is where
  the closure's fixed point lives, by Knaster–Tarski, and completeness for joins
  already forces it for meets;
- **distributive** — meet distributes over join, $a \wedge (b \vee c) = (a \wedge
  b) \vee (a \wedge c)$;
- **Heyting** — a bounded distributive lattice carrying an implication $a \to b$
  right-adjoint to $a \wedge -$ (the residuation $c \le a \to b \iff c \wedge a
  \le b$); its failure of $\lnot\lnot a = a$ is the order-side projection of
  Curry–Howard and the reason the corpus's truth is constructive rather than
  Boolean.

The [bilattice](../../../../../../../../../../pre-bilattice/content/bilattice/bilattice.md)
is two such lattices crossed on one carrier; each of its two axes is a lattice of
exactly this kind.
