## What this node is

A **fixpoint** of a map $f$ is a value $x$ with $f(x) = x$ — a point the map sends
to itself. On a [complete lattice](../../complete-lattice.md) the existence of
fixpoints is not a hypothesis but a theorem: the **Knaster–Tarski theorem** states
that every *monotone* map $f$ has both a least and a greatest fixed point, and that
the **least** one is
$$\mathrm{lfp}(f) = \bigwedge \{\, x : f(x) \le x \,\},$$
the [meet](../../../meet/meet.md) of all *pre-fixpoints* (the $x$ that $f$ does not
push upward). The meet exists because the lattice is complete, and it is itself a
fixed point — this is the whole content of the theorem, and the reason completeness
was the refinement worth reaching.

## Generated from the complete lattice

A fixpoint as such can be named on any map, but a fixpoint *guaranteed to exist and
to be least* requires exactly two things: a monotone map, and a
[complete lattice](../../complete-lattice.md) for the defining meet of pre-fixpoints
to land in. Completeness is the generating structure — strip it and the meet need
not exist, and the least fixed point can fail to be present. So this node is derived
under complete-lattice, not under the bare [lattice](../../../../lattice.md): the
complete lattice is precisely the setting Knaster–Tarski needs.

This is the destination of [settling](../../../../../../../../../../settling.md) itself. Settling
iterates the monotone closure $\text{possibilize} \circ \text{necessitate}$ on the
complete lattice of valuations, ascending from $\bot$ through the graded chain; the
value it converges to is the **least fixed point** of that closure — the smallest
valuation that the closure no longer changes. Everything settling is — monotone
ascent to a least upper bound — is summarized in the statement that it computes
$\mathrm{lfp}$ of its closure operator. The order, the lattice, the bounds, and the
completeness were all derived to make *this* point exist and be reachable.

## What it generates

The least fixed point is where settling's quiescence and its closure ordinal meet:

- the ascent from $\bot$ reaches $\mathrm{lfp}$ by transfinite iteration, and the
  stage at which it stabilizes is the closure ordinal — the *regime* signature
  (strict-finite, Scott-continuous $\kappa=\omega$, transfinite-bounded, aperiodic
  $\kappa \ge \omega_1$) classifies how far the iteration must run;
- on the four-valued
  [bilattice](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../pre-bilattice/content/bilattice/bilattice.md)
  the knowledge order has finite height, so the least fixed point is reached in at
  most $2n$ steps for $n$ assertions — the closure makes all derivable knowledge
  explicit and resolves every in-scope $\bot$.

## Orientation

Generated by [complete lattice](../../complete-lattice.md), and through it by
[lattice](../../../../lattice.md), [order](../../../../../../order.md), and
[settling](../../../../../../../../../../settling.md), whose least-upper-bound destination
this node *is*. The regimes that classify how far the iteration to the fixed point
runs are derived in settling's own children.
