# Diátaxis is a bilattice

### The documentation quartet as the Belnap–Ginsberg four-valued structure


## Abstract

The Diátaxis framework (Procida) classifies technical documentation into four
types — *tutorials*, *how-to guides*, *explanation*, and *reference* — using two binary
axes. We observe that these four types, together with their two axes, carry the
structure of the smallest nontrivial bilattice: the four-element Belnap–Dunn space
$\mathbf{4}$ studied by Belnap (1977), Ginsberg (1988), and Fitting (1991). We make
this precise. There is a canonical bijection between the Diátaxis types and
$\mathbf{4}$ that identifies Procida's two axes with the two coordinates of
$\mathbf{4} = \mathbf{2}\times\mathbf{2}$; under it, the two lattice orders of the
bilattice (the knowledge order and the truth order) and its Klein four-group of
symmetries (generated by *negation* and *conflation*) transport to documentation. The
transported structure is invisible in the usual flat $2\times2$ presentation: it
*grades* the four types — tutorial below how-to and explanation below reference, in the
knowledge order — and singles out the two *diagonals* of the square,
tutorial↔reference and how-to↔explanation, as the structure-preserving involutions,
while the two axis reflections are *not* symmetries. We then argue, drawing on the
philosophy of skill (Ryle; Anderson) and on cognitive-load theory (Sweller; Kalyuga),
that the two transported orders coincide with two gradients already named in the
learning sciences, and we give falsifiable predictions distinguishing the bilattice
model of documentation from the flat one. The mathematical core (Theorem 1) has been
verified in the Lean 4 proof assistant.

## 1. Introduction

Diátaxis [1] is a widely adopted framework for organising technical documentation. It
holds that there are exactly four kinds of documentation, each serving a distinct user
need: *tutorials* (learning-oriented lessons), *how-to guides* (goal-oriented
procedures), *reference* (information-oriented description), and *explanation*
(understanding-oriented discussion). Procida arranges these on a compass with two axes:
a **purpose** axis separating material the user studies (*acquisition*) from material
the user works with (*application*), and a **content** axis separating practical
*action* from theoretical *cognition*.

A $2\times2$ classification is, in the first instance, just a four-element set with two
cross-cutting binary partitions. Our point is that the Diátaxis four-element set is not
a bare $2\times2$: it is naturally the carrier of a well-studied algebraic object, the
four-element **bilattice** $\mathbf{4}$ of Belnap and Dunn [2, 3], organised as in
Ginsberg [4] and Fitting [5, 6]. The bilattice carries strictly more structure than the
flat classification: two partial *orders* rather than two partitions, and a
four-element *symmetry group* acting by order-dualities. Transporting that structure to
documentation yields statements about documentation that the flat picture cannot
express.

Section 2 recalls $\mathbf{4}$ and its symmetry group, with proofs, so the paper is
self-contained. Section 3 fixes the Diátaxis tetrad as a mathematical object. Section 4
states and proves the correspondence (Theorem 1). Sections 5–6 give the interpretation
and the empirical predictions; these are kept clearly separate from the mathematics.
Section 7 relates the result to other four-fold models of learning and to the broader
role of $\mathbf{4}$.

## 2. The bilattice $\mathbf{4}$

Let $\mathbf{2} = \{0,1\}$ with its usual order $0 \le 1$, meet $\wedge$, join $\vee$,
and Boolean complement $\overline{0}=1$, $\overline{1}=0$.

**Definition 1 (the four-element bilattice).**
Let $\mathbf{4} = \mathbf{2}\times\mathbf{2}$, the four pairs

$$N=(0,0),\qquad T=(1,0),\qquad F=(0,1),\qquad B=(1,1).$$

Read a pair $(a,b)$ as recording $a$ units of evidence *for* a claim and $b$ units of
evidence *against* it, so that $N$ is "none", $T$ "true only", $F$ "false only", and
$B$ "both". Define two relations on $\mathbf{4}$:

$$(a,b) \le_k (a',b') \iff a\le a' \text{ and } b\le b',$$
$$(a,b) \le_t (a',b') \iff a\le a' \text{ and } b'\le b.$$

We call $\le_k$ the **knowledge** (information) order and $\le_t$ the **truth** order.

This is Ginsberg's product construction $\mathbf{2}\odot\mathbf{2}$ [4]; $\mathbf{4}$ is
the canonical example of a distributive (indeed interlaced) bilattice [5, 7].

**Proposition 1.**
$(\mathbf{4}, \le_k)$ and $(\mathbf{4}, \le_t)$ are bounded lattices. In $\le_k$, $N$ is
the least and $B$ the greatest element, and $T, F$ are incomparable. In $\le_t$, $F$ is
the least and $T$ the greatest element, and $N, B$ are incomparable.

*Proof.* $\le_k$ is the product order on $\mathbf{2}\times\mathbf{2}$, hence a bounded
lattice with least $(0,0)=N$ and greatest $(1,1)=B$; $T=(1,0)$ and $F=(0,1)$ are
incomparable because $1\le 0$ fails in the relevant coordinate either way. For $\le_t$,
the map $(a,b)\mapsto(a,\overline b)$ is an order isomorphism
$(\mathbf{4},\le_t)\to(\mathbf{4},\le_k)$ — it turns the contravariant second coordinate
into a covariant one — carrying $F\mapsto N$, $T\mapsto B$, $N\mapsto T$, $B\mapsto F$;
transporting the previous statement gives least $F$, greatest $T$, and incomparable
$N, B$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Definition 2 (negation and conflation).**
Define $\lnot,\ {\sim}\colon \mathbf{4}\to\mathbf{4}$ by

$$\lnot(a,b) = (b,a), \qquad {\sim}(a,b) = (\overline{b},\overline{a}).$$

$\lnot$ is the **negation** and ${\sim}$ the **conflation** of the bilattice [6].

**Proposition 2.**
$\lnot$ and ${\sim}$ are involutions. $\lnot$ preserves $\le_k$ and reverses $\le_t$;
${\sim}$ preserves $\le_t$ and reverses $\le_k$; the composite
$\lnot\,{\sim} = {\sim}\,\lnot\colon (a,b)\mapsto(\overline a,\overline b)$ reverses
both. The set

$$G = \{\ \mathrm{id},\ \lnot,\ {\sim},\ \lnot\,{\sim}\ \}$$

is a subgroup of the symmetric group on $\mathbf{4}$ isomorphic to the Klein four-group
$\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$. Moreover $\lnot$ fixes $N$ and $B$ and transposes
$T$ and $F$, while ${\sim}$ fixes $T$ and $F$ and transposes $N$ and $B$.

*Proof.* $\lnot\lnot(a,b)=(a,b)$ and
${\sim}{\sim}(a,b)=(\overline{\overline a},\overline{\overline b})=(a,b)$, so both are
involutions. For monotonicity, read off Definition 1: $(a,b)\le_k(a',b')$ iff
$a\le a',\,b\le b'$ iff $b\le b',\,a\le a'$ iff $\lnot(a,b)\le_k\lnot(a',b')$, so $\lnot$
preserves $\le_k$; and $(a,b)\le_t(a',b')$ iff $a\le a',\,b'\le b$ iff
$\lnot(a',b')\le_t\lnot(a,b)$, so $\lnot$ reverses $\le_t$. The two facts for ${\sim}$
are symmetric, using $x\le y \iff \overline y \le \overline x$. Composing,
$\lnot\,{\sim}(a,b)=\lnot(\overline b,\overline a)=(\overline a,\overline b)$, which
reverses each coordinate's order, hence reverses both $\le_k$ and $\le_t$; the same
value is obtained as ${\sim}\,\lnot$, so the generators commute. Each of
$\lnot, {\sim}, \lnot{\sim}$ has order $2$, and the group they generate has order $4$
with no element of order $4$, so $G\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2$. Finally
$\lnot(a,b)=(a,b)\iff a=b$, giving fixed points $N, B$ and transposition of $T, F$; and
${\sim}(a,b)=(a,b)\iff a=\overline b$, giving fixed points $T, F$ and transposition of
$N, B$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

The last sentence of Proposition 2 is the structurally important point: the canonical
symmetries of $\mathbf{4}$ are *not* the two coordinate negations
$(a,b)\mapsto(\overline a, b)$ and $(a,b)\mapsto(a,\overline b)$ — which are neither
monotone nor antitone for either order — but the two maps $\lnot, {\sim}$, each of which
fixes one antipodal pair and transposes the other. We will see these are the two
*diagonals* of the documentation square.

## 3. The Diátaxis tetrad

**Definition 3 (Diátaxis tetrad).**
The **Diátaxis tetrad** is the four-element set

$$D = \{\ \text{tutorial},\ \text{how-to},\ \text{explanation},\ \text{reference}\ \}$$

equipped with two binary partitions [1]:

- the **purpose** partition $\pi$, separating *acquisition* (study) from *application*
  (work):
  $$\text{acquisition} = \{\text{tutorial},\text{explanation}\},\qquad
    \text{application} = \{\text{how-to},\text{reference}\};$$
- the **content** partition $\gamma$, separating *action* (practical steps) from
  *cognition* (theoretical knowledge):
  $$\text{action} = \{\text{tutorial},\text{how-to}\},\qquad
    \text{cognition} = \{\text{explanation},\text{reference}\}.$$

The two partitions are *independent*: each of the four $\pi$-by-$\gamma$ cells contains
exactly one type. Coordinatise by encoding *application* as $1$ (acquisition $0$) and
*cognition* as $1$ (action $0$):

$$\varphi\colon D\to\mathbf{2}\times\mathbf{2},\qquad
  \varphi(d) = \big(\,[\,d\in\text{application}\,],\ [\,d\in\text{cognition}\,]\,\big),
  \tag{1}$$

where $[\,\cdot\,]$ is the Iverson bracket. Explicitly,

| type | $\varphi$ | element of $\mathbf 4$ | purpose | content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tutorial | $(0,0)$ | $N$ | acquisition | action |
| how-to | $(1,0)$ | $T$ | application | action |
| explanation | $(0,1)$ | $F$ | acquisition | cognition |
| reference | $(1,1)$ | $B$ | application | cognition |

## 4. The correspondence

**Theorem 1.**
The map $\varphi$ of (1) is a bijection $D\to\mathbf{4}$ with the following properties.

- **(i)** Procida's purpose and content partitions are the two coordinate projections of
  $\mathbf{4}$: $\pi$ is the fibration $(a,b)\mapsto a$, and $\gamma$ is
  $(a,b)\mapsto b$.
- **(ii)** Pulling back $\le_k$ and $\le_t$ along $\varphi$ equips $D$ with two partial
  orders. In the knowledge order, *tutorial* is least and *reference* greatest, with
  *how-to* and *explanation* the two incomparable middles; in the truth order,
  *explanation* is least and *how-to* greatest, with *tutorial* and *reference*
  incomparable.
- **(iii)** Pulling back the symmetry group $G$, the negation $\lnot$ becomes the
  involution that transposes *how-to* and *explanation* while fixing *tutorial* and
  *reference* (the anti-diagonal of the square), and the conflation ${\sim}$ becomes the
  involution that transposes *tutorial* and *reference* while fixing *how-to* and
  *explanation* (the main diagonal). These two diagonals generate a Klein four-group of
  order-dualities of $D$; neither axis reflection (which fixes one partition and swaps
  the blocks of the other) belongs to it.
- **(iv)** No single axis linearly orders $D$, but the knowledge order grades it up to
  one antichain: $\text{tutorial} <_k \text{how-to},\,\text{explanation} <_k
  \text{reference}$.

*Proof.* $\varphi$ has four distinct values, hence is a bijection onto the four-element
set $\mathbf{4}$. **(i)** is immediate from (1): the first coordinate of $\varphi(d)$ is
the indicator of the application block of $\pi$, the second the indicator of the
cognition block of $\gamma$. **(ii)** Transport Proposition 1 along $\varphi$:
$\varphi(\text{tutorial})=N$ is $\le_k$-least and $\varphi(\text{reference})=B$
greatest, with $\varphi(\text{how-to})=T$ and $\varphi(\text{explanation})=F$ the
incomparable pair; while $\varphi(\text{explanation})=F$ is $\le_t$-least,
$\varphi(\text{how-to})=T$ greatest, and $\varphi(\text{tutorial})=N$,
$\varphi(\text{reference})=B$ incomparable. **(iii)** By Proposition 2, $\lnot$ fixes
$N=\varphi(\text{tutorial})$ and $B=\varphi(\text{reference})$ and transposes
$T=\varphi(\text{how-to})$ and $F=\varphi(\text{explanation})$; ${\sim}$ fixes $T, F$
and transposes $N, B$. Transporting along $\varphi^{-1}$ gives the stated diagonal
involutions, which generate $\varphi^{-1}G\varphi \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2$.
The purpose-axis reflection sends *tutorial* to *how-to*, i.e. $N\mapsto T$; but every
$g\in G$ satisfies $g(N)\in\{N,B\}$ (since $\mathrm{id}(N)=\lnot(N)=N$ and
${\sim}(N)=\lnot{\sim}(N)=B$), so the reflection is not in $G$; symmetrically for the
content axis. **(iv)** is read off the Hasse diagram of (ii). $\qquad\blacksquare$

The content of Theorem 1 is that the documentation square is not merely a $2\times2$
table but a copy of $\mathbf{4}$, and the move that adds information is the move from the
table (two partitions; symmetry group = the axis reflections) to the bilattice (two
orders; symmetry group = the diagonal dualities). The knowledge order $\le_k$ and the
two symmetry involutions are pictured below.

```
                    reference = B          (⊤ₖ : most decontextualised)
                    /         \
                  /             \
        how-to = T  · · ¬ · ·  explanation = F      ¬ : swap the middles
                  \             /
                    \         /
                    tutorial = N           (⊥ₖ : most scaffolded)

        ~ : swap bottom and top (tutorial · · · · reference)

   Solid edges: the knowledge order ≤ₖ, increasing upward.
   Dashed/labelled: the two symmetry involutions (the diagonals).
```

## 5. Interpretation

Theorem 1 is mathematics; the following identification of the transported orders with
named learning gradients is an interpretive thesis, supported but not proven by the
cited literature.

**The content axis is the knowing-how / knowing-that distinction.** Procida's
action–cognition axis ($\gamma$, the second coordinate) is Ryle's distinction between
knowing *how* and knowing *that* [8], given cognitive form by Anderson as the split
between procedural and declarative knowledge [9]. Tutorials and how-to guides build
procedures (knowing-how); explanation and reference state propositions (knowing-that).

**The purpose axis is the study / work distinction.** Procida's
acquisition–application axis ($\pi$, the first coordinate) separates encoding under
conditions arranged for the learner from retrieval in the user's own working context —
the axis along which instructional design predicts opposite optima (below).

**The knowledge order is the scaffolding gradient.** The transported order $\le_k$ ranks
the four types

$$\text{tutorial} \;<_k\; \{\text{how-to},\ \text{explanation}\} \;<_k\;
  \text{reference}.$$

We read $\le_k$ as the degree to which a document is *decontextualised* — how much it
spells out independently of the reader, as opposed to co-constructing meaning inside a
context built for the reader. A tutorial is maximally scaffolded and situated; a
reference is maximally decontextualised and self-contained. This is the variable that
cognitive-load theory manipulates. Heavy worked guidance — the tutorial pole — reduces
extraneous load for novices, the *worked-example effect* [10, 11]; and the
*expertise-reversal effect* [12] states that the same guidance that helps novices
hampers experts, who are better served by low-guidance, decontextualised material. In
the order of (ii) this says: *the load-minimising documentation type ascends $\le_k$ as
the reader's expertise grows*, from tutorial, through how-to or explanation, to
reference. Scaffolding is by definition meant to fade [13], and fading is upward motion
in $\le_k$.

**The truth order is the actionability gradient.** The transported order $\le_t$ ranks
$\text{explanation} <_t \{\text{tutorial}, \text{reference}\} <_t \text{how-to}$. We
read it as commitment to a single correct course of action. A how-to is a definite
recipe with a guaranteed outcome (top); an explanation weighs alternatives and commits
to no one action (bottom) — matching Procida's characterisation of explanation as the
mode that can hold different and even conflicting views [1].

**The diagonals are the meaningful transformations.** Theorem 1(iii) says the
structure-preserving involutions are the diagonals. Conflation ${\sim}$ is the
*learning ↔ lookup* duality (tutorial ↔ reference): it reverses the scaffolding order.
Negation $\lnot$ is the *doing ↔ understanding* duality (how-to ↔ explanation): it
reverses the actionability order. That these, not the axis reflections, are the
symmetries explains a known piece of documentation practice: the most common failure
Procida warns against is a tutorial drifting into reference [1] — movement *along a
diagonal*, between the two types most easily confused precisely because a symmetry of
the structure exchanges them.

## 6. Predictions

The bilattice model and the flat $2\times2$ model agree on the classification of any
single document but disagree on its structure, so they can be told apart empirically.

**Conjecture 1 (expertise monotonicity).** For a fixed task and learner population, the
documentation type minimising measured cognitive load moves monotonically up $\le_k$
(tutorial → how-to/explanation → reference) as measured expertise increases. The flat
model, having no order, predicts no such monotone trajectory.

**Conjecture 2 (diagonal editability).** Rewriting a document from one type to another
is easier and less error-prone along a diagonal (tutorial ↔ reference,
how-to ↔ explanation) than along an axis, because the diagonals are order-dualities and
the axes are not.

**Conjecture 3 (polar failure modes).** Because tutorial sits at the $\le_k$-bottom and
reference at the $\le_k$-top, their characteristic defects are opposite: tutorials fail
by under-specification (too little spelled out), reference by over-determination (more,
and more case-split, detail than any single use requires).

## 7. Related four-fold structures

The nearest four-fold model of learning is Kolb's experiential cycle [14], whose two
axes — perception (concrete/abstract) and processing (active/reflective) — also present
a $2\times2$ with a Klein four-group of reflections. Kolb's processing axis aligns with
our content axis $\gamma$, and his perception axis aligns with the scaffolding reading
of $\le_k$. The difference is that Kolb supplies a *cycle* — a dynamics on the four —
whereas Theorem 1 supplies the static *algebra*: the carrier, two orders, and symmetry
group. A bilattice carries canonical order-preserving closure operators, and modelling
the learning cycle as iterated closure on this bilattice is natural future work; we make
no claim here about Kolb's contested learning-*styles* instruments.

Finally, $\mathbf{4}$ is not an arbitrary four-element structure. It is the value space
of Belnap–Dunn four-valued logic [2, 3], the base of Fitting's bilattice-valued modal
and logic-programming semantics [5, 6], and the prototypical interlaced bilattice
[4, 7]. Identifying the Diátaxis types with $\mathbf{4}$ therefore places documentation
design in contact with a developed algebra of partial information, in which "none",
"true", "false", and "overdetermined" are exactly tutorial, how-to, explanation, and
reference under $\varphi$.
