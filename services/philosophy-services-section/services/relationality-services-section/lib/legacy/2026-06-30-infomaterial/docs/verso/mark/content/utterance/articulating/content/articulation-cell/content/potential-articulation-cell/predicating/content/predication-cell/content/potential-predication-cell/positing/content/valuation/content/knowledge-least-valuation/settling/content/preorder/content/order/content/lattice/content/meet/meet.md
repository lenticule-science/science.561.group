# Meet

## What this node is

The **meet** of two elements $a, b$ of a [lattice](../../lattice.md) is their
**greatest lower bound**: the largest element $c$ with $c \le a$ and $c \le b$,
written $a \wedge b$. Two conditions fix it — $c$ is a lower bound ($c \le a$,
$c \le b$), and $c$ is the *greatest* such ($d \le a$ and $d \le b$ imply
$d \le c$). It is unique when it exists, because [order](../../../../order.md)'s
antisymmetry forces two greatest lower bounds to be equal.

Meet is one of the two operations whose existence on every pair makes an
[order](../../../../order.md) a [lattice](../../lattice.md); the [join](../join/join.md)
is the other, and the two are dual. An order carrying only meets is a
meet-semilattice.

## What it means

Meet is **conjunction** — if the elements are propositions ordered by entailment,
$a \wedge b$ is the strongest proposition entailed by both. In sets ordered by
inclusion it is **intersection**; among the open sets of a space, again
intersection. It is the operation that answers "what do $a$ and $b$ *both* grant?"

## What it generates

The **Heyting implication** is defined through meet by residuation:
$$c \le (a \to b) \iff c \wedge a \le b.$$
Implication is the right adjoint of $a \wedge -$; that adjunction is what lifts a
distributive [lattice](../../lattice.md) to a Heyting algebra, and it is the same
residuation that, lifted to evidence pairs, gives the bilattice its implication.
Meet also generalizes from pairs to arbitrary subsets — the meet of a set $S$ is
the greatest lower bound of all of $S$ at once — and a lattice with all such meets
is complete.

## Orientation

Generated by the [lattice](../../lattice.md), dual to [join](../join/join.md),
right-adjoint partner the Heyting implication. The lattice is generated by
[order](../../../../order.md), which [settling](../../../../../../../../settling.md)
generates.
