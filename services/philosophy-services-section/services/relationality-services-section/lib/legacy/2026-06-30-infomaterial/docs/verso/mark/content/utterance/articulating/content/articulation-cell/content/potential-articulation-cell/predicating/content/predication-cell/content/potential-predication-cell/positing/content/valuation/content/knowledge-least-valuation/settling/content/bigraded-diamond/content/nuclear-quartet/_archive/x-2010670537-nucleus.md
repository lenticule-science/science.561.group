A **nucleus** on a Heyting algebra $A$ is an operator $j : A \to A$ satisfying three conditions:

1. **Extensive**: $a \leq j(a)$ for all $a \in A$.
2. **Idempotent**: $j(j(a)) = j(a)$ for all $a \in A$.
3. **Meet-preserving**: $j(a \wedge b) = j(a) \wedge j(b)$ for all $a, b \in A$.

The first two conditions make $j$ a [closure operator](../../closure-operator.md). The third is the condition that distinguishes a nucleus from a general closure operator: it forces the fixed-point set $\mathrm{Fix}(j) = \{a \in A : j(a) = a\}$ to inherit the full Heyting algebra structure from $A$, not merely the lattice structure.

## The fixed-point sub-algebra

$\mathrm{Fix}(j)$ is a Heyting sub-algebra of $A$: closed under $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\Rightarrow$, $\bot$, and $\top$. The inclusion $\iota : \mathrm{Fix}(j) \hookrightarrow A$ has a left adjoint given by $j$ itself:

$$
j : A \;\rightleftharpoons\; \mathrm{Fix}(j) : \iota
$$

This adjoint pair makes $\mathrm{Fix}(j)$ a reflective sub-algebra. The unit is the inequality $a \leq j(a)$ (extensivity). The counit is the identity, since $j$ restricted to $\mathrm{Fix}(j)$ is the identity.

The KZ-lax idempotence — $j \circ j = j$, but $a < j(a)$ strictly for $a \notin \mathrm{Fix}(j)$ — makes this a genuine reflection: elements not already fixed are strictly moved upward. The fixed-point set is reached by collapsing, not by reorganizing.

## Why these three conditions

Extensivity says $j$ moves propositions upward — it closes, it never weakens. Idempotence says one closure is enough — applying $j$ twice gives nothing new. Meet-preservation says $j$ commutes with conjunction. Together they characterize exactly the maps that arise as closure operators in lattice theory and as sheafification operators in topos theory.

## Commutativity of two nuclei

Two nuclei $j_1, j_2$ on the same algebra $A$ **commute** if $j_1 \circ j_2 = j_2 \circ j_1$. When two nuclei commute, their composite is again a nucleus and their joint fixed-point set $\mathrm{Fix}(j_1) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(j_2)$ equals the fixed-point set of the composite. Commutativity is the hinge condition for the [nuclear quartet](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../) construction.

## Origin in locale theory

The concept originates in locale theory. A nucleus on the frame of opens $\mathcal{O}(X)$ determines a sublocale: each nucleus $j$ on $\mathcal{O}(X)$ identifies a quotient locale whose frame of opens is $\mathrm{Fix}(j)$. The correspondence between nuclei and sublocales is bijective.
