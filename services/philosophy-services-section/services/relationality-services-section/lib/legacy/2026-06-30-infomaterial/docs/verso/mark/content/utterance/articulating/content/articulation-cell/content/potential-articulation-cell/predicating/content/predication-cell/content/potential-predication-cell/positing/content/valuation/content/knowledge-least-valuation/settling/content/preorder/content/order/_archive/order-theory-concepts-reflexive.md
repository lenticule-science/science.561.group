A relation R on a set S is reflexive if every element is related to itself: for all a ∈ S, aRa holds.

Reflexivity is one of the three defining properties of a [partial order](../objects/partial-order.md) (along with [transitivity](./transitive.md) and [antisymmetry](./antisymmetric.md)) and one of the two defining properties of a [preorder](../objects/preorder.md) (along with transitivity).

Reflexivity says: every element is at least as good as itself, every point is accessible from itself, every object has an identity [morphism](../../categories/theories/ordinary-categories/morphisms/morphism.md). In a poset viewed as a [category](../../categories/theories/ordinary-categories/objects/category.md), reflexivity corresponds to the existence of [identity morphisms](../../categories/theories/ordinary-categories/morphisms/identity-morphism.md): the [morphism](../../categories/theories/ordinary-categories/morphisms/morphism.md) a → a witnesses that a ≤ a.

In [modal logic](../../../disciplines/modal-logic/index.md), reflexivity of the [accessibility relation](../../logic/morphisms/accessibility-relation.md) validates the T axiom: □φ → φ. If every world accesses itself, then what is necessary is true — if φ holds at all accessible worlds and the current world is accessible from itself, then φ holds at the current world. The [modality](../../universes/semiotic-universe/terms/modality.md) j in the [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe is extensive (a ≤ j(a)), which is the order-theoretic expression of reflexivity for the corresponding accessibility structure.
