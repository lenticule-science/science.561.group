# Priestley space

A **Priestley space** is a triple `(X, τ, ≤)` consisting of:

- A topological space `(X, τ)` that is **compact** and **Hausdorff**.
- A partial order `≤` on `X`.
- The **order-disconnection** property: for every pair `x, y ∈ X` with `x ≰ y`, there exists a **clopen up-set** `U ⊆ X` with `x ∈ U` and `y ∉ U`.

A clopen up-set is a set that is both open-and-closed in `τ` and closed under `≤` (upward-closed: `a ∈ U ∧ a ≤ b ⟹ b ∈ U`).

Morphisms in the category `PriSp` of Priestley spaces are **continuous order-preserving maps** `X → Y`.

## The order-disconnection axiom in detail

The order-disconnection property is what distinguishes Priestley spaces from generic compact-ordered spaces. It says the order and the topology are **compatible**: any failure of `x ≤ y` (i.e., `x ≰ y`) can be witnessed by a clopen up-set separating them.

Equivalently:

- The set `{(x, y) : x ≤ y}` is closed in the product topology `X × X`. (Order is topologically closed.)
- Clopen up-sets generate the topology. (Topology is generated by order-compatible clopens.)

Together, these two equivalent properties pin down Priestley spaces among compact-ordered Hausdorff spaces.

## Priestley duality

**Theorem (Priestley 1970, 1972).** There is a contravariant equivalence of categories

$$\mathbf{DLat}^{\mathrm{op}} \;\simeq\; \mathbf{PriSp}$$

between bounded distributive lattices (with bounded-lattice homomorphisms) and Priestley spaces (with continuous order-preserving maps).

The functor `Spec: DLat → PriSp` sends a bounded distributive lattice `L` to its space of **prime filters** topologized by the spectral basis (open sets generated by `{F : a ∈ F}` and `{F : a ∉ F}` for each `a ∈ L`), ordered by inclusion.

The inverse functor `Clop_up: PriSp → DLat` sends a Priestley space `X` to its bounded distributive lattice of **clopen up-sets** under `∪` and `∩`.

The unit and counit of this equivalence are the natural isomorphisms `L ≅ Clop_up(Spec(L))` (every distributive lattice is its clopens) and `X ≅ Spec(Clop_up(X))` (every Priestley space is its prime filters).

## Why Priestley spaces matter for predication

Predication's value algebras are bilattices, which are built from distributive lattices via the twist-product. Priestley duality at the lattice level lifts to **Jung–Rivieccio duality** at the bilattice level:

$$\mathbf{DBiLat}^{\mathrm{op}} \;\simeq\; \mathbf{PairedPriSp},$$

where `PairedPriSp` is the category of paired Priestley spaces — Priestley spaces equipped with an order-reversing involution (the swap representing bilattice negation).

So Priestley spaces are the **lattice-level foundation** for the substrate's Priestley-side topology. A bilattice's dual is a paired Priestley space; the underlying Priestley space (forgetting the swap) is the algebraic dual of the lattice that the bilattice is built from.

## Distinguished examples

**Finite case.** Every finite poset is a Priestley space (discrete topology trivially satisfies order-disconnection). Conversely, every finite Priestley space is a finite poset.

For `L = 2 = {0, 1}`, `Spec(L) = {*}` (one point) — the trivial Priestley space.

For `L = 4` (the four-element distributive lattice), `Spec(L)` is a three-point poset with shape `*←*→*` (one bottom, two incomparable tops) or its dual — depending on which `L = 4` (there are two non-isomorphic four-element distributive lattices).

**Boolean case.** Boolean algebras correspond to **Stone spaces** — Priestley spaces where the order is discrete (trivial: `x ≤ y ⟺ x = y`). Stone duality is the special case of Priestley duality for Boolean lattices.

**Continuous lattices.** Continuous distributive lattices give Priestley spaces with specific completeness properties (every directed set has a supremum that is topological-limit).

## Why this lives at settling tier

The substrate's Priestley-side machinery — paired Priestley spaces, dual hyperdoctrines, sheaves on the bitopological settling site — is settling-tier. Cohomology of settlements is computed on Priestley-dual representations.

The bare Priestley space is the **lattice-level foundation** for that machinery. Paired Priestley spaces use Priestley spaces as carriers; settlement Priestley duals are paired-Priestley-valued; cohomology lives on Priestley topology.

So while Priestley spaces themselves are general categorical objects (not tied to any regime), their substrate-operational role is at the settling tier as the foundational space-type for the Priestley side.
