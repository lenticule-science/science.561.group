# Join

## What this node is

The **join** of two elements $a, b$ of a [lattice](../../lattice.md) is their
**least upper bound**: the smallest element $c$ with $a \le c$ and $b \le c$,
written $a \vee b$. Two conditions fix it — $c$ is an upper bound ($a \le c$,
$b \le c$), and $c$ is the *least* such ($a \le d$ and $b \le d$ imply $c \le d$).
It is unique when it exists, by the same antisymmetry argument as the
[meet](../meet/meet.md).

Join is the second of the two operations whose existence on every pair makes an
[order](../../../../order.md) a [lattice](../../lattice.md). An order carrying only
joins is a join-semilattice.

## What it means

Join is **disjunction** — for propositions ordered by entailment, $a \vee b$ is
the weakest proposition entailing both. In sets it is **union**; among open sets,
union of any collection. It answers "what does *either* of $a, b$ grant?"

Join and [meet](../meet/meet.md) are **dual**: every statement about joins becomes
one about meets under reversal of $\le$. This is the simplest instance of the
arrow-reversing duality that runs through order and category theory.

## Why settling reaches a join

The fixed point [settling](../../../../../../../../settling.md) ascends to is a **least
upper bound** — the join of the whole graded chain of valuations. That is why the
value structure settling needs is at least a [lattice](../../lattice.md): the
destination of the monotone orbit is a join. Join generalizes from pairs to
arbitrary subsets; a lattice with all such joins is complete, and completeness for
joins already forces it for meets — which is the order-theoretic ground of
Knaster–Tarski, under which the closure's fixed point exists.

## Orientation

Generated by the [lattice](../../lattice.md), dual to [meet](../meet/meet.md). The
lattice is generated by [order](../../../../order.md), which
[settling](../../../../../../../../settling.md) generates as the join of its closure
orbit.
