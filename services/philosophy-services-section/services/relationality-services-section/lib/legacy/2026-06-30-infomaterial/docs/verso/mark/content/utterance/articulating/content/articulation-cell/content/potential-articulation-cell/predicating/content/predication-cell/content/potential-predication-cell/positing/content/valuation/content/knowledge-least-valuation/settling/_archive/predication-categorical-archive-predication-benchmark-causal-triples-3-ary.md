# 3-ary causal triples settling

A concrete instance of [$n$-ary predication](../objects/n-ary-predication.md) with $n = 3$ — causal triples $(s, o, m)$ on alphabet $\Sigma = \{0, 1, 2\}$ with mechanism composition modulo 3. Verifies both the arity-collapse and arity-amplification cases of the [higher-arity cohomological rank theorem](../theorems/higher-arity-cohomological-rank.md).

## Setup

- *Alphabet*: $\Sigma = \{0, 1, 2\}$ (three events).
- *Cells*: triples $(s, o, m) \in \Sigma^3$, with $s$ = cause, $o$ = effect, $m$ = mechanism. 27 cells total.
- *Initial settling* $v_0$: bilattice values; specified seed triples have $\mathbf{t}$, others $\bot$.
- *Closure rule (genuine 3-ary)*: if $v(s, o, m_1) = \mathbf{t}$ and $v(o, q, m_2) = \mathbf{t}$, then $v(s, q, (m_1 + m_2) \bmod 3) = \mathbf{t}$.

The mechanism composes by addition mod 3 — treating $\Sigma$ as $\mathbb{Z}/3$. This is a **genuine 3-ary** closure (uses the third coordinate non-trivially).

For comparison: the *binary projection* closure on pairs $(s, o)$ alone is standard transitive closure on the support digraph.

Tool: `tools/predication_categories/examples/benchmark_causal_triples_3_ary.py`.

## Results

| Case | Seeds | $\kappa^{(3)}$ | 3-ary cells reached | Binary cells reached | Mechanism-diverse pairs | Arity behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\{(0, 1, 1)\}$ | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | (degenerate) |
| 2 | $\{(0, 1, 1), (1, 2, 1)\}$ | 1 | 3 | 3 | 0 | **collapse** |
| 3 | $\{(0, 1, 1), (1, 2, 1), (2, 0, 1)\}$ | 2 | 9 | 9 | 0 | **collapse** |
| 4 | $\{(0, 1, 0), (0, 1, 1), (1, 2, 1)\}$ | 1 | **5** | 3 | **2** | **amplification** |
| 5 | All 6 directed pairs, mechanism 0 | 1 | 9 | 9 | 0 | **collapse** |

## Empirical findings

### Arity-collapse: when seeds share a mechanism

Cases 1, 2, 3, 5: every seed uses the same mechanism (1 in cases 1-3, 0 in case 5). The closure stays within a *single mechanism stratum* — every reached cell $(s, o, m)$ has $m$ determined by the path's mechanism-sum. No diversity in $m$ for any $(s, o)$ pair.

In each case: 3-ary cells reached $= $ binary cells reached. **The 3-ary cohomology equals the binary cohomology** — Case A (arity-collapse) of the rank theorem, verified.

The intuition: if all seeds use mechanism $m_0$, the closure produces triples $(s, o, k \cdot m_0 \bmod 3)$ where $k$ is the path length. Since path length determines $m$ once $m_0$ is fixed, the third coordinate adds no information beyond the binary $(s, o)$. The 3-ary settling is the binary settling extended trivially to a single mechanism stratum.

### Arity-amplification: when seeds use multiple mechanisms

Case 4: seeds $\{(0, 1, 0), (0, 1, 1), (1, 2, 1)\}$ have *two* different mechanisms (0 and 1) for the pair $(0, 1)$.

Result: 3-ary settling reaches **5 cells**, while the binary projection reaches only **3 cells**. The reached 3-ary cells include:

- $(0, 1, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$ — both mechanisms for the seed pair.
- $(1, 2, 1)$ — the second seed.
- $(0, 2, 1)$ via composition $(0, 1, 0) + (1, 2, 1)$: $m = 0 + 1 = 1$.
- $(0, 2, 2)$ via composition $(0, 1, 1) + (1, 2, 1)$: $m = 1 + 1 = 2$.

So pair $(0, 2)$ has mechanism diversity $\{1, 2\}$ — *two* witnesses, generated by composition from the two seeds for $(0, 1)$.

**The 3-ary settling carries strictly more content than the binary projection.** This is Case B (arity-amplification) of the rank theorem, verified.

### Closure ordinal in the strict regime

All five cases have $\kappa \leq 2$ — the closure terminates in finite steps. Strict regime, $H^1 = 0$ at the cohomological level (no NSC firings on finite cell-space).

The interesting structural content is at the *cell-count* level: the number of cells reached, and the mechanism diversity, are arity-sensitive even in the strict regime. The cohomological theorem applies in the aperiodic regime; here in strict regime we see the corresponding *combinatorial precursor*: cells reached $\leq 9$ (binary), $\leq 27$ (3-ary), and mechanism diversity grows with seed-mechanism diversity.

## Connection to the rank theorem

The [higher-arity cohomological rank theorem](../theorems/higher-arity-cohomological-rank.md) predicts:

- *Case A (projection-only / arity-collapse)*: $\mathrm{rank}\, H^1$ doesn't scale with arity.
- *Case B (genuine $n$-ary / arity-amplification)*: $\mathrm{rank}\, H^1 \leq k^n$, with equality for primitive non-singular cases.

Here $k = 3$, $n = 3$, so the upper bound is $k^n = 27$. The strict-regime cell-count analog: 3-ary settlings can reach up to 27 cells (seen in principle, though our seed sets reach at most 9 in finite seeds).

The arity-collapse cases (1, 2, 3, 5) hit 1, 3, 9, 9 cells = same as binary. The arity-amplification case (4) hits 5 cells $> $ 3 binary cells — strictly more.

For an aperiodic-regime version (with $E = \omega_1$ or a substitution-flavored 3-ary settling), the corresponding cohomological rank inequality $\mathrm{rank}\, H^1(3\text{-ary}) \geq \mathrm{rank}\, H^1(\text{binary})$ would hold, with strict inequality in the genuine-3-ary case. Open: writing such an aperiodic-3-ary substitution explicitly.

## What this benchmark establishes

- The 3-ary causal-triple closure is a working concrete example of $n$-ary predication.
- Arity-collapse and arity-amplification are both empirically realizable: the dichotomy of the rank theorem is real.
- The amplification depends on **mechanism diversity in the seeds** — when seeds use multiple mechanisms, the closure preserves the diversity and amplifies it via composition.
- The arity-amplification effect is *genuine 3-ary content*, not derivable from the binary projection.
