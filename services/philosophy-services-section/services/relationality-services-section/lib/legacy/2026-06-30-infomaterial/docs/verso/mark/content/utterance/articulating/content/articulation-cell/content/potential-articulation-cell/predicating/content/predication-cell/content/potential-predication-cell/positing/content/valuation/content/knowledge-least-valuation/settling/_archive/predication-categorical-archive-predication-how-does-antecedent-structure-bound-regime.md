# How does antecedent structure bound settling regime?

The [closure-rule-language](../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/concepts/closure-rule-language.md) concept states that quantifier-free antecedents produce Scott-continuous closure operators, but does not prove this as a theorem. The settlement-path regime-descent engineering claim ([settlement-path-as-regime-descent](../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theories/agential-orchestration-platform/theorems/settlement-path-as-regime-descent.md)) and the MHC proof ([meaningful-human-control-as-charter-c3-extension](../../../engineering/theories/tech/theories/computing/theories/predicated-computing/theories/agential-orchestration-platform/theories/predicated-drone-swarm/theorems/meaningful-human-control-as-charter-c3-extension.md)) both depend on this as an unproved assertion.

## Answer

A single rule with a quantifier-free antecedent is Scott-continuous: the antecedent is a positive Boolean combination of $\geq_k$-atoms, each of which is Scott-open in the fiber algebra, so the antecedent commits at some finite stage of any directed join. The join of Scott-continuous rules is Scott-continuous. By the four-regime dichotomy, Scott-continuous closure gives $\kappa \leq \omega$.

The converse also holds: $\kappa > \omega$ requires a non-Scott-continuous firing, which requires a rule whose antecedent is satisfied at a limit stage but no earlier — exactly a universal quantifier over an infinite domain.

The formal theorem is [antecedent-regime-bound](../../../humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/antecedent-regime-bound.md).
