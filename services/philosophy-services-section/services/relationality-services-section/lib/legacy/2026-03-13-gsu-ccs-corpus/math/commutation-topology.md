### Theorem — The Commutation Topology {#commutation-topology}

*Geometry aspect of the history category: which families of sub-histories cover a history.*

The **commutation topology** $J$ is the Grothendieck topology on $T$ generated by
commutation covers. The pair $(T, J)$ is the **canonical history site**.

#### Commutation Covers {#commutation-covers}

A **commutation partition** of $t$ is a collection of histories $t^{(1)}, \ldots, t^{(n)}$
such that (i) $t = t^{(1)} \star \cdots \star t^{(n)}$ in $\mathbb{M}(\Sigma,I)$, and
(ii) the step-sets $\mathrm{steps}(t^{(i)})$ are pairwise disjoint and every step in
$t^{(i)}$ commutes with every step in $t^{(j)}$ for $i \neq j$.
When such a partition exists, the pieces $t^{(i)}$ can be reordered freely by commutation.

**Coverage morphisms.** Each piece $t^{(i)}$ is a prefix of $t$ in the poset $T$: since
the pieces commute pairwise, we can write $t = t^{(i)} \star u_i$ where
$u_i = t^{(1)} \star \cdots \widehat{t^{(i)}} \cdots \star t^{(n)}$ (omitting the
$i$-th piece). Hence $t^{(i)} \leq t$, so the unique poset morphism
$$
u_i : t^{(i)} \longrightarrow t
$$
exists in $T$ for each $i$. The family $\{u_i : t^{(i)} \to t\}_{i=1}^n$ is the
**commutation cover** of $t$ generated by the partition.

#### Lemma — Interval Distributive Lattice {#interval-distributive-lattice}

**Lemma.** For any history $t \in T$, the interval $[e, t]$ — the sub-poset of all
prefixes of $t$ in $T$ — is a finite distributive lattice, canonically isomorphic to
$\mathcal{J}(\mathrm{dep}(t))$, the lattice of order ideals of the dependency poset
of $t$.

The **dependency poset** $\mathrm{dep}(t)$ has the steps of $t$ as elements, with
$s_i \preceq s_j$ when $s_i$ must precede $s_j$ in every linearization consistent with
the commutation relation (i.e., $(s_i, s_j) \notin I$ and $s_i$ appears before $s_j$
in the dependency order). A prefix $t' \leq t$ corresponds to the order ideal of steps
that appear in $t'$.

*This is a classical result in the theory of free partially commutative monoids, due to
Cartier and Foata (1969) and developed further in Viennot's theory of heaps of pieces.
The isomorphism $[e,t] \cong \mathcal{J}(\mathrm{dep}(t))$ is the combinatorial foundation
for all pullback computations over $T$.*

#### Theorem — Commutation Coverage {#thm-commutation-coverage}

**Theorem.** The commutation covers form a **Grothendieck coverage** on $T$, satisfying
the identity, base-change, and transitivity axioms.

*Proof.*

1. **Identity axiom.** The singleton $\{\mathrm{id}_t\}$ is a commutation cover: it
   arises from the trivial one-piece partition $t = t$. ✓

2. **Base-change axiom.** Given a commutation cover $\{u_i : t^{(i)} \to t\}$ and a
   morphism $f : t' \to t$ (so $t' \leq t$), the pullback is
   $$
   t' \times_t t^{(i)} \;:=\; t' \wedge_{[e,t]} t^{(i)},
   $$
   the sub-history of $t'$ on the step-set of $t^{(i)}$. This meet is well-defined
   because $[e,t]$ is a distributive lattice (Lemma above). The restriction of $t'$
   to the steps of $t^{(i)}$ yields a commutation partition of $t'$ — the pieces are
   $\{t' \wedge t^{(i)}\}$, which have disjoint step-sets summing to all steps of $t'$.
   Hence the pulled-back family $\{t' \wedge t^{(i)} \to t'\}$ is a commutation cover
   of $t'$. ✓

3. **Transitivity axiom.** Sub-partitioning the step-sets of a commutation partition
   of $t$ into commutation partitions of each piece yields a finer commutation partition
   of $t$. The covering sieve generated by the finer partition contains that generated
   by the coarser. ✓

By the standard theory of sites, a Grothendieck coverage determines a unique Grothendieck
topology $J$ whose covering sieves are exactly those containing a commutation cover
(Johnstone, *Sketches of an Elephant*, C2.1.9). $\square$

#### Definition — The Commutation Topology {#def-commutation-topology}

**Definition.** The **commutation topology** on $T$ is the unique Grothendieck topology
$J$ on $T$ whose covering sieves are exactly those containing a commutation cover. Its
existence and uniqueness follow from the Theorem above.

The commutation topology $J$ and the canonical history site $(T, J)$ it determines are
the foundation of all sheaf-theoretic constructions in this framework. See §history-site for the site structure and what it licenses.

#### Connection to the Transfer Nucleus {#commutation-transfer-connection}

The independence relation $I \subseteq \Sigma \times \Sigma$ that generates $J$ also
generates the transfer nucleus $\Delta_t$ (§transfer-nucleus): where $J$ uses
independence **geometrically** — decomposing a history into commuting sub-histories
as a cover — $\Delta_t$ uses the same independence **algebraically** — intersecting
the images of restriction maps from independent one-step extensions. The Axiom of
Locality (§locality) is the coherence statement between these two expressions: $H$
being a sheaf for $J$ is what makes the fiber product decompositions underlying
$\Delta_t$ well-defined and the intersection in $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ coherent.

This same pattern propagates to the meta-level: at each hyperverse tower level $n$,
the profile topology $J^{(n+1)}$ on $\mathbf{RU}^{(n)}$ (§meta-universe-closure) is
the meta-level analogue of $J$, related to the meta-transfer nucleus by the same
correspondence.

That $J$ is canonically determined by the transfer nucleus structure of the fiber
doctrine is an open theorem.
