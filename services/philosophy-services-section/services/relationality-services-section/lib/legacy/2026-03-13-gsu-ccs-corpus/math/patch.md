### Definition — Patch {#patch}

A **patch site** is a full subcategory $U \subseteq T$ equipped with the induced
topology $J\!\restriction_U$ such that:

1. **Fullness and isomorphism-closure.** If $t \in \mathrm{Ob}(U)$ and $t' \cong t$ in
   $T$, then $t' \in \mathrm{Ob}(U)$. Every morphism in $T$ between objects of $U$ lies
   in $U$.

2. **Stability under covers.** If $t \in U$ and $\{u_i : t_i \to t\} \in J(t)$, this
   cover has a refinement by a family whose domains all lie in $U$.

Given a patch site $(U, J\!\restriction_U)$, define the **patch topos**
$$
R_U := \mathbf{Sh}(U, J\!\restriction_U).
$$
Restricting $H : T^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA_{nucl}}$ along $U \hookrightarrow T$
yields a sheaf $H_U : U^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA_{nucl}}$ with $H_U(t) := H_t$
and invariants $H_U^\ast \subseteq H_U$.

**Theorem — Patch Reconstruction.** For every patch site $(U, J\!\restriction_U)$:

1. $R_U = \mathbf{Sh}(U, J\!\restriction_U)$ is a topos.
2. $H_U \in R_U$ is an internal Heyting-modal algebra object with fixed subobject
   $H_U^\ast \hookrightarrow H_U$, obtained by restricting $H$ and $H^\ast$.
3. The inclusion of sites $\iota : (U, J\!\restriction_U) \hookrightarrow (T,J)$ induces
   a geometric morphism $j_U : R_U \to R$.
4. The internal logic of $R_U$ with respect to $H_U$ is exactly the restriction of the
   propositional-modal internal logic of $R$ (with respect to $H$) to histories in $U$.

In particular, each patch $(R_U, H_U)$ is itself a relational universe in miniature — a
local relational universe, fully determined by the restriction of the global structure to
the histories in $U$. The full proof is in §patch-reconstruction.

#### Remark {#patch-remark}

A patch site formalizes a bounded, locally coherent sub-universe. Algebraically: the
minimal sub-Heyting algebra of $H$ generated by a finite set of propositions at finitely
many histories determines (up to the topology) a patch site. The algebraic perspective
connects to the FiberNuclearLanguage term language; the geometric perspective gives access to sheaf
cohomology and geometric morphisms.
