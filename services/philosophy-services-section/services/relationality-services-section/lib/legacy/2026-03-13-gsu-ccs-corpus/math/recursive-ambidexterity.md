### Conjecture — Recursive Ambidexterity {#recursive-ambidexterity}

**Disambiguation.** The Ambidexterity Conjecture (§ambidexterity) names a
mathematical *phenomenon*: that the homotopy limit and homotopy colimit of the
hyperverse tower are equivalent. **Recursive ambidexterity** names the specific
*process* by which this phenomenon arises — the mechanism that produces the
equivalence at each successive level of the tower.

---

#### The Process {#recursive-ambidexterity-process}

**Inputs:**
- A relational universe $R^{(n)}$ at level $n$ of the hyperverse tower.
- The Beck-Chevalley conditions at level $n$ (§beck-chevalley-tower): the geometric
  morphism $R^{(n)} \hookrightarrow R^{(n+1)}$ is essential in both
  directions — it has both a left adjoint $\Pi_\to^{(n)}$ and a right
  adjoint $\nabla_\leftarrow^{(n)}$.

**The recursive step:**
1. Apply Universe Closure: $R^{(n+1)} := U_\mathcal{H}(R^{(n)})$.
2. Verify that the geometric morphism $i_n : R^{(n)} \hookrightarrow R^{(n+1)}$
   satisfies ambidexterity at level $n$: the counit map
   $\Pi_\to^{(n)} \circ i_n^* \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ is an equivalence.
3. Carry the Beck-Chevalley conditions forward: the conditions at level $n$
   imply the conditions at level $n+1$, by the self-similarity of the
   Universe Closure operator.

**Convergence:** After $\omega$ steps (at the colimit/limit of the tower),
the holim and hocolim agree — this is the Ambidexterity phenomenon
(§ambidexterity).

**Output:** The Relational Hyperverse $\mathcal{H} = \varinjlim_n R^{(n)}
\simeq \varprojlim_n R^{(n)}$ equipped with the ambient ambidextrous
structure.

---

#### Why "Recursive" {#recursive-ambidexterity-why-recursive}

Ordinary ambidexterity (in Lurie's sense) is a property that a single
∞-topos can satisfy for certain maps. The Relational Hyperverse achieves
ambidexterity not by satisfying a single property but by iterating a step
that preserves and accumulates ambidextrous structure at each level.

The recursion is:

$$
\mathrm{Amb}(R^{(0)}) \;\xrightarrow{U_\mathcal{H}}\; \mathrm{Amb}(R^{(1)}) \;\xrightarrow{U_\mathcal{H}}\; \cdots \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{Amb}(\mathcal{H})
$$

where $\mathrm{Amb}(-)$ denotes the ambidextrous structure. The self-generation
theorem $R = U_G(R)$ is the 1-categorical shadow: at the fixed point, the
colimit and fixed-point coincide because the generator is the thing generated.

---

#### Ground-Level Instance {#recursive-ambidexterity-ground}

The 1-categorical version of one step of this process is already established:
the depth-filtration tower $R_0 \subset R_1 \subset \cdots$ has $\varinjlim R_n
= R$ and $R = U_G(R)$. The colimit and the fixed point agree because the
ascending filtration is generated by a single closure operator applied
repeatedly. The Theorem of Least Fixed Point (§self-generation) is the
ground-level instance of recursive ambidexterity.

---

#### Status and Open Questions {#recursive-ambidexterity-status}

**Status.** Conjectured. The process is described; the proof that each step
preserves ambidextrous structure requires the Beck-Chevalley tower (§beck-chevalley-tower)
to be established at the ∞-categorical level.

**Key open question:** Is the ambidextrous structure inherited by the telescope
(the ∞-categorical colimit) from the individual steps? This is the content of
the Ambidexterity Conjecture (§ambidexterity) — recursive ambidexterity is
the process-level characterization of what that conjecture is claiming.

**Connection to the golden ratio criterion (§golden-hyperverse-criterion):** The
self-similar recursion here is structurally analogous to the Fibonacci
substitution matrix — which has $\phi$ as its leading eigenvalue. The claim
that the spectral invariant of the hyperversal Riemannian structure is $\phi$
may be a consequence of the recursive structure of this process.
