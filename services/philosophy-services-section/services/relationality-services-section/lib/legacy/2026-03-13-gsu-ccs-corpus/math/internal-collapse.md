### Theorem — Refuted Conjecture — Internal Collapse {#internal-collapse}

This conjecture claimed that the internal saturation nucleus $\mathbf{\sigma}^{\mathrm{int}}_t$
in $R = \mathbf{Sh}(T,J)$ equals the canonical retraction $\pi_t = \sigma_t \circ \Delta_t$
— that the two-stage external construction collapses to a single internal judgment.

**Refutation (§history-object-constant).** The constant presheaf $\underline{T}$ is
already a sheaf on $(T,J)$: covering families are commutation partitions with
$t^{(i)} \times_t t^{(j)} = e$, forcing compatible families to be constant. The internal
history object satisfies $\mathbf{T}(u) = T$ at every stage, with the internal prefix
order equal to the external one. Therefore $\mathbf{\sigma}^{\mathrm{int}}_t = \sigma_t
\neq \pi_t$, and the intermediate layer $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t) \setminus H_t^*$ is
visible in the internal language.

**What is true** (§internal-nucleus-definability): both $\sigma_t$ and $\Delta_t$ are
separately definable in the internal language — $\sigma_t$ via the internal prefix order
$\leq_{\mathrm{int}}$, and $\Delta_t$ via the internal commutation relation
$\perp_{\mathrm{int}}$. These are distinct internal predicates, and the fixed fiber $H_t^*$
is their jointly-defined intersection. The two-stage structure is not an external artifact;
it is faithfully present in the internal language.
