### Definition — Ontology {#ontology}

The **ontology** of a Relational Universe is its four-phase structure as organized by the
ontological quartet (§ontological-quartet): the algebra, language, geometry, and dynamics
that together constitute a complete construction cycle.

Concretely, the ontology of a Relational Universe $(T, J, H, R)$ consists of:

- **Algebra**: the history category $T$, the fiber algebras $H_t$, and the nuclei
  $\sigma_t, \Delta_t$ — the ground-level data from which everything else is built.
- **Language**: the fiber nuclear language FiberNuclearLanguage (§term, [fiber-nuclear-language](fiber-nuclear-language.md))
  and the syntactic relational universe — what can be said about accords at each history.
- **Geometry**: the commutation topology $J$ and the accord topos $R = \mathbf{Sh}(T,J)$
  (§accord) — the space in which the language's referents live.
- **Dynamics**: the universe closure $U_G$ and the self-generation theorem $R = U_G(R)$
  — the process by which the universe sustains itself.

An ontology is not a description of a universe from outside. It is the structure the
universe IS as a complete four-phase system. A relational universe has an ontology because
having one is what it means to be a relational universe: the four phases are not optional
components but the four constitutive aspects of the construction.

#### Relation to the Ontological Quartet {#ontology-quartet-relation}

The ontological quartet (§ontological-quartet) establishes that every accord in $R$ presents
the four-phase structure as a nuclear quartet — algebra and geometry as the two $\sigma$- and
$\Delta$-stable inputs, language and dynamics as the two outputs, with $R$ as joint fixed point.
The ontology of a relational universe is this quartet read as the structure of the universe
itself rather than as a fact about any particular accord within it.
