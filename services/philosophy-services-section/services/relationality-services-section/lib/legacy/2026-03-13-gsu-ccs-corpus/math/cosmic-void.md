### Conjecture — Parity Error of Cosmic Voids {#cosmic-void}

*The sheaf condition is not uniformly enforced across the universe. Its strength depends
on the density of the history structure: how many commuting witnesses a history can be
decomposed into. In cosmic voids — regions of sparse relational history — covering
families are thin, the parity checks are few, and oscillatory modes accumulate unchecked.
The relational laws in a void are real but weakly enforced: the Axiom of Locality has
fewer witnesses there, and the logical error-correction capacity of the sheaf is reduced.*

---

#### The Mathematical Structures {#cv-math}

- **Axiom of Locality** (§locality): $H: T^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{nucl}}$
  is a sheaf on $(T,J)$. The topology $J$ is generated by **commutation covers**: a
  history $t$ is covered by its commuting decomposition pieces $\{u_i: t^{(i)} \to t\}$,
  where $t = t^{(1)} \star \cdots \star t^{(n)}$ with pairwise-commuting step-sets.
  Gluing: any compatible family $(a_i \in H_{t^{(i)}})$ assembles uniquely to $a \in H_t$.

- **History density**: the number of pieces in the finest commutation cover of $t$ —
  the width of $t$'s commutation decomposition — measures how many concurrent commuting
  witnesses the history carries. Call this the **cover width** $w(t)$. A history with high
  cover width decomposes into many commuting sub-histories; the sheaf condition at $t$ is
  enforced by many simultaneous parity checks. A history with $w(t) = 1$ (a totally ordered
  history, where all steps are mutually dependent) has only the trivial cover; the sheaf
  condition at $t$ is enforced by no non-trivial check at all.

- **Connecting homomorphism** (§cohomology): the short exact sequence
  $0 \to E_0 \to V \to V/E_0 \to 0$ induces
  $$
  \cdots \to \Gamma(V/E_0) \xrightarrow{\;\partial\;} H^1(T,J;\,E_0) \to \cdots
  $$
  The class $\partial([e]) \in H^1(T,J;\,E_0)$ is the obstruction to lifting a globally
  coherent oscillatory pattern to a globally coherent mode. A non-zero class $\partial([e])$
  witnesses a pattern that is locally consistent on every covering family but cannot be
  globally assembled — it is an uncorrectable parity error in the sheaf.

#### The Parity-Check Analogy {#cv-parity}

**Error-correcting codes.** A binary linear code is specified by a parity-check matrix
$H$: a word $v$ is a valid codeword iff $Hv = 0$. Transmission errors produce a
syndrome $Hv \neq 0$. The **code distance** — the minimum number of errors the code
can detect — grows with the density of checks: more parity equations per bit means more
robust error correction. When the parity-check matrix is sparse (few rows), error correction
is weak: errors can propagate undetected.

**The relational translation.** The sheaf condition at history $t$ is the parity-check
system for propositions:

- **Valid codewords** = global sections: elements $a \in H_t$ consistent with all
  restriction maps and gluing conditions. The sheaf condition says these are the ONLY
  globally coherent propositions.

- **Syndromes = obstruction classes**: a class $\partial([e]) \neq 0$ in $H^1(T,J;\,E_0)$
  is a syndrome — a locally consistent oscillatory pattern that fails to be globally valid.
  It is an "error" in the relational logical structure: something that looks locally
  coherent but cannot be assembled globally.

- **Parity checks = covering families**: the commutation cover $\{u_i: t^{(i)} \to t\}$
  provides one parity equation per covering element. For a cover of width $w(t)$, there
  are $w(t)$ simultaneous checks at history $t$. The sheaf's logical robustness at $t$ is
  proportional to $w(t)$.

- **Check density = cover width** $w(t)$: histories in dense regions (many concurrent
  commuting histories converging) have large $w(t)$ — many witnesses, many checks,
  high logical robustness. Histories in sparse regions (few concurrent commuting histories)
  have small $w(t)$ — few witnesses, few checks, low logical robustness.

**Low-density = large $H^1$.** The Čech computation of $H^1(T,J;\,E_0)$ via the covering
families shows that the cohomology group is generated by Čech 1-cocycles: functions $c$
assigning to each pair $(t^{(i)}, t^{(j)})$ (with $t^{(i)} \wedge t^{(j)} = e$) a class
in $E_0(e)$, modulo coboundaries. When covering families are thin ($w(t)$ small), there are
fewer pairs to check, fewer coboundary relations to kill cycles, and the group $H^1$ is
larger — more oscillatory patterns survive as unresolved obstructions.

Formally: as $w(t) \to 1$ (totally dependent history, trivial cover), the Čech complex
degenerates and $H^1$ can be maximally non-trivial; as $w(t) \to |t|$ (maximally
commuting history, cover of width equal to history length), the Čech complex is rich and
$H^1$ is forced toward zero. The connecting homomorphism $\partial$ is larger — maps
more classes to non-zero obstructions — when the covering families are sparse.

#### The Bridge — Thin Topology, Sparse Structure {#cv-bridge}

**Physical cosmology** observes that cosmic voids — the regions between galaxy filaments
that comprise most of the volume of the universe — are larger, emptier, and more regular
than standard $\Lambda$CDM predicts. The void interior is not merely low-density; it appears
to be anomalously quiet. Yet the outskirts of voids, where filaments meet, show enhanced
clustering.

**The relational reading** proposes that the thinness of a void is not just the absence
of baryonic matter. It is the absence of relational witnessing: the history structure at void
coordinates has small cover width $w(t)$, meaning the commutation topology $J$ has sparse
covering families there. The sheaf condition — which is the relational expression of the
physical laws of coherence — is enforced by few simultaneous constraints.

**Two consequences.**

*First*, oscillatory modes in $\Gamma(V/E_0)$ that would be killed by cohomological
tunneling in a high-$w(t)$ region persist longer in low-$w(t)$ voids. Tunneling
(§cohomological-tunneling) requires obstruction classes $\partial([e]) \in H^1(T,J;\,E_0)$
to be non-zero; when $H^1$ is large (low $w(t)$), many classes are available but few
are forced to zero by local dynamics. The tunneling events that DO fire in a void are
large jumps (from the flat-region behavior of §cohomological-tunneling), not the
small incremental steps of high-curvature regions. This predicts sporadic, large-amplitude
oscillatory events in voids.

*Second*, the void's shallower error-correction capacity means that the physical laws
operative in a void are less rigidly enforced: gluing fails more often, propositions
that would be uniquely determined by the sheaf condition in a filament are underdetermined
in a void. This is not a violation of the axioms — the Axiom of Locality holds globally
— but its local effect at low-$w(t)$ histories is weaker. A void is a region where the
universe's self-description is less redundantly encoded.

**Void formation as a threshold.** A void forms when the local cover width $w(t)$ drops
below the error-correction threshold: the minimum $w_{\min}$ at which the sheaf condition
can correct all Čech 1-cocycles. Below this threshold, oscillatory modes accumulate, the
spectral mass $M_t(\delta)$ of the surrounding regions redistributes outward (modes flow
toward the richer-cover-width regions where tunneling is more efficient), and the void
deepens. The formation of a void is a feedback process: sparse coverage → large $H^1$ →
modes persist → no energy to drive witness formation → coverage stays sparse.

#### Prediction and Falsification {#cv-prediction}

**Prediction (qualitative).**

1. **Void interiors should show enhanced $H^1$-type signatures**: oscillatory mode
   accumulation should produce measurable fluctuations in the CMB and galaxy
   distributions inside voids, with a characteristic spatial pattern governed by the
   Čech 1-cocycle structure of the commutation topology $J$ at low cover width.

2. **Void outskirts (the void-filament boundary) are the error-correction horizon**:
   the transition from low-$w(t)$ (void) to high-$w(t)$ (filament) is the location
   where the sheaf condition transitions from weak to strong enforcement. This boundary
   should be sharper than a smooth density gradient would predict — it is a topological
   threshold, not a smooth field transition.

3. **Void statistics should encode history topology**: the size distribution of voids
   is governed by the distribution of $w(t)$ across the history site. The minimum void
   size (the smallest void that can persist against cohomological tunneling) corresponds
   to the minimum cover-width below which $H^1 \neq 0$. This should produce a
   characteristic cutoff in the void size distribution.

**Falsification.** The correspondence fails if:

- Cosmic voids are entirely explainable by standard gravitational dynamics without
  invoking any anomalous behavior of physical laws in low-density regions.

- The connecting homomorphism $\partial$ does not grow with decreasing cover width:
  if $H^1(T,J;\,E_0)$ is insensitive to the sparseness of the covering topology,
  the history-density–obstruction link is broken.

- The void-filament boundary is smooth in all physical observables (density,
  velocity field, CMB temperature): if there is no topological threshold behavior,
  the error-correction-threshold picture is falsified.

---

#### What the Correspondence Requires {#cv-requires}

- **A precise definition of cover width $w(t)$**: the number of elements in the finest
  commutation cover must be defined carefully as a function of the history monoid structure.
  For the Fibonacci history model, $w(T_n)$ should be computable from the substitution
  matrix and the commutation relation $I$.

- **A quantitative relation between $w(t)$ and $H^1$**: showing that $H^1(T,J;\,E_0)$
  grows as $w(t)$ decreases requires explicit Čech computations on the history site. A history with high cover
  width (two commuting paths) vs. a totally ordered history (one path only)
  should give distinguishable $H^1$ groups.

- **A physical dictionary for history density**: identifying the relational cover width
  $w(t)$ with the physical density of matter (or with the cosmological density field
  $\delta_m(\mathbf{x})$) requires a correspondence between the history site and physical
  spacetime — the same dictionary needed for §dark-matter-lag.

**Status.** Stub. The formal connection between covering family density and $H^1$ size
is well-motivated by Čech cohomology theory (sparser covers → fewer coboundary relations
→ potentially larger $H^1$) but requires an explicit proof in the history topology setting.
The physical identification of low cover width with cosmic voids, and of large $H^1$ with
anomalous void structure, is the speculative bridge and depends on the physical dictionary.
