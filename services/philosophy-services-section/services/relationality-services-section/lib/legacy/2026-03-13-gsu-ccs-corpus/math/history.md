### Definition — The History {#history}

#### Axiom of Histories {#axiom-histories}

A **history system** over a step set $\Sigma$ (§step) with commutation relation
$I$ (§commutation) is a monoid $(T, \star, e)$ equipped with an injection
$\mathrm{gen} : \Sigma \hookrightarrow T$ satisfying:

1. **Generation.** Every element of $T$ is a finite product of atoms
   $\mathrm{gen}(s_1) \star \cdots \star \mathrm{gen}(s_n)$ for $s_i \in \Sigma$.
2. **Commutativity.** For every $(s, s') \in I$:
   $\mathrm{gen}(s) \star \mathrm{gen}(s') = \mathrm{gen}(s') \star \mathrm{gen}(s)$.
3. **Unit-free.** $m \star n = e$ implies $m = n = e$.
4. **Left-cancellative.** $m \star u = m \star v$ implies $u = v$.

An element $t \in T$ is a **history**. The monoid $T$ is the **history category**
when equipped with the prefix order: $t \leq t'$ iff $\exists u : t' = t \star u$.
This partial order makes $T$ a thin category (§order) — a poset viewed categorically
— in which morphisms are prefix extensions.

The **Axiom of Histories** asserts that the history category $T$ of this paper is a
history system over a finite step set $\Sigma$ with commutation relation $I$.

The canonical free history system over $(\Sigma, I)$ — the history monoid
$\mathbb{M}(\Sigma, I)$ — exists and is the initial object in the category of all
history systems over $(\Sigma, I)$. Its explicit construction and the proof of
initiality are in §history-monoid.

#### What a History Is {#history-is}

Viewed from three perspectives:

- **Below** (constitutive): a history is constituted by the steps that produced it —
  it is a finite product of atoms $\mathrm{gen}(s_1) \star \cdots \star
  \mathrm{gen}(s_n)$. Two products that differ only by commuting adjacent steps with
  $(s_i, s_{i+1}) \in I$ name the same history: the commutation relation $I$ is
  exactly what makes the order of commuting steps irrelevant to the history produced.

- **Same level** (structural): a history is an element of the history category $T$ —
  a position in a thin monoidal category generated by a finite step set with partial
  commutativity. Every history is either the empty history $e$ (nothing has happened
  yet) or a prefix-extension of a shorter history (something more has happened).

- **Above** (functional): a history $t \in T$ is the **stage** at which the
  relational universe $R = \mathbf{Sh}(T, J)$ is locally evaluated. The fiber
  Heyting algebra $H_t$ (§fiber-doctrine) is the domain of meanings available at
  history $t$. The restriction maps $H_{t'} \to H_t$ for $t \leq t'$ carry
  propositions backward from richer histories to simpler ones, making $H$ a
  contravariant sheaf on $T^{\mathrm{op}}$.

#### What a History Is Not {#history-not}

A history is not a sequence of steps. It is an element of the history category —
which may be concretely constructed as an equivalence class of step sequences, but
is not a sequence itself. Two step sequences that produce the same history are
genuinely identical as histories; the order of commuting steps is not part of the
history's identity. The concrete construction is in §history-monoid.

A history is not a state, configuration, or snapshot. The propositions and
meanings associated with a history live in the fiber $H_t$ above $t$; the history
$t$ is the index, not the content. Confusing the index with the content conflates
the history category $T$ with the sheaf $H$ living on it.

A history is not a path in $T$. A morphism $t \to t'$ in $T$ (a prefix extension)
witnesses that $t'$ is an extension of $t$; it is not itself a history. The
morphism $t \to t'$ corresponds to the extension history $u$ satisfying $t' = t
\star u$, but $u$ is a history in its own right, not a morphism.

#### The Prefix Order and the History Category {#history-prefix}

The prefix order $t \leq t'$ (§order) makes $T$ into the **history category**: a
thin category (at most one morphism between any two objects) where the unique
morphism $t \to t'$ exists iff $t'$ extends $t$. The history category is:

- **Small**: $T$ has a set of objects (all histories over a given $(\Sigma, I)$).
- **Locally finite**: each history $t$ has finitely many immediate successors
  $\{t \star \mathrm{gen}(s) \mid s \in \Sigma\}$ — at most $|\Sigma|$ of them
  (§history-depth).
- **Directed**: the empty history $e$ is the least element; every pair of histories
  with a common prefix has a common upper bound.

This makes $(T, J)$ a well-behaved Grothendieck site (§history-site) whose topos
$R = \mathbf{Sh}(T, J)$ is the relational universe.

#### Canonical Representatives {#history-canonical}

Every history has a unique canonical representative — the Foata normal form
(§history-normalization). This form is specific to the concrete history monoid
$\mathbb{M}(\Sigma, I)$ and is used for decidable equality and depth measurement.
The abstract history system axioms do not depend on normal forms; normal forms
are a property of the free model (§history-monoid).

#### Depth {#history-depth-ref}

The **depth** of a history $t$ is the minimum number of steps required to produce
it from $e$: the length of the shortest step sequence representing $t$. Depth
defines the filtration $T^{(0)} \subseteq T^{(1)} \subseteq \cdots$ (§history-sublevel)
used to approximate $T$ by finite subcategories.
