### Theorem — Hull Uniqueness {#hull-uniqueness}

**Theorem.** $R^\mathrm{hull} = \mathbf{Sh}(\mathbb{N}^d, J^\mathrm{hull})$ is, up to
equivalence, the unique relational universe satisfying the locally-finite, fully-commutative
fiber doctrine.

Equivalently: $R^\mathrm{hull}$ is **initial** in the category of relational universe models
of the hull fiber doctrine.

This is the hull Uniqueness axiom of §hull-axiom-verification; its proof follows the same
structure as ground-level initiality (§initiality), with Hull History Separation
(§hull-history-separation) playing the role of the separation axiom.

#### Existence of a Morphism {#hu-existence}

Given any target model $S$ satisfying the hull fiber doctrine (locally-finite Heyting algebras
with commuting substitution nuclei $\sigma^\Sigma, \Delta^\Sigma$), a structure-preserving
morphism $F: R^\mathrm{hull} \to S$ is constructed exactly as in §initiality:

- Send each representable $y(v)$, $v \in \mathbb{N}^d$, to the corresponding atom in $S$.
- Extend to all of $R^\mathrm{hull}$ via the universal properties of each closure step.

Since $R^\mathrm{hull}$ is generated by its representables under $U_{G^\Sigma}$-closure
(§hull-self-generation), this assignment is well-defined and produces a morphism.

#### Uniqueness of the Morphism {#hu-uniqueness}

**Step 1 — Morphisms are determined on generators.** $R^\mathrm{hull}$ is generated by
$\{y(v)\}_{v \in \mathbb{N}^d}$ under $U_{G^\Sigma}$-closure (§hull-self-generation). Any
structure-preserving $F: R^\mathrm{hull} \to S$ is completely determined by its values on
these generators; each closure operation's universal property forces the value of $F$ on
derived objects.

**Step 2 — Generators are determined by hull history.** Each representable $y(v)$ must be
sent to the atom in $S$ at history coordinate $v \in \mathbb{N}^d$. Structure-preservation
requires $F(y(v)) = y_S(v)$, since $F$ must commute with the hull history site structure.

**Step 3 — Hull History Separation eliminates ambiguity.** By §hull-history-separation, the
hull satisfies History Separation as a theorem (not an axiom): the fiber algebras
$H^\mathrm{hull}_v$ vary faithfully across histories $v \in \mathbb{N}^d$ — no nontrivial
automorphism of $\mathbb{N}^d$ carries $H^\mathrm{hull*}$ to itself, because the hyperversal
quasicrystal $Q^\mathcal{H}$ is aperiodic. Therefore no automorphism of $S$ could produce a
second distinct morphism from $R^\mathrm{hull}$: the hull's atoms are distinguishable.

Combining Steps 1–3: any two structure-preserving morphisms $F_1, F_2: R^\mathrm{hull} \to S$
agree on all generators and are each determined by those generators, and no automorphism of $S$
relates one to the other. Therefore $F_1 = F_2$. $\square$

#### Ground-Level vs. Hull-Level Uniqueness {#hu-comparison}

At the ground level, uniqueness (§initiality) requires History Separation as an **axiom** on
the fiber doctrine — it is an additional condition imposed on the relational universe.

At the hull level, uniqueness requires History Separation as a **theorem** — it is derived from
the aperiodicity of the Fibonacci quasicrystal (§hull-history-separation). The hull uniqueness
does not impose an additional axiom; it inherits separation from the quasicrystalline structure.

This continues the pattern of §hull-axiom-verification: ground-level axioms become hull-level
theorems, with the quasicrystal providing the structural rigidity that the axioms must assert
at the ground level.
