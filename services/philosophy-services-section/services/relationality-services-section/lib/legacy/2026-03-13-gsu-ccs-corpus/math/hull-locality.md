### Theorem — Hull Locality {#hull-locality}

#### The Hull History Site {#hl-site}

The hull history site is $(T^{\mathrm{hull}}, J^{\mathrm{hull}}) = (\mathbb{N}^d, J^{\mathrm{hull}})$,
where $J^{\mathrm{hull}}$ is the Grothendieck topology generated by commutation covers of
$\mathbb{N}^d$.

A **commutation partition** of $v = (v_1, \ldots, v_d) \in \mathbb{N}^d$ is a decomposition
$v = v^{(A)} + v^{(B)}$ where $A \sqcup B = \{1, \ldots, d\}$ is a non-trivial bipartition and
$v^{(A)}_i = v_i \cdot \mathbf{1}_{i \in A}$. Since all generators commute in $\mathbb{N}^d$,
every bipartition of coordinates gives a valid commutation partition. The commutation covers
of $v$ are exactly the families
$$
\{v^{(A)} \to v,\; v^{(B)} \to v\}
\quad (\text{for all non-trivial bipartitions } A \sqcup B = \{1,\ldots,d\})
$$
together with their refinements (finer partitions into $k \geq 3$ groups). The cover morphisms
$v^{(A)} \to v$ are the prefix order inclusions $v^{(A)} \leq v$ in $\mathbb{N}^d$.

For $d = 1$: there are no non-trivial bipartitions ($d$ must be $\geq 2$ for $A, B$ to be
non-empty), so $J^{\mathrm{hull}}$ is trivial — every sieve is a cover. Locality holds
vacuously. ✓

#### Local Derivability of the Fibonacci Quasicrystal {#hl-local-derivability}

The Fibonacci quasicrystal $Q^\mathcal{H} : \mathbb{N}^d \to \mathcal{H}$ is **locally derivable**:
the values $Q^\mathcal{H}(w)$ for $w$ in any ball $B(v,r)$ are uniquely determined by the
values $\{Q^\mathcal{H}(w) : w \in B(v^{(A)}, r) \cup B(v^{(B)}, r)\}$ from the two pieces
of any bipartition.

This follows from two facts:
1. **Coordinatewise structure**: the $d$-dimensional quasicrystal $Q^\mathcal{H}$ is built
   coordinatewise from the 1-dimensional Fibonacci quasicrystal. Patches in the $A$-directions
   and patches in the $B$-directions are independent in the sense that the local structure
   in $A$-directions is not constrained by local structure in $B$-directions.
2. **Unique ergodicity**: the frequency measure $\mu_\Omega$ on $\Omega(Q^\mathcal{H})$ gives
   the same frequencies to all patches, uniformly across positions. Compatible patches in the
   two groups of directions uniquely determine the joint patch at $v$.

#### Separation {#hl-separation}

**Theorem.** If $P, Q \in H^{\mathrm{hull}}_v$ satisfy $\rho_{v^{(A)} \leq v}(P) = \rho_{v^{(A)} \leq v}(Q)$
and $\rho_{v^{(B)} \leq v}(P) = \rho_{v^{(B)} \leq v}(Q)$ for some bipartition $A \sqcup B$,
then $P = Q$.

**Proof.** The restriction $\rho_{v^{(A)} \leq v}(P)$ records the values of $P$ restricted
to the $A$-coordinate sub-neighborhood; similarly for $B$. Since $A \cup B = \{1,\ldots,d\}$,
the sub-neighborhoods cover the full neighborhood: every lattice point in $B(v,r)$ is in
$B(v^{(A)}, r) \cup B(v^{(B)}, r)$. A patch $P \in H^{\mathrm{hull}}_v$ is determined by its
values on all points of $B(v,r)$, hence is determined by the two restrictions. If the two
restrictions agree for $P$ and $Q$, then $P = Q$. $\square$

#### Gluing {#hl-gluing}

**Theorem.** Given a compatible family $(P_A \in H^{\mathrm{hull}}_{v^{(A)}},\; P_B \in H^{\mathrm{hull}}_{v^{(B)}})$
— compatible in the sense that $\rho_{0 \leq v^{(A)}}(P_A) = \rho_{0 \leq v^{(B)}}(P_B)$
(they agree at the common sub-history $0$) — there exists a unique $P \in H^{\mathrm{hull}}_v$
such that $\rho_{v^{(A)} \leq v}(P) = P_A$ and $\rho_{v^{(B)} \leq v}(P) = P_B$.

**Proof.** The patch $P$ is constructed as follows: at each lattice point $w \in B(v, r)$,
set $P(w) = P_A(w)$ if $w \in B(v^{(A)}, r)$, and $P(w) = P_B(w)$ if $w \in B(v^{(B)}, r)$.
Compatibility at $0$ ensures that on the overlap $B(v^{(A)}, r) \cap B(v^{(B)}, r)$, both
patches agree, so $P$ is well-defined.

The patch $P$ is a legal patch of $Q^\mathcal{H}$ at $v$ because: by local derivability, the
$A$- and $B$-direction data are independent constraints on the quasicrystal, and satisfying both
independently is sufficient to determine a unique global patch. Since $P_A$ and $P_B$ are both
legal patches of $Q^\mathcal{H}$ (elements of the respective fiber algebras), and they are
compatible, the combined $P$ is a legal patch. $\square$

#### The Locality Axiom Holds at the Hull Level {#hl-statement}

**Corollary.** $H^{\mathrm{hull}} : (\mathbb{N}^d)^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA}$ is a sheaf
on $(\mathbb{N}^d, J^{\mathrm{hull}})$.

The sheaf condition (§locality) holds for every commutation cover: separation follows from the
covering of $B(v,r)$ by the sub-neighborhoods, and gluing follows from the coordinatewise
independence of the Fibonacci quasicrystal.

#### Comparison with Ground-Level Locality {#hl-comparison}

At the ground level, the Locality axiom is imposed as a condition on $H$ — it requires the
presheaf to satisfy a gluing condition that is non-trivial because not all histories have
commutation partitions (only histories with pairwise-commuting sub-steps do).

At the hull level, locality is a **theorem**: it follows from the coordinatewise structure of
the $d$-dimensional Fibonacci quasicrystal and its unique ergodicity. The covering topology
$J^{\mathrm{hull}}$ is the total commutation topology (all pairs of directions are independent),
making every bipartition a valid cover.

This continues the pattern established in §hull-history-separation: axioms at the ground level
become theorems at the hull level, derived from the quasicrystal's structural properties.
