### Theorem — Uniqueness {#uniqueness}

**Theorem — Uniqueness.** Any two realizations of the same primitive data (the axioms of §history-and-fiber-algebra, §modal-calculus, and §sheaf-construction) are
uniquely isomorphic: there exists an equivalence of topoi $F : R \simeq R'$ such that
$F(H) \cong H'$, $F(\sigma) = \sigma'$, $F(\Delta) = \Delta'$, and $F(H^\ast) \cong
H'^\ast$.

*Proof.* By assumption there is an equivalence of sites $(T,J) \simeq (T',J')$. The
general comparison theorem for sheaf topoi asserts that any such equivalence induces an
equivalence of sheaf categories $F_{\mathrm{sh}} : \mathbf{Sh}(T,J) \to
\mathbf{Sh}(T',J')$. The fiber data $(H_t, \sigma_t, \Delta_t, H_t^\ast)$ transported
along the site equivalence coincides with the fibers underlying $H', H'^\ast$ by
hypothesis. Naturality of the fiberwise operations implies that $H, H^\ast$ correspond
to $H', H'^\ast$ under $F_{\mathrm{sh}}$. $\square$

**Remark — The Relational Universe as Sheafification.** The uniqueness theorem yields a clean
characterization: the relational universe is the Grothendieck topos of sheaves on the initial relational
quantale generated by the step set $\Sigma$ and commutation relation
$I \subseteq \Sigma \times \Sigma$, with $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ its canonical commuting
nuclei:
$$
R \;=\; \mathbf{Sh}(T, J).
$$
The axioms of §history-and-fiber-algebra, §modal-calculus, and §sheaf-construction are not additional data imposed on this structure; they are the
exactness and coherence conditions required for the relational quantale to support a
well-behaved sheaf theory. Every subsequent structure — the fixed subsheaf $H^\ast$, the
phase space, the fiber nuclear language, the defect operator — is determined by this single sheafification.
