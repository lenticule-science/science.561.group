### Construction — The Free Generative Act {#free-generative-act}

The generative act $U_G$ on $R$ (§generative-act, §closure-operator)
generates a canonical free object by transfinite iteration from the atomic class $A$.
This iteration is the depth-filtration tower; its colimit is $R$ itself. $R$ is
therefore both the **free generative act** and its own **fixed point** — the unique
place where free generation closes on itself.

#### Transfinite Iteration {#free-generative-iteration}

Starting from the atomic class $A = \{y(t) \mid t \in \mathrm{Ob}(T)\} \cup \{H\}$,
iterate $U_G$:

$$
R_0 \;:=\; U_G(A), \quad
R_1 \;:=\; U_G(R_0), \quad
R_n \;:=\; U_G(R_{n-1}), \quad
R_\omega \;:=\; \bigcup_{n < \omega} R_n, \quad
\ldots
$$

Each $R_n = \mathbf{Sh}(T^{(n)}, J|_{T^{(n)}})$ is the relational universe at
**depth $n$**: the full sheaf topos on histories of length at most $n$.
(§depth-filtration identifies this with the depth-filtration tower.)

The iteration stabilizes at the first fixed point above $A$. The
**Knaster–Tarski theorem** guarantees this: since $U_G$ is a monotone operator on
the complete lattice $P(\mathrm{Obj}(R))$, the least fixed point above $A$ is:
$$
R_{\mathrm{gen}} \;=\; \bigcap\,\{\, X \mid U_G(X) = X,\; A \subseteq X \,\}.
$$

#### $R$ as the Least Fixed Point {#free-generative-least-fixed-point}

The **self-generation theorem** (§self-generation) proves:
$$
R_{\mathrm{gen}} \;=\; \mathrm{Obj}(R).
$$
Combined with $U_G(R) = R$, this gives the complete picture:

- $R$ is **inflationary**: the iteration $A \subseteq R_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq R$
  is a tower of inclusions of full relational universes.
- $R$ is **a fixed point**: $U_G(R) = R$ (nothing new is generated from $R$'s own
  resources).
- $R$ is the **least fixed point above $A$**: any $U_G$-closed class containing the
  atoms contains $R$.

In the language of algebra: **$R$ is the initial algebra of the functor $U_G$** on
the category of $U_G$-closed classes containing $A$.

#### The Depth Tower as the Free Generative Sequence {#free-generative-tower}

The depth tower
$$
R_0 \;\hookrightarrow\; R_1 \;\hookrightarrow\; R_2 \;\hookrightarrow\; \cdots
\;\hookrightarrow\; R \;=\; \mathrm{colim}_n\, R_n
$$
is the **free generative sequence**: each $R_n$ is the universe freely generated by
the atoms after exactly $n$ applications of $U_G$. The colimit $R$ is the universe
freely generated without bound.

Each inclusion $R_n \hookrightarrow R_{n+1}$ adds exactly the objects constructible
from depth-$n$ data using one more application of the nuclear quartet. There is nothing
arbitrary about this sequence: it is fully determined by $U_G$ and $A$.

#### The Meta-Level Generative Act {#free-generative-meta}

The hyperverse tower operates $U$ at a **higher level**: feeding $R$ itself as
algebra-level input generates $R^{(1)} \supset R$; iterating produces the tower
$$
R \;\hookrightarrow\; R^{(1)} \;\hookrightarrow\; R^{(2)} \;\hookrightarrow\; \cdots
$$
whose colimit is the Relational Hyperverse $\mathcal{H}$ (§relational-hyperverse).
The meta-level generative act $U_{\mathcal{H}}$ then satisfies
$\mathcal{H} = U_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H})$ — the same fixed-point equation,
one level up.

The hyperverse is thus the **free generative act at the meta-level**: the least
fixed point of the meta-level closure operator starting from $R$ as the atom.

#### Summary: Free → Fixed Point for Generative Acts {#free-generative-summary}

| | Object | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Atoms | $A = \{y(t)\} \cup \{H\}$ | The seeds; no generation yet |
| Depth tower | $R_0 \subset R_1 \subset \cdots$ | Free generation, step by step |
| Ground fixed point | $R = \mathrm{colim}_n R_n$ | Self-generating; $U_G(R) = R$ |
| Meta-tower | $R \subset R^{(1)} \subset \cdots$ | Meta-level free generation |
| Hyperverse | $\mathcal{H} = \mathrm{colim}_n R^{(n)}$ | Meta-fixed point; $U_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H}) = \mathcal{H}}$ |

At every level, the pattern is the same: free generation from given atoms,
transfinite iteration, fixed-point colimit. The generative act is self-similar
across levels.
