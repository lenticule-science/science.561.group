# CLN Media Cycle
## Product Spec

---

### Project Overview

- Title: *Chunka Luta Network*
- Format: Rotating discussion series, 4-part cycles
- Core Function: Collective analysis of material events reshaping life on Turtle Island, produced through Indigenous and Black political frameworks and anti-imperial critique
- Cycle Model: One topic → four short discussions → four companion newsletters
- Runtime: ~20 minutes per episode
- Release Cadence: Weekly or biweekly
- Distribution: Existing CLN podcast channels
- Outputs: Audio episode + short newsletter per release; compiled PDF at cycle end

### Structure

#### Cycle Layout

- Episode 1: Introduction & History
- Focus: Frame the event within historical, economic, and colonial lineage.
- Episode 2: Critical Perspective
- Focus: Structural and class analysis; identify contradictions.
- Episode 3: Indigenous Perspective
- Focus: Place-based and relational reading of the issue.
- Episode 4: Current Actions
- Focus: What is happening now; how communities are organizing.

Each cycle is one coherent inquiry made by 2–4 participants per episode, with facilitation rotating across the series.

### Participant Roles

- Cycle Coordinator
- Handles scheduling, delivery, and continuity for one topic.
- Rotates every cycle.
- Episode Facilitator
- Guides discussion and ensures even participation.
- Different for each episode.
- Editorial Circle
- Collective that approves topics and reviews episodes before release.
- Standing group.
- Production Support
- Manages recording, editing, and uploading.
- Participates as available.
- Newsletter Editor
- Summarizes each episode and curates sources and commentary.
- Rotates as capacity allows.

### Topic Criteria

Each topic must:

1. Address a current event or process altering material conditions on Turtle Island.
2. Offer substance for analysis across historical, critical, and Indigenous perspectives.
3. Be time-relevant and useful for organizers, educators, or community members.
4. Allow coverage across all four lenses (intro/history, critical, Indigenous, current actions).

Examples of viable topics include:
- Resource conflicts or extractive projects.
- Strikes, labor movements, or workplace actions.
- Land reclamation and sovereignty struggles.
- Surveillance or policy shifts.
- Ecological and climate disruptions.

### Production Cycle

#### Pre-Production
- Editorial Circle proposes 2–3 potential topics each month.
- Collective selects one topic for development.
- Assign a cycle coordinator and facilitators.
- Prepare minimal outline and resource links for participants.

#### Recording
- Remote or in-person group recording, single session per episode.
- Target 20–25 minutes finished length.
- Keep setup simple and reproducible; use available tools.

#### Editing & Release
- Perform basic cleanup and volume leveling.
- Upload to CLN’s existing podcast feed.
- Include concise show description and participant names.

#### Newsletter
- One email per episode summarizing key points.
- Include 2–4 readings and 1–3 listener or community comments.
- The fourth newsletter of each cycle provides synthesis and action links.

#### Archiving
- After each cycle, compile episode transcripts and newsletters into a single downloadable PDF for the CLN Library.

### Roles & Practices

- Cycle Coordinator maintains schedule, ensures materials are gathered, and delivers files for release.
- Facilitators open and close episodes and maintain the throughline across the cycle.
- Editorial Circle meets monthly for topic selection, review, and planning.
- Production Support ensures consistency and documentation but holds no creative authority.
- Newsletter Editor translates discussion into a short public summary and reading list.

---

### Engagement Loop

1. Collect audience feedback via email, social media, or chat channels.
2. Select and feature listener comments in newsletters (with consent).
3. Produce a quarterly “Community Convergence” episode for reflection.
4. Publish periodic public updates on ongoing projects or upcoming cycles.

---

### Governance And Values

- Collective Voice
- No fixed host; episodes are dialogues, not performances.
- Transparency
- Credits, process, and finances documented within the network.
- Accessibility
- Written summaries and archives accompany every audio release.
- Non-Commercial Orientation
- No paid sponsorships; funding remains grassroots and disclosed internally.
- Continuity
- Each cycle builds upon the last to sustain long-term analytical development.

---

### Review and Iteration

After each cycle:
- Conduct an internal debrief between the coordinator and editorial circle.
- Identify what worked (topic framing, pacing, workload).
- Identify what requires adjustment (participation, structure, editing process).
- Update this specification as needed.

### Summary

The CLN Podcast functions as a modular, rotating analytical forum:
lightweight to produce, consistent in form, collective in voice, and responsive to unfolding events on Turtle Island.
Each cycle documents not only what is happening, but how communities analyze and respond together.

## Editorial policy

### Purpose

This document establishes the editorial principles and decision-making standards for the Chunka Luta Network podcast.

The podcast is an instrument of collective analysis and political education.
Its editorial practice must reflect the ethics and strategic clarity of CLN’s mission:
to study and transform the material conditions of life on Turtle Island through Indigenous, Black, and anti-imperial perspectives.

Editorial policy governs:
- what we cover
- how we cover it
- who participates in shaping the narrative
- how accountability is maintained

### Editorial mandate

1. Primary function
 - To analyze current events that materially alter social, ecological, and political relations on Turtle Island.

2. Core objective
 - To make these events understandable through historical, class, and Indigenous frameworks and to identify points of leverage for collective action.

3. Tone and intent
 - The podcast is not commentary or entertainment.
 - It is an act of study and coordination between movements, organizers, and educators.

### Political orientation

1. Anti-imperial
 - All analysis must trace events to structures of empire, resource extraction, and U.S. colonial governance.
2. Indigenous and Black centered
 - Indigenous and Black contributors must have structural priority in topic selection and discussion framing.
3. Materialist
 - Discussions must anchor in material conditions—labor, land, resource, and policy—not abstraction or individual psychology.

4. Liberatory
 - The goal of each cycle is to strengthen capacity for self-determination and collective survival, not to persuade the state or affirm liberal legitimacy.

### Topic selection policy

1. Eligibility criteria
 - The event must be current and have measurable effects on material conditions.
 - It must sustain four distinct analytical angles: historical, critical, Indigenous, and present action.
 - It must have accessible documentation such as news, primary statements, or movement sources.

2. Approval process
 - The editorial circle meets monthly to review proposed topics.
 - Consensus or three-fourths majority determines final selection.
 - Proposals must include a one-sentence summary, core questions, and potential participants.

3. Conflict of interest
 - Members directly involved in an organizing front may participate but must disclose affiliation.
 - The circle ensures balance and transparency.

### Content guidelines

1. Factual integrity
 - Verify claims with at least two independent or primary sources.
 - Cite data and quotations verbally or in show notes.
 - When information is uncertain, identify it as such.

2. Framing
 - Begin with context: who is affected, by what policy or structure, and why now.
 - End with material reflection: what responses exist or are possible.
 - Avoid sensationalism, moralizing, or simplification.

3. Language and form
 - Use accessible language while respecting technical precision.
 - Do not standardize dialects or suppress speech patterns.
 - Avoid dehumanizing language, slurs, or ridicule.

4. Perspective balance
 - At least one Indigenous and one Black contributor per cycle.
 - Encourage multiple positionalities and disciplines such as organizer, worker, scholar, or artist.
 - Facilitate disagreement as a method of study, not division.

### Participation and attribution

1. Authorship
 - Every participant is an author of the conversation; no hierarchy of host or guest.

2. Credit
 - Each episode lists all contributors by name or chosen pseudonym in show notes and newsletters.

3. Consent
 - Verbal consent to record and publish must be secured before recording.
 - Participants may withdraw permission before release without justification.

4. Compensation
 - Honoraria or mutual-aid contributions are provided when funds permit, following CLN’s transparent resource-sharing model.

### Editorial review process

1. Pre-release review
 - The episode facilitator and one editorial circle member review each episode before publication for:
 - political coherence
 - factual accuracy
 - balance of voices
 - respect for positional context

2. Post-release reflection
 - After each cycle, the editorial circle holds a short review to assess content quality, clarity, and alignment with mission.

3. Corrections policy
 - If factual or ethical errors are found after publication, corrections are made publicly in the following newsletter or episode.

### Community relation and feedback

1. Audience as participant
 - Listeners are part of the analytical process.
 - Feedback, commentary, and suggested sources may be published, with consent, in newsletters or follow-up segments.

2. Conflict resolution
 - Concerns regarding representation, accuracy, or harm are reviewed by the editorial circle.
 - Resolutions may include clarification, redaction, or public response.

3. Community consultation
 - For topics involving specific nations or localities, seek direct input from those communities before or during production whenever possible.

### Publication and accessibility

1. Content access
 - All audio and newsletters are freely accessible through CLN’s channels.
 - No paywall or exclusive content for donors.

2. Archiving
 - Transcripts and source lists are stored in the CLN library and compiled after each cycle.

3. Licensing
 - Published under Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 for free redistribution with attribution and no commercial use.

### Editorial accountability

1. Collective oversight
 - The editorial circle acts as the accountable body for all published work.
 - Major breaches of policy require circle review and documented resolution.

2. Transparency
 - A public note of this policy and any significant updates should be available on CLN’s site.
 - Internal revisions are reviewed quarterly.

3. Ethical standard
 - No content may advance or normalize settler nationalism, white supremacy, patriarchy, ableism, or extractive ideology.

## Operational infrastructure plan

### Purpose

This document defines the operational infrastructure supporting the Chunka Luta Network podcast.
It connects the editorial policy (how we think and speak) and the production spec (how we make things)
to the organizational systems that sustain the work over time.

The plan ensures that the podcast remains:
- collective: decentralized in authorship and leadership
- durable: consistent and reproducible with limited resources
- ethically grounded: aligned with CLN’s anti-imperial and Indigenous values
- integrated: embedded within the broader Chunka Luta Network ecosystem

### Governance and decision structure

Layers of responsibility include:
- editorial circle: decides topics, reviews content, and ensures quality control
- production circle: manages scheduling, recording, editing, and release logistics
- administrative coordinator: maintains archives, finances, and communication; rotates quarterly
- community liaison: gathers audience feedback, manages listener interaction, and represents community voice

Decision-making model:
- consensus when possible
- two-thirds majority for time-sensitive issues

Conflict resolution:
- two members outside the dispute mediate and report the outcome to the circle

Review cadence:
- monthly operational meeting
- quarterly open collective review

### Annual production cycle

Planned production rhythm:
- first quarter (January–March): produce two cycles, update archive and policy documents; complete annual equipment audit
- second quarter (April–June): one cycle plus mid-year reflection; apply for grants or funding if needed
- third quarter (July–September): two cycles with thematic or regional focus; train and onboard new contributors
- fourth quarter (October–December): one cycle plus year-end synthesis episode; finalize financial summary and infrastructure maintenance

Average goal: six to eight cycles per year (about 24–32 episodes).

### Budget framework

Estimate annual costs

A transparency report is issued every December. Any surplus supports collective projects or contributor aid.


### Communications infrastructure

Channels and their uses:
- Spotify and Substack: main publishing hub for episodes, transcripts, and updates
- social relays (for example, Mastodon, Instagram, or X): brief release announcements or quotes
- internal workspace (for example, Discord): coordination and file links
- public email contact: listener feedback and collaboration requests
- outreach kit: a one-page PDF describing mission, format, and participation options


### Community and audience development

Key practices:
- feedback loop: simple form or email for listener responses; include selected comments in newsletters (with consent)
- community convergences: quarterly virtual gatherings to reflect on cycles and plan new ones
- educational integration: study guides or reading packets created for organizers and classrooms
- partnerships: collaborations with Indigenous and anti-imperial media or presses
- metrics review: brief monthly check on reach and engagement, for assessment rather than competition

### Creative identity and documentation

Supporting materials:
- reusable logo and cover templates for each cycle
- optional short intro or outro sound for continuity
- consistent metadata fields for title, cycle, contributors, tags, and summary
- archive integration linking metadata to the CLN library
- short style note on written tone: collective, direct, and non-hierarchical

### Technical maintenance

Core maintenance checklist:
- use stable recording methods accessible to all participants
- standardize editing and export settings for consistency
- ensure transcription and alt text accompany every release
- verify RSS feed and link health quarterly
- maintain backups and review accessibility standards annually

### Training and onboarding

Resources and steps:
- contributor guide: two-page overview of cycle format, facilitation, and file delivery
- production checklist: step-by-step process from recording to publishing
- orientation call: quarterly introduction for new contributors
- mentorship pairing: new facilitators supported by experienced members during their first cycle

### Funding and partnerships

Main support sources:
- Patreon, mutual aid, and direct donations
- secondary sources may include aligned grants in media justice or Indigenous sovereignty
- no for-profit sponsorships or partnerships with state agencies
- financial summary and funding sources reviewed annually

### Evaluation and continuity

After each cycle:
- hold a short debrief to record lessons and improvements
- document outcomes in the cycle folder
- every fourth cycle, publish a public reflection or discussion on key learnings

Annually:
- review editorial and production policies
- rotate or renew roles
- confirm continued alignment with CLN’s mission and capacity

### Long-term sustainability

Foundational practices:
- maintain written documentation for every recurring role
- archive decisions, templates, and correspondence in the CLN library
- each new coordinator reviews the last three cycle debriefs before starting
- plan eventual donation of archives to an Indigenous or movement-run repository

### Operating principle

Every cycle of this podcast is both a record of study and a method of organization.
Infrastructure exists only to make collective reflection reproducible, never to professionalize or centralize it.
The podcast endures when its systems stay simple, transparent, and open to those living the realities it analyzes.
