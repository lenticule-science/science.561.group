# Settle

To settle is to reach a state where further processing produces no change — the fixpoint of a system's internal dynamics after perturbation.

The concept appears across domains:
- In **law**, settlement is the resolution of a dispute — the parties reach a stable agreement that neither side will contest further.
- In **thermodynamics**, equilibrium is the state where macroscopic properties stop changing — the system has settled into its lowest accessible free-energy configuration.
- In **cybernetics** (Beer 1972), a viable system continuously settles — sensing disturbances, responding, and adjusting until the system returns to homeostasis. Beer's Viable System Model is a settlement architecture.
- In **fixpoint theory** (Tarski 1955), the least fixpoint of a monotone function on a complete lattice always exists. If the function only adds information (monotonic in the knowledge order), iteration converges.

In a [relational machine](relational-machine.md), settlement is [respond](respond.md) applied transitively through the entity graph until fixpoint. A [message](message.md) perturbs one entity. That entity's response perturbs its neighbors. Their responses perturb further. Settlement is the process by which these perturbations die out — when every entity has absorbed the consequences and no fiber changes.

The mathematical structure is the composite nucleus π_t = σ_t ∘ Δ_t from the [dual nucleus](../../science/domains/relationality/terms/dual-nucleus.md). The [four-position taxonomy](../../science/domains/relationality/terms/fiber-nuclear-quartet.md) classifies every proposition after settlement: fully settled, backward-settled only, forward-settled only, or free.

The [settlement path](../../humanities/domains/sociology/domains/work/terms/settlement-path.md) applies this concept to work: inquiry settles into derivation, derivation into procedure, procedure into process — each step reducing the judgment required.
