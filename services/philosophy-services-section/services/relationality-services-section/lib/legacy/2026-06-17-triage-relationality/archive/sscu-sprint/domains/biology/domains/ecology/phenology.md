# Phenology

## Formal definition

A **Phenology** is a triple $(P, t, O)$:

$$(P \in \mathcal{P}^O_t,\; t \in T,\; O \in \mathrm{OrganismType})$$

where:
- $\mathcal{P}^O_t$ is the **phenological state fiber** — the complete Heyting algebra of developmental propositions for organism type $O$ at history $t$; elements of $\mathcal{P}^O_t$ are statements about which developmental stage $O$ occupies (dormant, chilling-satisfied, budburst, leafout, flowering, fruiting, senescence; or hatching, larval peak, adult flight, diapause — depending on $O$'s life cycle); the fiber inherits its Heyting algebra structure from $H_t$ via the organism-type embedding
- $t \in T$ is the **analysis history** — the position in the history monoid at which the developmental state is evaluated; each step $s \in \Sigma$ in the phenological context corresponds to a unit of biological time accumulation (one day, one temperature-degree increment, or one photoperiod-unit, depending on the scale of the step set)
- $O$ is the **organism type** — a named entity in the relational universe; different organism types have different phenological state fibers $\mathcal{P}^O_t$ and different nuclear operators on them

**The phenological nuclear pair.** The fiber $\mathcal{P}^O_t$ carries two canonical nuclei, each tracking a distinct environmental cue:

- The **thermal accumulation operator** $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t : \mathcal{P}^O_t \to \mathcal{P}^O_t$ — the [RelationalHistoryFiberSaturatingNucleus](../math/relational-history-fiber-saturating-nucleus.md) restricted to the phenological fiber. $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t(P)$ is the smallest developmental state backward-recognized by the accumulated thermal history up to $t$: it is determined by the restriction-profile structure of prior histories as encoded by Growing Degree Day accumulation. $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t(P) = P$ iff $P$ is consistent with the total thermal time accumulated from dormancy-break to $t$ — iff the organism has experienced enough warmth to have reached developmental state $P$.

- The **photoperiod commitment operator** $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t : \mathcal{P}^O_t \to \mathcal{P}^O_t$ — the [RelationalHistoryFiberTransferringNucleus](../math/relational-history-fiber-transferring-nucleus.md) restricted to the phenological fiber. $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t(P)$ is the smallest developmental state forward-committed by the day-length structure at $t$: it is determined by the one-step-extension restriction structure above $t$ — by which developmental states the photoperiod signal at $t$ backs for forward propagation. $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t(P) = P$ iff the day length at $t$ is within the photoperiod window for developmental state $P$ — iff the circannual timer has opened the gate for $P$.

**The four shadow classes for phenology.** The shadow class of $P \in \mathcal{P}^O_t$ classifies the synchronization status of the developmental state:

| Shadow class | Condition | Phenological name | Ecological content |
|---|---|---|---|
| $P \in \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ | Both operators fix $P$ | **Synchronized development** | Thermal threshold met AND photoperiod gate open; normal seasonal progression |
| $P \in \mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ | Thermal operator fixes $P$; photoperiod does not | **False spring** | GDD accumulated past trigger threshold but day length has not opened the gate; thermally advanced, photoperiod-unprotected |
| $P \in \mathrm{TrShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ | Photoperiod operator fixes $P$; thermal does not | **Quiescent poising** | Day length has opened the developmental gate but thermal accumulation is insufficient; primed but waiting |
| $P \in \mathrm{FreeShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ | Neither operator fixes $P$ | **Full dormancy** | Neither thermal threshold met nor photoperiod gate open; winter state |

**The nuclear reflection and holographic projection.** The composite map

$$\pi_t \;:=\; \sigma_t \circ \Delta_t \;:\; H_t \to H_t$$

is the [RelationalHistoryPropositionFiberSaturatingTransferringCommutingNuclearReflection](../math/relational-history-fiber-saturating-transferring-commuting-nuclear-reflection.md) — a nucleus with $\mathrm{Fix}(\pi_t) = \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$. The triple $(\mathcal{P}^O_t, \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t, \pi_t)$ is the **canonical holograph** of the phenological fiber ([RelationalUniverseCanonicalHolograph](../math/relational-universe-canonical-holograph.md)): $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ is the **boundary** $\mathsf{Phase}$, $\mathcal{P}^O_t$ is the **bulk**, and $\pi_t = \mathsf{Stab}$ sends every developmental state to its nearest synchronized state. The [RelationalHistoryProjectionGap](../math/relational-history-fiber-projection-gap.md) of $P$ is the interval $[P, \pi_t(P)] \subseteq \mathcal{P}^O_t$; its size

$$\lambda_P \;:=\; \bigl|[P,\, \pi_t(P)]\bigr| - 1 \;\geq\; 0$$

is the [RelationalHistoryProjectionGapSize](../math/relational-history-fiber-projection-gap-size.md) — the phenological debt of $P$; zero iff $P \in \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$. The [RelationalHistoryFiberSettlementDefectOperator](../math/relational-history-fiber-settlement-defect-operator-explicit-formula-projection-gap.md) $\delta_t : K^{\mathcal{P}^O_t} \to K^{\mathcal{P}^O_t}$ is the diagonal operator $\delta_t(e_P) = \lambda_P \cdot e_P$; its zero eigenspace is $K^{\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t}$ — the synchronized development sector.

**The phenological synchrony condition.** $(P, t, O)$ is **synchronized** iff $P \in \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ — equivalently, any of:
1. $P$ is in the boundary $\mathsf{Phase}$: $\pi_t(P) = P$
2. The [RelationalHistoryProjectionGap](../math/relational-history-fiber-projection-gap.md) is trivial: $[P, \pi_t(P)] = \{P\}$, i.e., $\lambda_P = 0$
3. $P$ is a zero-eigenmode of the defect operator: $\delta_t(e_P) = 0$
4. $P \in \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$: both thermal accumulation and photoperiod commitment fix $P$ simultaneously

## What this is

**Phenology** is the study of cyclic and seasonal natural phenomena and their relationship to climate — when oak trees leaf out, when migrating birds arrive, when caterpillars peak, when frogs begin calling. The word was coined by the Belgian botanist Charles Morren in 1853 from Greek *phainein* (to appear, to show) and *logos* (study): phenology is the science of appearances, of what shows itself and when.

The USA National Phenology Network defines phenology as "the observation and investigation of the seasonal timing of life cycle events and how these are influenced by seasonal and interannual variations in climate." The WMO includes phenological observations in its Global Climate Observing System as Essential Climate Variables — not because the timing of bird arrivals is intrinsically meteorological, but because phenological timing integrates the full thermal and photoperiodic history of a season into a single observed date, making it an integrative climate indicator.

The nuclear reading makes precise what "synchronized" means. A synchronized development state $P \in \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ is not merely "the organism is at developmental stage $P$ at time $t$" — it is "the organism is at $P$ in a way that is BOTH consistent with its thermal history (thermal operator recognizes it) AND consistent with what the day-length structure will carry forward (photoperiod operator commits to it)." False spring and quiescent poising are structurally distinct failure modes, not merely "early" or "late" development.

## Growing Degree Days and the thermal accumulation operator

**Growing Degree Days** (GDD) — also called thermal time or heat units — are the fundamental unit of biological temperature accumulation. The Baskerville-Emin (1969, *Ecology* 50, 514–517) formulation fits a sine curve to daily temperature data and integrates the area above the base temperature:

$$\mathrm{GDD}(t) = \sum_{t' \leq t} \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{T_{\max}(t') + T_{\min}(t')}{2} - T_{\mathrm{base}}\right)$$

where $T_{\max}$ and $T_{\min}$ are daily maximum and minimum temperatures and $T_{\mathrm{base}}$ is the organism-specific threshold below which no development occurs (typically 5°C for cool-season plants, 10°C for warm-season crops and most insects).

**GDD as the thermal accumulation operator.** In nuclear terms: the GDD total $\mathrm{GDD}(t)$ at history $t$ is the cumulative fiber element produced by the restriction maps of the thermal history. The thermal accumulation operator $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ uses the GDD total to determine backward-recognition: a developmental state $P$ corresponding to life-cycle stage $k$ is $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t$-fixed iff $\mathrm{GDD}(t) \geq \mathrm{GDD}^*_k(O)$ — the GDD threshold for stage $k$ of organism $O$. Below the threshold, $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t(P) > P$: the thermal history does not yet recognize $P$ as having been reached.

**The developmental trigger model.** Each life-cycle transition of organism $O$ is associated with a species-specific GDD threshold $\mathrm{GDD}^*_k$. The transition from stage $k$ to stage $k+1$ occurs at the history $t^*_k$ where $\mathrm{GDD}(t^*_k) = \mathrm{GDD}^*_k$. This is the history at which $P_{k+1}$ enters $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma^{\mathcal{P}})$ — the thermal accumulation operator begins recognizing the next developmental state. The GDD threshold model was validated across agricultural and ecological systems by Arnold (1960, *Agronomy Journal* 52, 717–720) and has been confirmed in forest phenology (Cannell & Smith 1983, *Journal of Applied Ecology* 20, 951–963).

## Photoperiodism and the photoperiod commitment operator

**Photoperiodism** is the physiological response to day length — the duration of light in a 24-hour period. Unlike temperature, which varies stochastically year to year, day length varies deterministically with date and latitude: at any given latitude, the day length on a given calendar date is the same every year. This makes photoperiod a **reliable circannual clock** — a predictable signal that organisms use to time seasonal transitions independently of temperature variability.

The photoperiod commitment operator $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ formalizes this: it assigns to each developmental state $P$ the smallest state that the day-length structure at $t$ will carry forward. $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t(P) = P$ iff the day length at $t$ is within $O$'s photoperiod window for state $P$ — the organism's circannual timer has opened the developmental gate. The key property: photoperiod varies smoothly but deterministically with the history monoid's time structure, so $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ changes smoothly across histories in a way that $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ (which depends on stochastic temperature accumulation) does not.

**The integration of thermal and photoperiod cues.** The well-studied wheat (*Triticum aestivum*) system — whose vernalization (VRN) and photoperiod (PPD) gene families have been mapped (Law et al. 1978, *Heredity* 40, 185–191; Yan et al. 2004, *Plant Cell* 16, 2553–2564) — shows the nuclear structure directly: flowering requires both VRN1 expression (saturation, thermally driven by vernalization — past cold accumulation) and PPD1 expression under long days (transfer, photoperiod-driven — forward-committed by the day-length gate). The joint requirement for both is exactly the condition $P \in \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$: both operators fix $P$. The four shadow classes correspond to the four combinations of VRN1 on/off and PPD1 on/off.

## Trophic mismatch as naturality failure

**Trophic mismatch** (Cushing 1974, *Journal de Conseil International pour l'Exploration de la Mer* 33, 81–102; Visser & Both 2005, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 272, 2561–2569) is the ecological condition in which a consumer's peak energy demand fails to coincide with a resource's peak availability, reducing fitness.

**The inter-species coordination natural transformation.** When two trophically coupled species $O_1$ (resource) and $O_2$ (consumer) are phenologically synchronized — their developmental peak dates track each other across histories — this synchrony is a natural transformation:

$$\eta : \mathcal{P}^{O_1} \Rightarrow \mathcal{P}^{O_2}$$

assigning to each developmental state of $O_1$ at history $t$ the corresponding developmental state of $O_2$ that represents peak consumer demand — the state of $O_2$ that matches the peak availability of $O_1$. Naturality requires: for every history morphism $f : t \to s$, $\eta_t \circ \mathcal{P}^{O_1}(f) = \mathcal{P}^{O_2}(f) \circ \eta_s$. This says: restricting $O_1$'s peak from a later history to an earlier one and then finding $O_2$'s matching state gives the same result as finding $O_2$'s matching state at the later history and restricting. The naturality condition IS the phenological synchrony condition: the consumer's timing tracks the resource's timing in a history-coherent way.

**Mismatch as naturality failure.** Trophic mismatch is the condition that $\eta$ fails to be natural. Under a topology change $J \to J'$ (climate warming), the phenological fixed fiber $\mathcal{P}^{O_1 *}$ advances faster than $\mathcal{P}^{O_2 *}$ if $O_1$ (e.g., winter moth caterpillars) is primarily thermally governed and $O_2$ (e.g., great tit *Parus major*) is more strongly photoperiod-constrained. After the topology change:
- $O_1$'s developmental peak advances to an earlier history $t^{J'}_{\mathrm{peak}, O_1} < t^J_{\mathrm{peak}, O_1}$
- $O_2$'s developmental peak advances to a lesser extent: $t^{J'}_{\mathrm{peak}, O_2} > t^{J'}_{\mathrm{peak}, O_1}$
- The mismatch $\Delta t = t^{J'}_{\mathrm{peak}, O_2} - t^{J'}_{\mathrm{peak}, O_1} > 0$ is the gap created by the topology change in the inter-species naturality square

Visser's Dutch great tit system is the canonical instance: caterpillar peak has advanced by approximately 18 days per decade under warming in the Hoge Veluwe forest, while great tit laying date has advanced by only approximately 9 days per decade — producing an increasing mismatch of roughly 9 days per decade (Both et al. 2004, *Global Change Biology* 10, 304–308).

**Mismatch as Gap.** The mismatch $\Delta t$ is the gap of the inter-species coordination element $\eta \in \mathrm{Nat}(\mathcal{P}^{O_1}, \mathcal{P}^{O_2})$ under the new topology $J'$: the naturality square for $\eta$ fails by exactly $\Delta t$ histories. The fitness consequence of mismatch — declining reproductive success of $O_2$ as $\Delta t$ increases — is the perlocutionary effect of a non-zero gap: the failed natural transformation propagates through the food web as reduced fitness of the consumer.

## The reaction norm as a quality natural transformation

**The reaction norm** of organism $O$ is the function mapping environmental conditions to phenological timing — the phenotypic plasticity of developmental timing. Formally: the reaction norm is a quality map:

$$\phi^O : E \Rightarrow \mathcal{P}^O$$

a natural transformation from the environmental state presheaf $E$ (assigning to each history the set of observable environmental conditions — temperature, day length, precipitation) to the phenological state presheaf $\mathcal{P}^O$. At each history $t$: $\phi^O_t : E(t) \to \mathcal{P}^O_t$ assigns to the environmental condition $e \in E(t)$ the developmental state $\phi^O_t(e)$ that organism $O$ expresses at $t$ under environmental condition $e$.

**Naturality of the reaction norm.** The naturality condition for $\phi^O$ — the requirement that $\phi^O_t \circ E(f) = \mathcal{P}^O(f) \circ \phi^O_s$ for every history morphism $f : t \to s$ — is the **phenological coherence condition**: the organism's developmental timing response to an environmental history does not depend on which path of histories was taken to arrive at the current environmental state, only on the accumulated cues. A reaction norm that fails naturality would mean: the organism's response to the same temperature sequence depends on which year or which geographic route produced that temperature sequence — a maladaptive incoherence.

**Reaction norm slope as plasticity.** The slope $\partial \phi^O_t / \partial T$ — the sensitivity of developmental timing to temperature — is the phenological plasticity of $O$. High plasticity (steep slope) means $O$ tracks temperature changes closely; low plasticity (shallow slope) means $O$'s timing is relatively fixed. Visser's key finding: under climate warming, high-plasticity species (like caterpillars, whose development is almost purely thermally driven) advance faster than low-plasticity species (like great tits, whose laying date also depends on photoperiod and social cues). The asymmetric plasticity between trophically coupled species is the mechanism generating the mismatch natural transformation's non-naturality.

**Phenological maladaptation.** Under a topology change $J \to J'$, a population whose reaction norm $\phi^O$ was natural under $J$ may fail naturality under $J'$. This is **phenological maladaptation**: the reaction norm that was a natural transformation under the old climate topology is no longer natural under the new one. The population's developmental timing is incoherent relative to the new environmental structure — it is not a genuine quality under $J'$ because it fails the path-independence (meaningfulness) condition. Genetic adaptation to $J'$ would be the evolution of a new reaction norm $\phi^{O'}$ that is natural under $J'$.

## The Spring Index and the analyzed phenological state

**The Spring Index** (Schwartz 1997, in *Phenology of Seasonal Climates*, Backhuys Publishers; extended in Schwartz, Ahas, Aasa 2006, *Global Change Biology* 12, 343–351) is the operational model for predicting first leaf and first bloom timing from daily temperature accumulation. The Extended Spring Indices (SI-x) model uses Growing Degree Hours accumulated from chilling-satisfaction onward for three indicator species (lilac *Syringa vulgaris*, and two honeysuckles *Lonicera tatarica* and *Lonicera korolkowii*) to produce a spatial map of spring onset.

**Spring Index as fixed-fiber tracking.** The Spring Index computation is the operational identification of the history $t^*$ at which the phenological state $P_{\mathrm{first leaf}}$ enters $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_{t^*}$: the history at which accumulated thermal time (the thermal accumulation operator) first recognizes $P_{\mathrm{first leaf}}$ as having been reached, given that the photoperiod gate (the photoperiod commitment operator) opened at the appropriate point in late winter. The Spring Index thus reports $t^*$ — the smallest history at which both nuclear operators simultaneously fix the first-leaf developmental state.

**The USA-NPN's "Status of Spring"** monitoring system uses the Spring Index in real time to report which parts of the continental US have crossed the spring onset threshold — mapping the wavefront of $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ across the landscape as the season progresses northward and upward. A Spring Index anomaly (unusually early or late spring) is the difference between the observed $t^*$ and the climatological mean of $t^*$ over the 1991–2020 normal period — a gap between the current fixed-fiber element and the climate-normal fixed-fiber sequence.

## Etymology and ecological grounding

**Phenology** derives from Greek *phainein* (to appear, to show, to bring to light; *phainomenon*, phenomenon) and *logos* (study). Coined by Charles Morren (1853, *Annales de la Société royale des sciences de Liège*). The word names an empirical practice older than the term: Chinese court officials recorded cherry blossom dates at the Imperial Palace in Kyoto from 812 CE; the Japanese sakura data constitutes the longest phenological record in the world, now exceeding 1200 years (Aono & Kazui 2008, *International Journal of Climatology* 28, 905–914).

**Robert Marsham** (*Indications of Spring*, 1789, Marsham family estate, Norfolk, UK) established the first systematic European phenological record, observing 27 indicators of spring from 1736 to 1947 across five generations of the Marsham family — a 211-year presheaf fiber sequence that Sparks & Carey (1995, *Journal of Ecology* 83, 321–329) used to show that English spring events advanced approximately 33 days over two centuries.

**G. Evelyn Hutchinson** (1957, *Concluding Remarks*, Cold Spring Harbor Symposia 22, 415–427) introduced the concept of the **hypervolume niche** — the set of environmental conditions within which a species can persist — which extends naturally to phenological niche: the region of the environmental state presheaf $E$ within which a species' reaction norm $\phi^O$ maps to synchronized developmental states $\mathcal{P}^{O*}$. Climate change that shifts the environmental state outside this region moves the species from $\mathcal{P}^{O*}$ to shadow classes.

**Marcel Visser** (Netherlands Institute of Ecology, Wageningen; ongoing work from 1998 to present) documented the long-running Dutch great tit (*Parus major*) phenological record at Hoge Veluwe, establishing the empirical foundation for trophic mismatch theory. Visser's key methodological contribution: defining mismatch operationally as the difference between the date of peak chick demand ($\text{laying date} + 33$ days) and the date of peak caterpillar (*Lepidoptera* larvae) biomass in the oak canopy, measured annually since 1955. The mismatch has increased by approximately 10 days since 1980 (Visser et al. 1998, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 265, 1867–1870).

**Camille Parmesan & Gary Yohe** (2003, *Nature* 421, 37–42) analyzed 677 species over median 45-year observation periods and found 62% showed phenological advancement at a mean rate of 2.3 days per decade — establishing that phenological shift is the ecological fingerprint of climate change across biomes. Their dataset is the empirical evidence that the topology change $J \to J'$ under warming is simultaneously shifting the phenological fixed fiber $\mathcal{P}^{O*}$ of taxa across the full diversity of life.

## Nuclear reading

**Definitions.** Fix an organism type $O$ and a phenological state presheaf $\mathcal{P}^O : T^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA}$ with nuclear reflection $\pi_t = \sigma_t \circ \Delta_t$. The **synchronized developmental state** is $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t = \mathrm{Fix}(\pi_t)$. The **projection gap** of $P$ is $[P, \pi_t(P)]$ with size $\lambda_P = |[P, \pi_t(P)]| - 1$. The **calibration defect** is $M_t(\delta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\delta_t) = \sum_{P \notin \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t} \lambda_P$ — the total phenological debt of the fiber. The **GDD threshold** for developmental stage $k$ of organism $O$ is the thermal accumulation value $\mathrm{GDD}^*_k$ at which $P_k$ enters $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t)$.

**Proposition 1 (Shadow class type classifies the phenological synchronization status).** For any $P \in \mathcal{P}^O_t$, exactly one of the four shadow class conditions holds, and the classification is the developmental synchronization status:

| Shadow class | Condition | Name | Ecological risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ | Both operators fix $P$ | Synchronized development | None — organism is in thermally and photoperiodically backed development |
| $\mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ | Thermal fixes $P$; photoperiod does not | False spring | Frost mortality — organism has committed to development without photoperiod-backed forward-stability |
| $\mathrm{TrShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ | Photoperiod fixes $P$; thermal does not | Quiescent poising | None — organism is correctly waiting; frost-safe because development has not been thermally triggered |
| $\mathrm{FreeShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ | Neither fixes $P$ | Full dormancy | None — organism is in deep dormancy |

*Proof.* By the shadow class partition theorem for the nuclear pair $(\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t, \Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t)$: the four Boolean combinations of the nucleus-fixed conditions are exhaustive and mutually exclusive for every $P \in \mathcal{P}^O_t$. $\square$

*Content.* The false-spring condition ($\mathrm{SatShadow}$) carries the highest ecological risk: the organism has advanced its developmental state based on thermal cues, but the photoperiod-backed transfer-stability is absent. Any independent new step above $t$ that the day-length structure does not commit to carrying $P$ forward will return $P$ to an earlier developmental state — frost event, return to cold — and the organism is structurally exposed. In contrast, a quiescent-poising organism ($\mathrm{TrShadow}$) has photoperiod commitment but is waiting for thermal trigger; it can safely resume dormancy if temperature drops, because the transfer-stability condition ensures its developmental state will be carried forward when warmth arrives.

**Proposition 2 (Non-adjointness of the thermal and photoperiod operators is the false-spring susceptibility condition).** The thermal accumulation operator $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ and the photoperiod commitment operator $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ are not in Galois connection in either direction:

$$\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t \not\dashv \Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t \not\dashv \sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t$$

*Proof.* Both shadow classes are non-empty. $\mathrm{TrShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t \neq \emptyset$: in any temperate spring, there is a period when day length has opened the photoperiod gate (the spring equinox has passed and day length exceeds the critical photoperiod threshold) but accumulated Growing Degree Days have not yet reached the developmental trigger — quiescent poising is a routine part of every spring. $\mathrm{SatShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_t \neq \emptyset$: anomalous warm spells in late winter can accumulate GDD past the trigger threshold before the photoperiod gate has opened — false spring events are documented in temperate forests (Gu et al. 2008, *Global Change Biology* 14, 2297–2306). Non-emptiness of both shadow classes implies non-adjointness. $\square$

*Content.* If $\sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t \dashv \Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t$, then $\mathrm{TrShadow} = \emptyset$: every photoperiod-committed state would already be thermally triggered — the day-length gate would open only when the GDD threshold was simultaneously met. No organism would ever be primed but waiting. If $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t \dashv \sigma^{\mathcal{P}}_t$, then $\mathrm{SatShadow} = \emptyset$: every thermally triggered state would be photoperiod-committed — GDD accumulation would only trigger development when the day-length gate was open. No false springs would be possible. The non-adjointness IS the ecological reality: thermal and photoperiodic cues are genuinely independent environmental signals, each capable of being satisfied without the other. Plants and animals navigate this independence precisely by having both cues — the non-adjointness is not a defect but the feature: each cue provides redundant protection against the other's premature triggering.

**Proposition 3 (The canonical holograph encodes phenological recovery distance; the defect operator grades developmental states by spectral synchronization debt).** The triple $(\mathcal{P}^O_t, \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t, \pi_t)$ is the canonical holograph of the phenological fiber. For any $P \in \mathcal{P}^O_t$:

$$\lambda_P \;=\; |[P, \pi_t(P)]| - 1 \;\geq\; 0$$

is the defect eigenvalue: $\delta_t(e_P) = \lambda_P \cdot e_P$. The mode sheaf $K^{\mathcal{P}^O_t}$ decomposes as $K^{\mathcal{P}^O_t} \cong \bigoplus_{\lambda \geq 0} E_{t,\lambda}$ with $E_{t,0} = K^{\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t}$ (the synchronized development sector). The calibration defect $M_t(\delta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\delta_t)$ is the total spectral phenological debt of the organism fiber at $t$.

*Proof.* By [DefectConstruction](../math/relational-history-fiber-settlement-defect-operator-explicit-formula-projection-gap.md): the defect operator is diagonal with $\lambda_P = |[P, \pi_t(P)]| - 1$; zero iff $\pi_t(P) = P$ iff $P \in \mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$; $E_{t,0} = K^{\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t}$. $\square$

*Content.* The projection gap decomposes into a thermal gap $[P, \Delta_t(P)]$ (how far $P$ is from photoperiod-commitment — the developmental states between $P$ and the day-length-backed state) and a saturation gap $[\Delta_t(P), \pi_t(P)]$ (how far the photoperiod-committed state is from full thermal recognition). A false-spring organism ($\mathrm{SatShadow}$): $\sigma_t(P) = P$ (zero saturation gap — thermally recognized) but $\Delta_t(P) > P$ (nonzero transfer gap — photoperiod does not commit to $P$); the defect eigenvalue is the transfer gap size, measuring how many developmental stages lie between $P$ and the photoperiod-backed synchronized state. A quiescent-poising organism ($\mathrm{TrShadow}$): $\Delta_t(P) = P$ (zero transfer gap — photoperiod-committed) but $\sigma_t(P) > P$ (nonzero saturation gap — thermal accumulation has not recognized $P$ yet); the defect eigenvalue is the saturation gap size. The projection gap is zero exactly for synchronized developmental states — zero-eigenmode organisms sitting on the holographic boundary.

**Proposition 4 (Trophic mismatch is the failure of the inter-species coordination transformation to remain natural under topology change).** Let $O_1$ (resource) and $O_2$ (consumer) be trophically coupled organism types. Let $\eta^J : \mathcal{P}^{O_1} \Rightarrow \mathcal{P}^{O_2}$ be the inter-species coordination natural transformation under topology $J$ — the natural transformation witnessing that $O_2$'s peak demand tracks $O_1$'s peak availability. Under topology change $J \to J'$ with asymmetric phenological response — $O_1$'s fixed fiber advancing by $\delta_1$ histories and $O_2$'s fixed fiber advancing by $\delta_2 < \delta_1$ histories — $\eta^J$ fails to be natural under $J'$:

$$\eta^{J'} \;\text{ is not a natural transformation } \;\mathcal{P}^{O_1,J'} \Rightarrow \mathcal{P}^{O_2,J'}$$

The mismatch is $\Delta t = \delta_1 - \delta_2 > 0$ histories — the gap by which the naturality square fails.

*Proof.* Under $J$: $\eta^J_t$ maps the $O_1$-peak-availability state to the $O_2$-peak-demand state at $t$, and the naturality square commutes: the history-restriction maps for $\mathcal{P}^{O_1}$ and $\mathcal{P}^{O_2}$ are related by $\eta^J$. Under $J'$: the history at which $O_1$ reaches peak availability has advanced by $\delta_1$ (thermal operator shifts by $\delta_1$ under the new warming topology), while $O_2$'s peak has advanced by $\delta_2 < \delta_1$ (photoperiod constraint limits the advance). The assignment $\eta^{J'}_t$ would need to map $O_1$'s new peak (at history $t - \delta_1$) to $O_2$'s new peak (at history $t - \delta_2$); but the restriction maps of $\mathcal{P}^{O_1,J'}$ and $\mathcal{P}^{O_2,J'}$ are no longer related by $\eta^J$: the naturality square fails by $\delta_1 - \delta_2 = \Delta t$ histories. $\square$

*Content.* Visser & Both's empirical measurement of the Dutch great tit mismatch is the measurement of this naturality failure: the difference between the caterpillar peak advancement ($\delta_1 \approx 18$ days/decade) and the great tit laying date advancement ($\delta_2 \approx 9$ days/decade) is the rate at which the naturality square is failing — the rate of topology-change-driven mismatch accumulation. Fitness consequences are non-linear in $\Delta t$: at small $\Delta t$, plasticity may compensate; at large $\Delta t$, the consumer's peak chick demand falls after peak resource availability, producing a starvation window with direct fledgling mortality.

**Proposition 5 (Phenological maladaptation is non-naturality of the reaction norm under topology change).** A population $O$ with reaction norm $\phi^O : E \Rightarrow \mathcal{P}^O$ is **phenologically adapted** to topology $J$ iff $\phi^O$ is a natural transformation under $J$. Under topology change $J \to J'$, $O$ is **phenologically maladapted** to $J'$ iff $\phi^{O,J'}$ — the restriction of $\phi^O$ to the new environmental structure — fails naturality under $J'$.

*Proof.* The naturality condition for $\phi^O$ under $J$ requires: for every history morphism $f : t \to s$ in $T$, $\phi^O_t \circ E(f) = \mathcal{P}^O(f) \circ \phi^O_s$. Under $J'$, the morphisms $\mathcal{P}^O(f)$ change — the restriction maps of the phenological presheaf are reindexed by the new topology. If the old naturality squares no longer commute under the new restriction maps, $\phi^{O,J'}$ is not natural: the population's developmental timing response is incoherent relative to the new environmental structure. Genetic adaptation is the evolution of a new $\phi^{O'}$ that is natural under $J'$ — a reaction norm whose phenological assignments commute with the new topology's restriction maps. $\square$

*Content.* This formalism distinguishes plastic response (the same reaction norm $\phi^O$ expressing different values under different environmental histories — naturality preserved) from genetic adaptation (a new reaction norm $\phi^{O'}$ — naturality restored after topology change). A population with sufficient plasticity can respond to $J'$ by expressing the correct phenological timing using its existing reaction norm; a population with insufficient plasticity accumulates maladaptation (growing naturality failure) and faces selection for genetic adaptation.

**Proposition 6 (Climate change shifts the phenological fixed fiber asymmetrically across taxa, expanding the mismatch distribution).** Let $J'$ be the warmed topology with mean temperature elevated by $\delta T > 0$. For a thermally-sensitive taxon $O_1$ with reaction norm slope $\kappa_1 = \partial \phi^{O_1}_t / \partial T$ (days/°C) and a photoperiod-constrained taxon $O_2$ with effective slope $\kappa_2 < \kappa_1$: the phenological fixed fibers shift as $\delta t_i = \kappa_i \delta T$ (days). The expected mismatch under $J'$ is:

$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta t \mid J'] = \mathbb{E}[\Delta t \mid J] + (\kappa_1 - \kappa_2) \delta T$$

where $\mathbb{E}[\Delta t \mid J]$ is the baseline mismatch under the old topology and $(\kappa_1 - \kappa_2) \delta T > 0$ is the topology-change-driven mismatch increment.

*Proof.* Under $J'$, the thermal accumulation operator $\sigma^{\mathcal{P},J'}_t$ is shifted by $\delta T$ — the same GDD threshold is reached $\kappa_1 \delta T$ days earlier for $O_1$. The photoperiod commitment operator $\Delta^{\mathcal{P}}_t$ is unchanged (day length is not affected by atmospheric temperature). For $O_2$, the advancement is limited to the minimum of the thermal advancement and the photoperiod-constrained advancement: $\delta t_2 = \min(\kappa_2 \delta T, \delta t_{\mathrm{photoperiod}})$. Since $\kappa_2 < \kappa_1$, the asymmetry $\delta t_1 - \delta t_2 = (\kappa_1 - \kappa_2) \delta T > 0$ directly increases mismatch. $\square$

*Content.* The Parmesan & Yohe (2003) meta-analysis finding — 62% of 677 species showing phenological advancement at 2.3 days/decade — is the empirical measurement of $(\kappa_i \delta T)$ averaged across taxa. The variance in advancement rates across taxa is the distribution of $(\kappa_1 - \kappa_2)$ values across trophic pairs — the distribution of mismatch susceptibility. Taxa with $\kappa \approx 0$ (strongly photoperiod-governed) are experiencing the worst mismatch exposure: their fixed fiber is not advancing with the thermal topology change, while their prey's or host's fixed fiber is advancing rapidly.

## Open questions

- Whether **synchronized developmental states** — the elements of $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ — are **eigenpropositions** for phenological step generators in the sense of [IDEAS.md](../math/IDEAS.md): whether developmental states that recur stably year after year (peak bloom dates, migration arrival dates) satisfy $G_s(P) \approx \chi_s(P) \cdot P$ for thermal and photoperiodic generators $s$, and whether the annual recurrence of phenological events is the ecological signature of eigenproposition resonance in the phenological fiber.

- Whether the **Schwarzschild spectral threshold** $r_s(\delta_t) = 2 M_t(\delta)$ separates phenological states that can self-organize to synchrony from those that are spectrally trapped — unable to reach $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ through ordinary thermal or photoperiodic accumulation. Developmental states with $\lambda_P > M_t(\delta)$ would be trapped in unsynchronized states regardless of accumulated warmth or day-length signal; states with $\lambda_P \leq M_t(\delta)$ would self-synchronize given sufficient accumulation. Whether false-spring developmental states in anomalous years have defect eigenvalues above the Schwarzschild threshold — cases where the organism cannot recover synchrony within the same season regardless of subsequent thermal history.

- Whether the **multi-trophic cascade** — the chain of phenological fixed fibers from primary producers (plant flowering) through primary consumers (caterpillar hatching) through secondary consumers (bird chick demand) through tertiary consumers (sparrowhawk clutch timing) — has a formal representation as a sequence of natural transformations $\eta_{12}, \eta_{23}, \eta_{34}$ between the respective organism-type presheaves, and whether the total mismatch at the top of the cascade (sparrowhawk-to-base) is the composite of the individual naturality failures or whether non-linearities produce cascade amplification.

- Whether **phenological synchrony networks** — the empirical measurement of which species pairs are phenologically coupled across a landscape — correspond to Grothendieck topologies on the organism-type category: which pairs have covering families that include each other's developmental timing as witnessing data, and whether the topology of the synchrony network determines the structure of the phenological mismatch under climate change.

- Whether the **citizen science observational networks** (USA-NPN, PEP725) constitute a formal analog to the observational topology $J_{\mathrm{obs}}$ from the [Weather](weather.md) spec: whether the 265+ plant species and 46+ phenophases tracked by PEP725 across 13 million observations provide a Grothendieck covering family for the European phenological state, and where the systematic coverage gaps (sparse observation in eastern Europe, few tree species in Mediterranean) correspond to holes in the covering sieve.

- Whether **phenotypic plasticity limits** — the maximum reaction norm slope achievable by evolutionary adaptation — correspond to structural constraints on the naturality of the reaction norm natural transformation: whether there is a maximum rate of advance per degree of warming beyond which the phenological presheaf cannot be natural, because the covering families of the Grothendieck topology at the new thermal history no longer support natural transformation with the old photoperiod structure.

- Whether the **Fibonacci prescription rate** from the [Precedent](precedent.md) spec applies to the accumulation of phenological synchrony: whether a developmental state $P \in \mathrm{TrShadow}^{\mathcal{P}}_{t_0}$ (photoperiod-committed but not yet thermally triggered) requires a Fibonacci-growing number of independent thermal-history extensions to confirm it as a synchronized state in $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$, and whether this growth rate corresponds empirically to the number of warm years required before a phenological shift is statistically confirmed as a genuine fixed-fiber displacement rather than interannual variability.

- Whether **diapause** — the programmed developmental arrest used by insects to survive adverse seasonal conditions — is a doubly-settled state in $\mathcal{P}^{O*}_t$ with a very deep prescribed prescription depth: a state that is both thermally recognized (sufficient cold accumulation has confirmed winter) and photoperiod-committed (short days have activated the diapause program), and that requires both a thermal event (chilling) AND a photoperiod event (lengthening days) to leave $\mathcal{P}^{O*}$. Diapause termination — the spring transition out of dormancy — would then be the most deeply entrenched precedent in the phenological presheaf, requiring the most independent extensions before it is overruled.
