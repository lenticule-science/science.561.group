# Abstract

A [sign](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md) system in which syntactic combination and semantic interpretation are mutually constrained — where every expressible form has a determinate meaning and every constructible meaning has a syntactic name — is a self-sustaining semiotic structure. This paper asks: what is the minimal such structure? The answer is a [mathematical universe](../../../../../../humanities/domains/general/terms/mathematical-universe.md) built within a [Grothendieck universe](../../../concepts/grothendieck-universe/index.md) $U$, constructed as follows:

- a meaning domain $H$ is constructed as the subobject lattice of a category of [sign relations](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md) within $U$, yielding a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) — intuitionistic rather than classical, because not every question about a sign's meaning resolves to yes or no. $H$ is equipped with:
	- a modal closure operator $j$, formalizing [habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md)-formation — the tendency of meanings to stabilize through repeated [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) into habitual interpretations (Peirce's ultimate logical [interpretant](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md)), and
	- a trace comonad $G$, formalizing the sign chain — the provenance that meanings carry from the [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) that produced them (each [interpretant](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md) is itself a [sign](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md), and the chain composes).
- syntactic operators are generated by a [typed lambda calculus](../../../disciplines/type-theory/curricula/typed-lambda-calculus.md), providing the grammar by which signs compose
- a fusion mechanism interprets those syntactic operators as semantic endomorphisms, enforcing coherence between syntax and semantics — the formal counterpart of [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md), the process by which syntactic forms acquire determinate meanings through their interpretive relations.

Three monotone, inflationary [closure operators](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md)
$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}}, \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}, \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ act on the complete [lattice](../../posets/terms/lattice.md) of partial structures. Their composite $\mathcal{S}$ has a [least fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md), which we define as the [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe.

We prove that this [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe is initial in a natural (2-)category of [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) structures: it is the free modal–comonadic [Heyting](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md) structure equipped with an interpretation of the given syntax. The semiotic universe is not a universe about signs — it is the structure that is signing: the minimal self-sustaining system where everything sayable is meaningful and everything meaningful is sayable.

# 1. Ambient Semantic and Algebraic Structure

## 1.0 Metatheory: ZFC + Grothendieck Universe

We work throughout inside a fixed model $(V,\in)$ of Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with Choice (ZFC) augmented by a single [Grothendieck universe](../../../concepts/grothendieck-universe/index.md) $U \subseteq V$. In particular:

- a **set** is any element of $V$;
- a **$U$-small set** is an element of $U$;
- a **structure** (category, lattice, algebra, etc.) is always a tuple of $U$-sets equipped with functions satisfying first-order axioms.

We do not use proper classes or NBG/MK class frameworks. All "smallness" claims are relative to $U$.

This choice ensures that all [closure operators](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) introduced later are bona fide set-functions, and that transfinite iteration is well-defined inside the ambient model. It also guarantees that all structures constructed below are $U$-small, so the complete lattice of partial structures on which the semantic, syntactic, and fusion closures act is a genuine set.

## 1.1 The Category of Sign Relations

The construction needs a domain of possible meanings. Meanings are not atoms — they stand in relations of containment, overlap, and entailment. A [sign relation](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md) is irreducibly triadic (representamen, object, interpretant), and the relations between signs form a structured domain. A category whose morphisms are inclusions gives exactly a domain ordered by containment; the subobject structure then provides the logical operations on meanings.

We fix a $U$-small category $\mathcal{R}$ of **sign relations** — regions of meaning produced by acts of distinction — whose [morphisms](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) are inclusions (monomorphisms). We require:

- $\mathcal{R}$ has a terminal object $1$;
- $\mathcal{R}$ has all pullbacks;
- $\mathcal{R}$ is well-powered: for each object $Y$, the collection $\mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ of subobjects is a $U$-small set.

For a fixed object $Y \in \mathcal{R}$, a **distinction** on $Y$ is a monomorphism $m : X \hookrightarrow Y$. Two distinctions are identified when they factor through each other (the standard equivalence of subobjects). The collection of all such equivalence classes forms a partially ordered set $\mathrm{Sub}(Y)$, ordered by inclusion: $m_1 \le m_2$ iff there exists $i : X_1 \to X_2$ with $m_1 = m_2 \circ i$.

## 1.2 Subobject Classifier and Heyting Structure

We need logical operations on meanings — conjunction, disjunction, implication. Classical logic would require every meaning-claim to resolve to true or false, but the domain of meanings does not have this property: not every question about a sign's meaning is settled. Some meanings are provisional, still undergoing interpretation. The appropriate logic is intuitionistic, whose algebraic semantics is the [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md). The implication $a \Rightarrow b$ is the largest region of the meaning domain compatible with "given $a$, we can reach $b$," without requiring classical decidability. The gap between a meaning $a$ and its [habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md)-formed stabilization $j(a)$ (Section 1.3) marks meanings not yet settled into [habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md) — the regime where [abductive](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/abduction.md) inference operates, generating new hypotheses that further [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) may confirm or discard.

We further require that $\mathcal{R}$ admits a subobject classifier: an object $\Omega$ and a distinguished arrow $\top : 1 \to \Omega$ such that for any monomorphism $m : X \hookrightarrow Y$, there is a unique characteristic [morphism](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) $\chi_m : Y \to \Omega$ making the square

$$
\begin{CD}
X @>>> 1 \\
@VmVV @VV\top V \\
Y @>\chi_m>> \Omega
\end{CD}
$$

a pullback. The existence of $\Omega$ ensures that $\mathcal{R}$ is a topos (given the finite limits above), and the subobject lattice $\mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ inherits the full internal logic.

### Lattice structure

The meet (conjunction) and join (disjunction) on $\mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ are:

- **Meet** $a \wedge b$: the pullback of $a$ and $b$ over $Y$ (intersection);
- **Join** $a \vee b$: the image of the coproduct $X_a + X_b \to Y$ (union);
- **Top** $\top$: the identity $\mathrm{id}_Y : Y \hookrightarrow Y$;
- **Bottom** $\bot$: the initial object $0 \hookrightarrow Y$ (empty subobject).

### Implication via residuation

For $a, b \in \mathrm{Sub}(Y)$, define:

$$
(a \Rightarrow b) := \max\{ x \in \mathrm{Sub}(Y) \mid a \wedge x \le b \}.
$$

This maximum exists because $\mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ is complete (arbitrary joins exist — $U$ contains the power set of any $U$-small set, so the union of any family of subobjects is in $U$). The residuation condition holds:

$$
c \le (a \Rightarrow b) \quad\text{iff}\quad c \wedge a \le b.
$$

### Theorem 1.1 (Sub(Y) is a complete Heyting algebra)

The structure $(\mathrm{Sub}(Y), \wedge, \vee, \Rightarrow, \bot, \top)$ is a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md).

*Proof.* Every topos has the property that $\mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ is a [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) for each object $Y$. The meet is given by pullback, the join by image of coproduct, and the implication by the exponential transpose of the characteristic map. Completeness follows from the [Grothendieck universe](../../../concepts/grothendieck-universe/index.md): for any $U$-small family $\{a_i\}_{i \in I}$ of subobjects, the join $\bigvee_{i \in I} a_i$ exists in $\mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ because $U$ is closed under unions and power sets, so the image of $\coprod_{i \in I} X_i \to Y$ is a $U$-small subobject.

We set $H := \mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ for the remainder of this paper. The underlying poset $(H, \le)$ is viewed as a thin category: objects are elements of $H$, with a unique [morphism](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) $a \to b$ iff $a \le b$.

## 1.3 Modal Closure Operator

We equip $H$ with a modal [closure operator](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) $j : H \to H$ satisfying:

1. (Extensive) $a \le j(a)$;
2. (Monotone) $a \le b \implies j(a) \le j(b)$;
3. (Idempotent) $j(j(a)) = j(a)$;
4. (Join-continuous) for any $U$-small family $\{a_i\}_{i\in I}$,

$$
j\Big(\bigvee_{i\in I} a_i\Big) = \bigvee_{i\in I} j(a_i).
$$

5. (Meet-preserving / nucleus) $j(a \wedge b) = j(a) \wedge j(b)$.

Axiom 5 is not independent — it follows from $j$ being a [Lawvere-Tierney topology](../../../disciplines/higher-topos-theory/terms/lawvere-tierney-topology.md) on the topos $\mathcal{R}$: an endomorphism of the subobject classifier $\Omega$ preserves meets by definition. We list it explicitly because the formalization uses it as a primitive (eliminating a proof obligation in the Heyting subalgebra lemma) and because it carries direct semiotic content: the habituation of a conjunction is the conjunction of the habituations — forming a habit of two meanings together is forming the habit of each.

The operator $j$ formalizes **[habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md)-formation** — the process by which meanings stabilize through repeated [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) into habitual interpretations. Peirce identified the ultimate logical [interpretant](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md) as a [habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md)-change: the deliberately formed, self-analyzing habit is what a sign ultimately produces. An element where $j(a) = a$ is a meaning that has become habitual: further [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) does not alter its interpretation. An element where $j(a) > a$ is a meaning still undergoing [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) — not yet habitual, still subject to revision by [abductive](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/abduction.md) inference. The modal fragment $H^{\mathrm{st}}$ (below) is the domain of habitual meanings within the larger domain of all possible meanings.

The stable / modal fragment is

$$
H^{\mathrm{st}} := \{ a \in H : j(a) = a \}.
$$

### Lemma 1.2 (Modal fragment is a Heyting subalgebra)

$H^{\mathrm{st}}$ is a complete [Heyting](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md) subalgebra of $H$, and the inclusion
$i : H^{\mathrm{st}} \hookrightarrow H$ is a [Heyting](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md) homomorphism.

*Sketch.* Join-continuity of $j$, combined with the [adjunction](../../../disciplines/higher-topos-theory/terms/adjunction.md) definition of $\Rightarrow$, ensures that $H^{\mathrm{st}}$ is closed under all [Heyting](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md) operations, and that the inclusion preserves them.

## 1.4 [Heyting](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md)–Comonadic Trace Operator

Meanings carry the [sign](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md) chains that produced them. In [Peirce's](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/charles-sanders-peirce.md) account, each [interpretant](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md) is itself a [sign](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md), capable of producing further [interpretants](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md) — this is unlimited [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md). Two meanings may have the same content but different semiotic histories (different chains of [signs](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md) and [interpretants](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md) that produced them), and that difference can affect further interpretation. A comonad on $H$ formalizes this: $G(a)$ is the meaning $a$ together with its semiotic provenance. The counit ($G(a) \le a$) says the provenance of a meaning is at most as informative as the meaning itself. The comultiplication ($G(a) \le G(G(a))$) says provenance composes — you can trace the trace, because the sign chain that produced a meaning is itself a chain of signs.

A comonad on the poset-category of $H$ is given by:

- a monotone endofunctor $G : H \to H$,
- a counit $\epsilon_a : G(a) \le a$,
- a comultiplication $\delta_a : G(a) \le G(G(a))$,

natural in $a$, such that:

- $G(\delta_a)\circ \delta_a \le \delta_{G(a)}\circ \delta_a$ (coassociativity),
- $\epsilon_{G(a)}\circ \delta_a \le \mathrm{id}_{G(a)}$,
- $G(\epsilon_a)\circ \delta_a \le \mathrm{id}_{G(a)}$ (counit laws).

We further impose algebraic compatibility:

### Axiom 1.3 ([Heyting](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md)–comonadic coherence)

The trace $G : H \to H$ is a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) endomorphism and join-continuous. That is, for all $U$-small families $\{a_i\}$, and all $a,b\in H$,

1. $G(\bigvee_i a_i) = \bigvee_i G(a_i)$ (join-continuous);
2. $G(a\wedge b) = G(a)\wedge G(b)$, $G(\top) = \top$, $G(\bot) = \bot$;
3. $G(a \Rightarrow b) = G(a) \Rightarrow G(b)$.

So $G$ is simultaneously:

- a comonad on the poset-category underlying $H$, and
- a homomorphism of complete [Heyting algebras](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md).

This coherence law ensures that provenance respects logical structure: the semiotic history of a combined meaning is the combination of the individual histories.

## 1.5 Modality–Trace Interaction and Stability

[Habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md)-formation and semiotic provenance must cohere: forming a [habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md) from the provenance of a meaning should not exceed tracing the [habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md), and a meaning is habitual if and only if its provenance is habitual. We require:

### Axiom 1.4 (Basic interaction)

For all $a\in H$,

$$
j(G(a)) \le G(j(a)).
$$

### Axiom 1.5 (Stability with respect to trace)

For all $a\in H$,

1. if $a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$ then $G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$;
2. if $G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$ then $a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$.

Thus

$$
G(a)\in H^{\mathrm{st}} \iff a\in H^{\mathrm{st}}.
$$

### Lemma 1.6

The restriction of $G$ to $H^{\mathrm{st}}$ is a comonad and a [Heyting](../../../../../../../../library/references/people/arend-heyting.md) endomorphism on the modal fragment, and

$$
(H^{\mathrm{st}}, j = \mathrm{id}, G|_{H^{\mathrm{st}}})
$$

is a modal–temporal substructure of $(H,j,G)$.

# 2. Syntactic Operator Algebra

Meanings do not exist in isolation — they combine according to rules. A [typed lambda calculus](../../../disciplines/type-theory/curricula/typed-lambda-calculus.md) provides a general framework for these rules: each syntactic operator is a procedure for building new meanings from existing ones, and the type system ensures that combinations respect the kinds of meanings they operate on. The operators generated by the calculus (§2.2) are the grammar of this sign system.

We now describe the syntactic side at an abstract level. All type and term sets introduced below are $U$-small.

## 2.1 Types, Terms, and Raw Operators

We fix:

- a type language $\mathsf{Ty}$, generated from
  - a base type $P$,
  - function types $A\to B$,
  - product types $A\times B$;
- a λ-calculus of terms with variables, λ-abstraction, application, pairing, projections, and chosen primitive constants;
- typing judgments $\Gamma \vdash t : A$, with standard rules and structural properties (subject reduction, weakening, exchange, substitution).

For each $n\ge 0$, define:

- $\mathsf{Op}_n$: the set of closed terms $f$ with type $P^n \to P$;
- $\mathsf{Op}_n / \equiv$: quotient by definitional equality (βη and any additional equational axioms).

Let

$$
\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{raw}} := \bigcup_{n\ge 0} \mathsf{Op}_n / \equiv.
$$

## 2.2 Definable Operators

### Definition 2.1 (Definable operators)

For each $n$, $\mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}\subseteq\mathsf{Op}_n / \equiv$ is the smallest set that:

1. contains all primitive operator constants of arity $n$;
2. is closed under λ-abstraction and application yielding type $P^n\to P$;
3. is closed under composition of operators at type $P$.

Set

$$
\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}} := \bigcup_{n\ge 0} \mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}.
$$

We treat $\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ as a finitary algebra freely generated by primitive operations under λ-definability and composition.

# 3. Fragments and Restriction

Interpretation is always local: a sign is interpreted in a bounded context, not against the totality of the sign system at once. Finitely generated modal-temporal subalgebras — fragments — formalize these bounded interpretive contexts. Fragmentwise reasoning says: if two operators agree on every finite context, they are semantically identical.

## 3.1 Modal–Temporal Heyting Subalgebras

### Definition 3.1 (Modal–temporal subalgebra)

A subset $F \subseteq H$ is a modal–temporal subalgebra if:

1. $F$ is closed under $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$;
2. $F$ is closed under $j$;
3. $F$ is closed under $G$.

By Axiom 1.2, $G$ preserves all Heyting structure, so this notion is coherent.

### Definition 3.2 (Fragments)

A fragment is a finitely generated modal–temporal subalgebra $F\subseteq H$: there exists a finite set $S\subseteq F$ such that:

- $F$ is the smallest modal–temporal subalgebra containing $S$.

Let $\mathcal{F}(H)$ be the set of fragments.

## 3.2 Restriction of Operators

Let $f : H^n \to H$ be a function.

- If $f(F^n)\subseteq F$, we define its restriction
  
$$
f|_F : F^n \to F
$$

  as the obvious induced function.
- If not, then $f$ is not fragment-preserving on $F$.

### Definition 3.3 (Fragment-preserving operators)

An operator $f : H^n \to H$ is fragment-preserving if

$$
f(F^n) \subseteq F
$$

for every fragment $F\in\mathcal{F}(H)$.

For fragment-preserving operators, restriction to fragments is always defined.

## 3.3 Fragmentwise Equality and Hereditary Extensionality

### Definition 3.4 (Fragmentwise equality)

For fragment-preserving $f,g : H^n\to H$ and a fragment $F\in\mathcal{F}(H)$, we say

$$
f \equiv_F g
$$

if $f(a_1,\dots,a_n) = g(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ for all $a_i\in F$.

### Definition 3.5 (Hereditarily extensional family)

A family $\mathcal{E}$ of fragment-preserving operators is hereditarily extensional if:

> whenever $f,g\in\mathcal{E}$ and there is a fragment $F$ such that $f \equiv_F g$, then for any fragment $F'$ obtained from $F$ by finitely many applications of:
> - Heyting operations,
> - the modality $j$,
> - the trace $G$,
> - any operator in $\mathcal{E}$,
> we have $f \equiv_{F'} g$.

This captures the idea that equalities valid on a fragment remain valid under all semantic construction steps relevant to closure.

# 4. Interpretation and Modal–Comonadic Operators

The interpretation mapping is the formal counterpart of [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) — the process by which syntactic forms acquire determinate meanings. An interpretation satisfying the seven conditions below is one in which syntax and semantics are structurally aligned across all dimensions: logical, modal, temporal, local (fragment-preserving), and extensional. Any mapping satisfying weaker conditions would allow syntax and semantics to diverge in ways that violate the coherence a sign system requires.

We now connect the syntactic algebra to the semantic structure $H = \mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ constructed in Section 1.

## 4.1 Interpretation of Definable Operators

An interpretation is a family of maps

$$
\llbracket - \rrbracket_n : \mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}} \to \mathrm{Hom}(H^n,H)
$$

such that, for each $f \in \mathsf{Op}_n^{\mathrm{def}}$,

1. (Monotone & join-continuous) $\llbracket f \rrbracket$ is monotone in each argument and preserves directed joins in each argument.
2. (Heyting compatibility) The operators corresponding to $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$ on $P$ are interpreted as the Heyting operations on $H$.
3. (Modal homomorphism)
   
$$
j(\llbracket f\rrbracket(a_1,\dots,a_n)) = \llbracket f\rrbracket(j(a_1),\dots,j(a_n)) \quad \forall a_i \in H.
$$

4. (Trace compatibility)
   
$$
G(\llbracket f\rrbracket(a_1,\dots,a_n)) = \llbracket f\rrbracket(G(a_1),\dots,G(a_n)) \quad \forall a_i \in H.
$$

5. (Definitional equality preservation)
   If the λ-theory proves $f \equiv g$, then
   
$$
\llbracket f\rrbracket = \llbracket g\rrbracket.
$$

6. (Fragment preservation)
   For every fragment $F$, we have:
   
$$
\llbracket f\rrbracket(F^n) \subseteq F.
$$

7. (Hereditarily extensional family)
   The family $\{ \llbracket f\rrbracket : f\in \mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}\}$ is hereditarily extensional in the sense of Definition 3.5.

The fragment preservation axiom (6) explicitly guarantees that definable operators preserve fragments as algebras; we do not attempt to derive it from weaker assumptions. Fragment preservation is explicitly required rather than derived, ensuring that fragmentwise reasoning is always well-typed.

### Proposition 4.1 (Modal fragment as type discipline)

For each definable operator $f$, the restriction

$$
\llbracket f\rrbracket : (H^{\mathrm{st}})^n \to H^{\mathrm{st}}
$$

is well-defined. Moreover, on the modal fragment, it commutes with $G$ and preserves the Heyting structure.

*Sketch.* If all $a_i\in H^{\mathrm{st}}$, then (3) implies their image is stable under $j$, so lies in $H^{\mathrm{st}}$. Axiom 1.2 and (4) ensure compatibility with Heyting structure and $G$. Fragment preservation (6) ensures that modal–temporal fragments remain closed under these operators.

# 5. Semiotic Fusion: Congruence and Naming

Fusion enforces coherence between syntax and semantics by:

- quotienting syntactic operators by fragmentwise semantic equality,
- adding new operators that name already-available semantic behaviors.

A sign system in which every available semantic behavior has a syntactic name, and every syntactically identical pair agrees semantically everywhere, is one in which what can be said and what can be meant are in complete correspondence.

## 5.1 Partial Semiotic Structures

A partial [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) structure is a pair

$$
X = (H_X,\Op_X)
$$

with:

- $H_X\subseteq H$ a set of semantic objects;
- $\Op_X\subseteq \mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ a set of syntactic operators.

We form the complete [lattice](../../posets/terms/lattice.md)

$$
\mathcal{U} := \mathcal{P}(H)\times\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}})
$$

ordered by $(H_X,\Op_X)\le (H_Y,\Op_Y)$ iff $H_X\subseteq H_Y$ and $\Op_X\subseteq\Op_Y$.

## 5.2 Fragmentwise Congruence

For a partial structure $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, let $\mathcal{F}(H_X)$ denote the set of fragments $F\in\mathcal{F}(H)$ with $F\subseteq H_X$.

### Definition 5.1 (Congruence $\sim_X$)

For operators $f,g\in\Op_X$, we write

$$
f \sim_X g
$$

if:

1. for every fragment $F\in\mathcal{F}(H_X)$,
   
$$
\llbracket f\rrbracket \equiv_F \llbracket g\rrbracket;
$$

2. this equality is hereditarily extensional: closed under applying Heyting operations, $j$, $G$, and any operator in $\Op_X$.

Because the interpretation family is hereditarily extensional, $\sim_X$ is a well-defined congruence relation.

## 5.3 Naming Existing Behaviors

For each partial structure $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, consider the closure (in the hereditarily extensional family) of
$\{\llbracket f\rrbracket : f\in\Op_X\}$. Any operator

$$
h : H^n \to H
$$

in this closure that is:

- monotone,
- join-continuous,
- fragment-preserving,
- modal and trace-compatible (commutes with $j$ and $G$),

is an admissible behavior.

### Definition 5.2 (Fusion-generated operators)

We define $\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$ as the smallest set satisfying:

1. $\Op_X \subseteq \Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$;
2. if $f,g\in \Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$ and $f\sim_X g$, then we identify them in $\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$ (or, equivalently, we treat them as a single operator up to definitional equality);
3. if $h$ is an admissible behavior as above, we may introduce a new [symbol](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/symbol.md) $f_h$ with interpretation $\llbracket f_h\rrbracket = h$, and include $f_h$ in $\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$.

Notice: new operators added by naming are semantic reifications of behaviors that are already modal, trace-compatible, join-continuous, and fragment-preserving. This ensures Modal Adequacy is preserved.

# 6. Fusion as a Reflection

The fusion operator we have described is not just some closure: it is a reflection into a full subcategory of “fusion-saturated” structures.

## 6.1 Fusion Closure Operator $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$

Define

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}} : \mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}
$$

by

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X) = (H_X,\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}),
$$

leaving $H_X$ unchanged and saturating $\Op_X$ as in Definition 5.2.

### Lemma 6.1 (Inflationary and monotone)

$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ is inflationary and monotone:

- $X\le \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X)$ for all $X$;
- if $X\le Y$, then $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X)\le \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(Y)$.

*Sketch.* Inflationary is by (1) of Definition 5.2. Monotonicity holds because enlarging $H_X$ and $\Op_X$ only increases the space of fragments and admissible behaviors, never removing existing ones.

### Lemma 6.2 (Idempotence of fusion)

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X)) = \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X) \quad\text{for all } X\in\mathcal{U}.
$$

*Proof.*  
By construction, $\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}}$ is already closed under:

- quotienting by $\sim_X$,
- naming admissible behaviors.

If we apply the same construction again to $(H_X,\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}})$, we do not obtain any new identifications (the congruence is already saturated) or new admissible behaviors (all such behaviors have already been named, subject to the same conditions). Thus $\Op_{X}^{\mathrm{fus}}$ is fixed under fusion, and so is $(H_X,\Op_X^{\mathrm{fus}})$.

Hence $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ is a [closure operator](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) on the poset $\mathcal{U}$ in the order-theoretic sense: monotone, inflationary, idempotent.

## 6.2 Fusion-Saturated Structures and Reflection

Let $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ be the set of [fixed points](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$:

$$
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{fus}} := \{ X\in\mathcal{U} : \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X) = X \}.
$$

These are the fusion-saturated structures: they contain all identifications and named operators required by fragmentwise semantic behavior.

We regard $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ as poset-categories (thin categories). There is an inclusion [functor](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/functor.md)

$$
i : \mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{fus}} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{U}.
$$

### Lemma 6.3 (Fusion is a reflector)

$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ is left adjoint to $i$. That is, for each $X\in\mathcal{U}$ and each $Y\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{fus}}$,

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X) \le Y \quad\text{iff}\quad X\le Y.
$$

*Proof.*  
In any poset $(P,\le)$, a [closure operator](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) $c:P\to P$ has the [property](../../../../../../humanities/domains/sociology/terms/property.md) that $c(x)\le y$ iff $x\le y$ for all [fixed points](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) $y$ of $c$. This is standard: if $x\le y$ and $c(y)=y$, then $c(x)\le c(y)=y$; conversely, if $c(x)\le y$, then $x\le c(x)\le y$.  

Applying this to $P=\mathcal{U}$ and $c=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$, and noting that $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ is exactly the set of [fixed points](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md), proves the [adjunction](../../../disciplines/higher-topos-theory/terms/adjunction.md).  

Thus $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ is the left adjoint $L_{\mathrm{fus}}$ to the inclusion $i$, and $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{fus}}$ is a reflective subcategory of $\mathcal{U}$.

This is the Fusion Reflection Lemma: fusion is not just closure, but a genuine reflection. A fusion-saturated structure is one in which no further coherence can be demanded — the sign system has fully reconciled what it can say with what it can mean.

# 7. Semantic and Syntactic Closure Operators

The three closure operators correspond to the three aspects of how a sign system grows. Semantic closure ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}}$) models the generation of new meanings from existing ones — [signs](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md) producing [interpretants](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md), which are themselves signs, generating further structure. Syntactic closure ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}$) models the growth of expressive power as new operators are recognized for behaviors already semantically available. Fusion ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}$) reconciles the two by enforcing that the syntactic and semantic sides agree at every scale. The [fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of their composite is the minimal structure that has become [habitual](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md) under all three processes — where no further [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) produces new structure.

We now define the semantic and syntactic closures, respecting finitary dependence. The complete lattice $\mathcal{U}$ of partial structures is $U$-small (it is a subset of $\mathcal{P}(H) \times \mathcal{P}(\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}})$, both of which are in $U$), so the closure operators below are genuine set-functions and transfinite iteration is well-defined.

## 7.1 Semantic Closure $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}}$

For $X = (H_X,\Op_X)\in\mathcal{U}$, define

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}}(X) = (H_X',\Op_X),
$$

where $H_X'$ is the smallest subset of $H$ such that:

1. $H_X \subseteq H_X'$;
2. (Interpretation closure)
   If $f\in\Op_X$ and $a_i\in H_X'$, then $\llbracket f\rrbracket(a_1,\dots,a_n)\in H_X'$;
3. (Heyting closure)
   $H_X'$ is closed under $\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\bot,\top$;
4. (Modal–trace closure)
   $H_X'$ is closed under $j$ and $G$;
5. (Fixed-point closure)
   if $h:H\to H$ is monotone, join-continuous, fragment-preserving, modal, trace-compatible, and lies in the hereditarily extensional closure of $\{\llbracket f\rrbracket : f\in\Op_X\}$, then any least or greatest [fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of $h$ that is above some element of $H_X'$ is included in $H_X'$.

### Lemma 7.1

$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}}$ is inflationary and monotone on $\mathcal{U}$.

---

## 7.2 Syntactic Closure with Finitary Dependence $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}$

We now make the compactness [property](../../../../../../humanities/domains/sociology/terms/property.md) precise.

### Definition 7.2 (Semantic finitary justification)

For $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, operator $f\in\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$, and a fragment $F\subseteq H_X$, we say:

> $f$ is semantically justified by $F$ over $X$ if there exists $g\in\Op_X$ such that
> 
$$
\llbracket f\rrbracket \equiv_F \llbracket g\rrbracket
$$

> and this equality is hereditarily extensional under:
> - Heyting operations,
> - $j$, $G$,
> - all operators in $\Op_X$.

This captures that $f$ behaves like an existing operator on a finite piece of semantics.

### Definition 7.3 (Syntactic closure)

For $X=(H_X,\Op_X)$, define

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(X) = (H_X,\Op_X'),
$$

where $\Op_X'$ is the smallest set such that:

1. $\Op_X \subseteq\Op_X'$;
2. $\Op_X'$ is closed under λ-definability and composition;
3. if the λ-calculus includes fixed-point constructs whose interpretations are monotone, join-continuous, fragment-preserving, modal, and trace-compatible, these fixed-point operators are included whenever their interpretations arise from $\Op_X'$;
4. (Finitary justification) if $f\in\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ is semantically justified by some finite fragment $F\subseteq H_X$, then $f\in\Op_X'$.

### Algebraic compactness

By (4), we have:

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(X) = \bigcup_{F \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} H_X} \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(F,\Op_X),
$$

where syntactic closure over $F$ only uses semantic facts inside the fragment $F$.

### Lemma 7.4

$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}$ is inflationary and monotone.

---

## 7.3 Global Semiotic Closure $\mathcal{S}$ and Fixed Point

Define the global operator

$$
\mathcal{S} := \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}} \circ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}} \circ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sem}} : \mathcal{U} \to \mathcal{U}.
$$

### Lemma 7.5

$\mathcal{S}$ is monotone and inflationary.

*Proof.* Composition of monotone inflationary maps.

### Theorem 7.6 (Existence of [least fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md))

$\mathcal{S}$ has a [least fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) $X^\star$ in $\mathcal{U}$, given by

$$
X^\star = \bigwedge\{ X\in\mathcal{U} : \mathcal{S}(X) \le X \}.
$$

*Proof.* $\mathcal{U}$ is a complete [lattice](../../posets/terms/lattice.md); by Knaster–Tarski, any monotone endomap has a nonempty complete [lattice](../../posets/terms/lattice.md) of [fixed points](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md), with a least element.

# 8. The Semiotic Universe and Its Universal Property

The fixed point $X^\star$ is the minimal self-sustaining sign system generated from the primitive data. "Minimal" has a precise meaning: $X^\star$ is the initial [semiotic structure](./terms/semiotic-structure.md) — the one that embeds into every other structure over the same data. It contains exactly what the axioms and primitive data force, and nothing more.

## 8.1 Primitive Data and Base Structure

The primitive [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) data consist of:

- the meaning domain $H = \mathrm{Sub}(Y)$ constructed in Section 1: a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) within $U$, with modality $j$ and trace $G$;
- a primitive set of syntactic operators $\Op^{\mathrm{prim}}\subseteq\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$;
- an interpretation of $\Op^{\mathrm{prim}}$ satisfying conditions of Section 4 (including fragment preservation).

We embed this into a base partial structure

$$
X_0 = (H_0,\Op_0)
$$

where $H_0\subseteq H$ is any chosen generating set for the Heyting–modal–comonadic structure, and $\Op_0 = \Op^{\mathrm{prim}}$.

We then apply $\mathcal{S}$ repeatedly.

## 8.2 Definition of the Semiotic Universe

### Definition 8.1 (Semiotic universe)

The [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe is the [least fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md)

$$
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md)}} := X^\star = (H_{\mathrm{[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md)}},\Op_{\mathrm{[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md)}})
$$

of $\mathcal{S}$ such that $X_0 \le X^\star$.

By construction:

- $H_{\mathrm{[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md)}}$ is closed under Heyting operations, $j$, $G$, interpreted operators, and [fixed points](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of admissible semantic operators;
- $\Op_{\mathrm{[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md)}}$ is closed under λ-definability, composition, finitary justification, fixed-point constructs, and fusion reflection (i.e. it is fusion-saturated).

Moreover, because fusion is a reflection, $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{[semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md)}}$ is fusion-saturated: $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{fus}}(X^\star)=X^\star$.

## 8.3 Semiotic Structures and Morphisms

To state initiality, we need to define what counts as a semiotic structure and what structure-preserving maps look like.

A [semiotic structure](./terms/semiotic-structure.md) over the given primitive data consists of:

- a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) $K$ with modality $j_K$ and trace $G_K$ satisfying analogues of Axioms 1.2–1.4;
- an interpretation $\llbracket -\rrbracket_K : \mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}} \to \mathrm{Hom}(K^n,K)$ satisfying analogues of Section 4;
- a partial [semiotic structure](./terms/semiotic-structure.md) $X_K=(H_K,\Op_K)\subseteq K\times\mathsf{Op}^{\mathrm{def}}$ that is a [fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of the induced [closure operator](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) $\mathcal{S}_K$ and extends the primitive data.

A [morphism](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) between [semiotic structures](./terms/semiotic-structure.md) $(K_1,X_1)$ and $(K_2,X_2)$ is a pair $(h,k)$ where:

- $h:K_1\to K_2$ is a complete Heyting homomorphism preserving $j$ and $G$:
  
$$
h(j_1(a)) = j_2(h(a)),\quad h(G_1(a)) = G_2(h(a));
$$

- $k:\Op_1\to\Op_2$ is a homomorphism of syntactic algebras that preserves definitional equality and closure operations;
- $h$ and $k$ are compatible with the interpretation:
  
$$
h(\llbracket f\rrbracket_1(a_1,\dots,a_n)) = \llbracket k(f)\rrbracket_2(h(a_1),\dots,h(a_n)).
$$

Two [morphisms](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) $(h,k)$, $(h',k')$ are viewed as 2-equal if they are fragmentwise extensionally equal in the sense of Section 3.

## 8.4 Initiality of the Semiotic Universe

### Theorem 8.2 (Initiality / Free [semiotic](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/index.md) universe)

The semiotic universe $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{semiotic}}$ is initial among all [semiotic structures](./terms/semiotic-structure.md) over the primitive data:

> For any [semiotic structure](./terms/semiotic-structure.md) $(K,X_K)$, there exists a unique (up to fragmentwise extensional equality) [morphism](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md)
> 
$$
(h,k) : \mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{semiotic}} \to (K,X_K).
$$

*Sketch of proof.*

1. Any [semiotic structure](./terms/semiotic-structure.md) $(K,X_K)$ is a [fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) of an induced [closure operator](../../../concepts/closure-operator/index.md) $\mathcal{S}_K$ extending the same primitive data. Thus it is a pre-[fixed point](../../../concepts/fixed-point/index.md) in the corresponding [lattice](../../posets/terms/lattice.md).
2. By minimality of $X^\star$, we have $X^\star \le X_K$.
3. This inclusion yields natural candidates $h,k$ on the semantic and syntactic components, and these maps preserve all structure because each closure step (semantic, syntactic, fusion) is preserved by any structure satisfying the same axioms.
4. Uniqueness follows because any [morphism](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) must agree with the inclusion on the primitive data and respect closure operations; but $X^\star$ is obtained from the primitive data by these operations alone.

Because fusion is a reflection (Section 6), the initiality extends to the fusion-saturated structures, making $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{semiotic}}$ the free modal–comonadic Heyting structure with interpreted syntax. Initiality means the semiotic universe is the freest possible sign system over the given primitives: every other sign system over those primitives receives a unique map from it, because $X^\star$ contains only what the closure operations force.

# 9. A 2-Category of Semiotic Universes

Equality of [morphisms](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) is fundamentally fragmentwise, so semiotic universes form a natural 2-category:

- 0-cells: semiotic universes compatible with the primitive data;
- 1-cells: [morphisms](../../categories/ordinary-categories/terms/morphism.md) $(h,k)$ preserving all structure;
- 2-cells: fragmentwise extensional equalities between 1-cells (hereditarily extensional under the target structure’s semantics).

The 2-cells — fragmentwise extensional equalities — formalize the fact that two interpretations of the same sign are "the same" if and only if they agree on every bounded context, which is the appropriate notion of observational equivalence for a sign system in which interpretation is always local.

The semiotic universe $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{semiotic}}$ is an initial 0-cell in this 2-category, refining Theorem 8.2.

# 10. Discussion

The construction produces a complete [Heyting algebra](../../../concepts/heyting-algebra/index.md) $H$ with modal closure $j$ and trace comonad $G$, a typed syntactic algebra with a coherent interpretation, and a least fixed point of three closure operators. This is the semiotic universe. Its defining properties — Heyting structure, modal closure, trace comonad, fusion-saturation, initiality — each correspond to a feature of a self-sustaining sign system.

The Heyting structure captures the fact that meanings stand in entailment relations that are constructive: not every question about a sign's meaning resolves to a definite answer, and implication ($a \Rightarrow b$) is the strongest conclusion compatible with "given $a$, we can reach $b$." Modal closure captures [habit](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md)-formation: meanings tend toward habitual interpretations through repeated [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md), and the domain of habitual meanings ($H^{\mathrm{st}}$) is itself a well-structured whole — a complete Heyting subalgebra. The trace comonad captures semiotic provenance: meanings carry the [sign](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md) chains that produced them (unlimited [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) — each [interpretant](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/interpretant.md) is itself a [sign](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/sign.md)), and those chains compose (trace of trace is well-defined) without altering the meaning's content (counit). The typed lambda calculus provides the grammar — rules for combining signs into new signs — and the interpretation mapping connects grammar to meaning in a way that respects all logical, modal, and temporal structure. Fusion ensures expressive completeness: every available meaning has a syntactic name, and every syntactic identification is semantically grounded.

The universal property states that the semiotic universe is initial: it maps uniquely into every other sign system satisfying the same axioms over the same primitive data. This means the construction characterizes what is unavoidable in any system where meanings are structured, [semiosis](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/semiosis.md) produces [habits](../../../../linguistics/topics/semiotics/terms/habit.md), sign chains carry provenance, and syntax and semantics cohere. Any such system contains a copy of the semiotic universe.

The semiotic universe does not include external handles, interaction with reality outside the sign system, failure semantics, or agents. The [interactive semioverse](../interactive-semioverse/interactive-semioverse.md) extends this foundation with those features, modeling how signs engage with the world beyond the sign system itself.
