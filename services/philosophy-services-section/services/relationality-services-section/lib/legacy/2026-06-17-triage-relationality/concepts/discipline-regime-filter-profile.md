A four-level hierarchy describing how structure constrains and organizes relational dynamics. Provides the bridge between [Flow](flow.md) (Movement III) and [cohesive geometry](cohesive-chain.md) (Movement IV).

## The four levels

### Discipline

A unary endomap $d: \text{Rel} \to \text{Rel}$ that commutes with all nuclei (closure operators) and with Flow. A Discipline is a way of filtering or constraining the relational field that respects all the structure already earned — it does not disrupt closure, does not interfere with directed motion, and does not break the modal operators. Formally: `(Commutes d j)` for every nucleus $j$, and `(Commutes d Flow)`. Disciplines are the *well-behaved* constraints.

### Regime

The fixed-point set of a Discipline: $\text{Regime}(d) = \{a \in \text{Rel} \mid d(a) = a\}$. What remains stable under a given Discipline — the recognitions the Discipline leaves unchanged. Regimes inherit the full relational structure (Heyting algebra, modal operators, etc.) restricted to their fixed points.

### Filter

A Discipline applied to a RelationalGeometry. When the [cohesive chain](cohesive-chain.md) (Shape ⊣ Discrete ⊣ Codiscrete ⊣ Global) is in place, a Filter is a Discipline that additionally respects the geometric structure — it commutes not only with nuclei and Flow but also with the cohesive adjunctions.

### Profile

The fixed points of a Filter. A Profile carries the full recursive RelationalStructure — the complete Heyting algebra, modal operators, Flow, and cohesive geometry — restricted to the recognitions that survive the Filter. Profiles are the most constrained and most structured level: self-contained relational subsystems.

## Why they matter

The hierarchy describes how relational structure can be *specialized without being destroyed*. Each level adds a stronger constraint:

1. **Discipline** — commutes with nuclei and Flow (respects logic and dynamics).
2. **Regime** — fixed points of a Discipline (what survives the constraint).
3. **Filter** — a Discipline on a geometry (respects cohesion too).
4. **Profile** — fixed points of a Filter (a self-contained relational subsystem).

This provides a theory of observation and perspective: a Profile is a viewpoint from which the relational field is seen, carrying all the structure needed to do relational dynamics within the viewpoint. The **Filter-Induced Reflexive Reconstruction** theorem — one of the three demonstrations of [self-grounding](self-grounding.md) — shows that every Profile reproduces the [four-part framework](four-part-framework.md) internally. Any sufficiently coherent perspective on the field reproduces the entire architecture.

## Note on the four-level vocabulary

This particular four-level hierarchy with these particular names is a specific articulation. Other articulations capture related structure under different names — *Stratify* and *Constructible* in the diagrammatic register cover similar ground. The question of whether these are the same hierarchy under different names, or distinct architectures with overlapping content, has not been fully resolved.
