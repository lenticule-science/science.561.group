The act of sustaining reflexivity over time. Reflecting holds open the reflexive relation `relating(S, S)` durably, ensuring stability of the reflexive operation within the ongoing dynamics of the system.

## The distinction from Reflexing

Reflexing and reflecting are genuinely distinct, following the same act/sustained-act pattern that runs throughout the framework:

- **[Reflexing](reflex.md)** — the instantaneous act of turning an act back upon itself: `reflex(S) = relating(S, S)`. A single self-application.
- **Reflecting** — the ongoing maintenance of reflexivity. Sustaining the reflexive relation over time so it can accumulate into a sequence.

The pattern parallels:

- including (act) → inclusion (sustained condition)
- excluding (act) → exclusion (sustained condition)
- reflexing (act) → reflecting (sustained act) → reflexivity (condition)

The shorter form: reflecting differs from mere reflexing by holding open the reflexive relation over time, ensuring stability.

## Why the distinction matters

Reflexive sequences $R(0) := S$, $R(n+1) := \text{reflect}(R(n))$ require that each step's reflexivity be *maintained* — not performed once and forgotten. Without reflecting, individual self-applications do not accumulate into a sequence.

In the formal framework:

- **Reflexing** corresponds to a single application of the Reflex endomap: $S(a)$.
- **Reflecting** corresponds to the iteration: $\text{Iterate}^n(a) = S^n(a)$.
- The reflexive sequence corresponds to the ascending chain $S^0(a) \leq S^1(a) \leq \ldots$ whose join is $\text{Close}(a)$.

This is why reflecting bridges Movements I and II: reflexing is a Movement I act (a single operation on recognitions), but reflecting produces the iteration that earns Closing (a Movement II structure). The path from reflexing to closing goes through reflecting.
