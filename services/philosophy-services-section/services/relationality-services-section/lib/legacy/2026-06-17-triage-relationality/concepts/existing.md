The minimal act required for a thing to act, be conditioned, or have relations. To claim that something exists is to claim it is not what it is not — a distinction, and distinction is relational in form.

## The phenomenological derivation

Claiming something exists specifies two things — that the thing is not some other thing, and that the thing is not nothing. These specifications compel a sequence of primitive acts and conditions:

1. **Including, excluding** — primitive acts producing the conditions inclusion and exclusion.
2. **Tension** — including and excluding must be held together in ongoing opposition; tension is the condition of that mutual opposition.
3. **Irreducible asymmetry** — every inclusion demands exclusion of its own negation, which is itself a condition requiring inclusion. The recursion ensures the derivation is never final.
4. **Difference** — tension induces the condition of coherent separation: the thing is set apart from what it is not.
5. **Relating** — to sustain tension over time, an overarching act is required. [Relating](relating.md) is this act.
6. **Relation** — relating produces the condition that binds the thing to its ongoing existing.
7. **[Relative](relative.md)** — relating and relation together produce the first structure. A relative holds relation open durably. The architecture of existing achieves closure here.

## Why it matters

Existing is what the phenomenological layer *is*. All other acts (including, excluding, [relating](relating.md), [reflexing](reflex.md), [differentiating](differentiate.md)) are derived as requirements of existing. The formal operators ([Together / Either](together-either.md), [Induces](induces.md), [Negate](negate.md)) are the algebraic encoding of the acts that existing compels.

Existing is a perpetual process, not a state. The [irreducible asymmetry](irreducible-asymmetry.md) ensures it is never complete — every inclusion generates new exclusions, sustaining the derivation indefinitely.
