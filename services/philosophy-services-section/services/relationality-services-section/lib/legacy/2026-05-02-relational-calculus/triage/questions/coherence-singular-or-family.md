The framework uses *coherence* in several places, sometimes as one condition that is *partially* achieved at one movement and *more fully* achieved at the next, and sometimes as a *new* condition specific to that movement.

## The two readings

**Coherence-singular.** There is one condition called *coherence* — the relational system being self-consistent — and each of the five movements achieves a *more complete* form of it. Movement I achieves coherence-of-distinction (the field of recognition is closed); Movement II achieves coherence-of-stability (the modal core is fixed); etc. The same word denotes the same condition at different depths.

**Coherence-family.** Each movement achieves its own condition, distinct from the others' conditions, and the framework uses *coherence* as a generic name for "whatever the closure of this movement produces." Coherence-of-distinction, coherence-of-stability, coherence-of-flow, coherence-of-cohesion, and coherence-of-conservation are five different conditions sharing a name.

## What the sources say

[coherence.md](../teaching/explanations/coherence.md) presents coherence as one condition with successive realizations (the singular reading). [incitement.md](../teaching/explanations/incitement.md) presents the derivation as "each movement achieves a coherence whose incompleteness incites the next" (which is the family reading — each movement has *its own* coherence). [five-movements.md](../teaching/explanations/five-movements.md) uses both phrasings.

## Why it matters

If coherence is singular, then the framework has one *order parameter* (a single number measuring how coherent the current state is) and the five movements are five thresholds along its trajectory. This is a strong claim — it says the framework's progression has a single-dimensional structure.

If coherence is a family, then the framework has five *independent* coherences, each with its own incompleteness, and the derivation's progression is multi-dimensional — Movement III's coherence-of-flow is logically independent of Movement II's coherence-of-stability.

The mathematical consequences differ: the singular reading suggests the existence of a *coherence functional* on the relational field whose extremization picks out the framework's content. The family reading does not suggest this and would make Noether-style conservation arguments (which need a single conserved quantity) harder to ground.

## Status

Open. The author appears to use both readings interchangeably; the framework would benefit from picking one and being consistent. The singular reading is more elegant and supports the [conserving](../teaching/explanations/conserving.md) law of Movement V; the family reading is more honest to how the derivation actually proceeds (each movement is its own closure, not a deeper cut of the previous).
