## Annotation

Kelly's 1974 *Doctrinal adjunction* (with Street 1972 *Formal Theory of Monads*) is the source of the *Kelly–Lack lax-idempotence* characterization the relational calculus uses for both Movement II's closure operator $\Box$ and the closure 2-monad of [predication-categories](../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/morphisms/closure-2-monad.md). The relevant content:

- The "K1" adjunction characterization: a monad $T$ is lax-idempotent iff there is an adjunction $\mu \dashv \eta_T$ in the 2-category of endofunctors.
- The "K2" property-likeness theorem: lax-idempotence implies that being a $T$-algebra is a property (at most one algebra structure per object up to unique iso), not additional structural data.
- The companion enriched-category framework that lets these results be stated and used uniformly across different base categories of enrichment.

The framework's source material refers to "Kelly's *Basic Concepts of Enriched Category Theory* (1974)" — the 1982 monograph is the more comprehensive reference, but the 1974 *Doctrinal adjunction* paper is the original source for the lax-idempotence result. Both are typically cited together as Kelly–Street or Kelly–Lack, depending on which extension of the original work is in view.

Together with Street 1972 and Riehl–Verity 2022, this is the categorical infrastructure underlying Movements II–IV of the relational calculus.
