## Annotation

The framework's *mark* / *unmarked* / *marked side* / *boundary* language is borrowed directly from Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form*. Spencer-Brown takes the act of distinction — drawing a boundary that creates a *marked side* and an *unmarked side* — as the primitive operation of form, and develops a calculus of indications (the LoF calculus) from it. The relational calculus' Movement I, particularly the [Differentiate](../teaching/explanations/differentiate.md) act and the [Articulation Theorem](../theorems/articulation-theorem.md)'s account of how distinction is forced from the void, is the closest contemporary recapitulation of Spencer-Brown's starting move.

The differences are substantive: Spencer-Brown's calculus is *Boolean* (the LoF calculus collapses to two-valued logic at re-entry), while the relational calculus' Movement I closure is *Heyting* (intuitionistic, with $\neg\neg a \geq a$ as inequality). The relational calculus also adds the *iteration* of the distinction act (Reflex, IterateReflex) as the engine of the derivation, where Spencer-Brown treats re-entry as a separate move at the end.

The framework's debt to Spencer-Brown is acknowledged in the source material: "In Spencer-Brown, the mark is the minimal operator of form, yet our [framework]…" (from the on-relationality unified draft, philosophical correspondences appendix).
