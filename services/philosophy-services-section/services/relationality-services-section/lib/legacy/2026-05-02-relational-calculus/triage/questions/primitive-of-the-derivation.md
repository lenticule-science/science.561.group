The opening of the derivation has three candidates for the *primitive* — the move that requires no prior justification — and the source dumps disagree about which one carries that role:

- **Relationlessness.** The empty trace `()`, the void, $\bot$. The starting condition before any act. Some sources name this as "the only thing that is given."
- **Reflex.** The act that arises when Relationlessness is forced to articulate itself. Some sources name this as "the only act that requires nothing prior."
- **Differentiate.** The first recognition; what Reflex acting on Relationlessness produces. Some sources (notably the formal-axioms doc) name *Differentiate* as Axiom 1 — the operator that the rest of the formal layer is built on.

[differentiate.md](../teaching/explanations/differentiate.md) acknowledges this directly: "Older treatments are sometimes ambiguous about which of {Relationlessness, Reflex, Differentiate} is 'primitive.'"

## Three coherent positions

1. **Relationlessness is primitive; Reflex and Differentiate are derived.** The derivation begins with a *condition* (the absence of relation), and the first *act* (Reflex) is forced by the [Articulation Theorem](../theorems/articulation-theorem.md). On this reading, the framework has no axioms — it has a starting condition and a theorem.

2. **Reflex is primitive; Relationlessness is the contextual ground.** Relationlessness is not an act, and the framework is built of acts; therefore the first *primitive* is the first act, which is Reflex. On this reading, the Articulation Theorem is the *naming* of the primitive, not its derivation.

3. **Differentiate is primitive; the prior trace is preface.** The formal layer starts at Differentiate because that is the first operator with a signature `(Entity, Entity) -> Rel` that the standard formal machinery (Heyting algebras, closures) can engage. Relationlessness and Reflex are pre-mathematical motivation; the mathematics begins at Differentiate.

## Why it matters

The choice changes what counts as a *theorem* of the framework. Under Position 1, the Articulation Theorem is the foundational theorem. Under Position 2, the Articulation Theorem is a *definition disguised as a theorem* — it names the primitive rather than deriving it. Under Position 3, the entire pre-Differentiate trace is informal, and the framework's formal content is exactly the Heyting-algebra-and-up structure.

The author's true position is probably some flavor of (1), based on the way the source material consistently presents Reflex as *forced* rather than *posited*. But the formal layer doesn't reflect this — it starts at Differentiate. Reconciling the philosophical narrative (Reflex is forced) with the formal layer (Differentiate is Axiom 1) is one of the load-bearing pieces of unfinished work.

## Status

Open. The three positions are each internally coherent; no source explicitly chooses among them.
