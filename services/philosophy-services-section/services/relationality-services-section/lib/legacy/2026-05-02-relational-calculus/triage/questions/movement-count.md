The number of *movements* in the derivation is given as five, nine, and eight in different source documents — none of these counts are obviously wrong, and they may all be correct under different individuation rules.

## What the sources say

- **Five movements** — the dominant scheme in [five-movements.md](../teaching/explanations/five-movements.md): Logical Origination, Structural Stabilization, Directed Dynamics, Geometric Cohesion, Emergent Containment.
- **Nine movements** — appears in some traces of the on-relationality unified draft, breaking each of the five into finer phases (e.g., Movement I splits into Differentiate / Together-Either / Induces-Negate; Movement IV splits into Cohesive Chain / Stratification).
- **Eight movements** — appears in the formal-axioms-of-relationing source, organized as a sequence: Differentiate → Order → Modal → Flow → Cohesion → Stratification → Observation → Conservation.

## What may be going on

The five-movement scheme is *philosophical*: each movement is one "act of recognition" — one self-contained closure that produces a coherence whose incompleteness incites the next. The nine and eight schemes are *technical*: they break the five into the finer steps each movement actually contains.

Three reconciliations:

1. **Five is the canonical count; nine and eight are sub-divisions for expository purposes.** The five-movement narrative is the reference; the nine- and eight-movement traces are particular zooms.

2. **Five is correct for "movements," but each movement contains multiple "acts."** This matches the [domain-emergence](../teaching/explanations/domain-emergence.md) account, which gives ~4 *domains* and ~9 finer phases.

3. **The counts are honest reports of the same content broken at different joints.** Just as a continent admits multiple coastline measurements depending on the resolution, the derivation admits multiple movement counts depending on what level of compression is being applied.

## Why it matters

Resolution matters for cross-referencing. When a downstream document says "Movement IV produces RITM," and another document says "Movement V produces RITM," the reader cannot tell whether they disagree or are using different counts.

Adopting a canonical *count of movements* (whatever the chosen number) and tagging each act in the granular trace with its movement-membership would resolve the cross-reference problem. The five-movement count is the most-used in current notes and is the natural choice.

## Status

Open. The dominant five-movement scheme should be made canonical, but the nine- and eight-movement breakdowns carry real content (which finer steps each movement contains) that should be preserved in the [domain-emergence](../teaching/explanations/domain-emergence.md) account or as a separate "phases" decomposition.
