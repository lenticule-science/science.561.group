Reduction Rules
===============

Definition — ***Reduction Rules***
---------------------------------

The one-step reduction ``→`` is the least relation closed under
evaluation contexts generated by:

- β: ``(λx.t)\ u → t[u/x]``,
- Projections: ``π₁⟨t, u⟩ → t``; ``π₂⟨t, u⟩ → u``,
- Case:

  - ``\mathrm{case}\ (\mathrm{inl}\ t)\ \mathrm{of}\ \mathrm{inl}\ x↦u\ |\ \mathrm{inr}\ y↦v → u[t/x]``,
  - ``\mathrm{case}\ (\mathrm{inr}\ u)\ \mathrm{of}\ \mathrm{inl}\ x↦t\ |\ \mathrm{inr}\ y↦v → v[u/y]``,

- Reflex stability: ``ρ(t) → ρ(t′)`` whenever ``t → t′`` (``ρ`` does
  not reduce by itself),
- Congruence: standard for ``λ``, application, pairs, sums, and
  elimination constructs.

At the implementation level, these rules are reflected as labelled
small steps: a single reduction ``t → t′`` is represented by a
``ReductionStep`` with a coarse rule name such as ``"beta"``,
``"fst-pair"``, ``"snd-pair"``, ``"case-inl"``, ``"case-inr"``, or
``"reflex-cong"``, and finite sequences of such steps form reduction
traces.

The multi-step reduction ``→*`` is the reflexive transitive closure of
``→``. A term is in normal form when no rule applies.
