This text, despite its depth and formality, is a story. It’s a story
I was taught in a Lakota way, but parts of it may sound familiar no
matter where you come from. Many traditions, mathematical and
otherwise, carry some version of it. At its core, it’s a story that
circles one of the oldest questions we’ve ever asked; Martin Heidegger
called it the “foundational question of metaphysics”::

   Why is there anything at all? Why something instead of nothing?

This text is a formalized expression of the way I was taught to answer
that question.

In brief: if Relationlessness (the absence of any relation) can be
recognized or described, that very recognition is already something.
From that smallest “something” follow the relations, patterns, and
dynamics that build up the worlds we study. Relationality develops
this idea not as metaphor, but as a formal system you can derive,
compute, and test.

The book gathers that work into three connected pieces:

- A **mathematical specification**, developing relational algebra,
  calculus, dynamics, and mechanics.
- A **computational implementation**, making those definitions
  concrete and runnable in finite and transfinite spaces.
- A **testing layer**, exploring their computational behaviour on
  explicit models.

These pieces each reinforce each other: reading the spec, running the
library, and examining the tests are all ways of telling and extending
the same story: a story that, because it is infinite, incites readers
to extend it, revise it, or break it.
