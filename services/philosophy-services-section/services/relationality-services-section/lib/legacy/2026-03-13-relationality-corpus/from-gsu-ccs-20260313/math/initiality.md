### Theorem — Initiality {#initiality}

**Theorem — Initiality.** Let $(R, H, \sigma, \Delta, H^\ast)$ be the relational universe
constructed from the axioms of §history-and-fiber-algebra, §modal-calculus, and §sheaf-construction.
For any relational universe model $(S, K, \sigma_S, \Delta_S, K^\ast)$ realizing the same history
site and satisfying those axioms, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) structure-preserving
morphism
$$
F : (R, H, \sigma, \Delta, H^\ast) \longrightarrow (S, K, \sigma_S, \Delta_S, K^\ast)
$$
in the relational universe model category. Equivalently, $(R, H)$ is **initial** among all
relational universe models of §history-and-fiber-algebra, §modal-calculus, and §sheaf-construction.

#### Existence {#thm-initiality-existence}

The syntactic relational universe $(R_{\mathrm{syn}}, H_{\mathrm{syn}})$ (§syntactic-relational-universe)
is the term model: its objects are FiberNuclearLanguage-equivalence classes, its fiber Heyting algebras
are $H_{\mathrm{syn},t} = H_t / {\sim}$, and it comes equipped with a canonical evaluation map
$$
\mathrm{eval}_t : \text{FiberNuclearLanguage} \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{syn},t} \longrightarrow H_t
$$
that interprets each term in any BiNucleus satisfying the axioms.

Given any target model $S$, define $F$ on objects by sending each
representable $y(t) \in R$ to the corresponding atom in $S$ determined by the history $t$,
and extend to all of $R$ via the universal properties of each closure step:

- **Colimit steps**: each object added by the closure operator $U_G$ at depth $n$ (§closure-operator)
  is presented as a colimit of objects already defined. $F$ sends the colimit to the corresponding
  colimit in $S$ (which exists by the axioms of $S$).
- **Fiber maps**: $F$ sends $H_t$ to $K_t$ via $\mathrm{eval}_t$ composed with the
  BiNucleus interpretation in $K_t$.
- **Nuclei and fixed fibers**: $F$ intertwines $(\sigma_t, \Delta_t)$ with
  $(\sigma_{S,t}, \Delta_{S,t})$ by construction of $\mathrm{eval}$.

Since $R$ is the least fixed point of $U_G$ (§self-generation) and $S$ also satisfies the
closure conditions, every object of $R$ lies in the $U_G$-closure of the atoms, so this
assignment is well-defined. The result is a geometric morphism $R \to S$ in the sense of
§sheaf-construction.

#### Uniqueness {#thm-initiality-uniqueness}

**Step 1 — Morphisms are determined on generators.** $R$ is generated by the representable
presheaves $\{y(t)\}_{t \in T}$ under the closure operations $U_G$. Any structure-preserving
$F : R \to S$ is completely determined by its values on these generators: since each closure
operation has a universal property, the value of $F$ on any object derived from generators
is forced by the requirement that $F$ commute with those operations.

**Step 2 — Generators are determined by history.** Each representable $y(t)$ must be sent to an
atom in $S$ at the corresponding fiber position $t$. Structure-preservation requires
$F(y(t)) = y_S(t)$, the representable in $S$ indexed by the same history $t$, since $F$ must
commute with the history-site structure (§history-and-fiber-algebra).

**Step 3 — History Separation eliminates ambiguity.** Without the Axiom of History
Separation (§history-separation), $S$ might have a non-trivial automorphism
$\alpha : S \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} S$ that is invisible to $H^\ast$: the fixed layers at
distinct histories could look identical, so $F$ could be followed by $\alpha$ to produce a
distinct morphism $\alpha \circ F : R \to S$ that is still structure-preserving. The Axiom
of History Separation asserts that no such $\alpha$ exists: $H^\ast_t$ varies faithfully
across histories, so the only self-symmetry of $S$ visible to the fixed layer is the identity.
This rules out all such alternative compositions.

Combining Steps 1–3: any two structure-preserving morphisms $F_1, F_2 : R \to S$ agree on all
generators (Step 2), are each determined by their values on generators (Step 1), and there is no
automorphism of $S$ that could relate one extension to another (Step 3). Therefore $F_1 = F_2$.
$\square$

#### Remark — Dependence on History Separation {#thm-initiality-faithfulness}

Initiality in its strong form (uniqueness, not merely existence of $F$) relies on the nuclear pair
$(\sigma_t, \Delta_t)$ varying faithfully across fibers (§history-separation). Without
it, a relational universe could have constant fibers $H_t = H_0$ for all $t$, making $S$
highly symmetric; the morphism $F$ would be defined only up to composition with automorphisms
of $H_0$. The same axiom is critical for §aperiodicity: both results hold in their strong
forms if and only if the Axiom of History Separation holds.
