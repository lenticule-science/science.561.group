### Corollary — Hyperverse-Fixed Universes Have Ontologies {#hyperverse-fixed-universe-ontology}

Every hyperverse-fixed universe $T \in \mathcal{H}$ (§hyperverse-fixed-universe) has an
ontology (§ontology).

#### Proof Sketch {#hyperverse-fixed-universe-ontology-proof}

A hyperverse-fixed universe $T$ satisfies $U_\mathcal{H}(T) = T$: the construction cycle
applied to $T$ returns $T$ unchanged. This means $T$ possesses all four phases — algebra,
language, geometry, dynamics — as its own internal structure, and these phases form a
completed ontological quartet (§ontological-quartet) with $T$'s own accord topos as joint
fixed point. This four-phase structure is $T$'s ontology (§ontology). $\square$

#### The Force of the Corollary {#hyperverse-fixed-universe-ontology-force}

The force is in what the self-referential fixed-point structure
$\mathcal{H} = U_\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{H})$ adds: a hyperverse-fixed universe's ontology
is **stable** — the cycle applied to it returns it. No phase is incomplete, no nucleus
has further work to do. A hyperverse-fixed universe does not merely happen to have the
four phases; it is defined as the kind of universe for which the four-phase cycle is
already done.

This stability distinguishes a hyperverse-fixed universe's ontology from the ontology of
any intermediate $R^{(n)}$ in the hyperverse tower (§relational-hyperverse). The
intermediate universes have ontologies, but their ontologies are not yet stable — the
cycle applied to $R^{(n)}$ produces $R^{(n+1)} \neq R^{(n)}$. A hyperverse-fixed
universe is at the fixed point where this progression terminates.

#### Conjectural Status {#hyperverse-fixed-universe-ontology-status}

This corollary is conjectural. It presupposes that $\mathcal{H}$ has hyperverse-fixed
universes (§hyperverse-fixed-universe), which is not established from the constructive
development of $R$. If hyperverse-fixed universes exist, each has a complete and stable
ontology. Having an ontology is not incidental to being hyperverse-fixed — it is
constitutive of it.
