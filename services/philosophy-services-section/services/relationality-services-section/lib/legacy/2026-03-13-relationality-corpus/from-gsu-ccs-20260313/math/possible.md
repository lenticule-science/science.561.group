### Definition — The Possible {#possible}

**Possible** is the cross-level name for the condition of non-closure: at any level of
the construction, something is possible when the relevant closure operator at that level
has not yet reached it as a fixed point.

The three instances — at the history, fiber, and universe levels — are distinct
mathematical concepts with different type signatures:

- **Extension possibility** (§extension-possibility): a step $s$ is possible at history
  $t$ when $s \star t \in T$.
- **Δ-possible proposition** (§delta-possible): a proposition $a \in H_t$ is Δ-possible
  when $\Delta_t(a) \neq a$, i.e., $a \notin \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$.
- **Generative possibility** (§generative-possibility): a universe level $R^{(n+1)}$ is
  generatively possible when $U_G^n(\emptyset) \subsetneq R$ — the closure iteration has
  not yet reached its fixed point.

In each case, possibility is the complement of the relevant fixed-point set; the relevant
closure operator maps every element to the least fixed point above it. Possible
carries no probability and no measure: an extension either exists in $T$ or does not, a
proposition is either in $\mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ or not, a level either satisfies
$U_G(X) = X$ or not.
