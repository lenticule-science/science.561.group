### Theorem — Normalization {#normalization}

We now relate syntactic normalization in FiberNuclearLanguage to semantic
normalization in $H$, focusing on the base type $\mathsf{Prop}$.

**Two-level distinction.** Syntactic normalization (reduction to no-redex form by R1–R13
of §reduction-system) is not the same as semantic normalization (projection to $H_t^*$).
A syntactically normal term such as an atom $p$ need not satisfy
$\sigma_t(\Delta_t(\llbracket p \rrbracket)) = \llbracket p \rrbracket$. The joint projection $\pi_t$ (§joint-projection) acts here as the semantic
normalizer $\mathsf{NF}_t = \pi_t$: the canonical projection onto $H_t^*$, defined
directly from the fiber algebra data, independent of syntactic canonical forms
(§universe-normalization).

Write $\to^*$ for the reflexive-transitive closure of $\to$ (§reduction-system), and
write $M \downarrow$ if $M$ is in $\to$-normal form.

#### Definition — Canonical $\mathsf{Prop}$-Terms {#def-canonical-terms}

A closed term $N : \mathsf{Prop}$ is **canonical** if:

1. No idempotency, top-preservation, or commutativity rule applies to any subterm:
   no subterm has the form $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-))$,
   $\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Transfer}(-))$,
   $\mathsf{Saturate}(- \wedge -)$, $\mathsf{Transfer}(- \wedge -)$,
   $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{True})$, $\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{True})$,
   or $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(-))$.
2. $N$ is built only from the Heyting-modal constructors
   $\wedge, \vee, \Rightarrow_t, \top, \bot, \mathsf{Saturate}, \mathsf{Transfer}$.
3. No disjunction absorption rule applies: no subterm has the form
   $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-) \vee -)$, $\mathsf{Saturate}(- \vee \mathsf{Saturate}(-))$,
   $\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Transfer}(-) \vee -)$, or $\mathsf{Transfer}(- \vee \mathsf{Transfer}(-))$.
4. No cross-type absorption rule applies: no subterm has the form
   $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-)) \vee -)$ or
   $\mathsf{Saturate}(- \vee \mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-)))$
   (or the symmetric $\mathsf{Transfer}$ versions).
5. No meet-absorption rule (R14–R19$'$) applies: no subterm has any of the forms
   $j((j(M)\wedge j(N))\vee P)$ or $j(P\vee(j(M)\wedge j(N)))$ for $j\in\{\mathsf{Saturate},\mathsf{Transfer}\}$
   (same-type, R14–R17), nor any of the forms
   $\mathsf{Saturate}((\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-))\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-)))\vee -)$,
   $\mathsf{Saturate}(-\vee(\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-))\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-))))$,
   $\mathsf{Transfer}((\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(-))\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(-)))\vee -)$, or
   $\mathsf{Transfer}(-\vee(\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(-))\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(-))))$
   (cross-type, R18–R19$'$).

We let $\mathsf{Can}(\mathsf{Prop})$ denote the set of canonical closed
$\mathsf{Prop}$-terms.

#### Theorem — Canonical Propositional Forms {#thm-canonical-forms}

**Statement.** Every closed term $M : \mathsf{Prop}$ reduces to a canonical term.
Uniqueness holds under the full reduction system R1–R19$'$: distinct canonical terms
denote distinct elements of the free nuclear Heyting algebra generated by
$\mathsf{Prop}$-variables.

More precisely:

1. For every $\vdash M : \mathsf{Prop}$, there exists a canonical $N : \mathsf{Prop}$
   such that $M \to^* N$ and $\vdash M \equiv N : \mathsf{Prop}$.
2. If $N, N' : \mathsf{Prop}$ are both canonical and $\vdash N \equiv N' :
   \mathsf{Prop}$, then $N$ and $N'$ are syntactically identical.

*Proof.* Strong normalization of R1–R19$'$ is proved in §strong-normalization by a
unified lexicographic argument: every rule strictly decreases the measure
$(\mathsf{fmlW}, \mathsf{ent})$ (§modal-weight-duality). Local confluence is established
there by checking all critical pairs. Newman's Lemma (SN + local confluence $\Rightarrow$
confluence) gives confluence, hence uniqueness of normal forms up to $\equiv$. Since the
free nuclear HA is the term model, two terms with the same normal form denote the same
element. $\square$

The syntactic normalization established here is fiber-level. The universe-level counterpart
— the semantic normalizer $\mathsf{NF} : H \to H$ as an internal map in $R$, and the
alignment between syntactic and semantic normal forms — is stated in
§universe-normalization in §sheaf-construction, after $R$ is constructed.
