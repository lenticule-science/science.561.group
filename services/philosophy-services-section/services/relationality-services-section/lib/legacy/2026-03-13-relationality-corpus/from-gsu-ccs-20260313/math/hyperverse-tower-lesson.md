### Definition — Lesson — The Hyperverse Tower

**The pattern you already know.** Within $R$, the depth filtration (§depth-filtration) builds $R$ by a tower of sub-universes:
$$
R_0 \hookrightarrow R_1 \hookrightarrow R_2 \hookrightarrow \cdots \hookrightarrow R.
$$
Each level $R_n$ contains histories up to depth $n$; $R_{n+1}$ is $R_n$ plus the new sheaves generated by one more application of $U_G$; the colimit is $R$ itself.

**One level up.** The hyperverse tower is the same construction, but each term is a complete relational universe rather than a sub-universe of a fixed $R$:
$$
R^{(0)} \hookrightarrow R^{(1)} \hookrightarrow R^{(2)} \hookrightarrow \cdots \hookrightarrow \mathcal{H}.
$$
$R^{(0)} = R$ is the first-turn universe. $R^{(1)}$ is built by applying $U_\mathcal{H}$ to $R^{(0)}$: treating $R^{(0)}$ as one object in the category $\mathbf{RU}$ of relational universes and taking sheaves on that category. $R^{(1)}$ contains everything $R^{(0)}$ can see, plus propositions *about* $R^{(0)}$ — objects that require $R^{(0)}$ as data, not just as context, and so cannot be represented inside $R^{(0)}$ itself.

**Why the tower must be infinite.** Each embedding $R^{(n)} \hookrightarrow R^{(n+1)}$ is strictly proper: $R^{(n+1)}$ contains objects not in $R^{(n)}$. This is the same reason the depth filtration is strictly increasing — the universe closure always produces genuinely new content from what it has. No finite level is self-universal; a universe that can see all relational universes must be able to see itself, which requires standing above every finite level.

**What the Beck–Chevalley conditions are doing.** The Yoneda embeddings $R^{(n)} \hookrightarrow R^{(n+1)}$ must preserve the nuclear structure — the $\sigma$- and $\Delta$-nuclei must cohere across levels. The BC conditions (§beck-chevalley-tower) are exactly this coherence requirement. Without them, the colimit would not be a well-formed relational universe. With them, the colimit $\mathcal{H} = \varinjlim_n R^{(n)}$ inherits a nuclear quartet from the tower, and the question of whether $\mathcal{H}$ is itself a fixed point of $U_\mathcal{H}$ becomes well-posed (§hyperverse-fixed-point).
