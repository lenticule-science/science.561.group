### Conjecture — Two Temperature Duality {#two-temperature-duality}

**Statement.** For a relational universe $R$ near a periodic orbit (§periodic-orbit), two temperatures are defined:
- **Thermodynamic temperature**: $T_{\mathrm{thermo}} \propto M_t(\delta)$ — proportional to spectral mass
- **Hawking temperature**: $T_H \propto 1/M_t(\delta)$ — inversely proportional to spectral mass

These are not contradictory. They describe the same settling process from inside and outside the spectral horizon, respectively.

**Motivation.** High entanglement means $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ are deeply nested, meaning the system is far from $H^*$. Large spectral mass $M_t(\delta)$ corresponds to high entanglement: the system is unsettled, and thermodynamic temperature is high. As the system settles (entanglement decreases, R7 steps fire), spectral mass decreases — the system cools thermodynamically.

The Hawking temperature goes the other way: as the system approaches its fixed point $H^*$, the horizon temperature rises. This is consistent: as the system settles, it releases energy. The Landauer cost (§landauer-limit) of each R7 step is paid as heat. The normalization proof (§strong-normalization) is running inside the horizon; Hawking radiation is what it looks like from outside.

The two temperatures are not measuring different things — they are measuring the same settling from two frames of reference separated by the spectral horizon:
- Inside (thermodynamic): temperature decreases as settling proceeds
- Outside (Hawking): temperature increases as the horizon shrinks toward the fixed point

**What a proof requires.**

1. Formal definition of the spectral horizon in terms of $M_t(\delta)$ and the stable sub-algebra $H^*$.

2. A derivation of the inside/outside temperature formulas from the relational universe axioms.

3. Connection to periodic orbits (§periodic-orbit): the horizon is the boundary of the periodic orbit's basin of attraction; the two temperatures measure approach to that boundary from opposite sides.
