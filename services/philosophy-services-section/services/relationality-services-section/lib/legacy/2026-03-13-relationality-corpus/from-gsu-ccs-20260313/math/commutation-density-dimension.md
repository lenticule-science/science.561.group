### Conjecture — Commutation Density Dimension {#commutation-density-dimension}

**Statement.** Let $(\Sigma, I)$ be the step set with independence relation of a relational universe $R$, and let $\mathsf{Phase}$ be its Invariant Locale (§invariant-locale). The Hausdorff dimension $\dim_H(\mathsf{Phase})$ is determined by the **commutation density** of $(\Sigma, I)$:
$$
\dim_H(\mathsf{Phase}) \;=\; f\!\left(\frac{|I|}{|\Sigma|^2}\right)
$$
for some monotone function $f : [0,1] \to [0, \infty)$ with $f(0) = 0$ (free monoid, discrete tree) and $f(1) = \dim_H(\mathsf{Phase}_{\text{free commutative}})$ (fully commutative case).

**Motivation.** The Grothendieck topology $J$ on the history category $(T, J)$ (§history-site) is generated by independence covers: a family $\{t^{(i)} \hookrightarrow t\}$ covers $t$ when the $t^{(i)}$ are independent sub-histories whose concatenation is $t$. The density of $I$ controls how many such covers exist.

When $I = \emptyset$ (free monoid), the history category is a tree — no non-trivial $J$-covers exist. The Invariant Locale is a totally disconnected Cantor-like space with Hausdorff dimension zero.

When $I$ is dense (many step-pairs commute), $J$ has many open sets — the geometry of $\mathsf{Phase}$ becomes continuous. In the fully commutative case ($I = \Sigma \times \Sigma \setminus \Delta$), every permutation of a history is equivalent; the space of orbits has a smooth structure.

The conjecture asserts that $\dim_H(\mathsf{Phase})$ is a topological invariant of the commutation graph of $(\Sigma, I)$, and that this dimension relates graph-theoretic properties of the independence relation directly to the macroscopic geometry.

**What a proof requires.**

1. A precise definition of Hausdorff dimension on $\mathsf{Phase}$ as a locale — the classical definition requires a metric, which must be constructed from the sheaf-theoretic structure.

2. Computation of $\dim_H$ for the base case: $\Sigma = \{a, b\}$ with $I = \{(a,b),(b,a)\}$ (the diamond monoid $\mathbb{N}^2$). This is the simplest non-trivial case and should yield a concrete numerical answer.

3. A monotonicity result: adding a commutation relation to $I$ does not decrease $\dim_H(\mathsf{Phase})$.
