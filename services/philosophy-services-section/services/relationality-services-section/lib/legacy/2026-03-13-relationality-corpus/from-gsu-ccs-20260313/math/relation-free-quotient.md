### Definition — Free, Quotient, and Fixed at the Relation Level {#relation-free-quotient}

The geometry phase — relations — also instantiates the free/quotient pattern. The
abstract side is the relational universe axioms; the free object is the syntactic
relational universe $R_{\mathrm{syn}}$; quotients are all models of the axioms; the
fixed-point layer is the invariant locale $\mathsf{Phase}$.

#### The Abstract Side: Relational Universe Axioms {#relation-abstract}

A **relational universe** is any structure satisfying the eight axioms of
§history-and-fiber-algebra–§sheaf-construction:
- Histories (§history): a history category $T$ with prefix order
- Fiber doctrine (§fiber-doctrine): a presheaf $H : T^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA_{nucl}}$
- Fiber language ([fiber-nuclear-language-axiom](fiber-nuclear-language-axiom.md)): an interpretation of FiberNuclearLanguage in each fiber
- Reindexing (§reindexing): nucleus-preserving restriction maps
- Trace separation (§history-separation): the presheaf $H$ distinguishes histories
- Locality (§locality): local data assembles uniquely (sheaf condition)
- Stability (§stability): the fixed fibers $H_t^*$ assemble into a stable sub-sheaf

This is what a relational universe **is**: no specific construction, no mention of
$\mathbf{Sh}(T,J)$. Any structure satisfying these conditions is a relational universe.

#### The Free Object: $R_{\mathrm{syn}}$ {#relation-free}

The **syntactic relational universe** $R_{\mathrm{syn}}$ (§syntactic-relational-universe)
is the **initial object** in the category of relational universes: it is the relational
universe freely generated by the axioms, with no additional identifications beyond what
the axioms require.

The initiality theorem (§initiality) states: for every relational universe $M$ satisfying
the eight axioms, there exists a unique morphism of relational universes
$R_{\mathrm{syn}} \to M$. This is the relational analogue of:
- $\mathbb{M}(\Sigma,I) \to$ any ATM (history level)
- FiberNuclearLanguage $\to H_t$ (language level)

#### Quotients: Relational Universe Models {#relation-quotients}

Every relational universe satisfying the eight axioms is a quotient of $R_{\mathrm{syn}}$
via the unique initiality morphism. The canonical relational universe $R = \mathbf{Sh}(T,J)$
is one such quotient — the one built from the specific history site $(T,J)$. The
existence theorem (§existence) proves it satisfies all eight axioms; the uniqueness
theorem (§uniqueness) proves it is the unique relational universe over $(T,J)$ up to
isomorphism.

#### Fixed-Point Layer: The Invariant Locale {#relation-fixed}

The **invariant locale** $\mathsf{Phase}$ (§invariant-locale) is the sub-object of $R$
consisting of globally stable sections: the sheafification of the assignment
$t \mapsto H_t^*$. It is the internal locale in $R$ whose points are the globally
doubly-stable propositions.

$\mathsf{Phase}$ is the fixed-point layer of the geometry phase in the following sense:
it is the largest sub-object of $R$ on which the nuclei $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ act as
the identity globally (not just fiberwise). It is the "most quotiented" relational
universe — the quotient of $R$ by all nucleus equations at the global level.

The quotient map $R \twoheadrightarrow \mathsf{Phase}$ is the joint projection
$\pi = \sigma \circ \Delta : H \to H^*$ (§joint-projection), sheafified globally.

**Status of this claim.** The identification of $\mathsf{Phase}$ as the fixed-point
layer of the geometry phase is a conjecture about the structure. The precise statement
— that $\mathsf{Phase}$ is the terminal object in the category of sub-locales of $R$
on which both Lawvere–Tierney topologies act trivially — needs proof.

#### Remark — The Geometry Level Has No Canonical NF {#relation-no-nf}

Unlike the history level (lexicographic NF) and the language level (FiberNuclearLanguage reduction
system), the geometry level has no canonical normal form in the syntactic sense.
The role of "canonical form" is played by the **patch structure** (§patch):
every sheaf in $R$ is determined by its profile (its restriction to the stable
sub-site), and the profile functor is the canonical "normalization" of sheaves.
But this is a semantic normalization (like $\sigma_t$ for propositions), not a
syntactic rewriting. The geometry phase is the one where the syntactic/semantic
distinction collapses — appropriate for the phase of **relations**, not qualities.
