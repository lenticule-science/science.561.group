### Definition — The Nuclear Language Reduction System {#reduction-system}

Fix a reduction relation $\to$ on FiberNuclearLanguage-terms, generated by:

- **Modal reduction rules** — the complete nucleus laws for $\mathsf{Saturate}$ and
  $\mathsf{Transfer}$, derived directly from the fiber doctrine (every nucleus is
  extensive, idempotent, and meet-preserving; $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ commute):

  *Idempotency* (the KZ / lax-idempotent identity for each nucleus):
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M)) \;\to\; \mathsf{Saturate}(M)
  \qquad
  \mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M)) \;\to\; \mathsf{Transfer}(M)
  $$

  *Meet-preservation* (each nucleus distributes over conjunction):
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}(M \wedge N) \;\leftrightarrow\; \mathsf{Saturate}(M) \wedge \mathsf{Saturate}(N)
  \qquad
  \mathsf{Transfer}(M \wedge N) \;\leftrightarrow\; \mathsf{Transfer}(M) \wedge \mathsf{Transfer}(N)
  $$

  *Top-preservation*:
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{True}) \;\to\; \mathsf{True}
  \qquad
  \mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{True}) \;\to\; \mathsf{True}
  $$

  *Commutativity* (canonical nesting order for mixed terms — orient to
  $\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(-))$):
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M)) \;\to\; \mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M))
  \tag{R7}
  $$
  > **Remark — Why this orientation.** {#remark-r7-temporal}
  > Because $\sigma_t \circ \Delta_t = \Delta_t \circ \sigma_t$, both forms denote the
  > same element; the choice of canonical orientation is principled rather than arbitrary.
  > $\mathsf{Saturate}$ ($\sigma_t$) faces backward: it asks what the accumulated history
  > forces. $\mathsf{Transfer}$ ($\Delta_t$) faces forward: it asks what every one-step
  > extension already contains in common. The canonical form $\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M))$
  > processes past before future — first settle $M$ against what history determines, then
  > find what the already-settled result stably carries into all extensions. The reverse
  > $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M))$ is semantically identical but inverts the temporal
  > order. R7 encodes the temporal direction the algebra already has.

  *Extensivity* is a pure semantic equation, not a reduction rule:
  $M \leq \mathsf{Saturate}(M)$ and $M \leq \mathsf{Transfer}(M)$ hold in every fiber
  $H_t$ by the nucleus axioms; they generate no syntactic redexes.

- **Disjunction absorption rules** — nucleus extensivity makes same-type inner applications
  in disjuncts redundant:
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M) \vee N) \;\to\; \mathsf{Saturate}(M \vee N)
  \qquad
  \mathsf{Saturate}(M \vee \mathsf{Saturate}(N)) \;\to\; \mathsf{Saturate}(M \vee N)
  $$
  $$
  \mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M) \vee N) \;\to\; \mathsf{Transfer}(M \vee N)
  \qquad
  \mathsf{Transfer}(M \vee \mathsf{Transfer}(N)) \;\to\; \mathsf{Transfer}(M \vee N)
  $$
  *Proof of validity.* For $\sigma(\sigma(a) \vee b) = \sigma(a \vee b)$: since $a \leq \sigma(a)$
  we have $a \vee b \leq \sigma(a) \vee b$, so $\sigma(a \vee b) \leq \sigma(\sigma(a) \vee b)$.
  Conversely, $\sigma(a) \leq \sigma(a \vee b)$ (by $a \leq a \vee b$ and monotonicity) and
  $b \leq \sigma(a \vee b)$ (extensivity then the same), so $\sigma(a) \vee b \leq \sigma(a \vee b)$,
  giving $\sigma(\sigma(a) \vee b) \leq \sigma(a \vee b)$. $\square$

- **Cross-type disjunction absorption rules** — commutativity
  ($\sigma \circ \Delta = \Delta \circ \sigma$) and idempotency together make inner same-type
  wrapping redundant even when trapped behind the other nucleus:
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M)) \vee N) \;\to\; \mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M) \vee N)
  \qquad
  \mathsf{Saturate}(M \vee \mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(N))) \;\to\; \mathsf{Saturate}(M \vee \mathsf{Transfer}(N))
  $$
  and the symmetric $\mathsf{Transfer}$ versions. *These rules are not derivable from R1–R11.*
  Inside $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M)) \vee N)$ the subterm
  $\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M))$ is already in R7-normal form, so no earlier rule fires.
  Validity uses $\Delta(\sigma(\Delta(a))) = \Delta(\sigma(a))$ (commutativity then
  $\Delta$-idempotency), so $\sigma(\Delta(\sigma(a)) \vee b) = \sigma(\Delta(a) \vee b)$
  by the disjunction absorption identity applied to $\sigma$ of
  $\Delta\!\circ\!\sigma(a) = \sigma\!\circ\!\Delta(a) \leq \sigma(\Delta(a) \vee b)$. $\square$

- **Meet-absorption rules** (R14–R17) — nucleus meet-preservation
  ($\sigma(m) \wedge \sigma(n) = \sigma(m \wedge n)$ and $\Delta(m) \wedge \Delta(n) = \Delta(m \wedge n)$)
  makes same-type meet images inside a disjunction argument collapsible, in both left
  and right positions:
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl((\mathsf{Saturate}(M) \wedge \mathsf{Saturate}(N)) \vee P\bigr) \;\to\; \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl((M \wedge N) \vee P\bigr)
  \tag{R14}
  $$
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl(P \vee (\mathsf{Saturate}(M) \wedge \mathsf{Saturate}(N))\bigr) \;\to\; \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl(P \vee (M \wedge N)\bigr)
  \tag{R15}
  $$
  $$
  \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl((\mathsf{Transfer}(M) \wedge \mathsf{Transfer}(N)) \vee P\bigr) \;\to\; \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl((M \wedge N) \vee P\bigr)
  \tag{R16}
  $$
  $$
  \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl(P \vee (\mathsf{Transfer}(M) \wedge \mathsf{Transfer}(N))\bigr) \;\to\; \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl(P \vee (M \wedge N)\bigr)
  \tag{R17}
  $$

  *Proof of validity.* For R14: $\sigma(\sigma(m) \wedge \sigma(n) \vee p) = \sigma(\sigma(m \wedge n) \vee p)$
  by $\sigma$-meet-preservation, then $= \sigma(m \wedge n \vee p)$ by the disjunction absorption
  identity (the validity underlying R8). R15 is identical with disjunct positions swapped. R16
  and R17 hold by $\Delta$-meet-preservation and the R10 validity. $\square$

  *These rules are not derivable from R1–R13.* The critical pairs that require them arise
  from the meet-distribution rules R3/R4 firing inside the disjunction argument of an absorption
  rule: R3 with R8 produces $\mathsf{Saturate}((\mathsf{Saturate}(M) \wedge \mathsf{Saturate}(N)) \vee P)$
  needing R14; R3 with R9 produces the right-position variant needing R15; R4 with R10 and
  R4 with R11 produce the $\mathsf{Transfer}$ analogues needing R16 and R17 respectively.

- **Cross-type meet-absorption rules** (R18–R19$'$) — the cross-type absorption rules R12/R13
  interact with R3/R4 to produce additional critical pairs not closed by R14–R17.

  From $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M \wedge N)) \vee P)$: applying R12
  gives $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M \wedge N) \vee P) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{R4}}
  \mathsf{Saturate}((\mathsf{Transfer}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(N))\vee P)$; applying R3 inside
  first gives $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(N))\vee P)
  \xrightarrow{\mathrm{R4}} \mathsf{Saturate}((\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M))\wedge
  \mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(N)))\vee P)$. Neither reduces to the other under R1–R17
  (R12 does not fire on a conjunction inside the outer $\mathsf{Saturate}$, only on a direct
  disjunct). Closing rules:
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl((\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M))\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(N)))\vee P\bigr)
  \;\to\;
  \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl((\mathsf{Transfer}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(N))\vee P\bigr)
  \tag{R18}
  $$
  $$
  \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl(P\vee(\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(M))\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Saturate}(N)))\bigr)
  \;\to\;
  \mathsf{Saturate}\!\bigl(P\vee(\mathsf{Transfer}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(N))\bigr)
  \tag{R18$'$}
  $$
  $$
  \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl((\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M))\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(N)))\vee P\bigr)
  \;\to\;
  \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl((\mathsf{Saturate}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(N))\vee P\bigr)
  \tag{R19}
  $$
  $$
  \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl(P\vee(\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M))\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(N)))\bigr)
  \;\to\;
  \mathsf{Transfer}\!\bigl(P\vee(\mathsf{Saturate}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(N))\bigr)
  \tag{R19$'$}
  $$

  *Validity of R18/R18$'$.* By commutativity $\Delta\circ\sigma = \sigma\circ\Delta$ and
  idempotency of $\sigma$:
  $\Delta(\sigma(a))\wedge\Delta(\sigma(b)) = \sigma(\Delta(a))\wedge\sigma(\Delta(b))
  = \sigma(\Delta(a)\wedge\Delta(b))$,
  so $\sigma(\Delta(\sigma(a))\wedge\Delta(\sigma(b))\vee p)
  = \sigma(\Delta(a)\wedge\Delta(b)\vee p)$. $\square$
  R19/R19$'$ hold by the same argument with $\sigma$ and $\Delta$ exchanged.

  *Note.* The patterns $\mathsf{Saturate}((\mathsf{Transfer}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Transfer}(N))\vee P)$ and
  $\mathsf{Transfer}((\mathsf{Saturate}(M)\wedge\mathsf{Saturate}(N))\vee P)$ (outer nucleus type different
  from inner, no inner same-type wrapping) are genuine stuck normal forms — no rule in
  R1–R19$'$ applies to them.

> **Remark — Trans and the KZ Identity.** {#remark-trans-kz}
> The rule $\mathsf{Transfer}(\mathsf{Transfer}(M)) \to \mathsf{Transfer}(M)$ is the
> term-level expression of the KZ (Kock–Zöberlein / lax idempotent) identity for
> the transfer nucleus $\Delta_t$. A nucleus on a poset is precisely a lax
> idempotent monad: the multiplication $\mu = \mathrm{id}$, witnessed by
> $\Delta_t \circ \Delta_t = \Delta_t$ (idempotency). At the term level this
> collapses every double application of $\mathsf{Transfer}$ to a single one. The same
> applies to $\mathsf{Saturate}$ via $\sigma_t \circ \sigma_t = \sigma_t$. Neither
> $\mathsf{Saturate}$ nor $\mathsf{Transfer}$ can accumulate depth through self-nesting:
> all such nestings are immediately reduced. The directed comonad $G_s$ in §closure-and-self-generation
> is also a KZ comonad (for the same reason — it acts on a thin category), and the
> structural parallel is not accidental.

The reduction rules R1–R7 are sound (they preserve denotation in every nuclear HA) and
strongly normalizing — see §strong-normalization, which supplies the weight-function proof.
R8–R19$'$ are also sound by the validities proved above, and all decrease either the modal
entanglement degree or the modal count, so strong normalization extends to the full system.

**Meet-absorption closure.** The rules R14–R19$'$ together close all critical pairs
arising from the meet-distribution rules (R3, R4) interacting with the absorption rules
(R8–R13). Same-type cases: R3 with R8/R9 require R14/R15; R4 with R10/R11 require
R16/R17. Cross-type cases: R12 with R3 requires R18/R18$'$; R13 with R4 requires
R19/R19$'$. No further cases arise: R7 prevents alternating-nucleus nestings beyond
depth 3 from accumulating new redex patterns (any $\mathsf{Saturate}(\mathsf{Transfer}(N))$
with $N$ itself nucleus-headed is first reduced by R7, after which the pattern falls
within the depth-$\leq 3$ cases already covered). The system R1–R19$'$ has no remaining
unresolved critical pairs.
