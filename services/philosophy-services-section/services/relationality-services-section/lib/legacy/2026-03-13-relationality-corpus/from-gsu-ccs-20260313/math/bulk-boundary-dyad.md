### Theorem — The Bulk–Boundary Dyad {#bulk-boundary-dyad}

The **bulk–boundary dyad** is the duality between $R$ as a full mathematical universe
(the bulk) and $\mathsf{Phase}$ as its stable sub-locale (the boundary).

#### The Two Poles {#bulk-boundary-dyad-poles}

| Pole | Object | Content |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Bulk** | $R = \mathbf{Sh}(T,J)$ | All propositions — stable and unstable |
| **Boundary** | $\mathsf{Phase}$ (§invariant-locale) | Only the stable propositions in $H^*$ |
| **Convergence** | $\pi : H \xrightarrow{\sim} H^*$ at the fixed point | Holographic completeness (§relational-holograph) |

**Bulk pole.** The full topos $R$ contains every proposition at every history: both those
that have settled ($a \in H_t^*$) and those still subject to nuclear action ($a \notin H_t^*$).
The bulk is the working universe — all the mathematical activity takes place here.

**Boundary pole.** The invariant locale $\mathsf{Phase}$ carries only the doubly-stable
propositions: $\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{Phase}) = H^*$. It is the stable residue — what survives
all nuclear action. The holographic projection $\pi = \mathsf{Stab}$ (§joint-projection)
is the canonical retraction $\pi : H \to H^*$ mapping each proposition to its joint
fixed-point image.

#### Convergence {#bulk-boundary-dyad-convergence}

At the fixed point $R = U_G(R)$: every proposition is already in $H^*$, so $H = H^*$
and the joint projection $\pi$ is the identity. The boundary $\mathsf{Phase}$ ceases to
be a proper sub-locale and becomes all of $R$. The holographic projection becomes an
isomorphism — the bulk and boundary coincide.

This is consistent with the holographic recovery theorem (§relational-holograph, §thm-holograph-recovery):
the canonical holograph $(R, \mathsf{Phase}, \pi)$ is complete at the fixed point in the
strongest sense.

#### Relation to the Construction–Observation Dyad {#bulk-boundary-dyad-relation}

The bulk–boundary dyad and the construction–observation dyad (§construction-observation-dyad)
describe the same $R$ from two perspectives:

- Construction–observation: the duality between building propositions ($H$) and testing
  them ($H^\vee$); the fixed point is the separating pairing on $H^* \times (H^\vee)^*$.
- Bulk–boundary: the duality between the full universe ($R$) and its stable sub-locale
  ($\mathsf{Phase}$); the fixed point is where the retraction $\pi$ is trivial.

The construction–observation dyad is internal to $R$; the bulk–boundary dyad is external —
it describes $R$ as a whole relative to its stable residue.
