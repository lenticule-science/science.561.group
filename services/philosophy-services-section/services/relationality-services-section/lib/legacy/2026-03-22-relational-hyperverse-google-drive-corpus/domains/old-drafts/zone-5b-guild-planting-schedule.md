# Zone 5b Guild Planting Schedule — Ironwood, Michigan

*A companion planting guide organizing the 120+ plant list from the zone-5b-growing-guide.md into ecologically functional guilds. A guild groups plants that mutually benefit each other, share compatible timing, and collectively deter pests and attract beneficials.*

**Key dates:**
- Last spring frost: ~May 25
- First fall frost: ~September 20
- Growing season: ~110-130 frost-free days
- Soil reaches 60F for warm-season planting: ~late May to early June

---

## Overview: The Nine Guilds

| Guild | Anchor Plants | Season | Key Function |
|-------|--------------|--------|-------------|
| 1. Three Sisters Indigenous Polyculture | Beans + Squash + Corn/Sunflower | Warm | Nitrogen fixation, ground cover, cultural practice |
| 2. Nightshade | Tomatoes, Peppers, Eggplant, Tomatillos | Warm | Maximum yield from heat-lovers |
| 3. Brassica | Cabbage, Kale, Broccoli, Brussels Sprouts | Cool (spring + fall) | Pest suppression via aromatic companions |
| 4. Cucurbit | Zucchini, Cucumbers, Squash, Pumpkins | Warm | Beetle and borer management |
| 5. Allium + Root | Garlic, Onions, Leeks, Carrots, Beets | Long-season | Alliums protect roots from flies |
| 6. Bush Bean | Bush beans, Chickpeas | Warm | Nitrogen fixation; separate from alliums |
| 7. Cool-Season Salad | Lettuces, Greens, Cresses | Cool (spring + fall) | Rapid succession harvest |
| 8. Herb & Insectary Border | Mountain Mint, Yarrow, Borage, Chamomile, Pollinators | Season-long | Beneficial insect infrastructure |
| 9. Perennial / Native Layer | Hops, Strawberries, Ramps, Sweet Grass, Hostas | Multi-year | Food forest, cultural plants |

**Critical spacing rules:**
- Guilds 5 (Alliums) and Guild 6 (Beans) must be **physically separated** — alliums stunt all legumes
- Guild 2 (Nightshades) must be **far from Guild 3 (Brassicas)** — both are heavy feeders and share pest pressure
- Isolate **fennel** completely from every guild — it is allelopathic to most vegetables
- Never plant **tomatoes near potatoes** (shared blight)

## Guild 1: Three Sisters Indigenous Polyculture

*Haudenosaunee-Anishinaabe polyculture. The anchor structure of the garden.*

### Composition

**Structural layer (trellis/vertical):**
- Corn — if grown; otherwise substitute **Chocolate Cherry Sunflower** or **Summertime Sunflower Mix** as corn-substitutes for beans to climb
- **Sunflower Mix** on north edge — "Fourth Sister" — attracts pollinators, draws aphids (and their predators) away from beans

**Nitrogen-fixing climbing layer:**
- **Seneca Bear Paw Runner Beans** (most culturally authentic for Three Sisters)
- **Sunset Runner Beans** (salmon-pink flowers for hummingbirds)
- **Succotash Beans** (Narragansett heritage)
- **Mouse Beans / Hog Peanut** (native to UP, dual aerial/underground harvest — naturalize as permanent guild member)

**Ground cover layer:**
- **Butternut Squash** (best keeping quality)
- **Black Beauty Zucchini** (fastest, most reliable for short season)
- **Mini Harvest Pumpkin Blend** (if space allows)

**Fifth Sister / edge plants:**
- **Hopi Red Dye Amaranth** — shade buffer, nitrogen accumulator, food and ceremony
- **Golden Giant Amaranth** — additional grain; 6-7 feet tall, north edge alongside sunflowers
- **Aurelia's Verde Amaranth** — fastest grain amaranth, safest bet for seed harvest

**Trap crops (outer ring):**
- **Tall Trailing Nasturtiums** — ring 4-5 feet from bean mounds; draw aphids and cucumber beetles

### Planting Sequence (critical timing)

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Late May (post-frost)** | Plant corn or sunflowers 1 inch deep, in mounds 4-5 feet apart. Alternatively, set out amaranth transplants at this time. |
| **Early June (corn/sunflower 6-12" tall)** | Sow pole beans: 4 seeds per mound at base of stalks |
| **Mid-June (1 week after beans emerge)** | Transplant or sow squash around mound perimeter, 2-3 feet from center |
| **Late May (same day as corn)** | Direct sow nasturtiums around outer ring; direct sow borage at guild edges |
| **Late May** | Direct sow Mouse Beans along shaded/woodland edges of property |

### Layout (per mound)
```
      [Sunflowers — north edge]
         [Amaranth]

 (nasturtium ring — 4-5ft out)
   [squash]  [squash]  [squash]
      [bean] [CORN] [bean]
      [bean]       [bean]
   [squash]         [squash]
 (nasturtium ring — 4-5ft out)
```

### Guilds to Keep Away
- Alliums (onions, garlic) — stunt beans
- Brassicas — mutual suppression with pole beans
- Tomatoes — share corn earworm pest (same insect as tomato fruitworm)

## Guild 2: Nightshade Guild

*The highest-maintenance, highest-reward guild. All members share similar timing and companion needs.*

### Composition

**Anchor plants (center, staked or caged):**
- Tomatoes: Spoon, White Cherry, Green Tiger, Black Strawberry, Paul Robeson, Cherokee Green, Green Zebra, Indigo Blue Chocolate, Moonglow
- Tomatillos: Purple and Rio Grande Verde *(must plant at least 2 for cross-pollination)*
- Peppers: Biquinho Yellow, Biquinho Red, Jimmy Nardello, Datil *(container)*, Piri Piri, Korean Hong-Gochu
- **White Garden Eggplant** (warmest microclimate, black plastic mulch)

**Inner ring companions (6-12 inches from base):**
- **Genovese Basil** — volatile compounds may deter thrips and hornworm moths
- **Thai Sweet Basil** — same function, anise scent
- **Dark Purple Opal Basil** — ornamental-functional dual purpose
- **Common Chives** — sulfur compounds deter aphids and early blight
- **Italian Flat Leaf Parsley** (let go to flower in year 2 — hosts parasitic wasps that attack hornworms)

**Middle ring (12-24 inches):**
- **French Marigolds (Petite Mix)** — limonene emission suppresses whitefly; hoverfly attraction
- **Blue Borage** — critical bumblebee attractor; bumblebees needed for tomato pollination (buzz pollination)
- **Murasakino Shiso** — aromatic; visual disruption of pest moths

**Outer ring / bed perimeter:**
- **Tall Trailing Nasturtiums** — trap crop ring; aphids and flea beetles strongly prefer these; remove when heavily infested
- **Garlic** (if not all beans nearby) — sulfur border deterrent; especially protects eggplant from Colorado potato beetle

**Interplanted between transplants (before nightshades go out):**
- **Carrots** — sow in rows between nightshade positions mid-May; roots loosen compacted soil; harvest early
- **Slow-Bolt Cilantro** — bolting cilantro hosts tachinid flies and parasitic wasps; let some go to flower

### Planting Schedule

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Late Feb / Early March** | Start Datil pepper, Biquinho Yellow/Red on heat mat (10-12 weeks) |
| **Mid-March** | Start Jimmy Nardello, Piri Piri, Hong-Gochu, eggplant (8-10 weeks) |
| **Mid-March** | Start Redarling Brussels (brassica guild, not this one) |
| **Late March** | Start all tomatoes, tomatillos (6-8 weeks) |
| **Mid-March** | Start all basils (6-8 weeks) — note: very frost tender |
| **Mid-March** | Start French Marigolds (6-8 weeks) — can transplant mid-May |
| **Early April** | Start parsley (8-10 weeks; slow germination, soak seeds overnight) |
| **Mid-May** | Direct sow carrots between future nightshade positions |
| **Mid-May** | Transplant marigolds (they tolerate light frost) |
| **Late May** | Direct sow nasturtiums at perimeter |
| **Late May** | Direct sow borage at guild edges |
| **June 1-7** | Transplant all tomatoes and tomatillos (soil 60F+, nights above 50F) |
| **June 7-15** | Transplant peppers and eggplant (nights above 55F) |
| **June 1+** | Transplant basils last (most frost-sensitive) |

### Layout (per tomato bed section)
```
[nasturtium perimeter]
  [garlic border]
    [marigold]  [borage]
  [chives] [TOMATO] [basil]
  [carrot rows interplanted]
    [marigold]  [borage]
  [garlic border]
[nasturtium perimeter]
```

### Guilds to Keep Away
- Brassicas (heavy feeding competition)
- Potatoes (shared blight)
- Corn and Three Sisters guild (shared corn earworm/tomato fruitworm)
- Mature dill near tomatoes (inhibits growth; okay young)

## Guild 3: Brassica Guild

*Best suited to Ironwood's cool climate. Two planting windows: spring and late summer/fall.*

### Composition

**Anchor plants:**
- **Kalibos Cabbage** (70-80 days)
- **Waltham Broccoli** (65-85 days)
- **Churchill Brussels Sprouts** (90-100 days — better fit for Ironwood than Redarling)
- **Redarling Brussels Sprouts** (130-140 days — start earliest of all; frost sweetens flavor)
- **Scarlet Kale** (30-55 days — also fall sow; outstanding for Ironwood)

**Between-plant companions (interplanted 12-18 inches apart through brassica bed):**
- **Garlic** (fall-planted, already in ground when spring brassicas go out — natural neighbor)
- **Common Chives** — sulfur volatiles mask brassica scent from cabbage white butterfly
- **Chamomile** — small composite flowers host parasitic wasps and hoverflies; traditional brassica health plant
- **Summer Savory** — anecdotally deters cabbage white butterfly; classic brassica herb

**Bed edge / border (trap crops and deterrents):**
- **Nasturtiums** — trap aphids and caterpillars; monitor and remove when infested
- **Radish / Watermelon Radish** — radish family draws flea beetles away from brassicas; radish sown densely at bed edges is sacrificial
- **Dill** (allow to flower at bed perimeter) — Braconid and Trichogramma wasps parasitize cabbage worms; keep dill far from carrots

**Supplemental:**
- **French Marigolds** — hoverfly larvae eat aphids; interplant at 18-24 inch intervals
- **Oregano** — provides beneficial insect habitat at bed corners

### Planting Schedule

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Mid-March (indoors)** | Start all brassicas on heat mat — 6-8 weeks before May transplant |
| **Early April** | Start chamomile indoors (or direct sow April when soil workable) |
| **Early April** | Start dill (or direct sow after last frost) |
| **Mid-April** | Direct sow radish at bed edges (grows fast — acts as flea beetle trap before brassicas are in) |
| **Mid-May** | Transplant all brassicas out (tolerates light frost) |
| **Mid-May** | Transplant marigolds alongside brassicas |
| **Late May** | Direct sow nasturtiums at perimeter |
| **Late July** | Direct sow Scarlet Kale for fall harvest (frost improves flavor) |
| **Early August** | Direct sow Watermelon Radish for fall harvest (55-65 days to October frost) |
| **August-September** | Fall greens succession: more kale, mustard, miner's lettuce as brassica understory |

### Guilds to Keep Away
- Tomatoes and all nightshades
- Pole beans (mutual suppression)
- Strawberries (inhibit brassica growth)
- Fennel

## Guild 4: Cucurbit Guild

*Cucumber beetles, squash bugs, and squash vine borers are the primary threats. This guild is structured around managing them.*

### Composition

**Anchor plants:**
- **Black Beauty Zucchini** (50-60 days — fastest, most reliable)
- **Summer Cucumber** (50-65 days)
- **Pickling Cucumber** (50-60 days)
- **Mexican Sour Gherkin** (60-75 days — drought-tolerant, pest-resistant; needs trellis)
- **Mini Harvest Pumpkin Blend** (85-100 days — needs early start)
- **Butternut Squash** (85-110 days — if not in Three Sisters guild)
- **Yellow Petite Watermelon** (70-85 days — warmest microclimate only; black plastic mulch required)

**Structural trap crop (plant 3-4 weeks before other cucurbits):**
- **Blue Hubbard Squash** (if available) — strongest evidence-based trap crop for squash bugs and cucumber beetles; plant 3-8 feet away, treat or destroy when colonized

**Interplanted companions:**
- **Blue Borage** — essential for cucurbit pollination; bees required for fruit set; bumblebees especially effective
- **Radish** — inter-row sowing between cucurbits deters flea beetles and some vine borers; quick to grow
- **Nasturtiums** — trap crop at bed perimeter for cucumber beetles and squash bugs; yellow-flowered varieties most attractive
- **French Marigolds** — interplanted at 18-24 inches; beetle deterrence and nematode management in rotation

**Border/nearby:**
- **Dill** (allowed to flower at bed edge) — hosts parasitic wasps
- **Oregano** — beneficial insect habitat at corners
- **Sunflowers** (if not in Three Sisters guild) — windbreak, aphid bank that draws predatory insects

**Nitrogen fixers interplanted:**
- **Bush Beans** — sow between squash mounds to fix nitrogen; keep away from allium guild

### Planting Schedule

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Early-Mid April (indoors)** | Start cucurbits in biodegradable pots (3-4 weeks before last frost; resent transplanting) |
| **Early-Mid April** | Start Blue Hubbard trap crop 1 week earlier than others |
| **Mid-May** | Sow radish between planned cucurbit positions |
| **Late May (after last frost)** | Transplant cucurbits; direct sow nasturtiums at perimeter |
| **Late May** | Direct sow borage at guild edges |
| **Late May** | Direct sow dill at bed corners (allow to flower) |
| **Late May - early June** | Direct sow bush beans between squash mounds |

### Guilds to Keep Away
- Potatoes (shared blight risk)
- Brassicas (chemical incompatibility)
- Sage (traditionally inhibits cucumber)
- Fennel

## Guild 5: Allium + Root Guild

*Garlic, onions, and leeks are the backbone of this guild. Carrots are the classic companion — their pest (carrot rust fly) is disrupted by allium scent.*

> **WARNING: This guild must be physically separated from Guild 6 (Beans) and Guild 1 (Three Sisters beans). Alliums stunt all legume growth.**

### Composition

**Allium anchor plants:**
- **Garlic** (Music, German Extra Hardy — plant October)
- **Shallots** (plant October or spring)
- **Walla Walla Onion** (long-day; sweet; poor keeper — use fresh)
- **White Spanish Onion** (long-day; good keeper)
- **Yellow Spanish Onion** (long-day; best keeper — up to 6 months)
- **Bulgarian Giant Leek** (start early; harvest past frost)
- **American Flag Leek** (bred for cold northeast winters; safer bet)
- **Egyptian Walking Onion** (perennial; earliest spring green)
- **Tokyo Long White Onion** (scallion type; day-neutral; sow repeatedly)
- **Common Chives** (perennial; bombproof)
- **Nodding Onion** (native perennial; ecological value)
- **Wild Leek / Ramps** (understory; separate woodland bed)

**Root companions (interplanted between allium rows):**
- **Korral Carrot**, **Rainbow Carrot Mix**, **Purple Haze Carrot** — carrot fly disrupted by allium scent; stagger rows: 1 allium row, 1 carrot row
- **Chioggia Beets** — compatible with alliums; harvest early before onions need full space
- **Radish** — quick-maturing gap filler; also trap crop for flea beetles

**Herbs in or near allium beds:**
- **Chamomile** — traditional companion; improves onion flavor (folk claim)
- **Summer Savory** — deters bean beetles; fine near alliums (unlike beans, savory is not harmed)
- **Oregano** — perennial; beneficial insect habitat at bed edges

**Do NOT plant nearby:**
- Any beans or peas
- Strawberries (alliums inhibit them)
- Fennel

### Planting Schedule

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Late October (by Halloween)** | Plant garlic cloves; mulch 3-4 inches straw |
| **October** | Plant shallot sets; mulch heavily |
| **Early March (indoors)** | Start Walla Walla onion (10-12 weeks; needs long start) |
| **Mid-March (indoors)** | Start White Spanish, Yellow Spanish onions (8-10 weeks) |
| **Mid-March (indoors)** | Start leeks (8-10 weeks) |
| **Mid-April** | Transplant Walla Walla onions outdoors (they tolerate frost) |
| **Early May** | Transplant White/Yellow Spanish onions |
| **Mid-April** | Direct sow carrots alongside allium bed positions (they like cool soil) |
| **Late April** | Direct sow beets (4 weeks before last frost) |
| **Late May** | Transplant leeks (when 8-10" tall) |
| **April-August** | Direct sow Tokyo Long White scallions successively every 3-4 weeks |
| **Mid-July** | Direct sow Watermelon Radish in gaps left by harvested garlic (garlic out by mid-July) |
| **Mid-July** | Sow fall carrot succession in garlic-cleared beds |
| **September (waning moon after Sep 26)** | Plant garlic for next year |

### Layout note
Garlic and shallots occupy beds all winter and spring, leaving by mid-July. Use those cleared beds for fall carrot/radish/beet succession immediately.

## Guild 6: Bush Bean Guild

*Nitrogen-fixing annuals. Must be placed away from all alliums. Pole beans belong in Guild 1 (Three Sisters) where they climb corn/sunflowers.*

### Composition

**Anchor plants (bush, no trellis):**
- **Blue Lake 274 Green Bush Beans** (50-58 days — fastest, most reliable)
- **Cherokee Wax Beans** (50-58 days — rust/mosaic resistant)
- **Red Swan Bush Beans** (55 days — ornamental red-purple pods)
- **Dragon Tongue Beans** (55-60 days — dual-purpose snap/shell)
- **Vermont Cranberry Beans** (60-75 days snap / 85-95 days dry — bred for short seasons)

**Marginal additions (longer season, start early):**
- **Kabouli Black Garbanzo Beans** (80-95 days — can sow 2-4 weeks before last frost; tolerate cool weather; well-drained spot)
- **1500 Year Old Cave Beans** (90-110 days dry — start indoors 2-3 weeks early in peat pots; may need to hang-dry indoors)
- **Succotash Beans** (as dry bean, 100-110 days — start indoors)

**Companions:**
- **Summer Savory** — "the bean herb"; interplant throughout; traditionally deters bean beetles and improves yield
- **Nasturtiums** — trap crop at bed edges for aphids and bean beetles
- **French Marigolds** — interplanted; beetle deterrence
- **Borage** — pollinator attractor for beans
- **Radish** — quick gap-filler and flea beetle trap
- **Potatoes** (separate row) — beans and potatoes are decent mutual companions for pest deterrence

**Do NOT plant nearby:**
- Any alliums (garlic, chives, onions, leeks) — stunt bean growth
- Brassicas — mutual suppression with pole beans; some effect on bush beans too
- Fennel

### Planting Schedule

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Early May (indoors)** | Start 1500 Year Old Cave Beans and Succotash in peat pots (2-3 weeks early) |
| **Late May - early June** | Direct sow Blue Lake 274, Cherokee Wax, Red Swan, Dragon Tongue (soil 60F+) |
| **Late May - early June** | Direct sow Kabouli Garbanzo (tolerates slightly cooler soil) |
| **Late May** | Direct sow nasturtiums at bed perimeter |
| **Early June** | Sow summer savory between bean rows |
| **Mid-July** | Succession sow Blue Lake 274 for second harvest before frost (50-58 days fits) |

## Guild 7: Cool-Season Salad Guild

*The first and last guild of the season. Two windows: early spring and late summer/fall. In summer, tuck under shade of taller guilds.*

### Composition

**Cut-and-come-again greens (succession sow):**
- **Merlot Lettuce** (25-55 days — deep burgundy, slow-bolting)
- **Speckled Lettuce** (45-55 days — Dutch butterhead)
- **Scarlet Kale** (30-55 days — cut-and-come-again; frost-sweetened)
- **Mustard Tendergreen** (35-45 days — bolt-resistant in cool weather)
- **Garden Cress** (15-20 days — fastest crop in guide; sow weekly)
- **Upland Cress** (50-60 days — watercress flavor; germinates below 70F only)

**Self-seeding/perennial greens:**
- **Miner's Lettuce** (37-40 days — native; shade-tolerant; naturalizes in cool moist spots)
- **Corn Salad / Mache** (40-70 days — possibly the single best-adapted salad green for Ironwood; cold-hardy to 5-10F)
- **Purple Orach** (40-50 days — spinach substitute all summer; heat-tolerant; dramatic 4-6ft)
- **Bloody Dock Sorrel** (40-55 days — perennial zone 4; lemony; plant once, harvest for years)
- **Strawberry Spinach** (60-80 days leaves — zone 2 hardy; self-seeds aggressively)

**Interplanting strategy:**
- **Radish** — quick succession between rows; marks carrot rows; trap crop
- **Chives** — perennial; plant at salad bed corners; deters aphids
- **Chamomile** — beneficial insects; flowers edible
- **Slow-Bolt Cilantro** — succession sow every 2-3 weeks through season; let some bolt for insects

### Planting Schedule

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Mid-April (as soon as soil workable)** | Direct sow all cool-season greens: merlot lettuce, mache, mustard, cress, upland cress, orach, scarlet kale, beets (thin for greens), cilantro |
| **Mid-April** | Direct sow radish as row markers and trap crop |
| **Mid-April onward** | Garden cress: sow every 7-10 days (15-20 day harvest — fastest in the guide) |
| **Cilantro** | Every 2-3 weeks mid-April through early August |
| **June** | Move salad guild under shade of brassicas, cucurbits, or taller plants to extend season |
| **Late July** | Resow all cool-season greens for fall: mache, lettuce, kale, spinach, mustard, cress |
| **Early August** | Final sowing: mache, kale, spinach (these will harvest through October and beyond) |
| **Do NOT sow mache in summer** | Seed dormant above 68F soil; wait until August for fall germination |

## Guild 8: Herb & Insectary Border Guild

*This guild doesn't occupy its own bed — it forms the edges, corners, and buffer zones of every other guild. Its purpose is to maintain beneficial insect populations throughout the garden.*

### Composition and placement

**Perennial insectary anchors (establish once, permanent):**

| Plant | Place | Key Beneficials |
|-------|-------|-----------------|
| **Mountain Mint** (Pycnanthemum) | Dedicated contained bed or root-barrier strip | Broadest spectrum: bees, wasps, beetles, flies; rated #1 in Penn State study |
| **Golden Yarrow** | Sunny perimeter; near every guild | Parasitic wasps, hoverflies, ladybugs, lacewings via flat landing-pad flowers |
| **Common Chives** (flowers) | Corners of all beds | Aphid deterrence; bee forage |
| **Nodding Onion** (native) | Sunny border strips | Bees, butterflies, predatory wasps; ecological companion |
| **Oregano** | Bed edges; especially cucurbit and brassica guilds | Range of predatory insects; drought-tolerant ground cover |
| **Bloody Dock Sorrel** | Part-shade perimeter | Perennial; low-maintenance; visual interest |

**Annual insectary plants (sow or transplant each year):**

| Plant | Timing | Key Function |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| **Blue Borage** | Direct sow late May (no transplant — taproot) | Bumblebee magnet; #1 for cucurbit and tomato pollination |
| **Chamomile** | Start indoors 6-8 weeks early or direct sow April | Parasitic wasps; tachinid flies; classic brassica companion |
| **Slow-Bolt Cilantro** | Succession sow April–August (let some bolt) | Bolting cilantro: tachinid flies, lacewings, parasitic wasps |
| **Italian Flat Leaf Parsley** (yr 2) | Leave overwintered plants to flower | Swallowtail host; parasitic wasp habitat |
| **Dill** (flowering) | Start indoors or direct sow; let go to flower | Braconid wasps for hornworm/looper control; keep away from carrots |
| **French Marigolds** | Start indoors March; transplant mid-May | Whitefly limonene suppression; hoverfly attraction |
| **Blue Morning Glory** | Start indoors 3-4 weeks early in peat pots | Hummingbird and bee forage; vertical trellis beauty |
| **Columbine Blue Star** | Direct sow fall or start spring | First-of-season hummingbird and bumblebee nectar |

**Trap crop permanents (monitor and manage, not passive):**

| Plant | Target pest | Placement |
|-------|------------|-----------|
| **Tall Trailing Nasturtiums** | Aphids (all), flea beetles, cucumber beetles | Ring 4-5 ft from all main guilds; monitor weekly; destroy when infested |
| **Radish (dense rows)** | Flea beetles, root flies | Edge of brassica beds; pull when infested |

**Aromatic deterrent herbs:**

| Plant | Function |
|-------|---------|
| **Korean Licorice Mint** | Aromatic insect deterrent; pollinator forage; tea herb |
| **Rosemary** (container) | Aromatic deterrent for brassica moths; move container to brassica bed in summer |
| **Purple Shiso** | Aromatic visual disruption for pest moths near nightshades |

### Placement Priorities

1. **Mountain mint**: Give it a permanent contained strip along the north or east border — it spreads by rhizome and needs root barriers. Plant once, benefit for decades.
2. **Yarrow**: Plant in full-sun perimeter spots between guilds. Leave flowers standing until late fall for seed heads.
3. **Borage**: Scatter self-sown plants throughout garden. Once established it will self-seed near tomatoes and cucurbits where it's most needed.
4. **Chamomile**: Allow to naturalize along brassica bed edges. Roman chamomile is perennial; German chamomile self-seeds.
5. **Marigolds**: Transplant from indoors mid-May into every guild that will tolerate them (not bean guild — some antagonism; not with fennel).

## Guild 9: Perennial / Native Layer

*These are long-horizon plants. Most take 2-3 years to establish, then provide for decades with little input.*

### Composition

**Fruiting perennials:**
- **Honeoye Strawberries** — plant bare roots late April/early May; remove ALL blossoms year 1; harvest year 2+; renovate every 3-4 years; companion: borage (pollination), chives (fungal deterrence)
- **Arapaho Blackberry** (thornless) — plant bare root spring or fall; fruit on prior-year canes; companion: comfrey (mulch plant), borage; avoid: alliums, fennel

**Climbing perennials:**
- **Hops** (Humulus lupulus) — plant rhizomes late May; bines to 20-30 feet; needs sturdy trellis installed BEFORE planting; harvest cones year 2+; lives 20+ years; companion: yarrow

**Native woodland plants (separate understory bed or forest edge):**
- **Wild Leek / Ramps** — deep shade, rich humus soil; plant bulbs spring or fall; 2-3 years to harvest; sustainable harvest: take one leaf per plant only; companion: hostas (same habitat)
- **Wild Garlic** — autumn planting, deciduous understory; spreads by bulbil (contain!)
- **Mouse Beans / Hog Peanut** — native nitrogen fixer; naturalizes at forest edges; harvest underground pods
- **Sweet Grass** (Hierochloe odorata) — plant divisions spring or fall; full sun to light shade; sacred medicine; companion: nodding onion, native grasses
- **Plantain** (Plantago major) — already in your lawn; no action needed; leave established patches

**Shade ornamentals:**
- **Hostas** — plant divisions spring or fall; part to full shade; same zone as ramps; companion: ramps, wild ginger

**Establishment schedule:**

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| **October (Year 0)** | Plant garlic, shallots, saffron corms, Egyptian Walking Onion bulbils, Nodding Onion bare roots |
| **Early spring (Year 1, late April)** | Plant strawberry bare roots; plant ramp bulbs in shade bed; plant sweet grass divisions |
| **Late May (Year 1)** | Plant hop rhizomes horizontally 2" deep; install trellis system beforehand |
| **Spring or Fall (Year 1)** | Plant hosta divisions; plant wild garlic bulbs in contained area |
| **Fall (Year 1)** | Plant blackberry bare roots; mulch crown heavily |
| **Year 2+** | Harvest strawberries, hops cones. Begin harvesting ramps conservatively (10% rule). |

## Quick-Reference: Known Antagonist Pairs

| Keep Apart | Reason |
|-----------|--------|
| **Any beans/peas + any alliums** | Alliums (garlic, chives, onions, leeks) stunt legumes — one of the best-supported antagonisms in companion planting |
| **Tomatoes + potatoes** | Share early and late blight pathogens |
| **Tomatoes + brassicas** | Competitive feeding; shared pest pressure |
| **Tomatoes + mature dill** | Growth inhibition (young dill is fine) |
| **Cucurbits + potatoes** | Shared blight risk |
| **Brassicas + pole beans** | Mutual suppression |
| **Brassicas + strawberries** | Growth inhibition |
| **Fennel + everything** | Allelopathic to most vegetables; grow in isolation or not at all |
| **Carrots + mature dill or parsnip** | Growth inhibition of carrot seedlings |
| **All Solanaceae + black walnut (within 50 ft)** | Juglone toxicity; lethal |

## Notes on Evidence Quality

Most companion planting relationships are well-tested traditional practice, not peer-reviewed clinical trials. The exceptions (strong evidence):
- **French marigolds + whitefly** — limonene emission measurably reduces glasshouse whitefly (PLOS One, 2019)
- **Marigold cover crop + nematodes** — effective as pre-plant rotation, not spot interplanting
- **Three Sisters nitrogen dynamics** — documented 24% caloric yield increase, 37% irrigation reduction (USDA)
- **Alliums + legumes** — stunt effect is consistently observed and widely reproduced
- **Mountain mint as insectary** — Penn State 3-year study, most diverse beneficial community of any native plant tested
- **Carrot rust fly disruption by alliums** — Horticulture Research International; mechanism is scent disruption, not pure repellency

The antagonisms are generally more reliable data than the benefits — chemical inhibition is easier to measure than diffuse ecological effects.
