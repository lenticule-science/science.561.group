Relational dynamics shifts attention from asking what a thing *is* to asking what must keep happening for it to continue. Every situation persists through relations: a conversation only if turns keep being taken, a club only if meetings are called and attended, even a stone only if gravity and friction hold it in place. Existence is maintained through ongoing relation.

Looking this way reveals how coherence emerges. Recognition grounds shared identity; units then connect and repeat until routines and boundaries form. Reflexivity lets boundaries shift when conditions change, overlaps and conflicts appear, and a whole field of relations solidifies.

Rooted in Lakota epistemologies and developed by emsenn, relational dynamics offers a grammar for describing how worlds stay intact under pressure. The hardest problems—in schools, communities, and organizations—are about sustaining coherence, not just generating ideas. Relational dynamics helps us notice the work that keeps things together so we can steward it.
