## Annotation

The directly relevant precedent for non-abelian sheaf cohomology in the cohomological layer's bilattice-valued direction. Initiates a discrete Hodge theory for cellular sheaves taking values in a category of **lattices and Galois connections**.

The technical heart is the **Tarski Laplacian** — an endomorphism on the cochain complex whose fixed points yield a cohomology that agrees with the global section functor in degree zero. Convergence of the diffusion process to harmonic cochains is guaranteed by Tarski's fixed-point theorem (the Laplacian is order-preserving on a complete lattice of cochains).

This framework gives non-abelian cohomology where the value category lacks abelian structure but retains lattice / fixed-point structure. Frames are special lattices; frame morphisms involve Galois connections. The d-frame setting therefore sits naturally in the Ghrist–Riess setup with the additional structure of paired frames plus consistency / totality relations.

## Position in predication categories

The candidate framework for d-frame-valued cellular sheaf cohomology in the [bilattice-valued cohomology chapter](prose/teaching/explanations/bilattice-cohomology-opener.md). The corpus's existing closure-operator structure (KZ lax-idempotent monads, Tarski-style fixed points) aligns naturally with the Tarski Laplacian framework — a structural resonance not coincidental, since both center on order-theoretic fixed-point machinery.

The paper's *direct* applicability is to cellular sheaves on cell complexes. The corpus's bitopological settling site has cellular structure on each fiber (paired Priestley spaces of finite distributive bilattices are cellular) but the full site is a fibration over $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$. Extending the Tarski Laplacian framework from cell complexes to fibered or stratified-cellular sites is a substantive open problem; Ghrist–Riess' work is the entry point but not the full answer.

The companion [@hansen-ghrist2019] paper develops the precursor spectral theory for cellular sheaves of vector spaces, with the Hodge Laplacian. Ghrist–Riess generalizes this to lattice-valued sheaves where Hodge theory's algebraic structure is replaced by Tarski's order-theoretic structure.
