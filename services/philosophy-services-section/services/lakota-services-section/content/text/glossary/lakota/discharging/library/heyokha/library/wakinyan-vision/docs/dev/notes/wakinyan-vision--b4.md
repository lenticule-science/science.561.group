# Wakíŋyaŋ vision

The Wakíŋyaŋ vision is the dream-event by which a person becomes bound to the Wakíŋyaŋ.
The dream is *not* a state the dreamer enters by choice; the Wakíŋyaŋ come, and once they have come, the person is a [heyokha-bearer](../heyokha-bearer/heyokha-bearer.md) for life.

## The unsought-ness

Walker's recordings make the unsought-ness emphatic.
The Wakíŋyaŋ vision is not produced by hanblecheya (vision quest), although it can occur during one.
It is not sought; it arrives.
This distinguishes the Wakíŋyaŋ vision from other wakhan-event types — from the sun-dance vision, from the curing-vision, from the conferring of a sacred-pipe — all of which can be sought through proper preparation.
The Wakíŋyaŋ-vision comes only when the Wakíŋyaŋ choose to come.

The unsought-ness encodes a substrate-fact: the heyokha bond is a *wakhan-event-installation* on the person's relational substrate, performed by the Wakíŋyaŋ themselves.
The substrate-mechanism is the Wakíŋyaŋ's wakhan-touch installing the ⊤-bearing principle into the dreamer; the dreamer cannot install this on themselves any more than a person can self-administer ceremony for themselves.

## The range of visions

Walker lists "snow bird, swallow, horse, dog, night hawk, frog, dragonfly" among the visions whose dreamers take on contrary conduct (Walker 1980, p.
101).
The Wakíŋyaŋ vision is the *most central*, but related visions also produce contrary-bearers.
Each of the listed animal-visions carries a wakhan-presence akin to the Wakíŋyaŋ's, and dreamers of these visions take up contrary conduct in the same structural register though sometimes in attenuated form.

This means the heyokha vocation has a *tiered* structure: the Wakíŋyaŋ-dreamer at the center, and other animal-vision-dreamers at adjacent rings.
The polity recognizes the tiers; the specific obligations vary by vision-type.

## The vision as morphism

In the categorical reading: wakinyan-vision is the *morphism* that converts a non-heyokha-bearer (a person without the wakhan-touch) into a [heyokha-bearer](../heyokha-bearer/heyokha-bearer.md).
Its domain is the person before the dream; its codomain is the person after the dream.
The morphism is one-way (the dreamer cannot un-dream) and is performed-by-Wakíŋyaŋ (not by the dreamer).

Per the bilattice reading, the wakinyan-vision injects the ⊤-bearing principle (from the Wakíŋyaŋ's ⊤-bearing character) into the dreamer's substrate.
The person was at some position before the dream; after the dream, the person's substrate carries a ⊤-bearing cell that requires perpetual discharging through [contrary-conduct](../contrary-conduct/contrary-conduct.md).
The discharging is the lived enactment of the cosmogonic principle in the dreamer's life.
