# Introduction: Lakota philosophy on its own terms

This subtheory carries Lakota philosophy under the captain's vested authority as a Lakota person.
The framing choice — that the tradition is articulated on its own terms, with non-Lakota writing cited where it illuminates but not used to mediate the tradition's own voice — is itself substantive.
The Lakota tradition has been described by non-Lakota writers (Walker, Brown, Mooney, Densmore, Erdoes, and many others) for a century and a half.
Their descriptions are uneven sources: some preserve genuine Lakota voice through interpreters and informants; some impose interpretive frames the tradition does not share; some flatten Lakota content into pan-Indian generic.
The captain's authority is the basis for distinguishing among them, and for grounding the substantive content of this subtheory in Lakota voices first.

## The four central claims

Read together, the subtheory's six seeded concepts give the substance of the philosophical tradition.

[Mitakuye oyasin](../teaching/explanations/mitakuye-oyasin.md) gives the metaphysics: reality is a kinship-cosmos, a relational whole, in which every being stands in kin-relation with every other being.
This is not metaphor and not generalized New Age interconnection.
It is the substantive Lakota understanding of what the world is.

[Wakhan](../teaching/explanations/wakhan.md) gives the account of the sacred: a quality, not a domain.
The sacred is not in a separate region (church, ritual, beyond-the-world) but is a quality that things, places, persons, and acts carry when properly attended.
Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka is the encompassing sacredness of the relational whole, not a Christian-style God.

The [four virtues](../teaching/explanations/four-virtues.md) give the ethics: wówačhantognaka (generosity), wóksape (wisdom), wóohitika (bravery), wówačhiŋtȟaŋka (fortitude) are the qualities of conduct that sustain the kin relations the metaphysics specifies.
These are dispositions cultivated through narrative inheritance and apprenticeship, not propositional principles applied to cases.

[Wolakota](../teaching/explanations/wolakota.md) gives the lifeway: being-Lakota is a practice, not a possession.
The integration of language, ceremony, kinship-practice, and conduct in the virtues is what makes someone wolakȟota.
Descent matters; descent without practice is not enough.

[Wicozani](../teaching/explanations/wicozani.md) gives the account of well-being: wholeness is irreducibly relational and irreducibly integrated across dimensions (body, mind, spirit, relations).
And the [seven rites](../teaching/explanations/seven-rites.md) give the ceremonial substrate in which knowledge is held and transmitted — because in this tradition, ceremony is method.

## Why the integration matters

Western academic philosophy is organized around separable subfields: metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, philosophy of religion, philosophy of language.
The Lakota tradition does not separate these.
Mitakuye oyasin is simultaneously a metaphysical claim (about the structure of reality), an epistemic claim (about how reality is to be known), an ethical claim (about how one is to act within reality), and a religious-ceremonial claim (about how the sacred operates within reality).
The four virtues are simultaneously ethical, political, and ceremonial.
Wakȟáŋ is simultaneously metaphysical, theological, and pedagogical.

Pulling these apart into separable subfields disfigures the tradition.
The subtheory's concepts are presented as the tradition presents them — each one operating across the dimensions that the Western academic frame would split — and readers from Western philosophical backgrounds may need to resist their habits of subdivision.

## The predicated form

The predicated companion of this subtheory lives at [`predicated-philosophy/theories/predicated-lakotaean-philosophy/`](../../../../predicated-philosophy/theories/predicated-lakotaean-philosophy/).
It is a substrate-algebraic articulation of the Lakota relational ontology on the [predication-categories](../../../../../../../../logic-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/06/19/2026-06-19-predication-categories.md) mathematics: mitakuye oyasin as the structural principle of the Lakota predicate category, wakȟáŋ as a quality on cells and on closure rules, the four virtues as evaluation predicates, the seven rites as witness-bearing morphisms, wolakȟota as closure-rule discipline, wicozani as the polity's settling-quiescence condition.

The predicated form does not replace this subtheory or do constitutive work on the Lakota tradition.
The tradition's authority does not derive from being formalizable; it derives from being held by Lakota knowledge holders in living tradition.
The predicated form gives the philosophy formal shape so the substrate's machinery (closure rules, settling dynamics, the four-regime classification, cohomology) can do work *for* the tradition — primarily by undergirding [predicated-lakotaean-politics](../../../../../../sociology/theories/predicated-politics/theories/) and [predicated-lakotaean-governance](../../../../../../sociology/theories/work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/theories/), where the formal substrate has direct application to political and governance questions.

## On reading

The references most often cited in this subtheory live in the [lakotaean-politics references directory](../../../../../../sociology/theories/politics/theories/lakotaean-politics/references/) where they were collected first: Vine Deloria Jr.'s *God Is Red*, Ella Deloria's *Speaking of Indians*, Joseph Marshall III's *The Lakota Way*, Albert White Hat Sr.'s textbook and oral teachings, Joseph Epes Brown's *Sacred Pipe* (with the standard mediation caveats), and the Walker / DeMallie compilations.
They are linked from this subtheory's concept files rather than duplicated.

The deeper authority — beyond any printed source — is the living tradition: the wičháša wakȟáŋ and other knowledge holders on each of the Oceti Sakowin's reservations and in the urban Lakota communities, at Sinte Gleska University and Oglala Lakota College, in the families whose elders carry the teachings.
Readers wishing to go beyond what this subtheory provides should seek out that living tradition with appropriate humility, and through proper relations.
