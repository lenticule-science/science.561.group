# Hunka

Huŋká — the making-of-relatives ceremony, one of the seven sacred rites in the form given to Black Elk by Joseph Epes Brown ([brown1953](../../philB/references/brown1953.md)).
Hunkapi (the ceremony being performed) establishes a formal relation of kinship between persons who are not biological kin, binding them with the obligations of relatives.

## Why this is a political concept

Hunka is not only a ceremony of personal bond.
It is the political technology by which the Lakota polity extends kinship — and therefore [legitimation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/legitimation.md) — to persons who would otherwise stand outside the kin network.
In a polity whose political organization *is* its kinship organization (Ella Deloria's central thesis [deloria-ella1944](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/reference-articles/bibliographies/deloria-ella1944.md)), the ceremony that creates a new kin relation is the ceremony that admits a person into the polity's organizing fabric.

Historically, hunka was performed in many contexts: to bind two persons (often boys) into a relation of brotherhood; to formalize a leader's relation to those for whom they spoke; to create cross-tiyospaye and cross-oyate kinship in support of confederate cause; and to incorporate adopted persons — including, on rare occasion, non-Lakota persons — fully into the people's life.
Joseph Epes Brown was given the Sioux name *Chanumpa Yuha Mani* (He Who Walks with the Sacred Pipe) and adopted by Black Elk and his family; that adoption was a hunka-grounded admission, with the bonds and obligations attached.

## Substrate significance

For the predicated form of this theory — [predicated-lakotaean-politics](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/predicated-politics.md), to be authored later — hunka is the canonical example of a *legitimation-bearing morphism*: an operation on the polity's relational substrate that creates a new kin-cell and the closure-rule obligations it entails.
The political substrate is not built only by birth and marriage; it is also built by ceremony, and hunka is the ceremony that makes this extension visible as substrate work.
