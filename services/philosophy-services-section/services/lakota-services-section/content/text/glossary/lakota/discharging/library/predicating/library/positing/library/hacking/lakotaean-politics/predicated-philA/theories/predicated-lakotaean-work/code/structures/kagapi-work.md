# Káğapi-work

Káğapi-work is the work-class whose cells fire on *making* predicates — quill-work, bead-work, leather-work and finished-hide-products, pottery, weapons-and-tools-making, regalia-creation, paint-and-decoration work.
The class is *women's-primary* in the complementary-gendering structure for the decorative-and-finished side (quill, bead, regalia, painted-tipi), with men's-primary firing on tool/weapon making.

## Cells in the class

$\Pi^\mathrm{káğapi}$ contains:

- *Quills* (porcupine-quill embroidery — pre-bead-trade primary decoration), *beads* (after bead-trade contact, ~1700s onward).
- *Sews-clothes*, *makes-shirt*, *makes-leggings*, *makes-dress*, *makes-moccasins*.
- *Tans-and-finishes-hide* (overlap with wówašake-w when including the wet-side of hide-processing).
- *Makes-cradleboard* (highly-decorated; significant intergenerational gift).
- *Makes-regalia* (sun-dance dress, war-shirt, headdress, peace-pipe-bag, medicine-bundle-bag).
- *Paints-tipi*, *paints-shield*, *paints-robe*.
- *Makes-bow*, *makes-arrows* (men), *makes-shield* (men), *makes-war-club*, *makes-knife-handle*.
- *Makes-drum*, *makes-flute*, *makes-rattle* — sound-instrument káğapi.
- *Makes-pipe-bag*, *makes-bundle-bag* (káğapi/wakȟáŋ-overlap).
- *Makes-courting-flute*, *makes-love-token* — relationship-and-marriage káğapi.

## Káğapi-work as substrate of the gift-economy

Most káğapi-output is *not retained by the maker*.
The substrate-pattern:

- The maker fires the káğapi-cell at $\mathbf{t}$ (the item is made).
- The item is *given* via the gift-economy redistribution morphism (cf. [gift-economy-redistribution](../../../../../../../settling/library/hacking/lakotaean-governance/philA/code/transformers/gift-economy-redistribution.md)).
- The maker's wówačhantognaka-conduct cells rise on the giving-discharge.
- The recipient's standing-receipt cells install (visible to the polity).

A woman who makes many fine quill-pieces over her lifetime gives most of them away — to kin at namings, to dancers at sun-dance, to mourning families during otúhaŋ.
Her personal-stored output is a small fraction of what she made.
Her substrate-record is in the *circulation* of her work — visible at council, at gatherings, in family-bundles across the tiyóšpaye.

The substrate-content: káğapi-work installs *value-in-relations* rather than *value-in-accumulation*.
The fine-beadwork dress is wealth in the substrate-sense — but the substrate-wealth is *relational*, not *property*.
Per [no-surplus-cell-firing](../../../../../predicated-philB/theories/predicated-lakotaean-work/prose/learning/claims/no-surplus-cell-firing.md), $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ has no rule firing accumulation-cells; the káğapi-work feeds directly into the gift-economy.

## Capability-installation through intergenerational teaching

Like wótakuye-work and wówašake-work, káğapi capability-cells install through teaching-under-kin chains:

- *Teaches-quill-work-to* — mother to daughter, aunt to niece; the most-skilled quillers had extensive teaching-cell-records to multiple younger kin.
- *Teaches-tanning-to* — mother-daughter, with grandmother often providing the most-detailed knowledge for fine-finishing.
- *Teaches-beadwork-to* — mother-daughter, often with cross-tiyóšpaye exchange at gatherings when a particular pattern was being learned from a particular tradition-bearer.
- *Teaches-weapon-making-to* — uncle-nephew, father-son.

The teaching-graphs have *cross-tiyóšpaye* edges more strongly than wótakuye-work does, because specific pattern-traditions (e.g., specific quill-color combinations distinctive to one band) propagate across tiyóšpaye through inter-band marriages and gathering-exchanges.

## Quill-society and the specialization-substrate

In some recordings (Walker 1980, Sicangu and Oglala material), women who were particularly skilled at quill-work formed a *quill-society* — a substrate-recognized society of master-makers with specific membership, specific protocols, specific shared apprenticeship-lines.
Substrate-algebraically, this is a *sub-institution* within the polity:

- Membership-cells: $\langle a, \texttt{member-of-quill-society}\rangle$.
- Shared closure-rules within the society (specific patterns reserved for society-members; specific quality-thresholds before a piece is "society-grade").
- Inter-society teaching protocols.

The structure parallels the wičháša wakȟáŋ apprenticeship-chain but with a different membership-condition (skill-based rather than vision-bound) and different scope (káğapi-work specifically rather than wakȟáŋ-work generally).

## Káğapi-work in reservation-era markets

The post-1880s reservation period saw káğapi-work become one of the *few* substrates that the settler-state did not directly attack.
Quill-and-bead-work continued; the U.S. military and settler households became markets for the work; rail-line tourist traffic created export markets.

The substrate-state during this period was *partially $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$-firing* (the work was done under traditional kin-teaching chains) and *partially $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wage}$-firing* (the work was sold for cash that fed the family).
The two subsystems fired simultaneously on the same cells; the substrate-state was at $\top$ on the gift-vs-sale question for most pieces.

The contemporary substrate has continued mixed-class firing: some pieces are made-for-gift (firing under $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$); some are made-for-sale-to-fund-family (firing under both $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ and $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wage}$); some are made-for-museum/collector (firing only under $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wage}$, often producing substrate-discomfort for the maker).
The mixed-class firing is a continuing reservation-era pattern that contemporary practice negotiates.
