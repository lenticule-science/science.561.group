The political life of the Lakota people: how the polity is constituted, how authority is held and circulated, how decisions bind, and how the polity stands in relation to other polities.
Subtheory of [politics](../../_index.md).
Authored under the captain's vested authority as a Lakota person.

## Scope and authority

Lakȟóta (most commonly anglicized "Lakota") refers to the western seven oyate of the Oceti Sakowin — the Seven Council Fires — namely the Oglala, Sicangu (Brulé), Mniconjou, Hunkpapa, Sihasapa, Itazipco (Sans Arc), and Oohenumpa.
The Dakota (Mdewakanton, Wahpekute, Wahpeton, Sisseton) and the Nakota (Yankton, Yanktonai) together with the Lakota form the Oceti Sakowin.
This subtheory's substantive focus is the Lakota political tradition; cross-reference to Dakota and Nakota practice is made where the sources speak to the broader Oceti Sakowin (notably Ella Deloria's writing).

The authority for substantive claims about Lakota political life rests with Lakota knowledge holders.
This subtheory is authored under such authority (the captain is Lakota and is vested by their people for this work); it cites non-Lakota scholarship where useful but does not derive its authority from non-Lakota writers.
Where contemporary Lakota scholars disagree with earlier non-Lakota ethnographers, the Lakota voice is given priority.

## What this theory commits to

The Lakota polity is not a deficient or pre-state political form.
It is a distinct polity-form with its own principles, durable across deep time, contemporary today.
Specifically:

- **Kinship is the political organization.** The [tiyospaye](concepts/tiyospaye.md) (extended family / band) is the basic political unit; relations of kinship are not auxiliary to politics, they are the organizing relation through which politics is conducted.
  This is Ella Deloria's central sociological observation ([deloria-ella1944](../references/deloria-ella1944.md)).

- **Authority is held distributively.** The [oceti-sakowin](concepts/oceti-sakowin.md) confederacy distributes authority across seven oyate; within each oyate, authority is further distributed across [naca](concepts/naca.md) (the council of respected leaders, including the wakichunza or shirt-wearers), [omniciye](concepts/omniciye.md) (the council assembled around the council fire), [akicita](concepts/akicita.md) (the warrior societies that enforce communal decisions during specific occasions), wicasa wakan (holy people), and eyapaha (criers).
  No single office holds all authority; no authority is held outside relational obligation.

- **Decisions bind through consensus, with withdrawal as the limiting move.** A council decision binds insofar as those bound have consented; a tiyospaye or person who disagrees beyond reconciliation can [withdraw](concepts/consensus-by-withdrawal.md).
  Withdrawal is not failure — it is the structural feature that makes coercive command impossible.

- **Ceremony is constitutive, not decorative.** The pipe ceremony binds council.
  The hunka ceremony makes political kin.
  The wiping-of-tears ceremony restores the polity after loss.
  Ceremony is the medium in which the polity exists; it is not symbolism layered atop a "real" politics.

- **The polity is in relation to land and to other-than-human relatives.** Lakota sovereignty is not abstract jurisdiction; it is the standing of the people in relation to specific places (most explicitly the Paha Sapa / Black Hills) and to the wider kinship-cosmos articulated by [mitakuye oyasin](../../../../../philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/mitakuye-oyasin.md).
  The relation to land cannot be commuted to property.
  The Lakota refusal of the Black Hills compensation award is the canonical contemporary case.

## Orthography

Lakȟótiyapi is written here with the modern standard orthography (Albert White Hat Sr. is the primary authority) where the captain has chosen this.
Where citing older sources that use earlier spellings (e.g., Walker's "naca yuha" or various transliterations in Brown's *Sacred Pipe*), the source's orthography is preserved within the citation and the standard form is given in the surrounding prose.
