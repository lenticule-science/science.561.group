# Claim. The cedar flute repertoire uses a small fixed vocabulary of named ornaments — warble, finger-pop, finger-slide, cut, tap, trill, and breath-pulse — each with definite articulatory mechanism, characteristic placement, and idiomatic role.

**Claim.** The [ornament](../../teaching/explanations/ornament.md) vocabulary of the [cedar-flute](../../../../b4/cedar-flute/cedar-flute.md) repertoire consists of seven principal named ornaments: the [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md), [finger-pop](../../../../b4/finger-pop/finger-pop.md), [finger-slide](../../../../b4/finger-slide/finger-slide.md), [cut](../../../../b4/cut/cut.md), [tap](../../../../b4/tap/tap.md), [trill](../../teaching/explanations/trill.md), and [breath-pulse](../../../../b4/breath-pulse/breath-pulse.md).
Each has a definite articulatory mechanism, a characteristic placement within the [phrase](../../teaching/explanations/phrase.md), and a specific idiomatic role.
The vocabulary is small (seven items) and fixed (the same seven ornaments appear across the tradition and across players); novel ornaments do appear occasionally in contemporary playing but are not part of the inherited repertoire.
The vocabulary is also *organized* — ornaments are not interchangeable, each occupies its own functional role, and the rules for ornament placement and combination are part of the music-theoretic content of the cedar-flute idiom.

**Argument.**

*The vocabulary has exactly seven items in the conventional core.* The documented repertoire and the teaching tradition agree on seven ornaments.
The [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) is the cadential alternation between [fundamental](../../teaching/explanations/fundamental.md) and second [harmonic](../../teaching/explanations/harmonic.md) via jet instability.
The [finger-pop](../../../../b4/finger-pop/finger-pop.md) is a rapid open-then-close action on a single finger hole producing a momentary upward pitch lift.
The [finger-slide](../../../../b4/finger-slide/finger-slide.md) is a gradual covering or uncovering of a hole producing a continuous pitch glide.
The [cut](../../../../b4/cut/cut.md) is a brief covering of the currently-lowest-open hole producing a momentary downward pitch dip.
The [tap](../../../../b4/tap/tap.md) is a percussive finger-action on a hole producing a rhythmic accent with minimal pitch change.
The [trill](../../teaching/explanations/trill.md) is a sustained rapid alternation between two adjacent scale-degrees via repeated fingering motion.
The [breath-pulse](../../../../b4/breath-pulse/breath-pulse.md) is a diaphragm-controlled modulation of breath pressure that articulates one tone from another within a continuous breath.
These seven cover the documented core; rare and marginal ornaments exist but are not part of the conventional vocabulary.

*Each ornament has a definite articulatory mechanism.* The articulatory mechanisms divide the vocabulary into three groups. *Finger-controlled* ornaments — [finger-pop](../../../../b4/finger-pop/finger-pop.md), [finger-slide](../../../../b4/finger-slide/finger-slide.md), [cut](../../../../b4/cut/cut.md), [tap](../../../../b4/tap/tap.md), [trill](../../teaching/explanations/trill.md) — operate by modifying the [finger-hole](../../../../b4/finger-hole/finger-hole.md) state and therefore by modifying the air column's termination point. *Breath-controlled* ornaments — [breath-pulse](../../../../b4/breath-pulse/breath-pulse.md) — operate by modulating the breath pressure at the sound source. *Jet-instability-controlled* ornaments — [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) — operate by tuning the breath pressure to the boundary between two [harmonic](../../teaching/explanations/harmonic.md) regimes.
Each mechanism produces a characteristically different acoustic effect, and the three groups are not interchangeable.

*Each ornament has a characteristic placement.* The [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) is principally cadential, applied to the held [tonic](../../teaching/explanations/fundamental.md) at the [cadence](teaching/explanations/cadence.md); it also appears occasionally on extended mid-phrase sustained tones (typically scale-degree 5 or b7).
The [finger-pop](../../../../b4/finger-pop/finger-pop.md) and [cut](../../../../b4/cut/cut.md) appear principally mid-phrase, as brief articulative chirps on sustained tones; they can also appear in cadential approaches.
The [finger-slide](../../../../b4/finger-slide/finger-slide.md) appears in three principal positions: as approach to a scale-degree from below (especially at phrase-beginnings), as cadential approach (slide into the b3 before the b3 → 1 cadence, or slide into the tonic at the cadence itself), and as smear between two scale-degrees mid-phrase.
The [trill](../../teaching/explanations/trill.md) appears as ornamental extension of a sustained tone (mid-phrase) and as cadential preparation.
The [tap](../../../../b4/tap/tap.md) appears as rhythmic articulation within a held tone.
The [breath-pulse](../../../../b4/breath-pulse/breath-pulse.md) appears between successive notes within a phrase, marking the articulation between them.

*The ornaments are not interchangeable.* A given position in a [phrase](../../teaching/explanations/phrase.md) admits some ornaments and not others.
A held [tonic](../../teaching/explanations/fundamental.md) at a [cadence](teaching/explanations/cadence.md) admits a [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) — that is the canonical ornament for that position — but does not idiomatically admit a [finger-pop](../../../../b4/finger-pop/finger-pop.md) (a pop would produce an upward pitch deviation, violating the cadential settled-on-tonic character).
A sustained b7 mid-phrase admits a [trill](../../teaching/explanations/trill.md) to 8 or a [cut](../../../../b4/cut/cut.md) to 5, but does not idiomatically admit a cadential [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) (which would mis-mark the b7 as a cadential terminus).
The choice of which ornament fits which position is part of the music-theoretic knowledge of the cedar-flute idiom — a player who applies the wrong ornament at the wrong position produces music that sounds unidiomatic even to a listener who could not articulate the rule being violated.

*The ornaments interact with each other.* Some ornaments combine; others do not.
A [finger-slide](../../../../b4/finger-slide/finger-slide.md) into a held tone can be followed by a [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) on the held tone (combine fine).
A [finger-pop](../../../../b4/finger-pop/finger-pop.md) cannot combine simultaneously with a [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) on the same tone (the warble's jet-instability regime is disrupted by the pop's finger-action).
A [trill](../../teaching/explanations/trill.md) cannot combine with a [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) (both occupy the entire duration of the host tone, and the bore cannot simultaneously sustain both the harmonic-mode alternation of the warble and the fingering-state alternation of the trill).
The space of admissible ornament combinations is therefore smaller than the product space of the individual ornaments.
The [ornament-glossary](../../../code/structures/ornament-glossary.md) structure documents both the individual ornaments and the combination rules.

*The vocabulary is the basis for strophic variation.* The [strophic-variation](../../../code/transformers/strophic-variation.md) operation that produces successive [strophes](../../teaching/explanations/strophe.md) of a [courting-song](../../../../b4/courting-song/courting-song.md) draws principally on [ornament-substitution](../../../code/transformers/ornament-substitution.md): a plain tone in one strophe may be ornamented in the next, or an ornamented tone may have a different ornament substituted in.
The seven-ornament vocabulary determines the range of variation that strophic variation can produce; an idiomatic strophic variation respects the placement and combination rules of the ornament vocabulary.

**Structures produced.**

- [ornament-glossary](../../../code/structures/ornament-glossary.md) — the named set of ornaments with articulation, placement-rules, and combination-rules.

**Transformers produced.**

- [ornament-substitution](../../../code/transformers/ornament-substitution.md) — the operation taking a plain (or differently-ornamented) note and producing an ornamented variant by selecting an ornament from the ornament-glossary appropriate to the position.
