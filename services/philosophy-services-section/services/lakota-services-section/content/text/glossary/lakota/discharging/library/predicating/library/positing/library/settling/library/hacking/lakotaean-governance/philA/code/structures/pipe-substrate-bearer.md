# Pipe (čhaŋnúŋpa)

The pipe is a substrate-bearing entity — an instance of $E^\mathrm{cer}_\mathrm{lak}$ (ceremonial objects) whose role in the polity's substrate is to *carry wakȟáŋ-cells across ceremonies*.
The pipe is the polity's primary substrate-bearer; many of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$'s wakȟáŋ-marking firings condition on a pipe's presence and its prior-ceremonial history.

A specific pipe entity $\check{C} \in E^\mathrm{cer}_\mathrm{lak}$ carries cells recording its history, its current keeper, its wakȟáŋ-marking status, and the set of ceremonies in which it has been used.

## Constituent predications

A pipe entity carries:

- $\langle \check{C}, \texttt{made-by}, m\rangle$ — the maker (typically a wičháša wakȟáŋ or designated stone-bearer).
- $\langle \check{C}, \texttt{stone-from}, p\rangle$ — the place of the bowl-stone (typically Pipestone, Minnesota — *Iyáŋša* — a confederacy-shared sacred quarry).
- $\langle \check{C}, \texttt{kept-by}, k\rangle$ — the current keeper.
- $\langle \check{C}, \texttt{used-in-ceremony}, R, \tau\rangle$ — each ceremony the pipe has been used in, with timestamp.
  The accumulation of this cell-list across years is the pipe's substrate-history.
- $\langle \check{C}, \texttt{wakhan-status}\rangle$ — the pipe's current wakȟáŋ-marking.
  For a properly-kept pipe, $w_\mathrm{lak}(\check{C}) = 1$ continuously.
- $\langle \check{C}, \texttt{class}, c\rangle$ — the pipe's class: personal-pipe, family-pipe, society-pipe, peace-pipe (treaty-bearing), the sacred Calf Pipe of the polity.

## The Calf Pipe

The Calf Pipe — *Pte-Hiŋčála Čhaŋnúŋpa* — is the original wakȟáŋ pipe brought to the people by White Buffalo Calf Woman (*Pte-Hiŋčála Wakȟáŋ Wíŋ*), the central cosmological substrate-installation event in Lakota tradition.
The Calf Pipe is held by a designated keeper (the *Čhaŋnúŋpa Yuhá*) in continuous tradition; the current keeper at writing is Arvol Looking Horse (19th-generation keeper, Eyapaha Lakota Oyate).
The Calf Pipe is the *foundational substrate-bearer* of the polity's wakȟáŋ-marking.

Substrate-algebraically: the Calf Pipe's wakȟáŋ-marking is the *source* of the polity's wakȟáŋ-marking discipline.
(W4) of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ ultimately traces wakȟáŋ-propagation chains back to firings involving the Calf Pipe directly (in the founding-substrate) or through derivative pipes whose making-cells trace via wičháša-wakȟáŋ-witness chains to the Calf Pipe's keepers.

## Yuhápi-bearing across time

The pipe is the paradigmatic [yuhápi](../../../../../../../philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/yuhapi.md)-borne entity.
The keeper's substrate-role is *bearing*: maintaining the pipe with attention, conducting its proper care, transmitting it at transition (death or formal succession).
The keeper's standing as wičháša-wakȟáŋ-or-equivalent rides on the pipe's continued wakȟáŋ-marking.

The transmission-pattern across keepers:

- The keeper trains a successor — often a kin-relative, sometimes a chosen-by-vision non-kin.
- The successor learns the songs, the protocols, the ceremonies the pipe participates in.
- At the keeper's death (or formal succession), the pipe transfers via a wakȟáŋ-bearing rite.
- The new keeper continues the bearing.

The chain is *continuous* — there is no gap in the bearing-cell-firing.
The Calf Pipe specifically has been continuously borne since the founding-cosmological-event; the bearer-chain is the unbroken substrate of the polity's wakȟáŋ-marking through millennia.

## The pipe as ceremony-participant

The pipe is not a passive object during ceremony; it is a *substrate-participant*.
The substrate-content:

- During a ceremony, the pipe is loaded with tobacco (and sometimes other sacred herbs).
- The pipe is offered to the directions, to the Wakȟáŋ-beings, to the participants.
- Each participant smokes (or in some cases pretends-to-smoke or touches the pipe), firing $\langle \mathrm{participant}, \texttt{smoked-with-pipe}, \check{C}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked.
- The smoke carries the prayer to the wakȟáŋ-beings (substrate-content of "smoke as message").

The pipe's *presence* fires wakȟáŋ-marking on the ceremony as a whole.
A treaty-signing with a pipe present is wakȟáŋ-marked; a treaty-signing without a pipe present is not.
The pipe is the *condition* for the wakȟáŋ-marking-firing, not just a participant.

## The pipe at treaty

For inter-polity treaty (cf. [treaty-as-wakhan-declaration](../../../philB/code/transformers/treaty-as-wakhan-declaration.md)), the pipe's role is structural:

- The treaty-pipe is typically a *specific* pipe, often newly-made for this purpose or designated by the council.
- During the negotiations, the pipe is passed between the delegations.
- Both delegations smoke; the smoke-witness cell fires for each participant.
- The treaty-cell's wakȟáŋ-marking depends on this firing.

Per [peace-pipe](../../../philB/code/transformers/peace-pipe.md) (the BC-reset morphism for inter-polity overlap), the pipe is what makes the BC-reset possible.
Without the pipe, the BC square does not reset; the polities remain at $\top$ on the overlap.

The treaty-pipe is *kept* after the treaty by both polities (or by a designated joint-keeper).
Its continued wakȟáŋ-marking is the substrate-record of the treaty's continued binding.
If the treaty-pipe is destroyed, lost, or de-wakȟáŋ-marked (e.g., used in a way that violates its protocol), the treaty's substrate-binding is structurally weakened.

## Pipe-class-specific protocols

Each pipe-class has specific protocols:

- *Personal pipes*: relatively few protocols; the keeper's own conduct sustains the wakȟáŋ-marking.
- *Family pipes*: shared use within tiyóšpaye; specific occasions only; transmission at parental death.
- *Society pipes*: each warrior-society or healing-society has its pipe(s); used at society gatherings; transmission within the society.
- *Peace-pipes* (treaty-bearing): inter-polity protocols; kept at the relevant border or at the council-grounds; used only at specific events.
- *The Calf Pipe*: kept perpetually by the Čhaŋnúŋpa Yuhá; used only at specific high-substrate occasions (most ceremonies use derivative-pipes, not the Calf Pipe directly).

## Confiscation and continuity

The settler-state's various pipe-confiscation events — at boarding-school enrollment, during the sun-dance-suppression era, in archaeological/anthropological collecting — operated by physical removal of pipes from their keepers.
The substrate-effect:

- The keeper-cell drops on the specific confiscated pipe.
- The pipe's continuing wakȟáŋ-marking depends on the keeper's continuing bearing; without bearing, the marking lapses.
- The polity loses a substrate-bearer.

The contemporary repatriation of confiscated pipes (under NAGPRA — Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act, 1990) is substrate-restoration: returning pipes to their proper keepers and rebearing them in active ceremonial use.
Many museum-held pipes have been repatriated to the original keeper-lines (or to designated successors when the original lines were broken) and have resumed substrate-bearing.
