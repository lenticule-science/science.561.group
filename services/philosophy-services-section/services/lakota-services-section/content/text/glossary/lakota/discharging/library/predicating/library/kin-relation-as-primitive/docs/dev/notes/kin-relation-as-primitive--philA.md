# Kin-relation as primitive

The structural commitment that distinguishes the Lakota predicate category from European-philosophical predicate categories: the *kin-relation* is a primitive predicate type, and properties of entities standing alone are not.

## The shape

In a standard Euro-philosophical predicate category, monadic predicates ($P(x)$ — "x is wise," "x is sacred," "x is brave") are primitive and dyadic relations ($R(x, y)$ — "x is the brother of y") are derived.
Lakota relational ontology inverts the priority: dyadic kin-relations are primitive, and apparently-monadic properties decompose into kin-relations.

Formally, the Lakota predicate category $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{lak}}$ contains a distinguished family of *kin-predicates*:

$$\mathrm{Kin} \;=\; \{\,\texttt{relative-of},\, \texttt{kin-class-of},\, \texttt{ancestor-of},\, \texttt{descendant-of},\, \texttt{relation-to-place},\, \texttt{spirit-relation-to},\, \dots\,\}$$

A cell $\langle e_1, p, e_2\rangle$ for $p \in \mathrm{Kin}$ takes a bilattice value in the usual way ($\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f}, \bot, \top$); the kinship structure on the populace is the section of these cells across the entity set.

What is non-standard: the predicates that look monadic — *is-wise*, *is-sacred*, *is-brave* — are read as relational. *Wise-with-respect-to* takes a relatum (wise toward whom? wise in what relational context?). *Sacred-to* takes a relatum (sacred to which people? sacred in which ceremony's frame?). *Brave-in-relation-to* takes a relatum (brave in defense of whom? brave in service to what relation?).
The substrate does not admit primitive monadic properties; every property is read relationally.

## Why this is the correct shape for the Lakota tradition

[Mitakuye oyasin](../../../../indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/mitakuye-oyasin.md) is the metaphysical claim that everything stands in relation.
A predicate category that admitted primitive monadic properties would carry an implicit metaphysical commitment to entity-properties-standing-alone — exactly what the tradition denies.
The substrate's predicate-category shape *is* the substrate's metaphysics; aligning it with mitakuye oyasin requires the relational primitive.

The structural payoff is significant.
Properties that are notoriously slippery in Western analytic treatment — virtue, sacredness, well-being — become concrete relational cells with the standard bilattice machinery available.
A person is not "brave" as a free-standing property; they are *brave-in-relation-to* specific relatives, and the substrate carries the witnessing structure for each such cell.

## Effect on closure rules

Closure rules in $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{lak}}$ quantify over kin-cells in their antecedents and consequents.
The [four virtues](four-virtues-as-evaluation-predicates.md) evaluation produces conduct-cell settlings that fire downstream closure rules over relation-cells.
The [wolakota closure discipline](wolakota-as-closure-discipline.md) operates as a coherent rule subsystem because the rules all share the kin-relational primitive.

## Relation to predicated-governance

Predicated-governance's [governing-by-kinship](../../../../../sociology/theories/work/theories/predicated-work/theories/predicated-governance/objects/governing-by-kinship.md) treats kinship as a *governance infrastructure layered onto* a substrate.
The Lakota predicate category takes the structurally stronger position: kinship is not infrastructure layered onto an entity-property substrate, it is the substrate's primary predicate type.
The predicated-governance object is a generalization that applies to many Indigenous traditions; predicated-lakotaean-philosophy's kin-relation-as-primitive is the specialization that fixes the Lakota predicate category as kin-relational at the root.
