Strophic form is a song-form in which a single melodic unit — the [strophe](./strophe.md) — is presented and then repeated, possibly several times, often with ornamental or rhythmic variation between repetitions.
The form is named from the Greek *strophē* (turn, verse) — the strophe is the unit that "turns" back to its beginning each time it is repeated.
Strophic form is one of the most widespread song-forms in the world's musical traditions, and it is the principal form of the Lakotaean [cedar-flute](../../../../b4/cedar-flute/cedar-flute.md) [courting-song](../../../../b4/courting-song/courting-song.md) repertoire and of much [wiilowan](../../../../b4/wiilowan/wiilowan.md) vocal song.

The musical content of a strophic-form song is therefore organized in two layers.
The first layer is the [strophe](./strophe.md) itself — a complete melodic unit consisting of several [phrases](./phrase.md), with internal organization, contour, and a terminal [cadence](./cadence.md).
The second layer is the *sequence of strophes* — the song's overall presentation, which is a series of repetitions of the strophe, often with [ornament-substitution](../../../code/transformers/ornament-substitution.md) and small rhythmic shift producing variation between repetitions.
The listener hears both layers simultaneously: the immediate strophe-internal melodic motion, and the longer-time-scale repetition of the strophe across the performance.

Strophic form stands in contrast to *through-composed* form (in which the song unfolds as a continuous melodic line without repetition of a definite strophe-unit), to *binary form* (in which the song has two contrasting sections, A and B, with the second following the first without repetition), to *ternary form* (in which the song has three sections, A-B-A, with the third returning to the material of the first), and to more elaborate forms like *rondo* (in which a recurring A section alternates with contrasting episodes).
The Lakotaean cedar-flute repertoire is predominantly strophic; through-composed form is rare or absent; binary, ternary, and rondo forms are not idiomatic.

The number of strophes performed in a given instance of a strophic-form song varies.
In solo [cedar-flute](../../../../b4/cedar-flute/cedar-flute.md) performance, the number is at the player's discretion — typical performances run two to six strophes — with the final strophe often distinguished by a slightly more emphatic [cadence](./cadence.md) or by an extended [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md) on the terminal [fundamental](./fundamental.md).
In vocal-ensemble [honor-song](../../../../b4/honor-song/honor-song.md) performance, more definite conventions apply: the four-times-through convention of many Northern Plains ensemble songs is widely observed, with the lead dancer or whistle-bearer able to extend the performance by signaling for additional strophes (see [grass-dance-whistle](../../../../b4/grass-dance-whistle/grass-dance-whistle.md) for the parallel mechanism in dance contexts).

The mechanism of strophe-to-strophe variation in cedar-flute strophic form is principally [ornament-substitution](../../../code/transformers/ornament-substitution.md): a melodic position that is plain in one strophe receives an ornament — a [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md), [trill](./trill.md), [finger-pop](../../../../b4/finger-pop/finger-pop.md), [cut](../../../../b4/cut/cut.md), or other [ornament](./ornament.md) — in the next strophe, or vice versa.
A given player's stylistic identity is in part the pattern of ornament-substitutions they characteristically apply across strophes; a song-tradition's stylistic identity is in part the set of ornament-substitution patterns that are conventional within the tradition.
A secondary mechanism is rhythmic shift: a melodic figure that is rhythmically square in one strophe may be slightly elongated, compressed, or syncopated in another, within the [rubato](./rubato.md) tolerance of the cedar-flute idiom.
Substantial melodic modification — adding or removing notes, changing scale-degrees — is rare across strophes of the same song; the strophe's melodic skeleton is preserved while the ornamental and rhythmic surface varies.

The [strophic-form](../../code/structures/strophic-form.md) structure formalizes this two-layer organization music-theoretically.
