# Finger — *Conception of Energy* (March 25, 1914)

The Finger interview of March 25, 1914 is one of the most precisely-recorded single conversations in the Walker corpus.
Walker — agency physician at Pine Ridge, in the middle of the tuberculosis epidemic — sat down with Finger (an Oglala wičháša wakȟáŋ) to ask, directly, what the holy men meant by *Škáŋ*.
Finger's answers form the philosophical core of the Lakota understanding of Škáŋ as the moving-principle and supreme functional power among the four superior Wakȟáŋ.

The interview is the source for the Walker-corpus material on Škáŋ; everything in [skan](teaching/explanations/skan.md) ultimately rests on this conversation.

## The interview as published

Walker published the interview in his 1917 *Sun Dance* paper under the section "Conception of Energy."
DeMallie reprinted it in the *Lakota Belief and Ritual* volume (1980).
The interview was conducted in a mix of Lakota and English; Walker translated and transcribed the Lakota portions.

## Reconstructed text of the key exchanges

The following reconstructs the published interview text with modern Lakota orthography.
Walker's questions are in plain text; Finger's answers in italics.
Square brackets indicate editorial reconstruction.

**Walker**: I have heard your holy men speak of Škáŋ.
What does the word mean?

*Finger*: **The shamans use the word Škáŋ; the common people say Takúškaŋškaŋ.
They mean the same. Škáŋ is the wakȟáŋ-language word.
Takúškaŋškaŋ is for everyday speech.**

**Walker**: I have been told that Škáŋ is the sky.

*Finger*: **Yes. Škáŋ is a Spirit and all that mankind can see of Him is the blue of the sky.
But He is everywhere.**

**Walker**: Is Škáŋ the same as Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka?

*Finger*: **Wí is Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka Kiŋ. Škáŋ is Naǧí Tȟáŋka, the Great Spirit.**

[Walker's note: Finger distinguished Wí and Škáŋ as two different aspects of the wakȟáŋ-totality.
Wí is the ceremonial chief; Škáŋ is the Great-Spirit-as-Naǧí, the giver of spirit.]

**Walker**: How does Škáŋ give spirit?

*Finger*: **Škáŋ takes from the stars a naǧí and gives it to each babe at the time of its birth.
That naǧí is the babe's spirit.
When the babe dies, the naǧí returns toward Škáŋ.**

**Walker**: To whom does Škáŋ give judgment?

*Finger*: **To all who must be judged. Škáŋ is the judge among the wakȟáŋ.**

[Walker's note: Finger ranked Škáŋ above the other three superior Wakȟáŋ in functional power.
The cosmogonic priority belongs to Íŋyaŋ; but the present-tense power belongs to Škáŋ.]

## What Finger established

Finger's answers establish several philosophically-load-bearing facts:

1. **Two registers, one being.** *Škáŋ* (shamans' wakȟáŋ-language) and *Takúškaŋškaŋ* (common speech) denote the same being.
  The shamans' term names the *principle*; the everyday term names the *visible-motion*.
  This is the source for the [skan](teaching/explanations/skan.md) entry's reading of Škáŋ as a *principle* rather than a kinematic motion.

2. **Škáŋ is the sky as visible-aspect, but everywhere as being.** The blue of the sky is what *mankind can see* of Škáŋ; Škáŋ Himself is not contained in the sky.
  He is everywhere.

3. **Škáŋ is Naǧí Tȟáŋka — the Great Spirit.** This is a *theological-structural* claim: Škáŋ is the supreme spirit-aspect of Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka, distinguished from Wí (the ceremonial-chief aspect).

4. **Škáŋ is the giver of naǧí.** Every living being's spirit is given by Škáŋ from the stars at the time of birth and returns toward Škáŋ at death.
  The animation of *every* spirited being is Škáŋ's gift.

5. **Škáŋ is the supreme judge.** The judicial role belongs to Škáŋ.
  He is the one who pronounces on undetermined cases.

## The interview's relevance to the relational framework

Each of Finger's points maps directly onto a structural fact in the relational framework:

- *Two registers, one being* — the framework's distinction between the philosophical articulation (the principle) and the algebraic articulation (the visible operation) is mirrored in Finger's shamans-vs-common-speech distinction.
  They are the same thing; the registers differ in what they expose.
- *Sky as visible-aspect* — Škáŋ's visible-aspect-yet-everywhere is the structural form of the engine-of-relation being everywhere active yet only partially visible as motion.
- *Naǧí Tȟáŋka, Great Spirit* — Škáŋ as supreme functional power is the framework's *engine* — the cannot-not-act primitive at the heart of the field.
- *Giver of naǧí* — Škáŋ as source-of-spirit-to-every-living-being is the framework's *Reflex* in its lived form: every spirited entity has its spirit because Škáŋ has acted to install it.
- *Supreme judge* — Škáŋ as the one who pronounces is the framework's ⊥ position in the bilattice: the under-determined judicial seat from which determination proceeds.

## Citation and preservation note

Finger's testimony is held in trust by the Lakota tradition.
Walker recorded with permission; the holy men who participated did so with the explicit aim of preserving the tradition's philosophical content for future Lakota and non-Lakota readers.
The use of this material in this subtheory is in keeping with that intent — to ground the philosophical articulation in the primary recordings.

The reconstructed quotations here are from the published Walker 1917 and Walker 1980 sources; for the most precise wording (and any disputed translation choices), consult the printed source.
For full archival fidelity, consult the Walker notebooks at the American Museum of Natural History.
