# Wičhóni

Wičhóni — *life*, *living* — names the perpetual act of dwelling-in-relation.
It is what every relative *does* rather than what every relative *is*: the ongoing maintaining-of-oneself in the relational substrate.
In the relational framework, wičhóni is *existing* — not being-as-a-state but the perpetual process compelling all the other primitives.

## Life as activity

Lakota grammar treats *being-alive* as an activity, not a state.
The verb *úŋ* — to dwell, to be-present, to remain-in-place — is what living beings *do*.
Wičhóni is the abstract-noun form built on this active stem (*wičhá-* personal/being prefix + *úŋ* dwelling), denoting *the activity of dwelling-as-a-being*.

This grammatical fact has metaphysical weight.
A being is not first *in existence* and then *additionally* in-relation; the being's *existing-at-all* is its dwelling-in-relation.
To exist is to be-doing the relational substrate work.
To stop the doing is to die.
There is no still life — no being that exists without relating.

This is the Lakota form of the relational framework's claim that *existing* is a perpetual act compelled by all the other primitives.
The framework's claim is structural — given the chain (relationlessness → reflex → differentiate → tension → relating → relative), existing is what the relative is *doing* continuously to sustain itself.
Wičhóni names this directly.

## Wičhóni vs. wicozani

Two closely-related forms appear in Lakota cosmological speech:

- **Wičhóni** — life, the activity of living, the perpetual dwelling-in-relation.
- **Wicozani** — well-being, wholeness, the substrate's quiescence condition.
  See [wicozani](wicozani.md).

The distinction:

- Wičhóni is *that one is alive at all* — the activity-of-existing.
- Wicozani is *the quality of that aliveness* — the relational-substrate condition that the activity is settling toward.

Every relative has wičhóni (else they would not be a relative).
Not every relative has wicozani at every moment; the substrate may be disturbed, may have persistent contradictions or unsettlements, may be far from quiescence.
Wicozani is the *good-condition* of wičhóni; wičhóni is the *fact* of living-at-all.

## Wičhóni at relational scales

Wičhóni runs at every scale of relational unit:

- A person has wičhóni so long as their dwelling-in-relation continues.
- A tiyospaye has wičhóni so long as the kin-conduct that constitutes it continues.
- An oyate has wičhóni so long as the polity-conduct that holds it together continues.
- The Oceti Sakowin has wičhóni so long as the confederate relations that hold it as one people continue.

The scale-property matters for the polity's continuance.
The Oceti Sakowin's wičhóni does not depend on any one tiyospaye or oyate being alive; it depends on the *confederate relations* being conducted.
So long as the council-fires are tended, the confederation has wičhóni — even if a particular oyate is dispersed, even if a particular tiyospaye has shrunk to a few members.
The activity that constitutes the polity is what wičhóni names at polity scale.

This is why language-revitalization, ceremony-reclamation, and kinship-rebuilding work are wičhóni-restoration at polity scale.
They are not *adding* aliveness to a polity that was dead; they are *resuming* the activity that constitutes the polity's continuing wičhóni.
The polity's wičhóni was not extinguished by the settler-state's century-and-a-half of attacks; it was *attacked* — the conduct was made difficult, dangerous, or illegal — but the polity continued and the conduct is now resuming.

## Wičhóni in everyday speech

The form runs through everyday Lakota. *Tákuye úŋ pi* — "my relatives, they are dwelling-here-living." *Wičhóni úŋ* — "they have life."
The grammatical pattern keeps the *activity* in view: living is what one *does*, not what one *is*.

In ceremony and prayer, the request for wičhóni for the people is a request for the polity's continuing-to-do the conduct that constitutes it. *Mitákuye iyúha wičhóni úŋ pi yelo* — "may all my relatives have life" / "may all my relatives continue to dwell-in-life."
The prayer asks for the activity, not for a state.

## What wičhóni is not

- *Not* mere biological aliveness in the bare-existence sense.
  Wičhóni is *dwelling-in-relation*; an organism cut off from all relations has its bare existence but not wičhóni.
- *Not* a possession.
  One does not *have* wičhóni as one has a body; one *does* wičhóni as the activity-of-existing.
- *Not* the same as wicozani.
  Wičhóni is the activity; wicozani is the quality of the activity at its quiescence.
