# Restorative system

The polity's response-to-harm subsystem.
The structural commitment: the polity has *no* closure rules that produce confinement-cells, sanction-cells-with-carry-over, or punitive-installation-cells.
The response-to-harm subsystem produces only *relation-repair* cells.
This subsystem is the specialization of [restorative-accountability](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/services/predicated-governance-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/restorative-accountability.md) to the Lakota polity-cell-system.

## Structural commitment

For every harm-event involving agents $a, b$ — assault, theft, killing, betrayal, public disrespect — the polity's response produces *some subset of* the following cells:

- $\langle a, \texttt{owes-repair-to}, b\rangle$ — the harming party owes relational-repair to the harmed party.
- $\langle \mathrm{tiyospaye}(a), \texttt{owes-repair-to}, \mathrm{tiyospaye}(b)\rangle$ — kin-collective owes-repair to kin-collective.
- $\langle \mathrm{relation}(a, b), \texttt{requires-ceremonial-repair}\rangle$ — the relation between the parties is marked for ceremonial work.
- $\langle a, \texttt{stands-as-restored-after-repair}\rangle$ — settles at $\mathbf{t}$ once repair is completed.

The polity does *not* produce:

- Confinement-cells over $a$.
- Sanction-cells over $a$ that carry over beyond the repair-scope.
- Property-extraction-cells against $a$ (the polity does not seize $a$'s possessions as fine).
- Standing-loss-cells of permanent character (some standing-cells temporarily drop during the repair-process; they re-rise when repair completes).

## The repair pathway

The standard repair-pathway for serious harm:

1. **Harm-cell-firing.** $\langle a, \texttt{harmed}, b\rangle$ settles at $\mathbf{t}$.
2. **Relation-requires-repair.** By (W7) of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$, $\langle \mathrm{relation}(a, b), \texttt{requires-repair}\rangle$ fires at $\mathbf{t}$.
3. **Tiyospaye involvement.** The tiyóšpaye of $a$ approaches the tiyóšpaye of $b$.
  The approach is itself an act of acknowledgment.
4. **Gift-restoration.** The tiyóšpaye of $a$ offers substantial gifts to the tiyóšpaye of $b$: horses, robes, food, sometimes a covering for the dead if the harm was killing.
  The gift-flow is substantively a wówačhantognaka-conduct cell-installation by $a$'s tiyóšpaye (cf. [gift-economy-redistribution](../transformers/gift-economy-redistribution.md)) and a recognition-of-harm cell.
5. **Ceremony.** If the harm was sufficiently grave (most centrally, killing), the [wiping-of-tears](../../../../../../../philosophy/theories/predicated-philosophy/theories/predicated-lakotaean-philosophy/morphisms/wiping-of-tears-as-polity-quiescence-restoration.md) ceremony or a parallel substrate-restoration ceremony is conducted.
  The wičháša wakȟáŋ presides; the relation's wakȟáŋ-marking is restored.
6. **Relation-restored cell.** $\langle \mathrm{relation}(a, b), \texttt{restored}\rangle$ settles at $\mathbf{t}$.
  The owes-repair cells drop to $\bot$ (the obligation has been discharged).
  The standing-as-restored cell for $a$ settles at $\mathbf{t}$.

The pathway is *not* triggered by ruling-of-court; it is triggered by the harm-event and runs through the kin-and-ceremony network.
The wakíčhuŋza and naca may *mediate* (assisting the tiyóšpaye in approaching each other, smoothing the gift-arrangement), but they do not *adjudicate* in the Western sense — there is no body that pronounces who-was-right.

## Why no punitive subsystem

The polity's *structural absence* of punitive infrastructure is not a deficit.
Substrate-algebraically:

- Punitive cells extract relation from the polity.
  A confinement-cell over $a$ severs $a$'s engagement-cells with the polity for the confinement-period; the relational-substrate is *reduced* by the punitive operation.
- Restorative cells *add* relation.
  The repair-pathway installs new wówačhantognaka-conduct cells, renews the relation between the parties' tiyóšpaye, and produces wakȟáŋ-bearing-ceremony cells.

The polity is constituted by kinship (cf. [populace-as-kinship](populace-as-kinship.md)).
Reducing the relational-substrate is reducing the polity itself; the polity's continuity-condition is the *non-reduction* of the relational substrate.
The punitive response would corrode the substrate the polity rests on.
The restorative response renews it.

## Banishment as the exceptional case

The polity does have a [banishment](../transformers/banishment-as-relation-severance.md) operation for cases where the harming party (a) cannot or will not complete repair, and (b) the harm is severe enough that continuing presence is intolerable.
Banishment is a *relation-severance* operation — explicitly removing the agent from the polity's kin-network.
It is structurally different from confinement: the banished agent is not held; they are removed.

Banishment is rare and is reserved for extreme cases.
The substrate-content of "rare": $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ does not contain a *closure rule* that fires banishment as an automatic consequence of any harm.
Banishment is always a *deliberate omníčiye decision* in response to the specific case.
The repair-pathway is always attempted first.

## The substrate-collision with U.S. criminal law

The reservation-period imposition of U.S. criminal law on the Lakota polity is the operational form of *forcing a punitive subsystem onto a substrate that has no rule-class for it*.
The substrate-content:

- U.S. criminal law fires under $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-crim}$ — a closure-rule subsystem that *does* install confinement-cells, sanction-cells, criminal-record-cells.
- The cells $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{US-crim}$ fires on overlap with cells $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$'s restorative subsystem also operates on: harm-cells, relation-cells, agent-cells.
- The cells are at $\top$ in the merged multi-observer settling: the U.S. asserts confinement; the Lakota polity asserts ongoing-relation-and-repair-pathway; both at the same cells.

The Lakota polity's continuing restorative practice, in cases where the federal-tribal-court overlay does not preempt, is the substrate-record of the polity's continuing capacity to fire $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ on harm-cells.
The peacemaking-court reforms on Pine Ridge, Rosebud, and other reservations (mid-twentieth-century onwards) are partial restorations of the restorative subsystem within the available legal-overlay.
