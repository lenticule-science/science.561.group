# Contrary-conduct

Contrary-conduct is the set of acts a [heyokha-bearer](../heyokha-bearer/heyokha-bearer.md) is obligated to perform as the lived discharge of the [wakinyan-vision](../wakinyan-vision/wakinyan-vision.md).
The acts are *contrary* in a specific sense: they invert ordinary conduct in ways that enact the cosmogonic contradiction in present time.

## The recorded acts

Walker records the canonical examples:

- Riding the horse backward — direction inverted while travel proceeds.
- Saying yes when meaning no, and vice versa — verbal sign inverted.
- Plunging the hands into boiling water (without burning) — sensation-expectation inverted.
- Dressing for cold when it is hot, and the reverse — temperature-response inverted.
- Pulling the bowstring with the *front* of the hand — bodily mechanic inverted.

The specifics vary by tradition, by dreamer, and by occasion.
The pattern is structural: take an ordinary act and invert it along one of its salient axes while keeping the act's social function operative.

## The acts are not whim

The contraries are obligatory.
The heyokha-bearer enacts them as the *social discharge* of the Wakíŋyaŋ-vision's wakhan-power.
Failure to enact them is dangerous: the wakhan-power, undischarged, builds up against the bearer and the people.
The acts are not the heyokha-bearer's personal style or eccentricity; they are required substrate-operations.

This is why contrary-conduct is read as a *morphism* in the categorical sense: it takes the wakhan-installation (the dream's residue on the bearer's substrate) and discharges it through socially-witnessed acts.
The morphism's domain is the undischarged wakhan-load; its codomain is the post-discharge state (load reduced, bearer-substrate stabilized).
The acts must be performed for the discharge to occur; private performance does not discharge — the witnessing is part of the operation.

## The sacred laughter

Contrary-conduct draws laughter — at the boiling-water scenes, at the inverted-conduct, at the comic-form the heyokha's discharge takes.
The laughter is *medicine*, per Joseph Epes Brown's *The Sacred Pipe* (1953).
The people who laugh are participating in the discharge; their laughter is the witness-act that completes the operation.
A heyokha-bearer performing contrary conduct to no audience would not discharge; the witnessing-laughter is part of the morphism's codomain.

This is the substrate-fact: contrary-conduct is not a one-person operation but a relational-substrate operation that requires the polity's participation through laughter-as-witness.
The heyokha-bearer cannot do the work alone; the people are necessary co-operators.
