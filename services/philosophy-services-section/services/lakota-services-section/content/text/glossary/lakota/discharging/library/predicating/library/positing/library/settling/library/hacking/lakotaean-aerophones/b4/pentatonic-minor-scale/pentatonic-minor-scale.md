# Pentatonic Minor Scale

The pentatonic minor scale is a five-note scale (Greek *penta* = five, *tonos* = tone) with interval pattern, in ascending order, of minor-third, major-second, major-second, minor-third, major-second — that is, 3 semitones, 2 semitones, 2 semitones, 3 semitones, 2 semitones — completing the octave at the sixth pitch.
Numerically, with the tonic as 0 and the octave as 12 semitones, the scale's pitch-class set is {0, 3, 5, 7, 10, 12}, with scale degrees commonly labelled 1, b3, 4, 5, b7, 8.
In Lakotaean musical terms — the terms relevant to the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) and the [wiilowan](./wiilowan.md) vocal song from which the flute repertoire derives — the pentatonic minor is the principal scale of the surviving solo and ensemble repertoire.

The scale is one of a small family of "anhemitonic" (no semitones) pentatonic scales found across many of the world's musical traditions — Chinese music's *jiào* and *yǔ* modes, Scottish and Irish folk music's pentatonic melodies, sub-Saharan African vocal traditions, North American Indigenous vocal traditions of many regions, the diatonic-derived pentatonic of European folk song, and the bluegrass-and-blues pentatonic that became foundational to American popular music.
The wide distribution of the anhemitonic pentatonic is partly a consequence of its acoustic properties — the scale's intervals (minor third, major second) are all reasonably stable acoustic ratios and the scale contains no semitones (which are unstable in unaccompanied solo vocal performance without an external pitch reference).
It is therefore a *natural* scale in the sense that it tends to emerge in oral musical traditions without external imposition.
Its appearance in the Lakotaean tradition is not a borrowing or convergence — it is the scale that an unaccompanied vocal tradition with the [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md)'s acoustic properties would tend to settle on, and it is the scale that the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md)'s conventional [finger-hole](./finger-hole.md) placement produces directly.

The scale admits *five* [modal rotations](./mode.md) — the same five pitch-classes can be heard as constituting five distinct scales, depending on which pitch-class is treated as the tonal center.
The five modes are: Mode I (tonic 1, intervals 3,2,2,3,2 — the *minor pentatonic* properly so-called), Mode II (tonic 2 of Mode I, intervals 2,2,3,2,3 — the *major pentatonic*), Mode III (intervals 2,3,2,3,2 — sometimes called the "blues" or "Egyptian" pentatonic, depending on tradition), Mode IV (intervals 3,2,3,2,2), and Mode V (intervals 2,3,2,2,3).
Of these five modes, the Lakotaean cedar-flute repertoire principally uses Mode I — the minor pentatonic in the strict sense, with the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md)'s [fundamental](./fundamental.md) as the tonal center.
Other modes are theoretically available on the instrument (the same pitches sound the same regardless of which is treated as tonal center) but are not idiomatic in the documented repertoire.
See the claim file [pentatonic-minor-mode-i-is-the-functional-tonic.md](../learning/claims/pentatonic-minor-mode-i-is-the-functional-tonic.md).

The intonation of the Lakotaean pentatonic minor on the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) is not 12-tone equal temperament.
The scale-degree 5 sits near the just perfect fifth (pure 3:2 ratio, ~702 cents from the fundamental, ~2 cents sharp of 12-TET); the scale-degree 8 (octave) is the just octave (2:1 ratio, exactly 1200 cents).
The scale-degrees b3 and b7, however, deviate noticeably from 12-TET — the b3 typically sits some 20–40 cents *flat* of 12-TET (closer to the just minor third's 316 cents than to 12-TET's 300 cents, and often flatter still, near the 6:5 minor third or even lower toward the 7:6 septimal minor third), and the b7 similarly sits flat of 12-TET (closer to the just or septimal minor seventh ratios).
These intonational characteristics are not "out of tune" — they are the intonation of the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) as built, and they are part of the scale's musical character.
See [cedar-flute-cents-table](../../code/structures/cedar-flute-cents-table.md) for the structural representation of these deviations.

The scale's musical character is shaped by its specific interval-content: the minor third between 1 and b3 (a comparatively unstable interval that wants resolution, often by descent to 1), the perfect fourth between 1 and 4 (a stable interval frequently used as a melodic pivot), the perfect fifth between 1 and 5 (the strongest stable interval, often the highest point of melodic ascent), and the minor seventh between 1 and b7 (an unstable interval often appearing as the phrase's high point, descending through 5 and 4 to b3 and 1).
The [cadence](./cadence.md) vocabulary of the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) repertoire makes use principally of the descending b3 → 1 and the descending 4 → 1 motions, with the 5 → 1 perfect-fifth descent also appearing as a strong cadential gesture.
