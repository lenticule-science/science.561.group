# Oceti Sakowin

Očhéthi Šakówiŋ — the Seven Council Fires.
The confederacy of seven oyate (peoples) that together form what is most commonly called the Sioux Nation in English, though that exonym should be set aside in favor of the people's own name.
The Oceti Sakowin is the polity-of-polities — the encompassing confederate form within which the seven oyate maintain their distinct political life while standing together in shared ceremony, kinship, and political business.

## The seven fires

The seven are (with common contemporary spellings):

- **Mdewakanton** (Bdewákhaŋthuŋwaŋ) — "Dwellers by Mystic Lake"
- **Wahpekute** (Waȟpékhute) — "Shooters Among the Leaves"
- **Wahpeton** (Waȟpéthuŋwaŋ) — "Dwellers Among the Leaves"
- **Sisseton** (Sisíthuŋwaŋ) — "People of the Marsh"

These four together are commonly called the **Dakota** (or *Isányathi*, "Knife-people"; the eastern Santee Sioux).

- **Yankton** (Iháŋkthuŋwaŋ) — "End-Village People"
- **Yanktonai** (Iháŋkthuŋwaŋna) — "Little End-Village People"

These two together form the **Nakota** (also called Yankton-Yanktonai).

- **Teton** (Thítȟuŋwaŋ) — "Dwellers on the Prairie"

The Teton are the seventh and (chronologically) youngest fire, and they themselves further differentiated into the seven Lakota oyate (Oglala, Sicangu / Brulé, Mniconjou, Hunkpapa, Sihasapa, Itazipco / Sans Arc, Oohenumpa).

## The confederacy as polity-form

The Oceti Sakowin is not a state.
It is a confederacy of oyate that meet — historically at gatherings, ceremonies, and council fires — to conduct shared political business.
Each oyate retains its own internal governance ([naca](naca.md), [omniciye](omniciye.md), [akicita](akicita.md), and so on); the confederacy operates by consensus across oyate the way each oyate's omniciye operates by consensus across [tiyospaye](tiyospaye.md).
The Oceti Sakowin's political life is the political life of a confederacy of self-governing peoples whose unity is constituted by shared origin, shared kinship, shared ceremony, and shared cause — not by a central state apparatus.

## Continuity into the present

The Oceti Sakowin is not historical.
It is a living confederacy.
Nick Estes ([estes2019](references/estes2019.md)) — Lower Brule Sioux, member of the Oceti Sakowin Writers Society — argues that Standing Rock and the broader contemporary movements of Indigenous resistance are continuous expressions of the same Oceti Sakowin political life that fought the Indian Wars, that refused the Black Hills compensation award, that has refused absorption into the United States.
The confederacy operates today on its own terms even where the settler state's recognition is partial, hostile, or absent.

## Note on naming

The standard English-language exonym "Sioux" derives, via French traders, from an Ojibwe term — *Nadowessi* — generally understood to carry a pejorative sense ("little snakes" or "lesser enemies").
The contemporary practice — followed in this subtheory — is to use the people's own name.
Oceti Sakowin for the confederacy; Lakota, Dakota, Nakota for the linguistic-cultural divisions; and oyate names (Oglala, Sicangu, etc.) for the specific peoples.
