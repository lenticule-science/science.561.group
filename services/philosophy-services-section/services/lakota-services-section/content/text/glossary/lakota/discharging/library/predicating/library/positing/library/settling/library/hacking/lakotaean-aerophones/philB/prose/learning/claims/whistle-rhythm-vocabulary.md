# Claim. Whistle calls compose from a small grammar of rhythmic figures — single tone, paired tones, triplet, sustained-with-pulse — that combine into the rhythmic structure of whistle tunes.

**Claim.** The [eagle-wing-bone-whistle](teaching/explanations/eagle-wing-bone-whistle.md) and related single-pitch Lakotaean aerophones organize their rhythmic content from a small grammar of *rhythmic figures*: (a) the *single tone* (one isolated whistle event), (b) the *paired tones* (two whistle events in close succession, typically with a definite short interval between them), (c) the *triplet* (three whistle events in close succession at approximately even spacing), and (d) the *sustained-with-pulse* (a single longer tone articulated by an interior sequence of [breath-pulses](teaching/explanations/breath-pulse.md)).
These four figures, combined with the four-archetype [whistle-envelope-grammar](./whistle-envelope-grammar.md), constitute the music-theoretic vocabulary from which whistle tunes are composed.

**Argument.**

*Rhythm is a music-theoretic dimension distinct from envelope.* Where the [whistle-envelope-grammar](./whistle-envelope-grammar.md) addresses the *shape* of an individual whistle event (its attack, sustain, decay), the rhythmic vocabulary addresses the *temporal arrangement* of whistle events relative to each other and to the surrounding musical context.
The two dimensions are orthogonal — a sharp-attack envelope can be deployed as a single tone, as part of a pair, as part of a triplet, or as the head of a sustained-with-pulse — and the combination of envelope choice and rhythmic figure choice gives the whistle's compositional content.

*The four rhythmic figures are identifiable and named.* The four rhythmic figures correspond to identifiable structural-rhythmic units in whistle performance, recognizable to knowledgeable listeners and reproducible by knowledgeable performers.

The *single tone* (a) is the simplest figure — one whistle event, isolated in time from any immediately surrounding whistle events.
Its musical function is principally as a *marker* — drawing attention to a specific structural moment, accenting a beat, marking a [phrase](teaching/explanations/phrase.md)-end.
The single tone may have any of the four envelope archetypes; combined with the sharp-attack-short-sustain envelope, it produces the alert-call; combined with the sharp-attack-long-sustain-with-warble envelope, it produces the signal; and so on.

The *paired tones* (b) are two whistle events in close succession — typically with an interval of roughly a quarter-second to half-second between them.
The paired-tones figure is rhythmically distinct from the single tone — it announces a *grouping* rather than a *point* — and is used for moments requiring a slightly more emphatic articulation than a single tone provides.
Two paired sharp-attack-short-sustain envelopes constitute a doubled alert; two paired sharp-attack-long-sustain-with-warble envelopes are uncommon (the long sustains would overlap and produce confusion).

The *triplet* (c) is three whistle events in close succession at approximately even spacing — the rhythmic equivalent of a triplet figure in European-classical notation, though without the implication of a specific note-value or meter (the whistle is not embedded in a metric grid in the European sense, even when it is embedded in [drum](teaching/explanations/drum.md)-anchored ensemble timing).
The triplet appears at moments requiring a more elaborate articulation than the paired-tones — typically as a structural climax or as the conclusion of a multi-strophe whistle pattern.

The *sustained-with-pulse* (d) is a single longer-duration tone (typically using the long-sustain envelope archetypes) articulated internally by a sequence of [breath-pulses](teaching/explanations/breath-pulse.md).
The pulse sequence may be even (regular spacing, producing a metric-like internal rhythm) or patterned (irregular spacing, producing a specific named rhythmic figure within the sustain).
The sustained-with-pulse figure is the principal rhythmic element when the whistle is integrated into the [drum](teaching/explanations/drum.md)'s metric pulse — the breath-pulses can be aligned to drum-beats, producing a [heterophonic](teaching/explanations/heterophony.md) relation between whistle and drum within a single sustained whistle tone.

*Combinations and sequences.* A whistle tune is typically a sequence of these figures, deployed across the structural moments of the song.
A typical sequence in a [honor-song](teaching/explanations/honor-song.md) might be: a rolled-attack-long-sustain on the song's opening; a sharp-attack-short-sustain single tone on the first phrase-end; a paired-tones figure on a strophe-internal honor-beat; a sharp-attack-long-sustain-with-warble on the strophe-final cadence.
The specific sequence is part of the song's identity — different songs admit different whistle-tune compositions, and the whistle-tune's structural placement is part of the song's overall form.

*Coordination with drum and voice.* In [heterophony](teaching/explanations/heterophony.md) with [drum](teaching/explanations/drum.md) and [voice](teaching/explanations/voice.md), the whistle's rhythmic figures align with the metric structure of the drum and the structural moments of the vocal melody.
The whistle does not provide the meter (that is the drum's role); the whistle articulates *against* the meter, at specific structural moments.
The rhythmic vocabulary is therefore subordinated to the song's structural organization rather than being freely independent.
This is what distinguishes the whistle's rhythmic role from a percussion role (which would establish the meter rather than articulating against it).

*The vocabulary is small and combinatorial.* The four-figure rhythmic vocabulary combined with the four-archetype [whistle-envelope-grammar](./whistle-envelope-grammar.md) gives 16 basic articulation types — small enough to be learned as a vocabulary, large enough to admit musically meaningful variation across songs and contexts.
This is the compositional vocabulary the whistle-bearer draws on in performance.

- [whistle-rhythm-figure-set](../code/structures/whistle-rhythm-figure-set.md) — the named set of rhythmic figures with their specific temporal organization.

**Transformers produced.**

None directly — this claim establishes the content of the rhythmic vocabulary rather than an operation on it.
