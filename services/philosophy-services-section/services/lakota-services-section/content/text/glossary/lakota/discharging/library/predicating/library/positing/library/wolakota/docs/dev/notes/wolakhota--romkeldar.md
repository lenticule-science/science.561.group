# wolakhota

## What it is

Wolakhota — living the Lakota way — is the substrate's closure-rule subsystem that produces conduct-cell settlings consistent with the four virtues (wówačhantognaka generosity, wóksape wisdom, wóohitika bravery, wówačhintȟaŋka fortitude), with mitakuye-oyasin's relational obligations, and with proper participation in the seven rites.

Formally, S_wolak is a subset of all available closure rules.
An agent is acting wolakhota when their conduct cells fire under rules in S_wolak.
Conduct produced under closure rules outside this subsystem may be effective in some narrow sense but does not contribute to wicozani.

## How it shows up

Conduct cells in the substrate carry the result of S_wolak firing on them.
The four virtues evaluate conduct cells — each conduct cell admits evaluation under each virtue, producing a bilattice value.
A conduct cell at t under wówačhantognaka means the conduct exemplifies generosity; at f means it fails it; at ⊥ means undetermined; at ⊤ means contradicted.

Cumulative virtue-evaluation across an agent's conduct history settles their standing as wolakhota.

## Relationship to wicozani

Wicozani is the substrate quiescence condition.
One of its four requirements is that conduct-cell settlements are produced under S_wolak.
Wicozani without wolakhota-conduct is not possible — conduct produced under different rules doesn't contribute to wicozani regardless of other factors.

## Heyoka exemption

The heyoka-vocation has a closure-rule exemption from the standard S_wolak conduct evaluation.
Heyoka conduct is wakhan-marked and fires under a separate rule-class, not the standard virtue-loss rules.
This is structural: a heyoka publicly failing a virtue does not lose virtue-standing because their conduct is doing a different substrate-job (bearing productive contradiction).
