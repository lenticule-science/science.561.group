# Hunka as kin-cell installation

The [huŋkápi](../../../../indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/seven-rites.md) ceremony — the making-of-relatives — is realized in the substrate as a *kin-cell installation morphism*: the rite, properly performed, installs new kin-cells across the populace and triggers the closure rules that flow obligation across the new kin-relations.

## The morphism

For two entities $p, q \in E_{\mathrm{lak}}$ (typically humans, but the morphism's structure does not restrict to humans) and a proper-conditions argument including a wičháša wakȟáŋ presence and a ceremonial setting, huŋkápi produces:

$$\mathrm{Huŋkápi}: (p, q,\, \mathrm{ProperConditions}) \;\mapsto\; \text{State update}$$

The state update consists of:

1. **New kin-cells at $\mathbf{t}$.** A cell $\langle p, \texttt{kin-of}, q\rangle$ and the symmetric cell $\langle q, \texttt{kin-of}, p\rangle$ receive $\mathbf{t}$ witness-increments.
  If the rite makes a specific kind of kin (brothers, parent-and-child by adoption, etc.), the appropriate specialization of *kin-of* receives the witness.

2. **Closure-rule firing over derived kin-cells.** The substrate's kinship rules — kin-of-kin is kin, kin-relations transit through descent and marriage — fire on the newly installed cells, producing the derived kin-cells the rite implies.
  The new kin-relations of $p$ and $q$ extend across both populations' tiyospaye and oyate.

3. **Obligation cells settled.** Kin-relations carry obligations under the [wolakȟota closure discipline](../objects/wolakota-as-closure-discipline.md): generosity to relatives, attention to relatives, support of relatives, restraint with relatives.
  The closure rules over kin-obligations fire on the new cells, producing $\mathbf{t}$ settlings of $\langle p, \texttt{obligated-to}, q\rangle$ and the reciprocal under the four virtues.

4. **Wakhan marking.** Because huŋkápi is one of the seven rites, the cells the morphism produces bear the wakȟáŋ quality (see [wakhan-as-cell-quality](../objects/wakhan-as-cell-quality.md)).
  The kin-cells installed by huŋkápi are not ordinary kin-cells — they carry the sacred quality, and the closure rules that fire on them are wakȟáŋ-bearing.

## Significance for the polity

The morphism is structurally how the polity extends.
Kin-relations by descent and marriage extend by ordinary substrate operations; kin-relations by ceremony extend by huŋkápi.
The polity that has access to the morphism is structurally able to admit new persons (and new groups) into its full kin-relational substrate without weakening the closure-rule discipline that defines the polity's lifeway.

Historically, the morphism was performed:

- To bind two boys (or, less often, two girls) into the relation of brothers/sisters.
  The closure rules of brotherhood fired immediately, with the lifelong obligations entailed.
- To formalize a leader's relation to those for whom they would speak.
  The naca-relation to the people was substrate-installed by ceremony.
- To create cross-tiyospaye and cross-oyate kinship in support of confederate cause.
  The Oceti Sakowin's confederacy is itself maintained by accumulated huŋkápi-installed cross-people kin-relations.
- To incorporate adopted persons fully into the people's life — including, on rare occasion, non-Lakota persons.
  Joseph Epes Brown's adoption by Black Elk and the Sioux name *Chanumpa Yuha Mani* is a recorded instance ([brown1953](../philB/references/brown1953.md)).

## Why ceremony is irreplaceable

A non-ceremonial declaration "I now consider you my kin" does not fire the morphism.
The substrate distinguishes the ceremony from its form: only the ceremony — performed by the proper wičháša wakȟáŋ, with the proper preparation, in proper place and time — fires the wakȟáŋ-bearing closure rule that installs the cells.
A declaration without the ceremony installs a cell that is not wakȟáŋ-marked and does not carry the full force of kin-obligation under the wolakȟota discipline.

This is the substrate-algebraic content of "ceremony is constitutive, not decorative."
The morphism *is* the ceremony; the form without the ceremony is a different morphism (which may or may not install cells, but does not install the kind of cells the polity recognizes as kin).
