# Walker 1917 — *The Sun Dance and Other Ceremonies of the Oglala Division of the Teton Dakota*

Walker's first major publication of his Pine Ridge fieldwork, issued during his lifetime.
The *Sun Dance* paper (1917) is the source for several core Lakota theological texts that DeMallie and Jahner later expanded in their 1980s editions of Walker's notebooks.
It contains material that Walker had earlier shared with Franz Boas at the American Museum of Natural History.

## Position in the Walker corpus

Walker (1849–1926) was agency physician at Pine Ridge from 1896 to 1914 during the tuberculosis epidemic among the Oglala Lakota.
Believing that understanding Lakota cosmology was inseparable from treating Lakota patients, Walker spent eighteen years collecting cosmological, ceremonial, and ritual material from Lakota holy men.
His principal informants included George Sword (Long Knife), Finger, Thomas Tyon, Little Wound, Bad Wound, No Flesh, Short Bull, American Horse, and others.
He learned Lakota and conducted interviews in Lakota and English.

The 1917 paper is what Walker published himself, in his lifetime.
After his death, his notebooks were preserved and eventually edited and published by Raymond DeMallie and Elaine Jahner as three further volumes — see [walker1980](walker1980.md), [walker1982](walker1982.md), [walker1983](walker1983.md).
The four volumes together are the deepest pre-boarding-school documentation of Oglala Lakota theology and ritual.

## Key sections relevant to this subtheory

- **§I**: "Tribal Organization."
  Background on Oglala social structure.
- **§II**: "The Sun Dance" (*wiwáŋyaŋg wačhípi*).
  Full ritual description including preparation, piercing, dance, and theological interpretation.
  The piercing-while-honoring as the cosmogonic paradox enacted in present-tense ceremony.
- **§III**: "The Conception of Wakan."
  The systematic theology of Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka as Tóbtób Kiŋ (the four-times-four).
  Sword's recording of the four superior Wakȟáŋ (Wí, Škáŋ, Makȟá, Íŋyaŋ) and the lesser twelve.
  The cosmogony of Íŋyaŋ's self-emptying.
  The pre-creation condition of Íŋyaŋ-and-Haŋ.
- **§"Conception of Energy"**: The famous Finger interview of March 25, 1914.
  Finger's recording of Škáŋ vs. Takúškaŋškaŋ (shamans' speech vs. common speech), Škáŋ as Naǧí Tȟáŋka (the Great Spirit), Škáŋ as giver-of-*naǧí* to every living being.
  See [finger-conception-of-energy](finger-conception-of-energy.md).
- **§"Visions and the Heyoka"**: Recording of Wakíŋyaŋ-dream visions and the heyókha vocation.
  The list of visions that produce heyókha (snow bird, swallow, horse, dog, night hawk, frog, dragonfly).

## On orthography

Walker's transcriptions use a missionary-era orthography without modern diacritics (Skan, Wakan Tanka, Inyan, Tobtob).
The modern Lakota standard (Boas-Deloria / Albert White Hat Sr. / Sinte Gleska / NLDC) writes: Škáŋ, Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka, Íŋyaŋ, Tóbtób.
The 1917 paper is therefore primary-source-authoritative for the *content* but uses superseded orthography.
Quotes in this subtheory's concept files modernize the orthography where the underlying word is unambiguous, with note where ambiguous.
