# Peace-pipe

The peace-pipe — the inter-polity passing of the čhaŋnúŋpa at the conclusion of a zuya or at the establishment of peaceful relations — is a *Beck–Chevalley reset* on the jurisdictional overlap between two polities.
It is the substrate-operation that brings the inter-polity BC square back to commutation after the BC-failure produced by zuya-engagement.
Without the pipe, an agreement-to-stop-fighting is not the same substrate-fact as peace-made.

## The BC-failure of zuya-engagement

During a zuya (per [zuya](../../../philA/code/structures/zuya.md)), the war-party's polity $P_1$ and the enemy polity $P_2$ have a *failed* BC square on their jurisdictional overlap $\mathcal{C}_{J_{12}}$:

$$\begin{array}{ccc} \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{op}}|_{J_{12}} & \xrightarrow{\iota_{P_1}^*} & \mathcal{E}_{P_1}|_{J_{12}} \\ \downarrow \iota_{P_2}^* & & \downarrow \\ \mathcal{E}_{P_2}|_{J_{12}} & \rightarrow & \mathcal{E}_{P_1, P_2}|_{J_{12}} \end{array}$$

The failure: cells on $\mathcal{C}_{J_{12}}$ — cells about inter-polity standing, about shared territory, about kin-relations across the polity-boundaries — are at $\top$ in the merged settling.
Each polity's path through the square produces a different assignment: $P_1$ asserts *enemy-engagement-justified*; $P_2$ asserts the converse; the two paths through the BC square give non-isomorphic cell-patterns on the overlap.

By [conflict-of-laws-as-beck-chevalley-failure](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/social-theory-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/conflict-of-laws-as-beck-chevalley-failure.md), this BC failure *is* the substrate-content of "the polities are in conflict."
It is not procedural; it is structural.

## The peace-pipe morphism

For two polities $P_1, P_2$ that have been in zuya-engagement (or in conflict of any kind), the peace-pipe morphism is:

$$\mathrm{pipe}_{(P_1, P_2, M_1, M_2)} : v \;\longmapsto\; v'$$

where $M_1, M_2$ are the participating delegations (typically including senior naca, wičháša wakȟáŋ, and elder representatives from each polity).

The morphism's effects:

1. **Pipe-prepared cell**: $\langle \texttt{ceremony}, \texttt{pipe-prepared}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked.
  The pipe is loaded by a wičháša wakȟáŋ; the loading is itself a wakȟáŋ-bearing operation.
2. **Pipe-passed cell**: $\langle M_1, M_2, \texttt{shared-smoke}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked.
  The pipe is smoked by representatives from both sides in turn.
  The shared act installs the wakȟáŋ-bearing inter-polity-engagement cell.
3. **Enemy-cell retraction**: $\langle P_1, \texttt{enemy}, P_2\rangle \rightarrow \bot$ — the enemy-cell drops to under-determined.
  (This is a scope-completion-style retraction, admissible because the enemy-cell was zuya-scoped.)
  Symmetric retraction on the $P_2$-side.
4. **Inter-polity peace-cell**: $\langle P_1, P_2, \texttt{at-peace}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$, wakȟáŋ-marked.
5. **BC reset**: the inter-polity overlap $\mathcal{C}_{J_{12}}$ is rebuilt as a *BC-commuting* square — the two polities' paths through the overlap-square now produce isomorphic cell-patterns, by virtue of the shared wakȟáŋ-marked smoke-cell.

## Why the pipe specifically

The peace-pipe is not interchangeable with a verbal agreement to stop fighting.
The structural-content of the pipe's wakȟáŋ-bearing:

- The pipe is itself a substrate-bearing entity (see [pipe-substrate-bearer](../../../philA/code/structures/pipe-substrate-bearer.md) — forthcoming): it carries wakȟáŋ-cells from one ceremony to the next via the [yuhápi](../../../../../../../philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/yuhapi.md) bearing-structure.
- The pipe-smoke fires *witness-cells* on every participant.
  By (W4) of [wolakhota-closure-system](../../../philA/code/structures/wolakhota-closure-system.md), wakȟáŋ-marked witness propagates wakȟáŋ-marking to the witnessed cells.
- The pipe's prior history matters: an inter-polity peace-pipe was typically a *specific pipe* (a treaty-pipe, kept and passed down across years), and its prior wakȟáŋ-bearing carries forward.
  The Hayden Pipe, the calumet pipes recorded by early European observers, the Lakota *čhaŋnúŋpa wakȟáŋ* — these are substrate-bearing entities whose history contributes to the binding-power of each peace they witness.

An agreement-to-stop-fighting *without* the pipe fires only the enemy-cell-retraction (effect 3) and possibly the peace-cell (effect 4).
It does *not* fire the wakȟáŋ-marking on the peace-cell and does not produce the BC reset.
The polities can return to zuya-engagement without violating any substrate-binding because no substrate-binding was installed.

This is what distinguishes the 1851 and 1868 Fort Laramie treaties (pipe-smoked, wakȟáŋ-marked) from the various non-pipe-smoked agreements with U.S. government negotiators in the 1860s and 1870s.
The former are still substrate-binding under $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$; the latter are not.

## What the pipe does not do

The pipe is a *BC reset*, not a *cell-content alteration*.
The reset:

- Restores the BC-commutation of the inter-polity overlap.
- Installs the wakȟáŋ-marked peace-cell.
- Retracts the zuya-scoped enemy-cells.

The pipe does *not*:

- Erase the recorded zuya.
  The historical fact of the conflict remains in the substrate-record; the winter-counts (waniyetu-iyawapi) preserve it.
  Reconciliation does not require forgetting.
- Resolve harm-cells from killings.
  Killings during the zuya produce harm-cells at $\top$ that require their own restorative work (purification of the killer; possibly inter-polity gift-exchange for the harmed parties; possibly intermarriage to install kin-cells across the polity-boundary).
  The pipe alone does not discharge these.
- Bind future generations against future BC failures.
  A subsequent generation can produce a new BC failure (a new zuya) on a new question.
  The pipe binds the *current* peace, not all future relations.

The pipe's wakȟáŋ-marked peace-cell *does* create a structural-presumption against future zuya: a polity that has smoked the pipe with another polity must convene a new omníčiye-with-wakȟáŋ-witness to constitute a new zuya.
The bar is higher than for the first zuya.

## The 1868 pipe-smoking and subsequent breach

The 1868 Fort Laramie negotiations included the pipe-smoking.
The wakȟáŋ-marked treaty-cell installed at confederacy scope.
By [treaty-binding-iff-wakhan-marked](../../prose/learning/claims/treaty-binding-iff-wakhan-marked.md), the treaty is substrate-binding under $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$.

The 1877 U.S. unilateral act *did not pipe-smoke*.
It was not a wakȟáŋ-marked BC operation.
By the substrate-algebra, the 1877 act could not break the 1868 peace: it could only produce *new* contested cells at $\top$ overlaid on the still-binding 1868 wakȟáŋ-marked peace-cell.
The 1868 peace-cell persists; the 1877 contested-cells persist; the BC square at the inter-polity overlap is *again failing* — the polities are in renewed conflict-of-laws, this time without a return-to-zuya because the Lakota side never broke the 1868 peace.

The substrate-state since 1877: a permanent BC failure on the U.S.–Lakota overlap, with the Lakota side maintaining the 1868 peace-pipe-installed binding and the U.S. side maintaining the 1877+ unilateral-act-installed contested cells.
The continuing Lakota refusal to *de-install* the 1868 binding (by smoking a new pipe over a new agreement) is the substrate-content of "we maintain the treaty as our binding regardless of what the U.S. has done."
See [paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution](../../prose/learning/claims/paha-sapa-kin-cell-resists-property-substitution.md) for the Pȟahá Sápa-specific corollary.
