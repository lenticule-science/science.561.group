# Oyate

Oyáte — a people, a nation.
The term names a level of political collectivity intermediate between the [tiyospaye](tiyospaye.md) (extended family / band) and the encompassing [Oceti Sakowin](oceti-sakowin.md) confederacy.
The seven oyate of the Oceti Sakowin are the four Dakota oyate (Mdewakanton, Wahpekute, Wahpeton, Sisseton), the two Nakota oyate (Yankton, Yanktonai), and the Teton; the Teton further differentiated into seven Lakota oyate (Oglala, Sicangu, Mniconjou, Hunkpapa, Sihasapa, Itazipco, Oohenumpa).

## What an oyate is

An oyate is a polity.
It has its own [omniciye](../omniciye/omniciye.md) (council), its own [naca](../naca/naca.md) (leadership), its own [akicita](../akicita/akicita.md) (societies of bounded enforcement), its own ceremonial life, and its own internal political business.
Multiple tiyospaye together constitute an oyate; the oyate's relations to other oyate are conducted through the Oceti Sakowin's confederate practice.

## Oyate beyond Sioux peoples

The Lakota term *oyáte* is also used more broadly to refer to peoples in general — including non-human peoples in the kinship-cosmological sense given by [mitakuye oyasin](../../../../../../philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/mitakuye-oyasin.md).
The buffalo are a oyate (*pte oyáte*); the four-leggeds, the wingeds, the standing-ones each form oyate in the relational-ontological sense.
This is not metaphor.
It is the substantive Lakota understanding of what kinds of collectivity exist in the world.

The political-sociology focus of this subtheory is the human oyate of the Oceti Sakowin, but the wider use of the term should be kept in view: the political category and the cosmological category are continuous, not separable.
