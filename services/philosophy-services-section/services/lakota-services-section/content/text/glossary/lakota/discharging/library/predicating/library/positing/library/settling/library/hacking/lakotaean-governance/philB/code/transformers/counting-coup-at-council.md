# Counting coup at council

The honor-cells installed by [count-coup](count-coup.md) during a zuya are *not static records*.
They are sustained by *continuing recitation* — the warrior recounting their coups at appropriate occasions (council, gatherings, public ceremonies).
The recounting morphism fires witness-cells forward in time, renewing the substrate-record.
Without continuing recitation, the honor-cell's $\leq_k$-value decays in the polity's substrate-recognition.

## The morphism

For a warrior $w$ with prior coup-cells $\{c_i\}$ and a recounting occasion $K$ (an omníčiye-active state, a gathering, a naming-ceremony, an installation):

$$\mathrm{recount}_{(w, c_i, K)} : v \;\longmapsto\; v'$$

with effects for each $c_i$ that $w$ formally recounts at $K$:

- $\langle a, \texttt{witnessed-coup-recounting}\rangle$ fires at $\mathbf{t}$ for each $a$ present at $K$.
  The audience-as-witnesses cells install.
- $\langle w, \texttt{coup-honor-cell-}c_i\rangle$'s wakȟáŋ-marking is *renewed* — the marking-cell's $\leq_k$-value rises to match the freshness of the witness-set.
- $\langle w, \texttt{standing-as-wóohitika}\rangle$ rises in $\leq_k$ in proportion to the audience-size and the audience-substrate-importance.

The recounting is *not* a fresh coup-firing — the coup-cell itself was installed at the original zuya.
The recounting is a *yuhápi-bearing-forward* morphism: the witness-cells, which sustain the honor-cell, are renewed.

## Why the honor-cell needs renewal

The honor-cell's $\leq_k$-value is sustained by the witness-set's *continuing* presence-of-witnessing-cells.
As the original witnesses age and die, their witnessing-cells in the polity's substrate-record decay (the substrate is $\leq_k$-monotone, but each individual cell's value-state at the substrate-record level depends on the witness being available to confirm).
The recounting morphism transfers the witness-substrate from the original witnesses to the audience at $K$ — younger people who become witnesses-of-the-recounting.

Substrate-algebraically: the honor-cell is a *yuhápi-borne cell*.
Per [yuhápi](../../../../../../../philosophy/theories/indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/yuhapi.md), bearing requires continuing attention.
The polity's honor-substrate is sustained by the recounting; without it, the honor-cell's substrate-recognition fades even though the original firing-cell persists in the strict sense.

## Multiple-recounting and the audience-graph

Across a warrior's lifetime, the recountings build a *witness-graph*: the set of audience-members who have heard a particular coup recounted.
The graph has structural properties:

- Coups recounted *more often* and at *larger gatherings* have *denser witness-graphs* — high $\leq_k$-value, high polity-recognition.
- Coups recounted *rarely* have *sparse witness-graphs* — lower polity-recognition over time.
- Coups *never* recounted after the original zuya have only the original-zuya-witness-graph — substantial decay.

The substrate-recognition of "this warrior's deeds" at the polity's current time-index reads off the witness-graph's density.
The polity remembers most-recounted coups; less-recounted coups fade.

This is structural memory architecture, not casual storytelling.
The polity's honor-substrate *is the recounting-pattern*.

## Recounting at specific occasions

The morphism fires at specific substrate-recognized occasions:

- **Omníčiye session**: when a council convenes and warriors are present, recountings can occur at appropriate moments (typically when the council is settling questions about polity-defense or about a specific warrior's standing).
- **Naming ceremonies**: a young person's naming may invoke the namer's coups as part of the substrate-installation.
  A name given by a notable warrior carries the warrior's recounted-coup-substrate.
- **Sun-dance gatherings**: confederacy-scope gatherings are major recounting occasions.
  The accumulated audience-substrate at sun dance is the largest the polity reaches.
- **Installation of offices**: when a new naca, wakichunza, or blotahunka is installed, the candidate's prior coups are recounted as part of the installation-substrate.
- **Death of the warrior**: the warrior's coups are recounted at the funeral and at the wanáǧi yuhápi keeping-of-the-soul rite.
  This is the *final* recounting in the strict sense — afterwards, the recounting passes to those who heard the dying-warrior tell their stories.
- **Wanáǧi yuhápi**: the soul-keeping period sustains the warrior's coup-substrate for the period — a kind of suspended-extension of the warrior's own recounting.

## Recounting-protocol

Recounting is not casual self-praise.
The substrate-protocol:

- The warrior introduces the coup with specific phrasing ("I am So-and-so, and I have come to recount...").
- The coup is recounted in specific narrative form — typically chronological, with named witnesses, named enemy (when known), named place.
- Physical gestures can accompany the recounting (striking a pole, dancing the count).
- *False recounting* — claiming coups one did not earn — is a substrate-violation that fires (W2)-negative cells on the warrior's standing-as-wóohitika and standing-as-wówičhake (truth-firmness).
  Witnesses present at the original event can challenge false recountings; the substrate-protocol gives them standing to do so.

The substrate's check on false recountings: the witness-graph itself.
A claimed coup whose witnesses cannot be named or whose named witnesses contradict the claim collapses the witness-cells; the claim fires $\mathbf{f}$ rather than $\mathbf{t}$.

## Contemporary recounting

Recounting continues in contemporary practice.
The substrate-recognized occasions are:

- Tribal-government gatherings.
- Wacipi (powwow) public-speech moments — particularly the *wopila* (gratitude) sections.
- Funerals — the deceased's accumulated wówašake-w-cells are recounted at the funeral and at the one-year *wíkhotahi* (memorial).
- Family gatherings — informal recountings that nonetheless propagate the witness-substrate to younger generations.
- Modern military veterans' return-rites — the veterans recount their service, including coups (the touching of the enemy without killing, when it happened) as part of the substrate-restoration.

The contemporary continuation of the recounting-morphism is *substrate-restoration* in the strict sense: every recounting fires the witness-cell-renewal that sustains the polity's honor-substrate.
The substrate-decay from disrupted recounting-occasions during the suppression period is being slowly repaired through the contemporary continuation.
