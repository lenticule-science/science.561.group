# Škáŋ

Škáŋ is the moving-principle — the cosmic animation that gives spirit to every living thing, the supreme judge among the four superior Wakȟáŋ, and the engine of the relational field.
He is the most-functionally-powerful of the four; Sword and Finger both rank him supreme in present-time authority though Íŋyaŋ is first in cosmogonic origin.
In the relational framework, Škáŋ is the *cannot-not-act* — the principle of motion-itself that must be in order for anything to move, live, or have spirit at all.

## What the holy men said

The clearest recorded teaching is the **Finger interview of March 25, 1914**, conducted by Walker and published in the 1917 *Sun Dance* paper as "Conception of Energy."
Finger is a distinguished shaman.
The interview includes:

- Finger establishes the register-distinction: "**The shamans use the word Škáŋ; the common people say Takúškaŋškaŋ.**" The two terms denote the same being. *Takúškaŋškaŋ* (literally "that-which-moves-moves," a reduplicated stative) is everyday speech for the visible-motion. *Škáŋ* is the shamans' wakȟáŋ-language form, naming the underlying principle rather than its observable effects.
- Walker: "I have been told that Škáŋ is the sky."
- Finger: "**Yes. Škáŋ is a Spirit and all that mankind can see of Him is the blue of the sky.
  But He is everywhere.**"
- Finger: "**Wí is Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka Kiŋ and Škáŋ is Naǧí Tȟáŋka, the Great Spirit.**"

George Sword, in the cosmogony recorded by Walker (published in *Lakota Belief and Ritual* and *Lakota Myth*), gives the structural reading: **Škáŋ is the source of all animated life; all things have a spirit, and this spirit is given to it by Škáŋ when the thing is created.** Finger adds: Škáŋ "takes from the stars a *naǧí* (ghost) and gives it to each babe at the time of its birth."

The exact phrasing matters. *Naǧí* (variantly *naǧí* / *nagi*) is the spirit-aspect, the shade that survives bodily death. Škáŋ is not just "motion"; he is the *giver of naǧí*.
Every spirited being has its spirit because Škáŋ has given it.
Without Škáŋ, no being is alive in the spirited sense.

## Škáŋ is not "motion" in the kinematic sense

Common English glosses render Škáŋ as "motion" or "energy."
Both are misleading.
The kinematic reading treats motion as something that happens *to* things; the energy reading treats it as a quantity *of* things. Škáŋ is neither — he is the *principle* that anything moves or lives at all. *Takúškaŋškaŋ* — the visible motion — is the *effect* of Škáŋ on what exists. Škáŋ himself is the source-of-motion, not motion itself.

Finger's phrase "**He is everywhere**" matters here.
The blue of the sky is what mankind *sees* of Škáŋ, but Škáŋ is not contained in the sky.
He is the moving-presence in everything spirited.
The blue dome is his visible-aspect, the way Wí (Sun) is the visible-aspect of his ceremonial-counterpart.

## Škáŋ as supreme judge

Finger explicitly attributes to Škáŋ the supreme judicial role.
In Sword's hierarchy of the four superior Wakȟáŋ, Škáŋ ranks highest in *functional* power (chief animator and judge), Wí ranks highest in ceremonial chief-role, then Makȟá, then Íŋyaŋ (first in origin but last in present rank).
The inversion is structural: Íŋyaŋ gave-rise-to-everything *by being emptied*, so his present-time rank is the rank of the spent-source.

The judicial role is significant for the relational framework.
The supreme judge is the one who *pronounces on undetermined cases* — who takes the under-determined and resolves it toward true or false.
This positions Škáŋ structurally at the bilattice ⊥ — the under-determined position from which determination proceeds.
See [tobtob-kin](tobtob-kin.md) for the four-superior-Wakȟáŋ as the four logic-values ⊥, t, f, ⊤ of the relational field.

## Škáŋ and Reflex

In the relational framework's articulation-chain, Reflex is the minimal act that cannot-not-happen when the originating paradox ([iyan-hanhepi](iyan-hanhepi.md)) is articulated.
The act is the engine, not the product; it is forced by the structural impossibility of the void's aloneness; it is what arises when the alone-condition contradicts itself.

Škáŋ is exactly this: the *power-rising-from-the-paradox*.
The cosmogonic recording is precise — Íŋyaŋ self-empties, his blood becomes Earth and water, and "the power that was in his blood rose as Škáŋ." Škáŋ is what the paradox *throws up*.
He is not a being-among-beings created by Íŋyaŋ; he is the consequence-of-Íŋyaŋ-emptying, the principle that *had to* rise once the paradox enacted.

This is why Škáŋ is both ⊥ (in the bilattice reading) *and* the engine of all subsequent determination.
The under-determined position is not passive; it is the active-source from which determinations issue. Škáŋ at ⊥ is the supreme-judge-holding-the-undecided; the moving-principle-that-must-resolve.

## Škáŋ in lived practice

The naǧí-giving is not a one-time event.
Every newborn receives a naǧí from Škáŋ "from the stars"; every death is the return of the naǧí toward Škáŋ's domain (with proper ceremony to maintain the ancestor-relation, e.g., the wanáǧi yuhápi keeping-of-the-soul rite).
The relational substrate is therefore continually-being-animated; Škáŋ is not a remote first-cause but a present-tense ongoing-source.

The blue sky is the daily visible-aspect; the wind (Tȟaté, one of the lesser Wakȟáŋ associated with Škáŋ) is a daily moving-aspect.
To stand outside under open sky is to stand within Škáŋ's most visible presence.
This is the substrate-fact behind why so much Lakota ceremony is conducted outdoors, oriented to the sky, with offerings raised toward the blue.
