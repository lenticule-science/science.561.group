The fundamental of a wind instrument is the lowest-frequency standing-wave mode that its [bore](./bore.md) supports — the pitch the instrument sounds when its air column vibrates in its simplest, longest-wavelength pattern.
For an open-open cylindrical [bore](./bore.md) of length *L*, the fundamental's wavelength is approximately *2L* (with small corrections for the bore's end effects), and the fundamental frequency is therefore *v/2L*, where *v* is the speed of sound in air.
The [cedar-flute](../../../../b4/cedar-flute/cedar-flute.md), being an open-open cylindrical bore in standard construction, has a fundamental that is set by its overall length.
The [eagle-wing-bone-whistle](../../../../b4/eagle-wing-bone-whistle/eagle-wing-bone-whistle.md), [crane-bone-whistle](../../../../b4/crane-bone-whistle/crane-bone-whistle.md), and other short bone whistles have correspondingly high fundamentals because their bores are short.

The fundamental of a wind instrument is not just one pitch among many; it is the *base reference* from which all other available pitches are derived.
The higher [harmonics](./harmonic.md) of the bore are integer multiples of the fundamental frequency (for an open-open bore), and the [overblowing](../../../../b4/overblowing/overblowing.md) technique allows the player to sound those higher harmonics by adjusting breath pressure.
Where the instrument has [finger-holes](../../../../b4/finger-hole/finger-hole.md), the additional scale-degrees produced by uncovering holes are also referenced to the fundamental — they are the pitches the shortened air column produces, with their relations to the fundamental determining the scale's interval structure.

On the [cedar-flute](../../../../b4/cedar-flute/cedar-flute.md), the fundamental is the pitch sounded when all declared required [finger-holes](../../../../b4/finger-hole/finger-hole.md) are covered (the air column extends from the [duct-flute](../../../../b4/duct-flute/duct-flute.md) sound source all the way to the foot of the instrument). This is musically the *tonic* of the [pentatonic-minor-scale](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/media-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/music/content/music-theory/fundamental/pentatonic-minor-scale.md): it is scale-degree 1, the tone to which phrases resolve at the [cadence](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../information-science-services-section/services/communication-science-services-section/services/media-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/research/2026/07/31/lenticule-import/library/arts/music/content/music-theory/fundamental/cadence.md), the tone that bears the most extended sustained tones, and the tone whose octave (2× fundamental, the second [harmonic](harmonic.md)) provides the upper boundary of the principal playing range. The fundamental's identification with the scale's tonic is not arbitrary — it is the consequence of the all-holes-covered fingering being the *resting* fingering of the hands, the easiest position to return to, and (more importantly) the position the breath naturally moves toward as the phrase's energy decays. The conventional descending phrase-shape of the [courting-song](../../../../b4/courting-song/courting-song.md) places the fundamental at the phrase's terminal position.

On the [cedar-flute](../../../../b4/cedar-flute/cedar-flute.md), the fundamental is the pitch sounded when all six [finger-holes](../../../../b4/finger-hole/finger-hole.md) are covered (the air column extends from the [duct-flute](../../../../b4/duct-flute/duct-flute.md) sound source all the way to the foot of the instrument).
This is musically the *tonic* of the [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md): it is scale-degree 1, the tone to which phrases resolve at the [cadence](./cadence.md), the tone that bears the most extended sustained tones, and the tone whose octave (2× fundamental, the second [harmonic](./harmonic.md)) provides the upper boundary of the principal playing range.
The fundamental's identification with the scale's tonic is not arbitrary — it is the consequence of the all-holes-covered fingering being the *resting* fingering of the hands, the easiest position to return to, and (more importantly) the position the breath naturally moves toward as the phrase's energy decays.
The conventional descending phrase-shape of the [courting-song](../../../../b4/courting-song/courting-song.md) places the fundamental at the phrase's terminal position.

The frequency of the cedar flute's fundamental — and therefore the absolute pitch of the instrument — varies across instruments.
Historical instruments and current makers produce instruments in a range from approximately F#3 to A4 (roughly 185 Hz to 440 Hz); F#4 and G4 (~370 Hz and ~392 Hz) are perhaps the most common keys for contemporary courting flutes.
The choice of key for a given instrument is partly a matter of the maker's preference, partly a matter of the wood and craft tradition, and partly a matter of the player's vocal range — a player who [instrumentalizes](./instrumentalization.md) [wiilowan](../../../../b4/wiilowan/wiilowan.md) vocal material may favor a flute whose fundamental sits in the vocal range that suits their own voice.

The whistle aerophones — [eagle-wing-bone-whistle](../../../../b4/eagle-wing-bone-whistle/eagle-wing-bone-whistle.md), [crane-bone-whistle](../../../../b4/crane-bone-whistle/crane-bone-whistle.md), and similar — have a fundamental and effectively no other pitch (the bore admits [overblowing](../../../../b4/overblowing/overblowing.md) and [warble](../../../../b4/warble/warble.md), but in standard ensemble use the whistle sounds only its fundamental).
For these instruments the fundamental is not a *base reference* in the cedar-flute sense (there are no additional scale degrees referenced to it); the fundamental *is* the instrument's pitch material, and the music-theoretic content is what is done with it (timbral envelope, rhythm, ensemble articulation).
