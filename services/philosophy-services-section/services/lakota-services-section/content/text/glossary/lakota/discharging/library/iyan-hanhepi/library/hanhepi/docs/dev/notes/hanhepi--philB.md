# Haŋ / Haŋhépi

Haŋ — primordial darkness — is the pre-creation condition in which [Íŋyaŋ](iyan.md) existed alone.
It is not "darkness" in the sense of an absence of light; it is darkness-as-being, the substrate-state before any distinction had been made.
Haŋhépi is the durational form — "night" as a span of time, what Haŋ becomes once time itself exists.

## The distinction Haŋ / Haŋhépi

Walker's informants use both words but with a distinction:

- **Haŋ** — the primordial dark, treated as a being/aspect, the *condition* of pre-creation aloneness.
  Recorded by Sword in the cosmogonic narrative: "In the beginning was Íŋyaŋ, who had no beginning, for he was there when there was no other, only *Haŋ*, the Darkness."
- **Haŋhépi** — "night," the durational expression of darkness once time exists.
  The night that returns, the dark half of the day.

The cleaner primary-source choice for the *principial* pre-creation condition is **Haŋ**. *Haŋhépi* already implies time and sequence and so does not quite name the prior-to-time void.
In practice, contemporary Lakota speech often uses *Haŋhépi* loosely for both, but the older recordings preserve the distinction.

## The companion of Íŋyaŋ

In Sword's cosmogony, the pre-creation condition is *only Íŋyaŋ and Haŋ*.
Two beings — but Haŋ is not other-to-Íŋyaŋ in the way later beings will be other-to-each-other.
Haŋ is the *medium-of-aloneness*: the dark within which Íŋyaŋ is alone.
Some recordings treat Haŋ as itself an aspect of Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka, the all-sacred.
The pre-creation state is then better described as *Íŋyaŋ-in-Haŋ* rather than *Íŋyaŋ and Haŋ as two beings*; see [iyan-hanhepi](iyan-hanhepi.md) for the genesis-paradox as a unitary cosmogonic position.

## After creation

Haŋ does not vanish when Íŋyaŋ empties himself.
The cosmogony produces a world that has *both day and night* — once Wí (Sun) is created to warm the earth, his absence each evening leaves Haŋhépi, the returning-night.
Haŋ becomes Haŋhépi; the pre-creation aspect becomes the durational night that cycles.
The dark remains as a continuing presence in the cosmos, not as a defeated condition but as a continuing co-presence with the light.
This is why night-ceremonies (the most powerful of the seven rites, including parts of the sun dance and the wanáǧi yuhápi keeping-of-the-soul) draw their power from Haŋhépi: they conduct in the substrate-condition closest to the cosmogonic origin.

## What Haŋ is not

- *Not* nothingness in the colloquial sense (an absence one could find).
- *Not* evil or merely-negative (the cosmogony does not moralize the dark; Western associations of darkness with evil are imported and do not belong here).
- *Not* prior in time (there was no time before the cosmogony; Haŋ is *structurally* prior, not temporally prior).
- *Not* the same as the void of relationlessness in Western philosophy.
  Haŋ is *with-Íŋyaŋ*: the pre-creation condition is two-in-one, not bare absence.

## Relation to other traditions' primordial-dark concepts

The Lakota Haŋ has structural cousins in many traditions — the Polynesian Te Pō, the Greek Nyx, the Egyptian Nun-as-primordial-waters.
The Lakota specificity is that Haŋ is *with-Íŋyaŋ from the beginning*: the dark is not what-was-before-being but the *medium-of-being-alone-before-relation*.
This matters for the genesis-paradox: the paradox is not "how does something come from nothing" but "how does the alone-in-dark cease to be alone, since articulating-the-aloneness is itself a relation."
See [iyan-hanhepi](iyan-hanhepi.md).
