A harmonic of a wind instrument's [bore](./bore.md) is a standing-wave mode at an integer multiple of the [fundamental](./fundamental.md) frequency — the second harmonic at 2×*f*, the third harmonic at 3×*f*, the fourth at 4×*f*, and so on, where *f* is the fundamental frequency.
The set of all harmonics for a given fundamental is the *harmonic series* of that fundamental: *f*, 2*f*, 3*f*, 4*f*, 5*f*, 6*f*, 7*f*, …

Which harmonics actually sound from a given [bore](./bore.md) depends on the bore's end configuration.
An *open-open* cylindrical bore (like the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md)) admits *all* integer harmonics.
An *open-closed* (closed at one end, open at the other) cylindrical bore admits only the *odd* harmonics (*f*, 3*f*, 5*f*, 7*f*, …).
A conical bore — whether overall open or closed — admits all integer harmonics (the closed-end behavior of a cone differs acoustically from that of a cylinder).
For the Lakotaean aerophone tradition the bores at issue are the cylindrical bores of the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md) and the bone whistles, both essentially open-open in their standard construction, both therefore admitting the full integer harmonic series.

The harmonic series is the basis for the [overblowing](./overblowing.md) technique.
At normal breath pressure a [duct-flute](./duct-flute.md) or end-blown whistle sounds at the fundamental — the bore vibrates in its longest-wavelength mode.
As the player increases breath pressure (and, for embouchure-controlled instruments, adjusts [embouchure](./embouchure.md) tension), the bore can be driven into a higher mode of vibration: the second harmonic, at 2×*f*, which is the *octave* above the fundamental; the third harmonic, at 3×*f*, an octave-plus-a-fifth above the fundamental; and so on.
On the [cedar-flute](./cedar-flute.md), the second harmonic of the all-holes-covered fundamental is the octave (scale-degree 8 of the [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md)), and the second harmonic of each hole-state's fundamental is the corresponding scale-degree an octave above — so the player can in principle play the entire pentatonic scale an octave higher by [overblowing](./overblowing.md).
In practice, idiomatic cedar-flute playing uses the first-octave fundamentals as the principal material, with [overblowing](./overblowing.md) to the second-octave fundamentals as an occasional expressive device.

The harmonic series is also the basis for the [warble](./warble.md) on the cedar flute and bone whistles.
The warble is rapid alternation between the fundamental and a higher harmonic — typically the second harmonic (the octave), occasionally the third — produced by jet instability at the [duct-flute](./duct-flute.md) splitting edge or at the [embouchure](./embouchure.md) of an end-blown whistle.
The player tunes the breath pressure to a regime in which the air-jet's behavior is on the boundary between fundamental-driving and harmonic-driving, and the bore "flips" between modes at audio-rate, producing the perceptually-distinctive warble timbre.
The musical use of the warble draws directly on the bore's harmonic structure — without the bore's admissibility of the higher harmonic, the warble would not be available.

For the bone whistles, the harmonic series is acoustically present (the bore admits its harmonics in the standard manner) but musically backgrounded: the whistles operate at the fundamental in standard ensemble use, with the harmonics appearing only in [overblowing](./overblowing.md) flares and [warble](./warble.md) ornamentation.
For the cedar flute, the harmonic series is foregrounded as the basis for the octave-up extension of the playing range, for the warble ornament, and for the acoustic stability of the [pentatonic-minor-scale](./pentatonic-minor-scale.md) (whose intervals — particularly the perfect fifth from fundamental to scale-degree 5, and the octave from fundamental to scale-degree 8 — are pure small-integer harmonic ratios).
