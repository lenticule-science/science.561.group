# Gift-economy redistribution

The gift-economy is the polity's substrate-mechanism for resource flow between agents and groups.
It operates without exchange-cells, without price-cells, and without property-cells over the gifted items in the conventional sense.
Instead, gifting installs *standing-cells* and *kin-obligation-cells* whose accumulation is the people's substrate-record of their relational standing.

## The gift morphism

For agents $a, b$ and a gift item $g$:

$$\mathrm{gift}_{(a, b, g)} : v \;\longmapsto\; v'$$

with effects:

- $\langle a, \texttt{gave-to}, b\rangle \geq_k \mathbf{t}$ — the giving is recorded.
- $\langle a, \texttt{acts-wówačhantognaka-toward}, b\rangle \geq_k \mathbf{t}$ — the giving is a wówačhantognaka (generosity) act, contributing to $a$'s standing-as-wówačhantognaka cell via (W2) of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$.
- $\langle b, \texttt{stands-in-receipt-from}, a\rangle \geq_k \mathbf{t}$ — the receipt is recorded.
- $\langle g, \texttt{came-from}, a\rangle \geq_k \mathbf{t}$ if the item carries a memory-cell (some items do, some do not — see below).

Crucially, the gift morphism does *not* install a *debt-cell* requiring $b$ to return-something-to-$a$.
Reciprocity is structural to the system (described below) but is not installed at the cell-level as a specific debt.

## What gifting is for, structurally

Three substrate-roles:

**(G1) Distributing access to resources.** A hunt-kill, a harvested patch of prairie turnips, a successful trade — these are nominally the producers' but flow through the gift-channel to the wider populace.
The flow is not a tax (no obligation-cell to give-a-share) but a *virtue-conducted* redistribution (the producer's wówačhantognaka cells rise as they give, and the producer's standing rises with them).

**(G2) Sustaining kin-obligation cycles.** (W3) of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ installs *owes-virtue-to* cells from kin-relations.
Gifting is the *discharge* of these cells.
The kin-link installs the obligation-cell; the gift-act discharges-and-renews it.
The cycle is structurally indefinite — the obligations are continuing.

**(G3) Marking honor and event.** Giveaways at significant events — naming ceremonies, mournings, sun-dance commitments, returns from a successful war party, public installations — are concentrated gift-morphisms.
They both *honor* the event (mark its substrate-significance) and *discharge* accumulated wówačhantognaka-conduct.

## Asymmetry with reciprocity

Reciprocity is structural to the gift-economy but *not* installed at the cell-level as a specific debt.
The substrate-mechanism is indirect:

- Each gift increases the giver's wówačhantognaka-standing.
- Each receipt is non-incrementing to the receiver's standing per se, but creates *visibility*: the populace sees that $b$ is in receipt-from $a$.
- The receiver's continuing conduct toward $a$ is now observed against that visibility.
  If the receiver fails to act-wówačhantognaka in their turn (toward $a$, toward others), their standing-cells lag and eventually drop.
- The pressure is reputational and substrate-recorded, not contractual.
  The receiver is not *owing $a$ a return gift*; the receiver is *under continuing observation by the polity* for whether they conduct wówačhantognaka in their turn.

This is the structural mechanism by which the gift-economy avoids both *gift-as-debt* (which would convert it back into an exchange-economy with implicit contracts) and *gift-as-pure-transfer* (which would lose the relational-standing content).

## What does not flow through gift

Certain item-classes do not flow through gift-economy redistribution.
The substrate-content of "what is unalienable":

- **Personal-named items.** A person's specific pipe (bound to them through dream or initiation), a person's medicine, a person's spirit-name — these are bound to the person and cannot be transferred via gift.
  Death-transition specifies their disposition (some destroyed, some interred with the deceased, some passed to a designated kin under specific conditions).
- **Tiyóšpaye-bundle items.** A tiyóšpaye-bundle (a substrate-bearing item that anchors the tiyóšpaye's continuity) does not gift outside the tiyóšpaye.
  The keeper-cell is the only relation-cell over it; gifting it would mean fundamental restructuring.
- **Wakȟáŋ-bearing items.** A pipe used in confederacy-scope ceremony, a treaty-pipe, the bundles of the seven rites — these have substrate-cells that condition on continuing wakȟáŋ-marking; gifting them requires wakȟáŋ-bearing operations (typically only at death-transition or by explicit wičháša-wakȟáŋ direction).

The gift-economy is *open-by-default* but has structural carve-outs for substrate-bound items.

## The giveaway as concentrated morphism

At a significant event — most centrally the *otúhaŋ* (giveaway) following a death, after a year of mourning — the gift-morphism is run at scale.
The family of the deceased gives away *most or all of their material possessions* to relatives, friends, and to strangers in attendance.
The substrate-content:

- The deceased's possessions are gifted out; the gift-cells settle across many recipients.
- The family's wówačhantognaka-conduct cells settle at very high levels; their standing-as-wówačhantognaka is permanently elevated.
- The family's *material* substrate is reduced; they begin rebuilding.
  The reduced-and-then-rebuilt pattern is structurally itself a substrate-restoration cycle.

The giveaway is *not* loss in the substrate-algebraic sense — the family's standing-cells rise as the property-cells fall.
The polity's standing-cell-record is enriched; the resource flow is the visible substrate of that enrichment.

## Sources

- Ella Deloria, *Speaking of Indians* (1944) — the gift-economy as structurally constitutive of Dakota/Lakota social life.
- Walker 1982, *Lakota Society* — recorded giveaway practice and the otúhaŋ structure.
- Marcel Mauss, *The Gift* (1925) — comparative gift-economy framework; the Lakota system has structural parallels to many Indigenous gift-systems Mauss surveys, with the specific virtue-cell-based-standing mechanism being distinctively Lakota.
- David Graeber, *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011) — comparative analysis of gift-economy vs debt-economy, with attention to Indigenous systems.
- Robin Wall Kimmerer, *Braiding Sweetgrass* (2013) — Anishinaabe-traditioned articulation of gift-economy as ecological-ethical substrate; structurally parallel to the Lakota system.
- Estes, *Our History is the Future* (2019) — contemporary giveaway practice and the substrate-continuity through reservation-period economic disruption.
