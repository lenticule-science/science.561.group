# Walker 1982 — *Lakota Society*

The sociological-political volume of the posthumous Walker trilogy.
Where *Lakota Belief and Ritual* ([walker1980](walker1980.md)) is the cosmological-theological volume, this is the volume on Lakota *social organization* — kinship, council, marriage, naming, the structure of the tiyospaye and oyate.

## What it covers

- The structure of the *tiyospaye* (extended family) and *oyate* (people).
- Kinship terminology and the relational substance of kin-conduct.
- Council structure: the *naca* role, *omniciye* (council deliberation), the akicita (camp marshals/peacekeepers).
- Marriage, naming, adoption (including huŋkápi as a formal adoption-as-relative rite).
- The right of withdrawal and consensus-by-withdrawal as political form.

## Key passages cited in this subtheory

### Tȟakúye as kinship-as-substance

DeMallie summarizing the Walker corpus on Dakota/Lakota kinship: **"Tȟakúye is 'something they have in common'** — *very broad, not restricted to blood or marriage but to relationships of all kinds*.
'To be a good relative is the most important thing that a Dakota can be… being Dakota is being related.'"

This is the source for the [takuye](teaching/explanations/takuye.md) entry's structural reading.

### Wóčhekiye — kinship-invoked-in-prayer

DeMallie: "Prayer in the Dakota sense is *wóčhekiye* — to invoke kinship with a spirit."
Prayer is therefore not request-of-a-deity but verbal-assertion-of-a-kin-relation with a being that is already kin.
This is the source for several points in [wotakuye](teaching/explanations/wotakuye.md) and [mitakuye-oyasin](teaching/explanations/mitakuye-oyasin.md).

### Consensus-by-withdrawal

DeMallie's reworking of Walker on Lakota council practice: deliberation continues until the speakers have spoken; those who cannot consent *withdraw* rather than dissent; the engaged majority proceeds.
The withdrawal does not sever kinship; the kin-link remains.
This is the source for [iyuha-sni](teaching/explanations/iyuha-sni.md) and the corresponding entry in the lakotaean-politics theory.

### Huŋkápi as installation-of-relatives

Walker's recording of the rite that adopts persons into full kin-relation, with the ceremonial conditions for the adoption to take effect (proper wičháša wakȟáŋ, proper preparation, proper participants).
See [seven-rites](teaching/explanations/seven-rites.md) and the politics-theory entry on [hunka](../../../../../sociology/theories/politics/theories/lakotaean-politics/concepts/hunka.md).
