The *tiyóšpaye-founder* is a Lakota political-theoretical role: the relative most-responsible-for-the-formation of a tiyóšpaye, particularly in the contemporary case of tiyóšpaye reconstitution under or after settler-state disruption.
The role is asymmetric in responsibility but not hierarchical in authority; the founder is a relative among relatives, not a sovereign over members.
Authority — to the extent any concentrates — flows from the membership's recognition of conduct under wolakȟota, not from a position-of-command.

## Theoretical grounding

Tiyóšpaye come into being in three structurally distinct ways:

1. *By descent from a founding ancestor* — the standard case in the pre-reservation tradition.
  The founder is a deceased relative whose descendants now organize collectively.
  Authority is constituted retroactively by the descendant-membership's recognition of a shared lineage.
2. *By fission from an existing tiyóšpaye* — a subset of members withdraws together, often when a tiyóšpaye grows past sustainable size.
  The "founder" is collectively the withdrawing membership; no single person holds founder-authority.
3. *By reconstitution in the diaspora or under disruption* — the contemporary case Estes 2019's *Our History is the Future* treats at length.
  After settler-state policies that displace, separate, and administratively re-categorize Lakota people, contemporary tiyóšpaye reconstitute themselves through specific persons taking responsibility for the formation: gathering members across distance, performing huŋkápi adoption to extend the kin-substrate, holding the practices of wolakȟota despite the disruption.

The third case is what the present concept names.
The founder in this case is a *living* person whose conduct is most-responsible-for-the-formation.
The role's content:

- *Inviting kin-relation.* The founder extends the kin-substrate to new members through huŋkápi adoption, marriage, and the ceremonial-substrate moves that the tradition supplies.
- *Holding the practices.* The founder conducts wolakȟota — performs wówačhantognaka (generosity), exercises wóksape (situated practical reason), demonstrates wóohitika (steadiness under difficulty) and wówačhiŋtȟaŋka (perseverance) across the years.
  This conduct is what the membership recognizes.
- *Accepting recognition.* The founder cannot dispatch to members the way a position-of-command dispatches down a chain.
  The founder can act, and the gathering membership returns recognition — or non-recognition — in response.
  The conjunction of that recognition is what installs, and can withdraw, the founder's standing.

## Related theory

Glen Coulthard's *Red Skin, White Masks* (2014) develops the politics-of-recognition critique that bears on this concept: settler-state recognition politics offers Indigenous peoples authority *from above* (federal recognition, state-granted sovereignty), and the politics of refusal that Indigenous traditions exercise refuses top-down recognition in favor of the bottom-up recognition that kin-relational traditions actually operate by.
The tiyóšpaye-founder role's reliance on membership recognition is a specific Lakota instance of the general Indigenous-political-theoretical claim that legitimate authority is constituted by those it serves.
