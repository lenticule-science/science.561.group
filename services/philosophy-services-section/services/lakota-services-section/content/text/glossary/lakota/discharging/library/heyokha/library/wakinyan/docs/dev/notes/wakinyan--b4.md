# Wakíŋyaŋ

The Wakíŋyaŋ are the Thunder-beings — wakhan-powers of the West, the sky-borne carriers of the sudden lightning-strike, the wakhan-source whose dream produces the heyokha bond.
They are the canonical ⊤-bearing wakhan: powers of suddenness, of the lightning-strike-without-warning, of the contradiction-of-clear-sky-suddenly-storming.

## Why the Wakíŋyaŋ are ⊤-bearing

The Wakíŋyaŋ embody contradiction in their characteristic act: the clear-sky-becoming-thunder.
Where Wí (Sun) is the steady luminous-affirmation (t) and Makȟá (Earth) is the grounded-complement (f), the Wakíŋyaŋ are the powers that *break* one state to deliver the other — clear-sky breaking-into-storm, calm breaking-into-strike.
The break is the contradiction; the strike is its discharge.

In the bilattice reading [tobtob-kin](../../../tobtob-kin/tobtob-kin.md), the Wakíŋyaŋ sit among the lesser wakhan but their character is ⊤-bearing — they carry the principle of contradictive suddenness that, applied at any cell, lifts that cell toward absurd.
This is why dreaming the Wakíŋyaŋ binds a person to a vocation of contrary conduct: the dream installs the ⊤-bearing principle into the dreamer's relational substrate.

## The relation to heyokha

The Wakíŋyaŋ are the source-of-the-bond, not the bond itself.
A person who dreams the Wakíŋyaŋ becomes a [heyokha-bearer](../heyokha-bearer/heyokha-bearer.md); the dream-event is the [wakinyan-vision](../wakinyan-vision/wakinyan-vision.md); the lived obligation is [contrary-conduct](../contrary-conduct/contrary-conduct.md).
The Wakíŋyaŋ are upstream of all three — the wakhan-power whose touch creates the heyokha vocation.

## Direction of the West

Walker's recording places the Wakíŋyaŋ at the West of the four directions.
The West is the direction of the spring storms that bring the year's first lightning, of the season-change that breaks winter, of the cosmogonic-renewal that re-enacts the original contradiction in time.
The Wakíŋyaŋ are *the powers that break the previous state to make way for the new*.
Heyokha's contradiction is therefore not generic; it is specifically the form of contradiction that *breaks-toward-renewal*.
