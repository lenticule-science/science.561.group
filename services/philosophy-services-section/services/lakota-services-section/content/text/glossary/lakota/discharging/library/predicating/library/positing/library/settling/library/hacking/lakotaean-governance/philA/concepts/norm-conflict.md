# Norm-conflict (Lakota form)

Norm-conflict in the Lakota substrate appears in three structural-kinds, distinguished by their substrate-mechanism.
Each kind is a specialization of [norm-conflict-as-top-valued-cell](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/undated/norm-conflict-as-top-valued-cell.md).

## Kind 1: $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$-internal conflict

A conflict arising *within* $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$'s own rule-set.
Substrate-form:

- A specific kin-pair has *competing-conduct* claims — e.g., two of an agent's relatives have incompatible requests (the agent owes wówačhantognaka to both, but the requests cannot be jointly discharged: the agent cannot be in two places at once).
- The conduct-cells fire at $\top$ on the *accountability-disposition* meta-cell: which obligation gets primary discharge?

This kind is *common* and *substrate-resolvable*.
Resolutions:

- **Cadence-resolution**: discharge one obligation now, the other later within the cadence-window.
  Most $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$-internal conflicts are time-resolvable.
- **Kin-priority resolution**: the kin-distance distinguishes priorities.
  Closer-kin obligations typically take precedence over farther-kin obligations within the same time-window.
- **Wakȟáŋ-priority resolution**: wakȟáŋ-marked obligations (a ceremony commitment) take precedence over non-wakȟáŋ obligations on the same time-cell.

These resolutions are *substrate-internal* — no external authority is required, no formal adjudication is needed.
The kin-network's own substrate-recognition reads which resolution is appropriate.

## Kind 2: Inter-pattern conflict

A conflict arising between $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$ and another governance-pattern firing on overlapping cells.
The most-load-bearing instance: the reservation-overlay-as-BC-failure (cf. [reservation-overlay-as-bc-failure](../prose/learning/claims/reservation-overlay-as-bc-failure.md)).
Other instances:

- **Lakota / Christian-mission overlay**: when a tiyóšpaye includes both traditional and Christian-practicing members, conflicts arise on baptism (Christian) vs naming-at-huŋkápi-or-traditional-rite (Lakota).
  The cells fire under different rule-systems.
- **Lakota / Cheyenne military-society overlay**: during joint zuya, the Cheyenne Dog Soldier society's protocols and the Lakota blotáhuŋka protocols can conflict on specific command-decisions.
- **Lakota / NAGPRA legal-system**: contemporary repatriation cases produce conflicts between $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$'s wakȟáŋ-keeper-protocols and federal-legal repatriation-protocols.

Inter-pattern conflicts may or may not be resolvable:

- *Compatible-coproduct* cases (Lakota+Cheyenne in joint zuya): resolution by negotiated-interleaving at the leader-council level.
- *Incompatible-overlay* cases (Lakota+US-federal-Indian-law): resolution-paths blocked; persistent $\top$ at the accountability-disposition cells (per [reservation-overlay-as-bc-failure](../prose/learning/claims/reservation-overlay-as-bc-failure.md)).
- *Partial-syncretism* cases (Lakota+Christian for some families): selective-firing of each pattern on different cells, with $\top$-residue at cells where both fire.

## Kind 3: Wakȟáŋ-marking conflict

A conflict over whether a specific cell is wakȟáŋ-marked or not.
Substrate-form:

- One party asserts $w_\mathrm{lak}(\phi) = 1$ (the cell is wakȟáŋ-bearing).
- Another party asserts $w_\mathrm{lak}(\phi) = 0$ (the cell is not).
- The cell's downstream firings differ depending on which assertion holds.

The most-load-bearing contemporary instances:

- **Pȟahá Sápa land-use**: is a specific mining operation in the Black Hills wakȟáŋ-violating or not?
  Different parties read the wakȟáŋ-marking differently.
- **Burial-site protocols**: at sites with ancestral burials, the wakȟáŋ-marking of the site has substrate-consequences for what conduct is permitted.
- **Pipe-use protocols**: should this specific pipe be used in this specific occasion?
  The wakȟáŋ-bearing capacity of the pipe and the wakȟáŋ-marking-status of the occasion both come into play.

Wakȟáŋ-marking conflicts resolve by *wičháša wakȟáŋ adjudication* — the substrate-authority for wakȟáŋ-marking-status determinations rests with the wičháša wakȟáŋ.
The omníčiye does not substitute for this; the council can hear the matter but settles it by inviting the wičháša wakȟáŋ to determine.

Some wakȟáŋ-marking conflicts are *unresolvable in the current substrate-state* — when wičháša wakȟáŋ themselves disagree, or when the wičháša-wakȟáŋ-chain is broken (cf. the substrate-emergency of suppression-period).
These persist at $\top$ until the substrate-emergency is resolved.

## Resolution-paths summary

| Kind | Standard resolution | When blocked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ($\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{wolak}$-internal) | Cadence / kin-priority / wakȟáŋ-priority | Rare; only at extreme strain |
| 2a (compatible-coproduct) | Negotiated interleaving | Rare |
| 2b (incompatible-overlay) | Authority-gate restructuring | Polity doesn't hold gate |
| 2c (partial-syncretism) | Per-cell selective firing | Family-internal disagreement |
| 3 (wakȟáŋ-marking) | Wičháša wakȟáŋ adjudication | Wakȟáŋ-chain broken |

The polity's *resilience under conflict* depends on which kinds are dominant.
Kind 1 conflicts are routine and don't accumulate cohomological residue.
Kind 2b (incompatible-overlay) is the source of the largest cohomological residue and the most-load-bearing contemporary challenge.
