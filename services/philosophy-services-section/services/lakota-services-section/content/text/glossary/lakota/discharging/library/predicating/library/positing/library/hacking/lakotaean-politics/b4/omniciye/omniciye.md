# Omniciye

Omníčiye — a council; an assembly that meets around an oceti (council fire) to deliberate matters affecting the community.
The omniciye is the deliberative body — Walker's *Lakota Society* ([walker1982](../../philA/references/walker1982.md)) describes it at the otonwahe (residential community) level: a group of older and respected men residing in the otonwahe convened regularly around the central council fire to deliberate matters of public interest, determine relations with other otonwahes, rule on disputes between residents, and decide where and when to move the camp.

The omniciye is not a fixed-membership legislature.
Walker records that it was not limited by a specified number nor inclusive of all eligible members; it tended to consist of those whose presence the community recognized as appropriate to the matter at hand.
Different matters drew different participants.
The dynamic membership is a structural feature, not a defect — the council that decides a matter is the council appropriate to that matter, not a fixed set of office-holders.

## Council and consensus

The omniciye operates by consensus, with deliberation oriented toward unanimity.
Decisions are reached through listening, reflection, prayer, and ceremony; a council does not adjourn with a decision the participants have not all consented to (or, in the limiting case, that some have not chosen to withdraw from rather than oppose — see [consensus-by-withdrawal](../consensus-by-withdrawal/consensus-by-withdrawal.md)).
The pipe is passed; the pipe's binding ceremony marks a decision as taken.

This procedural form distinguishes the omniciye from a parliament.
There is no majority that overrides a minority; there is no voting count that produces a binding outcome despite dissent.
The structural assumption is that the council's authority comes from the willing assent of its members and the relations among them, not from a procedural mechanism that converts headcount into command.

## Scales of council

The pattern recurs at multiple scales:

- A [tiyospaye](tiyospaye.md) deliberates internally around its own fire.
- An oyate's omniciye assembles representatives from across its tiyospaye to deliberate matters affecting all.
- The [Oceti Sakowin](oceti-sakowin.md) historically convened across all seven fires for matters affecting the confederacy.

The same procedural form — consensus orientation, dynamic membership, ceremonial binding — operates at each scale.

## Continuity into the present

Contemporary Lakota councils — including tribal councils operating under the Indian Reorganization Act constitutions imposed in the 1930s, but also the traditional councils that continue to operate alongside them — work within and around the procedural form of the omniciye.
The tension between IRA-imposed parliamentary procedure and traditional consensus procedure is itself a live political question on Lakota reservations.
Nick Estes ([estes2019](../../philB/references/estes2019.md)) writes on the contemporary form of Oceti Sakowin political life and the ways traditional council practice continues to operate.
