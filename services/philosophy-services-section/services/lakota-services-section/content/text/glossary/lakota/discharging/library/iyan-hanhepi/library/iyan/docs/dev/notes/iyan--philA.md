# Íŋyaŋ

Íŋyaŋ — Stone — is the originating being of the Lakota cosmos and, after his self-emptying, the relational field itself.
He is the only being who is in two cosmogonic positions at once: the void-alone-in-darkness *before* creation, and the everything-emptied-into *after* creation.
In the relational metaphysics, Íŋyaŋ is the *Top* — the full relational field of which every being is a relative.

## The two positions

**Íŋyaŋ alone in Haŋhépi.** Before any other being, Íŋyaŋ existed alone in the primordial darkness ([Haŋhépi](hanhepi.md)).
Walker's informants — most centrally George Sword (Long Knife) — record that Íŋyaŋ was "soft and shapeless," omnipresent, and possessed all powers.
But power with nothing to act upon is not exercised power; aloneness is the negation of the very powers Íŋyaŋ holds.
The condition is not stable.
This is the void-aspect of Íŋyaŋ: the originating paradox-condition.
See [iyan-hanhepi](iyan-hanhepi.md).

**Íŋyaŋ as the relational field.** To exercise his powers, Íŋyaŋ had to make a being other than himself.
He opened his veins; his blue blood flowed out and became Makȟá (Earth) and mní (water); the power that had been in his blood rose as Škáŋ (the sky); Íŋyaŋ himself shrivelled into hard, dry rock.
The cosmogony continues from there, but the structural point is this: every being that exists is *Íŋyaŋ-emptied-into-it*.
Stones, water, earth, sky, sun, moon, the four-winds, the spirits, the people — all are Íŋyaŋ given out.
This is the Top-aspect of Íŋyaŋ: the relational field as a whole, the everything-related.

## Why Íŋyaŋ is Top, not Mitákuye Oyás'iŋ

[Mitákuye oyás'iŋ](mitakuye-oyasin.md) is the *invocation* of the relational field — the phrase that closes prayer, the recognition spoken to acknowledge that one stands in relation.
The phrase points at the field. Íŋyaŋ *is* the field.
The invocation is the act of orienting toward what is already the case; Íŋyaŋ is what is already the case.

Vine Deloria Jr. notes in *The World We Used to Live In* that Lakota cosmology does not split creator from creation in the way Western theology does.
The creator is the creation — Íŋyaŋ poured himself out, and the world is the pouring-out.
There is no transcendent maker outside the made; the maker became the made.
This is why Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka is not a deity-figure ([wakhan](wakhan.md)) — Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka is the *encompassing sacredness* of the Íŋyaŋ-emptied field, not a being-apart-from-it.

## The reduction-by-emptying

After the emptying, Íŋyaŋ is *reduced*.
He is the hard, dry rock — the least powerful of the four superior Wakȟáŋ in present-time rank, though first in cosmogonic origin.
This inversion is structural: the act of giving-rise-to-everything *consumes* the one who gives rise.
The Top is also the most-emptied.
The relational field as a whole is the residue of the void's self-emptying; what remains of Íŋyaŋ in present time is the hard stone that *cannot give more* — every offering it could make has already been made.

This has a direct ethical-political consequence: when stones are gathered for ceremony (sweat-lodge stones, pipe stones), they are gathered with the understanding that one is handling the originating substance of all that exists.
The stones are not material; they are Íŋyaŋ-reduced.
Cannot-give-more does not mean *not-sacred*; it means *most-spent*.

## The four superior Wakȟáŋ

Íŋyaŋ stands as one of the four superior Wakȟáŋ in Sword's theology — Wí (Sun), Škáŋ (Sky/Motion), Makȟá (Earth), Íŋyaŋ (Rock).
These four together are not separate beings but the four positions of the relational field after the originating self-emptying.
See [tobtob-kin](tobtob-kin.md) for the four-times-four structure (sixteen wakȟáŋ aspects total) and the bilattice-valued reading of the four superior as the four logic-values ⊥, t, f, ⊤ of the relational field.

In that reading, Íŋyaŋ is ⊤ — the over-determined position; the both-true-and-false; the contradiction-bearer.
This matches Íŋyaŋ's cosmogonic role exactly: Íŋyaŋ is the being who *was* the void and *became* everything; who *is alone* and *is all relations*; who *cannot remain* alone and *cannot become* other-than-alone without self-emptying.
The contradiction is constitutive.
