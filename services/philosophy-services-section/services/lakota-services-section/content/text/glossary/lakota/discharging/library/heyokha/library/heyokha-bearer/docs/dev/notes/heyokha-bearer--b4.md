# Heyokha-bearer

In Walker's primary recordings, the Lakota word *heyókha* names persons, not an abstract principle.
A heyókha is a person who has dreamed of the Wakíŋyaŋ and is therefore obligated to enact the vision in lived practice.
The principle-reading (heyokha as the cosmogonic-contradiction-as-generative principle) is downstream — drawn out of the practice by the captain's reading.
The bearer-vocation is the primary referent.

## The standing is not chosen

The Wakíŋyaŋ dream comes unsought.
Once it has come, the dreamer is obligated; refusing the obligation is dangerous, both to the dreamer and to the people.
The standing is therefore conferred by wakhan-event rather than acquired by training or appointment.
There is no church-officer track that produces heyokha-bearers, nor a council that names them.
The polity recognizes a heyokha-bearer because the bearer's conduct discharges the vision in the recognized way.

This makes heyokha-bearer a category of persons whose identity-as-bearer is structurally distinct from acquired roles like the [naca](../../../../../../predicating/library/positing/library/hacking/lakotaean-politics/b4/naca/naca.md) (deliberative-executive) or [akicita](../../../../../../predicating/library/positing/library/hacking/lakotaean-politics/b4/akicita/akicita.md) (enforcement).
Where naca and akicita are constituted by recognition-following-conduct, the heyokha-bearer is constituted by the wakhan-event (the dream) and merely confirmed by the recognition-following-conduct.

## The wider ceremonial economy

The heyokha-bearer holds a position essential to the polity's ceremonial economy.
Joseph Epes Brown (*The Sacred Pipe*, 1953) records that the heyokha's clowning at ceremony — the boiling-water scenes, the deliberately-comic conduct that draws laughter from onlookers — is sacred.
The laughter the heyokha-bearer produces is *medicine*; the people's laughing is part of the rite's working.
Remove the heyokha-bearer from a ceremony and the rite is incomplete.

This is why heyokha-bearer is a bearer-class and not merely an aggregate of persons-who-happen-to-have-had-a-dream.
The class has *a polity-level role*: bearing the cosmogonic contradiction so that the rest of the polity can be relieved-by-witnessing rather than burdened-by-having-to-enact.
The heyokha-bearers carry the contradiction so the people don't have to.
