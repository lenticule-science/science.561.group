## Wolakȟota — the central concept

Wolakȟota is the Lakota way: the lifeway, the practice of being-Lakota.
Crucial features per the source:

- **Practice, not possession.** Wolakȟota is something one does, not something one is by descent.
  A Lakota person who fails the four virtues, treats relatives badly, or hoards rather than gives is acting *against* wolakȟota — even though they remain Lakota by birth.
  A non-Lakota person can act in keeping with wolakȟota (rare, conditional, formalizable through the hunka adoption ceremony) without thereby becoming Lakota in any complete sense.
- **Descent is necessary but not sufficient.** Descent matters; descent without practice is not enough.
  The lifeway has to be lived to be the lifeway.
- **Wolakȟota functions as a standard** in contemporary Lakota speech: *that is wolakȟota* / *šni wolakȟota* are substantive evaluative claims about whether conduct holds relations together or breaks them.
  The standard is internal to the tradition — the tradition evaluating its own practice — and it is *strong*: the tradition takes a clear stance on what counts.
- **The related phrase *lakȟól wičhoȟ'aŋ*** ("the way of Lakota doing-things") overlaps with wolakȟota; the first names the way more abstractly, the second the lived practice.
- **Continuity through disruption.** A century and a half of settler-state pressure (boarding schools, language suppression, religious suppression, displacement, dispossession) is pressure on wolakȟota.
  Because wolakȟota is a *lifeway-in-practice*, it can be reclaimed even when transmission has been disrupted: a Lakota person re-learning the language, returning to ceremony, taking up the virtues is *returning to the lifeway*.
  The descent does not need to be re-established (it was never lost); the practice does.

## The integration — what wolakȟota integrates

Wolakȟota is not a standalone concept.
It is the *integrative* name for living the Lakota tradition.
The source identifies what it integrates:

### Mitákuye oyás'iŋ — the metaphysics

Mitákuye oyás'iŋ ("all my relatives; we are all related") is the *invocation* of the relational field.
Reality is a kinship-cosmos: every being stands in kin-relation with every other being.
Not metaphor, not New Age "we are one" (distinct beings remain distinct), not generalized interconnection (the relations are specifically *kin* relations carrying specific obligations).

Three scales of relation: tȟakúye (one specific kin-link), mitákuye oyás'iŋ (invocation of the totality of one's kin-links), Íŋyaŋ (the cosmogonic source-and-field; the relational substance itself, the alone-stone whose self-emptying *is* the relations in which every being stands).

Wolakȟota is the lifeway that *lives in* mitakuye oyasin's metaphysics.
The kinship-organized polity of the Lakota is not a cultural choice — it is the political enactment of the metaphysical claim.

### The four virtues — the ethics

The four qualities of conduct constitutive of being-Lakota in the proper sense:

- **Wówačhantognaka — generosity.** The substantive practice of treating one's relatives as relatives.
  In a kinship-organized polity, generosity is the *substance* of how the polity holds together.
  A giveaway is not symbolic; it is the kin-relation enacted.
  Leaders are recognized as leaders by their generosity; those who hoard are not leaders.
- **Wóksape — wisdom.** The capacity to act well in the specific situation, drawing on relational and ceremonial formation.
  Not stockpiled propositional knowledge.
- **Wóohitika — bravery.** The steadiness of conduct in the face of difficulty.
  Not recklessness (recklessness is a failure of wóksape) and not violence-seeking.
- **Wówačhiŋtȟaŋka — fortitude / perseverance.** "Having a great mind."
  Steadiness across years; the disposition exercised at polity scale by a people that has persisted across continuous settler pressure.

Marshall (2002) treats twelve virtues; other sources reduce to four or expand differently.
The four are the most consistently named.

Virtues are taught through story (narrative inheritance) and apprenticeship — not as propositional principles applied to cases but as dispositions cultivated through repeated relational situation.
The virtues are *also* the qualifications for *naca* (recognized polity leadership): a naca's authority is the recognition of demonstrated conduct in the virtues; a naca who fails them is no longer a naca.

### Ceremony — the seven rites and wakȟáŋ

Wolakȟota includes "participating in ceremony."
The seven rites are the ceremonial substrate in which knowledge is held and transmitted.
Within the integrated framing, ceremony is *method*: ceremony constitutes the relations within which knowledge is held.

Wakȟáŋ is sacred-as-quality, not sacred-as-domain.
Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka — the Great Mystery, often (mis)translated as "the Great Spirit" — is the encompassing sacredness of the relational whole, not a Christian-style God.
The sacred is of this world, in the everyday properly attended.

The source did not pursue the individual rites in this session; they are referenced as the substrate that ceremony participation operates on.

### Language

Speaking Lakȟótiyapi is part of wolakȟota — "speaking Lakota where one can."
The predicated-companion file is explicit about *why*: language is not a separate addendum but part of the substrate the closure-rule discipline operates over, because the language *carries* the relational distinctions the rules quantify on.
White Hat's pedagogical integration of language-teaching and worldview-teaching is grounded in precisely this claim.

### Kin practice and kinship-cosmos

Wolakȟota's first integration item: "living in proper relation with one's relatives."
The kin-relations are not biological-pedigree relations; they are the relational obligations of the cosmogonic-relational ontology.
Ella Deloria's (1944) sociological observation that Dakota/Lakota social organization *is* kinship organization is the source for the polity-side enactment.

## The substrate-algebraic realization — wolakota as closure-rule discipline

The predicated-lakotaean-philosophy subtree carries the substrate-algebraic articulation.
Wolakȟota is realized as a *closure-rule discipline*: a coherent subsystem $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{wolak}} \subseteq \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{lak}}$ of the full closure-rule system on the Lakota substrate.

Key features of the algebraic realization:

- **Coherence.** Rules of $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{wolak}}$ do not contradict each other; an agent operating under the subsystem does not produce $\top$ cells through internal rule conflict (they may still produce $\top$ cells through external witness conflict, which is a different matter).
- **Recognized, not declared.** Membership of a rule in $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{wolak}}$ is the historical accumulation of closure rules that the tradition has recognized as wolakȟota over generations of practice.
  New rules enter by the tradition's own recognition processes (a teaching becomes wolakȟota when the elders carry it); rules revise or leave by the same processes.
- **Acting wolakȟota** is defined: an agent is *acting wolakȟota* at time $\tau$ when the closure rules whose firings produced the values of their conduct cells in the relevant interval are a subset of $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{wolak}}$.
  This is the substrate-algebraic version of being-Lakota as practice: descent determines substrate access; conduct determines which rules of that substrate are active.
- **The integration claim** (formal): the discipline integrates the four-virtues evaluation predicates, the kin-relational predicate primitive, the wakhan cell-and-rule quality, ceremony participation as witness-bearing substrate operations, and Lakota language as the relational distinctions the rules quantify on.
  The discipline is *coherent operation under all of these together*; that joint sufficiency is what makes the discipline a discipline rather than a list.
- **Disruption and reclamation as substrate operations.** Settler pressure is pressure on $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{wolak}}$: pressure to remove rules from the subsystem (suppress ceremony, suppress language, suppress kinship organization) and to install rules outside it (Christian-individual personhood, bureaucratic citizenship, property-relations to land).
  Reclamation is *closure-rule re-installation*: a teaching, ceremony, language fluency, or kinship practice returns to active membership in $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{wolak}}$ through being learned, performed, and recognized.

## What I synthesized this session that may not be in source

Flagging extrapolations the surveyor made beyond direct source testimony:

- **Orthography.** The user's prompt used "wolakhota"; the corpus uses "wolakota" in filenames and "wolakȟota" in frontmatter and body.
  Real Lakota orthography varies (with-h vs with-ȟ vs without).
  The survey adopts "wolakȟota" in running prose and "wolakota" in filenames.
  The source does not address the orthographic question explicitly; this choice is mine.
- **The five-part integration enumeration** (mitakuye-oyasin metaphysics, four-virtues ethics, ceremony, language, kin practice) is partly synthesized.
  The source's `wolakota.md` opens with "living in proper relation with one's relatives, conducting oneself by the four virtues, participating in ceremony, speaking Lakota where one can, and — broadly — living the philosophy of mitakuye oyasin in everyday conduct" — so the items are there, but the integration claim *as a five-part structure* and the gloss "metaphysics / ethics / ceremony / language / kin practice" is my framing.
  The source itself does not subdivide along Western academic lines (and the introduction narrative warns against doing so).
- **Calling the source "integrative."** The source describes wolakota as the name for the lifeway, not as an "integrative concept."
  Treating wolakota *as the integration* is a structural reading consistent with the predicated-companion's "integration claim" but tightened beyond what the concept file alone says.
- **The "naca" connection** — I drew the wolakota-virtues-to-naca chain from the four-virtues file's "Why this is political" section.
  The source supports this; my framing of it as part of wolakota's political enactment is mild but explicit.
- **The Status framing** that this material is "not AI cruft in the ordinary sense."
  This is my reading of the captain's authority-note in the lakotaean-philosophy `_index.md`.
  The note is in the source; the framing of the source-status problem for the survey spec is mine.
