# Question. What is the statistical distribution of pitch spread in a multi-whistle stochastic cluster?

The stochastic-cluster structure (see [stochastic-cluster](../code/structures/stochastic-cluster.md)) is a discrete measure on R⁺ with small finite support concentrated near a central frequency.
Open question: what is the typical *spread* of this support, measured in cents?

A cluster with 5–10 cent spread is acoustically perceived as a single thick pitch.
A cluster with 50–100 cent spread is perceived as multiple distinct pitches.
The auditory and music-theoretic character of the cluster depends critically on this spread.

Hypothesis: ceremonial multi-whistle ensembles produce clusters in the 20–60 cent spread range — wide enough to be perceived as multiple pitches (a recognizable cluster, not a thick unison), narrow enough that the cluster has a definite central pitch.

Sub-survey needed: acoustic measurement of recorded Sun Dance and peyote-ceremony multi-whistle ensembles; characterization of the spread distribution across a corpus.

This question feeds back into the [aggregation](code/transformers/aggregation.md) transformer, whose precise mathematical content is sharper if the spread distribution is known.
