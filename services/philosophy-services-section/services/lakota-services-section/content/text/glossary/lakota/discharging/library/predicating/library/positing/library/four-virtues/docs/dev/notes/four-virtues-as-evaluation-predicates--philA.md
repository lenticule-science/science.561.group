# Four virtues as evaluation predicates

The [four virtues](../../../../indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/four-virtues.md) — *wówačhantognaka* (generosity), *wóksape* (wisdom), *wóohitika* (bravery), *wówačhiŋtȟaŋka* (fortitude) — are realized in the substrate as a family of *evaluation predicates* on conduct cells.

This is a specialization of predicated-governance's [conduct-teachings-as-evaluation-predicates](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/services/predicated-governance-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/conduct-teachings-as-evaluation-predicates.md) — the parent treats conduct-teachings generally; this specialization fixes the specific four virtues that constitute the Lakota evaluation system.

## The four evaluation predicates

For each conduct cell $\langle a, \texttt{acts}, c\rangle$ in the Lakota substrate, four evaluation cells exist:

$$\begin{aligned} &\langle \langle a, \texttt{acts}, c\rangle, \texttt{exhibits}, \texttt{wówačhantognaka}\rangle \\ &\langle \langle a, \texttt{acts}, c\rangle, \texttt{exhibits}, \texttt{wóksape}\rangle \\ &\langle \langle a, \texttt{acts}, c\rangle, \texttt{exhibits}, \texttt{wóohitika}\rangle \\ &\langle \langle a, \texttt{acts}, c\rangle, \texttt{exhibits}, \texttt{wówačhiŋtȟaŋka}\rangle \end{aligned}$$

These are second-order cells — predications about conduct cells — and they take the usual bilattice values: $\mathbf{t}$ when the conduct exhibits the virtue, $\mathbf{f}$ when it fails, $\bot$ when not yet evaluated, $\top$ when the evaluation is contradicted across witnesses (one observer sees the act as generous; another sees it as performative).

## Standing-as-wolakota as cumulative settling

An agent's *standing as wolakȟota* — the recognition that they are living the Lakota way — is the cumulative settling of their virtue-evaluation cells across their conduct history.
Formally, for an agent $a$ and a time index $\tau$:

$$\mathrm{StandingAs\text{-}Wolakota}(a, \tau) \;=\; \bigwedge_v \mathrm{ProfileOf}_v(a, \tau)$$

where $v$ ranges over the four virtues and $\mathrm{ProfileOf}_v(a, \tau)$ is the meet of $a$'s conduct-cell evaluations under virtue $v$ up to time $\tau$.
Standing-as-wolakȟota is settled at $\mathbf{t}$ when an agent's conduct profile under each of the four virtues is at $\mathbf{t}$; it is $\mathbf{f}$ if any virtue's profile is $\mathbf{f}$; it is $\bot$ where evaluation is incomplete; it is $\top$ where the agent's conduct produces contradictions on the virtues (a person who is generous toward outsiders but stingy toward relatives produces a $\top$ on wówačhantognaka).

## Naca-standing as virtue-profile

[Naca](../../../../hacking/lakotaean-politics/b4/naca/naca.md) standing depends on the virtue profile.
A person can be recognized as a naca when their standing-as-wolakȟota settles at $\mathbf{t}$ and other recognition cells (community recognition, council acceptance, ceremonial confirmation) settle accordingly.
The substrate makes the relationship explicit: leadership-standing is a function of virtue-evaluation history, not of office-assignment or descent.

This is also the structural mechanism by which a naca loses standing: a conduct cell that fails one of the virtues (a leader who hoards, fails the council, abandons relatives, breaks down under pressure) shifts the virtue-profile, which shifts standing-as-wolakȟota, which shifts naca-standing.
The substrate-algebraic content of "a naca who fails his relatives is no longer a naca."

## Story-pedagogy as closure-rule installation

Joseph Marshall III's ([marshall2002](../../../../hacking/lakotaean-politics/philB/references/marshall2002.md)) method — teaching the virtues through stories — has a substrate-algebraic reading: stories are closure-rule installations.
A story embeds a virtue in a relational situation, and the listener's substrate absorbs a closure rule of the form "in situations like this, conduct of this kind exhibits this virtue."
Repeated narrative inheritance accumulates the closure-rule subsystem that makes a person's substrate produce virtue-aligned conduct in novel situations — the dispositional cultivation the tradition describes.

This is the substrate-algebraic distinction between propositional ethics (rules to apply) and dispositional ethics (closure-rules embedded in the substrate).
Lakota virtue-pedagogy installs closure rules; Western analytic-ethics propositional codification produces explicit rules to apply consciously.
Both can produce conduct that exhibits virtues, but the substrate machinery is different, and only the substrate-installed form produces virtue-aligned conduct *in novel situations the propositional code did not anticipate*.
Hence why the tradition teaches through story.
