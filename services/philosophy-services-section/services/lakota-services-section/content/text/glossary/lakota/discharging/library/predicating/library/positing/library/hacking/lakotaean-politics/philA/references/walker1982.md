# Walker 1982 — Lakota Society

**Walker, James R.
(1982). *Lakota Society*.
Edited by Raymond J.
DeMallie.
Lincoln, NE: University of Nebraska Press / Bison Books.
(Material compiled by Walker between 1896 and 1914; this volume edited and published posthumously from his papers.)**

## Annotation

The standard ethnographic compilation of Oglala Lakota social and political organization from the late pre-reservation and early reservation period.
Walker was the agency physician at Pine Ridge from 1896 to 1914 and recorded a substantial body of testimony from Oglala informants — most importantly his collaboration with George Sword, who served as an interpreter and a Lakota knowledge holder in his own right.
Walker also worked with anthropologist Clark Wissler.
The material includes documents on the Seven Council Fires, Oglala bands, camp organization, government and leadership, the akicita, the "Big Bellies" (the inner council), the shirt-wearers, and the structure of council decision-making.

The volume is in two main parts: primary documents (the informant accounts as Walker compiled them) and Walker's syntheses.
DeMallie's editorial work is itself a major scholarly contribution — including framing the material's context, the limits of Walker's access, and the relation between Walker's interpretations and the Lakota voices he recorded.

## Status as a source

Walker is a non-Lakota observer.
The material is filtered through interpreters, through Walker's interpretive frame, and through the editorial work of DeMallie.
The compilation is also a temporal snapshot: it records what informants in 1896–1914 remembered about pre-reservation practice, with the reservation period's disruptions already shaping memory.

These caveats notwithstanding, *Lakota Society* is the most-cited ethnographic source on Oglala political structure (the omniciye, naca, naca yuha, wakichunza, akicita) and on the band/oyate/Oceti Sakowin federation, and the primary documents preserve substantial Lakota voice.
This subtheory cites Walker for the late-19th-century structural record while treating contemporary Lakota voices ([deloria-vine-jr-1973](../../philB/references/deloria-vine-jr-1973.md), [marshall2002](../../philB/references/marshall2002.md), [white-hat1999](../../philB/references/white-hat1999.md), [white-hat2012](../../philB/references/white-hat2012.md), [estes2019](../../philB/references/estes2019.md)) as the primary authority on Lakota political life.
