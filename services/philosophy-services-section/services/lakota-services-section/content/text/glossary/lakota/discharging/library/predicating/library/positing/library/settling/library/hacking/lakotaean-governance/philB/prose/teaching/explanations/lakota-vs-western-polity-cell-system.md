# The Lakota polity-cell-system vs. the Western abstract polity

The Lakota polity-cell-system in [polity-cell-system](../objects/polity-cell-system.md) and the Western abstract polity in [`predicated-governance/objects/polity`](../../../objects/polity.md) are *not* the same shape stretched to different content.
The shapes themselves differ.

The Western abstract polity-cell-system takes $E$ = "agents" (humans, juridical persons, institutions) and $\Pi$ = "deontic relations" (Hohfeld's eightfold, plus the institutional-fact predicates Searle requires).
The entity-set is homogeneous on legal-personality; the predicate-set is deontic.
The polity is what installs and maintains the deontic relations on this restricted substrate.

The Lakota polity-cell-system is wider on the entity side and primitively relational on the predicate side: $E_\mathrm{lak}$ admits four-leggeds, wingeds, standing-ones, stones, places, ancestors, wakȟáŋ-beings as full substrate entities; $\Pi_\mathrm{lak}$ is dominated by kin-relations and conduct-relations rather than deontic relations.
The Hohfeldian deontic structure exists as the office-relations $\Pi^\mathrm{off}$ — one predicate-class among nine — not as the predicate-set's organizing logic.

The Western abstract polity is recoverable as the *restriction* of $(E_\mathrm{lak}, \Pi_\mathrm{lak}, \mathcal{C}_\mathrm{lak}, w_\mathrm{lak})$ to $(E^\mathrm{hum}, \Pi^\mathrm{off}, \mathcal{C}|_{\Pi^\mathrm{off}}, w \equiv 0)$ — keep only the human entity-class, keep only the office-and-deontic predicates, and set the wakȟáŋ-marking to constantly-zero.
The restriction is a quotient morphism on the substrate; it is content-bearing in what it discards.

The discards encode metaphysical commitments.
Restricting to $E^\mathrm{hum}$ commits to a polity-form in which non-humans are objects rather than parties.
Restricting to $\Pi^\mathrm{off}$ commits to a polity-form in which kinship is private rather than constitutional.
Setting $w \equiv 0$ commits to a polity-form without ceremonial substrate-marking — the secular state-form.
Each of these is a substantive choice, not a neutral generalization.
The Lakota polity-cell-system makes none of them; it sits on the wider shape that the Western form *narrows*.

The lift goes the other way too.
Given a Western polity-cell-system, one can attempt to extend it back to the Lakota shape: add non-human entity-classes, add kin-predicate-classes, add a non-trivial wakȟáŋ-marking.
The extension is structurally possible but the *closure-rule subsystems* of the original do not automatically extend — extending the polity-cell-system without extending $\mathcal{S}$ produces a polity-form that *holds* cells over non-human kin but has no closure rules that fire on them.
The wider substrate without the wider discipline is empty wider substrate.

The practical consequence for political theory: importing Western governance vocabulary into the Lakota polity-form requires choosing whether to restrict the Lakota substrate (loses content) or extend the Western closure-rule system to fire on the wider entity-and-predicate set (requires substantive work; rarely done in legal-theoretic literature on Indigenous polities).
The substrate-algebra makes the choice explicit and visible.
Most of the twentieth-century federal-Indian-law literature is in the restriction direction — analyzing the Lakota polity in Hohfeldian-restricted terms — which is why its findings persistently fail to capture polity-features that live in the discarded entity- and predicate-classes.
