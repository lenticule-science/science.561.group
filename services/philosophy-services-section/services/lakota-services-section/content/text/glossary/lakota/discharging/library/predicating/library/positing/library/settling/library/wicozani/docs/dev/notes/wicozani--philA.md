# Wicozani

Wičhózani — well-being, wholeness, health.
The Lakota understanding of what it is for a person, a tiyospaye, or a people to be *well*.
Wicozani is not the absence of disease in a Western-medical sense; it is the condition of integrated wholeness across the dimensions in which a being exists — physical, mental, emotional, spiritual, and relational.

## Why it is irreducibly relational

A person cannot be wičhózani alone.
Because reality is structured by [mitakuye oyasin](mitakuye-oyasin.md), a person's wellness is bound up with the wellness of their relatives — human and other-than-human.
A person physically healthy but in broken relation with their kin is not wičhózani in the full sense.
A people whose individuals are surviving but whose collective ceremony has been disrupted is not wičhózani as a people.

This integration means that interventions on one dimension that ignore others can fail.
A Western-medical treatment that addresses physical disease but leaves the relational and ceremonial dimensions untouched may not restore wičhózani.
Conversely, ceremony — wiping-of-tears, inipi, hanblecheya — is itself wellness-restoring work in the Lakota frame; it is not adjunct therapy.
The unity of dimensions is the point.

## Health and the polity

Because the people-as-polity has its own wičhózani, the political-historical traumas inflicted on the Oceti Sakowin (boarding-school assimilation, the massacre at Wounded Knee, the Pick-Sloan dams, the ongoing settler-state pressure on land and language) are public-health questions in the Lakota frame, not metaphorical ones.
The contemporary work of language revitalization, ceremony reclamation, kinship-rebuilding, and political refusal is wičhózani work — restoration of the people's wholeness — at the polity scale.

This framing has been substantially developed in contemporary Lakota and broader Indigenous public-health and social-work literature.
The Lakota term has entered some clinical contexts where it is treated with appropriate care (Sicangu Lakota Oyate's *Wicozani* initiative on the Rosebud Reservation is one example), though it should not be flattened into a clinical wellness-metric — the relational and ceremonial dimensions are not optional.
