# Extended entity set

The Lakota entity set $E_{\mathrm{lak}}$ is structurally larger than the entity sets implicit in standard Euro-philosophical metaphysics: it includes humans, all the named other-than-human oyate, places, the elements, ancestors, and spirits as full substrate entities.

## The structure

$$E_{\mathrm{lak}} \;=\; E_{\mathrm{hum}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{four-legged}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{winged}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{standing}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{rooted}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{swim}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{crawl}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{stone}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{elements}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{place}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{ancestor}} \,\sqcup\, E_{\mathrm{spirit}}$$

Each subset names a class of relatives in the Lakota sense.
Within each class, individual entities are distinguished: a specific buffalo is an entity, a specific stone is an entity, [Pȟahá Sápa](../../../../positing/library/hacking/lakotaean-politics/b4/paha-sapa/paha-sapa.md) is an entity, a specific ancestor is an entity.
The partition is not metaphysical hierarchy but classification — different classes of relatives, all entities, all in kin-relation with one another.

## Why the partition matters

Kin-predicates are partition-sensitive in some cases and not others. *Relative-of* applies across the partition: a human can be *relative-of* a buffalo, a place can be *relative-of* a people. *Spirit-relation-to* applies most prominently with $E_{\mathrm{spirit}}$ as one relatum. *Relation-to-place* takes $E_{\mathrm{place}}$ as the second relatum.
The partition gives the closure rules domain conditions to fire on.

Some predicates that look like they should be partition-restricted in fact are not. *Speaks-to* — in the substantive Lakota sense, not the narrow English sense — applies across the partition: a person can be spoken-to by an ancestor, by a place, by a spirit, by a buffalo.
The Lakota tradition's commitment to relational ontology means communication-cells, knowledge-cells, and counsel-cells admit relata from across $E_{\mathrm{lak}}$, not only from $E_{\mathrm{hum}}$.

## Status of the wider entity set

Some readers from Western philosophical backgrounds may treat the non-human entities as metaphorical or as anthropomorphism.
The substrate does not.
A predication cell $\langle \text{this stone}, p, e\rangle$ takes a bilattice value just as any other cell does, and closure rules treat the stone as a substrate entity.
The metaphysical decision is taken at the entity-set level, not finessed at the cell level.

This is the substrate-algebraic content of the Lakota tradition's *kin-cosmology*.
Mitakuye oyasin is not a sentiment; it is the structural specification of $E_{\mathrm{lak}}$ as the union of the relative-classes listed above.

## Place-entities and their standing

$E_{\mathrm{place}}$ deserves a note.
Specific places — Pȟahá Sápa most explicitly, but also the Missouri River (Mníšoše), specific lakes, specific buttes, specific ceremonial grounds — are full entities, not coordinates.
A cell $\langle \text{Pȟahá Sápa}, \texttt{heart-of}, \text{Oceti Sakowin}\rangle$ takes a bilattice value; the relation between people and place is substrate content.
Vine Deloria Jr.'s ([deloria-vine-jr-1973](../../../../positing/library/hacking/lakotaean-politics/philB/references/deloria-vine-jr-1973.md)) distinction between *spatial* and *temporal* religious orientation gets its substrate-algebraic shape here: place-entities are real, named, in relation, witnessable.

## Ancestors

$E_{\mathrm{ancestor}}$ also deserves a note.
Ancestors are full substrate entities.
The Lakota tradition does not partition the dead into a separate metaphysical regime; the seventh rite [wanáǧi yuhápi](../../../../indigenous/theories/lakotaean-philosophy/concepts/seven-rites.md) (keeping of the soul) is the ceremony of maintaining a specific ancestor-relation cell over time, then releasing it.
The polity carries living kin-relations with its ancestors as part of its substrate; ancestor-cells are not historical references but current relations.
