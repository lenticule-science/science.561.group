# The four virtues

The four qualities of conduct that the Lakota tradition holds as constitutive of being-Lakota in the proper sense: *wówačhantognaka* (generosity), *wóksape* (wisdom), *wóohitika* (bravery), and *wówačhiŋtȟaŋka* (fortitude / perseverance).

Different Lakota teachers organize the virtues differently.
Joseph Marshall III's *The Lakota Way* ([marshall2002](../../../../hacking/lakotaean-politics/philB/references/marshall2002.md)) treats twelve virtues (the four plus respect, honor, perseverance, love, humility, sacrifice, truth, and compassion).
Other sources reduce to four or expand differently.
The four above are the most consistently named across the tradition; the more expansive lists are not departures but elaborations.

## Each virtue, briefly

**Wówačhantognaka — generosity** (sometimes written *čháŋteyuke* — "having a heart").
The disposition to give.
Not as a tactical exchange or as a social-performance, but as the substantive practice of treating one's relatives as relatives.
In a kinship-organized polity ([lakotaean-politics](../../../../../../sociology/theories/politics/theories/lakotaean-politics/_index.md)), generosity is the substance of how the polity holds together — a giveaway is not symbolic, it is the kin-relation enacted.
Leaders are recognized as leaders by their generosity; those who hoard are not leaders.

**Wóksape — wisdom.** The disposition of one who has learned from elders, from experience, and from ceremony, and who can apply that learning to the matter at hand.
Wisdom in this sense is not stockpiled propositional knowledge; it is the capacity to act well in the specific situation, drawing on the depth of one's relational and ceremonial formation.

**Wóohitika — bravery.** The disposition to face what must be faced.
In traditional context, bravery in war was paradigmatic; in broader context, bravery is the disposition to do what one's relations require even when it is hard.
Bravery is *not* recklessness — recklessness is a failure of wóksape — and it is not violence-seeking.
It is the steadiness of conduct in the face of difficulty.

**Wówačhiŋtȟaŋka — fortitude / perseverance.** Sometimes glossed as "having a great mind."
The disposition to endure across time.
Where bravery is the steadiness of a moment, fortitude is the steadiness of years.
A people that has persisted across boarding schools, dispossession, and ongoing settler-state pressure has exercised wówačhiŋtȟaŋka at the polity scale.

## How they are taught

The virtues are taught primarily through story — the narrative tradition that Joseph Marshall III brings forward in *The Lakota Way*.
A story embeds the virtue in a relational situation; the listener absorbs the disposition by attending to how it appeared in the situation.
This is markedly different from propositional ethics: the virtues are not principles to apply but dispositions to cultivate, and the cultivation happens through repeated narrative inheritance and through apprenticeship.

## Why this is political

The virtues are the qualifications for [naca](../../../../hacking/lakotaean-politics/b4/naca/naca.md).
A naca's authority is the recognition of demonstrated conduct in the virtues; a naca who fails them is no longer a naca.
The virtues are not optional adornments on a polity that could exist without them; they *are* the polity's mechanism of [legitimation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/content/text/glossary/concepts/legitimation.md).

## Sources

Joseph Marshall III ([marshall2002](../../../../hacking/lakotaean-politics/philB/references/marshall2002.md)) is the most accessible contemporary treatment, with story-pedagogy as the method.
Albert White Hat Sr. ([white-hat2012](../../../../hacking/lakotaean-politics/philB/references/white-hat2012.md)) presents the virtues in oral-teaching form from Sicangu transmission.
The four-virtues structure is widely attested across Lakota teaching, including in non-Lakota ethnographic sources (Walker, Brown) where it appears with appropriate filtering.
