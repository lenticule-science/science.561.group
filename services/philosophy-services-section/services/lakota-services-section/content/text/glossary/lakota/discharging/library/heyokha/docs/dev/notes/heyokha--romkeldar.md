## The vocation

A heyoka is one who has dreamed of the Wakíŋyaŋ — the Thunder-beings — and through that dream is obligated to enact contrary conduct as the discharge of the vision.
The vocation is not chosen; the dream comes unsought, and once it has come, the contrary-conduct is required.
Failure to discharge it is dangerous.

## What contrary conduct looks like

Riding the horse backward.
Saying yes when meaning no. Plunging the hands into boiling water.
Comic performance at ceremony that produces laughter — laughter as medicine.
Public failure of a virtue as substrate-evidence of the virtue's importance.
The conduct is not parody; it is deeper in the sacred than ordinary conduct.

## Why the role is structurally necessary

A tiospaye whose substrate carries productive contradiction (predications at ⊤ — both true and false, contradicting and held) needs someone to bear that contradiction in lived practice.
Without the heyoka-vocation, the substrate's closure operation collapses to strict idempotence and loses its capacity to hold paradox.
The heyoka is the substrate's lax-idempotence-bearer in lived form.

## Protective immunity

The heyoka's contrary conduct does not fire the standard reputation-and-standing-loss closure rules of the polity's conduct-discipline.
The polity distinguishes heyoka-conduct cells from ordinary-conduct cells; heyoka-conduct is exempt from standard virtue-evaluation and fires the heyoka-specific rule-class.
