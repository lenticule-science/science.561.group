# How does Mayer-Vietoris federation behave in a mixed-governance-regime cover?

**Question.** Contemporary Lakota political life operates simultaneously across two structurally distinct governance regimes: the kin-relational tiyóšpaye (operating under wolakȟota) and the federally-recognized-tribal-government apparatus (operating under settler-state administrative closure rules — IRA tribal-government structures, federal-tribe interfaces, blood-quantum identification, BIA administrative categories). In framework terms, a contemporary Lakota polity's federation cover *mixes* arenas from these two regimes. How does Mayer-Vietoris federation cohomology behave in this mixed-regime cover, and what structural features does it have that single-regime federation does not?

## What is established

- Federation between arenas in $\mathbf{CGS}$ is parallel composition $\|$, with the Mayer-Vietoris long exact sequence decomposing the federated cohomology (per [`cross-arena-mayer-vietoris`](../claims/cross-arena-mayer-vietoris.md), [`federation-is-parallel-composition`](../claims/federation-is-parallel-composition.md)).
- In the single-regime case (e.g., a federation of tiyóšpaye under wolakȟota; or a federation of ships under kyriarchical command), the federated polity's cohomology has the standard Mayer-Vietoris decomposition.
- The corpus's [`dual-governance-context`](../../teaching/explanations/dual-governance-context.md) names the conflation failure mode: treating two regimes as if they were one. This is a distinct issue from cover-mixing — the present question concerns federations that *correctly* recognize the regimes as distinct and yet cover the polity with arenas from both.
- Glen Coulthard's *Red Skin, White Masks* (2014) treats the politics of the settler-tribe interface at length, with the recognition-politics critique as the load-bearing thesis. Nick Estes 2019 traces the historical-political navigation of this interface in the Oceti Sakowin case.

## What remains open

Three sub-questions:

1. *How do closure rules compose across regimes in the cover?* The tiyóšpaye-arena's closure rules are wolakȟota's; the federally-recognized-tribal-government-arena's closure rules are the settler-state's administrative rules. The federation's closure rules in the cover-mixed setting are... what? The framework's federation-convergence theorem assumes shared closure-rule subsystem; in cover-mixing it does not hold. What does the convergence look like when the closure-rule subsystems differ across the cover?
2. *What is the Mayer-Vietoris cokernel content in the mixed case?* In the single-regime case, cokernel content is overlap-content unrepresentable by constituent positions in the *same* vocabulary. In the mixed-regime case, the overlap may carry content that's representable in *one* regime's vocabulary but not the *other*. The cokernel structure is more complex; what new types of federation-only content arise?
3. *How does the asymmetry between regimes manifest?* The settler-state tribal-government apparatus is *not* structurally on equal footing with the tiyóšpaye's wolakȟota — one is imposed from outside (per Coulthard's recognition-politics critique), the other is internal to the kin-relational tradition. The federation cover is asymmetric; what categorical apparatus describes this asymmetry?

## What an answer would look like

A satisfactory answer would:

- Develop a *mixed-regime federation cohomology* — Mayer-Vietoris in the case where constituent arenas operate under different closure-rule subsystems, with explicit treatment of the cross-regime restriction maps.
- Identify the new classes of cokernel content that emerge in the mixed case — content that is *expressible in one regime but not the other* on the overlap, as a structural class distinct from the single-regime cokernel content.
- Address the asymmetry: provide a categorical apparatus that distinguishes *imposed* federation cover (settler-state administrative arenas added to a tiyóšpaye's federation without the tiyóšpaye's consent) from *consensual* federation cover (oyate-level federation across tiyóšpaye operating under shared wolakȟota). The former is what Coulthard's recognition-politics critique names; the latter is the tradition's own confederal structure.

## Why it matters

The Oceti Sakowin operates across this mixed cover continuously. Standing Rock organizing, federal-tribe negotiations, pipeline resistance, treaty-rights litigation — all occur at the interface where tiyóšpaye-arena content must be coordinated with federal-administrative-arena content. The framework's apparatus for single-regime federation does not address this case. A polity-theoretic analysis that misses the mixed-cover structure would misidentify what's structurally happening in contemporary Indigenous political life.

The connection to the broader Indigenous-political-theoretical literature: the Mohawk Interruptus framework (Simpson 2014), the politics-of-refusal apparatus (Coulthard 2014), the continuity-through-disruption thesis (Estes 2019) all engage with this interface from their respective standpoints. A framework-side mixed-cover-cohomology analysis would compose with these political-theoretical analyses to give cohomological content to the structural facts the theorists name politically.

## Position

The substantive political content draws on Coulthard 2014, Simpson 2014, Estes 2019; the framework-side mixed-cover-cohomology analysis is open theoretical work.
