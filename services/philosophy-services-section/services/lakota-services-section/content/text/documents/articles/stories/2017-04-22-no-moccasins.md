Three Horns was a warrior. No Moccasins was his wife.

Three Horns lived many years. He led great hunts; he led great attacks. He provided guidance to the elders.

Three Horns was an old man. He became sick, and would die. Many came to visit and mourn. His village doubled in size.

Three Horns gathered the elders from the visitors. He told them, “Before I die, I’m going to tell a story.

“When I was young I went exploring. I took a nap next to a trail. I woke up next to a beautiful face. She said, ‘What strange things grow on this trail.’ We fell in love. We married. I moved to her village.

“Another village attacked us and kidnapped two people. I joined the warband to get them back. The warband got them back, but I was captured. I was made a slave. I tried to escape, but was kept tighter with each attempt. I wept. I would never see my wife’s face again.

“But I did. I thought it was a dream until she touched me. She cut the ropes that bound me. We fled.

“My captors were humiliated. They didn’t know I had help. They sent their best to catch us. We hid in a bear den. I was too weak, but my wife went to misdirect them.

“When she returned she was barefoot, her feet muddy. I asked her what happened. She said she left her shoes by a creek and made false trails, but the enemies got too close and she had to hide.

“She dove into a creek and into a beaver dam. I laughed at her, muddy and wet. I said we should call her No Moccasins, to remember her bravery misleading the enemy.

“She agreed, but only if no one knew she had helped me escape.

“When we returned to the village, I didn’t tell anyone how I escaped. They assumed I did it on my own, and the humiliation of being captured was made even. So I was allowed to go on warbands and hunting trips, and help the community. But it was all with her help.

“I’m telling you elders this tonight so you can tell the rest of the people tomorrow, and share my wife’s good virtue with our people. And when I die, give me a plain scaffold, so that my deeds—which I couldn’t have done any of—will go to her.”

And so the elders told the story to the other people the next day, and when Three Horns died he was given a plain scaffold.

No Moccasins died seven years later, and was buried with all Three Horns’s regalia and prizes—and hundreds of moccasins, so she would never run out no matter where she went from here.
