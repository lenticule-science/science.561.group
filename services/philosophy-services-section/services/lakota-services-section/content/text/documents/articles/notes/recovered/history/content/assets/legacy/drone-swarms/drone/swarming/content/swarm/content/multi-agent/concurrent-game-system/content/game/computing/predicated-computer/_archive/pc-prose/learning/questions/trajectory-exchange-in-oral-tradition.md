# How does oral-tradition transmission realize the framework's trajectory-exchange discipline?

**Question.** The agentic-drone-swarm framework's four-regime federation discipline requires that in the aperiodic regime ($\kappa \geq \omega_1$), *trajectories* must be shipped between federated polities rather than just endpoint states — because the closure ordinal is non-finitary and intermediate stages carry information not recoverable from endpoints alone. How does the Lakota tradition's oral-tradition substrate — the ceremonial substrate (seven rites), storytelling apprenticeship through which the virtues are taught, the historical-narrative work that Estes 2019 articulates as central to contemporary Oceti Sakowin life — realize this trajectory-exchange discipline?

## What is established

- The four-regime federation discipline holds: in the aperiodic regime, trajectory must be shipped, not just endpoints (per the four-regime dichotomy and the federation-convergence-by-regime analysis in [`federation-is-parallel-composition`](../claims/federation-is-parallel-composition.md)).
- The 561 polity's function settles aperiodically; the tiyóšpaye is in the aperiodic regime; the Oceti Sakowin's continuity-through-disruption is the political-historical fact of this regime's resistance to forced termination.
- The Lakota tradition carries an extensive oral-tradition substrate — the seven rites, the formal storytelling and apprenticeship that transmits virtue-knowledge, the historical-narrative tradition that Estes 2019's *Our History is the Future* names as constitutive of contemporary political continuity.
- Vine Deloria Jr.'s body of work (especially *Spirit and Reason*) treats oral tradition not as a primitive substitute for writing but as a *different* kind of knowledge-transmission with its own structural properties — relational, contextual, performative.

## What remains open

Specifically: how does the structural form of oral-tradition transmission realize the framework's trajectory-shipping requirement? Three sub-questions:

1. *What counts as a trajectory in the increment-sequence sense?* The framework's *trajectory* is the full sequence $(v_\tau)_{\tau < \lambda}$ of valuations indexed by transfinite ordinals, plus the increment sequence $U = (u_\tau)_{\tau < \lambda}$ that drives the settling. A trajectory is *more* than the endpoint state; it carries the order-dependent information that intermediate stages contribute. How does oral tradition carry this — what is the oral-tradition analog of an increment-sequence?
2. *How do ceremonial events function structurally?* The seven rites are not arbitrary; they are structured events with specific positions in the polity's settling. Are ceremonies *trajectory-checkpoint events* — substrate-content moments at which the trajectory's state is made visible to the membership and to descendants? If so, the ceremonial substrate is the trajectory-exchange protocol itself.
3. *How does generational transmission realize parallel composition across time?* A tiyóšpaye persists across generations through storytelling apprenticeship and ceremonial substrate. This is, structurally, parallel composition across temporal scales — each generation a sub-arena, the tradition the parallel composition. The framework's nominal-set name-passing apparatus describes channel mobility *spatially* (across federated polities at one time); what is the temporal-name-passing apparatus that describes mobility *across generations*?

## What an answer would look like

A satisfactory answer would:

- Identify the structural unit of oral-tradition trajectory (the equivalent of $u_\tau$ in the framework's increment sequence) — likely a specific ceremonial event, narrative-telling, or apprenticeship-moment with substrate-content correlates.
- Establish that ceremonial events are *trajectory checkpoints* in a precise sense — that the ceremonial substrate's structure realizes the framework's trajectory-vs-endpoint distinction.
- Develop the temporal-parallel-composition apparatus: generations as sub-arenas; the tradition as the parallel composition; the structural shape of names-across-generations.
- Compose with media theory and oral-tradition scholarship to ground the analysis in the existing literature on what oral tradition structurally *is* (Albert Lord's *Singer of Tales* lineage; Walter Ong's *Orality and Literacy* — for the broader field; specifically Lakota oral-tradition scholarship for the relevant case).

## Why it matters

If oral tradition is the trajectory-exchange protocol, then the settler-state's various pressures on oral tradition (boarding schools, language suppression, separation of children from grandparents, religious suppression of ceremony) are structurally attempts to disrupt the trajectory-exchange — to force the polity into a state where only endpoints are available and the trajectory has been lost. The Oceti Sakowin's resistance to these pressures, and the contemporary reclamation work that Estes 2019 chronicles, is then the political-historical form of *restoring the trajectory-exchange protocol*. The framework's trajectory-exchange discipline and the tradition's oral-substrate continuity are the same structural commitment.

## Position

Open theoretical question crossing the agentic-drone-swarm framework with media theory and oral-tradition scholarship. The Lakota-side substantive content is grounded in Vine Deloria Jr.'s body of work and Estes 2019, with substantive ceremonial content remaining in emsenn's authorial domain. The structural framework question — what trajectory-exchange means in the oral-tradition substrate — can be developed independently. Surfaced in the tiyóšpaye-is-agentic-drone-swarm survey, §5.
