# Buddhist Services Section

The Buddhist Services Section holds philosophical traditions rooted in
Buddhist thought. Its own topic file
(`content/text/glossary/topics/buddhist.md`) is explicit that the
section's current content centers on logic — the catuṣkoṭi and pramāṇa
theory — while the tradition itself encompasses metaphysics, ethics,
epistemology, and philosophy of mind developed across South, East, and
Southeast Asian contexts over two and a half millennia, none of which is
represented here yet.

Its own `content/text/documents/articles/notes/buddhist-logic.md` is a
teaching-oriented overview contrasting Buddhist formal inference with
Western logic: Dignāga's three-condition schema (*trairūpya*) for valid
inference from the *Pramāṇasamuccaya*, and the catuṣkoṭi (tetralemma) —
affirmation, denial, both, neither — from Nāgārjuna's
*Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*, read against classical bivalence and the law of
excluded middle.

Two subsections carry this further:

- `madhyamaka-services-section/` — the catuṣkoṭi itself, as a glossary
  term and a note comparing it to Western logic.
- `pramana-theory-services-section/` — pramāṇa (valid-cognition theory)
  as its own topic and term.
